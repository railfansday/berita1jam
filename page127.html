Flagship Pioneering Launches London Lab to Build AI-Native Companies

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Flagship Pioneering announced on November 16, 2026 that it is establishing Flagship Origination Lab, U.K. in London, backed by an initial £100 million commitment. The lab will conceive, test, fund and build AI-native platform companies, and is Flagship’s first dedicated company-creation unit outside the United States.

Operating within Flagship Labs, the London unit will focus on human and planetary health and other frontiers of science and technology. It brings a company-creation model developed in Cambridge, Massachusetts, into a locally rooted UK ecosystem connected to Flagship’s global organization.

Founder and CEO Noubar Afeyan said Flagship anticipates scaling its commitment through partnerships to attract an additional £500 million. The additional amount depends on future partnerships and remains separate from the capital committed at launch.

Flagship expects to recruit and train approximately 50 scientist-entrepreneurs and operators for the initial UK team. The local company-creation ecosystem will remain connected to the broader organization, including its Pioneering Intelligence and Pioneering Medicines business units.

Flagship’s model starts with scientific questions and develops them into platforms, products and companies. The firm says it has created more than 130 companies over more than 25 years, including Moderna, providing the company-creation record it is now extending to the UK.

Avak Kahvejian, president of Flagship Labs and a general partner at Flagship Pioneering, described origination as a discipline led by teams of scientist-entrepreneurs who “begin with bold questions about what could be possible.” The London team will accordingly be involved in generating and testing ideas as well as financing and building the resulting companies.

That structure gives Flagship a role from the earliest stage of company formation. Rather than operating solely as an investor in existing startups, the lab is designed to originate the underlying scientific platforms and then build businesses around them within its own ecosystem.

The launch follows three years of Flagship partnering and conducting R&D across the UK. It opened a London office and signed a memorandum of understanding with the UK government in 2023 to extend the global reach and impact of its companies.

Flagship describes Quotient Therapeutics, launched during that period, as its first transatlantic company. Its UK activity also includes clinical trials involving Flagship-founded companies such as Generate:Biomedicines, alongside partnerships across academia, data and regulatory innovation.

Kahvejian said the UK’s science base, clinical-trial infrastructure, genomics and health-data assets, agile regulatory environment, and scientists and entrepreneurs made it a deliberate choice for the expansion. Those resources provide the foundation on which Flagship intends to build its local company-creation operation.

Chris McDonald MP, Minister for Science, Innovation and Investment, said the government wants more AI and life-sciences businesses to turn research and technology into jobs and economic growth, with Flagship’s commitment helping new UK companies take breakthroughs to international markets.

The expansion adds local company origination to Flagship’s existing UK research, partnerships and clinical activity. Its larger financial ambition depends on attracting partners, while the lab’s operational test is whether the new team can turn those scientific and clinical assets into new AI-native companies.

Mercari Plans AI-Led US Venture for Japanese Pop Culture Goods

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Mercari announced on November 16, 2026 that it had decided to form Mercari Surugaya Inc. with Suruga-ya, a US venture for Japanese entertainment and hobby products. Mercari will own 51% of the Delaware company, which is planning AI-supported appraisal, pricing, inventory and product-discovery operations.

The venture is planned for December 2026, with Shunsuke Sako as representative and Suruga-ya holding the remaining 49%. Its activities will cover online sales and purchases, procurement and physical-store operation. US activity is planned to start in Los Angeles, with products also sold through Mercari US and the Mercari Global App. Service launch dates remain undetermined.

Appraisal, product registration and price setting are among the retail processes the companies want to make more efficient with AI. The planned applications also cover purchasing, product catalogs and inventory management. Shoppers would see separate applications in item search and supplementary product information. The announcement provides no performance results for these planned uses.

Mercari CEO Shintaro Yamada said the venture would evolve “operations with AI at the core,” connecting that approach to Mercari’s ambition to build a global marketplace. Suruga-ya CEO Tsunashige Sugiyama described the entity as a major step toward his company’s long-held goal of expanding seriously in the US.

The retail operation also has a data purpose. Mercari says US market-price and demand data can only be obtained through actual transactions in that market. Suruga-ya’s catalog and appraisal knowledge will supply product context for records generated by those transactions. The combined information is intended to improve the user experience in search, recommendations and authenticity verification, while also supporting both parent companies’ businesses.

Mercari is contributing US distribution and its experience with consumer-service design and AI. Suruga-ya’s contribution is built around specialist inventory, procurement, appraisal and sales expertise. The release puts its retail network at 160 stores in Japan and overseas as of October 2026, with tens of millions of items in inventory and catalog data covering more than approximately 30 million products. Its specialties include figurines, trading cards, games and anime merchandise.

The two companies already had a capital and business partnership, concluded in December 2025. The earlier agreement covered inventory coordination and combined online and offline distribution, as well as plans to connect Suruga-ya’s catalog and authentication knowledge with Mercari’s transaction data and AI technology. Since then, Suruga-ya items have been sold through Mercari.

The planned entity would give that collaboration a jointly owned US operating base, with local procurement and store operations alongside Mercari’s sales channels. It also places AI inside the design of the retail business, with transactions intended to generate data for subsequent services. The central execution question is whether the companies can build that data loop from Los Angeles and combine Suruga-ya’s specialist knowledge with Mercari’s technology to deliver efficient retail work and more useful product information for US buyers.

VC Firm Plural Closes On $1.1B To Invest In European Startups With Potential For ‘GDP-Level Impact’

📅 Nov 15, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Early-stage startup investor Plural says it has closed on its largest fund to date — just over $1.1 billion, or 1 billion euros — to lead early-stage investments across Europe.

With the announcement, Plural joins a select group of European venture firms that have raised funds of a billion dollars or more in recent years. Others include Balderton Capital, Atomico and Index Ventures, which is co-headquartered in London and San Francisco. It also follows a Q3 that marked Europe’s strongest venture funding quarter in years.

“Our common theme is ambition. We go after the founders that are building the most ambitious things we can humanly find,” Sten Tamkivi, a co-founder of the firm, said in an interview with Crunchbase News. “Our stated objective remains to have a GDP-level impact on Europe with the companies that we back.”

Plural’s portfolio is a mix of energy, compute, defense, robotics, health and software. The firm first backed fusion energy company Proxima Fusion in its 2023 pre-seed round and has also invested in energy storage company Ore Energy. In defense and aerospace, its investments include European defense tech company Helsing and hypersonic flight and missile company Hypersonica, whose Series A it led in 2026. Plural also led the 2024 Series B for The Exploration Co., which develops space transportation.

In robotics and manufacturing, Plural has backed Monumental, which develops bricklaying robots; Sunrise Robotics, which builds robots for manufacturing; CircuitHub, which manufactures circuit board assemblies; and Upside Robotics, which develops agricultural robots. In compute, its investments include optical AI accelerator company Olix and photonic networking company Oriole Networks. More recently, Plural led the pre-seed round for Callosum, which is building a routing layer for heterogeneous compute.

Its investments have shifted year to year and fund to fund based on where the most ambitious founders are pulling the firm, said Tamkivi.

“Europe has agonized maybe a little bit too much about, ‘Hey, we missed this wave in the models, and the big labs are in the U.S. and China,’  and so forth. But if you look at the rest of the pyramid, we’re doing well,” he said.

The latest fund is the firm’s third and is double the size of its fund 2, raised in 2024, and 4x its first fund, closed in 2022.

Plural’s portfolio companies have had a breakout year, raising more than $3 billion in follow-on capital so far in 2026.

The team has six equal partners, all prior founders and operators based in London, Tallinn and Paris. Plural was founded by Taavet Hinrikus, co-founder of Wise, and Tamkivi co-founder of Teleport, both early employees at Skype, as well as Ian Hogarth, co-founder of Songkick, and Khaled Helioui, former CEO of Bigpoint.

The team has two other partners: Carina Namih, co-founder of HelixNano, and Pierre-Dimitri Gore-Coty, a former Uber executive.

Each partner aims to make one to three new investments per year, leaving  bandwidth to work hands-on with founders.

The firm’s entry point is seed and Series A, where it likes to lead. “We’re still early-stage investors, so two-thirds of this money that we raise goes into early stage. What we’ve tagged on top is that now we have more capital to follow through in our winners, and  double down and triple down,” Tamkivi said, adding: “there’s more reserves to do that.”

📅 2026-11-15  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →
HLTH26: Apple launches 200K-person study to examine how consumers use health AI" style="max-width:100%; height:auto; border-radius:8px;" loading="lazy">

LAS VEGAS—Consumers are rapidly adopting consumer health AI. Roughly 1 in 3 U.S. adults have turned to an AI chatbot for health information, according to a KFF survey, and the use of health AI is even more prevalent among younger adults.

But questions remain about whether consumers understand and trust AI-generated health advice, and whether it influences how they manage their health or seek care.

Tech giant Apple launched the Apple Health AI Usage Study today with plans to recruit 200,000 participants in the U.S. to gain a better understanding of how people interact with conversational health AI tools.

The study is open to individuals 21 or older who live in the U.S., and participants must have an Apple Intelligence-enabled iPhone (iPhone 15 Pro or later) running iOS 26 or later. Apple will run the virtual research study on a dedicated Apple Health AI Study App, separate from the Apple Health and Research apps. Participants may remain in the study for up to 12 months, Apple said.

By examining what drives user trust, the quality of responses, comprehension and everyday health actions, the study aims to inform the responsible design of health AI experiences for Apple and the industry, the company said. There are also privacy implications that need to be explored, Apple representatives said. Researchers also want to evaluate how AI chatbots complement the traditional healthcare system and the handoff to physicians.

The tech company is collaborating with OpenEvidence on the research initiative, as the startup provides the underlying clinical AI technology for Apple’s Health AI Usage Study. OpenEvidence is a widely used AI-powered clinical decision support tool and medical search engine for clinicians. The company's platform pulls directly from licensed, peer-reviewed literature and clinical guidelines through formal partnerships with The New England Journal of Medicine (NEJM), JAMA, Nature, Cochrane, and the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN).

Apple said it selected OpenEvidence as a partner for its high-quality medical sources, robust privacy controls, and advanced search models.

Through Apple’s study app, participants can ask everyday health questions and receive answers in approachable language, with links to peer-reviewed clinical sources, Apple said. Users can ask about symptoms, general health questions, recovery from procedures, fitness and other health concerns. They can rate the AI's answers and indicate what was useful or lacking, Apple said.

Study participants can also choose to share HealthKit data types—such as resting heart rate, activity and sleep—to get more personalized responses to health questions. If study participants have imported clinical records into the Health app and separately agree to share them with the Health AI Usage study, the app can incorporate that information, primarily lab results, Apple representatives said.

"At Apple, we believe responsible innovation starts with scientific rigor and clinical grounding. Health is deeply personal, and conducting a dedicated research study is the most thoughtful way to understand how people engage with, comprehend, and evaluate health AI in everyday life. Insights from this study will help advance the broader scientific conversation and ensure that as technology evolves, we continue building trusted tools that genuinely support people wherever they are on their health journey," Sumbul Desai, M.D., Apple’s vice president of Health and Fitness, said in a statement.

Apple described the research initiative as a formal study overseen by an independent review board.

Participants will complete a mandatory onboarding process that includes informed consent, and they'll be asked to share demographic information such as age, race and education so researchers can examine how these variables affect participant preferences and communication needs, Apple said.

The company said study participants will receive baseline and monthly surveys, allowing researchers to examine how people use the information they receive and what happens afterward.

The Health AI Usage study app also includes tools to help people prepare for healthcare appointments, along with surveys after visits to gauge whether the use of health AI chatbots makes patients feel better prepared or empowered and improves their interactions with providers, Apple said.

The study is designed from the ground up with privacy protections, transparency and clinical guardrails, the company said. Study data will not be used to train AI models, Apple confirmed.

Apple said it has implemented security measures to protect the study data it maintains, as well as the confidentiality of participants’ information. User queries on the Health AI Usage Study app are first processed on the iPhone to remove identifying information. The queries then flow to a secure study environment managed by a third-party study support contractor where they are de-linked from individual participant IDs and screened again before being sent to OpenEvidence where answers are generated using supporting reference materials, Apple said.

The study app also features safety guardrails that are intended to detect certain concerning questions and suggest that a participant follow up with a provider or other resources if needed.

Consumers are increasingly using general AI chatbots powered by LLMs to get answers to health questions. But research indicates a wide gap exists between an AI's technical accuracy and a patient's ability to safely use that information.

"Patients and clinicians are both turning to AI, but the tools they use are being built and tested in separate worlds. When patients and doctors draw on versions of the same trustworthy tools, patients arrive prepared, conversations improve, and next steps happen seamlessly. Connecting these tools, and rigorously studying what works, is how we’ll get there," Robert M. Wachter, M.D., UCSF’s professor and chair of the Department of Medicine, said in a statement.

The Health AI Usage study builds on Apple's growing research initiatives. In 2019, the company launched its Research app and three medical studies to test how well its Apple Watch can track a person's mobility and cardiovascular health, changes in hearing and women's health and fertility. Last year, it began the Apple Health Study to use its devices, including iPhones, Apple Watch and AirPods along with third-party devices, for a longitudinal, virtual study to monitor changes in participants’ health, spanning a wide range of health and disease areas.

it’s a bird, it’s a plane, it’s funny weather! by theo triantafyllidis

📅 Nov 15, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

as extreme weather becomes harder to predict, this installation imagines a future in which the weather itself becomes the narrator of our lives.

annalise kamegawa November 15, 2026 7 min

In Theo Triantafyllidis’s video-based installation, Funny Weather, software-generated birds, planes, and clouds drift across a dreamy sky. At any point, the weather can turn gray, a flock can burst across the screen, or a kite can whip into view. To create this work, the artist draws on real-time data local to Manchester, where the installation is presented, to generate an uncanny AI-powered simulation of the world outside the exhibition space. Funny Weather, which premieres in mid-November at Aviva Studios in the UK, will travel to Hong Kong, Melbourne, and New York. In each new city, the work will draw on local data, creating a site-specific reflection of the weather in that location.

In conversation with designboom, Triantafyllidis revealed the APIs he worked with, the parallels between weather forecasting and divination, and how weather is becoming ‘the main character’. On first receiving the commission, which will travel between four international hosts, he notes, ‘I had this idea of a weather oracle or this strange weather prediction machine as a start.’

above and banner by Theo Triantafyllidis, preview still for Funny Weather, 2026 | image courtesy the ArtistTheo Triantafyllidis’s Funny Weather at Aviva Studios | image by Michael Pollard, courtesy of Factory International

To create Funny Weather, Triantafyllidis drew on three data sets. The first was the Open-Meteo API, which provides weather information: blue skies, overcast conditions, and impending thunderstorms. The next was a flight radar that reported which flights were passing over Aviva Studios. The last, which Triantafyllidis admits was the ‘most interesting’ to work with, was eBird and Merlin Bird ID – tools that track and identify bird sightings. The sightings are self-reported by users, making them a very subjective data set to work with. He notes that the data collected in eBird is ‘a way of digitizing nature that is very personal and strange. It has a bit of competitiveness to it and a bit of true love for what people are looking at.’

‘These are getting pulled daily and within intervals and fed into our custom system,’ says Triantafyllidis of how this data is translated into the visuals seen on screen. ‘With each of these three data streams, we’re [feeding] into different components of the system and driving different things.’

Although he admits that these data sets feel disparate, the artist says he is creating a kind of uncanny universe. He explains, ‘what I’m trying to do is give this data to a custom language model that we’re running and ask it to [prophesize] based on this data.’ Inside Funny Weather is what Factory International describes as a ‘bespoke locally hosted AI Oracle’ that generates the environment within the simulation. Although powered by the latest technology, the process reflects a kind of ancient logic. Triantafyllidis says it is ‘like looking back at how forecasting and divination worked in earlier human history, when maybe people were more in tune with weather and natural patterns and the general flow of things.’ He calls the task he gives the program a ‘nonsensical premise’ when considering the seemingly arbitrary selection of bird, weather, and flight-path data points. But by merging this information and transforming it into a visual landscape, he hopes that it will ‘tell us something useful and meaningful about our future.’

When talking about the installation at Aviva Studios, the artist notes that ‘sometimes the weather of our system aligns with what we see outside.’ He describes the lobby where the massive screen sits, continually morphing in response to Funny Weather and the data fed into it. ‘You can’t see the sky exactly,’ he says of the Manchester space, ‘but you can get the sense of what’s the weather outside. And here is a system [Funny Weather] attempting to align itself with the weather outside – sometimes successfully, sometimes not so successfully.’ The data itself is sometimes a bit wonky or inaccurate, causing what Triantafyllidis calls ‘stress’ in the environment. He says that sometimes there will be an influx of planes or ‘there [will be] birds that are not supposed to be there either – too early for the season or very unique, rare birds that are not seen in the area very frequently. This will affect the mood of the system.’

‘I think climate change is such a hyper object that is very tough for the human mind to comprehend,’ says Triantafyllidis on the urgency of the themes in his work. ‘We’ve reached a tipping point with the instability of the weather where we’re starting to experience really crazy shifts and weird meteorological phenomena happening on a very day-to-day basis and in every part of the world.’ He goes on to talk about the increased frequency of these extreme climate patterns and how, inevitably, the weather will become ‘this main character in our lives.’ Funny Weather acknowledges this inevitability: ‘I’m trying to imagine a good way to let the weather be the narrator of these stories, giving it this strange voice that can communicate somehow through this weird system.’
