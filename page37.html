Why you should never reply with STOP when you receive spam texts

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

An honest opt-out method for marketing messages sends a different signal to shady scammers.

You've likely noticed the "STOP to cancel" note when you receive texts from short codes. And for legitimate communications that you asked to receive from restaurants, surveys, loyalty programs and similar, STOP is a fine way to cut them off. But it's not your only option for ending unwanted texts, and for many kinds, it can do more harm than good.

Nobody wants garbage texts about phony job offers, illicit encounters or other scams, but it's important to think carefully about how you respond to them. Rather than deleting them and dealing with more in the future, you have other options — and they're ones that won't prompt more incoming junk.

Remember to treat any messages from unknown numbers with caution, since they're used for "wrong number" scams and all kinds of other schemes. Don't tap an unexpected link, even if it claims you have an unpaid toll or need to confirm details to fix a delivery problem.

However, not all the texts you get from unknown numbers are like this. Spam texts that advertise lonely singles, too-good-to-be-true job offers, package delivery scams or other types of nonsense aren't operating within normal marketing laws. You might thus reply STOP out of habit, even if they don't include a note about that.

By doing so, you're confirming to the sender that your number is active, which will likely result in you getting even more junk texts. It costs spammers almost nothing to send out many thousands of texts to random numbers, but few of those are confirmed as live, so you're making yourself a target. Replying can also demonstrate that you aren't able to recognize a text scam, possibly tagging you for future efforts if they think they can hook you.

Only reply STOP when you want to stop getting alerts you specifically signed up for. For anything else, never respond; instead, you should use your phone's tools to deal with unwanted messages safely.

On iPhone, you should see a Stop or Report Spam button under messages from unknown senders that include a STOP note. Tap this, and you can choose Delete and Stop or Delete and Report Spam. Both erase the conversation from your phone; the former sends a STOP reply after doing this, while the latter reports the message to your carrier and Apple. For messages without a mention of STOP, the only option is Report Spam.

A similar option appears at the bottom of conversations with unknown senders; tap Delete followed by Delete and Report Spam. In case the number keeps contacting you, tap the number at the top of the conversation and choose Block Contact to cut off all communication.

On Android, you can report an entire conversation as spam in Google Messages by holding it in the list, then choosing Block > Report spam. Alternatively, open a conversation, tap the three-dot More button, then choose Details > Block & report spam. You'll also have the option to block the number.

For either platform, you can also long-press the message and forward it to 7726 (SPAM on a keypad). This reports the text to your carrier for analysis, helping it block spammers across the network. You might get a response asking for the sender's number, so wait to block it until you've confirmed this.

Even when you know what to do upon receiving junk texts, it's better if you don't get them in the first place. Android and iOS both have ways to filter low-quality messages so you aren't getting alerted for nothing.

If you have an iPhone, visit Settings > Apps > Messages, where you have a few choices. Turn on Filter Spam to send obvious garbage to a separate Spam folder. Enable Screen Unknown Senders, then messages from numbers not in your contacts will go to their own folder. And under Text Message Filter, enable the built-in Text Message Filter to put other texts into groups like Transactions and Promotions.

The Allow Notifications menu controls which of these groups send you alerts. Some third-party iPhone apps offer their own message filtering options, which will appear here. Once it's all set up, access the filtered groups from the three-line Filter button at the top-right corner of Messages.

On Android, open Google Messages, tap your profile picture at the top-right corner and choose Messages settings. Choose Protection & Safety, then you can choose from Standard protection or Enhanced protection for junk messages. In your profile picture menu, choose Spam & blocked to review filtered messages.

Many mobile carriers offer their own protections from spam messages (like T-Mobile ScamShield), which are worth a look if you're still receiving trash. Depending on your carrier, you might activate this by changing a toggle in your account, dialing a code to activate or installing a separate app. The base features should be free; it's almost certainly not worth paying for any premium subscriptions on offer. Third-party tools like Truecaller are another option.

Otherwise, be careful of where you give out your number. While spammers often get your number from leaks, other times your contact details spread around when you sign up on a shady website or a company sells them. When making a new account, always check the settings to see if you can limit your information from being shared for marketing purposes. That can help stop spam calls as well as texts.

Elon Musk once again promises Tesla’s new autonomous driving is better than humans

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Elon Musk has a new promise: Version 15 of Full Self-Driving drives better than a real driver. “Completely new architecture that deserves being called ‘super’ intelligence, as it drives better than human intelligence,” he wrote on X after Tesla started sending the update to a first batch of owners.

That’s great news.

It was great news back in November 2023 too, when he wrote: “Over time, Version 12 (end-to-end neural nets) will far exceed human safety even when unsupervised.” Also in August 2025, when he said: “v14 will be better than human for sure, but I don’t know if it will be 10X. Maybe 2X to 3X. v15 has a shot at 10X.” Back then, he offered no data, and no third-party study to support his claim. It’s the same thing now.

Tesla says that the new FSD v15—which Musk says goes to wide release in December—upgrades the neural network that processes the camera sensors into information for the car to drive. It has also swapped the old lo-fi grayscale schematic 3D view for an AI-generated live street scene that looks like GTA VI-ish. The latter is the only thing that we know is real—and I have to admit it looks pretty neat—but nobody knows if the new driving engine works as well as Musk claims.

Same song, new version number

It may not matter too much. The new v15 is still Level 2 autonomous driving, which means drivers will need to watch the road at all times. While Musk says it drives better than a human, it must be a very dumb human because Tesla’s own support page says the software “does not make the vehicle autonomous” and is meant for use “only with a fully attentive driver.” That matters because, when a person sits ready to grab the wheel, every avoided crash could be the human’s save as much as the machine’s.

Tesla claims that the new FSD is “25x better handling of safety critical cases.” Better than what? Phil Duan, who leads self-driving at Tesla, said on X that “v15 is 25x safer than v14 across the fleet.” Musk answered an X user too: “Some people thought we meant 25% improvement, no we mean 25 times!” But we don’t really know about how much “safer” v14 was either. As electric mobility site Electrek points out, without real published figures or a peer-reviewed study—like this on Waymo reducing its crashes with injured people by 85%—that “25x” claim is hard to substantiate.

In a post, the company explains that its new vision encoder—the part of the network that interprets the information from the car’s video cameras—as the reason for that “25x better handling.” Now shared by FSD, Robotaxis, and Optimus humanoids, the engineering team says the new software improves “the critical intervention rate by 25x.” Tesla says the new encoder was trained on “millions of internet videos.”

According to Tesla, v15 adjusts its speed better in bad weather and school zones, steers clear of deep standing water, and is quicker to spot signs, potholes, small animals, chains and junk on the road. It doesn’t say anything about fake Looney Tunes’ painted tunnels.

It can ease off for speed cameras. And it learns from you, so if you cancel the slowdown at the same speed camera twice, by accelerating, it will stop braking at that point of the road. The company also claims that the car’s interior camera—which checks if you are paying attention to the road or not—now works more often when you wear hats and sunglasses.

Neat!

As part of the update, Tesla Reality View has changed. It now looks very similar to the live situational awareness screens you get in some Chinese cars: a hyperrealistic view of the road and your car as seen from behind at a 45-degree angle. You can see vehicles and people in 3D hyperrealistic renderings. Even the buildings are textured and lit like the real thing. It feels like a high-end car racing game. Musk claims “it’s like GTA.”

It looks neat, but it doesn’t seem to convey information better than the older user interface, which showed a much clearer representation of reality. Seen from a high angle, it was a simplified, very stylized version of the road populated with semi-transparent versions of vehicles and nearby people. In the old version, despite its perception problems, it was easy to get instant situational awareness. In the new v15, the realism obscures the information with too much darkness and glitz.

The interface also features a blue ribbon that traces the route FSD intends to drive, so you can correct it. Chevron arrows float overhead before every turn. And the map now shows several parking spots at your destination. Some Chinese car manufacturers are already displaying this information directly on the windshield, projecting computer-generated wide-screen AR graphics over the real world.

Fortunately, you can switch to a split view or just pick the GPS map view. Musk claims “you can tap any point on the reality revisualizer to make your car go there.” 

Musk says that the new UI uses the same visual features the FSD v15 driving model uses: It’s all AI generated. The AI model renders short bursts of eight frames at 36 frames per second, using just half of one of the accelerators in the car’s AI4 computer.

Still, the video may lag behind reality because the AI is not fast enough. Musk admits the lag is “a bit high due to compute constraints.” He claims Tesla has a way to trim it by about 35%. More promises.

If December’s wide release arrives on time (which may or may not happen), it will put the new FSD in a lot more hands. In 2027 we will be able to test the real value of Musk’s promises once again. Let’s stay positive. Maybe this will be it. You know what they say: 15th time’s the charm.

ABRUZZO'S TORRE ZAMBRA MAKES HISTORY IN WINE SPECTATOR'S TOP 100

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Ranked n. 40, Colle Maggio Montepulciano d'Abruzzo 2024 achieves the highest placement ever for an Abruzzo wine in the prestigious ranking.

VILLAMAGNA, Italy, Nov. 24, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Torre Zambra, the historic family-owned winery in Villamagna, Abruzzo, has earned international recognition with its Montepulciano d'Abruzzo Colle Maggio 2024, ranked No. 40 in Wine Spectator's prestigious Top 100 Wines of 2026. Scoring 92 points, the wine is the sole representative of Abruzzo in this year's ranking and achieves the highest placement ever for a wine from the region in the history of Wine Spectator's Top 100 Wines of the Year.

The 2026 list includes 22 Italian wines, primarily from Piedmont and Tuscany. This milestone reflects a new chapter for Torre Zambra, a family winery whose roots date back to 1910. Founded in 1961 by Laurentino De Cerchio, the estate is now led by fourth-generation family member Federico De Cerchio, who has spearheaded a comprehensive renewal of the winery, combining its heritage with a contemporary vision focused on quality, sustainability, and international growth.

Today, Torre Zambra oversees more than 40 hectares of vineyards farmed according to organic agricultural practices in the hills of Villamagna. Located between the Adriatic Sea and the Maiella mountain range, the area offers particularly favorable conditions for Montepulciano d'Abruzzo, the region's signature red grape.

"Being recognized among the world's top 100 wines is a tremendous source of pride, but, above all, it confirms that we are moving in the right direction," says Federico De Cerchio. "We have invested significantly in bringing new energy to a winery that has been part of our family for generations, with quality and an international outlook at the heart of everything we do. Our goal is to demonstrate that Abruzzo can compete at the highest level while remaining true to its identity and authenticity."

The Wine Spectator recognition follows an exceptional year of critical acclaim for the winery. Torre Zambra was named Up-and-Coming Winery of the Year in Gambero Rosso's Vini d'Italia 2026. Its Villamagna DOC Classico 2024 received the prestigious Tre Bicchieri distinction in the 2027 edition of the guide, marking the winery's third consecutive year earning the honor.

Further underscoring the winery's consistent recognition on the international stage, Torre Zambra has earned strong ratings from the leading wine publications, including James Suckling, which awarded 94 points to Colle Maggio Montepulciano d'Abruzzo Riserva DOC 2025 and 92 points to Piana Marina Trebbiano d'Abruzzo DOC 2025. Vinous awarded 93 points to Piana Marina Trebbiano d'Abruzzo 2024 and 91 points to Pecorino Poggio Salaia 2025. Wine Spectator also recognized the winery's portfolio, awarding 94 points to Piana Marina Trebbiano d'Abruzzo DOC 2024 and 93 points to Villamagna DOC 2024.

Beyond winemaking, Federico's vision extends to showcasing Abruzzo through hospitality and gastronomy. The newly opened Torre Zambra RESORT, set amid the vineyards of Villamagna, offers visitors an opportunity to experience the region's landscape and culture. Its Ristorante Contemporaneo reinterprets traditional Abruzzese cuisine through a modern approach, celebrating local ingredients and pairing them with the winery's wines.

Through its continued investment in winemaking, hospitality, and its local community, Torre Zambra is strengthening its position as an ambassador for Abruzzo, bringing the region's wines and distinctive character to an international audience.

Founded in 1910, Famiglia De Cerchio traces its roots to Vincenzo De Cerchio, who planted the family's first Montepulciano and Trebbiano vines in Villamagna, Abruzzo. Following the destruction of the vineyards during World War II, his son-in-law Laurentino De Cerchio rebuilt the estate, and in 1961, the first vintage of Torre Zambra was bottled. Today, fourth-generation family member Federico De Cerchio carries on the family's commitment to native Italian grape varieties, sustainable farming and terroir driven wines. The group's estates include Torre Zambra and Idi di Marzo in Abruzzo, along with Pajaru and Peri Peri in southern Italy, with a focus on sustainable and organic vineyard management.

Scientists Discover an Ancient Herbal Remedy May Help Curb Binge Drinking

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

A supplement derived from kudzu, a plant used for centuries in traditional East Asian medicine, may help people with alcohol use disorder reduce binge drinking without taking medication every day.

In a clinical trial led by researchers at UC San Francisco (UCSF), people who took a standardized kudzu extract before anticipated episodes of heavy drinking experienced significantly fewer binge-drinking days than those given a placebo. The benefits persisted for three months after participants stopped taking the supplement.

The findings, published in the American Journal of Psychiatry on November 4, suggest that taking a treatment during periods of heightened risk could offer a new strategy for managing problematic alcohol consumption.

Binge drinking accounts for more than half of the roughly 80,000 annual U.S. deaths attributed to excessive alcohol consumption. Despite the scale of the problem, fewer than 10% of people with alcohol use disorder have ever received medication to treat the condition.

“Binge drinking is incredibly common, but we don’t have many tools to offer people who want to cut back,” said Glenn-Milo Santos, PhD, MPH, the study’s lead author and a UCSF professor of community health systems. “Our goal is to expand that toolkit. What’s exciting about this approach is that people can use it when they know they may be at risk of heavy drinking, rather than taking a medication every day.”

The trial involved 120 adults with alcohol use disorder who engaged in binge drinking at least once a week. Binge drinking was defined as consuming at least five alcoholic drinks on a single occasion for men and at least four for women.

Participants were divided into two groups, with half receiving a standardized kudzu extract and the other half receiving a placebo. Over 12 weeks, they were instructed to take their assigned treatment approximately 2.5 hours before they anticipated drinking heavily. Everyone also received brief weekly counseling sessions.

By the end of the trial, participants taking kudzu had reduced their average number of binge-drinking days from 2.15 to 1.42 per week. Those receiving the placebo showed little improvement, with their weekly average declining from 2.15 to 2.06 days.

Kudzu also reduced how frequently participants experienced weeks involving any binge drinking. The proportion of such weeks dropped from 88% to 58% among those taking the extract, compared with a decline from 88% to 81% in the placebo group.

Three months after the treatment period ended, participants who had received kudzu continued to report significantly fewer binge-drinking days than those in the placebo group. Researchers suggest that drinking less during the trial may have helped participants reconsider their drinking habits and develop greater confidence in their ability to control their alcohol consumption.

Participants tolerated the kudzu extract well, experiencing no more side effects than those receiving the placebo. No serious adverse effects were linked to the treatment.

However, the researchers caution against assuming that commercially available kudzu supplements will deliver comparable benefits. The trial used a standardized formulation, whereas the composition of products sold over the counter can vary considerably. Larger clinical studies are needed to confirm the results across more diverse populations.

Reference: “Targeted Use of Kudzu for Binge Drinking Among Adults With Alcohol Use Disorder: A Phase 2b Randomized Trial” by Glenn-Milo Santos, Phillip Coffin, Allie Dunham, Thomas Hoffmann, John Walker, Tim Matheson and Steven Batki, 3 November 2026, American Journal of Psychiatry. DOI: 10.1176/appi.ajp.20251354

‘Grand Theft Auto VI’ Album’s Secret Weapon? The ‘Barbie’ Soundtrack Creator

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Atlantic Music Group president Kevin Weaver helped craft the video game's wide and diverse soundtrack, which features the likes of Keith Richards, Morgan Wallen, Lana Del Rey and Travis Scott.

For the biggest game the studio has ever released, Grand Theft Auto VI developer Rockstar Games wanted an equally ambitious soundtrack. Like much of the entertainment business these days, they turned to Atlantic Records to help make that happen.

Over the past decade, the record label’s soundtrack efforts, spearheaded by Atlantic Music Group President Kevin Weaver, have established Atlantic as the de-facto landing spot for media companies looking to build a star-studded accompanying album for their work. Since 2015, Atlantic has handled the soundtracks for movies including Furious 7, Suicide Squad, Greatest Showman, F1, Twisters and most-famously Barbie, the latter of which became a cultural phenomenon alongside the film itself.

But the biggest opportunity Atlantic has seen to this point wasn’t a movie, but rather a video game.

“I went to Rockstar and said that they control the most culturally relevant IP on the planet, and that what they do with music is incredible,” Weaver told The Hollywood Reporter over the phone from Miami — where he’d spent much of last week attending launch events for the game — as he recalled the initial pitch to the company. “Music is such an endemic part of the game and what GTA is. Why don’t we endeavor to do something similar to what I’d done on Barbie and these other soundtracks and A&R a whole lot of new records?”

Weaver produced the soundtrack along with fellow Atlantic execs Joseph Khoury, Cody Verdecias, and Brandon Davis. GTA marked new territory for Weaver, the first time he’d taken on a video game soundtrack, which as he acknowledges, wasn’t on his purview before the game came into the fold.

“I’d never been interested in doing a video game soundtrack, to be honest with you,” Weaver says. “We license a lot of music to games, and I’ve had plenty of game companies come to me over the years, but if I’m not moving the needle and elevating the next thing I do, I’m not doing it. This was a natural choice, this was the one.”

Similar to the process with film soundtracks, early on, Rockstar showed Atlantic some key scenes from the game they wanted music for that were mostly set to temp tracks to help show the overall tone and feel they were looking for.

Among the first artists they reached out to, Weaver says, was Travis Scott, with some of the other earlier asks including Morgan Wallen and Dua Lipa. Scott, Weaver says, turned in the first song, with “Rhyno,” one of the soundtrack’s initial singles. Another Scott track, “Burn This Shit Down,” plays prominently during a key mission in chapter one.

The official soundtrack itself is just one aspect in the wider, rich musical world Rockstar has created for GTA VI. French electronic duo Justice, for example, provided the game’s score. GTA’s musical lifeblood, though, is the game’s radio stations, which feature hundreds of songs across two dozen channels, hosted by real-life celebrities like Robyn, Wallen, Bad Bunny, Keith Richards and Lana Del Rey among many more. Atlantic didn’t handle the radio stations, but Weaver says the company and Rockstar were “attached at the hip,” and some of those soundtrack offers came simultaneously with asks for the stars to host the radio shows.

Rockstar’s vision wasn’t set on any one genre, with the soundtrack spanning country, rock, hip-hop, blues, pop, electronic and latin among other genres. Just days into release, two of the game’s needle drops have gone viral, with the game breathing new life into Carly Rae Jepsen’s cult classic “When the Lights Go Out” and Dire Straits’ “Money For Nothing.” (Neither of those songs is on the official soundtrack, but they play in key missions in the game’s first two chapters.) Jepsen has already re-released her song, hoping to build off the momentum and finally land it on the Hot 100.

“Something Rockstar identified to us right away — this isn’t a rap game,” Weaver says. “It’s not a country game or dance music game. All of this music lives within the game in so many different facets, and that gives us a great opportunity to go out and create records across pretty much every genre we want. It’s GTA’s taste and tone that makes this work.”

The only real through-line, Weaver says, was capturing what he refers to as “the glitz and grime” of Vice City and Leonida, the game’s stand-ins for Miami and the broader Florida area.

While Atlantic’s more than used to handling a rollout for a film soundtrack, the strategy for video games is significantly different. While a movie’s lifespan in the immediate zeitgeist is typically a few weeks — pre-release marketing, the main theatrical window and post-theater streaming — a video game like GTA will hold a captive audience for years. GTA V had a dedicated audience for 13 years before the latest game finally came out last week.

“The tail on this never ends,” Weaver says. “Records are being discovered in the game in real time right now as the game is coming to market as it’s come to market in the last 24 hours, so we kind of have an opportunity to see which records really start to raise their hand as well.”

In the days since the game came out, Rockstar and Atlantic already surprise released a deluxe version of the soundtrack with 23 more songs atop the original 34, and today, they dropped four more songs from 100 gecs, Favored Nations, Joe Armon-Jones and Alex Frankel as iTunes exclusives.

As for any more musical plans going forward, Weaver says “the world’s just gonna have to wait and see.”

We love minivans, and one of the most compelling minivans on the market today is the hybrid-only Toyota Sienna. It's getting even more compelling for 2027, since Toyota has bestowed it with some significant drivetrain improvements including the automaker's sixth-generation hybrid system that uses high efficiency electric motors, a retuned engine, and a more energy efficient lithium-ion battery.

The 2027 Toyota Sienna isn't visibly distinguishable from the 2026 model since no changes were made to its exterior, but minivans have always been about what's beneath the skin, anyway. In addition to the new powertrain, all 2027 Siennas get the larger 12.3-inch infotainment screen and the larger 12.3-inch digital gauge cluster as standard, Toyota's latest suite of active-safety features, an integrated dash cam, a redesigned steering wheel and column, and new LED interior lights.

The Sienna's new hybrid powertrain has been totally recalibrated to operate the 2.5-liter four-cylinder at lower engine speeds when accelerating through the low and mid-range, and all Siennas now have a 1.15-kWh lithium-ion battery and a 166-horsepower electric motor. The powertrain produces a combined 251 horsepower, 6 more horses than last year, and Toyota reckons it's good for a 0.3-second quicker 0-to-60 sprint of 7.5 seconds in front-wheel-drive form. It can still be had with all-wheel drive, which adds a rear-mounted 54-hp electric motor, but its 0-to-60 time is actually a tenth slower than the front-drive model. Toyota now estimates both FWD and AWD Siennas will return 39 mpg in the city, 35 mpg on the highway, and 37 mpg combined, improvements of 3 mpg for FWD and 4 mpg for AWD.

Toyota says the new steering system is more direct and has better on-center feel while reducing steering effort at low speeds for easier parking thanks to a new steering gear box, motor control unit, and torque sensor. The Sienna's brakes have also been reworked with a new electronic control system, offering improved feel and response with less body roll. The latest Toyota Safety Sense 4.0 suite of active safety features has upgraded sensors and hardware for improved obstacle detection capabilities, and in case that all fails, a built-in dash cam comes standard.

Other little changes include the cancellation of the Limited model, now leaving base LE, XLE, Woodland, XSE, and top Platinum trim levels. Toyota has not yet release pricing for the updated 2027 Sienna, but we expect its prices to increase slightly from their current starting point of $42,915.

Inside Elizabeth Holmes’ Secret Oceanfront Rental Seen in ‘You Can See Everything’ Documentary a Month Before She’s Sent to Prison

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The Elizabeth Holmes documentary “You Can See Everything” takes place in the Theranos founder’s former Del Mar, CA, rental—and the property’s location in an exclusive, gated community could be just part of the reason the film’s unexpected release was such a smash.

The project—which is now available to stream on demand and was directed by Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim—was kept secret for the four years it was in production, leaving viewers in awe after the documentary’s surprise Sept. 6 premiere at Telluride Film Festival.

Holmes and her partner, Billy Evans, invited the film crew—which Oppenheim told Variety was intentionally kept intimate to keep the project a secret—to reside in the guesthouse of the home for the 34 days before she reported to Federal Prison Camp in Bryan, TX, on May 30, 2023.

“Keeping it under wraps was very important because we never wanted to get in front of what we were capturing,” the director shared.

The crew would typically shoot for 16-hour days, with Fielder telling Variety that cameras were strategically positioned to “cover the whole house” so nothing would be missed.

The four-bedroom, four-bathroom beachfront property is located in an ultraprivate community less than a half-hour from San Diego. Called Sandy Lane, it features just about a dozen residences.

“This is basically a hidden community. It’s a guard-gated community, which is so rare on a beach, which is why you have a lot of high-profile individuals up and down this strip,” celebrity real estate agent Josh Altman explained in a home tour of another property on the community’s sole street.

Given the home's private location—and considering California’s anti-paparazzi legislation protecting private property from capture—it’s plain to see why Holmes and Evans opted to rent the 2,200-square-foot dwelling in the time leading up to her imprisonment.

The New York Times also previously reported that the couple and their two children moved to the area to be nearer to Evans’ family. His parents, William L. Evans and Susan Evans, are the hotel magnates behind Evans Hotels, a San Diego–based hospitality company.

Although the property is currently off-market, a previous listing indicates the single-story home was built in 1948 and includes direct access to the private beach.

As seen in the film, the abode features tall ceilings with exposed-beams and sizable windows connecting the home to the serene surrounding landscape. Midcentury modern design is present throughout the home, which is paneled in honey-hued wood.

While Realtor.com® estimates the property’s current market value to be about $5.8 million, it’s unclear just how much Holmes and Evans shelled out to reside there temporarily. The most recent publicly listed rent was $5,650 a month in 2015, which is a decrease from the $5,950 rent it was listed for in 2012.

The Sandy Lane spot is not the only prominent rental property Holmes has retreated to since her legal troubles began. During her trial from Aug. 31, 2021, to Jan. 3, 2022, she resided in one of the seven homes of San Francisco’s notable Green Gables.

The sprawling 74-acre property was owned by the Fleishhacker family for over a century before it was sold for a record-breaking $85 million in 2025.

Before Theranos came crumbling down, Holmes and her former business and romantic partner, Ramesh “Sunny” Balwani, purchased a $9 million French provincial manor in Atherton, a wealthy enclave of Silicon Valley.

Privacy was likely a factor in that decision, as well, as the five-bedroom, 5.5-bathroom mansion was set behind a gated entrance and tall trees.

The home was considerably more lavish than the beach bungalow that serves as the backdrop for “You Can See Everything,” with a 6,800-square-foot main home accompanied by gardens, a pool, and a pavilion on a 1.16-acre lot.

Along with the couple’s young children, Evans has since relocated from California to Texas, so the family can visit Holmes in prison, where she is serving an 11-year sentence for three counts of wire fraud and one count of conspiracy to commit wire fraud.

The sentence was reduced to nine years for good behavior, and Holmes has since been approved for placement in a community correction center, ABC News reported, citing an update from the Department of Justice's Victim Notification System.

Holmes is scheduled to be placed in the Austin Transitional Center in De Valle, TX, in August 2027.

Jensen Huang tells staff that Nvidia doesn't need a succession plan

📅 2026-11-24  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang on Tuesday struck a conciliatory and remorseful tone while leading his first employee meeting since being reinstated, and said how sorry he was for what he had done.

Huang told employees that he let them down and promised to do a better job the second time around, according to people inside the room, who asked not to be named in order to share details.

The CEO also addressed succession planning during the event, the people said. He told staff that Nvidia has built a team that doesn't require succession planning the way a normal organization does, because the company operates a unique business model where there are multiple capable leaders at Nvidia that are leading different parts of the organization.

A succession plan would create unnecessary hierarchy, Huang said, according to the person familiar the matter.

Investors and analysts were left to speculate about the company's future leadership in the wake of Huang's resignation on Nov. 12.

On Monday, Huang was reinstated as CEO of the chipmaker by Nvidia's board of directors. Eleven days before, Huang had resigned from Nvidia after multiple reported affairs, including one with a Chinese businesswoman who is alleged to be a spy for Beijing.

Harvey Jones, Nvidia's newly appointed board chair, said last week that Huang resigned after "disclosure of personal behavior inconsistent with the company's code of conduct, standards and cultural norms."

In a press release announcing Huang's return on Monday, the company said the board "received input" from senior officials in the federal government about any potential national security risk around the CEO. President Donald Trump told CNBC that he gave the board assurances and "made the deal" to restore Huang to his post.

After the company had its worst week in the stock market since 2008, the stock surged more than 30% the day his return was announced. Shares fell on Tuesday, however.

An Nvidia representative didn't immediately respond to requests for comment.
