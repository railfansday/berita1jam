How to Build a Resilient Personal Investment Plan

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Home » Op-ed » How to Build a Resilient Personal Investment Plan

Build a resilient personal investment plan with clear goals, diversified assets, and practical risk management. Learn how to assess costs, liquidity, and alternative investments before committing your money.

A sound investment plan ties your financial goals, time horizon, risk tolerance, and available resources together into one coherent strategy. Without that connection, even well-intentioned investment decisions can end up working against you. Diversification—spreading investments across and within asset classes rather than concentrating in a single holding—remains one of the most consistently cited principles in personal finance education. Some investors, while researching ways to diversify, come across physical assets such as gold bars as one example of exposure outside traditional stocks and bonds. This isn’t a recommendation; it’s simply an illustration of the kind of asset category investors may compare when evaluating a broader plan. This article focuses on the decision-making principles, risk management practices, and due diligence steps that apply regardless of which specific assets you ultimately choose.

Before selecting any investment, figure out what the money is actually for. Emergency savings, a short-term purchase, retirement, education funding, and long-term wealth building each come with different requirements for accessibility and acceptable volatility. Money you’ll need within the next year or two generally calls for safer, more liquid holdings. Money earmarked for goals decades away can typically absorb more fluctuation in exchange for growth potential.

It helps to separate two related but distinct concepts: risk tolerance, which is how much market fluctuation you can emotionally stomach, and risk capacity, which is your financial ability to absorb losses without jeopardizing your goals. Income stability, outstanding debt, the size of your emergency reserve, number of dependents, and investment time horizon all factor into risk capacity. Writing down your goals and constraints before researching specific investments helps prevent decisions driven by excitement or fear.

Several types of risk deserve separate attention: volatility (short-term price swings), permanent loss of capital, liquidity risk (difficulty converting an asset to cash without taking a discount), inflation risk (the erosion of purchasing power), and credit risk (a counterparty’s inability to meet its obligations).

Asset allocation refers to dividing a portfolio among categories such as stocks, bonds, cash, real estate, and alternative assets. Each category tends to respond differently to economic conditions, which is why diversification can reduce concentration risk—though it can’t eliminate the possibility of loss altogether. A realistic target allocation, one you can stick with through market stress rather than abandon at the first sign of volatility, matters more than a theoretically optimal one you can’t maintain. Rebalancing—periodically returning a portfolio to its intended mix—is a discipline rooted in preserving your chosen risk profile, not in predicting short-term market movements.

One educational approach organizes a portfolio into broad functions: liquidity for near-term needs, stability for capital preservation, growth for long-term objectives, and diversification for assets that may behave differently from your core holdings. This framework is illustrative only and shouldn’t be treated as a universal formula.

Key takeaway: A resilient portfolio begins with goals and constraints—not with chasing the latest fashionable investment.

Alternative assets are sometimes considered for diversification, potential inflation resilience, or reduced dependence on traditional financial assets. Gold, for instance, is occasionally discussed as a diversifier with no direct credit exposure, though its price can still fluctuate meaningfully. Historical behavior should never be mistaken for a guarantee of future performance.

Exposure to an asset like gold can take several forms: physical ownership, exchange-traded products, mining-company shares, or futures and other derivatives. Each form carries its own market, operational, counterparty, liquidity, and regulatory characteristics. Owning a physical asset is fundamentally different from owning a financial product whose value is merely linked to that asset’s price.

Understanding the economics of physical ownership requires a few plain-language definitions: the spot price is the current market price for immediate delivery; the purchase premium is the amount charged above spot price; the resale spread is the difference between what you pay and what you’d receive upon selling; storage and insurance costs are ongoing expenses tied to safekeeping; and liquidity describes how quickly and efficiently you can convert the asset back to cash.

Looking only at the quoted purchase price overlooks the full picture. The total cost of ownership includes premiums, storage, insurance, and eventual resale spreads. Before purchasing, it’s worth checking:

Be wary of unsolicited offers, urgency-based sales tactics, guaranteed-return claims, or requests for unusual payment methods. Compare multiple independent sources and hold on to receipts and ownership records. Product specifications, legal treatment, and tax rules vary by jurisdiction and change over time, so verify details through authoritative sources and qualified professionals.

Physical assets come with a storage decision to make: home storage, bank or institutional safe-deposit arrangements, or professional third-party vaulting. Each option involves trade-offs in privacy, access, security, insurance costs, and estate planning complexity. Physical possession introduces practical risks—theft, loss, damage—that don’t apply in the same way to securities held in a regulated brokerage account. Maintaining a detailed inventory and folding relevant assets into your estate documents is worthwhile. The safest approach depends on individual circumstances, local laws, and available insurance coverage.

Consider two hypothetical investors. The first spends time comparing dealers, calculates her total ownership costs, and limits her purchase to a small percentage of her portfolio in line with a written plan. The second, swayed by a high-pressure phone pitch promising rapid appreciation, commits a large sum without comparing alternatives or understanding the resale terms. The contrast says something about process quality, not about any specific asset.

Revisit your plan after major life changes—a new job, marriage, inheritance, retirement, or a significant shift in debt. Market movements alone don’t necessarily call for a strategic overhaul. Scheduled reviews should cover goal progress, emergency reserves, asset allocation, fees and ownership costs, and beneficiary or estate information. For decisions involving significant assets or complex obligations, consulting a qualified financial, tax, or legal professional is a wise move.

Before adding an alternative asset, consider:

A durable investment plan rests on clear objectives, genuine diversification, reasonable costs, adequate liquidity, and informed risk management—not on any single product. Physical precious metals may suit some financial plans, but they’re not automatically appropriate for every investor. Compare alternatives objectively, verify claims independently, and steer clear of decisions made under pressure. Take time to review your overall portfolio, write down the intended role of each holding, and seek qualified advice whenever a decision exceeds your own expertise.

This article is educational in nature and does not constitute personalized financial, tax, or legal advice. Consult a qualified professional before making investment decisions.

US Soccer Statement After Pulling Team Out of U17 World Cup in Qatar

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

The FIFA under-17 World Cup kicks off in a week's time in Qatar, and it's now been revealed that the US will not be represented at the tournament after withdrawing its side due to security concerns, according to US Soccer Collective.

FIFA announced in 2024 that the tournament would take place annually rather than every two years, with the first five iterations of the new format set to be held in Qatar. The Middle Eastern nation has risen to prominence as a host country for football tournaments since holding the 2022 Men's World Cup.

While FIFA may benefit from working with the wealthy Qatari government, the decision to hold the next five U17 World Cups in the Arab nation appears to have come at the expense of the U17 USMNT.

Ongoing conflicts in the Middle East and political tensions between the US and the region have prompted the Stars and Stripes to withdraw.

One of football's showpiece youth tournaments, which has unearthed a number of talented players over the years, commences next Thursday, but it'll now be without one of the biggest nations on the planet.

The US, who had been drawn in Group J alongside Montenegro, Chile and Algeria, will not longer participate in the competition in the Middle East.

The current travel advisory for Qatar from the US Department of State is at a Level 3, with guidance urging people to reconsider travel to the country due to the risk of armed conflict.

"We are writing to inform you of the difficult decision to cancel our World Cup pre-camp and our planned participation in the World Cup," US Soccer's statement to their under-17 players read.

“We are extremely proud of the preparation by our players and coaches to represent the US in the World Cup. Unfortunately, in light of current circumstances including the US State Department’s Level 3 Travel Advisory for Qatar and related directives for Embassy staff and families, we are no longer able to participate in the tournament. The well-being of our players and staff will always be our top priority.”

The letter sent by US Soccer to the players on Wednesday was obtained by the Athletic.

FIFA reportedly have the ability to replace the North American country in the tournament, although a direct replacement is yet to be formally announced. Given that sourcing a replacement would require a team convening at incredibly short notice, it's possible FIFA keep Group J at just three teams.

With Pochettino heading to Man Utd, the US are not messing around.

The US were supposed to be one of the more exciting teams heading to the tournament, with the likes of Malik Jakupovic (Philadelphia Union), Robert Turdean (Chicago Fire) and Vinny Garcia (LA Galaxy) among the assortment of rising teen talents that coach Alex Aldaz was set to have at his disposal.

Palantir Grew Revenue 96%. The Stock Fell 5%. Here's What I'm Doing.

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Palantir (PLTR +2.12%) reported very strong earnings once again on Nov. 2. The highlight of the release was its accelerating 96% revenue growth. However, the market didn't love the results, and the stock sold off more than 10% in the days following earnings.

The stock has rebounded some, but recently was still down 5% from its price before earnings were released. Overall, Palantir is down around 20% from its all-time high, so some investors may be viewing this as a buying opportunity.

But what am I doing? I'm still staying patient. Palantir is a great business that's growing remarkably fast, but there's one huge holdup that keeps me from purchasing shares, and it could be some time before I decide to open a position.

At face value, Palantir's AI analytics business is doing fantastic. Its revenue is soaring, and it has 49% profit margins. The business is strong, and so is its outlook, so what's not to love about this stock? It all comes down to the price tag.

The best companies bought at the wrong price can still be bad investments, and that's exactly how I view Palantir's stock. Palantir trades at about 100 times this year's earnings and 67 times next year's earnings.

For reference, I can buy other AI stocks, like Nvidia or Alphabet, which trade at 11 and 19 times next year's earnings, respectively. These two are stalwarts in the industry, yet trade at huge discounts. Palantir's growth will be dedicated to reducing its valuation over the next few years, while Nvidia and Alphabet are cheap enough that every bit of growth they deliver should result in a corresponding rise in their stock.

That makes the long-term prospects for these two (and countless other AI investments) far stronger. As a result, I'm OK passing on Palantir. There are many great AI investments, and you don't have to invest in them all. The day of reckoning for Palantir's stock valuation could be coming soon, as it has maintained its high valuation even while many other AI stocks have seen their valuation premiums erased. Time will tell, but right now it seems like a precarious time to be a Palantir investor, with so many other great deals available in AI stocks.

I'd consider buying Palantir stock at around 30 to 40 times forward earnings, but we're a huge sell-off away from seeing those levels.

Huawei, Microsoft, AWS and AMD headline this year’s line-up, with robotics, cybersecurity and data centres getting their own showcases. Joby Aviation, Workday and Nexthink make their debut.

Joe Hart pinpoints ‘unbelievable’ Alisson Becker trait which makes him ‘one of the best’

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Joe Hart has proclaimed that Alisson Becker will ‘forever be regarded as one of the best’ goalkeepers to have graced the Premier League.

Liverpool’s number 1 has been rightly acknowledged as one of the foremost players in his position in world football since his move to Anfield in 2018 and has been pivotal towards the trophies won by the Reds in that time.

He produced one of his greatest LFC performances in the victory at Crystal Palace last weekend, with his tally of six saves including a world-class reaction stop from Daichi Kamada which left the Eagles attacker astounded.

In an interview with The Athletic, Hart lavished praise on Alisson for the consistency he’s shown throughout his time in England, and especially his prowess in one-on-saves.

The ex-Manchester City stopper said of the 34-year-old: “One of the best to do it in the Premier League. What I love is his simplicity. He’s not necessarily eye-catching in the way he moves, but it’s the way he deals with everything.

“He makes massive saves. His one-on-one technique is unbelievable, and he has made it his own. He very rarely gets beaten one-on-one, which in this Premier League is incredible because you’re up against some of the best strikers in the world.

“He’s struggled a bit with injuries lately, but I think he’ll forever be regarded as one of the best to play the position.”

Last weekend, Alisson produced a perfect example of the one-on-one ability that Hart pinpointed by thwarting Yeremy Pino when the Crystal Palace forward was through on goal, a significant intervention at a time that Liverpool were 2-0 up.

When it comes to ‘massive saves’, it mightn’t quite be in the same category as his legendary stoppage-time heroics against Napoli in December 2018 when our Champions League campaign was in the balance, but it showed that the 34-year-old remains a world-class goalkeeper.

His outstanding display at Selhurst Park came after a couple of costly misjudgements in defeats to Arsenal and Fenerbahce, which shows the Brazilian’s strength of character to put those unfortunate moments behind him and come up trumps for his team.

Alisson’s agent Ze Maria revealed in recent days that talks have begun over a prospective contract renewal at Liverpool, with the Reds’ number 1 having said after the win at Palace that he’d like to stay at Anfield beyond the expiry of his current deal next summer.

More heroics like those he produced in south London four days ago, and an extension to his existing terms should be a no-brainer for sporting director Julian Ward.

Lazio, derby da record: incasso stimato vicino ai 4 milioni

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

La previsione supera i tre milioni incassati contro il Bayern nel 2024; gli abbonati sono 4.200.

La Lazio potrebbe incassare circa 4 milioni di euro dalla vendita dei biglietti per il derby con la Roma di domenica 13 dicembre, in programma alle 12.30. Secondo quanto riportato da Il Messaggero, La stima, riportata da Il Messaggero, collocherebbe la partita al primo posto nella storia degli incassi al botteghino del club. La cifra potrebbe essere anche leggermente superiore.

La previsione è legata alla presenza di appena 4.200 abbonati: per questa gara, gran parte delle presenze sugli spalti si tradurrebbe quindi in biglietti acquistati. La società, da anni, non diffonde i dati relativi agli incassi al botteghino.

Il confronto più recente è con il derby del 21 settembre 2025, quando la Lazio incassò 2,2 milioni di euro. In quell’occasione, però, gli abbonati erano quasi trentamila. Per la prossima sfida con la Roma, la stima si avvicina invece ai quattro milioni.

Un incasso di quell’entità supererebbe nettamente i 3 milioni di euro registrati il 14 febbraio 2024, nella gara di andata degli ottavi di Champions League contro il Bayern. Quella sera la Lazio allenata da Sarri vinse 1-0.

La partita con il Bayern aveva già prodotto un incasso superiore di quasi un milione rispetto al debutto nella stessa competizione contro l’Atletico Madrid, il 19 settembre 2023. La sfida terminò in pareggio grazie al gol del portiere Provedel.
