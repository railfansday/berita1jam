Ohio State defensive line produces a season-high in sacks vs Northwestern

📅 Nov 15, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Ohio State's defensive line went into the Nov. 14 Northwestern game knowing its pass rush needed to improve.

"We had to get back there. We had to get pressure," defensive tackle Eddrick Houston said. "We had to get sacks and get that pressure off the DBs, who are trying to cover for that long. ... Coverage and rush work together, so we just had to do what we needed to do."

The Buckeyes snapped a two-game losing skid, defeating the Wildcats 28-19 and logging their highest sack total since September 2025 with five.

The defensive line, after failing to record a sack the past four games, accounted for all of the Buckeyes' sacks, which pushed the Wildcats back 25 total yards and led to two fourth downs.

In those games the defensive line went without a sack, Ohio State as a whole had just two, both credited to defensive backs, Jermaine Mathews Jr. and Jaylen McClain. The Buckeyes were ranked 121th in the nation in sacks with 11.

"It's something we've been emphasizing," Ohio State coach Ryan Day said. "Getting more pressure on the quarterback."

Against Northwestern, defensive end Kenyatta Jackson Jr. finished with two sacks despite remaining on the bench for nearly two full drives at the beginning of the game. Has a team-high five.

Alabama transfers Qua Russaw and James Smith Jr., both defensive ends, each recorded a sack, a first for Smith at Ohio State. Will Smith Jr. was the only defensive tackle to sack Wildcats quarterback Nicco Marchiol.

Houston, who has three sacks in his four seasons with the Buckeyes, still took pride in the Buckeyes hitting a season-high in sacks, even though a majority came from the edges rather than the middle of the line.

"It felt great, it all coming together," Houston said. "Rushing four as one, and allowing our ends to eat, but at the same time, while our ends are eating, we can work stuff in the middle to allow us to get sacks as well."

The veteran Buckeye admitted the defensive line had begun to shy away from the pass rush and began to put more emphasis on stopping the run after its performance against Illinois, in which Ohio State allowed 199 rushing yards.

"We've got some great pass rushers," Houston said. "Great guys in there that can pass rush, but we kind of got away from that and focused on stopping the run. Now that we've stopped the run, we've got to get to that quarterback. I just feel like we took that next step."

The run defense was progressing in the three games following Illinois, giving up an average 64.7 rushing yards. But in the losses to USC and Oregon, Ohio State allowed 112 rushing yards per game and conceded five rushing touchdowns, in addition to lacking a pass rush.

Northwestern had been averaging 190 rushing yards per game, the second-most in the Big Ten. On 29 run attempts against Ohio State, the Wildcats posted just 53 yards, marking just the second time they were held to under 100 rushing yards.

The Buckeyes limiting Northwestern's ground game was impactful on the pass rush, especially on long-yardage third down plays, on which Ohio State forced Marchiol into incomplete or short-yardage passes five times.

"When you can put them into a situation where they're one-dimensional, it certainly allows us more [pressure] opportunities," Day said. "There were some times in the game where we struggled on third down, getting off the field, but we got them off schedule a bunch, which allowed us an opportunity to get after the quarterback."

Wow, this is a nasty, dirty, polluting stunner I didn’t see until just now.

Anthropic, the AI company behind Claude, has reportedly signed a $3.4 billion contract to buy gas-fired engines (power plants) from OnePWR Solutions, a Texas-based energy company. These gas power plants would be able to provide Anthropic’s AI data centers with an enormous amount of off-grid electricity — with the real cost being an enormous amount of air pollution and global warming emissions.

If you thought Anthropic was supposed to be “the good guy” in the AI industry, perhaps think again.

The Financial Times, which broke the news on this, says that this is the “largest known single purchase of power-generation equipment by an AI lab.” It’s this line from Futurism that really sums up my feelings: “It isn’t just symptomatic of a desperate race to secure the necessary utilities: it’s also one of the clearest signs yet that the AI industry has turned its back on the growing climate crisis in favor of pollution-spewing gas generators it’s installing across the globe.” Indeed. It’s one thing to be racing to be a leader in this field, but to turn to polluting, climate-destroying gas power plants instead of solar and wind power, that’s absolutely disappointing and — frankly — morally disgusting.

For the heck of it, to see what this company’s AI bot thought about the power choice it made, I decided to ask Claude “Are gas power plants harmful to our climate?” The response was as follows:

“Yes, though they’re less harmful than coal and more harmful than most alternatives.

“Bottom line: Gas is a cleaner fossil fuel, not a clean one. Its climate impact is best judged by what it replaces (coal vs. renewables) and by how tightly methane leaks are controlled. Carbon capture could reduce plant emissions, but it’s still costly and not widely deployed.”

So, yeah, when you’re choosing to buy $3.4 billion worth of gas power plants instead of renewable energy power plants, that is without a doubt going to be a horrible decision for our climate. I’m sure if I asked Claude about the risk humans race from climate change, the answer would not be uplifting. But I’m not going to go there. Instead, I’ll turn back to Futurism for this summary: “Researchers have found that the effects of AI on the environment are nothing short of devastating. And the prognosis for the future is grim. Last month, environmental nonprofit Sierra Club estimated that upcoming AI data centers could release 2.1 billion metric tons of carbon dioxide by 2030, wiping out nearly 15 years worth of greenhouse gas reduction efforts.”

Indeed — we’re facing drastic increases in emissions from the very dirty AI industry. The good news is people are already showing they’re fed up with AI data centers. Apparently, out of 41 ballot measures related to data centers that were up for a vote in the recent midterm elections, only one (1) went the way of the AI industry. That said, I don’t see investments in growth stopping right now, as is evidenced by this $3.4 billion gas engine order and OpenAI and others burning through billions of dollars a quarter.

Miami's Scarlett Schremmer DQ'd from LPGA event for unauthorized yardage book

📅 Nov 15, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Scarlett Schremmer's LPGA debut will now end in disqualification, not with a missed cut.

The Miami sophomore was determined by LPGA officials to have used an unauthorized yardage book during competition at The Annika driven by Gainbridge, where Schremmer was competing on a sponsor exemption and shot 69-74.

The LPGA, which first learned about the potential infraction on Thursday before reviewing photo evidence following Friday's second round, issued the following statement: "Schremmer violated Model Local Rule G-11, which, per the LPGA Rules of Play Card, is in effect during all LPGA Tour competitions, except at off-site local qualifying events. This Model Local Rule requires that players are limited throughout their round to using only the yardage book that has been approved for use in the competition."

Schremmer later released her own statement, confirming that her yardage book had illegal green-reading materials in it.

"I had a green reading yardage book in my bag this week that's allowed in college golf but isn't permitted on the LPGA Tour," Schremmer wrote. "That's on me. I should have checked, and it's a lesson learned. I had a great week competing out here and I'm grateful to the Doyle family, Pelican and the LPGA for the opportunity. I am excited to continue to work with the goal of playing out here again. Thank you to everyone who came out and supported me this week."

Prior to Schremmer's DQ, one of her playing competitors, Perrine Delacour, took to social media on Saturday morning, commenting on a Monday Q Info post, "Can we talk about the yardage [book] illegal and LPGA trust the sponsor invite over 2 LPGA players and 2 caddies."

Delacour declined to elaborate when reached by Golfweek's Beth Ann Nichols, instead saying that she handled the matter with the LPGA.

Schremmer recently wrapped up her fall semester with the Hurricanes, during which she won twice, and she enters winter break ranked 17th in the country.

10 Hilarious Blondie Comics That Mock The Modern Day

📅 Nov 15, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Since 1930, Blondie and Dagwood Bumstead have been navigating domestic life, delighting readers with jokes and comedic moments that have stood the test of time. Blondie’s success is no small feat: it is one of the longest-running and most influential comic strips of all time, even making a cameo in other popular strips like Garfield.

Blondie achieved remarkable longevity because it proved fertile ground for satirical strips that mock modern life. These strips contrast traditional mid-century sensibilities with today’s hyperconnected, high-speed reality. These iconic characters are placed in situations that hold a mirror up to readers and mock what has become commonplace in modern life.

This article highlights 10 Blondie comics that mock the modern day, showcasing the long-running series at its sharpest and funniest. By juxtaposing classic strip tropes with the chaotic landscape of smartphones, selfies and modern trends, Blondie has perfected the formula for comedy gold.

This comic strip satirises how obsessed people have become with their smartphones. Something as simple as reading a book has become a difficult task in an era when people’s attention spans have been greatly reduced by social media and fast-paced, easily accessible content.

The strip opens with Dagwood asking his daughter what book she is reading, and she replies, “A book for my book report that’s due tomorrow.” Dagwood, still thinking that’s a good thing, replies, “Well, better late than never, right?” but she comically reveals that she hasn’t started the book at all, showing her smartphone hidden among the books.

This joke pokes fun at how smartphones have made procrastination so easy. It is now easier than ever to get distracted or spend hours scrolling, even when deadlines need to be met. Many people would rather scroll for hours than spend an hour reading a book and doing their homework.

Technology has made many things easier, both at home and in the workplace, yet it still cannot load your dishes for you. This strip satirises how the rise of new technology has made people lazier than ever. Even something as simple as loading dishes into a dishwasher is something people wish an app could handle.

In the strip, Dagwood comments that “there’s literally an App for Everything”, and Blondie agrees and immediately chips in, saying, “ Too bad there isn’t an app for loading the Dishes”, and the next panel is Dagwood loading up the dishes and lamenting that “there should be an app for this”.

Beyond being a hilarious strip, it mocks how dependent people have become on technology, even for everyday tasks. It also offers an ironic commentary on how men avoid household chores, even though they expect their wives to do them.

Social media platforms such as Facebook are a good way to stay in touch with friends and family; however, the access they provide can often allow others to criticise or comment on things that involve other people’s personal lives. Additionally, it can lead to unintended consequences, like flirting, in an attempt to defend someone online.

The strip opens with Blondie commenting on how much time Dagwood has been spending on the “New neighbourhood Facebook page”, and Dagwood chips in about how much people have been complaining about Barbie Babewlder sunning herself in her bikini. Blondie makes a snarky comment about how he couldn’t help defending her, and Dagwood claims he can’t help being a peacemaker.

Unfortunately, the tables turn when Blondie sits down at the computer and says, “Speaking of sunning, Hank Hunkerman is cleaning his pool”. It catches Dagwood by surprise, and he then understands how Blondie felt earlier. He had unintentionally been crossing boundaries and flirting, but social media normalises it and makes it possible.

It is commonplace these days to make fun of young people for being addicted to social media, and not without merit. Using Alexander, Dagwood’s teenage son, this strip highlights how people are less likely to go outside for a walk because they are too busy scrolling on social media.

In the strip, Alexander complains that “reels” on Instagram and Facebook are driving him crazy. He then asks his Dad how he can escape all the reels on Social media, and Dagwood replies, “Have you tried ‘real life’?. Nobody is forced to scroll on social media against their will; some people just need a reminder to log off.

The world today is full of so many issues that some may find it insensitive to have silly, goofy, lighthearted days at work. Still, employers continue to find ways to break up the monotony of a 9-to-5 with fun workplace activities. The current sociopolitical climate may be concerning, but having some fun at work won’t make things any worse.

In this strip, Dagwood’s office decides to have a funny T-shirt day to commemorate the first day of summer, much to the dismay of one of his coworkers, who comments, “Really? In today’s world?” The office is filled with people wearing fun T-shirts, like the ironic “I love money” T-shirt Dagwood’s boss wears and a coworker’s Garfield T-shirt.

It’s pretty much a fact that Dagwood is lazy and would rather do anything else than chores. Nonetheless, this strip makes fun of how people often put on television to gain motivation to clean and do chores. It’s very common today to throw on something to watch in the background because people need that extra stimulation.

In this strip, Blondie tells Dagwood they have a lot of spring cleaning to do, since she has prepared an official cleaning list. Dagwood, trying to avoid chores, offers “the perfect way to get motivated”, which turns out to be a suggestion to watch an hour of HGTV and westerns, which Blondie soundly rejects.

In the modern age, information is easy to access, but misinformation is just as widespread. The internet makes it easy for misinformation to spread through unregulated forums and social media platforms that let people post whatever they like. Blondie satirises the spread of celebrity rumours by using iconic Simpsons characters.

The strip features Dagwood in bed with Blondie, using an iPad, and he complains about fake online rumours about a celebrity couple. Asked who, he says Homer and Marge Simpson, and Blondie remarks that it must be a slow news day. Readers can relate to clickbait titles and articles engineered to farm engagement.

In this strip, Blondie perfectly captures how corny it is when middle-aged people use Facebook and chat-speak. Today, it’s not uncommon for people to spend hours chatting with friends and family, even at work, often neglecting important and pressing duties.

In the strip, Dagwood is on his computer and sees that his friend Woodley has been on Facebook for an hour. Unaware of the irony that he is also goofing off on Facebook, he tells Woodley to get off Facebook and do his job. But I’ve been typing like a madman all morning!” In all fairness, he wasn’t technically lying; still, goofing off at work like this has become a common occurrence these days.

Blogging about your personal life online or posting life updates has become normal in today’s society, but Blondie shows readers how bizarre it really is. People detail their entire lives online, yet still expect a certain level of privacy, which can seem contradictory.

It begins with a panel showing Dagwood’s daughter sitting at the computer, and Dagwood walks up to ask what she is doing. She says she is blogging about her personal life online, and in the next panel she covers the screen with her hands and asks her father not to look, saying it is private.

Ordinarily, if you’re too ashamed to show your father something, you probably shouldn’t post it online, but these days it may be stranger not to. Social media keeps blurring the line between what should be public and what should be private, making the strip a hilarious piece of satire and irony.

The telephone has long evolved beyond what its inventor, Alexander Graham Bell, envisioned. Comparing the first phone with a modern smartphone makes the smartphone seem like pure wizardry. Blondie uses this premise to create a strip that mocks how people mainly use these devices to send cute cat videos and text jokes to their friends.

The first panel of the strip shows Dagwood conversing with a co-worker by a water dispenser, when the co-worker asks, “What would Alexander Graham Bell do if he saw how his invention has turned into this crazy smartphone Mania?” Dagwood sharply replied that he would send his friend Watson a cute cat video.

His co-worker ironically decided to text the joke to his buddy Ziggy. Not only does the strip explore how far phones have evolved, but it also comments on people’s obsession with and reliance on smartphones. The co-worker couldn’t help but text his buddy a joke he had just heard.

Angela was last seen as a supporting character in Doctor Strange, helping the sorcerer during his time in Asgard. Now, she has a brand new mission as Marvel reveals:

It's possible that Angela will spin out of the newly launched Avengers Volume 10, in which Mephisto's scheming will seemingly unleash demons on the city.

Like cop shows and variety shows and classic situation comedies, the western is an enduring staple of the television landscape, and like those other genres, it never goes away; it just evolves. Neo-westerns can either bring the tropes of westerns into a modern-day setting, like Yellowstone and Justified, or they can do something else entirely, like the spacefaring adventures of Cowboy Bebop or the philosophical meditations of HBO’s Westworld.

Taylor Sheridan has singlehandedly revitalized the TV western with the Yellowstone saga. Vince Gilligan changed the way a story could be told on television with Breaking Bad. Gilligan told that story of moral downfall through the lens of a contemporary western. He set Breaking Bad on the dusty streets and sun-scorched deserts of Albuquerque; he put an armed standoff in every other episode; he even had Walt and his cohorts rob a train in the final season.

What are the limitations of eGPUs vs onboard graphics cards for laptops?

📅 Nov 15, 2026  |  Category: Travel  |  Read Source →

An eGPU can bring desktop-class graphics to a laptop, but cables, bandwidth limits and extra costs can eat into the advantage.

Connecting a desktop graphics card to your laptop seems like a neat solution to one of the oldest problems in portable computing. Use the thin machine for work and travel, and connect to a much more powerful GPU when you get home. An external GPU (eGPU) can do just that, but all that extra graphics power still has to travel through Thunderbolt or USB4 instead of the direct internal connection used by a built-in laptop GPU. That bandwidth bottleneck can eat into the desktop card's advantage, and the cable it's connected to holds sway as well.

In this comparison, "onboard" primarily refers to a dedicated GPU built into the laptop, not the integrated graphics in the laptop's processor. Even when a laptop GPU shares its name with a desktop GPU, the hardware can be vastly different. The RTX 5090 Laptop GPU features 10,496 CUDA cores, 24GB of GDDR7 memory, and a GPU subsystem power range of 95W to 150W. Meanwhile, you'll find 21,760 CUDA cores and 32GB of GDDR7 memory on NVIDIA's desktop RTX 5090. That's a significant gap before even considering bottlenecks, like Thunderbolt.

A modern eGPU typically communicates with the laptop via Thunderbolt or USB4, rather than directly on its PCIe bus, and that's where some of the desktop card's edge is lost. A built-in laptop GPU uses an internal PCIe connection instead.

Thunderbolt 4 has 40 Gbps of overall bandwidth and requires 32 Gbps of PCIe data throughput, while Thunderbolt 5 upgrades the base speed to 80 Gbps and doubles that PCIe requirement to 64 Gbps. Internal laptop GPUs can use wider multi-lane PCIe connections, depending on the laptop's design, giving them more room to move data without the Thunderbolt bottleneck.

That bottleneck is more noticeable when it comes to a powerful desktop card. NVIDIA's desktop RTX 5090 has 1,792GB/s of memory bandwidth and a 575W total graphics power rating, compared with 896GB/s and a 95W to 150W GPU subsystem power range for the RTX 5090 Laptop GPU. Those aren't PCIe bandwidth figures, but they show how much more raw hardware the desktop card brings before the external connection starts holding it back.

Thunderbolt 5 also has a 120 Gbps Bandwidth Boost mode, but that isn't 120 Gbps of PCIe bandwidth for the GPU. Intel says PCIe data throughput remains at 64 Gbps. While USB4 can also support PCIe traffic, a USB-C-shaped port alone doesn't guarantee eGPU compatibility. The host hardware needs the right support, and the enclosure, drivers, GPU and power requirements must all cooperate (because apparently one cable was getting too easy).

Using the laptop's built-in display can add another penalty because rendered frames may have to travel back through the same limited connection. This is why some brands suggest connecting an external monitor directly to the eGPU for better performance. There's no universal performance-loss percentage, since the GPU, laptop, connection, resolution and game all affect the result.

A laptop GPU begins with less raw hardware, but it doesn't have to deal with most of this plumbing. It operates within the laptop's cooling and power constraints, doesn't have to send rendered frames back over an external cable, and remains in the laptop when you pick it up. The portability benefit is more like a suggestion with a traditional desktop-card eGPU. You now have an enclosure, a desktop GPU, and maybe an ATX power supply on the desk, while a big card can add size and noise to the mix.

Let's not forget to talk about the cost associated with eGPUs. As an example, Razer's Thunderbolt 5 Core X V2 supports full-size NVIDIA and AMD desktop cards up to four slots wide. The enclosure also has its own 120mm cooling fan, but it doesn't come with a GPU or even the necessary ATX power supply to keep it running. Those would be added expenses on top of approximately $350 for the enclosure itself.

That math gets considerably friendlier if you have a suitable GPU sitting around after a desktop upgrade. At that point, the enclosure could significantly upgrade your laptop without buying another computer. It's more difficult to justify purchasing a high-end desktop card specifically for an eGPU, especially since you're paying for all that power and then sending it through a cable that might not allow you to use it all.

What A Road Sign With A Black 'Y' Means (And Other Letters, Too)

📅 Nov 15, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

We have covered all manner of weird road signs on Jalopnik before, including some seriously goofy ones. While oddities from around the globe are fun to explore and explain, it is a little more relevant to touch on road signs that crop up on U.S. soil. Some of these will no doubt be familiar, but others may not, so there is good reason to delve into them and provide a quick explainer. The black "Y" sign was the one that initially piqued our interest, which, by the way, highlights that a Y-shaped intersection is to be found up ahead. That led us to look at other weird letter- and symbol-focused road signs drivers may come across.

The obvious candidates are "P" for parking areas and "H" that marks hospitals, which are universally understood, but throughout this article, we will also explore what those weird "T" or upturned "U" signs mean, in addition to winding "S" and plus-symbol signs. That includes where drivers are most likely to find them and how best to react when they do. Listed in no particular order, here are our picks of eight letter- or symbol-focused road signs that we thought are worth exploring in a bit more detail.

As already explained briefly above, a sign that depicts a capitalized "Y" is there to let drivers know of a Y-shaped three-way intersection or a forked road ahead. The sign will typically be a diamond shape with a yellow background, which means it's a simple warning sign alerting drivers that the road splits. The choices of where to go are obvious — either left or right — but what might be less obvious is how the right of way is determined in a three-way intersection that isn't controlled by any other signs.

The general rule is that drivers must yield to the car on the right. Then there's always the first-to-arrive rule, which works as you would intend — the first to arrive at the split goes first. Of course, some drivers will assume right of way when it is not deserved, or perhaps they just do not see the other car(s). In such instances, it's best to yield to the other vehicle even if you believe you should have the right of way to ensure everyone gets past the intersection without incident.

When a road splits like this, it pays to be especially cautious. Drivers may attempt to dart from one lane to the next when correcting a last-minute wrong decision, plus motorbikes or cyclists could be hiding in your blind spots, so try to ensure you have sufficient space around you if possible. The NHTSA highlights that roughly one third of all traffic fatalities occur at signposted intersections, so doubling down on cautiousness here is never a bad idea, no matter how simple it may first appear.

Surprise surprise, the letter "T" on a road sign warns of a T-shaped intersection — we would hope that you managed to work that one out. The road you are traveling on is about to end and merge with another. Once again, this is a classic warning sign finished in a yellow and black colorway, on a diamond-shaped sign that lets drivers know something's coming up and they will have to act accordingly.

In this instance, drivers must yield to traffic on the intersecting road — they don't have the right of way here — before heading either left or right. There will usually be a stop or yield sign prior to the intersection itself, and to avoid any frustrating bumps with other drivers making late decisions, it's best to get into the required lane early rather than last-minute. Thankfully, of the deadliest intersections on U.S. roads, not many are T-shaped, but the same rules as above apply here. Give yourself space, keep an eye on your blind spots, and don't rush. Oh, and don't forget to use your turn signals either, even if you are in a dedicated turn lane.

Again, we won't be handing out prizes to anyone who is able to correctly guess this one. A cross or "plus" symbol indicates that a crossroad is coming up, and just like the other aforementioned sign, this too will be depicted on a black and yellow diamond-shaped warning sign. Here, traffic can and likely will be coming from all directions, so it's imperative to take the time that's needed and approach it with care. Be aware that drivers on the opposite leg might try to cross ahead of you as you pull out, and be sure to check traffic from both left and right directions. As always, make sure to follow the instructions depicted on any regulatory signs posted in your direction, as these might detail give-way instructions.

There is a good case to make here in favor of the roundabout. In many ways, it is a far better design than the traditional intersection, with fewer conflict points, no left-turns across the flow of traffic, and lower speeds. Roundabouts might seem perplexing to some, but according to data from the Federal Highway Administration, they have been proven to reduce injury and fatal crashes by 76% and 90%, respectively, plus they are far more time-efficient than traffic signals, so perhaps road users ought to just get used to them.

OK, that's not strictly true. This sign, which isn't an "S" at all — it's just a winding arrow — signifies that a winding road is up around. In other words, the natural habitat for a Miata owner who's looking for some engaging roads to make the most of their drop-top weekend car. Of course, the sign isn't actually there to tell drivers that the road is a great place to go hooning. Instead, it lets them know the road ahead has several curves one after another and that they should approach that section of road with some caution.

In fact, these "S" signs will frequently be accompanied by another advisory speed sign that suggests the maximum safe speed for the section. Drivers would be wise to pay attention to these speeds, as around 25% of all fatal road incidents happen on a curved section of road, with common causes being cars either drifting off into trees, rocks, or other fixed objects, or indeed drifting across the center of the road and causing a head-on collision with other traffic.

Anyone who can't figure this one out should probably start taking notes here. An upturned U-shaped arrow crossed out in red, of course, means that U-turns are not permitted. Drivers should continue on, take a left or right turn if available, and find another suitable route. Exceptions will not be made for those in classic red-and-white Gran Torinos.

This is the first sign featured on our list that strays from that typical yellow and black warning sign colorway. Instead, this is a rectangular regulatory sign with a white background. For those who may question the legality of U-turns when this sign isn't present, drivers can usually perform such a move in these circumstances: They should be in the leftmost or center-left lane, signalling their intention, and keeping an eye out for traffic as per any other manoeuvre.

A sideways "T" sign indicates that a side road is coming up. Depending on which way the T is oriented, the sign highlights whether the side road is on the right or the left. If the sign instead looks like an F or an E, but with an extended top or bottom, this means that two side roads are coming up on the right-hand side.

Specifically, this sign will appear when the side roads on the right are particularly close to one another. The hazard this poses to drivers on the major road is quite obvious; they should be wary of traffic entering from the right, and should be prepared to slow down. The sign itself is a typical diamond-shaped yellow-and-black warning sign, which does not indicate right of way. Typically, those on the major road will have the right of way, while those on the indicated side roads will have to yield to traffic on the major road.

The sign for a roundabout shows three arrows forming a circle, and once again, it will be a yellow-and-black diamond-shaped warning sign. Roundabouts always run in a counter-clockwise direction in North America. They can have numerous lanes and are an alternative to crossroads. Here, the right of way rules are simple and uniform: Drivers must always give way to traffic on the left — in other words, the cars that are already on the roundabout.

Getting the right lane is crucial for avoiding accidents; the leftmost lane is used for turning left, whereas the rightmost lanes are used for turning right, and both can be used for going straight on. These are the rules on multi-lane roundabouts unless signposted otherwise — this way, traffic in either lane does not cross unnecessarily.

As for whether drivers need to use turn signals on a roundabout or not, the answer is yes. It's never a bad idea to let other drivers know where you are going; even if you are comfortable in your own abilities to get across safely, it's best practice to help other motorists out by indicating to them your next intended move.

Danielle Fishel charmed audiences for seven seasons while playing the TGIF fan-favorite character, Topanga Lawrence in Boy Meets World. Not only was the character notable for being involved in a TV relationship that defined goals, but Topanga was also loved for being smart and true to herself. While Fishel has taken great pride in her BMW work, she recently mentioned one regret related to her character that, as a fan, I completely sympathize with.

At the beginning of the ABC sitcom, Topanga was introduced as Cory Matthews’ hippie-like classmate whose otherworldly views always sparked great comedic moments. But by the second season, th flower child had an extreme personality change, where she transitioned to an extremely motivated student and later the object of Cory’s affections by Season 3.

In Fishel, Rider Strong, and Will Friedle’s new book, Book Meets World: The Definitive Inside Story of the Hit Sitcom Boy Meets World (via People), she got real about her character's evolution. She explained how she felt about Topanga's trajectory at the time compared to how she feels now:

Teenage Danielle was more comfortable being the more mainstream Topanga because they wrote her more like me. So I probably didn’t have a problem with it then. Now I think, what a gift to be given a character that has such a sharply defined POV, to be so unique and to have been such a good role model. I knew that she was special. But then, when it changed, and she kind of evolved, I don’t think I necessarily knew to mourn it.

I get what the Dancing with the Stars contestant is talking about. When Fishel previously spoke about Topanga’s personality shift, she admitted that she didn’t even notice the change at the time. Looking back, it’s true that the show was focusing a lot on Topanga’s evolving look, like in Season 2 when Shawn was staring at her chest or Cory fixated so much on her beauty in Season 3. I truly believe Topanga’s complete change was when she cut her trademark long hair and started to look more like many other woman of the ‘90s.

While no one formally told the Dish host that her character would deviate from her weird girl persona, she said she remembered hearing a conversation from the crew about wanting to make Topanga “more normal” for Cory. Yet, even in the Season 6 episode, "Resurrection," Cory talked about missing the old Topanga. I feel like Cory would have grown to like the more off-beat version of his eventual partner.

Something that's interesting is hearing Fishel mention that as a teen, she didn't really mind her character being more streamlined and fitting her own personality. She may not have mourned the transition then, but she certainly does now:

Now I see it, and I so mourn it. I would have loved even little flashbacks to it.

That would have been great. Fans see a flashback scene in “Resurrection” of when Topanga put lipstick all over her face but, in the later seasons, I would have liked to have seen Fishel's character retain traits of who she was early on.

At the same time, we can be thankful that no matter who Topanga grew up to be, she remained throughout the series in general. Some may not be aware that she was originally written to be a recurring character. As a result of her sticking around, fans got to see her transform into a confident young woman, who even became an incredible lawyer, wife, and mother in the spinoff series, Girl Meets World.

While Danielle Fishel now regrets having leaned into Topanga’s personality change, I'd argue that her performance on the show still deserves praise. And, of course, we can love the iconic character for who she was when she was first introduced as well as who she grew into by the show’s series finale.
