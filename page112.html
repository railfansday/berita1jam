Iran relies on China and Russia as US pressure grows, but both powers are pursuing broader interests

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

As Donald Trump seeks to tighten economic pressure on Iran, China remains its biggest oil customer and Russia a key partner. But both powers are balancing support for Tehran against broader interests with Washington.

As Donald Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin meet in Shenzhen, Washington wants to tighten its blockade of Iran, but China remains Tehran's main oil buyer and Russia offers it political and logistical support.

And when Trump, Xi and Putin sit down together, Iran will not be at the table, even though Washington's pressure campaign against Tehran is expected to be among the issues discussed.

For Tehran, relations with both China and Russia are routinely described as strategic. That relationship looks more complicated from Beijing and Moscow.

Both countries oppose Washington's efforts to isolate Iran and maintain political and economic ties with Tehran. But both have broader interests in their relationships with the United States and may see Iran partly through the prism of their own negotiations with Washington.

That raises a central question: Can Trump's strategy of economically isolating Iran succeed if China and Russia refuse to fully cooperate?

China is currently the most important destination for Iranian oil.

In 2025, it was widely reported that Chinese buyers took more than 80% of Iran's seaborne crude exports, much of it through independent refiners and at steep discount because of sanctions.

And as China provided Tehran an economic lifeline, Iran also became increasingly dependent on Chinese manufactured goods, machinery and industrial inputs.

Mohsen Solhdoost, an assistant professor of international relations at Xi'an Jiaotong-Liverpool University, says China is important to Washington's strategy but not decisive on its own.

"If the objective is to reduce Iranian export earnings, restrict access to equipment and make economic and military recovery more difficult, the United States can achieve substantial results without Chinese cooperation," he told DW.

But forcing Iran to accept Washington's preferred nuclear or regional policies is another matter.

"The ability to interrupt trade is much easier to demonstrate than the ability to determine another government's political choices," Solhdoost said.

That distinction has become more important as US sanctions enforcement and maritime blockade efforts have increased pressure on Iranian crude oil exports.

In November, Washington announced Task Force Iron Gate, an initiative aimed at disrupting the movement of sanctioned goods, military equipment and revenue through Iran's land borders and the Caspian Sea.

Even so, these trade links have not disappeared. The commercial and security interests of countries including Iraq, Pakistan and Turkey mean that goods continue to move across the region, albeit under greater scrutiny.

As a result, China's role in Iran's economy remains significant, but Solhdoost argues that demand alone cannot sustain Iranian exports. The oil must be able to reach international markets.

"A Chinese buyer cannot keep Iranian export revenues flowing simply by remaining willing to buy if newly loaded tankers cannot reach the market," Solhdoost said.

This economic pressure is shaping Tehran's financial planning.

A senior official at Iran's Plan and Budget Organization recently said the government was preparing its 2027-2028 budget under several scenarios, including one in which oil revenue falls to zero.

That does not mean Iran has adopted a zero-oil budget, but considering such a scenario illustrates how seriously Tehran views the situation.

At the same time, Chinese trade can preserve critical parts of the Iranian economy even if overall commerce shrinks.

Machinery, electronics, spare parts and industrial components can still reach Iran by rail and road. Such trade cannot replace maritime commerce at comparable scale or cost, but it can keep selected factories and infrastructure operating.

Such Chinese support could help Iran endure pressure without preventing economic deterioration, says Solhdoost.

But even for Beijing supporting Iran economically does not mean supporting every Iranian policy.

Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi has maintained support for cooperation with Tehran while calling for negotiations, safe passage through the Strait of Hormuz and stable energy supplies.

A prolonged conflict around Hormuz threatens wider energy supplies and global shipping. China therefore benefits from continued access to Iranian oil but also from regional stability.

Russia creates another obstacle to Iran's isolation. The two countries share access to the Caspian Sea and have expanded military, transport and economic cooperation.

Iranian officials have discussed ways of using Russian and northern trade routes to reduce the impact of American restrictions. There has also been speculation about oil swaps and deeper Russian assistance, although these claims remain difficult to verify.

Ahmad Vakhshiteh, director of the Moscow-based Center for Strategic Research on Developing Relations with the Middle East, believes the summit should be viewed within a larger attempt to manage the wars in both Ukraine and the Middle East.

"Russia, China and the United States appear to be seeking constructive dialogue and results that could shape a new security architecture in the international system," Vakhshiteh told DW.

The summit could be significant if it establishes a continuing format for dialogue rather than producing symbolic statements, Vakhshiteh says, while cautioning against expecting a dramatic settlement from a single meeting.

Solhdoost also warns against overstating Russian support for Iran.

The strategic partnership between Moscow and Tehran provides for security cooperation and efforts to counter unilateral sanctions, but it does not require Russia to enter a war on Iran's behalf.

Since Russia is deeply engaged in Ukraine it likely has its own priorities in negotiations with Washington, says Solhdoost.

"Russia would prioritize its own national interests in negotiations with the US over Ukraine and Iran," he said.

Moscow could support Iran while simultaneously using its relationship with Tehran as leverage in talks with Trump over Ukraine.

For Iran, ties with China and Russia remain essential. They provide alternative trade routes, export markets and diplomatic support that complicate Washington's efforts to isolate Tehran.

Yet neither Beijing nor Moscow views Iran solely through the lens of strategic partnership. Both weigh support for Tehran against their own economic interests, security concerns and dealings with Washington.

In the end, the summit may be less about whether China and Russia support Iran than about how much that support is worth when larger bargains are on the table.

SSD prices will start going down the second half of next year

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Published September 17, 2026 • 11 min read • Forecasts and price data checked September 2026

It is the question we get asked more than any other right now, and it is the most searched phrase that brings people to this blog. So here is the straight answer: SSD prices should start to ease in the second half of 2027. Hard drives will take longer, probably into 2028, because the factories that make them have already sold that output.

That is not a guess. It comes from the analysts who track memory contract prices every quarter, and from what Seagate and Western Digital told their own investors this summer. Below we lay out the evidence, explain why spinning drives and SSDs are running on different clocks, and give you a realistic timeline with the signals to watch.

We have been following this since the start. Our April explainer on the storage price surge covered why prices took off. This article is the follow-up everyone keeps asking for: when does it end?

While you wait, the cheapest terabyte is the one you already own. DriveVault scans each of your external drives once and keeps a searchable catalogue of everything on them, so you can see free space, spot duplicate project folders and find old files without plugging anything in. For a lot of people that audit pushes the next drive purchase back by months, which is exactly what you want when prices are at a record.

NAND supply is forecast to catch up with demand from mid-2027. Expect a gradual slide, not a crash.

Capacity is contracted into 2028 and no new factories are planned. Relief comes via bigger drives, slowly.

Before looking forward, it helps to be honest about where we are. The German tech site ComputerBase has priced the same dozen popular drives every month since mid-September 2025, which makes its tracker one of the cleanest before-and-after records of this crisis. Its one-year check, published today, shows hard drives averaging 134% above their pre-crisis prices, up from 129% a month earlier. SSDs are 129% higher, up from 126%. Both are new records.

Source: ComputerBase price check, 16 September 2026. German retail prices, excluding eBay and Amazon Marketplace sellers.

The pattern in that table matters. The biggest hard drives, the 18TB to 24TB models that data centres also buy, have risen fastest. The smaller desktop drives most creatives actually use have "only" doubled. If you are shopping for a budget external drive, you are feeling the mild end of this.

The one encouraging detail: the monthly increases have shrunk to a few percentage points. Prices are still climbing, but the curve is flattening. That is usually the first stage before a market turns.

Most coverage lumps "storage prices" together. That hides the most useful part of the answer, because hard drives and SSDs are made by different companies, in different factories, and sold in different ways.

SSDs are built from NAND flash chips made by Samsung, SK hynix, Kioxia, Micron, Sandisk and YMTC. Those chips are priced quarter by quarter in contract negotiations, and some trade on a spot market that moves weekly. When supply catches up, SSD prices can respond within a couple of quarters. They went up fast. They can come down reasonably fast too.

Hard drives come from essentially two companies, Seagate and Western Digital, with Toshiba a distant third. Almost all of their output now goes to cloud providers on long-term agreements. Those agreements lock in volume and, crucially, price for years at a time. That is why hard drive prices rose later and more steadily than SSDs, and it is why they will be slower to fall.

TrendForce is the most widely cited memory pricing firm, and its forecasts have been the best guide through this cycle. Here is how its view has shifted during 2026.

At the start of the year, it was forecasting NAND contract prices rising 33 to 38% in a single quarter, with client SSDs rising at least 40% as suppliers moved capacity to data centre drives. By July the tone had changed. Its third-quarter survey forecast a much smaller 10 to 15% rise for NAND, and said plainly that PC and smartphone makers had hit the limit of what they could afford to pay.

Then came the forecast that actually answers the question. In its July NAND update, TrendForce said the market would run a 4 to 5% supply deficit through 2026, then move back into balance in the second half of 2027. The reasons it gives are ordinary ones: manufacturers squeezing more bits out of existing fabs, steady output growth, and consumer demand staying weak because everything costs so much. It expects notebook shipments to fall around 10% this year and shrink again in 2027.

New supply is also arriving from China. YMTC reached around 14% of global NAND shipments in the second quarter and has told investors it wants to be the world's largest NAND maker by the end of 2027, backed by a planned $4.9 billion share listing. Even if it falls short of that target, that much extra flash entering the market is exactly the kind of pressure that pushes SSD prices down.

Balance is not the same as cheap. TrendForce also warns that memory contract prices will remain broadly elevated in 2027, with cloud providers' spending projected to rise another 50% next year. And it flags that a breakthrough in agentic AI could soak up the extra supply. A market in balance means prices stop rising and start drifting down. It does not mean a return to 2024.

You may also have seen SK hynix's chief executive say that 2027 will be the worst supply year in the industry's history. That sounds like it contradicts TrendForce, but it mostly does not. His comments are about memory overall, and the worst of the squeeze is in DRAM and the high-bandwidth memory used in AI accelerators. TrendForce itself now describes a diverging market in 2027: DRAM staying tight while NAND eases. That is good news for SSDs and less good news for RAM, which is part of why Macs keep getting more expensive, as we covered in our piece on Apple's price rises.

For spinning drives, the most reliable evidence is what the two big manufacturers said on their earnings calls this summer. It is not encouraging for anyone hoping for a quick drop.

Seagate reported its fiscal year results on 28 July, with a record 52.7% gross margin. On the call, management said data centres now take around 90% of the capacity it ships, and that the vast majority of its high-capacity drive output is now allocated into calendar 2028. Seagate also told investors it has contracts setting both product configuration and pricing for the whole of 2027, and that it is not adding drive manufacturing capacity. Its plan is to cram more data onto each disk instead.

Western Digital tells the same story. Its fourth-quarter revenue rose 44% year on year, while the amount of storage it shipped rose only 22%. The gap is price. According to analysis of the earnings call, the company's average price per terabyte rose by a high-teens percentage, and its long-term agreements run through 2027 with two major customers and through 2028 with one, with price terms attached.

Put simply: the companies are sold out, they are making record margins, their contracts fix prices into 2028, and they have chosen not to build more factories. None of those conditions produce falling retail prices.

Density. Seagate's HAMR (heat-assisted magnetic recording) drives already make up around 40% of its data centre capacity shipments, and Western Digital plans full-scale HAMR production in 2027. As the cloud giants move to 36TB, 40TB and larger drives, the cost per terabyte falls for everyone, and older production lines can eventually be freed up for the consumer and mid-capacity drives you and I buy. That is a slow, grinding process. It points to prices levelling off through 2027 and easing from 2028, rather than a sudden correction.

Pulling all of that together, here is our best reading of how the next two years should play out. Treat it as a forecast, not a promise. Every forecast in this cycle so far has been revised at least once.

It could come sooner if AI infrastructure spending cools, Chinese NAND output ramps faster than expected, or consumer demand stays so weak that manufacturers start discounting to move stock. Module makers are already sitting on higher inventories because shoppers have stopped buying, which is the kind of pressure that eventually breaks prices.

It could come later if agentic AI takes off and data centres start storing far more data per user, if more manufacturers follow Micron's Crucial brand out of the consumer market, or if trade restrictions tighten on Chinese memory makers.

That depends entirely on why you need the storage. Here is how we would think about it.

Waiting has no clear end date, and a lost booking costs more than the price premium. Buy what the job needs, not more. Our 4TB drive roundup and rugged 4TB SSD guide focus on value at today's prices.

A failed drive with no second copy is far more expensive than any price spike, especially when a replacement costs double. A basic spinning drive is still the cheapest way to hold a second copy. Just make sure the backup is actually complete. Our guide to verifying a backup drive shows how.

SSDs are the category most likely to get cheaper first. If your current setup works, holding off until the second half of 2027 is a reasonable bet. You are accepting the risk that prices plateau rather than fall.

Buying spare capacity at record prices, to sit empty on a shelf, is the worst trade in this market. You would be paying the peak for storage you might not need until prices have eased.

The most useful thing you can do while prices are this high is find out exactly what is on the drives you already own. Most creatives with a few years of work behind them have a drawer of drives, a few with duplicate project folders, and at least one nobody is quite sure about. DriveVault turns that pile into a catalogue you can search in seconds, even when the drives are unplugged.

If you want a system for keeping it that way, our guide to organising multiple external drives covers naming, folder templates and archiving. Every terabyte you recover now is one you do not have to buy at 2026 prices.

Probably not before 2028 in any meaningful way. Seagate says the vast majority of its data centre hard drive capacity is already allocated into calendar 2028, and Western Digital has long-term agreements with price terms running through 2027 and 2028. Neither company is adding factory capacity. Relief is more likely to come from higher-capacity HAMR drives lowering the cost per terabyte than from a sudden price cut.

TrendForce expects the NAND flash market to stay in a 4 to 5 percent supply deficit through 2026 and to move back into balance in the second half of 2027. That makes late 2027 the earliest realistic window for SSD prices to start falling, with gradual easing rather than a sharp drop.

Not within the next two years on current evidence. Manufacturers have signed multi-year contracts that include pricing, and AI data centres now account for most of their output. Prices should ease from their peak, but planning around a return to 2024 prices is not supported by any current forecast.

If you need the storage for paid work or your backups are incomplete, buy now, because waiting for hard drive prices has no clear end date. If you are buying extra capacity speculatively, SSDs are the category most likely to get cheaper first, from the second half of 2027. Either way, audit the drives you already own first, since duplicated files often free up more space than people expect.

AI data centres are buying almost all of the high-capacity drives Seagate and Western Digital can build, under long-term contracts. Data centres now take around 90 percent of Seagate's shipped capacity. With consumer products a small slice of their business and no new factories planned, retail prices have more than doubled since September 2025 in ComputerBase's tracking.

Storage prices will come down. Just not yet, and not evenly. SSDs are on track to turn first, from the second half of 2027, as new flash supply catches up and consumer demand stays weak. Hard drives are locked into contracts that run into 2028, and their recovery will be slower and quieter, carried by bigger drives rather than price cuts.

The practical move for the next year is simple. Buy what you genuinely need, skip the stockpile, and get every usable terabyte out of the drives already on your desk. We will update this article as the forecasts change.

Dozens missing after boat carrying 100 migrants sinks off Senegal's coast

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The damaged boat washed ashore on a beach

Senegalese authorities are continuing search operations after a boat reportedly carrying about 100 migrants capsized off the coast.

Initial reports said that one person had died, but some local media have reported that at least four bodies have been recovered. Dozens of people are still missing.

Personal belongings were found on the badly damaged boat, which washed ashore near the village of Yenne, south of Dakar. Search operations continued throughout Wednesday and were due to resume on Thursday.

This comes less than two weeks after a shipwreck off the coast of the north-western town of Thieppe killed at least 39 people, in one of Senegal's deadliest migration disasters.

Oumar Sow, a representative of the village chief, said the latest tragedy happened around 02:00 local time (02:00 GMT).

"Cries could be heard coming from the pirogue that were audible even from the beach," the AFP news agency quoted him as saying.

A survivor questioned by the police said there were about 100 people on the boat, including five women, AFP reported.

Police recovered identity documents, several mobile phones and personal belongings from the wreckage, including a school enrolment document.

At a cabinet meeting on Wednesday, President Diomaye Faye expressed concern about a renewed increase in irregular departure by migrants hoping to reach Europe.

He instructed the interior ministry to prepare a report examining the factors driving the dangerous journeys and proposing measures to tackle the problem.

Following last month's tragedy, Faye had urged Senegalese people not to risk their lives attempting the dangerous sea crossings.

Many thousands of people from West Africa have died or gone missing trying to flee conflict, authoritarian rule or poverty in recent years.

The UN's migration agency IOM said last month that it had recorded at least 454 deaths and disappearances off the West African coast this year alone.

It warned the figures were likely to be an underestimate, as many boats disappear without leaving survivors or a trace.

As it has become more difficult to reach Europe through North Africa, due to tighter controls and increased co-operation with European countries, migrants are increasingly targeting Spain's Canary Islands.

They often travel in wooden boats known as pirogues that are frequently overloaded and not suited to such long voyages across the Atlantic Ocean.

Most migrants arriving in the Canary Islands this year are from Senegal, according to the UN.

They are driven to leave home by limited employment opportunities, family pressures and established migration networks, according to the IOM.

On 7 November, El Hierro, one of the Canary Islands, declared a state of emergency following a surge in migrant arrivals.

It said more than 3,300 people had reached the island in about 30 boats since the beginning of the month. This amounts to almost a third of the island's population.
