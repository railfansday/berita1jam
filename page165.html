Trezor Warns 27,000 Shipping Orders Were Exposed in Partner Breach, Not Wallet Keys

Hardware-wallet maker Trezor has disclosed a breach at shipping partner Skladon affecting data connected with approximately 27,000 customer orders. The incident is serious because a delivery address and product purchase history can reveal who may own crypto. It is also important not to turn a data-exposure story into a false claim that customer wallets were drained. Trezor says its own systems were not compromised, and the disclosed records did not include wallet backups, PINs or passphrases.

Trezor says Skladon informed it on November 15 of unauthorized access linked to an incident on November 7. The data at issue concerned orders placed between August 9 and November 7, 2026. The company estimates roughly 27,000 affected orders and says the investigation is continuing. The records included names, phone numbers, delivery addresses, email addresses and information about orders and products bought. A limited number of earlier August customers may also have been affected even though their information had been anonymized within Trezor’s own systems before the notification process.

Someone with a buyer’s name, address and exact device order can craft a convincing phishing message or impersonate customer support. The same combination may create physical-security concerns if a criminal believes the household controls valuable assets. None of this requires compromising the hardware wallet’s cryptographic security. Trezor says it never requests wallet recovery data by telephone and warns affected users to treat unexpected calls, texts, emails and letters with suspicion. If a message uses authentic order details, that does not make its instructions legitimate.

For related context, see our earlier report on the Unocoin wallet-key incident.

The company says it has contacted affected customers it can still reach and notified a data-protection authority. It is introducing or expanding anonymous pickup at collection points in much of Europe, reducing the amount of residential delivery information involved in future orders. Trezor also says it is working to shorten customer-data retention from 90 to 45 days, with further reductions under consideration. Those steps address the long-term exposure of operational records rather than claiming that a damaged secret key can be repaired after the fact.

Customers should go directly to the vendor’s known website for official support rather than follow contact details supplied in an unsolicited message. They should never type a recovery phrase into a web form or share it with a caller. Buyers can also consider whether their delivery address and household security require extra attention. Anyone receiving threats should contact appropriate local authorities. The precise number of affected people, rather than orders, may differ, and the final breach scope may change as investigators establish what was exported.

A legitimate package or delivery notice is especially convincing because it can refer to a real order. Criminals might use the information for emails, texts, calls or fraudulent web pages that look like shipping support. Wallet owners should be wary of any message that asks them to type a recovery phrase, install a replacement wallet application or approve an unexpected transaction. Those requests are unrelated to normal delivery updates. The strongest precaution is to verify a message through a separately opened official website, not by following a link in the message itself.

For related context, see our earlier report on Coinbase's discussion of cold-storage controls.

The distinction is straightforward: a shipping-partner breach can expose sensitive personal information without exposing the keys securing funds. For users, that does not make the incident harmless; it shifts the immediate risk toward targeted impersonation and privacy rather than a protocol-level wallet failure.

More than just a football club: Cardinale’s vision to make AC Milan a multi-billion empire

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

In case you weren’t aware from his repeatedly bold comments in the media, Gerry Cardinale has big plans for AC Milan.

The RedBird Capital founder and chief has made one concept very clear: while he is at the helm, Milan are no longer just a football club, but a platform that aims to integrate sports, infrastructure, and entertainment. Of course AC Milan will be at the centre of the web, but the idea is to expand.

Read SempreMilan ad-free and get access to exclusive news. Click here for a free trial!

The proof of this comes from a three-letter acronym: MSE. It stands for Milan Sports & Entertainment and it is the new holding company created by RedBird. The idea, simply put, is to bring together investments and activities in various sectors under one umbrella.

As La Gazzetta dello Sport recall, Cardinale explained to the Financial Times that the assets involved could be valued in the mid-single-digit billions, meaning several billion dollars. So, how does he plan to get there?

Cardinale linked the creation of MSE to the process initiated with the acquisition of Milan: “Milan Sports & Entertainment is the culmination of this work and of what we have built since we first invested in the club four years ago.”

In his interview with La Gazzetta, Cardinale clarified that the new structure is also created to support the Rossoneri’s sporting ambitions: “The driving force behind operations like this is to provide Milan with the financial strength needed to compete in the transfer market and secure the best players in the world.”

There are examples of this kind of model working already. Kroenke Sports & Entertainment, AEG and Fenway Sports Group are among the most prominent and – given his background in the USA – Cardinale knows each of them well.

According to CNBC valuations, KSE was worth $26.05bn in 2026, FSG $15.37bn, and AEG $11.99bn in 2025, excluding music and hotel businesses.

KSE boasts franchises such as the Los Angeles Rams, Denver Nuggets, Colorado Avalanche and Arsenal, as well as venues such as SoFi Stadium, Ball Arena, and Emirates Stadium. The group combines sports, infrastructure and real estate development such as Hollywood Park, the district built around SoFi Stadium.

AEG – founded by Philip Anschutz – have instead built its model on the management and development of venues. It owns or manages venues such as Crypto.com Arena and The O2 in London plus the Los Angeles Kings and LA Galaxy, and operates in concert promotion and ticketing. The LA LIVE project developed around Crypto.com Arena puts the venue at the centre of a greater ecosystem.

Fenway Sports Group – born around the Boston Red Sox and Liverpool FC – have developed a holding company with interests in media, marketing, real estate, and entertainment. It’s a model Cardinale knows first hand: in 2021, RedBird invested approximately $750m in FSG, acquiring a stake of around 11%, based on an enterprise valuation of $7.35bn.

MSE in theory can harness elements of all three of these experiences: KSE’s integration of sports and infrastructure, AEG ‘s commercial development of facilities and FSG ‘s multi-sport approach. The main difference is the purpose declared by Cardinale: to use all of it to funnel money back into Milan.

From the stadium shared with Inter to the possibility of an arena in San Donato, including the new NBA Europe venture and even tennis: MSE’s scope includes various initiatives that go beyond the playing field.

The main infrastructure project is the future Generali Stadium, the new 71,500-seat facility designed by Foster + Partners and Manica. Developed jointly by the two Milanese clubs, it represents a key element of the transformation of the San Siro area and will not be a Rossoneri-only facility.

The promoters intend for the stadium to host year-round activities, from matches to concerts, as well as catering, events, and hospitality services. In other words, like the stadiums and arenas mentioned above, it will become more than just a place to go for two hours on a Sunday.

In San Donato Milanese, RedBird are also considering the possibility of building a 20,000-seat arena. Cardinale explained that the fund already owns the land and has planning permission, but the idea is also linked to Milan’s potential involvement in NBA Europe. Another area of ​​focus concerns tennis, with the possibility of hosting a tournament in the city.

Finally, RedBird intend to develop a company dedicated to managing stadiums and arenas, with services including catering, merchandising and premium hospitality. The goal is to build commercial expertise to apply to the facilities connected to the group, but also to make it available to other owners and sports clubs.

This phase will be co-ordinated by Brett Yormark, appointed CEO of MSE and set to step down as head of the Big 12 (a conference in College Football) at the end of January 2027. He worked for 14 years at Brooklyn Sports & Entertainment, overseeing the development of the Brooklyn Nets and the Barclays Center. He has also gained experience in NASCAR and Roc Nation.

The choice to go for Yormark reflects the desire to entrust the platform to someone with expertise across multiple different fields in the sporting and commercial worlds.

All these grand plans are well and good, but how will ideas turn into money that drives AC Milan forward? MSE’s economic potential is measured not only through the club’s revenues, but also through the activities that RedBird intends to develop around sports, infrastructure and entertainment.

The starting point is obviously Milan as a club, which closed the 2025-26 financial year with revenues of €464.6m, over €100m of which came from sponsorships. The main infrastructure opportunity is the new San Siro, an investment of approximately €2.3bn shared with Inter.

Projections contained in the design documents indicate a potential annual revenue of approximately €120.4m for the facility as a whole (both for the Rossoneri and the Nerazzurri), through sporting, commercial, and complementary services

These are numbers that will be revised upwards by the two clubs when considering, for example, the record agreement with Generali for naming rights. It is worth a total of €650m over 25 years, divided into €10m per year for the first five years and €30m per season for the following 20, once the stadium is built.

One of the areas on which RedBird Capital are focusing most is hospitality, which is currently worth around €15m per year for the Rossoneri. The international benchmark has already been set by Legends Global, a company Cardinale knows well.

Legends are what the Milan owner sees as a model for developing premium services, restaurants, and commercial spaces. The most obvious example is at FC Barcelona: with the support of LG, the club have marketed nearly 5,000 VIP seats at Camp Nou, generating over €380m in contracted revenue.

They aim to generate another €700m through multi-year licenses for an additional 2,000 seats. This isn’t annual revenue, but rather contracts spread over 15 or 30 years, so a guaranteed flow of money coming in that Cardinale aims to replicate.

Another agreement which demonstrates the potential of this kind of arrangement is the one with Real Madrid for the Santiago Bernabéu. It involves an investment of approximately €360m in exchange for 20-year participation in some of the stadium’s businesses,.

Legends Global also operate in facilities and events management. Following the acquisition of ASM Global (who actually proposed renovating San Siro for non-football events in Italy) in 2024, the group claims a presence in over 450 venues, approximately 20,000 events, and 165 million guests annually. Their European partnerships include Arsenal, Chelsea and Porto.

The creation of MSE has a stated objective: to strengthen Milan’s financial capacity and reduce the gap with the richest clubs on the international scene, particularly those in the Premier League.

The gap is also evident when comparing revenues. AC Milan closed the 2025-26 season with €464.6m, a significant result considering their absence from European competitions , but still far from the top English clubs.

Speaking of the owner, it is now obvious he sees MSE as the name that will house the tools to try build a real sporting and commercial world around Milan. It remains to be seen how effective it will be, but the starting point is there.

What exactly are Flock cameras and how can you spot one?

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Flock cameras are everywhere, and they’re easier to miss than you think.

Basic descriptions of Flock cameras fall woefully short of their true capabilities and potential uses. Most people are familiar with the automated license plate reader (ALPR) version, usually pole-mounted and paired with a solar panel. If that were all there was to it, this would be a very short article. But Flock Safety (Flock Group Inc.) has become deeply intertwined with the defining terminology of ALPR cameras.

In truth, there are more than a dozen manufacturers and vendors of ALPRs; their products just aren't called Flock cameras, and each has its own aesthetic characteristics. For all intents and purposes, however, they are identical. If you see Motorola on the side of one, it likely does much of what a Flock does: read plates and log vehicles.

Most are vertically oval, narrow in width and fairly small, roughly the size of your hand. They're designed to capture your license plate and the time and place you passed by. Most are also accompanied by a solar panel. Leonardo and Genetec make the most visually distinct versions. Leonardo's are blockier, rectangular in shape, with some elongated versions. Genetec's are horizontally oval, with the same narrow width as Flock's variations, only flipped. Motorola manufactures a mix of the two.

The problem is that some Flock cameras can easily be mistaken for a standard traffic camera and vice versa. Because of this, it's difficult to accept the absolute accuracy of a project like DeFlock, which uses volunteers to identify Flock cameras visually. There's no centralized or formal verification system to independently confirm the sightings.

Flock also sells more than ALPRs. Depending on what the customer wants, the system can log details like roof racks on cars and bumper stickers. Some setups have microphones and others can recognize things like clothes and beards.

Flock cameras usually turn up where traffic funnels. They're often mounted on poles at intersections and busy traffic areas. Since many homeowners associations (HOAs) are major Flock camera customers, it shouldn't be a surprise to find them at neighborhood entrances. Shopping centers aren't off the list of potential locations either. The aesthetics aren't irrelevant, but since several manufacturers make them, there are a few other telltale signs to look for as well.

Flock cameras are meant to capture license plates, so look at where they're aimed. Check for Flock's bird logo on the camera and a solar panel above it. Because most are self-powered, there's usually little to no additional equipment nearby. Red light and speed cameras are typically larger and wired to traffic signals or enforcement poles. White, elongated cameras are another lookalike, but they mostly help traffic signals adjust to traffic flow. Some states also mount signs next to speed cameras so they're easy to identify.

Keep in mind that there are other variations of Flock cameras out there. The Flock Condor is a mounted sphere that rotates 360 degrees and tracks people. Naturally, these are mounted near areas with heavy pedestrian traffic. Flock also makes a mobile security trailer, a gunshot detector and a drone-as-first-responder. Because companies are using video integration tools to convert standard IP cameras into plate readers, it will be increasingly difficult to identify Flock or Flock-type cameras.

As a rule of thumb, if it's pointed at you, it's potentially watching you. But for a broader view, check the Atlas of Surveillance, a searchable database from the Electronic Frontier Foundation that shows what technology law enforcement agencies use. Search your town or county to see whether police are using plate readers.

While some services like MapQuest can help you find Flock cameras in your neighborhood, be wary of crowdsourced maps. DeFlock uses OpenStreetMap data collected from volunteers, but there's no official public list, so a missing pin doesn't mean there isn't a Flock camera sitting there.

Pins can also be wrong, since it's easy to confuse a Flock camera with the countless security and traffic camera variations. A Flock camera doesn't have to belong to a public agency. Flock and Flock competitors also sell to HOAs and retailers.

If you want to find every Flock camera in your city, county or state, you have to put in the work. Submit a public records request, attend the next city council meeting or ask your HOA and local businesses.
