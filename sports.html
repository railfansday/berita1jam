Portugal ‘Retire’ Cristiano Ronaldo’s 7 Shirt Despite FIFA Rule

📅 Nov 14, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Portugal's post-Cristiano Ronaldo era is set to commence on Saturday night when Jorge Jesus' side face Denmark in the Nations League, with a new number seven set to don the famous red shirt for the first time in over 20 years.

Ronaldo announced his retirement from international football earlier this month, bringing an end to his 23-year spell as the talisman and figurehead for the Selecao as Quinas. He bows out with 146 goals and 234 caps for his nation, alongside a Euro 2016 winners' medal and two Nations League titles.

Historically, players with such legacies who become synonymous with a specific shirt number often have that shirt number retired in their honour.

However, a FIFA rule stipulates that for international tournaments organised by the global footballing body, teams are required to designate consecutive numbers for every player in the squad from 1 to 26. This regulation essentially prevents national teams from retiring a shirt, meaning players will wear number seven for Portugal after Ronaldo — Rafael Leao or Carlos Forbs is expected to wear it during this international break.

However, this hasn't prevented the European national team from concocting an inventive way around this, as they look to preserve the iconic shirt number in a tribute to their greatest-ever player.

In the purest sense of the word, Portugal can't retire the number seven shirt. They cannot prevent players from wearing it in the future, but they can still do their best to ensure Ronaldo's image and brand are preserved.

Posting on their Instagram account on Saturday, the Portuguese Men's National Team shared a video revealing that from now on, they'll be printing their number seven shirts with a little cross through the middle of the number, distinguishing them from Ronaldo's crossless seven.

"Time to unveil a new 7. Because there is one that will be eternal," the caption read.

This mark of respect for Ronaldo's achievements is permitted by FIFA, as it's not eradicating the number seven from future use, but rather slightly modifying how it'll appear on the back of Portugal shirts moving forward.

Argentina reportedly had plans to retire the number ten shirt after Lionel Messi called time on his international career, but the South American nation ran into the same issue Portugal did.

Unfortunately for the 2026 World Cup finalists, there was little scope for ingenuity or invention regarding how they present the number ten shirt, so they've not been able to honour Messi in the same way. That said, Lionel Scaloni did refrain from selecting someone to wear ten during Argentina's first post-Messi match against Uruguay on Friday. Theoretically, they can keep doing this until their next tournament.

NFL reassigns 3 officials following nationwide FAA ground stop

📅 Nov 14, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

The ground stop order issued by the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) on Friday has now affected the NFL after having an impact on this weekend's college football games.

On Saturday morning, the NFL announced that several officiating assignments for Week 10 have been adjusted. Officials were reassigned to games that are within driving distance of their hometowns.

Barry Anderson and Duane Heydt, who normally serve as umpires, along with side judge Don Willard will take on referee duties on Sunday.

Anderson will officiate the Jacksonville Jaguars-Tennessee Titans game in Tennessee;; Heydt has been assigned to work the Carolina Panthers-New Orleans Saints game in New Orleans; and Willard will have double-duty first refereeing the Miami Dolphins-Indianapolis Colts game in Indianapolis at 1 p.m. ET before making the trip to Cincinnati for Sunday night's game between the Bengals and Pittsburgh Steelers at 8:20 p.m. ET.

"Each of the league's 17 officiating crews have a backup referee identified, and that individual trains throughout the year to work at the referee position should the need arise," the NFL said.

The ground stop order was put in place on Friday due to a "temporary telecommunications outage" affecting air traffic control, the FAA announced.

That stop was lifted as of 8:15 p.m. ET Friday, but even with flights allowed to resume, it led to kickoff changes for some college games. Almost all of the Power Four conferences have teams that were prevented from reaching their destinations. Only the ACC has every team already ready to go. One SEC matchup — Ole Miss vs. Oklahoma — was postponed to Sunday.

"It'll be slow," Secretary of Transportation Sean Duffy said Friday about the resumption of nationwide air travel.

It's the second nationwide ground stop since 9/11, per ABC News, which noted that this last happened in 2023. At the time, pilots were unable to access their pre-departure paperwork because the FAA NOTAM (Notice To all Air Missions) system — used to disseminate flight hazards and real-time restrictions to pilots — went down.

The 10 Most Ambitious Documentaries of All Time, Ranked

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Yes, documentaries can be a bit formulaic, and there are conventional ones that have interviews, voiceover, and fairly mundane visuals that all serve to depict something objectively, or give the impression that things are being explored objectively, but that’s not every documentary, by any means. You can put that criticism on any genre, really, because there are generic action movies, horror films, comedies, etc.

Documentaries seem to get that criticism the most often, though. It’s not really fair, and maybe one way to combat that sentiment is by highlighting some particularly cinematic and groundbreaking ones. So, the following might go some way toward that, because these feel like some of the most ambitious documentary movies of all time, with the lot of them being interesting for different reasons and certainly unexpected compared to what you might picture when you think of “ordinary” or “normal” documentaries.

Since Apocalypse Now is a tremendously ambitious – and unusual – war movie, it’s inevitable that any behind-the-scenes documentary about its production is going to be, at the very least, also quite interesting. Yet everything that happened behind the scenes with this movie was more chaotic than you'd expect, even if you were expecting chaos, and so Hearts of Darkness: A Filmmaker’s Apocalypse is ultimately a dizzying watch.

It doesn’t mess around in showcasing the hectic production of Apocalypse Now, and it’s about as psychologically intense and even as surreal as a good chunk of the actual movie is. Also, Apocalypse Now is already an easy movie to admire, but watching Hearts of Darkness: A Filmmaker’s Apocalypse is a pretty much guaranteed way to respect the fact that filming was completed – and the final product made some kind of sense – even more.

Tokyo Olympiad is a documentary about the 1964 Tokyo Summer Olympics, or might be better described as a look at said event, kind of neutral and without commentary or much of a narrative. It might sound daunting, just because of its approach and the fact that it’s almost three hours long, but it’s honestly as artistic and mesmerizing as sports footage can get, even if you're not hugely into watching footage of sports.

You notice the three-hour runtime to some extent, sure, but Tokyo Olympiad does remain interesting throughout, with everything captured in unbelievable detail. It's far more than just a clip show, as all the camera angles are a good deal more interesting than what you'd see on a TV broadcast, with the editing playing a huge role in enhancing everything and making it all feel like a largely hypnotic viewing experience.

Historical value/context is important when it comes to Man with a Movie Camera, since the existence of this film is such that the whole documentary format might look entirely different today had this movie never been made. No hyperbole, but it's likely one of the most important and influential documentaries of all time, using inventive visuals and creative editing techniques to show how life was in the Soviet Union during the 1920s.

That really is the premise, though. Man with a Movie Camera is simple, and outside the style, there isn't really a whole to this silent film. Yet it’s the style that makes it dazzling and engaging to this day, doing some interesting enough things with camera tricks and editing to make it feel exciting and very alive, all to the point where it’s worth checking out, even if you might assume that a listless documentary that’s nearly a century old would be dull. Maybe other listless 100-year-old documentaries would be. Not Man with a Movie Camera, though.

Two filmmakers were credited with making The Five Obstructions: Lars von Trier and Jørgen Leth. The former’s the one you're more likely to have heard of, and he does feel somewhat in control for much of this documentary, but that’s largely got to do with Leth being more of the subject here, since The Five Obstructions involves Lars von Trier challenging him to remake one of his old short films five different times, and with absurd rules on each occasion.

Well, technically, the rules get more unusual with each new remake, and then those shorts Leth did make (or seemed to make) are shown in quite a bit of detail, so that’s where his directing definitely comes in. There might be more involvement on his part, as it’s hard to know how much of The Five Obstructions is genuine and how much is two Danish filmmakers playing a feature-length prank on the audience, but in any event, it’s all very engaging to watch, and certainly interesting to think about, too.

Flee is an animated documentary, which is interesting enough on its own (see also another documentary from 2008 that’ll be mentioned a little further down). It’s about a man’s life experiences, basically, including his time as a refugee and how he grappled with his sexuality while surrounded by people who weren't always accepting of it… and the animation itself is a way to protect his identity, essentially.

Rightly sometimes regarded as one of the greatest films of all time, including non-documentaries, Shoah is a mammoth documentary and one that people owe to themselves to watch at least once in their lives, though you do need to devote a ton of time to finishing it. The runtime stands out and has to be addressed, since Shoah goes for more than nine hours, using that length of time to hammer home the enormity of the event it explores.

It’s made up entirely of interviews with people recalling the Holocaust, including survivors, witnesses/bystanders, and even a few perpetrators. There’s no archival footage or much else beyond those interviews, with the approach nevertheless being used to form a sprawling and daunting narrative by stringing together all those different testimonies. O.J.: Made in America could well be the greatest crime documentary of all time. However, it's also more than a documentary about one particularly publicized event and subsequent criminal trial, using the story of O.J. A film that covers a particular war within an extensive conflict that’s still relevant to this day, Waltz with Bashir is uniquely presented and shattering as a film. It has director Ari Folman taking a personal approach to covering the 1982 Lebanon War, wartime atrocities committed, and attached post-traumatic stress disorder, with the film being about his attempts to recover his memories from his involvement in the conflict as a young man.

Waltz with Bashir explores the conflict – in particular an infamous event known as the Sabra and Shatila massacre – not from the victims’ point of view, which has made it controversial. However, it doesn’t feel like it’s in favor of the attack’s perpetrators, showing the horror of what happened and condemning both it and other atrocities around the same time. Not everyone agrees with that, but in any event, what this does as a wholly distinctive and unusually presented documentary is impressive, and it does feel like Waltz with Bashir inevitably achieves what it set out to do.

Of all the documentaries here that feel like so much more than “just” documentaries, Koyaanisqatsi is perhaps the most groundbreaking, unusual, and compelling. There isn't anything here by way of voiceover or interviews, and you don’t even get all that much text explaining the things you're seeing and hearing, but Koyaanisqatsi does still get its message across through all its sights and sounds.

Different people will get slightly different things from it, yet the environmental message and the exploration of a conflict between humanity and the natural world are still very much effectively conveyed, not to mention ultimately hard to miss if you pay even the slightest amount of attention. And Koyaanisqatsi is worth paying much more than a slight amount of attention. How to Lose Weight Naturally, According to Experts

Here are 10 easy-to-follow tips to speed up the process—and make it more enjoyable.

Starting a weight-loss journey is no easy feat. There’s a lot to think about, between getting into a regular workout routine, changing up what you eat, and maybe even considering a GLP-1 medication.

But at its core, weight loss is about developing a healthy, sustainable relationship with food and movement. You don’t have to make a dozen changes all at once—the goal is long-term success, even if it’s a slow process. That’s why you should aim to lose one to two pounds a week, says Danielle Crumble Smith, RDN, a dietitian at Eat Well, Live Well. Working out at a manageable pace, eating balanced meals, and making maintainable changes can help you feel your best.

Meet the experts: Danielle Crumble Smith, RDN, is a nutritionist at Eat Well, Live Well. Kami Blease, CPT, is a personal trainer at Fyt Personal Training. Tatyana Johnston, CPT, is a former Nike master trainer and event specialist at Lululemon. Jordan Hill, RD, is a nutritionist with Top Nutrition Coaching.

Ahead, experts share their 10 best tips to make your weight-loss journey successful.

First, be sure to consult your doctor—they’ll help you set mindful, attainable goals that will keep your body healthy and strong. Then, try implementing more of these habits into your lifestyle:

Take the stairs every day at work or hold a quick plank first thing in the morning. "All these things add up over time," says Kami Blease, CPT, a personal trainer at Fyt Personal Training. "I recommend finding things you could do almost daily.”

Additionally, try adding a brisk 30-minute walk into your day (even if it’s broken up into three 10-minute walks) to increase calorie burn and push you closer to your weight-loss goals, per the Mayo Clinic. "It's low impact and not a super strenuous form of movement, which increases the chances that someone will stick with it,” says Tatyana Johnston, CPT, a former Nike master trainer and event specialist at Lululemon. "Just remember to recover with hydration, fuel, and stretching.”

Strength training “helps with balance and stability, improves metabolism, and even lowers your risk of certain diseases," says Blease. Resistance training has also been found to help with weight loss and body recomposition, according to a 2026 study in Frontiers in Endocrinology that had 304 adults follow a calorie-restricted diet and either resistance train, do aerobic exercise, or no exercise for 1 to 18 months.

To get comfortable with form, try practicing bodyweight exercises first, Blease recommends, then add weights after you’ve mastered the move. This can help you focus on progressive overload—which means gradually increasing the weight, frequency, or number of reps over time, Blease says. Eventually, you should aim to work all major muscle groups at least twice a week, per the new ACSM resistance training guidelines.

Cardio can also help with weight loss—the CDC recommends 150 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic activity (such as brisk walking) per week, or 75 minutes of vigorous-intensity activity (like running) each week. However, this number can vary based on someone’s individual health, and the intensity of the cardio. "Consistency is key. If you can establish and stick to a cardio schedule, you have a better chance at losing weight and keeping it off," Johnston says. Playing sports, working out with friends, or listening to a podcast during cardio can help boost your motivation too.

Also, if you enjoy high-impact exercises and want to lose weight fast, HIIT workouts can be a great way to expend a lot of energy in a short period of time. "You can ramp up the intensity by trying to get in more reps during a certain amount of time or using heavier weights while maintaining the same time goal,” says Blease. “Both will increase your intensity and continue to challenge your body."

If the gym or workout classes aren’t really your speed, choose a form of movement that you love so you can stay consistent with it. “Hiking, swimming, skiing, surfing, or biking are all forms of movement that might feel more sustainable,” says Blease.

"Start smaller than you might think," Blease says. "Often, people overcommit because they are excited. However, it's then easy to get burnt out." After a few weeks, add in a second day, and when that feels seamless, try a third, and over time, you'll find a cadence that works for you, she adds.

Just make sure you always have at least one rest day. "People can get so fixated on weight loss that they end up [overdoing exercise and] hurting themselves," Blease says. "Take time to stretch, rest, and recover as needed."

"Having a trainer create a program that is specific to your needs ensures that you are more likely to get the results you are looking for—and keep your body safe in the process," says Blease. They can help you lose fat while building muscle, which can better your overall metabolic health and insulin sensitivity, and regulate blood sugar to support a healthier body recomposition.

A trainer will also make sure you maintain proper form and that you stay consistent. Decide on a number of days per week that fits your goal and budget, and ask your trainer to help hold you accountable, Blease says.

Research suggests that working with a personal trainer may have more profound effects on weight loss: A 2024 study in Heliyon had 66 adults train individually, with a partner, or a personal trainer over 12 weeks, and they monitored their improvements in bench presses, squats, skeletal muscle mass, and fat mass. The group that worked with a personal trainer improved their leg training and reduced fat mass compared to the other groups.

Protein can help you stay full throughout the day, stabilize your blood sugar levels, and synthesize muscle mass, Smith says, helping with weight loss. While individual dietary needs vary, you can generally aim for 30 to 35 grams of protein per meal to help you stay full throughout the day. Some good sources include eggs, poultry, seafood, red meat, beans, peas, lentils, legumes, nuts, seeds, and soy, per the current Dietary Guidelines for Americans.

Additionally, "fiber is more slowly digested and helps keep you feeling full for a longer period,” Smith adds, recommending vegetables. Increased vegetable intake is also linked to lower blood sugar levels, lower triglycerides, and can help promote liver function. If you don't love veggies, you can also puree them and add them to a sauce or weight-loss-friendly smoothie.

Lastly, don’t forget about healthy (unsaturated) fats—which can help provide energy, per the Mayo Clinic. Try avocados, extra-virgin olive oil, nuts, seeds, soybeans, and seafood.

"Drinking enough water can help you to feel full and prevent overeating," says dietitian Jordan Hill, RD. Aim to drink about half your body weight in ounces of hydrating fluids per day.

Also, avoid sugary beverages. "Juices, sodas, and fancy coffees are often the calorie-equivalent of a meal, yet they will still leave you hungry," says Smith. "The sugar content is often more than a candy bar, which will cause blood sugar levels to rise." Instead, replace your beverages with fruit-infused water, herbal tea, or coffee with cinnamon or zero-calorie sweeteners.

Avoid foods that are high in added sugars, unhealthy fats, and empty calories, which can contribute to weight gain, says Hill: "Instead, focus on eating whole, nutrient-dense foods that will provide your body with the nutrients it needs."

Listening to your internal cues of hunger and fullness can help you control portion sizes, which can also aid weight loss. "Honoring fullness can help prevent overeating and ensure that you're not consuming more calories than your body needs," says Hill. Keeping a food journal can also help you become more mindful of what you're eating, when, and how you're feeling throughout the process of losing weight.

“When you skip meals or go too long in between meals or snacks, you get overly hungry. This makes it much more difficult to make healthy choices, and it also makes it harder to not overeat later," says Smith. Skipping meals can also result in a slower metabolism and leave you feeling fatigued, which can negatively impact your workouts both in effort and recovery.

If you struggle to eat consistent meals, Smith recommends munching on small snacks throughout the day. “Set yourself up for success by bringing healthy snacks with you when you’re away from home, so you’re not caught in a situation where you are really hungry but have no access to healthy food,” she says.

If you need extra assistance, a dietitian can analyze your diet and recommend personalized tips for how you can eat to lose weight, which may be more helpful than doing it on your own. A 2025 review of articles on weight-loss interventions provided by a dietitian for overweight and obese adults in the Journal of the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics looked at how working with a dietitian improved their health markers, as opposed to not working with one (the control group). The group that worked with a dietitian had improved weight loss, waist circumference, blood pressure, and quality of life compared to the control group.

When you encounter a setback (like when you skip a few days of workouts), it can be discouraging and all-consuming, and can cause you to fall off track. "One of the most crucial components of successfully reaching a goal is to not let setbacks ruin our progress,” says Johnston. “The setback is often not as severe as we think, and will not impact our end goal very much at all.”

Just take a breath and realize we’re all human and mistakes are normal; then move forward with your health plan in mind.

And don’t forget to be kind to yourself. Practicing self-compassion and returning to your plan will help reinforce your goal and remind you that this weight-loss journey requires patience and perseverance. "Practice giving yourself grace when [you] slip up,” Johnston says. “As long as it doesn’t become a habit, you will continue moving forward towards your goal.”

Harry Kane was asked this week to name the lowest moment of his 125 England caps, and his mind travelled back to that evening in Qatar when he sent a penalty over Hugo Lloris’s crossbar and up to the heavens. Kane being Kane, he would score the next 30 penalties of his career. “It made me improve,” he said. “A lot of the bad things have helped me get to where I am now.”

Perhaps it was apt, then, that on the night of his record-breaking 126th England cap, Kane scored his 92nd England goal from the penalty spot to settle this 2-1 win over Croatia and book a spot in the Nations League quarter-finals. It was a little display of his work ethic, a simple kick of the ball honed to a fine point through thousands of repetitions on the training ground.

Kane dinked his penalty down the middle and a diving Dominik Livakovic almost blocked the ball with his trail leg. The goalkeeper beat the ground in frustration as if to say, I knew you’d do that. But perhaps this is the point: since that World Cup miss against France, Kane has trained every penalty into his armoury – the stutter or the run, the blast or the dink, left or right or the space a goalkeeper leaves behind – and Livakovic couldn’t cover them all.

It came after a rare miss for Bayern Munich last month. “The next one is always important to bounce back,” Kane said. “I did my usual routine yesterday in training and looked at the goalkeeper. I haven’t gone down the middle with a penalty for maybe three or four years. With penalties, you have to keep things fresh, keep things moving. I thought it was a good time to do it, looking at his clips. Thankfully, it paid off today.”

What did we learn from this slightly underwhelming performance? England are better than Croatia, but then we saw that at the World Cup when England beat them 4-2, and more emphatically in Rijeka last month when England won 7-0. This was naturally a more cautious Croatia, against an altogether sloppier England who made a series of uncharacteristic errors all over the field.

Trent Alexander-Arnold gave the ball away on the edge of his own box, Lewis Hall played a fancy flick straight off the pitch and Jarell Quansah assisted Croatia’s equaliser with a deft, daft header inside his own six-yard box. Jude Bellingham showed frustration towards some of his teammates’ errors, yet his best game largely deserted him too.

The England fans who shivered through these 90 minutes might instead remember the few moments beforehand. The commemorations began with a giant Tifo behind one goal displaying England’s captain roaring into the night sky with the words “England’s finest” and “Kane 126” emblazoned top and bottom. Kane emerged from the tunnel with his four children as mascots, and then spent a couple of minutes waving around the Ballon d’Or before being presented with three gifts, like a scene from the nativity. The three wise men were played by Peter Shilton (gift one: a golden crest), Croatia captain Luka Modric (gift two: an old football), and Prince William (gift three: a framed shirt).

"It's been a crazy few weeks,” Kane said. “It takes a lot out of you emotionally, physically and mentally ... it’s a hard balance because you want to take it in. Breaking the record is a massive thing in the history of our game, seeing the tifo there, having my kids with me, and being presented by all the people was beyond dreams, so of course you have got to take it in. Once the whistle goes and you’re on the pitch, you just start focusing on what your job is.”

England started haphazardly but began to get a grip midway through the first half and it was Kane who unlocked Croatia’s defence with a raking pass to Bellingham arriving at the far post. Bellingham’s first-time cross found Morgan Rogers, who managed to impart just enough energy to the ball to bounce it home from three yards.

Kane had to wait. His first big chance came early in the second half when he received a cutback in the box from Declan Rice and a half-empty goal prepared to bulge, but Josip Stanisic wasn’t feeling sentimental and he lunged in front to block Kane’s goalbound shot.

Croatia drew level a few minutes later when Quansah took the extraordinary step of attempting a header back to Jordan Pickford inside a crowded penalty area. Pickford understandably wasn’t expecting the pass, and he was beaten to the ball by Petar Sucic who knocked it into the roof of England’s goal.

But Quansah went some way to making amends when his run drew some suspicious shirt pulling from Ivan Smolcic. Danish referee Morten Krogh missed it at first look but VAR intervened, and when Kane placed the ball down on the spot, the rest felt inevitable.

England now travel to Madrid to face Spain on Sunday, where victory would clinch top spot in the group. It would also serve as a sign of England’s standing in the game right now if they were to beat the world champions on their own turf, after a narrow but encouraging 3-2 defeat by Spain at Wembley in September.

Kane will earn cap number 127 and he is intent on gathering many more. Since winning the Ballon d’Or he has made his remaining career targets abundantly clear: scoring 100 England goals, winning the Champions League with Bayern Munich and winning a major trophy for England.

Whether the Nations League counts as “major” is debatable – Cristiano Ronaldo might say yes but just about everyone else says no – yet it presents an opportunity to end England’s 60-year wait for a men’s trophy. It would be fitting if Kane, now England’s greatest player by any measure, was the man to lift it.
