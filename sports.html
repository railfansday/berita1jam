Know Your Foe: Oklahoma Sooners vs. Ole Miss Rebels

📅 Nov 14, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

The Oklahoma Sooners will be back at home in Week 11, with a record of 5-4 overall and 3-3 in the SEC. Head coach Brent Venables and his team will be looking to win consecutive games for the first time all season and get their first home win in conference play in 2026.

The up-and-down season began with a win over UTEP, but then a loss to Michigan. The Sooners beat New Mexico, but lost to Georgia. After the bye week, OU beat Texas, then lost to Kentucky. Oklahoma then beat Mississippi State, but lost to South Carolina. After beating Florida last week, the Sooners are looking to build on their first win of the season.

This week, the Ole Miss Rebels will be in Norman. They went 13-2 last year, made the College Football Playoff with an 11-1 record, and beat Tulane at home and Georgia in the Sugar Bowl in the first two rounds. A Fiesta Bowl semifinal loss against Miami ended their season just short of the title game.

It's been a whirlwind 12 months for the Rebs, beginning with the infamous end-of-regular-season departure of former head coach Lane Kiffin, who left Oxford to coach LSU. Kiffin's defensive coordinator, Pete Golding, was hired as the full-time head coach and led the team through the CFP run. Now, Golding is in his first full season as the program's head coach. He arrived at Ole Miss after stints as the defensive coordinator at UTSA and Alabama. He was the defensive play-caller for 'Bama's 2020 national title team that went undefeated. He was Ole Miss' defensive coordinator for three seasons before landing the head-coaching job.

In 2026, the Rebels are 6-3 and 4-3 in the SEC. A strong start to the season saw the Rebels beat Louisville (41-38), Charlotte (41-9), and get revenge against Kiffin and LSU with a 32-24 win at home. A loss to Florida (52-28) followed, but Golding's team bounced back after its bye week, beating Vanderbilt (52-17) and Missouri (31-3). However, the last three weeks have seen the Rebels lose to Texas (34-27), beat Auburn (45-15), and get dominated last week by Georgia in a 42-7 home loss.

This game pits two teams coming off of very different emotional results in great primetime atmospheres, and if the SEC has taught us anything this year, it's that it is definitely a week-to-week league in 2026.

On offense, the Rebels are led by co-offensive coordinators John David Baker and Cody Woodiel. Baker is the play-caller, with Golding having hired him away from East Carolina ahead of this season. He spent two years there, after he'd been on Kiffin's staff in Oxford from 2021 to 2023. He'll be matched up with Venables, who will call the defense for the Sooners. Woodiel comes over from Miami, where he'd coached for the last four years on Mario Cristobal's staff.

Baker's offense is very similar to what Ole Miss has run for the past few years with Kiffin and former offensive coordinator Charlie Weis Jr. at the controls. There are elements of the Art Briles-style veer-and-shoot that OU has already seen this year against Mississippi State and South Carolina, but there are also plenty of pro-style or more traditional spread elements in the mix. Baker and the Rebels put a lot of pressure on opposing defenses, and they have the talent to play well against anyone. Wide receivers coach L'Damian Washington has worked under Venables before, as he was OU's interim WRs coach in 2022.

It all starts with star quarterback Trinidad Chambliss, who came out of nowhere to take the starting job and run with it for Ole Miss last year. He won a court case this offseason to get to come back and play one more collegiate season, and he is one of the most dynamic dual-threat QBs in the nation. Chambliss' playmaking ability and accuracy gave the Sooners fits last year, and it's yet another week against an athletic signal-caller for Venables' defense.

Star running back Kewan Lacy is out for the season, which means the ground game will lean on Makhi Frazier (if he's able to play) and Shekai Mills-Knight. The Rebels certainly won't be shy about running Chambliss as well. Through the air, wide receivers Deuce Alexander and Horatio Fields are the top targets, along with tight end Caleb Odom. The Rebels have a dynamite passing attack, and OU's secondary will have to be up to the challenge this week. Offensive lineman Troy Everett will return to Owen Field this week after a long career as a Sooner, including serving as a captain last year.

On the other side of the ball, OU interim offensive coordinator Jason Witten will be in his second game as the play-caller, after taking over for Ben Arbuckle last week. He'll match wits with Golding, who calls Ole Miss' defense himself. Golding, a great defensive mind and longtime play-caller, consulted Venables when he took the head coaching job about continuing to call plays, as Venables has wrestled with the play-calling duties at OU. Upon Venables' suggestion, Golding decided to continue calling his own plays.

Co-defensive coordinators Bryan Brown and Marcus Woodson will be aiding Golding as he looks to get the best of Witten. Brown has worked for Golding since 2024, after serving as defensive coordinator at both Louisville and Cincinnati. Woodson is in his first season in Oxford, after stints at Memphis, Auburn, Florida State, and Arkansas.

Golding's unit has experienced peaks and valleys this season, and linebackers Keaton Thomas, Luke Ferrelli, and Tah'j Butler lead the team in tackles, though Thomas won't play this week. Three players do most of the getting after the quarterback for this team. Defensive end Blake Purchase (4.5 sacks), linebacker Suntarine Perkins (3.5 sacks), and defensive end Will Echoles (2.5 sacks), will draw plenty of attention from the OU o-line, especially as it's looking likely that Echoles will play this week.

Oklahoma quarterback John Mateer needs to build on his performance against Florida last week, and you can expect that Oklahoma is going to try to continue to bleed the clock, shorten the game, and keep the ball on the ground. It worked well last week against the Gators, and that's where the Sooners would love to live again this week against the Rebels.

OU special teams coordinator Doug Deakin will face off against Ole Miss special teams coordinator Jake Schoonover this week, and he's in his fourth year at Ole Miss, arriving at the same time as Golding, though he came from Arkansas State. Schoonover's unit is one of the nation's best, led by star kicker Lucas Carneiro, who is one of the nation's best. Oklahoma's special teams had a poor day in bad weather against the Rebels last year, and with rain in the forecast again this weekend, kicker Tate Sandell and company will have to be on point against a very strong group.

OU and Ole Miss have just three meetings all-time between, but they're now annual rivals for the next four years, at least according to the SEC. The first meeting came in the 1999 Independence Bowl in Bob Stoops' first season at the helm of the Sooners. OU lost a tight 27-25 game that night in Shreveport, but went undefeated and won the national championship the next year.

The last two meetings have come in the last two seasons as SEC foes. Oklahoma went to Oxford in 2024, losing 26-14. Ole Miss came to Norman last year and again defeated OU, improving their record against the Sooners to 3-0 all-time. The 34-26 loss was the final loss of Oklahoma's 2025 regular season, as Venables' team won out in November to advance to the CFP.

Ole Miss is wounded, desperate, and hungry heading into Saturday afternoon's game. They must win out to have any shot at returning to the playoff, and they got embarrassed on a big-time stage last week. Venables' Sooners have typically played better football in his tenure when their backs are against the wall, and they've struggled when the other team is the one who has to punch its way out. Building on success is not something Oklahoma has done well this season, and it's certainly been a talking point inside the walls of the Switzer Center this week.

Ole Miss is talented and explosive enough to pull away in this game, especially if OU's offense reverts back to its old self for long stretches of this game. However, if the Sooners get help on offense, contain Chambliss, and get a few breaks on defense, they are good enough to win this game against a Rebel team that has a few more issues than it did last year.

One more win would ensure Oklahoma's 28th-straight bowl berth, but the focus right now for Venables' Sooners is on finishing out this year in the best possible fashion. Last week's cathartic win over Florida was impressive and an encouraging sign after last week, but can Oklahoma build on that success and string together wins? It's something this team has been unable to do all season long, but the next opportunity to change that comes this week. Seven or eight wins looks a whole lot better than five or six wins, and a strong finish to the year could at least salvage a little pride from a 2026 season gone wrong.

Kickoff for Oklahoma-Ole Miss is scheduled for 3:15 p.m. CT in Norman on Saturday on the SEC Network.

FAA ground stop delays KC Current’s flight to NWSL semifinal against Gotham FC

📅 Nov 14, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

The Federal Aviation Administration's ground stop Friday affected the travel of the Kansas City Current, who were scheduled to fly to New York City for their National Women's Soccer League semifinal against Gotham FC on Sunday.

The team planned to charter Friday evening and boarded their plane shortly before the ground stop, according to a source with knowledge of the Current's travel plans who spoke on the condition of anonymity due to the ongoing nature of the team's planning. The team deplaned after it became clear the delay was indefinite.

A spokesperson for the Current confirmed the team's travel disruption.

"We are collaborating with key stakeholders, including the NWSL and our charter partner, to identify next steps in order to arrive safely ahead of our match against Gotham FC on Sunday," the spokesperson said.

The Utah Royals, who will face the Washington Spirit in the other semifinal, at noon ET on Saturday, traveled Thursday and were not affected by the nationwide ground stop, a Royals spokesperson confirmed to The Athletic.

The NWSL semifinals are part of a full weekend of sports, including college football and basketball games, and NBA, NHL and NFL matchups. The U.S. men's national team is scheduled to play Haiti on Saturday in Miami. That match will not be affected, a spokesperson for Concacaf, the regional governing body in charge of the match, told The Athletic. Both teams and match officials are in Miami, and the U.S. team's flight from Atlanta touched down just before the FAA issued the nationwide ground stop.

"Everyone is already on-site, and the match will proceed as scheduled," the spokesperson said.

U.S. Secretary of Transportation Sean Duffy said in a social media post that FAA technicians are investigating a "communication issue impacting all Air Route Traffic Control Centers."

This is not the first disruption NWSL teams have faced ahead of this weekend's penultimate weekend, although the other was unrelated to travel.

After Gotham FC and the Washington Spirit secured hosting rights to the semifinals last weekend, the league and teams had to scramble to secure venues and locations. The Spirit were able to negotiate with D.C. United and organizers for the Secretaries' Cup, an annual NCAA Division III college football game between the U.S. Coast Guard Academy and the U.S. Merchant Marine Academy, to play at their typical home venue of Audi Field at noon ET. The Spirit will play ahead of the football game, which will start at approximately 4:30 p.m. ET. The field will be lined for both sports (NWSL lines in yellow and football yard lines in white) to avoid having to repaint between games. The bases for the field goal posts will also be in place and padded during the NWSL match.

In New Jersey, Gotham had to move their home game from Sports Illustrated Stadium to Icahn Stadium on Randall's Island in New York due to poor pitch conditions at their usual home stadium. That match is scheduled to take place at 3 p.m. ET on Sunday with Gotham providing complimentary transportation services to the 5,000-seat venue for fans in the New York area.

The 10 Most Ambitious Documentaries of All Time, Ranked

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Yes, documentaries can be a bit formulaic, and there are conventional ones that have interviews, voiceover, and fairly mundane visuals that all serve to depict something objectively, or give the impression that things are being explored objectively, but that’s not every documentary, by any means. You can put that criticism on any genre, really, because there are generic action movies, horror films, comedies, etc.

Documentaries seem to get that criticism the most often, though. It’s not really fair, and maybe one way to combat that sentiment is by highlighting some particularly cinematic and groundbreaking ones. So, the following might go some way toward that, because these feel like some of the most ambitious documentary movies of all time, with the lot of them being interesting for different reasons and certainly unexpected compared to what you might picture when you think of “ordinary” or “normal” documentaries.

Since Apocalypse Now is a tremendously ambitious – and unusual – war movie, it’s inevitable that any behind-the-scenes documentary about its production is going to be, at the very least, also quite interesting. Yet everything that happened behind the scenes with this movie was more chaotic than you'd expect, even if you were expecting chaos, and so Hearts of Darkness: A Filmmaker’s Apocalypse is ultimately a dizzying watch.

It doesn’t mess around in showcasing the hectic production of Apocalypse Now, and it’s about as psychologically intense and even as surreal as a good chunk of the actual movie is. Also, Apocalypse Now is already an easy movie to admire, but watching Hearts of Darkness: A Filmmaker’s Apocalypse is a pretty much guaranteed way to respect the fact that filming was completed – and the final product made some kind of sense – even more.

Tokyo Olympiad is a documentary about the 1964 Tokyo Summer Olympics, or might be better described as a look at said event, kind of neutral and without commentary or much of a narrative. It might sound daunting, just because of its approach and the fact that it’s almost three hours long, but it’s honestly as artistic and mesmerizing as sports footage can get, even if you're not hugely into watching footage of sports.

You notice the three-hour runtime to some extent, sure, but Tokyo Olympiad does remain interesting throughout, with everything captured in unbelievable detail. It's far more than just a clip show, as all the camera angles are a good deal more interesting than what you'd see on a TV broadcast, with the editing playing a huge role in enhancing everything and making it all feel like a largely hypnotic viewing experience.

Historical value/context is important when it comes to Man with a Movie Camera, since the existence of this film is such that the whole documentary format might look entirely different today had this movie never been made. No hyperbole, but it's likely one of the most important and influential documentaries of all time, using inventive visuals and creative editing techniques to show how life was in the Soviet Union during the 1920s.

That really is the premise, though. Man with a Movie Camera is simple, and outside the style, there isn't really a whole to this silent film. Yet it’s the style that makes it dazzling and engaging to this day, doing some interesting enough things with camera tricks and editing to make it feel exciting and very alive, all to the point where it’s worth checking out, even if you might assume that a listless documentary that’s nearly a century old would be dull. Maybe other listless 100-year-old documentaries would be. Not Man with a Movie Camera, though.

Two filmmakers were credited with making The Five Obstructions: Lars von Trier and Jørgen Leth. The former’s the one you're more likely to have heard of, and he does feel somewhat in control for much of this documentary, but that’s largely got to do with Leth being more of the subject here, since The Five Obstructions involves Lars von Trier challenging him to remake one of his old short films five different times, and with absurd rules on each occasion.

Well, technically, the rules get more unusual with each new remake, and then those shorts Leth did make (or seemed to make) are shown in quite a bit of detail, so that’s where his directing definitely comes in. There might be more involvement on his part, as it’s hard to know how much of The Five Obstructions is genuine and how much is two Danish filmmakers playing a feature-length prank on the audience, but in any event, it’s all very engaging to watch, and certainly interesting to think about, too.

Flee is an animated documentary, which is interesting enough on its own (see also another documentary from 2008 that’ll be mentioned a little further down). It’s about a man’s life experiences, basically, including his time as a refugee and how he grappled with his sexuality while surrounded by people who weren't always accepting of it… and the animation itself is a way to protect his identity, essentially.

There's quite a bit of time covered throughout Flee, and also numerous weighty subjects discussed and explored.

It’s simple animation, sure, but it does still lead to interesting presentation, and there’s quite a bit of time covered throughout Flee, and also numerous weighty subjects discussed and explored. The whole thing ends up being rather moving and quite memorable. Though it’s currently in an awkward middle-ground between being a new release and something old enough to be considered a classic, there’s a good chance time will ultimately be kind to Flee.

Rightly sometimes regarded as one of the greatest films of all time, including non-documentaries, Shoah is a mammoth documentary and one that people owe to themselves to watch at least once in their lives, though you do need to devote a ton of time to finishing it. The runtime stands out and has to be addressed, since Shoah goes for more than nine hours, using that length of time to hammer home the enormity of the event it explores.

It’s made up entirely of interviews with people recalling the Holocaust, including survivors, witnesses/bystanders, and even a few perpetrators. There’s no archival footage or much else beyond those interviews, with the approach nevertheless being used to form a sprawling and daunting narrative by stringing together all those different testimonies. Shoah is a challenging watch, but it has to be, also feeling about as essential and historically significant as war documentaries can get.

O.J.: Made in America could well be the greatest crime documentary of all time. However, it's also more than a documentary about one particularly publicized event and subsequent criminal trial, using the story of O.J. Simpson and his much-discussed trial to unpack a whole range of things, including the American dream, the country's history of racial conflict, and, more broadly, investigating American culture throughout the second half of the 20th century.

Sure, it’s seven and a half hours long, which means it has room to do a lot, but it’s still impressive how much is packed into that runtime. Also, it has to be acknowledged that it exists in a strange middle-ground between movie and miniseries, as it was screened theatrically and therefore won an Oscar for Best Documentary, but most would've watched it as a miniseries in five parts, and the Academy Awards changed the rules after O.J.: Made in America won. Oh well… point is, it deserved to win, and it’s easily one of the most monumental documentaries of the century so far.

Depending on where you spend your time online in 2026, you may feel like You Can See Everything is one of the biggest movies of the year. At least in some circles, it’s one of the most discussed, but then also if you want to look at things more objectively, or outside certain circles, it has already earned a lot for a documentary (and, at the time of writing, it’s only just starting to get released outside the U.S.), and the level of critical acclaim has been similarly remarkable.

It’s co-directed by Nathan Fielder, who uses the documentary to build on some of the stuff he was doing in The Rehearsal, which was a show where he built upon the stuff he was doing in Nathan for You, but what Lance Oppenheim contributes here as the other co-director shouldn’t be overlooked (he’s had a good year, since he was also behind the recent Primetime). As for the content of You Can See Everything… it’s wild. It’s best not to say anything about why it’s wild. But it is. It’s a big deal. It’s hard to stop thinking about, once seen, and moves so well for such a long and static documentary, if it can even be called a documentary in the first place.

A film that covers a particular war within an extensive conflict that’s still relevant to this day, Waltz with Bashir is uniquely presented and shattering as a film. It has director Ari Folman taking a personal approach to covering the 1982 Lebanon War, wartime atrocities committed, and attached post-traumatic stress disorder, with the film being about his attempts to recover his memories from his involvement in the conflict as a young man.

Waltz with Bashir explores the conflict – in particular an infamous event known as the Sabra and Shatila massacre – not from the victims’ point of view, which has made it controversial. However, it doesn’t feel like it’s in favor of the attack’s perpetrators, showing the horror of what happened and condemning both it and other atrocities around the same time. It feels distinctly anti-war, with Folman addressing what he feels he can personally speak to, doing so in a way that feels self-reflective and critical. Not everyone agrees with that, but in any event, what this does as a wholly distinctive and unusually presented documentary is impressive, and it does feel like Waltz with Bashir inevitably achieves what it set out to do.

Of all the documentaries here that feel like so much more than “just” documentaries, Koyaanisqatsi is perhaps the most groundbreaking, unusual, and compelling. There isn't anything here by way of voiceover or interviews, and you don’t even get all that much text explaining the things you're seeing and hearing, but Koyaanisqatsi does still get its message across through all its sights and sounds.

Different people will get slightly different things from it, yet the environmental message and the exploration of a conflict between humanity and the natural world are still very much effectively conveyed, not to mention ultimately hard to miss if you pay even the slightest amount of attention. And Koyaanisqatsi is worth paying much more than a slight amount of attention. It’s easily among the most memorable and radically presented documentary movies of all time, and it’s lost none of its power when watched today, more than four decades on from its initial release.

How to Lose Weight Naturally, According to Experts

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Here are 10 easy-to-follow tips to speed up the process—and make it more enjoyable.

Women's Health may earn commission from the links on this page, but we only feature products we believe in. Why Trust Us?

Starting a weight-loss journey is no easy feat. There’s a lot to think about, between getting into a regular workout routine, changing up what you eat, and maybe even considering a GLP-1 medication.

But at its core, weight loss is about developing a healthy, sustainable relationship with food and movement. You don’t have to make a dozen changes all at once—the goal is long-term success, even if it’s a slow process. That’s why you should aim to lose one to two pounds a week, says Danielle Crumble Smith, RDN, a dietitian at Eat Well, Live Well. Working out at a manageable pace, eating balanced meals, and making maintainable changes can help you feel your best.

Meet the experts: Danielle Crumble Smith, RDN, is a nutritionist at Eat Well, Live Well. Kami Blease, CPT, is a personal trainer at Fyt Personal Training. Tatyana Johnston, CPT, is a former Nike master trainer and event specialist at Lululemon. Jordan Hill, RD, is a nutritionist with Top Nutrition Coaching.

Ahead, experts share their 10 best tips to make your weight-loss journey successful.

First, be sure to consult your doctor—they’ll help you set mindful, attainable goals that will keep your body healthy and strong. Then, try implementing more of these habits into your lifestyle:

Take the stairs every day at work or hold a quick plank first thing in the morning. "All these things add up over time," says Kami Blease, CPT, a personal trainer at Fyt Personal Training. "I recommend finding things you could do almost daily.”

Additionally, try adding a brisk 30-minute walk into your day (even if it’s broken up into three 10-minute walks) to increase calorie burn and push you closer to your weight-loss goals, per the Mayo Clinic. "It's low impact and not a super strenuous form of movement, which increases the chances that someone will stick with it,” says Tatyana Johnston, CPT, a former Nike master trainer and event specialist at Lululemon. "Just remember to recover with hydration, fuel, and stretching.”

Burn fat and build muscle with the WH+ 4-week walking plan

Strength training “helps with balance and stability, improves metabolism, and even lowers your risk of certain diseases," says Blease. Resistance training has also been found to help with weight loss and body recomposition, according to a 2026 study in Frontiers in Endocrinology that had 304 adults follow a calorie-restricted diet and either resistance train, do aerobic exercise, or no exercise for 1 to 18 months.

To get comfortable with form, try practicing bodyweight exercises first, Blease recommends, then add weights after you’ve mastered the move. This can help you focus on progressive overload—which means gradually increasing the weight, frequency, or number of reps over time, Blease says. Eventually, you should aim to work all major muscle groups at least twice a week, per the new ACSM resistance training guidelines.

Cardio can also help with weight loss—the CDC recommends 150 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic activity (such as brisk walking) per week, or 75 minutes of vigorous-intensity activity (like running) each week. However, this number can vary based on someone’s individual health, and the intensity of the cardio. "Consistency is key. If you can establish and stick to a cardio schedule, you have a better chance at losing weight and keeping it off," Johnston says. Playing sports, working out with friends, or listening to a podcast during cardio can help boost your motivation too.

Also, if you enjoy high-impact exercises and want to lose weight fast, HIIT workouts can be a great way to expend a lot of energy in a short period of time. "You can ramp up the intensity by trying to get in more reps during a certain amount of time or using heavier weights while maintaining the same time goal,” says Blease. “Both will increase your intensity and continue to challenge your body."

If the gym or workout classes aren’t really your speed, choose a form of movement that you love so you can stay consistent with it. “Hiking, swimming, skiing, surfing, or biking are all forms of movement that might feel more sustainable,” says Blease.

"Start smaller than you might think," Blease says. "Often, people overcommit because they are excited. However, it's then easy to get burnt out." After a few weeks, add in a second day, and when that feels seamless, try a third, and over time, you'll find a cadence that works for you, she adds.

Just make sure you always have at least one rest day. "People can get so fixated on weight loss that they end up [overdoing exercise and] hurting themselves," Blease says. "Take time to stretch, rest, and recover as needed."

"Having a trainer create a program that is specific to your needs ensures that you are more likely to get the results you are looking for—and keep your body safe in the process," says Blease. They can help you lose fat while building muscle, which can better your overall metabolic health and insulin sensitivity, and regulate blood sugar to support a healthier body recomposition.

A trainer will also make sure you maintain proper form and that you stay consistent. Decide on a number of days per week that fits your goal and budget, and ask your trainer to help hold you accountable, Blease says.

Research suggests that working with a personal trainer may have more profound effects on weight loss: A 2024 study in Heliyon had 66 adults train individually, with a partner, or a personal trainer over 12 weeks, and they monitored their improvements in bench presses, squats, skeletal muscle mass, and fat mass. The group that worked with a personal trainer improved their leg training and reduced fat mass compared to the other groups.

Protein can help you stay full throughout the day, stabilize your blood sugar levels, and synthesize muscle mass, Smith says, helping with weight loss. While individual dietary needs vary, you can generally aim for 30 to 35 grams of protein per meal to help you stay full throughout the day. Some good sources include eggs, poultry, seafood, red meat, beans, peas, lentils, legumes, nuts, seeds, and soy, per the current Dietary Guidelines for Americans.

Additionally, "fiber is more slowly digested and helps keep you feeling full for a longer period,” Smith adds, recommending vegetables. Increased vegetable intake is also linked to lower blood sugar levels, lower triglycerides, and can help promote liver function. If you don't love veggies, you can also puree them and add them to a sauce or weight-loss-friendly smoothie.

Lastly, don’t forget about healthy (unsaturated) fats—which can help provide energy, per the Mayo Clinic. Try avocados, extra-virgin olive oil, nuts, seeds, soybeans, and seafood.

"Drinking enough water can help you to feel full and prevent overeating," says dietitian Jordan Hill, RD. Aim to drink about half your body weight in ounces of hydrating fluids per day.

Also, avoid sugary beverages. "Juices, sodas, and fancy coffees are often the calorie-equivalent of a meal, yet they will still leave you hungry," says Smith. "The sugar content is often more than a candy bar, which will cause blood sugar levels to rise." Instead, replace your beverages with fruit-infused water, herbal tea, or coffee with cinnamon or zero-calorie sweeteners.

Avoid foods that are high in added sugars, unhealthy fats, and empty calories, which can contribute to weight gain, says Hill: "Instead, focus on eating whole, nutrient-dense foods that will provide your body with the nutrients it needs."

Listening to your internal cues of hunger and fullness can help you control portion sizes, which can also aid weight loss. "Honoring fullness can help prevent overeating and ensure that you're not consuming more calories than your body needs," says Hill. Keeping a food journal can also help you become more mindful of what you're eating, when, and how you're feeling throughout the process of losing weight.

“When you skip meals or go too long in between meals or snacks, you get overly hungry. This makes it much more difficult to make healthy choices, and it also makes it harder to not overeat later," says Smith. Skipping meals can also result in a slower metabolism and leave you feeling fatigued, which can negatively impact your workouts both in effort and recovery.

If you struggle to eat consistent meals, Smith recommends munching on small snacks throughout the day. “Set yourself up for success by bringing healthy snacks with you when you’re away from home, so you’re not caught in a situation where you are really hungry but have no access to healthy food,” she says.

If you need extra assistance, a dietitian can analyze your diet and recommend personalized tips for how you can eat to lose weight, which may be more helpful than doing it on your own. A 2025 review of articles on weight-loss interventions provided by a dietitian for overweight and obese adults in the Journal of the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics looked at how working with a dietitian improved their health markers, as opposed to not working with one (the control group). The group that worked with a dietitian had improved weight loss, waist circumference, blood pressure, and quality of life compared to the control group.

When you encounter a setback (like when you skip a few days of workouts), it can be discouraging and all-consuming, and can cause you to fall off track. "One of the most crucial components of successfully reaching a goal is to not let setbacks ruin our progress,” says Johnston. “The setback is often not as severe as we think, and will not impact our end goal very much at all.”

Just take a breath and realize we’re all human and mistakes are normal; then move forward with your health plan in mind.

And don’t forget to be kind to yourself. Practicing self-compassion and returning to your plan will help reinforce your goal and remind you that this weight-loss journey requires patience and perseverance. "Practice giving yourself grace when [you] slip up,” Johnston says. “As long as it doesn’t become a habit, you will continue moving forward towards your goal.”

Perhaps it was apt, then, that on the night of his record-breaking 126th England cap, Kane scored his 92nd England goal from the penalty spot to settle this 2-1 win over Croatia and book a spot in the Nations League quarter-finals. It was a little display of his work ethic, a simple kick of the ball honed to a fine point through thousands of repetitions on the training ground.

Kane dinked his penalty down the middle and a diving Dominik Livakovic almost blocked the ball with his trail leg. The goalkeeper beat the ground in frustration as if to say, I knew you’d do that. But perhaps this is the point: since that World Cup miss against France, Kane has trained every penalty into his armoury – the stutter or the run, the blast or the dink, left or right or the space a goalkeeper leaves behind – and Livakovic couldn’t cover them all.

It came after a rare miss for Bayern Munich last month. “The next one is always important to bounce back,” Kane said. “I did my usual routine yesterday in training and looked at the goalkeeper. I haven’t gone down the middle with a penalty for maybe three or four years. With penalties, you have to keep things fresh, keep things moving. I thought it was a good time to do it, looking at his clips. Thankfully, it paid off today.”

England are better than Croatia, but then we saw that at the World Cup when England beat them 4-2, and more emphatically in Rijeka last month when England won 7-0. The England fans who shivered through these 90 minutes might instead remember the few moments beforehand. The commemorations began with a giant Tifo behind one goal displaying England’s captain roaring into the night sky with the words “England’s finest” and “Kane 126” emblazoned top and bottom. Kane emerged from the tunnel with his four children as mascots, and then spent a couple of minutes waving around the Ballon d’Or before being presented with three gifts, like a scene from the nativity. The three wise men were played by Peter Shilton (gift one: a golden crest), Croatia captain Luka Modric (gift two: an old football), and Prince William (gift three: a framed shirt).

"It's been a crazy few weeks,” Kane said. “It takes a lot out of you emotionally, physically and mentally ... it’s a hard balance because you want to take it in. Breaking the record is a massive thing in the history of our game, seeing the tifo there, having my kids with me, and being presented by all the people was beyond dreams, so of course you have got to take it in. Once the whistle goes and you’re on the pitch, you just start focusing on what your job is.”

Whether the Nations League counts as “major” is debatable – Cristiano Ronaldo might say yes but just about everyone else says no – yet it presents an opportunity to end England’s 60-year wait for a men’s trophy. It would be fitting if Kane, now England’s greatest player by any measure, was the man to lift it.

At the heart of his story is conflict, as he has to make choices in life and deal with his family.

That key part with snow had yet to be shot. The filmmakers were hoping it would snow in Madrid in December. It didn’t. Gutiérrez says, “We went into January thinking it would snow.” Again, it didn’t.

The clock was ticking on whether they would meet the deadline to finish the film and submit it to Cannes.

It still hadn’t snowed in Madrid. But it did snow in Granada, the southern region of the country. They decided they would go there to shoot it. “But it was February and we had one month before the Cannes deadline,” Gutiérrez says.

Gutiérrez explains, “We couldn’t do screenings of the movie because we couldn’t get people to see two hours of a movie and then tell them to watch the ending in theaters.”

The team had one month to work together to pull it off. “We said, ‘We have to trust each other and our instincts because if we start listening to everybody’s opinion on the film, we won’t make it to Cannes.’”

They worked around the clock in that last month to finish the film.

One person they did trust was Penélope Cruz. In the film, she plays Nené Romero, a cabaret singer, and they brought her into the equation.

But Gutiérrez felt immense pressure. “I had this huge responsibility because the script was so good, and I had to be as good as the rest of the film,” he recalls, crying in the next room as Cruz watched. “When the film stopped, someone knocked at my door. “We had two weeks to make changes, and I remember even then we were not sure about it,” he says.

Gutiérrez remembers the film premiering at the Lumiere Theater, with Tilda Swinton seated in front of him. He laughs, “I was watching the movie through the eyes of Tilda Swinton for two and a half hours. And I thought, ‘OK, this is the movie. This is what we’ve made.’”

After the film ended, it received a 16-minute standing ovation and the Best Director Award.

“We decided there that this was the final edit of the movie,” Gutiérrez says. “I think it’s good because maybe if we had continued editing, we wouldn’t have this special movie that we have now.”

Destiny brought Mauricio Pochettino to Man United - but one huge question remains

The Argentine has come close to becoming Manchester United manager on three separate prior occasions. Now the stars appear to have finally aligned, but is it too late for either the man or the club?

Want to bookmark your favourite articles and stories to read or reference later? Start your Independent Membership today.

On Sunday evening, Mauricio Pochettino got the call he had been waiting for.

He and long-time assistant Jesus Perez had frequently asked about Manchester United over the last decade. They would often contact friends in the game who were connected to Old Trafford, looking to find out what was happening, whether there could be an opening.

Pochettino came very close to taking one of those openings on at least three occasions: 2018, 2021 and 2024. Now, elements might finally have aligned.

It is a call that others at Old Trafford have long waited for, too. Back when they had a much publicised dinner in 2016, Sir Alex Ferguson essentially told Pochettino he should be the United manager. The Argentine was enthralled at that meeting by stories from one of the game’s great glory eras, and a sense of magic he had felt just standing in Old Trafford had been deepened.

Pochettino had been especially taken by Ferguson’s account of rebuilding United from the 1980s, initially drawing comparisons with what he was doing at Tottenham Hotspur. Now, he’s set to take on that exact same job, if one that has become so much bigger than in 2016. It is arguably even bigger than in 1986.

Pochettino, however, is fired by the idea of being the man to finally fix United. It has become one of the great managerial quests. The United hierarchy have ultimately gone for him because of that personality, and his standing. They don’t want a coach who hasn’t worked at this level. They want someone ready. Both the Glazers and Sir Jim Ratcliffe can sense that, since the two sides of the United ownership have known Pochettino for years.

If this is an appointment that always felt destined, though, that does raise one big question. Is this the right moment for it? Is it like Jose Mourinho, in that it came too late? Is it typical of modern United in that sense? Is it the same old “magical thinking” of hoping a famous name saves them, when the game has changed and the actual problems are now so structural?

Pochettino probably should have gone to United in 2016 or 2018, when he was at a peak in terms of being one of the most exciting coaches in football. The club did consider a move in the summer of 2016, but sources say an exit clause was allowed to expire. How different things could have been.

Old Trafford insiders are now talking about the value of “a name”, but the hierarchy did pass on Pochettino in 2024, when they initially considered sacking Erik ten Hag before ultimately deciding to stick with the Dutchman. His interview wasn’t seen as convincing as that of Thomas Tuchel or Roberto De Zerbi. Those who know him also say he is closer to being “a manager” now, in terms of a wider outlook, rather than the on-the-grass coach that so drove Spurs.

The very fact United are negotiating to pry him from the United States national team, without wishing to be disrespectful, also illustrates how his stock has recently dropped. Some close to Pochettino would nevertheless put that down to necessary choices at certain moments. Taking charge of the hosts of the biggest World Cup ever, that had so much pomp around it, was too enticing. Before that, he probably should have pushed to go to United or Real Madrid in 2018 but he was convinced he could do something special with Spurs. He’d put too much into it.

And that does point to one of the other great twists of fate in modern football, where Pochettino’s profile could be completely different. His Tottenham team were genuinely one of the best in Europe over 2016-18, and one of the great Premier League teams. To achieve 86 points in 2016-17, on what was then only the sixth highest wage bill in English football, was genuine alchemy. It was immense overperformance, fostered by a side that could just overrun you.

We also now know that the wage bill of the eventual champions for that season, Chelsea, had in part come from signings that had been secured by payments against the rules. The punishment from that guilty verdict still looks weak. There is a strong argument that Chelsea should have been stripped of the 2015 and 2017 titles. Pochettino himself said as much in a wide-ranging interview with The Independent and other media in September: “I’ve said that, that we should win one title, one Premier League. We should have been awarded it… for sure, we deserve to be champions for that year.”

Had that justice been done, Pochettino would not just be a “name” who created an exciting Spurs once. He would be an English champion, and one responsible for one of the most historic titles given the much wealthier competition. A great institution would have won just their third title, and first in 56 years.

Both he and Spurs could have had their futures transformed, as well as their place in history. It would have been a modern equivalent of what Ferguson himself did with Aberdeen.

Now, he wants to follow Ferguson in a different way. This is the job he has been waiting for.

A certain apathy towards the US job was displayed in that September interview when he spoke about how his contract didn’t necessarily mean he’d stay the full term.

For all of that, and how their World Cup campaign ended in the chaos of Donald Trump’s disruptive interference in the Falorin Balogun case, there were very impressive spells when the US really looked like a classic Pochettino team. The way they ripped into Paraguay was reminiscent of Spurs 2016-18, right up to some of the flourishes. And that was in a much more limited and restricted environment than a club team.

This is the other thing about just taking the job, too. Regardless of United’s current problems, and perceptions of where Pochettino is in his career, he is energised by this. He has that bite. As one figure who knows him well says, “he has got the IP, he has got the brand, he has got the experience”.

Having already worked for Chelsea and Paris Saint-Germain, he’s been at big clubs with demanding owners. He has the mettle for that. And he now looks set to have the big job he’s always wanted.

The FIFA under-17 World Cup kicks off in a week's time in Qatar, and it's now been revealed that the US will not be represented at the tournament after withdrawing its side due to security concerns, according to US Soccer Collective.

FIFA announced in 2024 that the tournament would take place annually rather than every two years, with the first five iterations of the new format set to be held in Qatar. The Middle Eastern nation has risen to prominence as a host country for football tournaments since holding the 2022 Men's World Cup.

While FIFA may benefit from working with the wealthy Qatari government, the decision to hold the next five U17 World Cups in the Arab nation appears to have come at the expense of the U17 USMNT.

Ongoing conflicts in the Middle East and political tensions between the US and the region have prompted the Stars and Stripes to withdraw.

One of football's showpiece youth tournaments, which has unearthed a number of talented players over the years, commences next Thursday, but it'll now be without one of the biggest nations on the planet.

The US, who had been drawn in Group J alongside Montenegro, Chile and Algeria, will not longer participate in the competition in the Middle East.

The current travel advisory for Qatar from the US Department of State is at a Level 3, with guidance urging people to reconsider travel to the country due to the risk of armed conflict.

"We are writing to inform you of the difficult decision to cancel our World Cup pre-camp and our planned participation in the World Cup," US Soccer's statement to their under-17 players read.

“We are extremely proud of the preparation by our players and coaches to represent the US in the World Cup. Unfortunately, in light of current circumstances including the US State Department’s Level 3 Travel Advisory for Qatar and related directives for Embassy staff and families, we are no longer able to participate in the tournament. The well-being of our players and staff will always be our top priority.”

The letter sent by US Soccer to the players on Wednesday was obtained by the Athletic.

FIFA reportedly have the ability to replace the North American country in the tournament, although a direct replacement is yet to be formally announced. Given that sourcing a replacement would require a team convening at incredibly short notice, it's possible FIFA keep Group J at just three teams.

With Pochettino heading to Man Utd, the US are not messing around.

The US were supposed to be one of the more exciting teams heading to the tournament, with the likes of Malik Jakupovic (Philadelphia Union), Robert Turdean (Chicago Fire) and Vinny Garcia (LA Galaxy) among the assortment of rising teen talents that coach Alex Aldaz was set to have at his disposal.

Joe Hart pinpoints ‘unbelievable’ Alisson Becker trait which makes him ‘one of the best’

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Joe Hart has proclaimed that Alisson Becker will ‘forever be regarded as one of the best’ goalkeepers to have graced the Premier League.

Liverpool’s number 1 has been rightly acknowledged as one of the foremost players in his position in world football since his move to Anfield in 2018 and has been pivotal towards the trophies won by the Reds in that time.

He produced one of his greatest LFC performances in the victory at Crystal Palace last weekend, with his tally of six saves including a world-class reaction stop from Daichi Kamada which left the Eagles attacker astounded.

In an interview with The Athletic, Hart lavished praise on Alisson for the consistency he’s shown throughout his time in England, and especially his prowess in one-on-saves.

The ex-Manchester City stopper said of the 34-year-old: “One of the best to do it in the Premier League. What I love is his simplicity. He’s not necessarily eye-catching in the way he moves, but it’s the way he deals with everything.

“He makes massive saves. His one-on-one technique is unbelievable, and he has made it his own. He very rarely gets beaten one-on-one, which in this Premier League is incredible because you’re up against some of the best strikers in the world.

“He’s struggled a bit with injuries lately, but I think he’ll forever be regarded as one of the best to play the position.”

Last weekend, Alisson produced a perfect example of the one-on-one ability that Hart pinpointed by thwarting Yeremy Pino when the Crystal Palace forward was through on goal, a significant intervention at a time that Liverpool were 2-0 up.

When it comes to ‘massive saves’, it mightn’t quite be in the same category as his legendary stoppage-time heroics against Napoli in December 2018 when our Champions League campaign was in the balance, but it showed that the 34-year-old remains a world-class goalkeeper.

His outstanding display at Selhurst Park came after a couple of costly misjudgements in defeats to Arsenal and Fenerbahce, which shows the Brazilian’s strength of character to put those unfortunate moments behind him and come up trumps for his team.

Alisson’s agent Ze Maria revealed in recent days that talks have begun over a prospective contract renewal at Liverpool, with the Reds’ number 1 having said after the win at Palace that he’d like to stay at Anfield beyond the expiry of his current deal next summer.

More heroics like those he produced in south London four days ago, and an extension to his existing terms should be a no-brainer for sporting director Julian Ward.
