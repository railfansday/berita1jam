Longtime Rutgers football player 'elated' Greg Schiano returning in 2027

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

PISCATAWAY – In the transfer portal era where player movement is at an all-time high, it's rare for a player to stick with one program their entire college career.

Rutgers football defensive lineman DJ Allen is one of those exceptions.

The 6-foot-7, 275-pound Allen joined the program as part of the 2022 recruiting class, meaning he was one of the earliest recruits that coach Greg Schiano brought into the program.

For that reason, Schiano means a lot to Allen, who had an offer list that included Michigan, Iowa, Michigan State, Nebraska and others.

Allen, an Indiana native, has been with the Scarlet Knights for now five seasons, seeing the program go through plenty of ups and downs, including this season's 2-7 output through the first nine games – Rutgers plays Nebraska on Nov. 14 at SHI Stadium.

But Allen was glad to hear he'll be able to continue to play for Schiano next season, which became official on Nov. 10 when athletic director Keli Zinn announced Schiano would return in 2027.

"We're super exited," Allen said following practice on Wednesday, Nov. 11. "I've been here for five years, and the reason why I'm here is because of Coach Schiano. Hearing that news I was elated. I'm excited to see what we're capable of doing."

Allen said he's confident in the program's direction under his head coach.

"I'm excited because of what he's built here," Allen said. "You look at his track record, he's done great things here. I have full confidence and belief in Coach Schiano, what he's bringing to us and what we're going to continue to do."

It's been a rough season for Rutgers in many ways, and the team now has three games remaining with no chance of securing the six-win mark for traditional bowl eligibility.

As disappointing as it's been, Allen echoed a message Schiano has conveyed several times this season – the team is only focusing on trying to win now, not on what's already happened.

"It's frustrating, but I think the biggest thing is we're focused on this week and what we can do now," Allen said. "We can't focus on the past. Being prepared for Nebraska is our biggest task right now."

Cortaca Jug rivalry returns to Yankee Stadium. What to know

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

For the second time in four years, one of college football's biggest rivalries will be played at one of pro sports' most recognizable venues.

Unbeaten SUNY Cortland will face Route 13 rival Ithaca College in the annual Cortaca Jug game at 1 p.m. Saturday, Nov. 14 at Yankee Stadium in the Bronx.

Cortland, three years removed from a national championship, brings a 9-0 record into the contest and is ranked ninth in Division III by D3football.com and 10th by the American Football Coaches Association. The Red Dragons finished with a 7-0 record in Empire 8 play with last week's 42-24 victory over SUNY Brockport, securing an automatic berth into the NCAA Division III Tournament while extending their conference winning streak to 40 games.

Ithaca is 7-2 and comes off a 14-10 loss to Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute in a game that decided the Liberty League title. The Bombers' seven-game winning streak came to a close and they ended up 6-1 in league play.

This regular-season finale will be the third meeting between the teams in the New York City area. Ithaca beat Cortland, 32-20, in front of an announced Division III record crowd of 45,161 fans at MetLife Stadium in East Rutherford, New Jersey in 2019. In 2022, Ithaca beat Cortland, 34-17, in front of 40,232 fans at Yankee Stadium.

"Cortland and Ithaca both help set the standard in Division III athletics, and our Cortaca Jug rivalry demonstrates that shared excellence," Mike Urtz, Cortland's athletic director, said in a press release when it was announced the rivalry would return to Yankee Stadium.

"It's been called the 'biggest little game in the nation' for a reason, and it's fitting to be played in one of the most iconic sports venues in the entire world."

The Ithaca-Cortland rivalry dates back to 1930, when Cortland topped Ithaca by a 12-0 score. Ithaca won last year's meeting, 26-21, at home.

This will be the 67th Cortaca Jug game and the 84th all-time meeting between the schools. Ithaca leads the series 44-36-3.

The Cortaca Jug made its debut in 1959, the first Cortaca Jug game. There are now three Cortaca Jugs after the first two ran out of space to chronicle scores.

Nicholas Lang, an all-state pick in football and basketball at Union-Endicott, is the top receiving weapon for the Bombers with 45 catches for 712 yards and seven touchdowns this season. Lang, a senior, caught touchdown passes of 44 and 7 yards in last year's triumph over Cortland. He has 108 yards and a touchdown on the ground this season.

IC quarterback Michael Reed has completed 134 of 222 passes for 15 touchdowns, with four interceptions. He is second on the team with 516 rushing yards on 102 carries, including seven touchdowns.

Chris Scully is the Bombers' top rusher with 516 yards and 12 touchdowns on 102 rushes.

Red Dragons quarterback Mike Rescigno, who starred at Arlington High in New York's Section 1, has rushed 120 times for 902 yards and 10 touchdowns. The junior's passing numbers include 115 completions in 182 attempts, with 16 touchdowns and four interceptions.

Ethan Gallo is second on the team with 587 rushing yards on 118 attempts, including 14 touchdowns.

Four players have at least 300 receiving yards, led by Jacque LaPrarie with 19 catches for 415 yards and six touchdowns.

Aidan Lochner leads the defense with 62 tackles, and Joe Galea has a team-high 5 1/2 sacks.

Along with Lang, other Section 4 high school alumni on Ithaca's roster are defensive back Cooper Hughes from U-E, receiver Jack Gannon from Vestal, and defensive lineman Lucas Boccasini from Owego Free Academy.

Section 4 players on Cortland's roster include receiver Will Brainard from Trumansburg, offensive lineman Talan O'Connor from Groton, tight end Evan English from Owego, and running back Jay Miles from Dryden.

By Oliver Thomas | 12 Nov 2026 09:00

The Singapore National Stadium stages an international friendly fixture between Japan and Brazil on Saturday.

These two nations renew acquaintances just five months after locking horns in the last 32 of the 2026 World Cup, with the South American giants coming from behind to win 2-1 in Houston.

Just over a week after edging past Japan in June, Brazil’s World Cup journey was ended by Norway (2-1) at the last-16 stage, and they have since taken part in three friendly matches between September and October, scoring nine goals in the process.

An experimental Selecao outfit were held to a 1-1 draw by Australia, though a stronger, fired-up side responded by securing a 4-2 comeback victory over the Socceroos in a heated encounter just four days later.

Carlo Ancelotti then celebrated his 12th victory in 20 matches as Brazil head coach with a comfortable 4-0 success against India, courtesy of first-half strikes from Estevao Willian and Pedro, before Samuel Lino netted a second-half brace.

Brazil will conclude 2026 with two more friendlies against Japan and Singapore this month, facing the latter next Tuesday, before Ancelotti turns his attention to 2027's warm-up friendlies and the start of 2030 World Cup qualifying, all while building a fresh generation of talent ahead of the 2028 Copa America.

Ranked sixth in the world by FIFA, Brazil boast an impressive head-to-head record over Saturday’s opponents Japan, winning 12 of their 14 meetings in all competitions (D1 L1). The Selecao have also scored at least twice on 12 of those occasions.

Since their round-of-32 elimination at the World Cup this summer, Japan have put together a four-game unbeaten run in friendly fixtures, including three triumphs over South American opposition.

The Samurai Blue secured back-to-back home victories against Uruguay and Venezuela in September, beating the former 3-1 and the latter 2-1, with Ayase Ueda scoring three goals off the substitutes’ bench across both matches.

Hajime Moriyasu’s men then prevailed 5-4 on penalties against Ecuador following a goalless stalemate at the start of October, before winning 2-1 against New Zealand in their most recent friendly fixture courtesy of goals from Ueda and Ritsu Doan.

Since September 2025, Japan have only been beaten in one of their last 15 international matches across all competitions (W10 D4), and that lone defeat was the aforementioned World Cup loss to Brazil in June when they conceded a 95th-minute goal.

However, this impressive run did include their first and only victory over the five-time world champions in October last year, when the Samurai Blue came from two goals down at half time to secure a memorable 3-2 friendly win – Ueda (again) scored what proved to be the winner.

Japan will follow up their latest battle with Brazil this weekend by rounding off 2026 against Paraguay next Tuesday, playing once again at the Singapore National Stadium just a few hours after the Selecao have faced the hosts.

Japan’s 28-man squad does not include star striker Ueda, winger Kaoru Mitoma or defender Takehiro Tomiyasu as they all remain sidelined with injuries.

Aston Villa goalkeeper Zion Suzuki could continue between the sticks and captain Ko Itakura is likely to start at the heart of the defence, while Daichi Kamada, Ao Tanaka and Kaishu Sano could all link up in midfield.

Koki Ogawa has scored 11 goals in 18 international appearances for Japan and will hope to lead the line this weekend, while Takumi Minamino, Junya Ito, Takefusa Kubo and Daizen Maeda are also contenders to start in the final third.

As for Brazil, Ancelotti has named a youthful 26-man squad that does not include senior stars including Alisson Becker, Marquinhos, Matheus Cunha and Joao Pedro, the latter of whom remains sidelined through injury.

However, Raphinha has been included despite having recently recovered from a muscle injury. Barcelona boss Hansi Flick was left frustrated by the attacker’s “not ideal” selection, but it is understood that his minutes will be managed during the international break.

Raphinha could therefore begin as a substitute this weekend, with Real Madrid duo Endrick and Vinicius Junior linking up in attack alongside either Rayan or Estevao.

Arsenal pair Bruno Guimaraes and Gabriel Magalhaes are both contenders to play in midfield and defence respectively, while Allan, Alisson Santana, Marlon Gomes, Andre, Jonathan Jesus and Lucas Perri are six uncapped players who could make their Brazil debut.

US Soccer Statement After Pulling Team Out of U17 World Cup in Qatar

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

The FIFA under-17 World Cup kicks off in a week's time in Qatar, and it's now been revealed that the US will not be represented at the tournament after withdrawing its side due to security concerns, according to US Soccer Collective.

FIFA announced in 2024 that the tournament would take place annually rather than every two years, with the first five iterations of the new format set to be held in Qatar. The Middle Eastern nation has risen to prominence as a host country for football tournaments since holding the 2022 Men's World Cup.

While FIFA may benefit from working with the wealthy Qatari government, the decision to hold the next five U17 World Cups in the Arab nation appears to have come at the expense of the U17 USMNT.

Ongoing conflicts in the Middle East and political tensions between the US and the region have prompted the Stars and Stripes to withdraw.

One of football's showpiece youth tournaments, which has unearthed a number of talented players over the years, commences next Thursday, but it'll now be without one of the biggest nations on the planet.

The US, who had been drawn in Group J alongside Montenegro, Chile and Algeria, will not longer participate in the competition in the Middle East.

The current travel advisory for Qatar from the US Department of State is at a Level 3, with guidance urging people to reconsider travel to the country due to the risk of armed conflict.

"We are writing to inform you of the difficult decision to cancel our World Cup pre-camp and our planned participation in the World Cup," US Soccer's statement to their under-17 players read.

“We are extremely proud of the preparation by our players and coaches to represent the US in the World Cup. Unfortunately, in light of current circumstances including the US State Department’s Level 3 Travel Advisory for Qatar and related directives for Embassy staff and families, we are no longer able to participate in the tournament. The well-being of our players and staff will always be our top priority.”

The letter sent by US Soccer to the players on Wednesday was obtained by the Athletic.

FIFA reportedly have the ability to replace the North American country in the tournament, although a direct replacement is yet to be formally announced. Given that sourcing a replacement would require a team convening at incredibly short notice, it's possible FIFA keep Group J at just three teams.

With Pochettino heading to Man Utd, the US are not messing around.

The US were supposed to be one of the more exciting teams heading to the tournament, with the likes of Malik Jakupovic (Philadelphia Union), Robert Turdean (Chicago Fire) and Vinny Garcia (LA Galaxy) among the assortment of rising teen talents that coach Alex Aldaz was set to have at his disposal.

Portugal vs. Denmark: Head-to-head record and past meetings ahead of UEFA Nations League clash

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

By Oliver Thomas | 12 Nov 2026 08:51, Last updated: 12 Nov 2026 08:51

Portugal and Denmark will lock horns in the UEFA Nations League on Saturday.

Denmark and Portugal have faced each other a total of 19 times on the international stage across all competitions, including friendlies.

Portugal comfortably lead the overall head-to-head record with 13 victories, while Denmark have only beaten A Selecao das Quinas on four occasions, and there have also been two draws played out between the two nations.

The very first meeting between Denmark and Portugal was a friendly that took place back in June 1966 when Jose Torres scored twice in a 3-1 victory for A Selecao das Quinas in Esbjerg.

Over a 34-year period, Portugal won seven and drew one of their first eight encounters with Denmark, including a thumping 5-0 home victory in Euro 1972 qualifying - their biggest win over the Danes to date.

Like London buses, two Danish victories then came at once, with a 4-2 friendly win on home soil in September 2006 followed two years later by a 3-2 away success in Lisbon in 2010 World Cup qualifying.

Portugal won only one of their five meetings with Denmark over a five-year period up until October 2011 when they suffered a 2-1 home defeat in Euro 2012 qualifying.

A Selecao das Quinas then posted three straight wins over Denmark, before the Danes claimed a slender 1-0 first-leg victory in the 2024-25 UEFA Nations League quarter-finals courtesy of a second-half strike from Rasmus Hojlund.

However, Portugal bounced back just three days later and prevailed 5-2 after extra time in the second leg in Lisbon, with Cristiano Ronaldo and Francisco Trincao (2) among the goalscorers in the thrilling contest.

The last meeting between the two teams came in the 2026-27 Nations League, with Portugal posting a 4-2 win over Denmark.

Saturday's match will be Portugal's first fixture since Cristiano Ronaldo announced his international retirement.

We have to go back to the 1994-95 strike to see the last example of the most drastic moves both the league and MLB Players Association took around the salary cap dispute.

After the league canceled the World Series in September of '94, President Clinton announced in October that he was appointing a mediator in an attempt to resolve the conflict. The efforts went nowhere, with each side moving incrementally but coming nowhere near an agreement. By the end of 1994, the league declared an impasse in negotiations and declared that they were implementing a cap system against the players' will. The union immediately filed an unfair labor practice charge with the National Labor Relations Board (NLRB). Frustrated by the lack of progress through mediation, Clinton ordered the sides to the White House and declared that if the sides didn't reach a deal by February 7, 1995, he would take the recommendations of mediator William J. Usery, Jr. on a settlement. The problem was, Clinton's pressure had no teeth as any recommendation would not be binding.

Ultimately, on March 26, 1995, the NLRB voted 3-2 to seek a court injunction forcing the owners to reinstate the provisions of the old collective bargaining agreement. That ultimately landed the case in a Manhattan courtroom in front of U.S. District Court Judge Sonia Sotomayor (yes, the same Sonia Sotomayor who now sits on the Supreme Court of the United States). On March 31, 1995, Sotomayor granted the NLRB's request for the preliminary injunction against the owners, saying, "When a contract ends, the parties must not alter mandatory subjects until a new agreement is reached or a good-faith impasse is reached."

Fast forward to a potential scenario in 2027 with regular-season games lost, the NLRB and the president could, once again, play a role. Trump has already commented on the matter.

"If you don't have a salary cap, you don't have a sport, because they can't help themselves," Trump told reporters on June 5 aboard Air Force One. "Football has a salary cap. They should have done it a long time ago.

"It's shocking, frankly, that they didn't put a cap on many years ago," Trump added, seemingly referring to the MLB strike in 1994 and 1995. "They had a chance to do a cap, but they blew it."

Trump has not been shy about using the bully pulpit in the past and could do so again, applying pressure on the NLRB if the union claims an unfair labor practice against the league. And in Trump's second term, he's aggressively reshaped the NLRB, making it more employer-focused through board filings and appointments.

Remember, the ruling in 1995 by the NLRB board was as a razor thin 3-2 ruling in favor of the players. Now, a ruling for the owners could be more favorable.

Back to the 1994-95 strike, Congress was a passive player. At the time, House Speaker Newt Gingrich stated, "I'm not sure Congress is the right place to try to organize the national pastime." Senate Majority Leader Robert Dole added, "We're very, very reluctant to have Congress get involved."

Note that at the time, both the House and Senate were controlled by the Republicans. By next year, Democrats will have a sizable majority in both as the midterm blue wave will dramatically alter Congress's dynamic.

It's unclear how much stomach the new coalition has for inserting itself into MLB's labor battle, but it is decidedly more union-friendly than it has been in the past.

As a means of applying pressure, one or more bills could be introduced to remove MLB's antitrust exemption, something the league has continually fought to maintain.

Threats of repealing MLB's antitrust exemption have come from both sides of the aisle in the past. In April 2021, several Republicans signed on to the Competition in Professional Baseball Act in an effort to strip MLB of its antitrust exemption. U.S. Senator Cory Booker (D-NJ) also signed on via amicus brief, saying, "For over a century, professional baseball has enjoyed a free pass to break the rules of fair competition because of a flawed Supreme Court ruling—one that the Court itself has acknowledged was a mistake," said Senator Booker. "The one-of-a-kind antitrust exemption gives leagues the power to artificially control salaries, ticket prices, team relocations, and more, harming players and baseball fans alike. Snow, ice, and short days didn’t stop Michelle Baxter from regular runs outdoors.

When winter arrives, it’s tempting to put your running goals on hold until the temperatures warm up, the days get longer, and the roads clear. Cold temperatures make it harder to leave a warm bed, while snow and ice can turn a familiar route into a much slower—and more slippery—run.

For months at a time, Michelle Baxter’s running routes in Anchorage, Alaska, were covered in packed snow and ice. After a snowstorm, plows could take days to reach every neighborhood, and by then, cars had compressed the fallen snow onto the roads. Even after the plows came through, the slick surface often remained.

Baxter—now an RRCA- and V.02-certified run coach—spent 15 years living in Alaska and training through winters like these, figuring out how to keep running through harsh, unmotivating conditions.

She trained for 12 marathons in that time, continuing to build fitness year after year. Eventually, she even achieved her most ambitious running goal: breaking three hours in the marathon. After eight years of attempting to break the barrier—and a heartbreakingly close 3:01:14 at Humpy’s Marathon in Anchorage in August 2021—Baxter finally ran a 2:58:24 at the California International Marathon in December 2021. She has since lowered her PR to 2:56:57 at the Indianapolis Monumental Marathon.

Your winter running routes may not be covered in snow and ice for six months straight, but chances are you’ll face your own challenges as the temperature drops in the coming weeks and months. The good news: Baxter’s experience can help you navigate tough terrain and make the most of your training.

Here’s how you can put her lessons to work this winter so when the ice thaws, you’re ready to run your fastest race yet.

What you wear can make or break a winter run, Baxter says. When you’re too cold, too warm, or worried about taking a spill around every tight corner, it’s hard to focus on any other aspect of the run. Baxter spent years figuring out what works, and her gear advice starts with two essentials: finding reliable footwear and appropriate layers for the conditions.

In Anchorage, finding reliable footing was Baxter’s biggest winter challenge. She initially resisted buying studded shoes, assuming they wouldn’t make much difference. But once she tried them, she was surprised by how much more confident she felt in every step.

For packed snow, Baxter often wore regular trainers fitted with screws in the outsoles, which she added herself.

On sheer ice, Baxter preferred shoes made with built-in carbide studs; she swears by the Icebug brand. “You can wear these shoes and make a 90-degree turn and feel so confident running on ice—like sheer ice,” Baxter says. “They were a game-changer for me.”

You may not encounter sheets of ice on your regular running routes, but consider the conditions you encounter most often and look for an option suited to them.

Now living in Minnesota, Baxter says she reaches for studded shoes if she expects at least half of a run to cover potentially snowy or icy ground. She notes that while the shoes may be slightly less comfortable than your normal trainers, protecting your body from harm is the number-one priority during the winter months of training.

“I just think it’s worth it to feel confident running on the slippery surfaces and not risk injuring yourself,” she says.

One of the most common winter gear mistakes Baxter sees runners make is overdressing. It’s easy to look out the window, see cold weather, and pull on every layer you own in an effort to stay warm on the run. But remember you’ll warm up once you start moving.

Baxter’s rule of thumb echoes the advice of other run coaches: Start your run feeling a little chilly. After about 10 minutes, you should warm up and feel comfortable for the rest of the run.

Don’t forget to consider the wind, too; Baxter generally recommends dressing for the wind chill temperature. She recalls learning this lesson during one particularly windy Alaska run when she dressed for the ambient temperature rather than the wind chill temperature and ended up feeling sick from potential hypothermia.

If you’re unsure where to start when it comes to gearing up for a winter run, use our temperature-based layering guide, then pay attention to how you feel after each run. Were you still cold after warming up? Did you get sweaty and uncomfortable? Use your responses to adjust your layers on your next run.

For Baxter, keeping her legs warm as temperatures approached subzero was a recurring problem. She first turned to wearing multiple pairs of tights, which helped with the cold, but the layers restricted her movement. Eventually, she found a thick, fleece-lined pair of running tights that solved the problem, recommending them to other runners for temperatures ranging from 15 all the way down to minus 10 degrees Fahrenheit.

She made a similar change on top: Instead of wearing a base layer, fleece layer, and wind layer, she found a high-quality warm jacket to wear over a base layer.

Your biggest frustration might be cold hands or wet feet. For these issues, Baxter suggests putting hand warmers in your gloves, running-specific wool socks, and a waterproof shoe.

For visibility during the winter months, Baxter wears a lighted vest. She began treating it as a non-negotiable in her running wardrobe after noticing, as a driver, how much easier it was to see runners wearing blinking lights rather than simple reflective clothing items.

“Investing in a few quality pieces [of winter gear], I think you’re just going to enjoy your run more,” Baxter says. “You’re going to be safer and more comfortable.”

Even with the right layers, leaving your warm house and getting out the door often proves difficult when it’s cold and dark.

Baxter understands the struggle better than most. Anchorage gets less than six hours of daylight during the darkest winter months, and the city’s winter high temps hover around 25 degrees Fahrenheit.

So, how did she keep getting out there? “Running just became a habit of mine,” she says. “It’s how I start my day. I get up, I go for a run.” Even during peak winter in Alaska, Baxter often ran seven days a week simply because it was a part of her daily routine.

Baxter says each individual needs to find what helps them keep going, whether that’s a future goal, a training partner to run alongside, or a plan that lets you check off workouts.

For Baxter, her motivation and discipline to make running a habit often came from looking ahead.

“I just know how important those base miles are during the winter,” she says. “So I know I need to keep putting in the work, keep doing some workouts, because it’s only going to pay off down the road.”

If you have a spring marathon, a summer 10K, or another goal in mind, remind yourself how your winter routine fits into that bigger picture. You can also adjust your expectations for the season: Baxter says it’s okay to scale back during winter if that’s what works for you. A simple maintenance routine can help you arrive at your next training cycle ready to build.

The point is, doing something is better than doing nothing.

A goal months away isn’t always enough to get you out the door on a cold morning. Baxter suggests finding something closer to look forward to, too.

Consider signing up for a winter race, even if it isn’t one of your main events for the year. A Turkey Trot, holiday run, or another low-pressure race with friends can provide a checkpoint and make winter training more fun.

Soon after moving to Anchorage, Baxter joined a training group that held workouts at The Dome, an indoor athletic facility featuring an indoor track. The group helped her settle into the running community while giving her a reason to show up for a workout each week.

Once she developed those friendships, other runners expected her to show up, and those weekly track workouts were no longer optional.

If you’re looking for a similar boost, check for a local run club or training group. A standing workout appointment can give your week structure, while supportive company can make a difficult session feel less daunting.

Baxter generally followed the workouts on her schedule, but she changed where she ran to make them possible. The day after a substantial snowfall, she would head to the treadmill rather than plodding through fresh snow, for example. She also took many of her harder workouts to the treadmill or indoor track, where she felt more comfortable running fast.

Her easy and long runs, meanwhile, almost always happened outside.

You can take the same approach by considering the purpose of the day’s run. If you have a faster workout planned and the outdoors only offers slippery conditions, an indoor option may help you complete it with more confidence. If you’re running easy outside, give yourself room to slow down when conditions make your usual pace unrealistic.

Most of all, remember that one winter is part of a much longer running journey. Baxter’s progress—and eventual sub-three breakthrough—came from conquering multiple training cycles and years of running. The work you do this winter can be another step in that process.

“You simply can’t deny the power of consistently stacking marathon block upon marathon block or just years and years and years of running,” Baxter says. “Endurance running is a long game.”

From the academy coaches at Arsenal who released him aged eight, to the Tottenham coaches who didn’t think he could handle first-team football, to the fans who labelled him a one-season wonder and the pundits who prematurely called time on his England career, each served to motivate Kane. You might think he would finally feel satisfied, vindicated, ready to drop his guard, to take his foot off the pedal, to eat a Twix or two.

Far from it. Kane is speaking to a huddle of journalists at Tottenham’s training ground, back where his career began, and insists he still has a point to prove.

“I don’t think that will ever leave me,” he says. “It’s probably part of what makes me push myself to the limits because in football, you’ve never completed it. No matter what you’ve achieved, there is always a trophy that someone else has won or a goal record that someone else has. There’s always something. Whether it’s noise from the outside, media or the fans or whatever it may be, it just helps motivate me to realise there’s a lot more to achieve.”

Where does that come from? How can the unassuming man who stood on stage at the London Palladium last month, nervously twiddling his buttons and repeatedly sweeping away specks of nothing from his pristine tuxedo, who spent his 12-minute speech naming dozens of people who had helped his career, also be a ferociously driven world-class athlete who carries a kind of burning wrath inside him?

“I think it’s just my mindset, because I’ve had to prove people wrong my whole career. From early academy days proving coaches wrong, proving scouts wrong, it has just been instilled in me. If someone tells me I can’t do something it just motivates me more to do it. Of course, you have to combine the mentality with being able to produce it, so that’s where the self-belief and the training and all the consistent work I do has allowed me to do that.

“I’ve spoken about Tom Brady before and that unwavering mentality to be the best. Maybe with me, because I’m a calm person, you don’t see the ego of some of these great players and I’m a bit more relaxed, but it doesn’t mean that I don’t want it as much as everyone else. It doesn’t mean I don’t put in the work and drive myself and drive everyone around me to be the best.

“Maybe getting released that early and proving them wrong and then maybe proving my early coaches at Tottenham [wrong], just bit by bit, loan moves that didn’t go quite well, again, same thing – ‘I’m going to show them’ – using all the negative stuff as motivation to push me to another level.”

There is something else behind Kane’s drive, a different kind of emotion: the dread of leaving a single stone unturned.

“My biggest fear is finishing my career and thinking I could have done a bit more if I could have just done that or done that. I want to make sure that I cross every bridge so that whenever that day does come, I can say I’ve done everything possible, I have this record, I don’t have this record – but it doesn’t matter, I can sleep well at night.”

Maybe tomorrow will feel a little different, actually breaking the record and being the one that sits alone on top.

“But in this period especially when there are so many games, I don’t think I’ve taken it all in. What’s happened in the last few weeks, I don’t think I’ve accepted it all, I guess. But maybe during the winter break when I’m away somewhere, on the beach, it’ll be a moment to reflect and then really soak it all in.”
