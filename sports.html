Reeling Oregon Ducks remain positive, committed to the grind

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

EUGENE — The 2026 season is on the verge of completely unraveling for the Oregon Ducks. One more loss, and any shred of postseason hopes would be extinguished.

Yet even after two upset losses and falling out of the rankings — despite starting the season at No. 2 — the Ducks (3-2, 1-1 Big Ten) remain focused, together and committed to salvaging the season.

"One thing I really love is the way the people in the locker room have been responding to this adversity," sophomore cornerback Brandon Finney Jr. said.

After his team's 17-15 loss to No. 12 UCLA on Saturday, Oregon coach Dan Lanning said the team would be measured by how it responds moving forward.

On Wednesday, he said the team has elevated its work ethic and embraced accountability.

"I've been proud of the way that they've worked," Lanning said. "I don't see a lot of hanging heads, right? I think that's challenging, too, especially when you're young, to not lose a little bit of gusto and not lose a little bit of belief."

Oregon linebacker Devon Jackson (#9) drops UCLA running back Wayne Knight (#3) for no gain as the No. 13 Ducks face the No. 21 Bruins in a Big Ten college football game at Autzen Stadium in Eugene on Saturday, Oct. 10, 2026. Sean Meagher/The Oregonian

The Ducks, who host Nebraska (5-1, 2-1) at 8 p.m. Saturday, responded well to adversity following a 39-31 loss at Oklahoma State on Sept. 12. Oregon won its next two games, including a 41-27 win at USC on Sept. 26.

That victory seemed to give the Ducks a fresh start. But two weeks later, they lost to the Bruins and dropped out of the rankings.

Now, Oregon must run the table to have a legitimate shot at the College Football Playoff, which seemed like a given for a team that returned most of its starters from last year's semifinal squad.

It's not uncommon for teams with grand expectations to unravel in tough times. So far, that hasn't happened.

"The team is very connected and maintains its composure whether we're winning or losing," punter Bailey Ettridge said. "We haven't let the losses last week and against Oklahoma State bring us down. I think we've handled them well."

Wide receiver Dakorien Moore said the team is filled with players who came to Oregon to win and be great. He added that the team's goals remain attainable, and team leaders have worked to encourage togetherness and mutual trust.

"If you can't trust your teammate, then everything ain't going to form in the way that it's supposed to," he said.

This also means trusting the process, the plan and the team's goal to get better instead of blaming one another.

Oregon head coach Dan Lanning talks to the defense during a timeout as the No. 13 Oregon Ducks face the No. 21 UCLA Bruins in a Big Ten college football game at Autzen Stadium in Eugene on Saturday, Oct. 10, 2026. Sean Meagher/The Oregonian

"Everybody knows what we want to attack this next week, being that Nebraska is a good team," he said.

The outside noise has been deafening. To say that many Oregon fans have melted down on social media would be an understatement.

Virtually all of the players have social media accounts. Moore said he is active on social media but ignores negativity.

Finney said he isn't very active on social media and doesn't care what people say outside the program.

"Why would I let your opinion inflict me or my thoughts on my performance or my thoughts on our performance," he said. "Because people are going to be there when you're doing good, when you're bad; when you're doing bad, they're going to leave you."

For that reason, the team lives by the motto F.E.B.U., which stands for "Forget Everyone But Us".

"F.E.B.U. is a true statement that we all follow," Finney said. "… Just back to work. We got to figure out what we did wrong, go to the doctor, and just get back to work."

Fifth-year linebacker Devon Jackson said before the season that his main goal was team-driven — winning the national title.

"At the end of the year, what happens, happens, but I promise if we focus on the day and focus on one game at a time, we will be okay," Jackson said.

On Saturday, the Ducks' defense played one of its best games of the season, while the offense played its worst without starting quarterback Dante Moore, who remains in concussion protocol.

On teams that lack closeness, such a contrast can cause issues. But that is not the case with Oregon.

"I mean, we're all in this together," Jackson said. "It's not like, the defense won the game and offense lost, or special teams won or lost. We all lost together and we all win together. So, we got each other's back no matter what."

Oregon offensive players celebrate a touchdown run by running back Jordon Davison (#0) as the No. 13 Ducks face the No. 21 UCLA Bruins in a Big Ten college football game at Autzen Stadium in Eugene on Saturday, Oct. 10, 2026. Sean Meagher/The OregonianSean Meagher/The Oregonian

Jackson said that after Saturday's loss, he and senior center Iapani Laloulu talked for 20 minutes about the team. He told Laloulu that he didn't care what was going on with the offense; the defense had its back.

"I wouldn't trade that group with anybody else in the country despite what other people may think," Jackson said. "And we're going to continue to lean on each other because it's we all we got and we all we need."

Realistically, Oregon can't expect much sympathy from outsiders. The Ducks have top-notch facilities, infinite uniform options, a roster with an estimated payroll of $50 million, and a coach who isn't afraid to speak his mind, as Lanning did after beating the Trojans.

Many in the football world certainly wouldn't mind seeing Oregon fall.

"Ain't nobody going to feel sorry for us," linebacker Matayo Uiagalelei said. "So just came back in the next day with the mindset ready to work."

Dame Time is back in Portland — and the Blazers look dangerous

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

A new head coach will roam the sidelines. A franchise icon will make his long-awaited return. An All-Star will begin his redemption tour.

And a new ownership group will watch its first season opener from Paul Allen's former seats along the Moda Center baseline.

The Blazers open the 2026-27 NBA season Wednesday night against the Phoenix Suns — a team they upset in April in the play-in tournament — and if you're just now tuning in for the start of the regular season, well, there's a lot you missed.

The Blazers are expected to unveil a new starting lineup that features a pair of notable All-Star additions: Damian Lillard and Ja Morant. The backcourt duo is joining a fivesome that includes returning starters Deni Avdija, Toumani Camara and Donovan Clingan. It's a versatile, star-studded lineup that features three All-Stars, a former All-Defensive Second Team selection, one of the best young centers in the NBA and loads of scoring potential.

Micah Nori, the Blazers' first-year head coach, said he plans on using a 10-man rotation to start the season, which will allow the Blazers to capitalize on their depth and versatility. In addition to the starters, Jrue Holiday, Scoot Henderson, Sidy Cissoko, Robert Williams III and Vit Krejci are expected to open the season in the rotation. The group could change depending on matchups and Williams' availability — he did not play in back-to-backs last season — and it's likely that Micah Potter, Branden Carlson and Yang Hansen will occasionally see spot duty, as well.

The Blazers stabilized their turbulent coaching situation in June, hiring longtime NBA assistant Nori to replace interim coach Tiago Splitter. Nori, who interviewed for head coaching jobs seven times before finally landing his first chance, is entering his 29th season in the league and is universally revered in NBA circles for his positive approach, ability to connect with players and sharp basketball mind. Early indications have been encouraging, as the Blazers players seem to love him and the front office works well with him. The Blazers' coaching position had been embroiled in controversy, of course, as former coach Chauncey Billups — who was placed on indefinite leave by the NBA — is facing federal felony charges related to money laundering and wire fraud conspiracy for his alleged involvement in a rigged underground poker game.

In a stark contrast to recent seasons, the Blazers are constructed around their offense rather than their defense. The roster is unbalanced, featuring five dynamic ball-handlers and playmakers (Lillard, Morant, Avdija, Holiday and Henderson), a solid center combination (Clingan and Williams) and little wing depth. As a result, the team is expected to play fast and free under Nori, bringing lots of speed, passing, cutting and flow. Nori said he hopes to rotate his players liberally in shorter shifts during games — perhaps as short as three minutes — which will enable them to play with maximum effort before giving way to teammates. The interior defense is strong with Clingan and Williams patrolling the middle, Camara remains one of the NBA's best and most versatile defenders and Cissoko will be asked to guard one-through-five. Holiday is still capable of locking down opposing guards and Avdija has pledged to be more of a two-way threat now that he doesn't have to serve as the team's primary ball-handler. But the backcourt combination of Morant and Lillard, while lethal on offense, is not imposing on defense and relatively small (both stand 6-foot-2).

Portland defied pundits last season, winning 10 of its last 14 regular season games to secure a spot in the play-in tournament, where it beat the Suns in Phoenix to earn the seventh seed in the Western Conference. The San Antonio Spurs dismissed the Blazers with a gentleman's sweep in the playoffs, but the strong run — combined with the additions of Lillard and Morant — has raised expectations in Portland. How much? Camara said in training camp that the Blazers' goal is to win 50 or more games and secure home-court advantage in the postseason. It won't be easy. The league is as deep and talented as ever, and new rules surrounding the lottery should discourage teams from tanking, meaning most of the league will be chasing wins instead of lottery ping-pong balls late into the season. It seemed like a third of the league had given up last year by the All-Star break.

And he has not played with Portland since March 22, 2023, six months before it traded him to the Bucks. Lillard showed no ill effects from his injury during the exhibition season, when he played in three of the Blazers' four games and averaged 17.3 points, 2.7 assists and 22.7 minutes. If anything, he looked like the same old Dame, swishing three-pointers, creating off the dribble, initiating offense and drawing defensive attention. Lillard joked earlier this week that he's "not an old man," but also acknowledged that he isn't likely to play upward of 38 minutes a game like he used to and may even take a night off here and there for rest, most likely on a random back-to-back. His addition not only should help cover up two of the Blazers' biggest weaknesses last season — three-point shooting and shooting in the clutch — but also inspire a thirsty fan base, which has rarely loved a player more. All the while, Lillard says he intends to play with more "joy" than ever.

Oregon has 3 quarterbacks who could start Saturday. Illinois has a freshman who's never started before

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

The Oregon Ducks will face a team on Saturday with an even less desirable quarterback situation.

Illinois (3-3, 1-2 Big Ten) quarterback Katin Houser suffered a broken hand that will sideline him for four to six weeks during a 38-37 overtime win over Michigan State on Oct. 10.

Oregon has played its last two games without starting quarterback Dante Moore, who suffered a concussion in the second quarter of the Ducks' 41-27 win at USC on Sept. 26.

However, while Oregon can turn to junior Dylan Raiola — a Nebraska transfer with 23 career starts — and senior Brock Thomas, Illinois is relying on freshman quarterback Michael Clayton II, who will make his first start against the Ducks.

A positive for Illinois is that Houser was injured before the team's bye week, giving coach Bret Bielema two weeks to prepare Clayton.

"It was just good timing," Bielema told reporters during his Monday media session.

Illinois is off to a disappointing start after going 9-4 in 2025 and 10-3 in 2024. The defense is below average, while the passing combination of Houser and wide receiver Collin Dixon is one of the nation's best. Dixon is tied for third nationally with nine touchdown receptions.

Illinois head coach Bret Bielema celebrates their overtime win over Michigan State as he walks off the field after an NCAA college football game, Saturday, Oct. 10, 2026, in East Lansing, Mich. (AP Photo/Lon Horwedel) AP Photo/Lon HorwedelAP Photo/Lon Horwedel

Now Illinois heads into Saturday's game against a resurgent Oregon defense, trying to get Clayton up to speed.

His performance against MSU elevated expectations.

Houser left the game late in the third quarter with Illinois leading 23-10. Clayton completed 6 of 8 passes for 88 yards and two touchdowns. The final touchdown came in overtime on a 25-yard pass that put Illinois up 38-31.

Sophomore quarterback Carson Boyd also played. He rushed once for seven yards but did not attempt a pass.

Bielema and offensive coordinator Barry Lunney Jr. both said Boyd could play against Oregon (4-2, 2-1), but Clayton is the starter.

Usually, Bielema said, his starting quarterback gets a lot of rest during the bye week while the staff works on player development. Given Clayton and Boyd's inexperience, both got valuable reps in practice last week.

"I think those two guys in particular took advantage of every rep," Bielema said.

Bielema has leaned heavily on transfer quarterbacks during his six years at Illinois, including Houser. But that could change if Clayton, a former three-star recruit, earns the job.

"I've said several times that hopefully the next starter comes from within our program," Bielema said.

Lunney said Clayton will be more prepared than he has in the past given the circumstances.

Michigan State Spartans defensive back Charles Brantley (4) tackles Illinois Fighting Illini wide receiver Collin Dixon (17) in a Big 10 football game at Spartan Stadium in East Lansing, Mich. on Saturday, October 10, 2026.Isaac Ritchey | iritchey@mlive.com

"He knows he's going to play now," Lunney told reporters.

"We feel like we have another guy on campus in that room that can help us win and we're very confident in that," Bielema said.

Oregon coach Dan Lanning said it's difficult to scout Clayton given his limited body of work. Oregon has watched some of his high school game film. But mostly, Oregon will focus on Illinois' system.

"There might be wrinkles, but very rarely do people change the identity of their offense based on a change of the position," Lanning said. "So you prepare and then you make sure you have answers for what you think that skill set is."

If Moore doesn't return this week, Raiola and Thomas will compete for the starting job against Illinois.

Raiola replaced Moore at USC and thrived in the win, but following a bye, struggled for three quarters during a 17-15 loss to UCLA on Oct. 10. Thomas performed better in the fourth quarter and almost pulled out the victory.

Thomas started Saturday's 17-14 home win over Nebraska, but struggled for three quarters before Lanning turned to Raiola. He led the Ducks from a 14-6 deficit to a win with a four-yard touchdown pass to tight end Jamari Johnson with 28 seconds remaining.

Lanning said he's confident in all four quarterbacks, including sophomore Akili Smith Jr., who had a play package ready for Nebraska if needed.

Illinois is most familiar with Raiola, who, as a Nebraska freshman in 2024, passed for 297 yards, three touchdowns and one interception in a 31-24 overtime loss to Illinois.

"Raiola is obviously a very accomplished player," Bielema said. "Kind of almost unusual. You got to almost prepare for possibly three quarterbacks on Saturday. But we'll kind of handle that during the course of the week and see where that goes."

10 takeaways from Oregon’s loss at Illinois and firing of OC Drew Mehringer

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

CHAMPAIGN, Ill. — The Oregon Ducks (4-3, 2-2 Big Ten) suffered their worst loss of the season at Illinois on Saturday.

The 21-10 defeat came against a marginal Illinois team that started a true freshman at quarterback, rushed for 225 yards, and held Oregon to two scoring drives.

As a result, on Sunday, Oregon coach Dan Lanning announced the firing of offensive coordinator Drew Mehringer.

What that means for the rest of the season remains to be seen. Mehringer had to contend with several key personnel issues, including starting quarterback Dante Moore missing three games while in concussion protocol.

Here are 10 takeaways from Saturday's game and the firing of Mehringer:

Criticizing play-calling is an armchair quarterback's delight, but doing so under Oregon's current circumstances is somewhat obtuse.

Few teams excel without their starting quarterback. The next factor is an offensive line that was clearly mismanaged in the offseason after losing three starters to the NFL. Next is inconsistent play from backup quarterbacks Dylan Raiola and Brock Thomas. Then sprinkle in losing top wideout Jeremiah McClellan (broken foot) after three games, starting wide receiver Evan Stewart (undisclosed injury) for the last two games, and two more rotational wide receivers heading into Illinois.

Does all of that excuse Mehringer for Oregon scoring just 42 points over the past three weeks? No. But those are a lot of obstacles for any offensive coordinator to overcome.

Lanning will probably announce his new offensive coordinator during his Monday press conference.

He could promote from within to get through the season and then conduct a national search in the offseason. Or he may have someone in mind to take over now and beyond.

Whatever Lanning decides, the next play-caller had better hope Moore returns, or little will change.

Lanning did well by hiring younger offensive coordinators, Kenny Dillingham and then Will Stein. But maybe it's time to bring in an accomplished, seasoned veteran coach to add a new perspective to a relatively young staff.

Someone with a track record of success as an offensive coordinator and maybe as a head coach.

Someone like former Oregon coach Chip Kelly, Northwestern's offensive coordinator. No, don't expect Kelly to want to return to Oregon.

But someone like him could fit perfectly.

After Saturday's game, Raiola was asked twice how difficult it was to build timing with the receiving crew, which lacked McClellan, Stewart, and freshmen Messiah Hampton and Jalen Lott.

Raiola avoided throwing anyone under the bus but acknowledged what the question implied. Passing games are built on timing. If the quarterback can't fully trust the receivers running the routes, synergy suffers and efficiency wanes.

That's less a knock on the receivers than an acknowledgment that it's easier to develop a connection with a team's more polished top receivers than with those thrust into action by circumstance.

At times, running back Dierre Hill Jr. played wide receiver alongside freshmen Hudson Lewis and Gatlin Bair. Neither had played much until injuries hit the group.

Lanning said Stewart would return. McClellan is out indefinitely.

Much has been made about the failed gadget play in the third quarter. Many have blamed Mehringer for the play-call and the negative result hurting momentum.

Oregon trailed 14-10, forced an Illinois punt and took over at the Ducks' 9-yard line. Five plays later, Oregon moved the ball to its 47.

Oregon lined up with four offensive linemen to the far left, with wide receiver Dakorien Moore behind the linemen. Three Illinois defenders lined up on that side with a fourth closer to the box.

Raiola checked to a run play and handed the ball to Moore on a jet sweep to the right, where the Ducks were outnumbered. The poorly-blocked play resulted in a one-yard loss.

After the game, Lanning was asked whether the failed play hurt the team at that moment.

"Yeah, it's a great question," he said. "It certainly wasn't successful. There's a pause in operation there, right?"

Yes, but who is at fault for the failed play? Is it Raiola for reading it wrong and not passing to Moore? Is it Mehringer for calling the play? Or is it Lanning for allowing the play to happen?

For that play to be called, it must be accepted by Lanning, practiced, reviewed on film and further stamped by Lanning to appear on the play sheet. Then, when Mehringer calls it, Lanning must let it go through. He can hear every play call through the coaches' headsets.

If Lanning didn't like the play, he could have vetoed it. He did not.

The concept is not much different from when Lanning chooses to go for two after touchdowns. If Oregon gets the favorable look it wants, it takes the shot.

That's what happened with the gadget play. But Oregon made the wrong choice and the play failed.

Regardless of the process, had Lanning not liked the play, he could have stopped it from being called.

Oregon gained 180 yards on the ground against a team that ranked 12th in the Big Ten in rushing defense, allowing 134.4 yards per game.

Still, too many drives started well before getting stuffed, putting more pressure on Raiola, who couldn't consistently deliver.

As a result, Oregon converted on just four of 15 third-down attempts.

With most of the team's top receivers out, the rushing attack needed to be special, and it wasn't.

The junior entered the game 9-for-9 on field-goal attempts and went 1-for-4. But two of his three misses came from beyond 50 yards.

Hurych missed his first attempt from 52 yards out. He made a 20-yard field goal in the second quarter to put Oregon up 10-0. He missed a 53-yarder with 6:04 to go in the fourth quarter and later missed a 47-yarder with 52 seconds to go.

"He's human," Lanning said. "The reality is, those are long kicks. Some of those are really tough. The wind's a little bit different in this stadium. I think we put them in a position that wasn't the easiest to operate, and we've been really fortunate this year."

Oregon's defense kept the Ducks in the games against UCLA and Nebraska until the offense started to come alive.

Oregon lost to the Bruins 17-15 and beat Nebraska 17-14.

On Saturday, the Ducks led 10-0 late in the second quarter. At that point, nobody knew Oregon wouldn't score again. But the defense's job was to keep dominating, just in case.

Illinois running back Aidan Laughery got loose for a 75-yard touchdown run, making it 10-7 with 2:15 left in the half. Illinois had rushed for just 14 yards before that run, but finished the game with 225 rushing yards.

That production took pressure off freshman starting quarterback Michael Clayton II, who threw for two second-half touchdowns.

A dominant defense doesn't let that happen against a team it has on the ropes.

The loss was more about the Ducks' offense than the defense. But the defense got sloppy—several defenders made mistakes on Laughery's long run—revealing a lack of four-quarter focus.

"In moments today, they played really well as well," Lanning said of his defense. "But ultimately, they still had 225 yards rushing, or whatever. The final total was there. We can play better than that."

Many fans frustrated with the quarterback play have called for Smith to get a shot. The sophomore former four-star recruit flashed some skills against Portland State and again last spring.

Is he ready to take on a Big Ten team? Only the coaches know for sure. They see him in practice every day.

Even if the Ducks have no shot at the playoffs, Lanning will likely play to win, not to develop. That means he has to believe Smith can win before Smith gets on the field.

However, if Moore does not return, Lanning should at least give Smith some game reps. Lanning said he had a Smith package ready for Nebraska, so we know Lanning has some plays he believes Smith can run effectively.

Truth be told, what do they have to lose? Raiola has been so inconsistent that giving Smith a series or two couldn't hurt much.

Understandably, Oregon left Illinois dejected. It will be interesting to see how the team responds. The loss probably ended the Ducks' playoff chances.

Although a 9-3 team could qualify for the playoffs given the level of national parity, Oregon winning nine games appears unlikely.

With many players returning this season to make a national title run and key players with NFL futures to consider, it's conceivable that many players shut down emotionally for the season.

It's doubtful that many marquee players have any interest in playing in a random bowl game. Those headed to the NFL or the transfer portal wouldn't play in a bowl game anyway.

If the team loses its zeal, the Ducks could fail to win seven games in a full season for the first time since 2016, when Oregon went 4-8.

At the heart of his story is conflict, as he has to make choices in life and deal with his family.

That key part with snow had yet to be shot. The filmmakers were hoping it would snow in Madrid in December. It didn’t. Gutiérrez says, “We went into January thinking it would snow.” Again, it didn’t.

The clock was ticking on whether they would meet the deadline to finish the film and submit it to Cannes.

It still hadn’t snowed in Madrid. But it did snow in Granada, the southern region of the country. They decided they would go there to shoot it. “But it was February and we had one month before the Cannes deadline,” Gutiérrez says.

Gutiérrez explains, “We couldn’t do screenings of the movie because we couldn’t get people to see two hours of a movie and then tell them to watch the ending in theaters.”

The team had one month to work together to pull it off. “We said, ‘We have to trust each other and our instincts because if we start listening to everybody’s opinion on the film, we won’t make it to Cannes.’”

They worked around the clock in that last month to finish the film.

One person they did trust was Penélope Cruz. In the film, she plays Nené Romero, a cabaret singer, and they brought her into the equation.

But Gutiérrez felt immense pressure. “I had this huge responsibility because the script was so good, and I had to be as good as the rest of the film,” he recalls, crying in the next room as Cruz watched. “When the film stopped, someone knocked at my door. After the film ended, it received a 16-minute standing ovation and the Best Director Award.

How President Trump And The Blue Wave Could Factor In MLB’s Labor Battle

In less than a month, Major League Baseball is expected to impose a lockout on players as it pushes to implement a salary cap. If the lockout drags into Spring, political forces could push on either side of the labor battle to break an impasse.

On December 1st, just before midnight, it's expected that the owners will lockout the players. In the offseason, the pressure to reach a new labor deal is limited, and not until spring training is threatened will there be urgency for the sides to reach a deal.

Currently, all management proposals have been tied to a salary cap, a red line for players who have consistently opposed one in Major League Baseball. If the game of chicken pushes into missing regular-season games, politicians on both sides of the aisle are likely to jump into the conversation.

We have to go back to the 1994-95 strike to see the last example of the most drastic moves both the league and MLB Players Association took around the salary cap dispute.

After the league canceled the World Series in September of '94, President Clinton announced in October that he was appointing a mediator in an attempt to resolve the conflict. The efforts went nowhere, with each side moving incrementally but coming nowhere near an agreement. By the end of 1994, the league declared an impasse in negotiations and declared that they were implementing a cap system against the players' will. The union immediately filed an unfair labor practice charge with the National Labor Relations Board (NLRB). Frustrated by the lack of progress through mediation, Clinton ordered the sides to the White House and declared that if the sides didn't reach a deal by February 7, 1995, he would take the recommendations of mediator William J. Usery, Jr. on a settlement. The problem was, Clinton's pressure had no teeth as any recommendation would not be binding.

Ultimately, on March 26, 1995, the NLRB voted 3-2 to seek a court injunction forcing the owners to reinstate the provisions of the old collective bargaining agreement. That ultimately landed the case in a Manhattan courtroom in front of U.S. District Court Judge Sonia Sotomayor (yes, the same Sonia Sotomayor who now sits on the Supreme Court of the United States). On March 31, 1995, Sotomayor granted the NLRB's request for the preliminary injunction against the owners, saying, "When a contract ends, the parties must not alter mandatory subjects until a new agreement is reached or a good-faith impasse is reached."

Fast forward to a potential scenario in 2027 with regular-season games lost, the NLRB and the president could, once again, play a role. Trump has already commented on the matter.

"If you don't have a salary cap, you don't have a sport, because they can't help themselves," Trump told reporters on June 5 aboard Air Force One. "Football has a salary cap. They should have done it a long time ago.

"It's shocking, frankly, that they didn't put a cap on many years ago," Trump added, seemingly referring to the MLB strike in 1994 and 1995. "They had a chance to do a cap, but they blew it."

Trump has not been shy about using the bully pulpit in the past and could do so again, applying pressure on the NLRB if the union claims an unfair labor practice against the league. And in Trump's second term, he's aggressively reshaped the NLRB, making it more employer-focused through board filings and appointments.

Remember, the ruling in 1995 by the NLRB board was as a razor thin 3-2 ruling in favor of the players. Now, a ruling for the owners could be more favorable.

Back to the 1994-95 strike, Congress was a passive player. At the time, House Speaker Newt Gingrich stated, "I'm not sure Congress is the right place to try to organize the national pastime." Senate Majority Leader Robert Dole added, "We're very, very reluctant to have Congress get involved."

Note that at the time, both the House and Senate were controlled by the Republicans. By next year, Democrats will have a sizable majority in both as the midterm blue wave will dramatically alter Congress's dynamic.

It's unclear how much stomach the new coalition has for inserting itself into MLB's labor battle, but it is decidedly more union-friendly than it has been in the past.

As a means of applying pressure, one or more bills could be introduced to remove MLB's antitrust exemption, something the league has continually fought to maintain.

Threats of repealing MLB's antitrust exemption have come from both sides of the aisle in the past. In April 2021, several Republicans signed on to the Competition in Professional Baseball Act in an effort to strip MLB of its antitrust exemption. U.S. Senator Cory Booker (D-NJ) also signed on via amicus brief, saying, "For over a century, professional baseball has enjoyed a free pass to break the rules of fair competition because of a flawed Supreme Court ruling—one that the Court itself has acknowledged was a mistake," said Senator Booker. "The one-of-a-kind antitrust exemption gives leagues the power to artificially control salaries, ticket prices, team relocations, and more, harming players and baseball fans alike. It's time the Court fix this error and ensure that baseball, like every other sport, plays by the same rules of fair competition."

📅 Nov 11, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Snow, ice, and short days didn’t stop Michelle Baxter from regular runs outdoors.

When winter arrives, it’s tempting to put your running goals on hold until the temperatures warm up, the days get longer, and the roads clear. Cold temperatures make it harder to leave a warm bed, while snow and ice can turn a familiar route into a much slower—and more slippery—run.

For months at a time, Michelle Baxter’s running routes in Anchorage, Alaska, were covered in packed snow and ice. After a snowstorm, plows could take days to reach every neighborhood, and by then, cars had compressed the fallen snow onto the roads. Even after the plows came through, the slick surface often remained.

Baxter—now an RRCA- and V.02-certified run coach—spent 15 years living in Alaska and training through winters like these, figuring out how to keep running through harsh, unmotivating conditions.

She trained for 12 marathons in that time, continuing to build fitness year after year. Eventually, she even achieved her most ambitious running goal: breaking three hours in the marathon. After eight years of attempting to break the barrier—and a heartbreakingly close 3:01:14 at Humpy’s Marathon in Anchorage in August 2021—Baxter finally ran a 2:58:24 at the California International Marathon in December 2021. She has since lowered her PR to 2:56:57 at the Indianapolis Monumental Marathon.

Your winter running routes may not be covered in snow and ice for six months straight, but chances are you’ll face your own challenges as the temperature drops in the coming weeks and months. The good news: Baxter’s experience can help you navigate tough terrain and make the most of your training.

Here’s how you can put her lessons to work this winter so when the ice thaws, you’re ready to run your fastest race yet.

What you wear can make or break a winter run, Baxter says. When you’re too cold, too warm, or worried about taking a spill around every tight corner, it’s hard to focus on any other aspect of the run. Baxter spent years figuring out what works, and her gear advice starts with two essentials: finding reliable footwear and appropriate layers for the conditions.

In Anchorage, finding reliable footing was Baxter’s biggest winter challenge. She initially resisted buying studded shoes, assuming they wouldn’t make much difference. But once she tried them, she was surprised by how much more confident she felt in every step.

For packed snow, Baxter often wore regular trainers fitted with screws in the outsoles, which she added herself.

On sheer ice, Baxter preferred shoes made with built-in carbide studs; she swears by the Icebug brand. “You can wear these shoes and make a 90-degree turn and feel so confident running on ice—like sheer ice,” Baxter says. “They were a game-changer for me.”

You may not encounter sheets of ice on your regular running routes, but consider the conditions you encounter most often and look for an option suited to them.

Now living in Minnesota, Baxter says she reaches for studded shoes if she expects at least half of a run to cover potentially snowy or icy ground. She notes that while the shoes may be slightly less comfortable than your normal trainers, protecting your body from harm is the number-one priority during the winter months of training.

“I just think it’s worth it to feel confident running on the slippery surfaces and not risk injuring yourself,” she says.

One of the most common winter gear mistakes Baxter sees runners make is overdressing. It’s easy to look out the window, see cold weather, and pull on every layer you own in an effort to stay warm on the run. But remember you’ll warm up once you start moving.

Baxter’s rule of thumb echoes the advice of other run coaches: Start your run feeling a little chilly. After about 10 minutes, you should warm up and feel comfortable for the rest of the run.

Don’t forget to consider the wind, too; Baxter generally recommends dressing for the wind chill temperature. She recalls learning this lesson during one particularly windy Alaska run when she dressed for the ambient temperature rather than the wind chill temperature and ended up feeling sick from potential hypothermia.

If you’re unsure where to start when it comes to gearing up for a winter run, use our temperature-based layering guide, then pay attention to how you feel after each run. Were you still cold after warming up? Did you get sweaty and uncomfortable? Use your responses to adjust your layers on your next run.

For Baxter, keeping her legs warm as temperatures approached subzero was a recurring problem. She first turned to wearing multiple pairs of tights, which helped with the cold, but the layers restricted her movement. Eventually, she found a thick, fleece-lined pair of running tights that solved the problem, recommending them to other runners for temperatures ranging from 15 all the way down to minus 10 degrees Fahrenheit.

She made a similar change on top: Instead of wearing a base layer, fleece layer, and wind layer, she found a high-quality warm jacket to wear over a base layer.

Your biggest frustration might be cold hands or wet feet. For these issues, Baxter suggests putting hand warmers in your gloves, running-specific wool socks, and a waterproof shoe.

For visibility during the winter months, Baxter wears a lighted vest. She began treating it as a non-negotiable in her running wardrobe after noticing, as a driver, how much easier it was to see runners wearing blinking lights rather than simple reflective clothing items.

“Investing in a few quality pieces [of winter gear], I think you’re just going to enjoy your run more,” Baxter says. “You’re going to be safer and more comfortable.”

Even with the right layers, leaving your warm house and getting out the door often proves difficult when it’s cold and dark.

Baxter understands the struggle better than most. Anchorage gets less than six hours of daylight during the darkest winter months, and the city’s winter high temps hover around 25 degrees Fahrenheit.

So, how did she keep getting out there? “Running just became a habit of mine,” she says. “It’s how I start my day. I get up, I go for a run.” Even during peak winter in Alaska, Baxter often ran seven days a week simply because it was a part of her daily routine.

Baxter says each individual needs to find what helps them keep going, whether that’s a future goal, a training partner to run alongside, or a plan that lets you check off workouts.

For Baxter, her motivation and discipline to make running a habit often came from looking ahead.

“I just know how important those base miles are during the winter,” she says. “So I know I need to keep putting in the work, keep doing some workouts, because it’s only going to pay off down the road.”

If you have a spring marathon, a summer 10K, or another goal in mind, remind yourself how your winter routine fits into that bigger picture. You can also adjust your expectations for the season: Baxter says it’s okay to scale back during winter if that’s what works for you. A simple maintenance routine can help you arrive at your next training cycle ready to build.

The point is, doing something is better than doing nothing.
