In fantasy football, you have to look back to move forward. Fantasy points are the goal, but how a player gets there is the key. Analyst Joel Smyth breaks down 8 numbers to know from Week 10 in order to win your matchup in Week 11 and beyond.

Touches for Emmett Johnson in Week 10, the fourth-most on the week. Johnson took full advantage of Kenneth Walker III being absent for the week, taking on 22 of the 24 RB carries and all seven of the RB targets. The rookie became a 1:1 replacement and a week winner for those who picked him up this weekend. He should be kept no matter the availability of Walker in Week 11, and can even be used as a flex next weekend, with Walker likely being limited against a Cardinals defense that will be significant underdogs in Kansas City.

This should be a wake-up call for the importance of fantasy insurance RBs late in the season. Bye weeks are largely behind us, the waivers are shrinking and the upside for top RB2s is clear. It was very well known that if Walker was out of the picture, even for a game, Johnson would become the favorite for workhorse volume. The rate of late-round running backs posting RB1 numbers doubles over the last month of the season, making now the next best time to pick one up for your bench.

Targets and routes for Josh Downs in his first game as a Kansas City Chief. It's a new day for the young WR, going from a struggling offense led by Daniel Jones to an immediate season-high performance on eight routes with Patrick Mahomes. Downs was not only the go-to guy when on the field, but also capitalized on his opportunities with four receptions, 53 yards and a touchdown.

The Chiefs brought in Downs to help as a desperately needed man-coverage beater, but also as a downfield target so far. Three of Downs' five targets were 10+ yards downfield, tying a team high in such targets out of the gate, catching all three. Rashee Rice will get a lot of the work around the line of scrimmage while Downs can continue to help Mahomes push the ball downfield after starting dead last in air yards per attempt. That usage wasn't working with Jones in Indianapolis; however, the QB upgrade often fixes that.

Targets per route for Chris Olave the last two weeks when Jordyn Tyson has been on the field. Before Tyson's return, his 25.9% targets per route was one of the best in the NFL, leading him to WR1 numbers. The two-week sample becomes more dangerous in my eyes with it not being just okay production, but dominant target volume in a WR's first two career games. This offense has been perfect for Olave because of the volume, rather than being a top-10 offense. Tyson being targeted on 38% of routes so far is a major problem, not that it will stay that extreme, but that his routes are still limited.

The Saints will have weeks where their high-volume passing attack will be enough to satisfy both, but there will be more days where Olave isn't saved by top-five WR volume as the Saints' offense struggles as a whole.

Snaps for Travis Etienne Jr. in his return. The playing time can certainly increase, but the three-RB committee looks strong and much tougher to overcome than simply one RB. Audric Estime still played a major role, leading the backfield with 27 snaps. Kamara was third, but did have four of the six red-zone snaps, and Etienne at least led in touches (11).  Even worse than the volume issue is the efficiency issue. The Panthers entered 31st against RBs, it can't get much better. The Saints helped that ranking improve on Sunday with 17 carries for 24 rushing yards. They had negative yards before contact, zero carries over five yards and combined for 59 scrimmage yards. Although the schedule will have some bright spots, the New Orleans backfield is likely to continue to be messy, splitting three ways in an offense that ranks 27th in rushing yards per game. ​

Target share for Justin Jefferson in his first game with Kirk Cousins this season. Jefferson benefitted from T.J. Hockenson and Jordan Addison being unavailable, but almost everything else was positive for future outlook. His 13 targets and nine receptions were immediate season highs, with Minnesota having its second-highest scoring game on the season, resulting in an expected 23.9 fantasy points, the fourth-highest on the week. The production as a team was there and should show up stronger in the fantasy box score soon. In terms of fit, it was a great start to the change.

Cousins loves to target receivers on in-breaking routes and over the middle of the field compared to Kyler Murray, and that showed immediately. Jefferson was targeted five times over the middle of the field, over twice as much as his average to begin the year, an area of his game that has lost the most value since Sam Darnold left town. The beginning of Cousins saving this offense and Jefferson's fantasy season look promising.

Of the Colts RB touches went to Jonathan Taylor on Sunday, lower than it's been on average the last few weeks. Not according to plan! What was expected was that now that the Colts have no reason to save Taylor, and they don't have a first-round pick to tank for, their star RB would go back to getting 85%+ of the workload. Seth McGowan thought otherwise. The RB split in Indianapolis stayed nearly the exact same as the previous few weeks, resulting in Taylor being vulture-picked in the red zone and posting a very modest fantasy performance given the opponent. This was Taylor's best opportunity the rest of the season, and was largely wasted without the high-end RB volume we're used to seeing. He's talented enough to overcome low volume, or a low offense, but not many can do both. A road matchup versus a Texans' run defense on Thursday Night Football will be a much more difficult task at hand.

Targets for Trey McBride on Carson Beck's 18 passing attempts Sunday, his lowest in a game since 2024. The worry wasn't necessarily that Beck wouldn't target McBride as often, but that a high target share wouldn't equal high targets. McBride's 28% target share is pretty normal for the fantasy superstar; it just doesn't work on only 34 offensive plays with 11 completions. The Cardinals aren't just on their third-string QB, but their WR5 is the new WR1, their offensive line is banged up, their top-five drafted RB is on IR and it's showing on the scoreboard. After taking five sacks, Beck only completed a pass on 45% of his dropbacks, much below Jacoby Brissett's 58%, or even Gardner Minshew's No. 1 rate in a small sample. The volume will come back at times when the offense can sustain more drives, but it's just the start of the issues, with scoring upside and receiving efficiency also raising concerns.

The snap differential between Zach Charbonnet and Jadarian Price in Week 10, favoring Charbonnet. After a 50-50 split the last two weeks, the veteran made a real leap over the rookie, resulting in 2x as many touches (20-10). When Price was on the field, he was very involved in the offense, touching the ball on 10 of his 15 snaps; it's just hard to get much more when you're barely on the field. The Seahawks relied on Charbonnet in the passing game, running 15 routes versus Price's six, and turned their first-round pick into a worse version of 2025 Kenneth Walker III. Yes, he got the TD, but the score only resulted in 10.9 points and modest hope for future volume. Price has averaged under 10 fantasy PPG since returning, with the worst part being that it was versus a very friendly schedule. Upcoming, the Seahawks have the bye week before San Francisco, the No. 2 team against rushing fantasy points.

How Frank can restore reputation and save Crystal Palace

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Crystal Palace are Thomas Frank's third London club after previous spells with Brentford and Tottenham

"When the time is right I will look forward to my return as a manager, ready to embrace the job with great energy and dedication," said Thomas Frank in June.

That time is now after the Dane was confirmed as the new manager of Crystal Palace, who are two points and a goal difference deficit away from safety at the bottom of the Premier League.

Former boss Pierre Sage was sacked last week after just 16 league and cup games.

The role offers Frank an opportunity to rebuild his reputation after he was sacked by Tottenham in February after eight months.

Frank described it as "an honour" to be "in charge of this fantastic football club" as Palace announced his arrival.

"Crystal Palace is an iconic club in England and in the Premier League," Frank told Palace's website.

"I will do everything I possibly can to help and support the players and everyone around the place, and I'm really looking forward to meeting them."

Palace chairman Steve Parish said Frank was the "perfect fit for our club".

But how big is the challenge at Selhurst Park that awaits the man who made his name at Brentford?

"It's going to be a bit of a shock for Thomas Frank," former West Ham and England goalkeeper Rob Green tells BBC Sport.

"He had his success at Brentford - a very well-run club where players are brought in with a lot of thinking and planning to take over the players they sell for profit.

"They lose players and then have a mad scramble to get players in. They have a team that has been decimated by sales and not replaced by the same quality.

"Thomas Frank needs to find something within that team that builds and garners the belief that means that they can compete in the Premier League, because it feels a long way short of that right now."

Will Frank prove the right fit for Palace and haul the Eagles out of the relegation zone?

Since getting the sack at Tottenham after 26 league games last season, Frank has been out of management longer than he was in charge of Spurs.

In the summer he released a statement in which he said "there have been conversations and opportunities" to return to management, but that he had "decided not to rush into the next role".

Frank's name was heavily linked with Palace before they went for a head coach with no Premier League experience to replace Oliver Glasner - the man who delivered an FA Cup, a Conference League title and Community Shield before moving to Nottingham Forest.

"Palace had one of the best managers they have had, if not forever, silverware coming across the doorstep. Look at what they achieved last season and instead of building on that, they dismantled a team," said former Tottenham and England goalkeeper Paul Robinson.

HIs coaching staff will include current assistant manager Paddy McCarthy and goalkeeping coach Andy Quy, who previously worked with him at Brentford.

So why does he feel now is the right moment to return to management at a club propping up the table?

Frank, who was part of BBC Sport's World Cup punditry team during the summer, will be coming into the job refreshed and with renewed energy and enthusiasm following several months out of the game.

Between his appointment as Brentford boss in October 2018 and the sack at Tottenham earlier this year, he managed without a break for seven years and four months.

Spending the past few months away from Premier League pressure has allowed Frank, in his own words, to "study, observe and learn from other leaders both within sport and beyond".

Will his positivity and passion rub off on a Palace team who have lost seven times in 10 top-flight matches?

Would Thomas Frank be a good fit for Crystal Palace?

Frank, who places a major emphasis on set-piece organisation, will have to hit the ground running at Palace, who have lost their past four league games.

They have conceded more goals than anyone else in the Premier League (24) and have scored only 10 themselves.

"If you want someone to go in and restore some stability and defensive solidity while improving the set-piece element of the team, then Thomas Frank is the perfect man for the job," Times football writer Tom Allnutt told BBC Radio 5 Live's Football Daily podcast.

With the Premier League season just 10 games old, Frank certainly has time to turn it around and will have targets in mind when the transfer window opens in January.

Another challenge for Frank is getting midfielder Adam Wharton, who has not played for England since March, back to something like his best form.

These are all challenges the new manager will relish.

After his bruising experience at Tottenham - having established Brentford in the Premier League - Frank has decided to climb back on the hamster wheel with Palace.

"Crystal Palace and Thomas Frank feels like a very good fit," added Robinson.

Thomas Frank has a lot of work to do as Crystal Palace have undoubtedly been the worst team in the Premier League so far this season, ranking bottom not just in points (seven) but also goal difference (-14) and expected goal difference (-8.1).

It's the defence where Frank will have to focus most of his attention. Not only have Palace conceded the most goals (24) but they've also faced the most shots (189) and have the worst expected goals against (xGA) figure in the league (21.9).

Their attack has actually not been too bad. While only Hull have taken fewer shots, the Eagles have created really good chances and have perhaps been unlucky not to score a few more goals given the opportunities they've had.

There is also one significant factor in Frank's favour. Although Palace may be bottom of the Premier League, they are certainly not cut adrift as they sit just two points from safety.

The side propping up the table at this stage have had an average of just three points in the past 10 seasons, but for as bad as they've been, Palace have seven - the most the bottom side have had after 10 games for 24 years.

Prediction: Wales vs Portugal

Portugal travel to Cardiff on Tuesday night knowing that avoiding defeat against Wales will be enough to guarantee first place in UEFA Nations League Group A4.

While the visitors have already secured their place in the quarter-finals, Wales are facing a very different battle. Craig Bellamy’s side are certain to finish bottom of the group, but a point could prove crucial in helping them secure a place in the relegation playoffs.

Wales head into their final group match on the back of a disappointing 3-1 defeat away to Norway, a result that ended any lingering hopes of climbing the standings.

Bellamy’s side had known that a draw in Oslo would have kept them in third position, but the loss confirmed that they would finish behind Portugal, Denmark and Norway in Group A4.

However, there is still something to play for. The Dragons remain in a strong position to secure a relegation playoff berth, and avoiding defeat against Portugal would strengthen their chances considerably.

Recent form has been a concern, though. Wales have won just one of their last nine matches, recording three draws and five defeats since March. Despite those struggles, Bellamy will hope that home advantage can help his side finish the campaign on a positive note.

Ethan Ampadu is suspended and will miss the match, with Josh Sheehan expected to replace him in midfield.

Connor Roberts is likely to come into the side at right-back after Neco Williams suffered an injury.

David Brooks could also be rewarded for his goal against Norway, with Bellamy considering changes in attacking areas.

Portugal’s hopes of finishing top of the group remain firmly in their own hands despite suffering a surprise setback at the weekend.

Jorge Jesus’s side were heavily beaten 4-1 at home by Denmark, a result that ended a four-match winning streak in the Nations League. Portugal found themselves four goals behind before eventually finding the net late in the contest.

The defeat was particularly disappointing given the momentum they had built following Cristiano Ronaldo’s international retirement. Prior to the Denmark game, Portugal had won all four of their group matches, including a 1-0 victory over Wales on matchday one. Jesus expectedly had to answer some uncomfortable questions after the game.

Even so,  Portugal are still, realistically, the favourites to finish top of the group. A draw or victory in Cardiff would ensure Denmark cannot overtake them on the final day.

One slight concern for Jesus is that the only clean sheet of his tenure so far came in that opening victory against Wales.

Ruben Dias is suspended and unavailable, meaning Antonio Silva is expected to move into the centre of defence.

Nazinho is an option if Portugal decide to rotate their full-back positions.

Joao Neves and Bernardo Silva could both be recalled to the starting lineup, while Ruben Neves and Pedro Neto may drop to the bench.

Both teams will be looking to respond after disappointing results at the weekend, but Portugal appear better equipped to do so.

Wales have struggled for consistency throughout the campaign and continue to find victories difficult to come by. Portugal, meanwhile, have already shown their superiority in this fixture and possess significantly more quality across the pitch.

The visitors may not have everything their own way in Cardiff, but they should have enough to end the group stage with a victory and secure first place.

The ballot for the BBWAA portion of the 2027 Baseball Hall of Fame vote was revealed on Monday, featuring 13 holdovers and 14 newcomers.

Newcomers to the ballot are in bold.

As a reminder, players must receive votes on at least 75% of the ballots cast to be inducted into the Hall of Fame with the 2027 class. Those who get at least 5% of the vote can remain on the ballot the following year, and so on, for a maximum of 10 voting cycles. Players who are on the ballot for 10 years without getting 75% of the vote, in addition to players not getting 5% of the vote, fall off the ballot in ensuing years, though that doesn't mean the doors to the Hall are permanently shut.

The results will be announced on Jan. 19, with the induction set for Cooperstown over the summer.

Here's everything else you need to know about the Baseball Hall of Fame this offseason.

As alluded to earlier, there are two different votes each offseason. The BBWAA vote is for former players who have been retired from Major League Baseball for at least five years. It's the most common method in mainstream Hall of Fame ballot discussions. When you hear stuff like "first-ballot Hall of Famer," it means via the BBWAA vote.

Players who don't make it via the BBWAA vote can get extra chances via committees. That's how, for example, Jeff Kent made it last year. Formerly called the Veterans Committee, the Hall of Fame now uses Era Committees, which are divided into two categories: contemporary baseball (1980 to present) and classic (prior to 1980). The contemporary ballot is then split into two, one for players and one for non-players like umpires, executives, pioneers and managers.

Sure enough, this time around, it is a non-player Contemporary Baseball Committee, which looks at "managers, umpires and executives whose most significant career impact was realized since 1980."

We'll dive further into this group as we get closer to the Dec. 6 vote.

Giants legend Buster Posey joins the ballot, and he's one of those cases where I don't think a bunch of people need to delve deeply into his numbers. More than statistics, sometimes we get "feel" guys, as in, you just know he's a Hall of Famer. Posey qualifies there, I would think, for 75% of the electorate. We'll obviously do a deeper dive in the coming weeks, but even without the longevity component (Posey only played in parts of 12 MLB seasons), I think he's in.

As for the rest of the first-timers, the best case would be Jon Lester. Given the way a lot of similar starting pitchers have been treated in voting in recent years, my hunch is he'll be able to hang around on the ballot, but he has a major uphill battle in order to get in.

Then you're looking at Brett Gardner, Ryan Zimmerman, Kyle Seager, the short peak of Jake Arrieta and relief work of Andrew Miller. Good players worth mentioning, of course, but they won't sniff the Hall and it seems likely all behind Posey and Lester are one-and-dones on this ballot, as they'll fall short of 5% of the vote.

This is the sixth year for Alex Rodriguez on the ballot, which means it's the sixth time that I point out that we don't really need much time to go over his case. His numbers say he's one of the 10 best position players of all time, but his ties to PEDs are keeping him out to this point.

He did rise from 37.1% of the vote in 2025 to 40% last time, a new high-water mark. The progress is too modest to believe it's trending toward him getting in, but the number this year is certainly worth watching. If he somehow makes a leap to the 50% range, a deeper conversation might be in order.

Spain vs England was a game that had almost everything, and may yet mean more; perhaps the most significant moment might have come before it had even started.

The hosts took the opportunity of a first match back in Madrid since the World Cup to showcase the trophy, in what almost felt another homecoming.

The mood was very much of revelling in their embossed status, something that would have had a particular edge for this specific nation.

No country that has won the World Cup has waited as long for another trophy as England.

And, as the 2010 winners Iker Casillas, Fernando Torres and Fernando Llorente presented Unai Simon that with that precious piece of brilliant gold, it was almost literally in England’s faces.

Thomas Tuchel’s side had to watch the goalkeeper wave the World Cup about right in front of them.

It had been noted on the Spanish side that Jude Bellingham was the only player who clapped, this now being the city that he calls home with Real Madrid. He then played like he was demanding the same appreciation, and he was not the only one.

As one figure with knowledge of the England camp said: “They will have been thinking ‘that’s what we want’.”

Seeing the World Cup so close and yet so far, with more than three years until the next edition, only deepened a mood that had been felt in the build-up to this game.

England, in the words of both Declan Rice and Thomas Tuchel, knew they were going to win.

“I could feel the true belief in the dressing room before the match that this is exactly the moment where we want to prove ourselves and push our limits,” the manager said.

They had that conviction to show what they were really about, and to show they would be the only team in the world not cowed by Spain. It was that mentality that comes when you feel ready to beat a rival that has long had it over you; that has been a benchmark.

A pace was set just moments after seeing that trophy, as a ferocious England subjected Simon to a flurry.

Just as importantly, they displayed a new level of their own, even beyond Tuchel describing it as the best performance of his time.

This wasn’t, after all, the usual counter-attacking approach to playing Spain - at least not for when England actually got so far ahead.

It was England imposing their game, an evolving ideology, on Spain.

“We are well aware which steps we took after the World Cup,” Tuchel said, conspicuously repeating this more than once.

The manager was deeply stung after all the criticism following the Argentina defeat.

And yet, much as he might bristle about it in public, it does feel as if some of that criticism has been acknowledged.

England look much more technical, but still with pace.

Throughout the World Cup, after all, there was a definite sense of his team just seeking to force their way through.

This was something much more considered and coherent.

“We wanted to get better in the deep build-up, we wanted to get better at defending in a low block and on top of it, we wanted always to impose our identity and the way we want to play on every opponent,” Tuchel remarked.

This similarly feeds into another theme of the night, which was how James Trafford’s performance may start a path that sees him become England’s No 1.

It was conspicuous that, when asked about the goalkeeper’s brilliant late double-save, Tuchel immediately pointed to his pure football effect.

“But there was a lot to do in the deep build-up as well, to overcome the first line of press, which he did very well with everyone around him,” he said. “So there was a lot to do, never lose the concentration, never lose the focus, never lose the vision, to find the free man to overcome the press and put the game into the opponent’s half.”

Tuchel clearly wants to change the very focus of this team.

And, in doing so, he wants to change its entire outlook.

There was naturally a lot of talk about “confidence” after a victory like this, which Tuchel was only too happy to immerse himself in.

Spain had beaten France in a series of high-profile games, above all the Euro 2024 and World Cup 2026 semi-finals. Lamine Yamal had been right to emphasise that, because he knew it played on French minds.

It gave Spain an edge. They knew they could beat France, but France were unsure if they could beat Spain.

That’s also why the frantic ending to this game in Madrid, so at odds with the first hour, may have been so important.

Had Spain equalised, or potentially won, England would have just been another team to fail to beat them. It could similarly have been the deepening of England’s own complex about Spain, since this would have been three high-profile set-backs in succession.

Even if this had just been a draw, after all, it would have felt like a defeat given it was a 3-0 lead.

It was a win, and a first defeat that Spain have endured in 44 games over two and a half years.

As such, England are not just another team the Spanish are reigning over.

They’re a team that know how to beat them, that can carry that through to a home Euros.

It might even create a bit of consideration on the Spanish side, even allowing for how assured they are in their own approach.

It might be the beginning of a proper modern rivalry.

Might, of course. England have to go and use this to do what Spain have made customary, and actually win one of those trophies.

One of the main issues is defensive protection. City have conceded 17 goals in eight matches and have not kept a clean sheet in any of their last nine games. The high defensive line, a hallmark of Maresca’s teams, demands coordination between the units and a quick ability to react to losing the ball. Another is to review the team’s positioning without the ball, especially the distance between the defensive line and the most advanced players. It is not necessarily a question of abandoning the style of play, but of finding ways to make it less vulnerable.

The calendar and the physical condition of the squad are another important aspect. City lost rhythm during a demanding period of fixtures, and the recovery of players can help the team regain the intensity needed to execute Maresca’s model.

The break arrives at a time when City need to recover not only results, but also confidence in their own way of playing. City do not need to abandon their identity to react. Above all, they need to find a better balance between what they do with the ball and what they can do when they lose it. If the break allows Maresca to progress with this work and recover important players, the team could return better prepared to face a decisive stretch of the season.

Why do birds flap before landing? It may be about more than slowing down

📅 Nov 15, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

New calculations suggest birds flap during landing not just to control speed, but to stop within limited space.

Most birds flap their wings as they come in to land, but those final wingbeats may do more than slow them down. New calculations suggest they also help birds stop within a tight space.

A bird the size of a pigeon could glide to a complete stop with its wings held still.

To manage it, though, the bird would have to begin its landing maneuver at about 80 mph (36 meters per second). It would also have to climb roughly 65 feet (20 meters), the height of a six-story building.

Flapping, the researchers propose, lets a bird stop inside the space it has.

Tom Rottier carried out the study at the University of Manchester and the University of Toronto Institute for Aerospace Studies. He worked with Philippe Lavoie of Toronto and Ben Parslew of Manchester.

No birds were flown for the work. Instead, equations were written for a bird gliding on fixed wings.

Only gravity, lift, and air resistance acted on this model bird. The team then solved the equations for the slowest possible touchdown.

The forces on a wing depend on how fast the wing moves through the air. Those forces shrink as a bird slows down. Its control over where it goes shrinks with them.

Arriving fast is no better. Whatever speed is left at touchdown is usually absorbed by the legs. A bad landing can damage muscle and bone, or it can kill the bird.

The difficulty, said Parslew, an aerospace engineering lecturer at Manchester, is that “landing isn’t as simple as just slowing down.”

A wing also has a minimum working speed. In level flight, the bird’s whole weight has to be carried by lift. A wing that moves too slowly cannot make enough of it. Engineers call that minimum the stall speed.

The stall speed is not fixed, however, as the team’s equations show. A bird that climbs more steeply puts less of its weight on its wings. The stall speed drops as a result. In a climb straight up, it is zero.

A climb that ends straight up is how a gliding bird could stop without flapping. The bird comes in fast and pulls up. It then climbs more and more steeply while gravity and air resistance slow it down.

At the top, the bird is pointed straight up and has no speed left.

More lift also means more drag, and drag takes away the speed the climb depends on. For that reason, the tilt of the wings has to keep changing on the way up. By the end, the wings are turned broadside to the air.

The team calculated what such a landing would take for a rock pigeon weighing 14 ounces (400 grams).

Starting from level flight, the bird would need that 80 mph speed. This is more than double a pigeon’s typical cruising speed.

The bird would travel about 200 feet (60 meters) horizontally, roughly two-thirds of a football field, while climbing 65 feet.

City pigeons fly between buildings. A landing of that size is not feasible in many of those places, the team wrote. Bigger birds would need even more room.

The researchers therefore propose that flapping is not simply a way to slow down. A glide can do that. Flapping does it in a small space.

A perch can be approached from below, but the ground cannot. That changes the answer.

The researchers then tested landings in which the model bird could not climb at any point. No path brought it to a stop.

The slowest landing allowed by the equations came in at an angle of about 34 degrees below level.

A bird that glides down to the ground therefore arrives with some speed left. That speed has to be removed by flapping, by braking with the legs, or by both.

The legs need enough muscle and bone to absorb the impact. But that adds weight the bird must carry in flight.

The researchers suggest this trade-off may have shaped how birds evolved. The need to withstand landing impacts may have set a minimum strength for their legs.

Leftover speed may also help explain why ducks, geese, and swans land differently on water than on solid ground.

More of a bird’s energy is absorbed by water than by the ground. The bird can therefore arrive faster.

Some birds of prey do finish a landing without flapping.

A 2022 study observed Harris’s hawks flying between two perches. They learned to dive low and then glide up to the perch.

A wing that is tilted very steeply is stalled, meaning air no longer flows smoothly over it.

The authors of that study proposed an explanation for the swoop. In their view, it keeps the distance a hawk flies with stalled wings as short as possible.

Rottier’s team tested the idea by giving their model bird one goal at a time. For this test, the model bird was allowed to flap during the dive, as the hawks did.

First, it was told only to spend as little time stalled as possible. The model bird climbed and then came down onto the perch. It arrived at speeds that would harm a real hawk, the team wrote.

Next, the only goal was to land as slowly as possible. This time the model bird swooped deep.

A mix of the two goals generally matched the real hawks’ paths best. Touchdown speed did more than stall time to produce the swoop.

Diving builds speed, and speed allows a steeper climb. A steeper climb ends in a slower arrival.

The pigeon figures come from equations, not from a bird in the air. In its main form, the model has no wind, no body or tail, and no flapping.

A wing gets a brief boost in lift and drag when it tilts up quickly. The wings of landing birds do this, yet the boost is also left out of the model. To see how much it could matter, the team doubled the model’s maximum lift.

For a slow touchdown, the starting speed the bird needed fell by 44%. The forward distance fell by 65% and the climb by 55%. When the maximum drag was doubled instead, the speed needed went up.

A living bird may therefore be able to glide to a stop in less room than the pigeon numbers suggest. How much less cannot be said from the model.

The equations were written so that wind can be added later. The team also described the model as a base for studies of single species.

The same physics may apply to animals that never flap, such as flying squirrels and flying snakes. It may also apply to drones built to land on perches.

The full study was published in the journal Proceedings of the Royal Society A.

As the NFL embarks on the second half of its current 18-week regular-season schedule, it’s just a matter of time before the current structure changes.

It’s no secret that the NFL wants to expand the regular season from 17 to 18 games. The league is sufficiently confident about it happening that folks like Cowboys owner Jerry Jones speak of it as a given.

“I don’t think we’re far away from it,” Jones said Friday on 105.3 The Fan in Dallas, regarding the addition of an extra game. “I don’t know how — what is far away. Next month? No. Next year? Maybe. [The] next year? Maybe. That has to be negotiated with the players.

“But it’s in everybody’s best interests.  You’d cut back on the preseason games. You’d have teams more develop in the first few games as the type of thing you were trying to get done in preseason. But it’s in everybody’s interest, and it certainly is in the players’ best interest in my mind, for sure.  And having said that, I would anticipate that coming.”

His comments presume the NFL Players Association will happily agree to 18 regular-season games. They likely won’t. At a minimum, they’ll seek major concessions in exchange for the willingness to add yet another game that counts.

The unspoken goal had been 2027. The NFL delayed picking a date for Super Bowl LXII in Atlanta in order to accommodate the possibility that the game will slide a week or two deeper into February. That would make 2028 the earliest year for expansion, as a practical matter. (Adding a game for 2027 would be possible only if the dead week between the conference championships is wiped out or if the season starts a week earlier. Expansion by 2027 would require expedited negotiations. Expansion by 2028 would hinge on the NFL and NFLPA striking a deal within the next year or so. The union has shown no inclination, however, to engage in talks aimed at extending the current Collective Bargaining Agreement, which runs through the 2030 season.

Still, if push comes to shove, there will be 18 games. The blueprint was created in 2011. The NFL will lock the players out until the players agree to the things the owners want. Most notably, 18 regular-season games and 16 annual international games.

Beyond that, Jones’s comment about the early games of the season is odd. “You’d have teams more develop in the first few games as the type of thing you were trying to get done in preseason.”

He says that as if it’s a good thing. It definitely is not. Already, teams struggle in September to get up to speed, given that most teams (with the exception of those assigned to the Hall of Fame Game) play only three preseason games. When the preseason reduces to two games, that’s even fewer chances to prepare.

It’s also fewer chances for the non-starters to get the attention of coaches and General Managers in games that don’t count in the standings. It will become harder for undrafted free agents to make the 53-man rosters.

Regardless, the motivation isn’t football. It’s money. More inventory equals more weekends equals more broadcast windows equals more revenue.

And that likely will continue until the preseason shrinks to zero games — and the regular season expands to 20 games.

"We miss our brother," Luke said on The Bachelorette. "We just are trusting that God brings things full circle. And just wish that everything would just get back to us being a family."

"They go on a bulls--t show and leave two empty chairs," he said in Enigma. "They all agreed this was like a good thing to do, to leave two empty chairs at a stupid dating show that my brother just went on to get famous—his words, not mine—that he ended up winning."

Aaron reiterated in the series that he was never even invited to the dinner, which was held during the NFL season.

A dinner "I was never asked to go to," he said, "not that I would have gone."

E! News reached out to Jordan, Ed and Darla for comment on what Aaron had to say in Enigma but has not yet heard back.

Jordan told E! in 2016 after his hometown episode aired that he "made a commitment to go on there and be honest with JoJo and make sure that she knew everything" about where he was coming from.

Aaron has embraced taking ayahuasca—"It's the hardest medicine possible, that I've tried"—as an essential part of his journey, also touting its healing power for "mind, body, spirit."

"I was searching, for sure," he said in Enigma. "My life was through one lens, of organized religion. Where are people in life finding deep peace and centeredness and presence outside of what I knew?"

Speaking of the divide between him and his family, Aaron said he "found a lot of resistance" once he started researching other religions and plant medicine, "doing things that, compared to what I grew up in, would be considered an alternative lifestyle."

As for his family, he continued, "You know, they’re living as best they can. That is, still engulfed in organized religion, which works for them. That's great. So as much as they might not like what they see, love and respect and gratitude for how I was raised, because it wouldn’t have turned me into who I am today."

"People ask me, 'Is there hope for a reconciliation?'" Aaron said. "I say, 'Yeah, of course. Of course.'"

He added, "I don’t want them to fail, to struggle, to have any strife or issues. I don't wish any ill will on them at all. It's more like this: We're just different steps on the timeline of our own journeys."
