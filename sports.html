The Cowboys will play “multiple weeks” without starting tight end Jake Ferguson and backup running back Tyler Goodson, coach Brian Schottenheimer said Monday.

Ferguson sprained the posterior cruciate and the medial collateral ligaments in his left knee in the third quarter Sunday, and Goodson injured his knee in the second quarter but played through it into the second half.

Goodson had a career-high 77 yards on 12 carries against the Colts.

Bears vs. Bucs Game Balls: Some How, Some Way

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

The Chicago Bears walked away from Sunday Night Football this week with their seventh win of the season (and first on Sunday night since 2018).

And yet, many fans went to bed on Sunday evening with some uneasiness about their favorite football team. Caleb Williams, after a masterful start in the first quarter, ended up having his worst game as a pro. Rome Odunze once again had a case of the drops. And the Bears suffered more injuries that left some big question marks moving forward.

Bears fans are in a very different place as a fan base than they were a couple of years ago when most would have done just about anything for a win on prime time, no matter how it came about. These days, if a win isn't dominant enough, some sections of the fan base are calling for heads.

But even though some (many) things were undoubtedly sloppy in this week's game against the Bucs, the Bears never trailed and Williams delivered a game winning drive in crunch time when the team demanded it most. Let's just hope this is an experience that results in growth down the stretch.

Burden suffered a concussion near the end of regulation when he took a helmet-to-helmet hit from Rueben Bain Jr. along the sideline. The hit caused Burden to drop the ball, resulting in a turnover that led to the Bucs tying the game with a touchdown and successful two-point conversion.

While the result of the play was costly, I'm not sure there's a player in the league that could have taken that hit and not dropped the ball. And there's certainly an argument to be made that the hit should have resulted in a flag, whether the helmet-to-helmet hit was intentional or not.

But I digress. Prior to this play, Burden was once again the Bears' number one and most reliable receiver throughout the game. He had seven catches for 97 yards and one touchdown.

Burden's 56-yard touchdown was the Bears' second of the night and a beautiful sight, including a couple of broken tackles. At this point, it appeared the Bears were going to run the Bucs out of the stadium.

Even though the Bucs would end up coming back, and credit to their defense for forcing several turnovers, Burden was the steady hand throughout most of the night on the offensive side of the ball.

Much has been written about Jeffery Simmons serving as a "multiplier" for a defense, and Bush has been one of the largest benefactors of said multiplication here in Chicago.

With Simmons drawing double teams and attention up front, there is less of an opportunity for blockers to get to the second level and get their hands on Bears linebackers. This has led to Bush being able to utilize his top notch speed and become a real weapon on defense.

That was certainly the case on Sunday night. Bush ended the night with 9 tackles, one tackle for loss, and two passes defended.

Honorable mentions on defense: DT Jeffery Simmons, DE Austin Booker, CB Jaylon Johnson, and CB Cam Lewis. This one really was a close call this week. Booker would have likely been my next choice.

A former teammate of Harry Kane and Luka Modric has this week been convicted of fare dodging in London, with his career taking a very different turn from that of his two Ballon d’Or-winning counterparts.

The news comes in the same week that Kane became the first Englishman in 25 years to win football’s most prestigious individual award, following in the footsteps of Michael Owen, who claimed the famous Golden Ball in 2001.

Kane’s journey began in humble surroundings, with the striker turning out for the likes of Fleetwood Town before climbing the footballing ladder at Premier League side Tottenham and, later, Champions League contenders Bayern Munich.

That rollercoaster ride through the football pyramid has seen him share the pitch with all sorts of characters from different backgrounds. One former teammate has now been found guilty of a rather unexpected offence - dodging a £7 train fare, despite having played a sport where lucrative contracts are not rare.

According to the Wimbledon Guardian, Sebastien Bassong, now 41, was caught following a friend through the ticket barriers at St Paul’s Station on May 2.

A subsequent investigation by a Transport for London (TfL) officer revealed that Bassong had been using a train driver’s nominee pass that did not belong to him.

In a statement to the court, TfL revenue control officer Adebukola Anifowose said she spotted Bassong at 1.08pm “pretend to tap an Oystercard enclosed in a white wallet before tailgating through the wide-aisle gate behind another male”.

Bassong appeared at Wimbledon Magistrates’ Court on Friday, October 30, where he was convicted of travelling without a valid ticket. He was fined £220 and ordered to pay the £7 fare, £250 in prosecution costs and an £88 victim surcharge, bringing the total to £565.

The friend who accompanied him later told the TfL officer that he had given Bassong the pass because he was “experiencing financial and personal hardship due to a divorce and unemployment”.

Bassong's career never really took off after he left Tottenham following just three seasons in north London. Before that decision, during lockdown, he featured in a pre-season friendly for Isthmian League side Haringey Borough.

These days, Bassong works as a strategic performance and leadership advisor, motivational coach and public speaker. He has also taken part in charity boxing events, including one organised by Pro Project Promotions, where he knocked out former Arsenal defender Armand Traore.

He Played For Arsenal at 19, Then He Was Sent to Prison For 4 Years

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

15 years after making his Arsenal debut at the age of 19, Jay Emmanuel-Thomas was sentenced to four years in prison.

Born in East London, Emmanuel-Thomas joined the Gunners' academy at the age of eight. He steadily progressed through the various youth ranks before going on to make five appearances for the first team under Arsene Wenger.

After a series of uninspiring loan spells, Arsenal parted ways with the striker, selling him to Ipswich Town for an undisclosed fee. He'd go on to enjoy stints at Queens Park Rangers, MK Dons and Gillingham, before spending time in Scotland, Thailand and India.

However, his career at the professional level was essentially brought to an end when he was arrested and charged with orchestrating the attempted importation of drugs in 2024.

An extremely prolific forward at youth level, big things were expected of Emmanuel-Thomas as he approached adulthood. He became the captain of Arsenal's under-18s when he was just 16, and signed his first professional contract in 2008, at the age of 17.

Wenger, who was known for blooding young talent at every opportunity, started to take note of Emmanuel-Thomas' exploits in the academy during the 2008/09 season. The Frenchman named the centre-forward in multiple matchday squads, although he kept him at arm's length from the pitch, prioritising the player's physical development instead of rushing him into senior football.

However, such was the level of Emmanuel-Thomas' performances in the reserves that he became irresistible to Wenger in the following campaign. In January 2010, the striker made his senior debut in an FA Cup tie against Stoke City. He started as Arsenal's centre-forward, but was substituted after 66 minutes.

Although this was Emmanuel-Thomas' only appearance that season for the Gunners' first team, as he was shipped out on loan to Blackpool and Doncaster Rovers at various points during the 2009/10 campaign, Wenger showered the youngster with praise at the time.

"Jay is knocking very hard on my door - with two hands," the legendary manager said.

"He has outstanding quality. He works very hard to get his fitness right. When his fitness is right, Jay will be not only a good player but a great player. One thing is for sure - he can score goals. That is a massive talent you cannot give to people, his right foot, left foot, he is unbelievable in front of goal."

Despite this effusive praise, Wenger's words didn't translate into his team selection the following season. Emmanuel-Thomas was granted just four opportunities to play for Arsenal in 2010/11, including cameos alongside the likes of Jack Wilshere and Cesc Fabregas, before again being shunted out temporarily to Cardiff City.

At the end of that season, the Gunners opted to cash in on their promising young striker, selling him to Ipswich in a move that hurt many supporters, who had believed the number nine could eventually succeed Robin van Persie at the Emirates.

A below-par stint in the Championship with the Tractor Boys saw Emmanuel-Thomas leave for League One Bristol City in the summer of 2013. Revitalising his career with the Robins, the Englishman enjoyed two productive years in Bristol in the third tier, before returning to the Championship with QPR.

However, a flat spell in West London proved to be the beginning of the end for Emmanuel-Thomas at the top level. Within four years of signing for the Hoops, he was in Thailand playing for PTT Rayong.

This was the start of his 'journeyman' era, which involved six clubs in six years, ending in the Scottish Championship with Greenock Morton. Then came the controversy.

The Gunners got this one massively wrong.

In September 2024, Emmanuel-Thomas was arrested and accused of being responsible for importing 60kg of cannabis, worth an estimated £600,000. This cannabis was unearthed in suitcases arriving at Stansted Airport from Bangkok, carried by the footballer's girlfriend, who had been duped into believing she was transporting gold.

In May 2025, the Hale End academy graduate pleaded guilty to smuggling drugs from Thailand at Chelmsford Crown Court. He was sentenced to four years in prison but was released on parole after spending just a month behind bars.

He has since started playing for Whitehawk, a semi-professional club in Brighton, in the 7th tier of English football.

He is especially eager to support aspiring Dungeon Masters who hope to develop their skills and become as accomplished as he is, even appearing on the official Dungeons & Dragons YouTube page to provide assistance.

Mercer’s wisdom is well documented across multiple interviews, but only recently, as a fellow DM, did I find the most inspiring quote that everyone (regardless of whether you're a Dungeon Master or a player) should internalize as soon as humanly possible.

In an interview with Variety published in January 2026, Mercer and Mulligan sat down for a sincere conversation about everything and anything D&D. They reflected on their respective work as Dungeon Masters on Critical Role and Dimension 20, discussing the experiences and paths that shaped their careers.

One topic that came up was preparing a tabletop session. Mulligan asked Mercer what his philosophy is for preparing a tabletop session, and Mercer’s answer perfectly defines one of the core beliefs that we think should inspire players on either side of the table in their future games.

“Like many things, it comes with experience, and sometimes you have to fail to learn better.”

After decades of playing tabletop games and building a successful career from them, Mercer’s words capture the spirit of what makes role-playing tabletop games, particularly D&D, so engaging — our desire to perfect how we tell stories through interactive play.

We’ve all heard similar sayings about the benefits of failure, such as Henry Ford’s “Failure is only the opportunity to begin again, this time more intelligently,” or Chris Bradford’s “There is no failure except in no longer trying.”

Giving yourself permission to fail in D&D, however, is one of the most essential lessons a player needs to learn. Without it, the striving for perfection puts tremendous pressure on not just you, but everyone at the table with you.

For example: if you’re the DM, you’ll constantly be assessing whether your players are having fun, or feeling like you have to nudge them in a specific direction, and getting more and more alarmed when that doesn’t turn out to be the case. If you’re a player, you’ll be so busy overthinking whether your decisions will work out that you'll bring the game to a halt.

When I first started DMing, I scripted scenarios that I thought would completely derail the campaign and the story I wanted to tell if the players didn’t follow them to the letter. But that mindset was selfish: this wasn’t just my story, and I wasn't the only player at the table. It led me to make terrible, sometimes unfair calls as I tried to get everything back on track. Half the time, it didn’t work and only made things harder. Why didn’t I stop and pause to tell my players they’d thrown me a curveball once again — and that I, a Brit with only a passing knowledge of American sports, had fumbled it so badly?

The truth was that my anxiety about not doing things exactly as I’d planned in my head was making games feel far more terrifying than they needed to. I didn’t want to be wrong, but being wrong, and having people accept that about you and still want to sit at your table because they want to be part of the story you’re telling together, is one of life’s greatest pleasures.

Regardless of which side of the table you’re on, the fear of failure and the pursuit of perfection can gradually take over and distract from what matters most. When you try so hard to make every decision, encounter, or moment just right, it’s easy to lose sight of the reason everyone gathered to play in the first place: to have fun.

That’s why it’s worth keeping Mercer’s advice in mind. After all, failure is a gift that keeps on giving.

Per the posting agreement with MLB, posted NPB and CPBL players get a 45-day window to negotiate and sign with an MLB team. KBO players get a 30-day window. Players who do not sign during that posting window return to their previous team in NPB, CPBL, or KBO. The MLB team that signs a posted player pays a fee to the player's former NPB, CPBL, or KBO team.

$5M plus 17.5% of contract value over $25M

$9.375M plus 15% of contract value over $50M

For example, the White Sox paid Munetaka Murakami's former team, the Yakult Swallows, a $6.575 million posting fee on his $34 million contract last offseason. That's $5 million on the first $25 million plus 17.5% of the remaining $9 million, which is $1.575 million. The $5 million plus $1.575 million equals the $6.575 million posting fee paid to the Swallows.

Unlike last lockout, MLB will not be pausing the signing window during the expected lockout, according to The Athletic. In other words, if owners lock players out on Dec. 1 as expected, that date will essentially serve as the same deadline for already posted NPB players unless the lockout ends before their 45-day window expires.

Michael Carrick's men led Crystal Palace by that metric, while sitting two points clear of rock-bottom Sunderland and one better off than Forest after nine rounds.

The Tricky Trees were unsurprisingly tipped to suffer defeat to Enzo Maresca's men at the City Ground, but in an unexpected turn of events, Forest claimed a 3-1 triumph on home soil.

Jair Cunha and Liam Delap propelled the Garibaldi into a 2-0 lead before Rayan Cherki pulled one back, but the Frenchman's response was futile as Delap struck his second of the game in the second half.

Forest's win propelled Oliver Glasner's men out of the Premier League drop zone, while simultaneously forcing Man United into the bottom three, two points adrift of 17th-placed Coventry City.

The Red Devils therefore find themselves in the bottom three after nine rounds for the first time since November 1986, when they were in 21st place after 15 games of the 1986-87 season.

November 1986 was the same month that Ron Atkinson was dismissed and Sir Alex Ferguson took over as head coach, and the latter ultimately led Man Utd to an 11th-placed finish in the First Division.

The 20-time English champions will rise to 16th place if they beat Aston Villa by any scoreline on Sunday, though, while victory by three goals or more will also lift them above Fulham into 15th spot.

Nevertheless, Carrick remains a man under major pressure after Man United's new 40-year worst, in spite of Tuesday's extraordinary 3-2 Champions League comeback triumph over Roma.

Man United always intended to get to the November international break before making any firm managerial decisions, so to say Carrick bought himself more time with Tuesday's fightback may be inaccurate.

However, another pitiful Premier League performance and result on Sunday could force the hierarchy to take action, especially with the current bookies' favourites available for hire immediately.

The odds on Eddie Howe replacing Carrick are now as low as 8/13 on some operators, while Julian Nagelsmann is the second-favourite to take charge at 11/4.

Antonio Conte and Unai Emery are not far behind, the latter's links with Old Trafford representing a fascinating sub-plot ahead of tomorrow's fixture.

Report Cagliari fan in hospital after fall from stands

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

There was an unfortunate incident after Cagliari’s 1-0 victory over Frosinone, as a supporter fell from the stands trying to salute the players, who rushed to his aid.

According to Sky Sport Italia, the fall occurred as the squad was walking over to thank the fans for their support several minutes after the final whistle of today’s Serie A encounter.

Captain Alessandro Deiola and goalkeeper Elia Caprile were the first to realise what had happened, and rushed over to help him.

An ambulance is always ready on the sidelines in top flight matches, and was called in take the supporter to the local hospital.

It’s reported the fan has been diagnosed with a fractured clavicle, but there are no more serious complications.

If all goes well, he should be released on Sunday to continue his recovery at home.

The Unipol Domus where the Sardinians play their home matches is a temporary structure, and was put in place while plans to reconstruct the Stadio Sant’Elia were worked through.
