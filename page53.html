Former Serie A player takes up MMA

At 34, Diego Demme found a new field of expression after hanging up his boots last summer. The former Naples midfielder, crowned Italian champion with the club in 2023, started practicing MMA in Berlin. Having played for RB Leipzig and Hertha Berlin, the former German international now trains four to five times a week at the Choke Athletics club. A radical change of discipline for someone who has played for a long time at the highest level of European football.

However, the beginnings were not easy. Demme admitted to having suffered from the holds of his partners and the physical intensity of this new practice. But his progress is already visible: he recently completed a course without being forced to drop out, a first for which he was delighted. His coach, Saman Darvish, highlights his ability to learn, his endurance and his determination. If the German now plans to join intermediate to advanced level grappling sessions, he does not plan to become a professional fighter. Above all, he sees this experience as a new personal challenge and a way to learn to better defend himself.

Rodrigo Mora: ‘Football is crazy sometimes, Cristiano Ronaldo my idol’

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Rodrigo Mora concedes ‘football is crazy’ after scoring his third consecutive Serie A goal for Roma and looks forward to a senior Portugal debut even without ‘idol’ Cristiano Ronaldo.

The 19-year-old talent was a big investment this summer from FC Porto, secured for €25m for effectively only half his contract.

It took a while for him to make his mark in the Giallorossi jersey, but this evening he scored in the 3-0 win over Sassuolo, his third consecutive Serie A match on target.

“It was a very good pass from Malen, I tried my luck, and I was happy to score another important goal, so Roma keep pushing with another win,” Mora told Sky Sport Italia.

He now has four goals in nine Serie A games, plus two assists in four Champions League appearances under Gian Piero Gasperini.

“When I arrived here, I thought we were strong. Every time I training I thought we were a very good team, we are proving that in the match and need to keep this momentum going.”

Mora seems to have really hit his stride in the last month, as after struggling to even get into the starting XI, he has flourished in recent weeks.

“Football is crazy, because one day you don’t play, the next you play. I was ready, now it’s my third goal in a row, so it’s important to always be ready when the moment comes.”

Rodrigo Mora has also received a call-up for the senior Portugal squad, which will be a very different team now that Cristiano Ronaldo has retired from international duty.

“It’s a very proud moment for me and my family, it is always a pleasure to play for Portugal. Now Cristiano is not there, but is an idol for everyone in Portugal, including me. I learned a lot when I came to the national team, I am very happy to be there.”

Why your screen sharing drops to 5 fps instead of lowering resolution

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

When a screen share gets choppy, most people assume the network is bad. Sometimes it is. But there is a quieter failure mode where the network is fine, the encoder is fine, and the picture still collapses to single-digit frame rates while resolution never moves. The user ends up with the worst of both worlds: a soft frame that also stutters.

The reason is a default you probably never set: which dimension the pipeline is allowed to sacrifice under pressure.

WebRTC exposes degradationPreference on a sender, with three values: maintain-resolution, maintain-framerate, and balanced. The names suggest a symmetric trade-off. The behavior is not symmetric in practice.

For screen content, many stacks lean toward preserving resolution, because text and tables are unreadable if you halve the dimensions. That reasoning is sound for a still image. Video is not a still image. When the encoder is told to keep 3024×1964 and the bitrate budget tightens, it cannot keep quality by magic. It keeps the pixel count, spends fewer bits per frame, and the result is visibly soft. At the same time, the pacing logic has to shed something, and what it sheds is frame rate.

So "maintain resolution" does not actually protect legibility. It protects a number. What the viewer sees is a soft, low-frame-rate stream that is neither sharp nor fluid.

Read those together. The encoder is not struggling — 12.3 ms per frame leaves headroom. No frames were dropped at the encoder. The bandwidth estimator never once asked the pipeline to constrain itself. And yet the delivered frame rate bottomed out at 3.

That is the important part: adaptation never engaged. The 3 fps was not the controller choosing a lower frame rate under a bandwidth limit. It was the absence of any decision. Resolution stayed at full size the entire run because nothing touched it, and frame rate fell for reasons upstream of the decision the controller makes.

If you only watch average frame rate, this looks like "bad network." It is not. It is a pipeline with no owner for the resolution-versus-frame-rate choice.

None of these are solved by a preference flag. A preference says what to do if the engine decides to act. It does not make the engine act, and it does not remove capture-side load.

For screen sharing where motion and responsiveness matter, maintain-framerate is often the better default — a soft frame that keeps updating is easier to follow than a sharp frame that freezes. For a document being read line by line, the opposite may be true. Either way, the choice is a product decision, not something to leave to the engine.

Instead of waiting for adaptation, step resolution yourself on a schedule tied to observed conditions:

Call applyConstraints with a reduced width/height, or use scaleResolutionDownBy on the sender. The point is that each step is a real, bounded reduction that lowers encode cost and queue depth — not a preference the engine may ignore.

The cheapest fix is upstream: do not capture at native size and hope the encoder manages it. Cap the capture dimensions to what the viewer can actually resolve. A 3024-pixel-wide frame on a 1440-pixel viewer is mostly wasted bits, and it makes every downstream stage more expensive. The pipeline gets easier at every stage when the source is already the size you intend to send.

The uncomfortable summary: when the engine keeps resolution and drops frames, it is making a choice you did not make. That choice is defensible for a photograph and wrong for a live screen. A number that preserves legibility at 3 fps is an illusion — a sharp frame no one can read because it never changes.

Instrument the frame-rate distribution, not the average. Watch whether limit ever leaves none. If it never does, adaptation is not saving you, and the resolution ladder is yours to build.
