LeBron held to single digits for only 10th time on Cleveland return

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

LeBron James held to eight points as Philadelphia 76ers lose 119-95 on his return to Cleveland; the 41-year-old received smattering of boos from Cavaliers fans during first half; James spent 11 seasons over two stints with the Cavs

LeBron James was held to single-digit points for only the 10th time in 1,630 NBA regular-season games as his Philadelphia 76ers were beaten on his return to face his former team the Cleveland Cavaliers.

James was held to eight points as the Cavaliers routed the 76ers 119-95 in an NBA Cup group stage game.

The 41-year-old, a native of Akron, Ohio, who elected to sign with Philadelphia in the offseason, instead of returning to Cleveland for a third stint, was 3 of 12 from the field, including 0 for 4 on three-pointers, while he committed six turnovers in 31 minutes.

"Definitely not feeling a good rhythm offensively," James said. "We didn't look like much offensively. We had a couple spurts, but too many turnovers on my behalf."

James received a smattering of boos from Cavaliers fans every time he touched the ball during the first half.

And this time there was no video tribute to the four-time NBA champion - who won one of those titles with Cleveland in 2016. When visiting the Cavaliers last season, they showed highlights of him scoring 25-straight points during Game 5 of the 2007 Eastern Conference finals against the Detroit Pistons, which prompted him to tear up.

James spent 11 seasons over two stints with the Cavs. He left as a free agent in 2010 to join the Miami Heat, came back in 2014 and departed again in 2018 to join the Los Angeles Lakers.

"At the end of the day, nothing can get as worse as December 2, 2010, from a reception standpoint," James said. "I've seen them near the depths of hell when it comes to reception here all the way to the heavens above. So anything in between, it's easy money."

The 76ers' loss was James' second-worst in Cleveland as a visiting player. He is 9-5, but has been on the losing end in his last three trips. Philadelphia will return to Cleveland on March 1.

The Sixers came in with the NBA's best record but fell to 8-2. Head coach Nick Nurse was already looking ahead to Sunday's game at the Chicago Bulls.

"We were lacklustre and they were very, very good. And I think we expected them to be very good tonight considering that they had a couple tough losses in a row," Nurse said.

"We were not very good. Probably once in a while that's going to happen. Important thing is that we get over it and bounce back on Sunday."

Not playing at Man City 'horrendous' - Trafford

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

'I had to play regularly for my mental wellbeing' - Trafford

James Trafford says not playing for Manchester City last season was "horrendous" before he moved to Leeds United in the summer in search of first-team football.

City re-signed academy graduate Trafford in July 2025, two years after he had left the club to join Championship side Burnley.

But less than six weeks after his arrival, Italy international Gianluigi Donnarumma arrived from Paris St-Germain.

The 27-year-old, who helped the French club win the Champions League just weeks earlier, immediately became City's first-choice goalkeeper, leaving Trafford short of playing time.

After starting the first three matches of the campaign, 24-year-old Trafford made just one further Premier League start in the 35 games that followed Donnarumma's arrival.

Speaking on Football Daily's The Football Interview, Trafford reflected on the difficulties he encountered during a season on the fringes of Pep Guardiola's squad.

"What a horrendous, horrendous feeling. Not playing," said Trafford.

"You are ringing a lot of people and just venting. Like most people, you have a [bad] day at work and you ring someone up.

"I ring my dad, I ring my mum, I ring my missus, I ring my mates. I like ringing my mates and we can just have a good talk about it all. It's quite funny."

Despite his limited playing time, Trafford believes he still gained plenty from his final season at City, having helped the club win the League Cup and FA Cup after starting both finals at Wembley.

"Going into any season, I always have the highest of hopes and always the belief that it will go the best way it could," he added.

"Unfortunately, we didn't win all four trophies. It didn't go how I expected it to go, but it happens.

"In the moment, I just tried to make the best of it as I could. Looking back, I don't think it was a disappointing season in the end because I learned so much, I improved so much and I won two trophies in one year.

"There were moments when I was very down, regretting things and feeling disappointed, but looking back, I'm very thankful for the experience. What an incredible year."

Trafford was part of England's squad for the 2026 World Cup and, although he did not get game time, being part of Thomas Tuchel's squad was an "incredible experience" that he says he will not forget.

"I didn't play, but it is what it is," said Trafford. "I didn't expect to play when I got called up. I knew what my role was, but you just take as much experience as possible, learn from it and keep trying to win the day, because the more days you win, the better position you're in."

Next time around, though, Trafford hopes to be England's starting goalkeeper and believes that if he continues his impressive performances with Leeds, that ambition is entirely achievable.

"It's my aim and it's something I've always dreamed of," said Trafford.

"I always believe that one day I could be. Every day I try to keep improving to put myself in the best position to be England's number one one day.

"I think everyone in the goalkeeping department with the national team wants to be number one because it's an amazing position to be in."

'Everyone's got an opinion on goalkeeping'

Since arriving at Elland Road, he has relished the opportunity to play regularly in the Premier League.

The former Bolton Wanderers and Accrington Stanley loanee has started all nine of Leeds' Premier League matches this season, keeping three clean sheets, with only Brighton (seven) and Arsenal (five) having conceded fewer goals than Daniel Farke's side (eight).

His performances have helped Leeds to 12th in the table before this weekend's fixtures - just two points off the European places - and he says the club are only going to get better.

"I just think it's an incredible club," he said. "You look at the fanbase, everyone here - it's an incredible place. It's just somewhere that's only going and getting better.

"We'd love to get back into Europe. I think every club you ask would like to get into Europe, so the main goal is just to keep on improving."

Agent Distraction

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

What is easy for humans to skim past in code, agents see as a call to action. They do not know better, latching on to that which is littered across the files read during the planning and research phase of the coding journey.

As agents' agency through tools and skills grows the ability for side-quests scales proportionally. Obsolete specs, long resolved bugs, and lagging documentation no longer rest in long forgotten corners of the codebase, but rather are unintentionally ingested as context indirecting and misdirecting the next wave of coders - the agents. This indirection is not free: in the best case it only costs tokens, steps, and time - in the worst case it distracts and grounds the agent on falsehoods leading to incorrect logic and decisions during implementation.

Measuring how often agents fall into these traps across millions of monthly sessions is impossible with manual human review - we need an objective signal derivable from the code itself. Inline TODO(bug/...) comments provide the perfect proxy. TODOs tend to be left behind long after the underlying bug is resolved or abandoned, while the linked bug ticket acts as the canonical record. By cross-referencing every TODO an agent encounters against the TODO's bug status and the user's prompt, we can automatically separate valid work from wasted detours at scale.

By leveraging the distributed traces of agent interactions across thousands of engineers and over hundreds of millions of individual agent steps, we quantify the impact of TODO distractions in the codebase and how agents act on them:

Controlling for developer, codebase complexity, task type, model tier, number of files read, initial prompt length, and the step where the bug reference appeared, using a multi-way fixed-effects regression with developer-clustered standard errors, we see that unprompted bug detours add substantial overhead (all stat sig p<0.0001). While in both of these cases they were only trivial to minor detours - they were real distractions costing time, tokens, and human intervention.

As part of addressing this finding at scale we created agents that scrubbed the totality of our codebases, finding bug references in the code that were marked as fixed or obsolete for cleanup. That step alone however was not enough to determine if the TODOs in the codebase were load-bearing or not. The agents had to go further looking at the history of the bug, the file, and changes to the codebase, at times spanning years of history. Only in cases where the agent was able to confirm that the TODO and the intent behind the TODO was obsolete did our agent send a change for review to delete it from the codebase. As a result of this multi-layer approach, our todo cleaner has been able to remove thousands of actually obsolete references over the last 6 months exceeding 95% acceptance from human reviewers.

In recap, as we task agents on more and more ambitious problems, "agent friendly codebases" require more than just the code in the hotpath being healthy. The forgotten corners of the codebase now more than ever carry a significant tax levied against agents.

Tooling like presubmit analyzers, entire corpus scanners, and automated coding agents to remediate dead-todos offer out of the box ready to scale solutions to address the tax enabling a healthier and distraction free codebase for agents to thrive.

Projects that once were on the backburner to cleanup lingering debt but deprioritized due to perceived low value need to be reevaluated with the new agents acting at scale on your codebase.

Livery of the Week: JetBlue’s Florida Panthers Design

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Edited By: Vanni Gibertini

Editor’s Note: AirlineGeeks is proud to present our ‘Livery of the Week’ series. Every Friday, a team member will share an airline livery, which can be from the past, present, or even a special scheme. Some airline liveries are works of art. The complexity associated with painting around critical flight components and the added weight requires outside-the-box thinking from designers. The average airliner can cost upwards of $200,000 to repaint, creating a separate aircraft repainting industry as a result.

Have an idea for a livery that we should highlight? Drop us a line.

Since the beginning of its operations in 2000, New York-based airline JetBlue has always considered South Florida as one of its primary markets, with a presence at Fort Lauderdale-Hollywood Airport that has grown during the past 25 years to include up to 25 aircraft based overnight at the airport and over 2,000 people supporting its operations in Broward County.

As a sign of its commitment to the South Florida community, JetBlue has announced a multi-year extension for an expanded partnership with the Florida Panthers, the NHL’s southernmost franchise that captured back-to-back Stanley Cups in 2023-24 and 2024-25. The partnership will see JetBlue present a newly redesigned Champions Club at the Amerant Bank Arena in Sunrise, Florida, home of the Panthers, and to celebrate the announcement, the airline unveiled a special Panthers-themed livery for one of its Airbus A320 aircraft.

The new livery draws its inspiration from the Panthers’ jersey, therefore sporting the iconic Panthers’ red across the entire fuselage, with the outline of a golden panther all across the central part of the fuselage, right next to JetBlue’s logo. The Panthers’ shield logo is on the aircraft tail in navy and gold.

Bold red letters spelling FLORIDA appear on the bottom of the aircraft, where there’s also a leaping cat, the team’s logo. More symbols of the Panthers and the State of Florida appear in other parts of the fuselage: a simplified Florida flag appears near the front of the aircraft together with a palm tree and a hockey stick, which represent the secondary logo for the team.

“South Florida has been part of JetBlue’s story since our very first flight more than two decades ago, and we’re continuing to build on that history in a big way as Broward County’s largest airline,” said Marty St. George, JetBlue’s president, in a press release. “The Panthers know a thing or two about winning in South Florida, and we’re bringing that same energy as we expand our network, elevate the customer experience and deepen our commitment to the community. ‘Go Cats Go’ is a fun way to celebrate our shared passion for South Florida and team up with a franchise that knows how to make some noise.”

The aircraft chosen to display this special livery is an Airbus A320, registered as N784JB, delivered to the airline on Feb. 11, 2011.

The original name of the aircraft was “Blue And Infinity And Beyond,” which has been changed to “Go Cats Go” following the repainting in this new livery.
