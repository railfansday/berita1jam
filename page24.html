French Senate Rejects Plan to Make Crypto Holders Declare Self-Custody Wallets

France's Senate has rejected a proposal that would have required crypto holders to declare self-custodied wallets worth more than €100,000 ($110,000) to tax authorities, according to the official record of Tuesday's debate on the 2027 budget.

Two identical amendments, one from the Communist-led CRCE-K group and one from a group of senators led by Anne-Sophie Romagny, were voted down after both the government and the Senate's finance committee opposed them. The vote was earlier reported by Cointelegraph.

The amendments would have obliged holders to declare self-custodied crypto above the threshold. French taxpayers already have to declare crypto accounts held on platforms, but not crypto held in their own wallets.

"The shop window is illuminated, but the back room remains in the dark," CRCE-K senator Alexandre Basquin said, in comments translated from French. The more closely platforms are monitored, the greater the incentive for large holders to keep their crypto themselves, he argued.

Minister for Public Action and Accounts David Amiel said the EU's DAC8 directive, which requires crypto service providers to report users' transactions to tax authorities, had already brought transparency. A purely declarative obligation would not let authorities track how crypto is used in money laundering or tax fraud cases, he said.

The proposal had already passed through France's lower house. The National Assembly's finance committee backed it in October, prompting a backlash from the crypto industry.

The budget was later rejected in the lower house, so the Senate is debating the government's original bill, without the crypto amendments that deputies had added.

On Tuesday, senators also rejected an amendment that would have made swaps from crypto into stablecoins a taxable event. Amiel argued that stablecoins are not money, as they have no legal tender status and are often unstable. A proposal to extend France's exit tax to crypto holdings worth more than €800,000 ($877,000) was also defeated.

Critics of the reporting plan had warned that a register of large crypto holders could become a target in France, which has become the global epicenter of "wrench attacks," in which criminals use violence to force victims to hand over crypto.

France's national anti-organized crime prosecutor's office recorded more than 70 crypto-linked kidnappings and unlawful detentions in the first eight months of 2026, Decrypt reported in September. Authorities charged 88 people, including minors, in a crackdown in April.

Leaks of personal data have added to those fears. A breach of France's tax authority in August exposed records tied to more than 678,000 people and businesses. In January, a tax agent was accused of looking up crypto investors and prison guards in tax databases for an unidentified buyer, Le Parisien reported, while a June attack in Nancy followed a data breach at French crypto tax platform Waltio.

Only one club has had a better start to their first Champions League season than Como

Como currently sit in third place in the Champions League table with four victories from their first five games in 2026-27, and only one team has had a better start to their first season in the competition after the Champions League rebrand in 1992.

Como defeated AEK 1-0 at home on Tuesday evening to pick up their fourth win in their first five Champions League matches; Luis Milla providing the only goal of the game.

Cesc Fabregas’s side have also beaten RB Leipzig 4-1 this season, Manchester United 4-1 and Lens 9-1 away from home, the biggest winning margin by an away side in Champions League history. Feyenoord are the only team to have got the better of Como in Europe so far.

Sitting comfortably in third place with 12 points after five matches, and now targeting a top eight and a guaranteed spot in the round of 16, Como have started their first ever season in the Champions League in impressive fashion.

In fact, since 1992, only one team has enjoyed a better start to their first season in the Champions League: That was Leicester City back in 2016-17, off the back of their against-all-odds Premier League victory the season prior.

Under the guidance of Claudio Ranieri, the Foxes won four and drew one of their first five matches in the Champions League, which was enough to secure qualification to the knock-out rounds with one group stage match to spare.

A New Discovery is Re-Drawing the Map of the Milky Way

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

Star formation is a galaxy's foundational process. Astronomers study how star-forming gas moves around in a galaxy, where it's compressed, and in which parts of a galaxy stars form. In a barred spiral galaxy like the Milky Way, star formation is mostly concentrated in the spiral arms. In the central bar, star formation is more sluggish.

Recent research shows that though the central bar contains many stars, star formation there is mostly concentrated in the bar ends. Shear forces, streaming motions, and overpowering shocks that make the gas in the bars turbulent all combine to to inhibit star formation.

The Milky Way is a typical barred spiral galaxy, and astronomers have studied it intently to understand where star formation is concentrated. There's ongoing scientific debate about what happens in a barred spiral galaxy's central bar. Observations of other spiral barred galaxies show that star formation suppression there isn't necessarily universal. Part of the difficulty in understanding our own galaxy is observing the region. Since we're embedded in the galaxy, it's difficult to observe the bar. In many ways, astronomers have an easier time observing other galaxies.

Researchers from Australia and Sweden have found an unusual structure in the Milky Way's center that appears to set it apart from many other barred spiral galaxies. It's a vast collection of rotating stars in the central bar that's generating powerful and large shock fronts in the gas. These shock fronts are triggering star formation in the structure, which goes against the idea that stars can't form there.

The results are in new research titled "The nature of the Norma arm as a bar-driven star-forming shock front," and it's published in Nature Astronomy. The U.S. has proposed new tariffs on 14 partners including China, the European Union, Japan and India, as President Donald Trump doubles down on levies, despite a large chunk of his previous duties being struck down by the Supreme Court.

The targeted economies create, sustain, or contribute to structural excess capacity and production in manufacturing sectors which burden or restrict American commerce, a senior U.S. official said.

The 14 partners have been notified of these actions, with neither country's tariffs exceeding rates announced as part of framework deals on reciprocal trade, the official added.

The administration says the tariffs target what it calls "overcapacity" — excess industrial production, often supported by government subsidies, that allows cheaper goods to flood global markets and undercut U.S. producers.

"The so-called 'overcapacity' does not really exist," China's foreign ministry said on Wednesday before the official reports were released by the U.S. Trade Representative, according to Reuters.

The new levies are being imposed under Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974, which allows Washington to counter foreign trade practices deemed unfair, discriminatory, or burdensome to U.S. commerce.

CNBC earlier reported that the new tariffs would be "country specific," rather than a blanket rate, citing a source familiar with the matter.

Tariffs have been a signature of the Trump administration since the president unveiled sweeping "Liberation Day" duties in April 2025 before pausing them after a sour reaction from bond markets.

In February, the Supreme Court struck down tariffs that Trump had enacted under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act.

Trump used Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974 to impose a 10% global tariff after the court's decision, before replacing them with levies of up to 12.5% on 60 economies under Section 301, alleging forced labor practices.
