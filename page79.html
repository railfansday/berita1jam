The Best Coffee-Table Books to Gift Your Beautiful, Discerning Friends

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Every product on this page was chosen by a Harper's BAZAAR editor. We may earn commission on some of the items you choose to buy.

These books will please everyone on your gift list this year

Every year holidays come around, and a slight panic sets in at the thought of finding gifts that the people you love will get excited about—and that will make them feel seen. The real trick is to keep a list throughout the year of everything you see that reminds you of this person or that person. But because sometimes you don’t write things down because you’re “sure you’ll remember,” and because even if you do, we need all the help we can get in discovering cool things, we’ve put together a list of our favorite coffee-table books to give (and to receive, if we’re being honest) this year.

From fashion monographs to punk rock album covers to a guide to medieval wellness, we promise that we’ve found something that will thrill even your most cynical friend.

Richard Avedon was undoubtedly one of the greatest photographers of the 20th century and this collection gathers his work for Harper’s Bazaar from 1944 to 1965. As the world experienced generation-defining shifts, Avedon was zeroed in on his style, creating indelible images that would go on to change fashion. Bonus? It pairs perfectly with Ron Howard’s documentary on the photographer, also titled simply Avedon.

This massive pink tome, assembled by Coppola herself from her own collection of photographs, scripts, collages, and all manner of feminine detritus, offers a revealing and inspirational look at the director’s process and world-building.

With wit and humor, medievalist Larisa Grollemond sheds light on the way humans lived—and thrived(?)—during the middle ages. Is it true they never bathed? Did they have practice birth control? Were witches in fact, doctors? This books holds the answers.

Raymond Pettibon may now be a certified blue-chip artist, but he began his career in the 1970s drawing flyers and record covers for punk bands in Southen California like Minutemen and Black Flag. (He also designed their iconic four-bar logo, which has now been meme-fied.) This book collects his musical body of work, which also includes the Foo Fighters and Lana del Rey—that’s his work on the cover of Norman Fucking Rockwell.

The elusive Martin Margiela may very well be the designer that has most influenced contemporary fashion. Nowhere is that more evident than in this book that looks at the time when the Belgian designer was at the helm of Hermés. The enduring appeal of the collections he created from 1997 to 2002 would go on to set the tone for the whole quiet luxury moment we’ve been living under—except better.

Jeff Goodwin was the makeup artist for David Lynch’s iconic film Blue Velvet. His personal trove of Polaroid photos capture impromptu behind-the-scenes moments and reveal how he refined the makeup looks; they’re accompanied by his own memories of the filming experience.

How many people’s fashion obsessions began as they endlessly dressed and undressed their dolls—be they Barbie, Bratz, or even American Girl dolls? This tome looks at the symbiotic relationship between dolls and real-life grown-up fashion, zeroing in on the works of designers like Comme des Garçons’ Rei Kawakubo and Viktor & Rolf’s Viktor Horsting and Rolf Snoeren, among others.

This is a book designed to inspire. For “Kitchen Piece,” Ono suggests one “hangs a canvas on a wall” and “throws all the leftovers you have” on it, with the caveat that “you may prepare special food for the piece.” After spending time with Ono’s instructions, one realizes there is art to be found literally everywhere.

This is the only book about Carolyn Bessette Kennedy that one may ever need. Sunita Kumar Nair talks to CBK’s friends and, through a carefully curated edit of images, breaks down her style. She also reveals the true essence of the person behind all those infinitely rebloggable images.

Past Continuous is the name of an exhibit at the Metropolitan Museum of Art in New York that brings together the world of Lee Krasner and her husband Jackson Pollock and looks at their career arcs in tandem. It begins with a selection of their paintings before the pair met, and traces their influences, taking a closer look at how their presences wove in and out of each other’s work.

This tome celebrates America’s 250th anniversary through over 200 pages filled with iconic images that have defined the country throughout its history, from images of cowboys wearing their worn-in Levi’s and immigrants landing at Ellis Island to Martin Luther King and Elvis Presley.

Diane Arbus’s unflinching photographs captured humanity in all its beautiful and exacting rawness. Documents tells the history of her short-lived career through essays, criticism, and other ephemera from 1967 through the present day; it’s a comprehensive guide to understanding her work.

Glen Luchford photographed a few of Prada’s most iconic ad campaigns of the 1990s. This book brings them all together along with alternate shots and test shots, and is an essential for both Prada aficionados and lovers of the decade.

Created in 1909 by Arthur E. Waite and Pamela Colman Smith in London, the appropriately titled Waite Smith tarot deck is the most popular deck in the world. This book looks at its history, uncovering the details and meaning of each individual card—and it comes with its own set of cards.

This year, the Fashion Museum Antwerp is celebrating its 40th anniversary with an exhibition dedicated to the “Antwerp Six,” the name the fashion media gave to a group of designers—Dries van Noten, Ann Demeulemeeter, Marina Yee, Dirk Bikkembergs, Walter Van Beirendonck, and Dirk Van Seene—that arrived in London in the mid ’80s fresh after their graduation from the Royal Academy of Fine Art. For those who can’t make it to Belgium to see the show, the book may prove to be the next best thing, chock full of photographs, illustrations, newspaper clippings, and essays. They may not have all become household names, but their influence can still be felt on the runways today.

Brianna Capozzi’s unique view of womanhood has made her one of the most sought-after photographers in fashion. Her latest monograph features a mix of magazine shots of famous singers and actors with images of impromptu shoots staged with her best friends. The result is a visual shot of adrenaline and a reminder that girls—well, women—just wanna have fun!

Beer With a Painter: Ouattara Watts

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

The Ivorian-born painter works only at night, when the veil with the spiritual realm is thinnest and the outside voices of the physical world are quietest.

Last month, just a couple of weeks before Ouattara Watts’s solo show opened at Karma, I was directed to knock on the cracked glass door of his Bushwick studio. We met in the late afternoon, which is when his studio time begins. The Ivorian-American artist has coffee and a light evening meal, and works all night, before returning home to sleep during the day. To learn why Watts works only at night, one need only look at his paintings. He views art as a portal to the divine; night is when the veil with the spiritual realm is thinnest, and the outside voices of the physical world are quietest.

Although a gallery representative had told me Watts would be putting “finishing touches” on the paintings for his exhibition at the time of our meeting, I came to realize that he thinks about the work, and his process, without a beginning or end. The centrifugal energy of his rhythmic compositions does not obey edges. Totemic forms continue beyond the bounds of the canvases; arrows point in varying directions; seed pods are split open to reveal concentric circles. His compositions are stained and dripped fields populated by a lexicon of imagery and symbols, developed over the past four decades. They include coffee beans, sacred books, musical notes, serpents, bursting petals encased in atoms, diagrammatic cubes, and mathematical codes.

Juiced Scrambler Mini launched as a cooler (and safer) e-bike option for teens and parents

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Finding an electric bike for a teenager can be surprisingly tricky. Parents generally want something safe, manageable, and from a reputable company. Teenagers, meanwhile, generally want something that doesn’t look like it was picked out by their parents.

The newly launched Juiced Scrambler Mini looks like it could do a pretty good job of threading that needle.

The new youth-sized e-bike takes the mini-motorcycle styling of Juiced’s larger Scrambler models and shrinks it down around 16-inch fat tires, creating a bike designed for riders between 4’0″ and 5’3″ (122 to 160 cm). But perhaps more importantly for parents, Juiced also built in a PIN-protected speed limiter known as Speed Lock that lets adults decide just how fast the bike can go.

The Scrambler Mini ships set to a 10 mph (16 km/h) Beginner Mode, but its maximum speed can be adjusted anywhere from 1-20 mph (32 km/h) and then locked behind a four-digit PIN. Juiced notes that riders aged 8-12 should not exceed 10 mph, while riders 13 and older can use the bike at speeds up to 20 mph, subject of course to local laws and regulations. In almost every state and jurisdiction though, the bike will fit Class 2 designation for street-legal riding and bike lane operation.

That setup seems like a pretty smart way to approach youth e-bikes. Instead of buying a slow bike that a teenager may quickly outgrow – or handing them a much faster machine before a parent thinks they’re ready – the same bike can essentially grow with the rider (with speed titrated by a parent or guardian).

And from the kid’s perspective, it certainly doesn’t look like a safety-first children’s bike.

The Scrambler Mini gets the same sort of moto-inspired styling that has defined Juiced’s Scrambler lineup, including a long bench seat, dual-crown-style fork, chunky 16 x 4-inch tires, and several appropriately loud color options including Blackberry, Fruit Punch, Blueberry, and Pink Lemonade.

There’s some decent hardware underneath that styling, too. The rear hub motor is rated at 500W nominal and 1,092W peak with 80 Nm of torque. A removable 48V 10Ah battery using LG cells provides 480Wh of capacity, with Juiced claiming up to 40 miles (64 km) of range. Four levels of cadence-based pedal assist are included, as is a right-side thumb throttle.

The bike also features Talon hydraulic disc brakes with large 180mm rotors front and rear, 50mm of front suspension travel, an integrated backlit LCD, a headlight, and a rear taillight with brake-light functionality. The single-speed bike weighs 59 lb (26.8 kg), has a 27.15-inch seat height, and is rated for up to 275 lb (125 kg) of payload.

Those parts are far above what we normally see on e-bikes targeted towards teens, with most teen-focused e-bikes sporting cheaper components like mechanical brakes and rigid forks.

The Scrambler Mini is listed at $1,099, though Juiced is currently offering a Black Friday sale that drops the price by another $150 to just $949. The company says preorders are expected to begin shipping the week of December 7.

That price also comes with something increasingly important in the crowded youth e-bike market: an actual company standing behind the bike. Juiced was founded in 2009, quickly becoming a leader in the market for powerful and stylish e-bikes. But after the brand’s financial troubles a few years ago, Lectric eBikes stepped in to take Juiced under its wing in 2025. The relaunched Juiced now offers a three-year manufacturer warranty and US-based support, plus the peace of mind that comes with backing from North America’s largest e-bike company, Lectric eBikes.

That last part may be just as appealing to parents as the speed limiter, though the 3-year warranty is definitely a deal sweetener, too.

There’s always going to be some controversy around the idea of e-bikes marketed towards teens, but I’m for it when done responsibly. Let’s face it: e-bikes are incredibly popular and teens are already going to be riding them, even if it means borrowing a friend’s e-bike because they don’t have their own. So if they’re going to be riding, the least we can do is put them on a safer, higher-quality bike so they learn the right skills in the right environment. And hey, it sure beats teens sitting alone in their rooms texting each other all afternoon.

When it comes to choosing an e-bike, there are already plenty of cheap mini e-bikes and electric dirt bike-style machines floating around online, and many are undoubtedly going to look tempting to teenagers. The Scrambler Mini seems to take a different approach, giving young riders the styling, fat tires, throttle, and mini-moto attitude they actually want, while giving parents control over how much of that performance they can access.

It’s still an e-bike capable of 20 mph, and that means parents need to make decisions about where, when, and how their kids ride it. And there’s even an argument to be made for removing the throttle and turning it into a Class 1 e-bike in the beginning, requiring a teen to pedal it to engage the motor. But I’d much rather see that conversation happening around a bike with hydraulic brakes, lights, a programmable speed limit, and a real company standing behind it than around some anonymous electric mini-bike bought from the depths of the internet.

And if the teenager thinks it looks cool enough to actually ride, that’s probably the point.

Football Association chair Debbie Hewitt is set to inspect documentation in person regarding Fifa’s deeply contentious World Cup private investment proposal, it is understood.

The global governing body dispatched a letter on 2 November to members of the Fifa Council – where Hewitt serves as a Fifa vice-president – offering them the opportunity to examine the files at its headquarters in Zurich.

Hewitt is believed to have taken up the offer, though a firm date for the trip remains unconfirmed. Uefa president Aleksander Ceferin is likewise thought to have agreed to attend.

Relations between Uefa and Fifa president Gianni Infantino broke down over the project, which involved creating a new entity named Fifa Forward Enterprise (FFE) to manage commercial and operational activities for both the men’s and women’s World Cups alongside other Fifa tournaments.

Under the blueprint, a minority shareholding in FFE was to be acquired by private investors, led by Thrive Capital, an entity established by Joshua Kushner, whose brother is the son-in-law of United States President Donald Trump.

The initiative was ultimately scrapped following opposition from Uefa and a pair of other continental confederations.

Hewitt’s career experience in private equity provides her with direct insight into the standard documentation and financial details expected in proposals of this nature.

Fifa issued the invitation after European representatives on the Fifa Council sent a formal letter on 19 October requesting clarity on several matters, including the FFE arrangement and disciplinary proceedings involving Folarin Balogun.

Provisions regarding non-disclosure agreements and the specific terms governing document inspection in Zurich have yet to be finalised.

Earlier on Tuesday, the French Football Federation formally endorsed Philippe Diallo’s bid for the Fifa presidency.

On Monday, Uefa confirmed that the 63-year-old had secured the support of national association delegates across its executive committee and the Fifa Council to challenge Infantino.

Disquiet over the FFE proposals prompted European football’s governing body to promise in August that it would put forward a credible challenger to Infantino.

Diallo’s domestic federation has now unanimously backed his campaign, stating: "World football is going through a pivotal period in its history. The challenges affecting its governance demand clear thinking and responsibility.

"Restoring trust, preserving the universality of football, rebuilding Fifa’s credibility, and placing the development of football in all regions of the world at the heart of its actions are essential issues for the entire international football community.

"By accepting this invitation, Philippe Diallo is responding to a desire to open a new chapter for the governance of world football."

Figures within Uefa had previously hoped that former Arsenal manager Arsene Wenger, who currently serves as Fifa’s chief of global football development, might enter the race, though he is believed to have declined the prospect last week.
