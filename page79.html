Spot The Papaya: Etihad’s McLaren F1-Liveried Boeing 787 Hits The Skies

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Etihad Airways has revealed a stunning new livery for one of its Boeing 787-10 Dreamliner widebody twinjets. The Abu Dhabi-based UAE flag carrier has chosen to paint the aircraft in a distinctive orange and black color scheme, with the bulk of the jet sporting the famous 'papaya' shade worn by the McLaren Racing Formula One Team. The livery was teased this year as Etihad and McLaren teamed up.

Etihad unveiled the new livery on the Boeing 787-10 Dreamliner, which bears the registration A6-BMI, at a glamorous event at its engineering facilities in Abu Dhabi. As well as celebrating the first time that a widebody jet has featured an F1 team's livery, the event also played host to a successful Guinness World Record attempt for the largest single-piece kabuki reveal. The jet re-enters service today.

The McLaren name is one of the oldest and most iconic in F1, and, over the years, its cars have worn a wide range of legendary liveries. Most recently, however, the team's name has been associated with the striking 'papaya' shade of orange, which it has worn since 2017, including in McLaren driver Lando Norris's 2025 championship-winning season. Now, Etihad's Boeing 787-10 shares this color.

Etihad describes the orange and black paint job as "one of the most prominent motorsport liveries ever carried by a commercial aircraft and a new way for guests and fans to connect with McLaren Racing." With its 787s serving a wide range of destinations all around the world, there will certainly be no shortage of attention for the striking orange scheme. Antonoaldo Neves, Etihad Airways' CEO, said that:

"Etihad and McLaren Racing are two ambitious global brands with a shared focus on performance and a drive to keep moving forward. This aircraft will proudly carry that story from our home in Abu Dhabi to cities around the world."

While the recent reveal represented the first time that Etihad's new Dreamliner livery saw the light of day, it was actually teased by the Abu Dhabi-based UAE flag carrier earlier this year, more than eight months ago. At the end of February 2026, the airline announced that it had become an official partner of McLaren Racing, supporting both its Formula One and United Autosports WEC Hypercar teams.

With this in mind, in its role as an Official Partner of McLaren Racing, Etihad's logo is emblazoned all over the team's cars and helmets, such as on the MCL40’s rear wing and halo. The carrier has long had close ties to Formula One, having been the title sponsor of the Abu Dhabi Grand Prix since 2009. However, putting pen to paper on a deal to become an Official Partner of McLaren shifted its role up a gear.

On this front, McLaren Racing CEO Zak Brown welcomed the livery reveal as the latest development in his team's work with Etihad Airways, saying that "it's fantastic to see our partnership with Etihad Airways brought to life in such a unique way." He added that "the livery brings together two iconic global brands and will connect McLaren Racing and Etihad with fans and customers around the world."

The Abu Dhabi-based carrier will also be launching a special livery Boeing 787.

According to Flightradar24, A6-BMI spent around a month on the ground as it received its new paint job. As seen in the animated route map above, flight tracking data shows that its last pre-repaint flight took place on October 11, as the Boeing 787-10 flew from Jakarta Soekarno Hatta International Airport (CGK) in Indonesia back to Abu Dhabi International Airport (AUH) in the UAE as flight EY475.

Why Bloom Energy Stock Slumped 26.6% in October, and Why the Pullback is a Buying Opportunity

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Hyperscalers and data center operators are rushing for Bloom Energy's fuel cells.

Bloom Energy (BE +11.91%) posted the best quarter in its history, but the stock still lost 26.6% of its value in October, according to data provided by S&P Global Market Intelligence.

Bloom Energy makes fuel cells that can generate power on-site at facilities such as artificial intelligence (AI) data centers without waiting years for the grid. Even though its core technology is fundamentally solid, a mix of project hiccups, cash burn, and macro jitters dragged the stock down.

One of the biggest company-specific overhangs was Project Jupiter, Oracle's (ORCL +3.18%) roughly 2.45-gigawatt AI campus in New Mexico, designed to run on Bloom fuel cells.

The project's gas supply depended on a 17-mile Energy Transfer pipeline whose rights-of-way across state trust land had been repeatedly denied, pushing its targeted in-service date from August 2026 to February 2027. Oracle issued a force-majeure notice in September. Without the pipeline, Bloom's fuel cell servers cannot operate.

In October, Bloomberg reported that Oracle was trucking natural gas to some data centers while it waited for pipelines, and considering the same approach at Project Jupiter. Investors already knew about the permitting delay, and this new development was a reminder Bloom's growth opportunity still depended on infrastructure arriving on schedule.

Oracle later confirmed Bloom's fuel cells remain the primary power source for Project Jupiter. But that reassurance did little to offset a broader sell-off in AI-power and data-center stocks.

The slide happened even as business fundamentals looked incredible on paper. During its late-October earnings call, Bloom Energy reported a jaw-dropping 138% year-over-year jump in third-quarter revenue to a record $1.2 billion.

Adjusted operating margin jumped to 23.1% from 8.9%, with the company earning more in operating profits in one single quarter than it did in all of 2025.

Management also raised full-year revenue outlook to $4.5 billion, or about 122% growth.

Bloom Energy stock still tumbled more than 10% after earnings, partly because the company posted negative free cash flow of $181 million in the quarter. With a backlog growing faster than revenue, the company is spending heavily to expand capacity. That's a good problem to have.

Investors began to reevaluate high-flying AI stocks, including infrastructure plays, following political shifts and midterm election cycles. When market sentiment turns defensive, richly valued stocks are often the first to be heavily trimmed, regardless of how strong their long-term narrative looks.

That's what happened with Bloom Energy. There's no demand or fundamental problem here. Customers continue to sign up. This month, NTT DATA agreed to deploy 900 megawatts of Bloom Energy's fuel cells across data centers worldwide, with shipments starting in 2027. The companies called it Bloom's largest international project yet.For long-term investors, Bloom Energy's business looks stronger than the near-term stock price chart suggests. I'm convinced this stock is a buy-and-hold at current prices.

Big Walk developer House House reveal genAI use in final stretch of development, only revealing it now as "it has become a matter of public interest"

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Friendslop du jour Big Walk is the latest in an unfortunately long series of games that have been confirmed to use generative AI in their production, developers House House clarifying their use of it in a statement to Aftermath (thanks!). House House state that "LLMs were used sparingly by House House to help work through difficult technical challenges and bugs", and that "well under 0.1% of Big Walk’s code had anything to do with this usage", but are quick to note that "generative AI was not used in any other context when making the game."

A specific example is given in the case of Unity's apparent weirdness around shadow casters in its rendering pipeline, as it's seemingly difficult to have unnecessary shadow casters behind the player culled, which resulted in the game running at half speed around sunrise and sunset. House House explain that they couldn't find a solution themselves, asked a Unity engineer for help who said it was impossible, and then decided the only answer was to ask an LLM chatbot; "after a few dead ends, it suggested a possible fix, and while we had to modify the suggestion extensively, it worked. This fix represented about 10 lines of code." While absolutely not the most pressing thing to mention here, it is very funny to me for House House to clarify that they "had to modify the suggestion extensively", almost as if this tech isn't actually helpful in giving useful answers.

So, House House touched the stove, despite how easy it is to simply not touch the stove. The thing is, this wasn't a case of being forced to by upper management, as is sometimes the case. This was a choice made entirely voluntarily, and House House are attempting to have their cake and eat it by ending their statement with the reflection that "We have some concern that voluntary disclosures like this might contribute to the normalisation of this technology, or its treatment as “inevitable”. We support the work of those who are trying to limit the harm generative AI is doing to videogames and our world, and apologise that Big Walk’s LLM usage may not meet their standards." But then, you know, that's followed up by saying that Big Walk was developed "without an internal AI usage policy in place, and we consider that lack of policy a failure. We will work to have a more comprehensive policy going forwards." So, not to worry, they learned touching the stove is bad by touching the stove, and definitely won't do it again in future. If only there were thousands of other cases informing them why touching the stove is bad.

Very relevant to this reveal is the recent statement by select jurors of the Independent Games Festival which, after much internal deliberation, came to the conclusion that those who sign the statement would decline to vote for any game that uses generative AI in any capacity, with signees including Austin Walker, Sam Barlow, Lena Reine, Brendon Chung, Aura Triolo, aurahack and more. The statement specifies that "AI-generated code is not an exception to these harms." Fellow juror Liz Ryerson confirmed that Big Walk was being considered by the IGF, and was a matter of discussion in judging as to whether its use of genAI would be acceptable.

As for House House only disclosing this now, they state it was not only because it was necessary for their IGF submission, but also that "we have not made any disclosure on Steam as the platform does not request disclosure of code or chatbot usage", and that they have spoken on it publicly to Aftermath because "it has become a matter of public interest."

If I were to be cynical, I would say that House House would prefer to be the ones breaking this news themselves rather than having the IGF reveal that they had been hiding their use up until now; it's usually easier to control PR if you release it yourself. Big Walk had already been securely in everyone's good books for its support for the BDS boycott, for example, so why ruin that clean rep with something like a genAI disclosure?

I don't know how better to close this article than with an excerpt from that very IGF statement:

"We know that change does not come at the individual level, but as independent developers, we also know firsthand the value of pushing back on what an industry has deemed "inevitable." As jurists, we feel a responsibility to push past defeatism and aspire to a higher ideal.

The future is not yet settled. You are not alone. We can stand together."

FDA Initiates Labeling Changes for GLP-1s Due to Rare But Serious Eye Disease Risk

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Follow Healthline on our socials for all things health.

GLP-1 users have a new symptom to watch for: Sudden vision loss in one eye.

The Food and Drug Administration (FDA) announced on November 9 that it will require drugmakers to add information to the warning labels of Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, Zepbound and other GLP-1 medications about “a potential increased risk” of a rare eye condition called nonarteritic anterior ischemic optic neuropathy (NAION).GLP-1 medications are approved to treat type 2 diabetes and to help people lose weight and keep it off.NAION joins a growing list of side effects linked to GLP-1s, adding a second eye warning to several labels.The U.S. labels for Ozempic, Rybelsus, Trulicity, Mounjaro, and Zepbound already warn about diabetic retinopathy, a diabetes-related eye disease, according to each drug’s prescribing information.The two conditions affect different parts of the eye. Diabetic retinopathy damages tiny blood vessels in the retina, the layer at the back of the eye that senses light, according to the National Eye Institute. NAION damages the optic nerve when it doesn’t get enough blood, according to the FDA’s announcement.Mihail Zilbermint, MD, director of endocrine hospitalists at Johns Hopkins, told Healthline he hasn’t seen NAION in his patients. He has seen vision worsen in some people after they start a GLP-1.“But we believe that’s probably related to rapid improvement in their sugar control, which can also be seen in someone who is starting insulin,” Zilbermint said.

A 2024 study published in JAMA Ophthalmology “initially triggered” the FDA’s review, the agency told Healthline.

The study found a higher risk of NAION in people prescribed semaglutide, the active ingredient in Ozempic and Wegovy, than in people prescribed other diabetes or weight-loss drugs.The authors wrote that more research is needed to determine whether semaglutide causes NAION.Joseph F. Rizzo III, MD, director of the Neuro-Ophthalmology Service at Mass Eye and Ear in Boston, served as the study’s corresponding author.

In a 2024 podcast, Rizzo said he and a resident saw three patients with NAION who were taking semaglutide for about a week.“And because NAION is a fairly uncommon disorder in the general population, the fact that we had three within a relatively short period of time led me to suspect that there was a higher risk of developing NAION in patients on semaglutide,” Rizzo said. The FDA analyzed clinical trials for multiple GLP-1 drugs and noted that NAION cases were reported with every GLP-1 on the market, according to its announcement. Those findings led the FDA to require the warning for the entire drug class, the agency told Healthline.

No treatment has been proven to reverse vision loss from NAION, according to StatPearls.

“Patients experiencing sudden, painless vision loss in one eye should contact their healthcare professional immediately and should not stop their medication without consulting their provider first,” the FDA told Healthline.

In the announcement, the agency told doctors to promptly refer patients with suspected NAION to an ophthalmologist.

The Airlines With The World's Newest Premium Economy Seats In 2026

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Around the world, premium economy is growing and more airlines are expected to increase their capacity in this class, although it can take years for an airline to fully transition to a new cabin type. Broadly speaking, premium economy is confined to widebody aircraft assigned to longer-haul routes.

However, there are exceptions like American Airlines' transcontinental A321XLRs and Emirates' widebodies that are used for short-haul routes. Widebodies generally fall into three categories as far as rolling out a new seating class is concerned. One category is new aircraft being delivered, which are typically received from the manufacturer with the latest airline cabins.

Another group of aircraft is those already in service with years of service ahead of them. These are retrofitted as they are cycled through their scheduled maintenance. Finally, there are older aircraft in service that are scheduled to retire that an airline may not bother retrofitting.

Originally, all commercial fares were effectively first class, as flying was only something the elite could afford to do. Over time, economy class emerged, and then in the 1970s, business class emerged as a bridge between first and economy. For many business class passengers, first class was excessively expensive, while economy was not befitting of business representatives on business trips.

The class also introduced the notion of working on the aircraft. As air travel became mainstream and everyone could fly, flying lost its prestige status symbol. Private jets may be the domain of the wealthy, but not commercial flying, and no longer did passengers dress up for the occasion and treat it as something of a first-class voyage.

Without the status of the past, the first class became seen as excessively expensive, while also being squeezed by an improving business class. This has led to the decline of first class around the world. Meanwhile, pressures mounted in two directions. One was the increased demand for ultra-cheap and utilitarian flights, which gave rise to low-cost and ultra-low-cost carriers.

Another was the demand for a class better than economy, but not as prohibitively expensive as business class. This led to the rise of premium economy as a 'sweet spot' between affordability and comfort. It fits today's mainline market, where flying needs to be comfortable and affordable, but not overly lavish.

Premium economy is the youngest of the four classes, and many airlines were reluctant to introduce it. Some airlines saw it as a threat that would undermine their profitable business class, as premium economy works if it gets economy-class passengers to upgrade, but it is counterproductive if it convinces business-class passengers to downgrade.

Some mainline carriers, like Emirates, were also concerned about how it would affect their brand image as a luxury mainline airline. Now, however, that dam has burst, and airlines are rushing to retrofit their aircraft with premium economy. Emirates CEO Tim Clark said that "our Premium Economy product was carefully developed in keeping with Emirates’ brand positioning as a full-service airline of the highest quality."

The force is understood to be using both Kraken’s K3 Scout, and the SM400 from SYOS Aerospace, known by the Royal Navy as the Rattler.

The K3 Scout is an 8.4 m boat capable of 55 knots and can carry a payload of up to 600 kg. It has a range of up to 650 nautical miles and  endurance of up to 7 days. The maker describes it as capable of a range of missions, including anti-ship and anti-submarine to detecting and countering other drones and even casualty evacuation.

The Rattler boat is 7.2 m and has a 500 kg modular bay, with a range of up to 950 nautical miles. It was used last year in a demonstration during which 5 of the uncrewed boats were remotely-piloted to escort patrol ship HMS Tyne and mine countermeasures mothership HMS Stirling Castle

Part of the new squadron’s mission will be to act as pathfinder for drone operations, working on bringing additional types of uncrewed surface vessel (USVs) into fleet service and conducting a range of operations alongside other organizations – both as single vessels and as part of a task group.

The “Hybrid Navy” concept was outlined by First Sea Lord General Sir Gwyn Jenkins earlier this year, who made the case that uncrewed vessels would be vital in filling the gaps left by the Royal Navy’s much depleted surface fleet.

Speaking at the launch of the autonomous squadron, Fleet Commander Vice-Admiral Steve Moorhouse hailed it as an historic occasion, but warned that the seas are becoming more contested, more dangerous and harder to operate in.

“A hybrid navy is about giving our people better tools. It means more reach, more striking power and more options,” he stated.

Last week, the force also launched strike drones from one of its aircraft carriers for the first time. Nyan one-way drones made by BAE Systems were launched from the deck, as well as a Tekever AR5 fixed-wing uncrewed aircraft, the same kind the British Army has selected to replace its troublesome Watchkeeper fleet.

Elsewhere, the Ministry of Defence (MoD) says it has launched a British-built satellite called Titania into orbit, claiming it “gives the Armed Forces a new pair of eyes in space.” However, the original purpose of the orbiter was understood to be to test high-speed optical communications from low Earth orbit directly to Earth, using laser beams that are harder to intercept than radio links.

Titania is the second satellite in the UK’s growing constellation of military satellites in orbit, following the launch of Tyche in October, and will be operated by UK Space Command as part of the ISTARI* program.

The latter aims to deliver satellites and supporting ground systems to expand space-based intelligence, surveillance and reconnaissance, the MoD said.

However, while the satellite is British-made, it got into space via an American rocket, riding on SpaceX’s Transporter-18 mission. ®
