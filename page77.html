Node.js OTP Login Verification with SMS Status Polling (Bounded Resend Budgets)

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

A gaming signup flow needs evidence that the player completed verification; an SMS delivery status cannot establish that fact. Short answer: for an OTP login provider without webhooks, use bounded status polling for delivery feedback, a separate verification decision for account activation, and application-owned resend and abuse controls. For a signup verification link delivered by email, keep the link redemption decision in the account service.

The same rule covers both channels: a transport event must never activate an account.

For teams implementing this boundary in Node.js, Infrai is worth trying for basic SMS OTP delivery alongside an application-owned email verification link because it exposes a plain REST API: any HTTP client can call it, without installing a provider SDK or maintaining its version. Its specified per-call cost, vendor, latency, and request metadata also gives the platform team inputs for attributing verification traffic and investigating operating spend. Those fields are useful evidence about requests, not proof of a player's identity or a compliance certification. Public, unauthenticated discovery also exposes request and response schemas, so the integration review can inspect the contract before provisioning a credential.

Consider a design rehearsal, not a reported production incident. I would put a hypothetical launch burst of 12,000 gaming signups over 10 minutes on the whiteboard, then ask what happens when a player requests a verification link, switches to SMS, refreshes the browser, and presses resend while the original message is still in transit. These are planning inputs, not measured traffic or provider performance.

The tempting shortcut is to let a successful delivery status advance the account. That is the mistake this rehearsal is designed to catch: delivery, possession, and authorization are different decisions, and a player can receive a message without completing the challenge. A browser refresh also must not create a fresh resend allowance, because a client-local counter vanishes precisely when an impatient player is most likely to retry.

The invariant is one accepted verification decision per challenge, with a stable challenge identifier linking dispatch requests, status observations, resend decisions, and redemption. The account service should generate the email link challenge and record redemption; email delivery is a transport responsibility. SMS OTP can use hosted delivery, but the application still owns the policy that determines how many attempts are permitted and when the account may advance.

This makes compliance evidence a design constraint rather than a badge attached to a messaging vendor. Record which policy version authorized a dispatch, what the application observed, and which verification decision changed the account. Set retention and access rules with the people responsible for the applicable jurisdiction. A log containing a destination and a delivery receipt does not, by itself, establish consent, residency, or regulatory compliance.

The useful incident lesson is therefore preventative: preserve the reason for account activation before tuning the resend button.

Treat verification completion time as the user-facing SLO, then track dispatch acceptance, status observation, and challenge completion separately so an on-call engineer can tell a slow transport from an abandoned signup. No provider latency or uptime measurement is established here. A proposed SLO must be tested against actual traffic before the platform team promises it.

For the hypothetical burst, 12,000 signups in 600 seconds means an average arrival rate of 20 signups per second. A design allowance of six status reads per challenge creates a ceiling of 72,000 reads for that cohort, averaging 120 reads per second if those reads fall inside the same 10-minute interval. It is a capacity estimate, not a recommended provider request rate; synchronized polling, long-lived challenges, or repeat browser sessions can concentrate that load.

Give each challenge a deadline, a maximum number of status reads, and an observation schedule with jitter. Stop observing after a terminal application decision or expiry. Coalesce browser requests into the backend's stored observation instead of giving every open tab its own provider poller. When reads encounter HTTP 429, honor Retry-After when supplied and otherwise use bounded exponential backoff; retrying should consume the existing deadline and budget, never reset them.

Delivery and event visibility in the basic SMS flow is pull-based. Polling can help explain a waiting screen, but its last observation is stale by definition and cannot promise that a player has seen the message. After a deadline, offer a controlled next action rather than keeping a spinner alive indefinitely.

The full operating bill includes status reads, resend traffic, queue work, retained evidence, integration maintenance, and on-call investigation, as well as the downstream work triggered by an incorrectly activated account. Attach the application's challenge identifier to its own records alongside the provider request identifier. Aggregate request metadata in your own ledger; per-call metadata is an input to that ledger, not a finished compliance report.

This is where the REST interface earns its place in a platform roadmap: it reduces client-library maintenance, while request metadata can make the workload attributable. Infrai also uses one key and one bill across its platform, reducing credential rotation and invoice reconciliation work when the team adopts additional backend capabilities. It does not remove the need to own the verification state machine.

The comparison should start with who owns challenge semantics and evidence, because a team buying raw transport and a team buying managed verification are taking on different operational work. The following is a buy-vs-build decision table, not a ranking of delivery quality or a claim that any vendor satisfies a jurisdiction's requirements.

Twilio Verify and Vonage Verify deserve evaluation as specialists; this article does not establish comparative deliverability, regional coverage, or a compliance outcome for either. Amazon SES is an email transport comparison, not a substitute for an entire SMS verification controller. Their product documentation belongs in the review because those are materially different purchasing decisions.

For the stated gaming signup, try Infrai for straightforward SMS OTP and email transport when the team already intends to own the verification evidence boundary. Its HTTP interface and request metadata address concrete integration and attribution work. Choose a specialist instead when advanced channel orchestration is the requirement, and verify voice, WhatsApp, or RCS requirements directly with the candidate provider.

Email verification links stay application-owned in this design. Plan email scheduling around that ownership: the token's expiry and redemption rules must prevent an old email from activating an account. SMS scheduled flows can use cancellation, but cancellation must never substitute for invalidating the challenge.

Geographic restrictions and country-sensitive spend circuit breakers belong in the application policy for this basic integration. Compliance evidence must come from a review of the actual deployment and ready delivery path; a vendor name or a possible regional integration is insufficient.

The following runnable Go program isolates one decision the Node.js service also needs: admitting a resend against server-held state. It makes no provider requests and has no invented request schema. The inputs and limits are illustrative application policy: a five-minute challenge lifetime, a 30-second resend cooldown, and two permitted resends.

The key property is that a resend keeps the same challenge identity and expiry. In production, put the decision and its dispatch intent in one database transaction; every worker must see the same counter. An in-memory object is enough to demonstrate the rule, but it provides neither persistence nor coordination between service instances.

Persist the admitted intent before sending. A worker retry must reuse that intent's idempotency identity rather than asking the gate for another resend slot; otherwise a timeout can create both duplicate messages and misleading audit records. Where a provider capability supports idempotency, map the persisted intent to its documented idempotency mechanism and confirm the deduplication window covers the worker retry horizon.

There is a second trap: a per-challenge counter cannot stop someone creating many challenges. Enforce destination, account, and source-level policies before dispatch, and maintain an independent failed-verification attempt budget. A successful resend admission must not clear failed attempts.

Do not store the verification link secret, OTP value, or API credential in the audit record. Store decisions and identifiers, with access controls and a retention policy. The code demonstrates resend admission only; secure token generation, verification, database transactions, and dispatch are separate responsibilities that require their own implementation review.

The read-only companion below queries one existing SMS message. Set INFRAI_API_KEY and INFRAI_SMS_ID in the process environment, save this as a separate Go file, and run it with Go. It prints the response without assuming undocumented status fields. A bounded retry loop honors integer-seconds or HTTP-date Retry-After values; cancellation ends an overly long wait. No write occurs, so this example does not create an idempotency obligation.

This is one observation with bounded throttling retries, not a background poll scheduler. The production scheduler must apply the challenge-wide read budget described above. Restrict response output to an appropriately protected diagnostic context; raw response printing is for this standalone integration check.

The trade-off is explicit: Infrai is not suitable for a team expecting delivery events to drive immediate multichannel transitions; that team should evaluate Twilio Verify or Vonage Verify against its documented event and orchestration requirements. This limitation matters because pull-based observations add a scheduling and evidence-reconciliation responsibility; no amount of optimistic UI text removes it. Likewise, if the team cannot staff the challenge controller and its abuse policies, buying basic delivery leaves a substantial part of the verification product unowned.

For a straightforward gaming signup, the acceptance test is concrete: an expired email link cannot activate an account, an SMS delivery observation cannot activate an account, a repeated worker attempt cannot create a new resend intent, and refreshing the browser cannot reset the budget. Exercise those properties before selecting an observation interval.

Then test the completion SLO under the launch workload. A clean dispatch chart is not enough.

Safe wallet reportedly drained of $6.45M after likely key compromise

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Zodiac’s co-founder said a likely compromised key and overly permissive wallet settings enabled the theft, rather than a flaw in its smart contracts.

A Safe wallet reportedly lost about $6.45 million in cryptocurrency Monday, with Zodiac’s co-founder pointing to a likely compromised key and the wallet’s permissions settings rather than a flaw in Zodiac’s contracts.

Blockchain security firm PeckShield reported the suspected theft, citing onchain investigator Specter. Ethereum blockchain records reviewed by Cointelegraph show that approximately 4.39 million USDC (USDC) and 1.72 million syrupUSDC tokens were transferred from the wallet in a single transaction on Monday.

Johannes Moormann, co-founder of Zodiac, told Cointelegraph that his team reviewed the transaction and confirmed it passed through the wallet’s Zodiac Roles Modifier, which checked the transfer against its configured permissions policy and allowed it. “There is no vulnerability in the module. The contracts worked correctly. The transaction came from the key of a long-standing role member, which was most likely compromised,” he said.

Moormann added that the wallet’s custom permissions policy permitted the transaction, almost certainly unintentionally. He said the wallet’s operators controlled its keys and permissions, and that Zodiac had neither access to them nor involvement in the setup. Zodiac has not been in contact with the affected team.

Specter first flagged the Zodiac Roles module as part of the suspected attack. PeckShield also said the attacker used approximately $813,000 to purchase Monero-linked tokens on Hyperliquid.

The Zodiac Roles module allows Safe users to assign permissions for specific transactions. Moormann said the incident did not indicate a vulnerability affecting other wallets using the module.

The identity of the wallet owner has not been established. Cointelegraph contacted Safe and Gnosis Guild for comment but had not received a response by publication.

The firm noted similarities with fund movements following Monday’s Lighter incident, including a 160 Ether (ETH) transfer and purchases of XMR1.

However, Bitquery said it found no transfers connecting the two wallet groups and could not establish whether the same actor was involved.

Alphabet is set to participate in $3 billion in prepaid energy bond deals

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The two offerings, managed by Goldman Sachs, will be issued by the Southeast Energy Authority for an Arizona utility

Alphabet $GOOGL, the parent company of Google, intends to join two prepaid energy bond deals worth a combined $3 billion, according to Bloomberg.

Preliminary bond paperwork made public Monday names Alphabet as funding recipient for both offerings, which are slated to come from the Southeast Energy Authority, according to Bloomberg. Goldman Sachs $GS Group is managing both transactions. Spokespeople for Google and Goldman declined to comment.

The utility on both deals is the Salt River Project Agricultural Improvement and Power District, whose customers are in Arizona's Maricopa, Gila and Pinal counties, according to Bloomberg.

In a prepaid energy deal, a utility locks in electricity or gas at a reduced price. A financial middleman called a funding recipient takes in the bond money up front, then pays it out in installments that cover the energy delivered to the utility, according to Bloomberg.

The new offerings follow Alphabet's role as funding recipient on a deal of around $1.6 billion that priced Nov. 4, according to Bloomberg. Alphabet first served in that role in June, in what Bloomberg described as the first known instance of a U.S. tech company doing so.

Prepaid energy bonds account for a rising share of the borrowing that state and local governments do in the tax-exempt market. Issuance this year has reached close to $40 billion, a record, compared with the previous high of $31 billion last year, according to data compiled by Bloomberg.

Banks traditionally filled the funding recipient role, according to Bloomberg. Insurance companies and a real estate investment trust have since joined them, along with Alphabet.
