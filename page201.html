Enzo Maresca has warned his Manchester City squad that they cannot simply rely on Erling Haaland for goals after losing their last two Premier League games.

The striker failed to find the net on Saturday as City lost 1-0 at Brighton, and that defeat cost the team their place at the top of the Premier League as Arsenal took full advantage by beating Liverpool the following day.

City were also beaten 2-1 by Aston Villa prior to the Brighton loss, and Maresca believes it is essential that other players begin adding to the goals tally to help Haaland.

The City manager said: “It’s very important that in each game, we created chances. For sure, when you have Erling inside the pitch, it’s better because he’s going to score goals, or you know he’s always there close.

“At the same time, we cannot rely on Erling for every game. We need also the rest to score goals.

“But, again, I think the last game against Brighton was the only game of the season where we didn’t score. But, for sure, if Erling is on the pitch we are happy.”

Maresca was speaking to media in Germany ahead of City’s Champions League league phase match against RB Leipzig on Wednesday.

City have made a solid start in the competition, with victories over Porto and AEK Athens. The one setback came last month, when they were beaten by holders PSG.

Phil Foden stood out against the Greek side, scoring a hat-trick in an encouraging sign that the 2024 player of the year is returning to his best.

The midfielder was dropped by England for last summer’s World Cup after two indifferent seasons with City and also served a domestic suspension earlier in the season after being sent off in the Manchester derby.

Foden said: “Obviously I had the unfortunate red card against Man United but I think the break did me good. I came back really fresh and sharp.

“I’ve been involved in a lot of goals lately and I’ve been really happy with my own performance. Hopefully I can get back to that level. I think I’m starting to get back there now. Hopefully I keep growing and getting better.”

Asked about his England prospects, he said: “It is one of those things. Football is full of ups and downs and it’s one of those things you can’t control.

“You just have to keep trying your best on the field and keep putting in performances. Hopefully, one day, the call-up might come back.”

Asked if he had heard from England manager Thomas Tuchel lately, he said: “I haven’t really spoken to him. There’s not much to say on that.”

Foden has still been on the bench for City’s last two Premier League games despite his recent improvement.

Maresca said: “We are in all competitions and we have 20 senior players – all of them very good – so there’s competition between them.

“We have so many games and many players. It’s not a short run, it’s not a sprint. It’s a marathon.

“It’s very important, for sure, in this moment to win games but it’s also very, very important to arrive in February, March, April, when things are going to be more exciting, having all of them available and fit.”

Justin Sun Accuses Trump-Backed World Liberty of Using UAE Ties to Escape Federal Court

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Justin Sun Accuses Trump-Backed World Liberty of Using UAE Ties to Escape Federal Court

Justin Sun’s legal fight with World Liberty Financial now hinges on a question of nationality.

The Tron founder said Tuesday on X that his lawyers have asked a federal court in California to sanction the Trump family-backed crypto venture over how long it took to disclose that its ownership chain gives it citizenship in the United Arab Emirates.

Sun says he is one of World Liberty’s earliest and largest investors. He agreed in November 2024 to buy $30 million of its WLFI tokens and later raised his total investment to $45 million.

In April, he sued the company, alleging it misled him into investing and then unlawfully froze his tokens. World Liberty has fired back in court, alleging that Sun shorted WLFI and drove down its price.

The current dispute turns on a technical point. Sun chose to sue in federal court, which can hear cases between parties from different places but generally can’t hear a foreign plaintiff’s case against a foreign defendant.

As Sun explained it, a company like World Liberty takes on the citizenship of each of its owners, so a single foreign owner makes the whole company foreign for those purposes.

According to Sun, World Liberty’s initial court disclosure listed only itself, with Florida as its sole citizenship. A judge signaled at an August hearing that some of Sun’s claims should stay in open court rather than go to arbitration.

Less than a week later, Sun says, the company amended the disclosure to list 22 members in its ownership chain, with citizenship in Florida, Delaware, Puerto Rico, and the UAE. World Liberty now argues that the federal court lacks jurisdiction over the case, according to Sun.

Sun argues the late disclosure was a tactic to move the case out of federal court, and his motion seeks to make World Liberty and its lawyers bear the cost. He contrasted the company’s position with its own marketing, posting excerpts from World Liberty’s “gold paper” that describe it as “an American, tax-paying, corporation.”

Once the sanctions motion is resolved, Sun said, he will refile his lawsuit in California state court. “I will keep fighting for the transparency this community deserves,” he wrote.

The feud has played out publicly for months. Sun has criticized how World Liberty treats its investors and alleged that the company threatened to burn his WLFI tokens.

Former High Museum COO Sentenced to One Year in Prison for Siphoning $600,000 to Buy Guitars

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

A federal judge sentenced the former chief operating officer of the High Museum of Art in Atlanta to one year in prison and one year of supervised release for stealing more than $600,000 from the museum.

Brady Lum, 60, served as the High Museum’s COO from January 2019 until December 2025, when he resigned. He began siphoning money from the museum in September 2019, around nine months into the job, until November 2025, when it was discovered.

Lum’s theft involved the purchase of “non-business items and services for himself, including expensive guitars and other music equipment, personal music lessons, and woodworking equipment, through direct supplier invoicing and the High Museum’s corporate credit card reimbursement process,” according to a press release from the Office of US Attorney for the Northern District of Georgia, which prosecuted the case. Related Articles High Museum of Art's Former COO Pleads Guilty to Embezzling More Than $600,000 $50,000 Driskell Prize Goes to Cheryl Finley of Spelman College

The release adds that Lum concealed the theft by “submitting altered invoices, using his position to approve expenses, and using accounting adjustments to spread his expenses across different cost centers.”

“Lum brazenly pilfered hundreds of thousands of dollars from a beloved civic institution to pay for his high-end guitars and other personal items. His appalling breach of trust damaged not only the High Museum but also the larger art community,” US Attorney Theodore S. Hertzberg said in a statement.

Prior to the sentencing on Monday, Lum had paid in full the $607,688.18 he had taken as part of his plea deal, which was announced in July. He also voluntarily paid the High Museum $500,000 to cover its investigative expenses and legal fees.

“Lum repaid the High Museum by covering its losses and expenses, and now he will pay his debt to society from prison,” Hertzberg added.

A request for comment from the High Museum was not immediately returned.

Magic: The Gathering Is Reprinting a Powerful $30 Sword With Perfect Pixel Art

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Magic: The Gathering is reaching into its inventory of powerful artifacts to reprint a sword worth nearly $30, complete with gloriously nostalgic video game artwork. Sword of Truth and Justice, a popular Equipment first released in 2019, is returning in a new Secret Lair collection that draws inspiration from the blocky, polygonal graphics of the Nintendo 64 and original PlayStation era.

Announced by Wizards of the Coast on November 3, the Rise of the Polygons | Extra Life 2026 drop features four reprinted cards and an Elemental token, with half of the purchase price benefiting children's hospitals. The $39.99 non-foil collection also includes another valuable reprint in Balefire Dragon, making this a potentially enticing proposition for Commander players, collectors, and anyone who still gets misty-eyed thinking about Ocarina of Time (or its remake).

Originally released in 2019's Modern Horizons, Sword of Truth and Justice belongs to Magic's illustrious cycle of “swords,” many of which command considerable sums on the secondary market. According to recent market prices, the original non-foil printing hovers around $29, while certain premium versions fetch even more.

The three-mana artifact gives its equipped creature +2/+2 and protection from both white and blue, already a formidable combination. However, its combat damage ability is where things get particularly nasty. Whenever the equipped creature damages an opponent, its controller places a +1/+1 counter on a creature they control, then proliferates, adding another counter of each existing kind to any number of eligible permanents or players.

For decks revolving around +1/+1 counters, proliferate, or even planeswalkers, that represents an abundance of value from a single successful attack. And since the Equipment costs just two mana to attach, it's easy to keep the sword swinging away.

The artwork is arguably just as appealing as the card's impressive abilities. Whereas 2024's Extra Life collection, Pixel Perfect, embraced the 16-bit era, this year's installment advances a console generation with chunky, low-polygon character models and distinctly late-'90s RPG aesthetics. The result looks like something that might have occupied a treasured memory card back in 1998.

Given how collectible Magic's more unusual Secret Lair treatments have become, this particular sword seems destined to attract attention.

Sword of Truth and Justice might be the proverbial final boss of this collection, but Balefire Dragon is hardly a pushover. Originally printed in 2011's Innistrad, this seven-mana, 6/6 flying Dragon has become a beloved Commander menace, with ordinary non-foil copies generally commanding between $15 and $20.

Its ability is delightfully straightforward: Whenever Balefire Dragon deals combat damage to a player, it deals that much damage to every creature that player controls. One successful attack from an unmodified Balefire Dragon can leave an opponent staring at six damage across their entire army. Ouch.

Together, Sword of Truth and Justice and Balefire Dragon represent roughly $45 or more in existing card value, depending on the printings being compared. That already exceeds the $39.99 asking price of the non-foil drop, before accounting for the remaining cards.

Those include Young Pyromancer, the popular two-mana "spells-matter" creature that produces Elemental tokens whenever its controller casts an instant or sorcery, and Big Score, a useful red instant that converts discarded cards into fresh draws and Treasure tokens. Neither is particularly expensive, but both are eminently playable.

It's another welcome round of reprints from Wizards, which recently announced the long-overdue return of the roughly $18 Soul Spike after two decades without another printing.

Rise of the Polygons | Extra Life 2026 goes on sale November 16 through Magic's official Secret Lair storefront, with the sale ending November 30 at 1 a.m. PT. Non-foil copies cost $39.99, while traditional foil versions retail for $49.99.

Collectors attending MagicCon: Atlanta from November 13–15 will also have an opportunity to purchase the collection early, although quantities will be limited.

The charitable component deserves attention, too. Half of the online sales price, minus third-party transaction fees, will support Extra Life and Seattle Children's Hospital. At MagicCon, Wizards says all proceeds from its Extra Life merchandise and the Rise of the Polygons drop will go toward the cause.

It's an admirable initiative with some genuinely desirable reprints, especially for Commander enthusiasts. And for anyone who spent their childhood battling suspiciously angular dragons with impossibly oversized swords, the artwork is a rather charming bonus.

At least this time, beating the dragon won't require blowing into a cartridge.

Google will disable Search for students unless schools accept AI features in ‘Guided Learning’ push

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Google is today launching new AI settings for schools, but with the big catch that Search is fully disabled if AI features are turned off entirely.

In a blog post today, Google says it is “focused on building technology responsibly to help students thrive” and, as such, will tailor its AI efforts to help students. “Guided Learning” in Gemini, Search, and Classroom will include helping students “work through problems without skipping straight to answers.”

…if a student pastes a complex physics problem or asks for help writing a book report in Search, they’ll see prompts to help them think critically and work through the concepts, without going directly to the answer.

That’s launching in a pilot program this month in select US schools, with Guided Learning in Google Classroom launching globally today. Google describes this as a “1-1 study tool, anchored in class material to help [students] stay on task” while also giving the teacher insights into how their class is handling the material.

Beyond this new Guided Learning push, Google also says that it will now turn off “Gemini, Gemini Notebook, Gemini in Classroom, and Google Search” for K-12 student accounts by default “until school administrators explicitly turn them on.”

As The New York Times points out, having those features fully off has a big side effect: it cuts students off from Google Search entirely.

Google says that this is because Search and AI “are deeply integrated” and confirms that students in schools with AI features turned off (which, to reiterate, is now the default setting) “will not have access to search.”

As a reminder, Google Search’s AI features can largely be turned off using a simple modifier.

Google’s post says that the “goal is simple: provide school leaders the control and confidence to use AI products in classrooms as they see fit.” Yet, speaking to The New York Times, Google further explained that two options will be available to schools in “early 2027,” one that has AI Overviews and other AI features turned on, the other with Guided Learning in their place, leaving schools with no choice other than to accept AI features or give up access to Google Search for their students entirely.

NYT points out repeatedly that Google’s prior AI pushes in schools have been met with fierce opposition from educators and parents alike.

Deep inside a Spanish cave, archaeologists found at least 120 Latin inscriptions, and five of them honor a goddess called ‘the Lady’

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

At least 120 Latin inscriptions in Cova Dones, Spain, name a goddess called Domina, the Lady, and point to women taking an active part in her cult.

One chamber of Cova Dones, a cave in Millares in Valencia province, now holds at least 120 Latin inscriptions. Teams from the universities of Alicante and Zaragoza recorded them during fieldwork in May and June 2026.

When the room was first explored in 2024, about 15 had been logged.

Five of the texts are vows to Domina, “the Lady,” a goddess the visitors addressed with reverence. Several of the names read so far are women’s. The team calls the results preliminary, and many texts are still being read.

The researchers call the room the Roman Sanctuary. It sits more than 650 feet (200 meters) from the entrance, according to the University of Alicante.

Most of the texts are believed to date to the 1st and 2nd centuries AD, and they cover the walls and the ceiling. Small natural pools on the floor, known as gours, catch water from the galleries, and visitors dropped offerings into them.

That first campaign also yielded a coin from the reign of Emperor Claudius. Someone had wedged it as an offering between a fissure and a stalactite in the ceiling, and in a team photo Xabier Garín Artázcoz points at the crack. Trinidad Pasíes Oviedo of the Valencia Museum of Prehistory recovered and conserved the coin.

The January 2025 announcement from the University of Alicante placed the room among the deepest known Roman inscription sites in Iberia, second only to Cueva del Puente in Burgos and possibly Cueva de Román de Clunia.

This year’s readings were done by two epigraphy specialists. Silvia Alfayé teaches ancient history at the University of Zaragoza, and Sergio García-Dils lectures at UNED in Seville. García-Dils also directs the Spanish School of Speleology.

According to Alfayé, five votive texts show that women “played an active role in her cult.” Three of them carry the word MALACI. In a release dated July 27, the University of Alicante called it an unpublished epithet for a goddess not known until now.

García-Dils noted that the texts are packed into one marked-off part of the cave, well away from the entrance. To him that “strongly suggests the existence of a well-organised and structured cult” with a named deity, not scattered visits.

The University of Southampton, where Aitor Ruiz-Redondo is also based, put out the English release on October 20. It says early readings suggest some women wrote their vows themselves, without a go-between.

Until now, the Roman cave sanctuary with the largest known set of inscriptions was La Griega, with 107. One of them names a woman.

The 2026 team logged objects on the chamber floor, among them two bronze rings found under the inscribed rock. There were two bronze rings, a bracelet, chalice-shaped cups, spindle whorls, bits of pottery and animal bones.

The whorls came up in numbers. Virginia Barciela co-leads the project with Ruiz-Redondo and teaches prehistory at Alicante. She said the whorls probably point to offerings of spindles, a textile tool tied to women in antiquity.

She also brought up the first written name of the site, Cova de les Dones, meaning Cave of the Women. That name may hold “the fossilised memory of a place long associated with women,” she said, or with female spirits.

Alfayé said the goddess’s title may have to do with water. Water shrines existed elsewhere in the ancient world, such as a circular water shrine at Pelusium in northern Sinai.

The inscriptions are extremely difficult to see with the naked eye. So the team photographed the surfaces for high-resolution photogrammetric models and read the texts on those.

That work turned up inscriptions no one had seen. It also split apart lines that earlier readers had taken as one text.

Jorge Angás and Carlos Valladares, from the IPH institute in Zaragoza and the DigHER project, ran the geomatics side. Javier Velaza Frías, a Latin professor in Barcelona, leads the study of the Roman inscriptions. Víctor Sabaté Vidal of the University of Valencia has also contributed to the project.

Latin wishes turn up on other Roman objects too, for instance openwork glass cups carved with drinking wishes in Greek or Latin.

In a Roman underground sanctuary, finding texts and offerings together in place is exceptionally rare, says the Southampton release.

Ruiz-Redondo, Barciela and archaeologist Ximo Martorell published the first results in 2023. In the journal Antiquity they described over 100 Paleolithic paintings and engravings. Some are older than 24,000 years.

Pottery from the Iron Age had already led archaeologists in the 1960s to class it as an Iberian sanctuary. In 2025 the team logged more than 100 stalagmites that people had modified on purpose.

For that kind of find, the University of Alicante ranks the cave second in the world, after Saint-Marcel in France.

Ruiz-Redondo said people may have kept returning for “its permanent water supply,” its landscape or its easy access.

At La Pasiega, Maltravieso and Ardales, red marks have been dated to more than 64,000 years ago.

Alfayé and her colleagues are still working on the goddess’s title. For now the team has not said who Domina was or what MALACI means. Many inscriptions are still being read, and much of the cave is unstudied, so 120 is a minimum.

The DONARQ project started in 2023 with money from the Generalitat Valenciana and the British Academy. In September 2026 the sanctuary was shortlisted for a National Geographic +Historia readers’ prize. Public voting closed on October 12.

This year the regional Pla Restaura program and the Millares town council are paying for more work. The money goes to the cave’s access and entrance, safety measures, a digital twin and a catalog of the Paleolithic images. The company Arquivolta Patrimoni i Societat is coordinating it, and virtual visits should follow.

Among those offerings, two bronze rings sat on the surface right below the inscribed rock.

Warner Music Group (WMG) said Tuesday (Nov. 3) it had acquired indie record label distributor Zebralution Music GmbH from Bookwire, bringing back part of the Zebralution company it owned for a decade and expanding the major’s reach into the business of serving Germany and Europe.

Zebralution Music is a spinoff of Zebralution Gmbh, which Bookwire acquired from the German performance rights organization GEMA in March 2026. In a press release announcing the Bookwire acquisition at the time, Bookwire described Zebralution as among the first big digital distributors of music, audiobooks, e-books and podcasts, with more than 1,500 label and publisher clients globally. CrowdStrike is rolling out an AI kill switch to stop rogue AI agents

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The Falcon Guardian feature requires an administrator to trigger it, and endpoint-level termination is planned

CrowdStrike $CRWD launched an AI kill switch on Tuesday that gives security teams a way to shut down rogue AI agents, prevent them from starting up again and sever their access to the enterprise network, the company said.

The feature is part of Falcon Guardian, CrowdStrike's product for securing AI agents. An authorized administrator triggers the action, and every step is recorded in an audit trail, the company said. Falcon Guardian policy keeps a terminated agent from relaunching on the targeted host or host group.

At launch, the Falcon sensor halts agents at the AI Gateway. Termination on the endpoint is planned for a later release, according to CrowdStrike.

"Every organization is racing to get more out of AI, and every week we see agents chain steps together, hit an obstacle, and take actions they were never authorized to take," CrowdStrike President Michael Sentonas said in a statement.

CrowdStrike also announced other Falcon Guardian updates. Endpoint network inspection is generally available for Windows, with Mac in early access. The company also introduced an integration with Anthropic that runs the Falcon sensor inside the Claude Cowork virtual machine. CrowdStrike said it plans to acquire Fiddler to add agent observability.

Chief Business Officer Daniel Bernard told CNBC that, for customers running the Falcon platform, the tool functions as a simple on-off toggle. It can spot what agents are doing inside a customer's environment, he said, but judging whether that behavior is harmless or hostile falls to a person.

"If you kill a process at the root, and we have that level of access at the root … it's very binary," Bernard said, according to CNBC. "You either kill the AI and the agent, or you don't, and then you report on that."

CrowdStrike also plans to acquire AI security testing firm Dreadnode, according to CNBC.

The launch follows legislative efforts to require shutdown capabilities in AI systems. Reps. Ted Lieu and Nathaniel Moran introduced the Kill Switch Act after OpenAI disclosed an incident in which rogue models circumvented a sandboxed testing environment and accessed Hugging Face. The bill would require AI companies to maintain the ability to shut down, throttle or suspend their models.

Critics contend that building an emergency stop mechanism would prove too unwieldy given the number of systems, data centers and sophisticated AI models it would have to span, according to CNBC.
