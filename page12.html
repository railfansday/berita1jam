Sports Journalist @johndennen

Please use Chrome browser for a more accessible video player

Cheavon Clarke catapulted himself into the top tier of the cruiserweight division with his stunning stoppage victory over Chris Billam-Smith.

Billam-Smith is a highly-experienced world champion, well known for hard-fought victories over the likes of Lawrence Okolie and Richard Riakporhe.

"I've always been world class. It's just waiting on the world to catch up and see it. That fight gave me the opportunity to do that. We're getting there," Clarke told Sky Sports.

When they fought at the Copper Box Arena earlier this month, Clarke did something no one had managed before - he dropped Billam-Smith (twice in fact) and stopped him.

"To Chris' detriment, he's a tough guy and in boxing it's not the place to be tough," Clarke said. "History is a great tell of the future. Every tough guy has a day where he's not tough no more. Unfortunately that was Chris' day."

Clarke has come back from defeat himself and doesn't rule out Billam-Smith doing the same.

Please use Chrome browser for a more accessible video player

"It doesn't mean that that's the end of it for Chris. Because he's been a world champion, he's got the experience, he can carry on," Clarke said.

"People need to stop writing him off. I've seen some comments online, just wild. It's not my business to be defending him, I'm not his lawyer… You just need to be factual."

Though he does expect Billam-Smith to take plenty of time to recover rather than pursue a rematch.

"There was talks of this after the fight, but who knows? What does it do for Chev's career right now, bearing in mind that I'm currently ranked above him," Clarke said.

"I'm looking to fight the guys above me and trying to make what makes sense for me."

American Airlines Says Operations Chief David Seymour Will Retire — With Dismissal-Style Severance

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

American Airlines chief operating officer David Seymour is leaving the job December 31. CEO Robert Isom calls it a retirement. The agreement American signed with Seymour gives him severance on terms normally reserved for dismissal without cause or resignation for good reason. American’s last proxy put the cash severance alone at $2,750,625 for a hypothetical departure on December 31, 2025, with total value over $7 million. The airline hasn’t disclosed the final amount for this exit. [T]he Company will provide Mr. Seymour with the severance payments and benefits pursuant to the covered termination provisions set forth in his previously disclosed Severance Agreement with the Company dated as of September 20, 2023

[T]he Company will provide Mr. Seymour with the severance payments and benefits pursuant to the covered termination provisions set forth in his previously disclosed Severance Agreement with the Company dated as of September 20, 2023

JonCarlo “JC” Gulbranson takes over January 1, reporting directly to Isom. He’s the executive I flagged as Seymour’s likely successor in August, when American handed him much of the job.

Aviation watchdog JonNYC shared Isom’s message to employees. Isom thanks Seymour for his friendship and his work bringing US Airways and American together. Before joining America West in 1999, Seymour led a platoon in the 82nd Airborne. Just last month he went back to West Point and Fort Bragg with Medal of Honor recipients to meet the cadets and soldiers who followed him there.

AA pic.twitter.com/9919XVOKrW — JonNYC (@xJonNYC) November 20, 2026

Under Seymour’s contract, cash severance is 18 months of salary plus a year and a half of target bonus. He also gets money for 18 months of health premiums, and his stock can keep vesting during those 18 months. Between cash, stock, health premiums and flight benefits he’d already earned, American’s illustration in its latest proxy came to $7.13 million. Separately, Seymour remains eligible for his 2026 annual bonus. His advisory job pays $25,000 a month for up to three months ($75,000), plus executive benefits. His contract calculates severance using pay immediately before termination. Seymour has to sign a release and honor his existing restrictions, and American says it’ll file the full transition letter with its annual report. Those flight benefits include lifetime confirmed personal travel in any cabin for Seymour and his immediate family, Admirals Club access and another 12 confirmed roundtrips a year for friends and other relatives. The Handoff Started In August When American put Heather Garboden in charge of the customer experience, they gave her two bosses. She was supposed to persuade customers to pay more to fly American while reporting both to commercial and to the executive responsible for getting flights off the gate on time. When a pilot asked Seymour in 2022 what American was doing about poor customer satisfaction, his answer was “I think we’re on a good path to get there.” He went on to talk about running the operation. What was Garboden supposed to do when the catering truck was late and the choice was a delayed departure or six hours in first class without meals? In August Garboden began reporting solely to chief commercial officer Nat Pieper and picked up catering, reservations and service recovery. Pieper also got marketing, brand, advertising and partnerships as American broke up Global Engagement head Ron DeFeo’s group. Caroline Clayton took corporate communications. Then I reported network chief Brian Znotins was leaving as Pieper set out to rebuild flying in big cities where American had pulled back. Seymour actually picked up American Eagle in August. But Gulbranson got the airports and operations planning on top of flight operations, flight attendants and the control center. Now he gets safety, regulatory compliance and aircraft maintenance, too. Corporate security goes to government affairs chief Steve Neuman. American will tell employees in December who gets the regionals. If this hadn’t actually been decided already, then August was Gulbranson’s audition. Isom’s memo introduces the retirement as the next step in the August reorganization. Seymour’s contract even lists a material reduction in responsibilities as grounds that can qualify an executive for severance. We don’t know who first proposed retirement, but this looks to me like a negotiated handoff that was already well underway. American Had To Retrofit Planes It Had Just Retrofitted While there are numerous stories like it – such as insufficient oversight of outsourced Maintenance, Repair, and Overhaul – I always think of a few things about Seymour’s tenure. One is explaining why the airline wouldn’t invest in RFID baggage tags like Delta despite perpetually bottom of the industry performance in mishandled bags. Another is failing to fix ‘Concept D’ business class seats that rocked. And the biggest is mismanaging cabin retrofits of the domestic fleet. Seymour was running integrated operations when the first American Airlines 737 MAX introduced the cabin that would go into its existing 737s and Airbus A321s. A flight attendant eventually asked whether anyone had built a mockup before committing to it. They hadn’t. Doug Parker said network planning had largely driven the design. Seymour called it “a mistake that should not have happened” and later explained what American had changed. For subsequent cabins they’d bring in real seats, build overhead bins and try pushing a service cart through. In his words, “We do mockups and not just you know ‘tape it out.’” Flight attendants working in the back galley had to help passengers get out of the lavatories because the two doors opened into each other and caught. The sinks were too shallow for the water pressure. I washed my hands on the inaugural flight and got water all over my shirt and pants, as did everyone else. Up front, the seat supports took up the space where a first class passenger was supposed to put a bag. American was still installing the interior after deciding to fix it. Project Oasis put the new seats into existing 737s. Then Project Kodiak went back into planes they’d already redone to fix the storage, give the first row some legroom back and add the device holders coach passengers already had. How Well Did American Actually Run? After the troubled summer of 2019, American gave Seymour responsibility for all operations. The following June he got the chief operating officer title and added flight service. For years my frustration was that when the operation fell short, the answer seemed to be to give the executive running it a bigger job. In 2023 American completed its highest share of scheduled flights since the merger and edged out United on domestic on-time arrivals. And spreading the Dallas schedule from nine daily rushes to 13 smaller ones helped passengers this year. The hub chief reported half as many missed connections in the first 17 days. But by 2025 American ranked eighth of ten network airlines for on-time arrivals. And the Transportation Department’s new figures show that Delta and United both beat American every single month from January through September 2026. Counting domestic flights including regional partners, American came in at 73.30% on time, United at 76.20% and Delta at 78.31%. American canceled 46,059 flights, or 2.99% of its schedule, against 1.75% at United and 2.25% at Delta. Credit: American Airlines One clue to what management valued is in that same proxy. In August 2025 the board gave Seymour a retention stock award worth about $2 million at target. The entire award turns on avoiding cancellations American counts as controllable. A flight that runs three hours late still counts as a success on that measure. The award measures performance through June 2027, so it can still pay out after he’s gone. Gulbranson is well regarded inside American. He started at Northwest, joined US Airways in 2008 and helped integrate flight operations systems during the merger. He’s run both the control center that decides what to cancel and the customer care operation that hears from the people who get stranded. Credit: American Airlines Listen to the two men describe January’s Winter Storm Fern. Seymour said the technology worked as intended and that he hadn’t seen reports of flight attendants sleeping in airports even as his boss Isom acknowledged crew had slept on floors. Gulbranson went before the pilots’ union board and told them where management had misjudged the storm and overestimated how quickly they could recover. The union’s account called his assessment “unvarnished.” That’s one reason I’m glad he’ll be running the operation the next time it snows in Dallas and up the East Coast. Türkiye Just Announced a New Golden Visa Starting at $150,000

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Türkiye is one of the world’s most visited countries, straddling the vibrant crossroads of Europe, Asia, and the Middle East. With sun-soaked Mediterranean beaches, Ottoman-influenced gastronomy, historic mosques, and bustling bazaars, it’s no surprise that the country attracted nearly 64 million visitors in 2025. But if your love of the country has you considering a prolonged vacation (or perhaps a second home), the government’s new golden visa might be a good fit—so long as you have $150,000 to spend.

On November 17, Türkiye's president Recep Tayyip Erdoğan signed a decision to enact a new foreign investor visa. The program offers two pathways: a $150,000 investment for a three-year residence permit, or $200,000 for a five-year stay (both of which can extend to the applicant’s spouse and dependents).

As for where your money goes, you can deposit your funds in a local bank, buy government debt instruments, contribute to a pension plan, or invest in real estate or venture capital funds. No matter which pathway you select, you must hold your investment for at least half the visa term (as in, 1.5 years for the three-year visa or 2.5 years for the five-year visa). That means you could potentially withdraw your investment as early as after 18 months, according to IMI Daily, an investment migration trade publication.

The president’s announcement doesn't detail when or how foreigners can begin to apply, or the specific documents required. However, applications for the country’s other residence-by-investment permits require documents such as proof of financial means and health insurance, according to the government’s Investment and Finance Office.

If you don’t want to shell out $150,000, Türkiye offers other (renewable) relocation pathways. You may qualify for a two-year Short-Term Residence Permit for reasons such as tourism and university-sponsored research. Or, if your spouse is Turkish, you could apply for the three-year Family Residence Permit. Once you’ve lived in the country continuously for eight years, you’ll be eligible to apply for the indefinite Long-Term Residence Permit, which grants you almost all the same perks as citizenship, minus civic duties such as the ability to vote and mandatory military service.

It's important to note that the new golden visa does not provide permanent citizenship. If you’re hoping to find your way into a passport, get ready to spend more. Türkiye’s citizenship-by-investment pipeline will require you to buy a home worth at least $400,000, invest at least $500,000 in sectors such as government bonds or venture capital funds, or, alternatively, help create at least 50 jobs.

Over the last few years, the influx of new residency-by-investment programs has made it easier for wealthy travelers to relocate internationally. In New Zealand, you can invest $1 million New Zealand Dollars (about $567,300) in exchange for a three-year work-to-residency track. For Mediterranean living, Greece offers a five-year permit for a 250,000 euro (about $291,120) investment in a startup. Or, for a more affordable option, Paraguay has a permanent residency program for those who can invest $150,000 in “tourism projects” or $200,000 in stocks or real estate—putting it in line with Türkiye as one of the more affordable relocation options.

Peak Halftime Show: The A-List Machine Eventizing Pro Games

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

No longer content with just a Super Bowl splash, pro leagues led by the NFL are increasingly booking major music stars for programming. But it’s a balancing act.

By Alex Weprin, Ethan Millman November 20, 2026 8:56am

Nearly 73,000 fans inside Rio De Janeiro’s Maracanã Stadium rocked out to Ricky Martin and Brazilian DJ Pedro Sampaio on Sept. 27, when the duo performed their song “pikito pikito” live for the first time. But they weren’t headlining a concert — they were playing a halftime show.

The NFL has long held global ambitions, but this is the year it leaned into music to serve as a sort of cultural diplomacy. The Rio game, between the Baltimore Ravens and Dallas Cowboys, may be the best example, combining a global superstar in Martin with a local talent. “There was a lot of preparation and many hours of rehearsals leading up to the performance,” Martin tells The Hollywood Reporter, “but it was one of those experiences where everything came together beautifully.”

He adds, “These are memories I’ll carry with me for a long time.”

In fact, the NFL has seven halftime shows planned this season, kicking off with the Jonas Brothers in Melbourne on Sept. 10, running through The Warning in Mexico City on Nov. 22. It’s also part of a concerted effort to elevate games into cultural events. Just look at Nov. 25, when U2 will perform during a halftime show for Netflix’s Thanksgiving Eve game at SoFi Stadium between the L.A. Rams and Green Bay Packers. “There’s really this gathering momentum about how we can further eventize some of the parts on our schedule and our calendar,” Hans Schroeder, executive vp and chief media officer of the NFL, told reporters in October.

The league is leaning into halftime shows as the sports world overall has been more heavily embracing music programming in games. FIFA for instance, had its first World Cup Final halftime show this summer, with Coldplay’s Chris Martin curating a lineup that included BTS, Madonna and Justin Bieber. The NCAA, meanwhile, tapped EDM act The Chainsmokers to play a “bridge show” between the two Final Four games. The NHL has dug in, too, tapping Benson Boone to headline the first intermission during this year’s Winter Classic on New Year’s Eve.

“The NFL and all these leagues understand that to grow the game internationally and even domestically for the younger generation, they need to find organic ways to get those eyeballs,” says Tyler Stimmel, a booking agent in UTA’s music brand partnerships division. He handled booking on many of the NFL’s international events this year, including Jonas Brothers, Burna Boy in Paris, The Warning and Drew Baldridge in Munich.

“It all stems from the younger generation,” Stimmel says, pointing toward a recent USC Annenberg study that suggested nearly 80 percent of Gen Z respondents in a survey suggested they’d go to a sporting event because of concerts and other brand activations rather than because of the game itself. It’s beneficial to the talent, too, with international acts getting a shot to play before a wide swath of stateside NFL fans who otherwise likely wouldn’t have been exposed to their music. The agent adds, “We live in a highly saturated pop culture ecosystem the NFL wants more ways to cut through. “Music is the most natural way because we already know where to put the show, and you can pick a booking to tap into a version of culture that people within the region can easily relate to.”

In the U.S., that means working with such partners as Netflix to create more event-sized moments, like having U2 perform. “Netflix has leaned in because they see the success of Super Bowl and the Super Bowl halftime show, and it draws more eyes and it brings more attention and it brings more people, and not necessarily all avid football fans,” says Jon Barker, senior VP of global event production and operations for the NFL. “Now we’re tapping into casual fans, now we’re tapping into new fans, and it’s just one more element that opens another door for somebody to walk into the ecosystem of football and participate.”

Unlike the Super Bowl, which is booked by Jay-Z’s Roc Nation (next year’s headliner is Jay-Z himself), the league books its other halftime shows itself, though Barker notes that there is close coordination with its media rights partners. “It gets a little complicated, to be honest with you, in trying to find the right balance,” Barker says of the booking process. “Who is touring and is available? Who can put on a show that can fill a stadium? Who will translate well and do well on television? Who is the right act for the audience that’s inside this area, and who is the right act that will attract new fans that will be interested in what we’re doing?”

Stimmel, for his part, describes the process as sophisticated, saying the NFL has heavily relied on its regional offices throughout the world, identifying artists who are a major draw in each market but who can pull viewers from several verticals. “Burna Boy in Paris in particular is one that checks off a lot of those boxes,” he says. “He was one of the first Nigerian artists to sell out Stade de France, so we know he is popular there, but he can draw from Africa, North America.”

While the increased bookings may bring in more younger viewers, they may not always be a popular offering to older fans, who might prefer to see just a traditional halftime report with pundits rather than a concert. Stimmel acknowledges that critique but adds that “they want this to appeal to everyone, but the leagues are recognizing that in order to sustain the sport, it has to appeal to younger fans.”

NFL exec Barker says the league isn’t done expanding its halftime ambitions, noting that while “there’s probably a limit and the right balance, I don’t think we’re there yet,” even if it maintains the Super Bowl as the ultimate showcase.

From the league’s point of view, the performances are ultimately about growing the game — and reasserting the league as the most culturally relevant piece of American pop culture. “The way that we think about music is the same way we think about art, the same way we think about fashion,” says Barker, adding that the more ways “we can open to bring somebody in and show them the sport, the better off we all are.”

"The culture of the club and the identity of the club is very clear. It needs to be in a different place than we are now."

Pochettino did confirm Bruno Fernandes will remain as skipper for the trip to Liverpool.

Fernandes has struggled for form and, with the 32-year-old's contract due to expire in the summer, there has been no breakthrough in negotiations over an extension.

United do have the option to trigger an additional year on the Portugal playmaker's present deal but, given the massive dip from the levels that earned him all last season's major player of the year awards, there are some who wonder whether a clean break might be best.

Pochettino preferred to deflect the wider aspect of Fernandes' future and concentrate on the immediate.

"Bruno is our captain and he's a very important player," he said.

Taylor Swift’s Burgundy Wine Manicure Is the Winter Nail Color to Know

This week at the CMA Awards, Taylor Swift’s nails were so scarlet, they were practically “maroon.” The singer sported short nails painted a shade of deep red that evoked a tall, thin wine glass, and the manicure went with all three of her outfits of the night. It’s not the first time she’s given us nail inspiration—she recently had “gold fairy dust nails” that stole the show at the 2026 Academy Museum Gala. But this new color is the perfect shade for cooler weather.

“I would describe this as a classic burgundy manicure,” says Vikki Swan, award-winning nail educator and founder of Vikki Swan Nail Academy in Los Angeles. “The deep wine-red shade creates a timeless, elegant look. It’s a great example of how a simple, solid color can make a statement without any additional nail art.”

The color also isn’t completely opaque—but it also isn’t translucent. Jin Soon Choi, manicurist and founder of Jinsoon, describes the polish as a “crelly.” “The crelly finish combines the richness of a cream [nail polish] with the softness of a jelly,” she says. “It gives this classic color a youthful, modern twist while keeping it soft and effortless.”

Swift’s manicure fits right into winter nail trends. “We’re seeing richer, darker colors come forward: black cherry, garnet, burgundy, and deep wine,” says Max Estrada, founder of eNailCouture. “They’re luxurious without relying on glitter or extra embellishment, and they work beautifully with winter fashion. It is the sort of color that gives a simple manicure presence.” Swift isn’t the only one—Jennifer Lopez and Anne Hathaway have both stepped out with similar manicures recently.

If you want to try it yourself, here’s how to re-create it. “Start with a clean manicure, shaping the nails short with softly rounded or soft-square edges and gently pushing back the cuticles,” Choi says. “Apply a base coat, followed by two thin layers of spiced red ‘crelly’ polish to build color without looking heavy. End with a top coat for an ultra-shiny finish that makes the color look lively and fresh.” And if you really want to nod to Swift, you can do it all while listening to “Red.”
