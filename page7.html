GTA 6 chapter 1 is so good that Im afraid to move on to chapter 2

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

I've run into a very stupid problem with Grand Theft Auto VI, a game I love so far.

Namely, I'm having a surprisingly difficult time moving on from chapter 1 of the game's sprawling crime story. For a couple of real-life days now, I've been sitting on the edge of finishing GTA 6's opening stanza with very little motivation to move on. At the time of writing, I have literally one story mission left to go before the first chapter ends, and almost nothing of substance left to do otherwise.

But as much as I know I need to break through and see the rest of what GTA 6 has to offer, it's hard to resist the call to just live out Jason Duval's mundane daily routine for eternity.

Warning: There are some light spoilers for early parts of GTA 6's story in the following section.

For those who haven't played GTA 6 yet or are still in the early stages like me, chapter 1 follows Jason's meager existence as a sort of paid goon for Brian, a local boatyard owner who dabbles in drug trafficking on the side. The other protagonist, Lucia, gets out of prison partway through the chapter, but for most of it, she's not playable yet. Most of the story and side missions in chapter 1 focus on Jason doing little odd jobs that occasionally spiral into chaotic violence. He spends time with his girlfriend or buddies and otherwise just kind of chills when he's not doing all the crime stuff.

GTA 6 chapter 1 immediately establishes an endearingly ordinary routine for Jason, at least by action video game protagonist standards. Every in-game day I wake up, feed the pet snake, drink coffee, work out, drink a protein shake, and take a shower before heading out to do whatever it takes to get enough money to set up a nest egg for Jason and Lucia.

While there are some minor gameplay incentives to doing all of that, GTA 6 doesn't outright demand any of it from the player. I'm just doing it because it's a legitimately interesting loop, especially by the standards of big-budget mainstream games. I'm not just endlessly traversing between icons on a map and shooting some bad guys when I get to each one. I feel like an active participant in the everyday lives of two adults, which at the very least is novel for this type of game. It also helps that Brian is voiced by Stephen Root, so you spend most of chapter 1 being yelled at by Buck Strickland, and that's lovely.

I mean, most of my favorite missions so far have barely involved crime at all. The one where Jason and Lucia go to a bar after she gets out of prison is a remarkable virtual rendition of a romantic night out, while I've already lavished the Carly Rae Jepsen mission with praise on this website.

All of that is why I'm having such a hard time moving forward with the main story. I know in my heart that chapter 2 will likely introduce a new setting with new stakes, new mechanics, and an increased focus on crime. And all of that will probably be fantastic in its own right, but it won't be the same as the game I've loved up until now.

And if that's the feeling Rockstar intends to elicit in players when chapter 1 draws to a close, I would say they pulled it off. Maybe even a little too well. Why you should never reply with STOP when you receive spam texts

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

An honest opt-out method for marketing messages sends a different signal to shady scammers.

You've likely noticed the "STOP to cancel" note when you receive texts from short codes. And for legitimate communications that you asked to receive from restaurants, surveys, loyalty programs and similar, STOP is a fine way to cut them off. But it's not your only option for ending unwanted texts, and for many kinds, it can do more harm than good.

Nobody wants garbage texts about phony job offers, illicit encounters or other scams, but it's important to think carefully about how you respond to them. Rather than deleting them and dealing with more in the future, you have other options — and they're ones that won't prompt more incoming junk.

Remember to treat any messages from unknown numbers with caution, since they're used for "wrong number" scams and all kinds of other schemes. Don't tap an unexpected link, even if it claims you have an unpaid toll or need to confirm details to fix a delivery problem.

However, not all the texts you get from unknown numbers are like this. Spam texts that advertise lonely singles, too-good-to-be-true job offers, package delivery scams or other types of nonsense aren't operating within normal marketing laws. You might thus reply STOP out of habit, even if they don't include a note about that.

By doing so, you're confirming to the sender that your number is active, which will likely result in you getting even more junk texts. It costs spammers almost nothing to send out many thousands of texts to random numbers, but few of those are confirmed as live, so you're making yourself a target. Replying can also demonstrate that you aren't able to recognize a text scam, possibly tagging you for future efforts if they think they can hook you.

Only reply STOP when you want to stop getting alerts you specifically signed up for. For anything else, never respond; instead, you should use your phone's tools to deal with unwanted messages safely.

On iPhone, you should see a Stop or Report Spam button under messages from unknown senders that include a STOP note. Tap this, and you can choose Delete and Stop or Delete and Report Spam. Both erase the conversation from your phone; the former sends a STOP reply after doing this, while the latter reports the message to your carrier and Apple. For messages without a mention of STOP, the only option is Report Spam.

A similar option appears at the bottom of conversations with unknown senders; tap Delete followed by Delete and Report Spam. In case the number keeps contacting you, tap the number at the top of the conversation and choose Block Contact to cut off all communication.

On Android, you can report an entire conversation as spam in Google Messages by holding it in the list, then choosing Block > Report spam. Alternatively, open a conversation, tap the three-dot More button, then choose Details > Block & report spam. You'll also have the option to block the number.

For either platform, you can also long-press the message and forward it to 7726 (SPAM on a keypad). This reports the text to your carrier for analysis, helping it block spammers across the network. You might get a response asking for the sender's number, so wait to block it until you've confirmed this.

Even when you know what to do upon receiving junk texts, it's better if you don't get them in the first place. Android and iOS both have ways to filter low-quality messages so you aren't getting alerted for nothing.

If you have an iPhone, visit Settings > Apps > Messages, where you have a few choices. Turn on Filter Spam to send obvious garbage to a separate Spam folder. Enable Screen Unknown Senders, then messages from numbers not in your contacts will go to their own folder. And under Text Message Filter, enable the built-in Text Message Filter to put other texts into groups like Transactions and Promotions.

The Allow Notifications menu controls which of these groups send you alerts. Some third-party iPhone apps offer their own message filtering options, which will appear here. Once it's all set up, access the filtered groups from the three-line Filter button at the top-right corner of Messages.

On Android, open Google Messages, tap your profile picture at the top-right corner and choose Messages settings. Choose Protection & Safety, then you can choose from Standard protection or Enhanced protection for junk messages. In your profile picture menu, choose Spam & blocked to review filtered messages.

Many mobile carriers offer their own protections from spam messages (like T-Mobile ScamShield), which are worth a look if you're still receiving trash. Depending on your carrier, you might activate this by changing a toggle in your account, dialing a code to activate or installing a separate app. The base features should be free; it's almost certainly not worth paying for any premium subscriptions on offer. Third-party tools like Truecaller are another option.

Otherwise, be careful of where you give out your number. While spammers often get your number from leaks, other times your contact details spread around when you sign up on a shady website or a company sells them. When making a new account, always check the settings to see if you can limit your information from being shared for marketing purposes. That can help stop spam calls as well as texts.

Founded in 1961 by Laurentino De Cerchio, the estate is now led by fourth-generation family member Federico De Cerchio, who has spearheaded a comprehensive renewal of the winery, combining its heritage with a contemporary vision focused on quality, sustainability, and international growth.

Today, Torre Zambra oversees more than 40 hectares of vineyards farmed according to organic agricultural practices in the hills of Villamagna. Located between the Adriatic Sea and the Maiella mountain range, the area offers particularly favorable conditions for Montepulciano d'Abruzzo, the region's signature red grape.

"Being recognized among the world's top 100 wines is a tremendous source of pride, but, above all, it confirms that we are moving in the right direction," says Federico De Cerchio. "We have invested significantly in bringing new energy to a winery that has been part of our family for generations, with quality and an international outlook at the heart of everything we do. Our goal is to demonstrate that Abruzzo can compete at the highest level while remaining true to its identity and authenticity."

The Wine Spectator recognition follows an exceptional year of critical acclaim for the winery. Torre Zambra was named Up-and-Coming Winery of the Year in Gambero Rosso's Vini d'Italia 2026. Its Villamagna DOC Classico 2024 received the prestigious Tre Bicchieri distinction in the 2027 edition of the guide, marking the winery's third consecutive year earning the honor.

Further underscoring the winery's consistent recognition on the international stage, Torre Zambra has earned strong ratings from the leading wine publications, including James Suckling, which awarded 94 points to Colle Maggio Montepulciano d'Abruzzo Riserva DOC 2025 and 92 points to Piana Marina Trebbiano d'Abruzzo DOC 2025. Vinous awarded 93 points to Piana Marina Trebbiano d'Abruzzo 2024 and 91 points to Pecorino Poggio Salaia 2025. Wine Spectator also recognized the winery's portfolio, awarding 94 points to Piana Marina Trebbiano d'Abruzzo DOC 2024 and 93 points to Villamagna DOC 2024.

Beyond winemaking, Federico's vision extends to showcasing Abruzzo through hospitality and gastronomy. The newly opened Torre Zambra RESORT, set amid the vineyards of Villamagna, offers visitors an opportunity to experience the region's landscape and culture. Its Ristorante Contemporaneo reinterprets traditional Abruzzese cuisine through a modern approach, celebrating local ingredients and pairing them with the winery's wines.

Through its continued investment in winemaking, hospitality, and its local community, Torre Zambra is strengthening its position as an ambassador for Abruzzo, bringing the region's wines and distinctive character to an international audience.

Founded in 1910, Famiglia De Cerchio traces its roots to Vincenzo De Cerchio, who planted the family's first Montepulciano and Trebbiano vines in Villamagna, Abruzzo. Following the destruction of the vineyards during World War II, his son-in-law Laurentino De Cerchio rebuilt the estate, and in 1961, the first vintage of Torre Zambra was bottled. Today, fourth-generation family member Federico De Cerchio carries on the family's commitment to native Italian grape varieties, sustainable farming and terroir driven wines. The group's estates include Torre Zambra and Idi di Marzo in Abruzzo, along with Pajaru and Peri Peri in southern Italy, with a focus on sustainable and organic vineyard management.
