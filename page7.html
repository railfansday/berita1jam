The crypto market is struggling to regain momentum as Bitcoin and other digital assets face continued pressure. Slowing US liquidity and regulatory uncertainty are two major concerns weighing on the market, according to Real Vision founder Raoul Pal and investor Kevin O’Leary.

While Pal believes tighter financial conditions are limiting money flowing into crypto, O’Leary says the lack of clear US regulations is preventing businesses from adopting blockchain technology on a wider scale.

Raoul Pal believes slowing US liquidity is one of the main reasons behind the crypto market’s weak performance.

Pal said US liquidity has remained largely flat since July 2026. A stronger dollar, rising interest rates and a flatter yield curve have slowed money creation through the banking system.

He explained that crypto needs increasing liquidity to support price growth, but current conditions are not providing enough support.

“Markets have gone nowhere. Narrow liquidity, so this is total liquidity including the banking system loans and leases. It’s not growing as fast,” Pal said.

Despite the current market downturn, he is continuing to buy crypto. He revealed that he made two purchases in 2026, including one during the February sell-off.

He believes Bitcoin is still undervalued based on its long-term liquidity trend, but does not expect an immediate recovery. He expects the US dollar to weaken towards 93 on the dollar index. Lower interest rates and a weaker dollar could improve market conditions and support crypto prices.

Pal also remains positive about Ethereum and Solana. He outlined a long-term scenario in which Ethereum could reach $18,000 by Q2 2027 and $60,000 by mid-2028 if its trend continues. However, he described these levels as a possible market path, not guaranteed targets, and identified $1,500 as an important risk level.

Kevin O’Leary points to regulatory uncertainty in the US as another reason the broader crypto market is struggling.

He believes businesses are hesitant to adopt blockchain services and stablecoins because they lack clear regulatory guidelines. The delay in passing the CLARITY Act is adding to this uncertainty.

“We really need to get that done,” O’Leary said, referring to the CLARITY Act.

O’Leary explained that his holding companies already use USDC for treasury management and cross-border payment settlements. However, many other businesses are waiting for approval from their compliance departments before adopting similar services.

He noted that the GENIUS Act has established a legal framework for stablecoins in the US, but broader rules covering blockchain infrastructure and other crypto services are still missing.

“The rest of the infrastructure and blockchain and everything else in treasury management isn’t done till the compliance department of institutions say you can do it,” he said.

According to O’Leary, this regulatory uncertainty is slowing institutional adoption and limiting the wider use of blockchain technology.

Resident Evil 1 Remake Gets Official Update From Capcom

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Capcom has officially provided an update regarding the remake of the first Resident Evil game. The developer/publisher has been steadily remaking the classics into modernized versions, more similar to what the franchise looks like today. That started back in 2019, with the release of Resident Evil 2 Remake, but we’re now looking at a new Resident Evil Remake collection for 2027. The company recently announced that it is also working on the remake of one of the most impactful entries in the series, but until now, talks about a remake of Resident Evil 1 hadn’t occurred.

As part of a special Nikkei Entertainment issue focused mainly on the 30th anniversary of Resident Evil, according to a report by Games Radar, series producer Jun Takeuchi provided some information about the remake of Resident Evil 1. The developer did not confirm or deny the long-standing rumors about the remake, but he did explain why Capcom did not choose the first entry in the series as the first remake the series would handle, and it has everything to do with how different the first game is from the second and all other installments later on.

According to Takeuchi, Resident Evil 1 wasn’t chosen as the first option for the remakes at Capcom “because it’s such a stoic game.” The producer details that the first entry in the franchise has its horror fueled by that same “stoicism,” and that the company was concerned that a remake of that game would “not resonate with today’s game fans.” The rumored remake of Resident Evil 1 would be, indeed, drastically different from Resident Evil 2 Remake, on all fronts.

What Takeuchi seems to describe is how much the first game’s horror is built around a continuously tense atmosphere and ambiance. The sequel’s horror comes mostly from a large stalking presence. Moreover, the series sees a huge pivot. While the first game is about surviving without getting caught, the franchise has constantly evolved into something a lot more action-oriented, where players have tons of different tools at their disposal to deal with the infected. Those who played the latest core Resident Evil games would see something completely different in a potential Resident Evil 1 Remake.

If a fan’s first contact with Resident Evil came with Resident Evil 5, Resident Evil 6, or Resident Evil 7: Biohazard, they’d likely expect a bit more adrenaline and action, and Resident Evil 1 is more about getting haunted by your surroundings. This isn’t a confirmation that the first entry won’t get a remake, but an explanation as to why it wasn’t Capcom’s starting point. The rumors about a Resident Evil 1 Remake are strong, but there is still something officially announced before that: the upcoming Resident Evil: Code Veronica Remake.

Given how successful the remake installments have proven to be, despite the well-deserved criticisms of Resident Evil 3 Remake, it wouldn’t be a surprise if Capcom were to continue making remakes of the series. After the Code Veronica remake, the most logical step would be to remake the oldest entry in the Resident Evil series, and if the rumors are to be believed, it’s already an unannounced reality we just have to wait for.

Is SpaceX Stock a Buy Before Its First Try at Catching a Starship Upper Stage?

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

SpaceX might catch both halves of Starship for the first time by Saturday. How much would that change for a $1.9 trillion stock?

SpaceX (SPCX -2.33%) wants to get its whole rocket home. On Starship's 15th test flight, set for as early as Friday, Nov. 13, the company plans to catch both halves of the rocket using its launch tower. If both catches work, SpaceX says it'd be the first full recovery of an orbital launch system.

The bottom half, the Super Heavy booster, has been caught on three past test flights. The top half hasn't. That part's the upper stage (the section that takes satellites the rest of the way to orbit, which SpaceX simply calls the ship).

Since its IPO, SpaceX's stock has traded as high as $225.64. Shares trade around $143 as I write, about 37% under that high. With a potentially historic flight a day away, is the stock a buy?

I think a successful catch would be a big milestone for Starship. But I doubt it would change much about what investors are paying for now.

SpaceX's mission page shows a 75-minute launch window starting at 4:45 p.m. Central time on Friday, pending regulatory approval as I write. The booster is due to be caught around six and a half minutes after liftoff. The ship, meanwhile, is meant to deploy 26 of Starlink's bigger V3 satellites and spend over 22 hours in orbit before trying to land at the tower in Texas. If Starship takes off on Friday, that would put the ship's catch on Saturday afternoon.

Even that final step isn't a given. SpaceX said the ship will only come back to the launch site if its flight control team confirms that the systems it needs for reentry and landing are healthy. Otherwise, it can be brought down over the ocean instead.

"Success is not guaranteed on these first flights, but I'd say it's more likely than not that this succeeds next flight," CEO Elon Musk said on the Oct. 20 earnings call.

A rocket that returns can fly again. On the same call, President Gwynne Shotwell said SpaceX's aim is to "refly the second stage by early next year." Reusing the ship is what's meant to change Starship from an expensive test program into a far cheaper way to reach orbit.

For now, the program is still getting more expensive. Showing how hard SpaceX is pushing, the space segment's research and development spending grew 44% year over year to $1.2 billion in the third quarter. According to SpaceX's quarterly filing, that spending mostly goes to developing, building, and testing Starship. And it's risen every quarter this year, from $930 million in the year's first quarter to around $1.1 billion in the June quarter.

Still, the segment nearly broke even. More customer launches helped drive space revenue up 84% sequentially to $1.8 billion, and the segment's operating loss shrank to $7 million from $542 million in the second quarter.

But a year before the same segment turned a $259 million operating profit.

The bigger payoff from a reusable ship could arguably show up in Starlink. SpaceX's own payloads made up 358 of the 476 metric tons it sent to orbit in the third quarter. Plus, SpaceX says the 26 V3 satellites on Starship's first orbital flight added around 10 times the downlink capacity of a Falcon 9 Starlink launch.

SpaceX's valuation is around $1.9 trillion at the current price. Even annualizing its third-quarter revenue of $14.6 billion, the shares trade at around 33 times sales.

And most of that revenue has little to do with Starship now. The super intelligence segment, SpaceX's artificial intelligence (AI) business, pulled in $7.6 billion last quarter, around half the total. Connectivity, home to Starlink, added $5.2 billion. The space segment's $1.8 billion was around 12%.

So, a catch would likely help the long-term case more than the next few quarters. Starship still needs to fly again, fly often (Musk said on the call he expects weekly flights sometime in the first half of next year), and show lower costs in the numbers.

Sure, a miss or a delay probably wouldn't hurt today's business much. After all, Falcon 9 already launches Starlink satellites. A setback could mainly cost time and more quarters of growing Starship spending.

Is SpaceX stock a buy ahead of Friday's launch? Not for a tower landing alone, in my view. At around 33 times sales, the growth stock's price depends mainly on AI computing and Starlink, and the Starship tab is still rising.

I wouldn't buy shares just ahead of the flight.

Fallout Officially Returns With New Deathclaw Expansion

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Devin Coull is a Toronto-based writer, producer, and on-camera host. He spent over six years working in gaming and esports, most recently at theScore Esports, created content for millions of viewers around the world.

Fallout 76 players have a two-word reason to log back in: Pet Deathclaws. That's right, players on Xbox Series X|S and PlayStation 5 can now experience traveling through Appalachia alongside one of the series' most iconic creatures in the game's Public Test Server (PTS), ahead of the World Pets update officially hitting live servers on December 1.

Fallout 76 didn't exactly get off to the best start. But credit to Bethesda, the studio has been fairly consistent about updating the game with gameplay fixes and new content. Not every update has been a hit, but this upcoming release gives fans another reason to hop back into the Wasteland while we wait for information on the Fallout 3 and Fallout New Vegas remakes. Pets were previously limited to just chilling around your C.A.M.P., but now you can take any pet you own out into the world as a companion by simply summoning them from your Pip-Boy. And since this is the Public Test Server, Bethesda has kindly given players access to one pet of each species to try out. However, keep in mind that once the test ends and the update hits live servers, you'll only be able to keep the pets you've previously obtained.

Like companions from the mainline entries in the Fallout series, players will be able to command their pets to stay, follow, attack enemies, and protect them from harm. Pets can also have their HP and damage increased through leveling up and combat upgrades, with progression eventually reaching a level cap of 200. As for which pets you'll be able to take on your adventures, there are cats, dogs, Radhogs, and, of course, the aforementioned Deathclaws. Deathclaws have long been some of the most dangerous creatures in the Fallout universe. I'll personally never forget the first time I ventured into Quarry Junction a little too early and got torn to shreds. Having one of these apex predators as a friend this time around is quite the reversal and a welcome power fantasy.

Deathclaws also come with two unique passive abilities. Dark Titan lets them find Black Titanium while exploring, while Fun-Festation grants extra Legendary Items for completing Infestations. The latter can eventually reward three-star items as it levels up. Other pet species also offer their own buffs, which are detailed in Bethesda's PTS announcement. The PTS also includes an update to Skyline Valley, which first appeared in the June 2024 update of the same name. The region is getting some much-needed TLC, with reworked mountains and hillsides, new pathways, and additional vegetation. Players can also pick up bounty hunting quests and start a new collectible questline involving mysterious glass canisters.

Finally, players can get an early look at Hideouts, large-scale enemy outposts that offer rewards for clearing them. Right now, the PTS features two locations: The Forever Farm in Burning Springs and Vault 63's Immortality Sector in Skyline Valley. Each has four difficulty levels, with tougher challenges offering better rewards. Hideouts are still in early testing, however, and aren't expected to fully launch until spring 2027. Any rewards earned during the test are temporary.

To try out the new content, install the Fallout 76 Public Test Server through your Xbox or PlayStation game library. You'll also need to join the Bethesda Game Studios Discord server and follow the instructions to obtain an access code. Once again, the World Pets update is currently planned for December 1, while Hideouts are still a ways off.

Füllkrug Admission Raises Uncomfortable Question About West Ham’s Striker Recruitment

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Ordinarily, I wouldn’t be in too much of a hurry to pay attention to what Niclas Füllkrug says about West Ham. However, the fact that he currently tops the Bundesliga goalscoring charts with nine goals in nine games for Werder Bremen does lend some credibility to his latest comments.

Speaking while on international duty with Germany, Füllkrug has suggested that a lack of service was partly responsible for his struggles at West Ham and AC Milan. In an interview published by the German Football Association, he explained that his Bremen teammates actively look to find him with the ball, something he felt was missing at his previous two clubs.

The German striker cost West Ham £27 million when he arrived from Borussia Dortmund, and there’s little doubt in my mind that the Hammers failed to have a proper plan for incorporating him into the team. However, Füllkrug must also take his share of the blame because his first season was blighted by injury, and he also spoke about feeling homesick.

The truth is that Füllkrug was never really on the pitch for long enough for us to discover whether anything could have come from his transfer. His constant fitness problems made it almost impossible to establish any consistency, and he has now acknowledged that staying fit was the key issue during his time abroad.

That said, there is little in West Ham’s performances under Julen Lopetegui, Graham Potter or Nuno Espírito Santo that suggests we would have been able to get the best out of what is clearly a talented striker.

There may well be something in his assertion that he wasn’t given a decent supply line when he did play for West Ham. Indeed, we’ve previously looked at how Bremen have built their attacking play around Füllkrug, with teammates providing the service and support he so often appeared to lack in East London.

As for his fitness, I’m afraid that’s very much on him. West Ham have consistently demonstrated over the past few years that their medical team, physiotherapists and fitness staff have generally done a good job of keeping players available.

Füllkrug’s injury record was a significant part of the problem, and whilst it’s perfectly reasonable for him to question the service he received, it doesn’t explain why he spent so much of his West Ham career unavailable for selection.

Nevertheless, watching a striker who managed just three goals for West Ham now leading the Bundesliga scoring charts is bound to raise questions about the original recruitment decision. The fact that the club are still dealing with the financial consequences of his move only adds to the frustration.

Füllkrug has rediscovered his goalscoring touch at Bremen, and good luck to him. But I suspect the truth about his West Ham failure lies somewhere between the club not knowing how to use him and the player simply not being fit enough to give himself a proper chance.

Custom Ad Algorithms Are Helping Schibsted Media Win Budgets Back From Meta

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

For years, ad budgets have been shifting from the open web and independent publishers to walled garden platforms like Meta.

But recently, that shift has begun to reverse a bit.

Nearly two thirds – 63.9% – of advertisers have moved mobile budgets from walled gardens to independent partners in the past year, according to a July survey of 166 brands and agencies by eMarketer and Datum Intelligence. Only 1.2% of the survey respondents shifted their budgets the opposite way.

That being said, eMarketer projects Meta, Google and Amazon will attract 62.3% of worldwide digital ad spend this year, with Meta topping Google in ad revenue for the first time.

Still, publishers around the world, including Norwegian-based broadcaster and TV news conglomerate Schibsted Media Group, are trying to take advantage of any shift away from Meta and the other walled gardens they can get.

Schibsted is working with Nodals AI, a London-based company that creates custom ad optimization algorithms for publishers in Europe and the US, to help court brands who’ve been wooed by Meta’s own optimization tools, Christer Ljones, director of ad data capabilities at Schibsted Marketing Services, told AdExchanger.

Schibsted owns VG, the largest digital news publisher in Norway, boasting about 2.4 million daily visitors. It also owns Aftonbladet, one of the largest digital news publishers in Sweden, which claims 3.6 million daily visitors. And it acquired Sweden’s TV4 and Finland’s MTV Oy – two of the largest TV broadcasters in those countries – from Swedish telecommunications group Telia last year.

These properties, combined with the rest of Schibsted’s local newspaper network, gives the company roughly 50% audience reach in the geos it covers, Ljones said.

To put Schibsted’s audience size in perspective compared to US publishers, its largest properties, VG and Aftonbladet, have wider reach than the Los Angeles Times, which attracts a combined weekly online and print audience of 4.4 million, or about 11% of California’s population.

VG and Aftonbladet make most of their money from advertising, with the local publications more reliant on subscriptions, he said. Online display ads and branded content account for most of the company’s ad revenue, with a roughly even split between the two.

Like other large publishers with strong reach concentrated in specific markets, Schibsted has long pitched advertisers on working with the company directly to take advantage of its market saturation and its deep knowledge of its audiences, Ljones said. The direct ad business comprises roughly two thirds of Schibsted’s ad revenue, with programmatic making up the remaining third.

But audience reach isn’t the only thing that matters to performance-minded brands. The walled gardens have won advertisers over with AI-powered campaign optimization features like Meta’s Advantage+, Ljones said, and it’s been hard for open-web publishers to keep up on the ad tech side.

It’s also been harder for open web publishers to compete when it comes to direct sales, said Nodals Founder and CEO Aly Nurmohamed.

“Advertisers have been trained by Meta to just care about ‘I put money in and I see how much money comes out,’” Nurmohamed said. “So the problem for Schibsted was advertisers were saying, ‘I don’t want to buy on a CPM, and you’re really expensive relative to Meta. Here’s what I’m doing with Meta. Can you replicate it?’”

Schibsted has always seen Meta as a competitor and not a “partner,” Ljones said. While some publishers experienced short-term gains from working with Meta in the past, he added, the platform is no longer shy about cutting publishers out of its ad business.

That dynamic is part of the reason Schibsted has been leading the charge against Meta’s planned terms of service update. The update would have required all publishers active on Meta platforms to share data for training the company’s AI models – essentially, training the optimization tools that are helping Meta take publishers’ portion of ad revenue.

Schibsted, along with 180 other publishers, joined the Norwegian Media Businesses’ Association in suing Meta over its TOS update at the end of October. Within two days of the lawsuit being filed, the Oslo District Court ruled in the publishers’ favor, imposing a freeze on Meta’s TOS update in Norway until its legality could be investigated. Legal decisions like this one likely prompted Meta to pause its TOS update across all regions earlier this month.

But legal battles are just one way Schibsted is fighting against Meta’s market dominance. Custom algorithms, like those Nodals creates, are another weapon in its arsenal.

Schibsted sees these custom algorithms as a way to remind advertisers of its effectiveness in driving performance and brand awareness by using similar tools to those offered by Meta, Ljones said.

Nodals pitches itself as a solution for publishers to create their own Meta-esque optimization engines.

And Schibsted could be in a strong position to win back some of the business it’s lost to Meta. It’s maintained a direct relationship with its audience through its owned and operated properties, even as other publishers struggle with AI search stealing their traffic, according to Ljones. As a result, it’s only reliant on search for a single-digit percentage of the traffic on its largest sites.

That direct relationship with its audiences gives Schibsted the first-party data insights it needs to compete with Meta’s campaign optimization capabilities, Nurmohamed said.

“We take their data and feed it into their own models, collecting everything they know about what their audiences are, any logged-in data they have, how far you scroll down the page, how often you click on content or ads,” he said. “All of that goes into our model, and then you can have a powerful prediction on who is likely to click and buy on a particular advertiser.”

Plus, because the publisher owns the optimization algorithm and the data that powers it, Nodals’ solutions shouldn’t be affected by Apple’s recent efforts to crack down on third-party audience data vendors and ID graphs, Nurmohamed said.

Schibsted first tested Nodal’s tech last summer and has been incorporating it into its direct sales efforts since those successful tests, Ljones said. It’s been building a new product line of direct-sold native ad formats with AI optimization built in, priced on a cost-per-click basis, rather than on a CPM model.

The new products are resonating with some of Schibsted’s traditional advertisers, such as grocery stores serving the Norway and Sweden markets, who are looking for a mix of performance and brand awareness from their ad campaigns, he said. And, while Schibsted “won’t be necessarily as good as Meta on average” when it comes to driving performance, “you don’t need to beat them on the average. You just need to beat them with meaningful scale at the margin.”

For example, Schibsted has found success pitching advertisers that have been pouring more money into Meta lately but seeing diminishing returns, Ljones said.

“We get feedback from advertisers that they’ve seen their Meta ROI, their conversions, flatten out, or maybe even decline slightly,” he said. “So, the overall idea that we see initially working is, ‘Let’s take 10% of whatever you spend on Meta, and you can probably keep most of your conversions and get more [from us].”

Ljones feels confident that Schibsted can build on those margins going forward.

“We’re in an early phase, but we’re seeing the right results,” he said. “For us, that’s taking new budgets from existing clients, defending existing budgets in a few cases, but also addressing clients we basically lost.”
