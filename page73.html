Strategy funded its Bitcoin purchases with common-stock sales during November 2–8, even as STRC, its variable-rate preferred stock, reached $100. Strategy had said it would not issue STRC below that price, but its November 9 disclosure reported no STRC sales under its at-the-market offering program.

The allocation shows where the week’s financing went. Of $283.8 million in net proceeds from MSTR common shares, $188.3 million funded STRC repurchases, $81.9 million funded Bitcoin purchases and $13.6 million went to USD Cash, according to the weekly financing update.

That put about two-thirds of the new common-stock money into buying back preferred shares. Less than a third went into Bitcoin. STRC’s market-price recovery had yet to become reported STRC ATM financing within that week.

Strategy’s November 2–8 MSTR proceeds went mostly to repurchasing preferred shares. No STRC ATM sales were reported for the week.

STRC buybacks received 2.3 times the common-stock proceeds allocated to Bitcoin.

Strategy’s July 27 policy announcement said the company would not issue STRC below $100. It also described a long-term objective of healthy, sustained trading near that level. The issuance and dividend policies were statements of intent that could change.

Strategy receives new equity capital when it sells shares and collects proceeds. In a secondary-market trade, a buyer instead pays another investor for an existing share. The market price and the issuer’s financing proceeds therefore describe separate transactions.

For November 2–8, the reported financing came from selling 2,051,348 MSTR shares. That share-sales figure includes shares sold but not yet settled as of November 6.

CryptoSlate’s November 5 coverage described renewed STRC financing as a possibility and included an unconfirmed tracker estimate that issuance might have financed 191 BTC. The subsequent weekly disclosure reports no STRC ATM sales for the period containing November 4.

Strategy bought 1,036 BTC for $81.9 million during the week, at an average $79,012 per coin including fees and expenses. Its Bitcoin purchase disclosure expressly identifies MSTR sale proceeds as the funding source.

Bitcoin purchases absorbed 28.9% of the common-stock proceeds. STRC repurchases took 66.3%, or about 2.3 times the Bitcoin allocation, while USD Cash received 4.8%. That makes the buyback program a substantial competing use of newly raised capital.

It also changes the mix of claims on the company. Issuing MSTR expands the common-share base, while repurchasing STRC reduces preferred claims. Strategy bought back 1,887,464 STRC shares during November 2–8.

The company’s July policy described discounted repurchases as a way to reduce future preferred dividend requirements. That gives the spending an economic purpose alongside its immediate cash cost. The eventual dividend savings depend on future rates and declarations.

The policy also anticipated a greater repurchase pace at deeper discounts, tapering as STRC approached $100. It left decisions subject to liquidity, market conditions and capital-allocation priorities.

The preceding week also paired MSTR fundraising with STRC repurchases, but disclosed a different funding route. In its November 2 filing, Strategy reported $164 million in MSTR proceeds: $65.5 million funded Bitcoin purchases and $98.5 million went to USD Cash. It separately used $208.2 million of USD Cash for STRC repurchases.

5 Years Later, 'VisionQuest' Officially Fixes 'WandaVision's Disappointing Ending

📅 Nov 11, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

When WandaVision debuted on January 15, 2021, it blew people away. Up until that point, the Marvel Cinematic Universe had been very successful, but it sometimes had been criticized for playing it safe with somewhat formulaic, “hero's journey” stories. WandaVision was entirely different. Those first two episodes clearly laid out the idea that each episode would portray Wanda (Elizabeth Olsen) and Vision (Paul Bettany) through the filter of a different sitcom era, with episode 1 covering the likes the earliest shows including I Love Lucy and The Dick Van Dyke Show from the 1950s and early 1960s. Episode 2 tapped into the high-concept sitcoms like Bewitched and I Dream of Jeannie from the mid-1960s.

A week later, episode 3 followed suit with a parody of 1970s sitcoms. And while episode 4 broke the format to explain a bit of what was going on outside of Wanda’s hex, episodes 5, 6, and 7 got back into the inventive sitcom formula with episodes in the style of ‘80s, ‘90s, and early 2000s TV. Then came that eighth episode, which gave Agatha Harkness (Kathryn Hahn) a backstory, which was fine, but not as much fun as the sitcom stuff.

Fortunately, tonight’s finale for VisionQuest finally did what WandaVision’s finale should have done, even if it was five-and-a-half years too late.

Warning: Spoilers ahead for VisionQuest episode 8.

While VisionQuest is considered the conclusion of the story that began with WandaVision, showrunner Terry Matalas hasn’t delved into any of the sitcom stuff that WandaVision did so well. Instead, he's been telling a supremely well-crafted sci-fi story about AI (and family) that feels closer to a classic Marvel movie than the form-breaking of WandaVision. The only real mention of sitcoms in the episodes preceding the finale had to do with Ultron (James Spader) harboring a surprising love for the medium. Having been trapped in Vision’s mind for more than a decade since the end of Avengers: Age of Ultron, Ultron has mostly been watching American television in the virtual reality loft/dungeon Vision created for him.

In the series finale, Vision decides to use Ultron’s familiarity with that format to reach him at a pivotal moment. After Ultron’s love, Jocasta (T’Nia Miller), is killed by a human named Virgil Horton (also James Spader), Ultron is freed and immediately starts acting like his old self, which means he again wants to wipe out all of mankind. So Ultron launches a swarm of nanobots to kill everyone in London.

With just eight minutes until the nanobots reach their destination, Vision, with help from E.D.I.T.H. (Emily Hampshire), crafts a simulation of a typical multi-camera sitcom to trap Ultron in the format and allow him to process his grief over Jocasta. Time is compressed in this simulation, with Vision explaining that it can last centuries with only minutes passing in the real world. No matter how much time it takes, Vision hopes that if he can reach Ultron on an emotional level, he can convince him to spare humanity.

The fake TV show's name is UltraVision. It's a typical family sitcom with Vision as the sitcom dad-type figure, E.D.I.T.H. as his daughter, and Ultron as Vision’s grumpy dad in a chair. It’s a really cheesy show with its own cornball catchphrase and a raucous studio audience. E.D.I.T.H. even says “Sitcom entrance!” when she enters the room to enormous cheers.

Vision starts the simulation with one of the most common sitcom tropes, where he says he was supposed to watch E.D.I.T.H.’s pet goldfish, Sparkles, while she was gone, but he forgot and it died. This kind of story was featured on countless sitcoms and it always ended up teaching a lesson about grief. Vision hopes to do the same here, so that he can spark a conversation about Ultron’s grief and begin to process things.

But once E.D.I.T.H. gets home and Vision is fumbling for a way to talk about the fish, Ultron just blurts out, “Sparkles is dead, roll credits,” and the simulation obliges, with the credits appearing on the screen.

Vision is confused. This is his simulation. E.D.I.T.H. explains that Ultron is trying to take over the trap, and he’s succeeding.

From there, Ultron zips through hundreds of episodes of UltraVision, with the seasons flying by at a dizzying pace and a fast-forward montage through it all. He finally stops at episode 500, where Ultron says they need some new castmates. Paladin (Todd Stashwick), Lisa Molinari (Lauren Morais), Tommy (Ruaridh Mollica), D.U.M.-E. (Henry Lewis), and U (Jonathan Sayer) come in, all under Ultron's control. E.D.I.T.H. then says that Ultron has killed the fake studio audience.

The whole sequence is bonkers. It reminded me of the way certain sitcoms in the past have ironically roasted their own medium. For example, The George Burns and Gracie Allen Show from the 1950s was breaking sitcom rules just as they were being written, with characters breaking the fourth wall and showing off that they were on a set. It's Garry Shandling's Show from the late 1980s also comes to mind, where the comedian was poking fun of the same cheesy sitcom tropes WandaVision and UltraVision do, with a rowdy studio audience, a self-aware lead and meta commentary on the whole format. The thing that it reminded me of the most, however, was Too Many Cooks, the viral Adult Swim short by Casper Kelly that began as a seemingly never-ending sitcom introduction sequence and devolved into a meta horror slasher that transcended a dozen different TV genres.

While the UltraVision sequence of VisionQuest wasn’t quite as absurdist — or gory — as Too Many Cooks, it was still a very meta deconstruction of the sitcom genre. And while it was a welcome aberration in VisionQuest, it’s the kind of thing WandaVision needed to do to save itself. Rather than abandon all of its originality in that final episode, WandaVision should have gone haywire through the sitcom genre, tearing down what the previous episodes had built up. Instead we got paint-by-number superhero stuff that took more than half a decade to make up for. Thankfully, VisionQuest is here to give us the ending we deserve.

US House blocks bid to impeach Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth, for now

📅 Nov 11, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Watch: Republicans vote to block Hegseth impeachment from reaching full vote

An effort to impeach US Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth, accusing him of waging war on Iran without congressional authorisation, has failed on Capitol Hill.

A majority of the House of Representatives agreed to refer impeachment articles to a committee - essentially killing the effort introduced by dissident Republican congressman Thomas Massie of Kentucky - for now.

In his first news briefing from the Pentagon in six months, Hegseth called the impeachment effort a "farce" and "sabotage".

The bid to find the cabinet secretary guilty of misconduct and ultimately remove him from office was seen as a long shot as it could have required a number of other Republicans to defy President Donald Trump.

Massie was the only Republican to vote against moving the resolution to the Republican-led House Judiciary Committee on Tuesday.

His effort failed in a 211-204 vote, with more than a dozen members, including 11 Democrats, missing the vote.

The move to send the measure to the panel effectively kills the effort, as there is only a short period of time left in this Congress before Democrats take control in January.

President Trump celebrated the vote in a post on Truth Social, saying the effort "has miserably FAILED".

"Again, Victories in Venezuela and Iran, the Best Recruitment Numbers in the History of the Military (Long waiting lists to get in!), and Spirit like rarely seen before, led to the EXONERATION of a Great American Patriot," Trump said, referring to Hegseth.

After the vote, Massie said he "wasn't surprised" but "was disappointed", according to BBC's US media partner CBS.

"My diagnosis of Congress is these people know what to do," he added. "They just can't bring themselves to do it, even when it would benefit them politically."

Massie had previously acknowledged his impeachment bid may not succeed immediately, but argued it could help push Trump to replace Hegseth before Democrats can launch their own impeachment process.

Hegseth: Impeachment effort is "farce" and "stunt"

The Kentucky congressman also said that removing Hegseth now would give the Senate time to approve a replacement while Republicans were still in power.

Impeachment resolutions can be carried past the end of a congressional term, meaning Democrats would be able to take it up next year.

The Democrats will take control of both chambers of Congress in January following a successful midterm election that allowed them control of both chambers.

House Speaker Mike Johnson, a Republican, had predicted earlier in the day that the impeachment bid would fail.

In the eight articles of impeachment formally introduced in the House on Monday, Massie said Hegseth was "breaking the law and must be held accountable" for fighting a war in Iran for more than 90 days without lawmakers' approval.

The impeachment articles also accused Hegseth of overseeing "extrajudicial killings" in the US campaign against boats alleged to be carrying drugs in the Pacific Ocean and Caribbean Sea.

At a news conference on Tuesday, Hegseth called efforts to impeach him a political stunt by a Trump administration opponent who was "disgruntled and defeated".

Hegseth also argued Massie's resolution endangered national security, because it communicated to US adversaries "that the United States will actually punish its own warriors and its own civilian leadership for successfully executing lawful military operations".

"This is not patriotism. This is sabotage," he said.

He echoed some statements Trump had made during a surprise appearance at the White House briefing room on Monday, including touting a rise in military recruitment levels.

But Hegseth said Massie was motivated by policy differences and "not high crimes, not misdemeanours, not treason, not bribery, not the kinds of offences the founders intended when they wrote the impeachment clause".

Massie, who lost re-election earlier this year to a Trump-backed opponent, has been critical of Trump and the war in Iran since it began in February.

He had already incurred the president's ire for demanding the Trump administration release files on deceased sex offender Jeffrey Epstein.

While Hegseth has survived politically for now, many lawmakers - including some Republicans - have questioned how long he can last in the administration.

Texas Senator Ted Cruz, a Republican, said on Tuesday he expected Hegseth to resign or be fired by Trump.

Non può non giocare Spence esterno a destra. In generale non può non giocare un esterno destro in quella zona di campo. E l'Inter è cambiata completamente da quando questo giocatore sta trovando sempre più continuità. È vero che ha giocato anche nel derby, ma nel derby l'invenzione tattica ha completamente scompaginato quelle che sono le certezze di un'Inter. Sento spesso dire che Chivu vuole mettere il suo tocco, che vuole modificare qualcosa. Io credo che l'Inter sia perfetta così. Cioè nel senso bisogna continuare con il 3-5-2 che è uno dei punti di forza dell'Inter che è probabilmente una delle squadre che al mondo esprime meglio questo modulo. Andare a modificare adesso qualcosa credo sia assolutamente un errore perché è una squadra disegnata per giocare solo così. Se cambi qualcosa fa davvero tanta fatica. Potrebbe secondo me al massimo giocare 4-3-3 con Diouf largo e Thuram dall'altra parte, ma è un po' una forzatura. Perderesti Dimarco che ieri ha fatto finalmente un'ottima partita.

Laval, goulûment !

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

En l’emportant samedi soir (2-0), les Verts de Ian Cathro ont prolongé une série d’invincibilité historique.

En effet, l’ASSE recevait Laval pour la 22e fois à Geoffroy Guichard et le bilan est désormais de 17 victoires et 5 matchs nuls.

C’est la plus longue et plus belle série d’invincibilité des Verts dans le Chaudron contre un club de L1, L2 ou L3.

En Ligue 1, chacun des 18 clubs est déjà venu l’emporter au moins une fois à GG. En Ligue 2 en revanche, Annecy (6 matchs) et Dunkerque (5 matchs) n’ont, comme Laval, jamais connu la victoire à Sainté.

En Ligue 3 parmi les clubs déjà venus dans le Chaudron, Concarneau (1 match), La Roche sur Yon (1 match), Bourg-Péronnas (1 match), QRM (2 matchs) et Le Puy (5 matchs) complètent cette liste.
