Lindye Galloway Brings Intimacy to a 10,000-Square-Foot Home

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

At 10,000 square feet, a home can easily feel more expansive than inviting. For a new residence in Dana Point, California, interior designer Lindye Galloway approached that challenge through a careful balance of natural materials, layered textures, and sculptural furnishings. The goal was to create a home that felt as warm and comfortable as it was refined, with a relaxed atmosphere suited to everyday family life.

Galloway drew inspiration from the architecture and natural surroundings, aiming to create a timeless home. The interiors are organized around texture and natural materials in a soft, understated palette. Warm woods, soft neutrals, and natural stone run through the rooms alongside linen, wool, and mohair.

Tactile upholstery and darker accents contrast with the lighter woods and stone, adding depth and dimension to each room. Furniture was selected for comfort and sculptural appeal, with clean lines and soft, organic forms, giving rooms a relaxed, lived-in quality.

The attention to detail extends to the home’s fixtures and finishes. Hardware from Rocky Mountain Hardware and Nanz is complemented by mirrors from Claude Home and Croft House, while lighting comes from Ravenhill, Atelier de Troupe, and Roll & Hill.

The kitchen, primary bathroom, and living room carry the same combination of wood, stone, and natural fibers. Beyond the main rooms, the property includes a separate guest house with its own bedroom and an indoor-outdoor living area. Galloway extended the same material palette to these spaces, creating continuity throughout the property.

Rather than relying on dramatic gestures, Galloway lets the interplay of materials, textures, and forms define the interiors. It’s an approach that brings warmth and intimacy to a home of considerable scale.

3 ways DDoS attacks knock websites offline, from clogged links to busy servers

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Volumetric, protocol and application layer attacks each exhaust a different part of a site. Here is what fails first and why.

A website can go offline without anyone breaking into it. No data is stolen and no password is cracked. The site simply receives more traffic than it can handle, and real visitors cannot get through. Attackers no longer need much skill or equipment to do this. Help Net Security reports that DDoS-for-hire platforms and hijacked internet-connected gadgets, known as IoT devices, make large attacks easier to launch. The danger to a site therefore depends less on who the attacker is than on how much traffic that person sends and which part of the site's connection it is aimed at.

Defending a site is mostly a problem of recognition, not strength. Any operator can block traffic. The hard part is blocking only the hostile traffic, because a flood of requests from an attacker can look just like a rush of real customers after a sale or a news story. An operator who shuts out everyone during an attack ends up with the same outage the attacker wanted, only by the operator's own hand. That is why a crude fix rarely helps. The defender has to keep serving the people who belong on the site while turning away the people who do not, and that has to happen while the attack is still under way.

Cloudflare, which sells protection against these attacks, sorted the main ways attackers use DDoS attacks against different parts of a connection, from the work of generating web pages to the bandwidth that links a site to the wider Internet and the firewalls between them. "The key concern in mitigating a DDoS attack is differentiating between attack traffic and normal traffic," Cloudflare said. That concern applies to every kind of attack, but the failure itself differs from one kind to the next. In one case, "the site went down" means a link too full to carry anything. In another, it means equipment too busy keeping track of connections. In a third, it means a server too occupied answering requests to serve real visitors. Knowing which failure is under way shapes what a defense has to tell apart and how quickly it has to act.

The crudest way to take a site offline never touches the site itself. In a volumetric attack, attackers fill the link that connects a target to the rest of the Internet until it has no capacity left, Cloudflare explains, and requests from real visitors never get through. The attack becomes a contest of raw size. A server can work perfectly and still be unreachable when the attacker sends more traffic than its connection can carry.

Few attackers control enough traffic to win that contest, so they borrow it. Some send requests from botnets, which are networks of hijacked machines that send traffic from many places at once and can't be shut off at a single source. Others use amplification. Floods and amplification attacks that abuse DNS, the Internet's address-lookup system, made up 34.3% of network-layer attacks, according to Help Net Security. Attacks built on DNS are therefore a large part of what defenders face at the network layer. Either method leaves a connection with no room for legitimate traffic.

The typical flood is small and short, and the giant attacks are rare. "Despite the hyper-volumetric growth, the median DDoS attack Cloudflare mitigated in the first half of 2026 remained short and small with 96.62% of network-layer attacks remaining under 500 Mbps and 90.60% ending in under 10 minutes," Cloudflare said, as reported by Help Net Security. The rare cases are becoming more common, and attacks larger than 1 Tbps rose significantly over the same period. A site that handles routine attacks has passed the common test but not the hard one, and that record says little about whether it could withstand an attack large enough to use up all its bandwidth.

A short attack leaves people no time to react. Many attacks were over in less than a minute, according to Help Net Security. An attack that brief can end before any engineer has a chance to step in. A defense against floods has to run automatically and all the time, and it has to decide what to let through without waiting for a person.

Against the bluntest attack, how fast a defender judges traffic matters as much as how much bandwidth it has.

A protocol attack takes a site down by overworking the machines that sit between its Internet connection and its web pages. These attackers don't try to fill the link to the wider Internet, and they don't try to make the server build pages until it runs out of effort. Cloudflare reports that these attacks use weaknesses in network layers 3 and 4, and that they use up the capacity of a site's servers, its firewalls or both. Those layers are the networking machinery beneath the web pages themselves, where traffic gets handled before any visitor sees a page. What gives way, then, is the site's own equipment. The machines run out of room to work, even though nobody has broken into them and nothing has been stolen.

Some of the machines that attackers overload exist to guard and organize the very traffic that swamps them. "Protocol attacks cause a service disruption by over-consuming server resources and/or the resources of network equipment like firewalls and load balancers," Cloudflare said. A firewall meant to screen out hostile traffic, or a load balancer meant to spread visitors across servers, can become the bottleneck itself. That changes how a site operator should read an outage. When attackers exhaust the machines rather than the bandwidth, a site that goes dark isn't simply short of connection space. Buying a bigger connection on its own leaves the weak point in place, because the equipment behind that connection still can't keep up with the work it is handed.

For a site under this kind of attack, the useful question is not only how much traffic arrived but which machine ran out of capacity to handle it.

In an application layer attack, attackers go after the server's own effort to build web pages, not the connection to the Internet or the equipment in front of the server. Cloudflare reports that this attack targets web page generation, the work a server does to answer HTTP requests. Attackers keep those requests coming until the server has no effort left for the people who actually want to use the site. These are the same HTTP requests a visitor's browser sends to load a page, which makes the hostile traffic the same kind as the legitimate traffic. A site under this attack is not jammed. Its server is too busy doing its normal job for the wrong people.

Attackers here count on the cost of answering a request as much as on the amount of traffic. Building a page for a visitor takes work, and attackers aim at that work. "The goal is to overwhelm the target with requests, since a single HTTP request can be expensive for the target server to respond to," Cloudflare said. The danger in this attack depends on how much work each request forces the server to do, not only on how many requests arrive.

Defending against this attack means judging requests, not only limiting them. Rate limiting, a general defense against DDoS attacks, lets a server take only so many requests within a given period. That cap keeps the workload within what the server can handle, but it limits how many requests get through, not which ones. Another general defense is a web application firewall, which filters traffic by rules. That filtering is the step that tries to separate hostile requests from real ones.

Against an attack made of the same requests a real visitor sends, protecting a site comes down less to absorbing traffic than to recognizing it.

Craigslist founder Craig Newmark has given away $500M. He hopes the midterms were ‘the beginning of the end’ for big money in politics

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Craig Newmark, who founded Craigslist in 1995, has given away more than $500 million, with some of that money going toward protecting American elections—and pigeons. Now Newmark is hoping the 2026 midterms mark a turning point for big money in politics.

Asked what Elon Musk’s midterm spending says about the limits of big money, Newmark said Tuesday at the Fortune 500 Innovation Forum in Detroit that he wants to see it as “the beginning of the end of the influence of really big money.”

“That may or may not be realistic,” added Newmark, who signed the Giving Pledge late last year. “We won’t know for another 10 years or more.”

Musk spent roughly half a billion dollars on the midterms and had little to show for it, considering most of his donations went to Republicans. Historically, money usually wins. In 2020, the top-spending House candidate won about 88% of the time.

Newmark said he has stepped back from trying to influence Washington himself.

“I just decided that for myself, Washington politics is no country for old nerds,” he told Fortune Editorial Director Kristin Stoller, who moderated the panel. “My prior efforts have all failed, with the exception of where I chat with the people on the front lines of fighting off attacks from our foreign adversaries.” Even there, he said, “I’ve been told to keep my mouth shut.”

The foundation lists voter protection among its priorities, and election security and fighting disinformation have also been focuses of his giving. Just last week, he made a five-year, $28 million pledge to the Bob Woodruff Foundation.

Stoller opened the panel with a clip of billionaire Pershing Square CEO Bill Ackman telling Fortune Editor-in-Chief Alyson Shontell that philanthropy is “vastly less efficient at solving problems than capitalism,” from an interview published in August.

Asked whether Ackman had a point, Newmark said: “I do think he’s right at times.” But Newmark added that AI safety and cybersecurity have become “a kind of big national security thing,” and that “neither industry nor government is getting the job done.” He said he’s spending the next two days in Washington on the issue, “helping people much smarter than me.”

Cybersecurity has long been a priority for Newmark. In 2022, his foundation committed more than $50 million to a coalition of groups working to protect Americans from cyberattacks.

“I really am a nerd, and I’m not the right guy for this,” Newmark said. “But I’m building back-channel relationships with AI companies and others to get the work done. Like the Batman says, I’m not the nerd you want, but I’m the nerd you got.”

Meanwhile, Ford Foundation President Heather Gerken—whose organization was created in 1936 by Henry and Edsel Ford and funded with Ford company stock—argued philanthropy and capitalism aren’t at odds.

“Philanthropy is the product of capitalism, but it also is what makes capitalism possible,” Gerken said during the panel. She’s currently leading the biggest overhaul of the $18 billion foundation since 2015, and on Tuesday, said the Ford Foundation is making big bets on democracy and the rule of law, economic mobility, and innovation, while changing how it works to be more nimble and deliver “surge funding” where it’s needed.

But Gerken also said philanthropy shouldn’t take credit for the midterm results.

“Philanthropy should not be taking any credit for a win of one party or another,” she said. “If they did, they’re breaking the law.” But when people say the election went smoothly, “what they’re missing is the invisible work that made that possible,” she said, pointing to lawyers who fought challenges to state election systems.

As for what’s next, Newmark said he’ll keep working to protect the country from AI-based cyberattacks while supporting veterans and military families—“and seriously, pigeon rescue.”

Sydney Lake is an associate editor at Fortune, where she writes and edits news for the publication's global news desk.

Should I buy a gaming console just to play GTA 6? That depends on your FOMO.

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Grand Theft Auto VI is out extremely soon, if you hadn't heard. Are you prepared?

By that, we mean do you have a PlayStation 5 or Xbox Series console at the ready for Thursday's big launch? If not, you're going to miss out on the biggest gaming launch of this year and many other years. FOMO is a natural human reaction to these sorts of things, and here at Mashable, we fully understand the pain of not being sure whether or not you should buy a console just for GTA 6.

So, let's lay out the cases for and against doing that.

The primary and most obvious reason to buy a console for GTA 6 is that it's the only real way to play it at launch on Nov. 19. There is technically another way that we'll cover in a little while, but for all intents and purposes, you cannot have the same experience everyone else is having without buying a PS5 or Xbox Series console.

We don't know this yet for sure, but there's a possibility that GTA 6 is an incredible video game. You probably wouldn't want to miss out on that. What are you going to do while everyone else is having an amazing time parading through Leonida, read books? Come on, that sounds boring.

One thing that's also worth remembering is that other video games exist. A lot of them, in fact. If you buy a PS5 or Xbox Series console, you'll have access to the last six years of current-gen releases, some of which are extremely good. They might even be better than GTA 6! As a fun bonus, through the magic of backward compatibility, ports, remasters, and remakes, you'll also be able to play countless video games from previous generations of hardware, too.

Lastly, both consoles (at least the models with disc drives) work as 4K Blu-ray players, if you're into collecting physical versions of movies. That's cool, too.

Both consoles have been hard to find in stock (thanks in large part to GTA 6), but the Xbox Series S and PS5 Digital Edition are both in stock at Amazon, as of this writing.

First, foremost, in the biggest, boldest font you can imagine: You shouldn't buy a console just for GTA 6 because they're stupid expensive these days.

Both Xbox and PlayStation have recently increased the prices of their consoles due to real-world economic concerns. The cheapest model right now is a $499 Xbox Series S, and Rockstar hasn't shown us that version of the game yet, leading to speculation that the game is better on PS5, which starts at $599. If you want the best possible version at launch, you'll need a PS5 Pro, which costs $899 — and is currently out of stock at most retailers.

In other words, there just isn't a particularly affordable way to play GTA 6 traditionally at launch, unless you already own the hardware.

There are, of course, other reasons to not get a console for GTA 6. There exists a slim likelihood that the game just isn't that good, though again, we have no idea at the time of publication. It also won't have an online component at launch, and no one knows when it will get one. For a lot of people, GTA Online is the only GTA experience that matters, and that audience will have very little to do in the new game until some nebulous point in the future when Rockstar decides to cater to them.

Maybe you just don't care that much about non-GTA video games, either. If that's the case, it's hard to recommend spending this much money on a box that will only play one game.

There are two main ways to play GTA 6 without buying a PS5 or Xbox Series machine. One of them doesn't sound all that great, while the other one will require some patience.

To be clear, there are multiple massive reasons why you might not want to do this. For starters, it involves a monthly subscription. Beyond that, Microsoft recently imposed strict monthly hour limits on Cloud Gaming. With this system, you will be able to play at most 15 hours of GTA 6 per month. Many people are going to play more than that in the first weekend it's out. Even if those aren't concerns, game streaming is subject to the limits of your home internet, meaning that latency and buffering can and will occur. That version of the game will also be visually downgraded to some degree, per Rockstar.

If game streaming (understandably) doesn't work for you, the last option is to wait for the PC version. Unfortunately, Rockstar has not said anything about when GTA 6 will launch on PC, though a recent report pegged it as coming in early 2027. That will likely be the best version of the game in terms of visuals, but only if you have the requisite hardware. For those who already have capable gaming PCs, this might be the best option for playing GTA 6, provided you're patient enough to wait. For those who don't, this is going to be even more expensive than buying a console, so maybe don't bother.

Emergency Broadcast System Test Still On For Today, Homeland Security Says

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Politics  |  Read Source →

Executive Editor, Legal, Labor & Politics

Despite a series of technology outages the past week on email systems, aviation communications, airlines, banks, national security networks, and more, the Federal Emergency Management Agency says the long scheduled test today of the Emergency Broadcast System will go ahead.

“This is a nationwide test of the Emergency Alert System, issued by the Federal Emergency Management Agency,” the one-minute long message will states, FEMA said. “This is only a test. No action is required by the public.”

Set to hit at 11:20 a.m. PT/2:30 p.m. ET, the test will appear on “radio and television broadcasters, cable systems, satellite radio and television providers and wireline video,” notes the Department of Homeland Security. According to FEMA, there will be a brief interruption, but “No blackouts, no secret emergencies” connected to the test. Watch on Deadline

FYI – FEMA falls under the jurisdiction of the DHS.

We’ve heard a lot of rumors about tomorrow’s EAS test. 📺📻 Let’s clear things up. It lasts 1 minute, and no action is required. No blackouts, no secret emergencies. Just our legally mandated, routine test on TV and radio at approximately 2:20 PM ET: https://t.co/WGD7xCuRDB pic.twitter.com/HEErLQVKga

“During an emergency, every second is crucial,” FEMA Administrator Cameron Hamilton said in a statement Monday. “FEMA’s alert and warning systems ensure that no second is wasted getting you the critical information needed to keep you safe. Operational tests, like the one to be conducted tomorrow, allow us to identify and fix potential issues before an actual emergency occurs.”

As you might recall from 2023, the feds are obliged by the IPAWS Modernization Act of 2015 to conduct a EAS test every three years. A more localized test of today’s test took place in Alaska with no problems.

A further safeguard is in place that if today’s test encountered difficulties. FEMA explained back in September: “If an actual emergency or significant weather event occurs on test day, FEMA and the FCC may postpone the test. The backup date for the nationwide test is December 3, 2026.”

8 Biggest Snubs in the 2026 Game Awards Nominations

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The Game Awards has announced its official list of nominees for this year’s prizes. Among the nominees are plenty of obvious contenders, such as Grand Theft Auto 6, Resident Evil Requiem, and The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time. There was stiff competition across several categories, meaning that some worthy games — and Polygon favorites — didn’t make the shortlist for certain prizes. These are the biggest TGA nomination snubs of 2026.

As one eagle-eyed Resetera user pointed out, Sony just broke a streak that dates all the way back to 2015. This marks the first time in 11 years that a first-party PlayStation release hasn't received a Game of the Year nomination. During that 11-year streak, Sony took home three GOTY trophies: God of War (2018), The Last of Us Part 2 (2020), and Astro Bot (2024). This year's big first-party game from PlayStation was Marvel's Wolverine, which is currently sitting at a 75 on Metacritic and incited a whole lot of debate about difficulty and UI design. The game was nominated in two categories: Best Performance for Liam McIntyre and Innovation in Accessibility.

This outcome won't come as a surprise to Polygon's Oli Welsh, who predicted Marvel's Wolverine missing out on a GOTY nomination and snapping Sony's impressive streak all the way back in September.

There were a ton of fantastic video game performances this year, and somebody’s favorite was bound to get left out. Fire Emblem: Fortune’s Weave fan favorite Jimmie Yamaguchi gave an outstanding performance as the oddball scholar Fabio, and by every measure, it deserved a nomination. In less capable hands, Fabio’s starry-eyed devotion to Fortune’s Weave co-protagonist Dietrich could have been played for laughs, but Yamaguchi makes the budding romance between these men believable and heartfelt.

Manni L. Perez was nominated for GTA 6, presumably for portraying Lucia Caminos. (Funnily enough, developer Rockstar Games has not yet officially confirmed Perez is actually playing Lucia, despite longstanding online rumors.) Apparently, the actor who played co-lead Jason Duval didn’t make enough of an impact with players to score a nomination.

Blizzard’s World of Warcraft has had a massive year, with the launch of its reimagined Classic experience, World of Warcraft: Forever. The team also released major updates for the modern version of the beloved MMORPG, like March’s Midnight expansion. Those changes weren’t enough to move the needle with voters, and WoW was also left out of the Best Community Support category.

The popular farming sim, which entered early access in 2024 before its full release this year, may have more than 26,000 “overwhelmingly positive” reviews on Steam, but that wasn’t enough for it to snag a nomination in any category. Best Debut Indie might have been Fields of Mistria’s best shot at a prize, but it wouldn’t have been out of place in Best Sim/Strategy or the broader Best Indie category, either.

Despite a wobbly third act, Fortune’s Weave is one of our favorite games of 2026, one of the best games available for the Nintendo Switch 2, and one of the best-reviewed games of the year. Surprisingly, developer Intelligent Systems’ epic strategy RPG only managed to pick up one nomination, for Best Role-Playing Game. It stands a decent chance of taking this one home: other nominees include Final Fantasy Resonance, Monster Hunter Stories 3, The Blood of Dawnwalker, and Trails in the Sky 2nd Chapter.

Pearl Abyss’s open-world adventure game was an early-year frontrunner for a GOTY nom, even after middling reviews and a rocky launch. While it wasn’t a critical darling, it did have a pretty massive online following that made the prospect of several nominations seem likely. Surprisingly, Crimson Desert will compete in just one category: Best Community Support.

Bungie’s extraction shooter managed to snag a nomination for Best Multiplayer Game, which it frankly stands little chance of winning against a field that includes two indie chart-toppers and a well-regarded Call of Duty. However, the stylish game was overlooked in the Best Art Direction category, perhaps partly due to the shadow cast by a 2025 plagiarism scandal where Bungie admitted to using an artist’s designs without proper credit. (Bungie later credited her as a Visual Design Consultant.) Marathon’s retro-future meets Y2K cyberpunk aesthetic truly stands out in a sea of Unreal Engine 5 homogeneity, and it’s a shame to see it overlooked here.

Both the Best Indie and Best Debut Indie categories have some stiff competition this year. However, no independent game managed to get a nomination for Game of the Year in 2026. While there weren’t a ton of indie contenders for the top category this year, Polygon’s Oli Welsh had the co-op adventure Big Walk pegged as a dark horse, though developer House House’s recent AI controversy likely didn’t help matters.

The lack of an indie GOTY nominee represents a pretty massive shift from the 2025 crop, which included a record-setting three games developed by independent studios: Hades 2, Hollow Knight: Silksong, and Clair Obscur: Expedition 33. Sandfall Interactive’s much-loved RPG managed to take home the top prize and a total of nine Game Awards, the most wins by a single game. 2024 also saw one indie crack the category, Balatro, though the top honors went to Astro Bot.

The Game Awards 2026 will stream live on Disney+, YouTube, and Twitch, on Thursday, Dec. 10, starting at 7:30 p.m. EST / 4:30 p.m. PST.

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Prime Video's Madden gets flagged with a record penalty.

In the new sports biopic, Christian Bale and Nicolas Cage will portray NFL legends Al Davis and John Madden, respectively. The story follows the titular real-life legend who gets involved in developing the now-famous video game franchise, Madden NFL. Now, ahead of its release, the first reactions are in.

With 13 reviews, Madden debuts with an abysmal 15% on Rotten Tomatoes, currently setting a record as Bale's lowest-rated movie. The previous low was a 25-year-old movie, Captain Corell's Mandolin, which also featured his co-star Cage in the lead role. However, the score will still fluctuate as more reviews become public.

Additionally, it's the second-lowest Rotten Tomatoes score for controversial director David O. Russell, who reunites with Bale for the fourth time. This would also be their second straight dud together after Amsterdam received a poor reception in 2022.

Unfortunately, the approach to Madden didn't bode well with critics, who found the biopic tonally inconsistent, lacking dramatic stakes, and one-dimensional. Even the star-studded ensemble couldn't salvage much of the movie's substantial flaws, as Cage's fat suit and prosthetics were deemed too cartoonish rather than sophisticated. However, Bale and Kathryn Hahn's sharper performances are the few bright spots.

In ScreenRant's review of Madden, Matthew Rudoy was one of the few critics who enjoyed the sports biopic, praising Cage for capturing the legend's "high energy, verbose passion, and many eccentricities." More importantly, he and Bale have "fantastic chemistry that successfully makes Davis and Madden's friendship the heart of the movie." Although the movie "struggles more in the second half," it earned a 6 out of 10 stars.

Written by Cambron Clark and Russell, Madden isn't without controversy. When the movie was filming in May 2025, there was a report that an actor had walked off set after refusing to do a nude scene and that the filmmaker allegedly used a racial slur during a monologue. This incident apparently caused other crew members to exit the set. However, those reports have been disputed.

Unfortunately, 2026 just hasn't been a great year for the Oscar winner. On top of Madden's poor reviews, his Frankenstein adaptation, The Bride!, also polarized critics, though it landed a higher 57%. Nevertheless, Bale hasn't had his best filmography in the last few years, with his projects earning mixed reviews.

However, that could turn with his upcoming appearance as LAPD Detective Vincent Hanna in Michael Mann's sequel Heat 2. Filming is expected to start this month, and the film will feature a star-studded cast of Leonardo DiCaprio, Ebon Moss-Bachrach, and Jason Clarke. Bale also takes over the role from Al Pacino, who previously portrayed the character in the original 1995 movie.

On the other hand, Cage also has a packed filmography for the next few years, including Best Pancakes in the Country, Lords of War, and Fortitude.

Madden will be released on Prime Video on November 18, 2026.
