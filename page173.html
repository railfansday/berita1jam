A Hidden Cancer-Fighting Switch Could Supercharge the Human Immune System

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Temporarily activating a protein best known for preventing cancer helped engineered human immune cells kill melanoma cells more effectively in the lab, suggesting a way to improve existing cell therapies.

Engineering a patient’s immune cells to recognize cancer does not guarantee that they will keep fighting it. Repeated exposure to cancer can gradually weaken T cells’ ability to multiply and attack tumors, a decline known as exhaustion. The laboratory process used to prepare these cells for treatment can also make them mature too quickly, reducing their chances of surviving and remaining effective over time.

These problems limit engineered T-cell therapies, including chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T-cell therapy and T-cell receptor (TCR) therapy. Both involve collecting a patient’s immune cells, genetically modifying them in a laboratory to recognize cancer, and infusing them back into the patient to attack tumors. Such treatments have transformed care for certain blood cancers and are increasingly being investigated for solid tumors.

Researchers at the UCLA Health Jonsson Comprehensive Cancer Center have identified a possible way to improve these cells during preparation. Their study, published in Frontiers in Immunology, found that temporarily activating a protein called p53 helped human T cells develop characteristics associated with longer-lasting immune responses and become more effective at killing melanoma cells in the laboratory.

Often called the “guardian of the genome,” p53 is best known for preventing damaged cells from becoming cancerous. Scientists have extensively studied that protective role, but its involvement in regulating human T cells is less understood.

The team collected human T cells and treated them with navtemadlin, an investigational drug that increases p53 activity by blocking another protein, MDM2. Normally, MDM2 keeps p53 levels under control. Comparing treated and untreated cells allowed the researchers to assess how activating p53 affected their development, energy production, and ability to fight cancer.

To test cancer-killing performance, the researchers genetically engineered T cells to recognize a protein found on certain melanoma cells. They then used specialized imaging equipment to monitor the cells for nearly seven days.

The engineered T cells prepared with navtemadlin were significantly more effective at killing melanoma cells than untreated cells. Their advantage became apparent after approximately 40 hours and continued throughout the experiment. They also showed improved cancer-fighting activity without additional interleukin-2, a protein commonly used to support T-cell growth and survival.

“This is one of the first studies to link p53, one of the most extensively studied proteins in biology, to T-cell exhaustion in humans,” said Joseph Crompton, MD, PhD, assistant professor of surgery at the David Geffen School of Medicine at UCLA and senior author of the study. “While p53 has long been known for its role in preventing cancer, our findings suggest it may also play an important role in helping immune cells maintain their ability to fight it. This opens up a new avenue for developing more effective cancer immunotherapies.”

The treated cells had greater energy reserves and were more likely to produce interferon-gamma and granzyme B, two cancer-fighting proteins that help immune cells coordinate an attack and destroy cancer cells.

They were also more likely to develop into central memory T cells, which have a greater capacity to survive and multiply when they encounter their target again. Along with fewer molecular signs of exhaustion, this shift suggested that the cells might remain effective for longer. Those characteristics indicate potential for lasting immune responses, although the experiments tested human cells in the laboratory rather than treatment outcomes in patients.

Adding a drug such as navtemadlin during cell preparation could potentially fit into existing manufacturing processes. That would offer a way to improve engineered T-cell therapies without developing an entirely new treatment or introducing additional genetic modifications.

At lower concentrations, navtemadlin encouraged the development of healthy memory T cells without substantially reducing the overall number of viable cells. Higher concentrations, however, caused more cells to die, showing why any use of this approach would require careful control of p53 activation.

Reference: “p53 activation via pharmacologic MDM2 inhibition constrains exhaustion and enhances antitumor function of human CD8+ T cells” by Alexandra Boix de Jesus, Hy B. American Airlines Quietly Removes Trump From West Palm Beach Gate Monitors

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

American Airlines has updated the gate monitors for flights to West Palm Beach, and they no longer mention President Trump. This seems like a sensible change (unrelated to politics), and the timing sure is… interesting.

Over the summer, we saw Palm Beach International Airport (PBI) renamed President Donald J. Trump International Airport. We knew that Trump wanted an airport named after him, and some conservative politicians in Florida came together to make it happen.

It’s interesting to see how airlines handled this. At the time, American updated its gate monitors for flights to West Palm Beach. The monitors initially read “West Palm Beach, FL.” American then updated the monitors to say “Pres DJT, W Palm Beach.” Now JonNYC reports that as of this week, the airport name listed on the monitors has been changed again, and is back to the original name, with no mention of Trump.

JonNYC specifically emphasizes that he’s not claiming there’s any connection between this and the election results, though the timing of it happening this week sure is interesting.

Personally I’m not a fan of airports being named after even semi-controversial figures, and particularly politicians. It’s something I’ve said over and over, regardless of the party affiliation of the person. Unless a city has multiple airports, I think it’s also stupid to identify the airport based on the name rather than the place.

Like, it makes sense to clarify London Heathrow (LHR) vs. London Gatwick (LGW), but it’s not like there are multiple airports in West Palm Beach. For that matter, saying you’re “flying to President Trump” just sounds a little weird, in my opinion.

JonNYC notes that he’s not suggesting that this had anything to do with the recent election, though everything about the timing here is interesting:

Nvidia Investors Need to Be Paying Attention on Nov. 17

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Mark your calendar. Nvidia is on the verge of a business update that will be incredibly important for the stock market.

Nvidia (NVDA -3.17%) is on the verge of a crucial moment. On Nov. 17, it will publish its third-quarter earnings results, and this event has huge implications for the whole stock market.

As the leading provider of the high-powered processing hardware used to train and power today's most advanced artificial intelligence (AI) models, Nvidia has become the single most important company to watch when it comes to the health of both the AI industry and the broader market. That means that its upcoming report could spur substantial movement in the company's share price, and could also have a reverberating impact on many other holdings in investors' portfolios.

Stock valuations in the AI industry have experienced an incredible run-up, but investors have recently been questioning the sustainability of their growth-dependent valuations. Along with macroeconomic dynamics, the market is wavering on AI-specific dynamics, including monetization efficiency, financing, and electricity sourcing. Nvidia's stock price has pulled back as these concerns have intensified, and it's now down roughly 13.5% from the high that it reached last month. In this environment, the company's upcoming report is poised to be an important valuation test.

With the Q2 report that it published at the end of August, Nvidia issued strong guidance for the third quarter. Sales were forecast to grow by roughly 106% year over year to approximately $108 billion. The company also said that it expected a non-GAAP (adjusted) gross margin of roughly 74%.

Nvidia's history of big earnings and revenue beats is creating high expectations for its third-quarter report. Simply hitting the company's guidance targets or equaling the average Wall Street analyst estimates would probably provoke a bearish response. Investors are looking for the company to deliver strong indications that demand for advanced AI processors and related technologies remains sky-high. With AI stocks faltering recently, Nov. 17 won't just be an important date to watch for Nvidia investors -- it will be an important day in determining where the stock market heads next.

He Trolls AI Labs for a Living. Now They All Want to Work With Him

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

OpenAI, Anthropic, and xAI have all asked creator Matt Husk to collaborate following the viral success of his videos trolling AI models.

During the WIRED World Fair on Wednesday, Husk told the crowd “all the big AI companies” have inquired about working with him, including Elon Musk’s xAI, which Husk claimed offered a Tesla for a week if he made videos exclusively for the firm.

The comedian has gained some 2 million followers combined across Instagram, TikTok, and YouTube, as well as a deal with the talent agency Unicorn, through videoing himself making deadpan requests to ChatGPT’s voice model—asking it to count to 100, or to beatbox, for example. His viral videos have exposed the weaknesses of OpenAI’s LLM, which comically botches the tasks and swings between sycophancy and gaslighting Husk, and earned him the title of “Sam Altman’s worst nightmare.”

In April, Altman, nodding and smiling, admitted on tech podcast Mostly Human that the model’s inability to set an accurate timer was a “known issue” and would be for another year, after being shown a Husk video in which he asked ChatGPT to time him running a mile and found that it measured a span of a few seconds as lasting “around 10 minutes.” When Husk says “I swear I was faster than that,” it replies with a sympathetic tone: “It can feel faster in the moment.”

During his interview at WIRED World Fair with Shell Game podcast host Evan Ratliff, Husk said OpenAI offered him early access to a patch for the timer issue if he shared his account. He declined. The company had previously emailed Husk asking to collaborate on a video. “They offered me the smallest amount,” he said. “I was just like, ‘No.’” The firm then offered to send him gifts and asked for his home address; Again, Husk declined.

OpenAI, Anthropic, and xAI (which was recently rolled into SpaceX to create SpaceXAI and then SpaceXSI after President Trump renamed artificial intelligence “super intelligence”) did not respond to requests for comment.

AI labs are increasingly pitching their products as capable of handling everyday chores. On Tuesday, OpenAI announced its model had scored “very highly” on a benchmark built to model its ability to help people in their personal lives, such as meal prep and enrolling a child in kindergarten. “The way they promote it is like ‘It can do anything,’” Husk said, “so I’ll put it to the test—see if it can order bread for me.” In one of his videos, Husk asks AI to help him negotiate some bread on Craigslist down from a starting price of $10,000 to $5. The model accepts a price of $400.

Husk began publicly trolling chatbots about a year ago. He told Ratliff, a WIRED contributor, at the time he liked to make TikToks asking people questions, but there weren’t many around while he was working his landscaping job, and he ran out of ideas. So he recorded himself telling ChatGPT a nuclear bomb had gone off. The model’s response, “I’m rooting for you,” and advice to take shelter “was not helpful at all,” he said. “I thought it was a pretty bad video,” he told Ratliff, “and the next morning I had 100,00 views, and I was like, ‘Let’s tell it I got hit by a train.’”

Since then, Husk has secured brand partnerships with CeraVe and Ninja. He also has an upcoming original series with The Onion, where he learns complicated skills, like being a mime, over Zoom. “I'm spending too much time talking to AI,” Husk explained, “so I'm going to branch out and learn stuff from humans.”

While Husk finds himself talking to AI a lot to scope out ideas for videos, “I don't really use it in that creative sense,” such as to generate ideas, he said, because they would all be “too similar.” “I'd rather just have my worst idea at least be my idea rather than AI's idea for a video,” he said.

Lamar Jackson's latest injury raises obvious questions about his future in Baltimore

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

With quarterback Lamar Jackson’s ninth NFL season possibly over after three full games and two first-half exits, obvious questions have emerged regarding his future in Baltimore.

The facts fuel the speculation. He’s under contract for one more season, and the Ravens can’t use the franchise tag or the transition tag to limit his ability to become an unrestricted free agent in March 2028, which is only 16 months away.

If Jackson wants a fresh start at age 31, he’ll be able to have one.

The Ravens made it clear in January that they wanted to extend his contract by the start of the new league year in March 2026. It didn’t happen, for reasons that never became clear because neither Jackson nor the Ravens (at Jackson’s behest) share information about the negotiations.

Before the season began, it seemed that a new contract wa just a matter of time. The Ravens surely would like to do it before the start of the new league year in March, when Jackson’s cap number spikes to $84.34 million. Although the team has the right to do another restructuring that would reduce the 2027 cap number, that would make the dead-cap reckoning even larger as of 2028.

A trade would avoid the final $52 million in cash and cap charges, but the cap realities would make a post-June 1 move the far better option. And there have been whispers of at least one team doing advance homework on a potential pursuit of Jackson in 2027.

The overriding question is whether Jackson’s lingering availability issue changes things for the Ravens. For now, there’s no reason to think it has. When the dust settles on the season, the Ravens will have to revisit that question.

But that will lead to another question. What would they do at quarterback without Lamar Jackson? That simple reality prompted them to give him a market-level deal in 2023, and to want to do it again.

As long as that desire to avoid a future without Lamar Jackson remains, there’s no reason to think he won’t be back in Baltimore in 2027.
