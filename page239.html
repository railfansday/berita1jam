West Ham were right to be ruthless with Nuno, but are they right about Scott Parker?

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Please refresh the page or navigate to another page on the site to be automatically logged inPlease refresh your browser to be logged in

Comment: Some feel it was harsh to sack Nuno now, but truthfully, he could have gone in the summer. The bigger question is whether his reported replacement is the right pick

Want to bookmark your favourite articles and stories to read or reference later? Start your Independent Membership today.

“I am always under pressure, every day,” Nuno Espirito Santo said in the wake of Saturday’s 3-1 loss to West Brom. Not any more, he’s not.

An optimistic outlook on his removal as West Ham coach is that it offers respite. Respite from that pressure, which has never truly waned in his 13 months in charge. It might seem a ruthless move by the east London club to fire him “so early” into the season, but West Ham were right to be ruthless.

But let’s not forget that the Championship season is already a quarter of the way done, and true ruthlessness would have entailed sacking the Portuguese over the summer, after he failed to stop West Ham from plummeting into the Championship – despite taking over from Graham Potter in late September.

The former Nottingham Forest and Tottenham coach never truly steadied the ship in the Premier League. There was barely even a new-manager bounce to be buoyant about, never a moment when Hammers fans could breathe easily, and even the seas of the second tier (which he navigated so well by winning the league with Wolves in 2017/18) proved too choppy this time around.

That is not to say his West Ham side have been disastrous this season, but with immediate promotion essential from a financial standpoint – and in order to keep the best of this squad together – six wins, four draws, and three losses were never going to instil adequate confidence. Eighth place, one quarter of the way through the season, is too risky a spot.

It is OK to drop points intermittently in such a long, arduous campaign, but the move to sack Nuno was motivated by West Ham’s fear of those points practically spilling out underneath them, like marbles.

It would, of course, have been nice for fans to see a move not motivated by fear; perhaps a change in the summer would have felt harsh, but it would have been proactive, and there is an argument it would have felt encouraging in a different sense.

On the topic of encouragement, West Ham’s free-scoring start to the season was a refreshing sight after recent times at London Stadium, and they remain the highest-scoring team in the division, but their goal difference has already been matched.

And for Nuno, 52, and his players, the issue has not just been conceding too many goals, but also the timing of those errors: they have conceded first in nine out of their 16 games in all competitions. That has signalled a concerning fragility, which has tasked West Ham with fighting back far too often – in a season when many expected them to lead from the front, no less.

Admittedly, there was a degree of naivety to this sentiment. Over the summer, it was all too easy for neutral fans to assume that West Ham would walk the Championship, when supporters of the east London club knew how misguided these claims were. You need look no further than West Ham’s previous outing in the Championship, in 2011/12, when they let a top-two finish slip away before going life-and-death with Blackpool in the play-off final.

Again, that is far too risky a spot this time around, and cannot be justified when you own the most expensive squad in the league (at an estimated £282m, with the £18m gap to Wolves proving substantial at this level).

There is surely greater quality at West Ham than any other club in the Championship, and some observers will feel a coach would not need to get too much right to promote the Hammers. In that regard, Nuno was doing too much wrong.

With Scott Parker immediately emerging as the frontrunner to replace him, perhaps the conversation can move away from risk. But only in the short-term, you would think.

Parker, a former Hammer, secured promotion with Fulham, Bournemouth and Burnley in his first season in charge of each club. That record is remarkable. It is simply a shame the stories didn’t end there. Fulham and Burnley were immediately relegated in their subsequent seasons, while Parker left Bournemouth by the end of August after a shocking start to life in the Premier League.

Of course, as a West Ham fan, you might not care to look that far ahead. The priority is purely promotion to the Premier League.

Moreover, a feelgood factor could stem from the nostalgia of Parker’s return. To quote a great modern sketch by Exploding Heads: “He knows the club. He knows the ground... even though it’s different than when he was there.”

In all seriousness, there would be genuine appreciation towards the former midfielder, who won the fan-voted Hammer of the Year award three years in a row. Fans might hope fortunes have been hiding in plain sight.

“Mr West Ham” Mark Noble played alongside the 46-year-old at Upton Park and is now sporting director (he will oversee preparations for Wednesday’s game versus Cardiff). Would Noble and Parker have the chemistry in the office that they had on the pitch? Well, it’s worth noting that they were relegated together in 2011.

Against what you might assume, they would also be unlikely to settle the debate about the “West Ham Way” (chiefly, whether it even exists). While West Ham have scored an unusually high number of goals under Nuno this season, Parker’s MO is clean sheets; that would mark a jarring about-turn, stylistically.

But maybe an about-turn is what West Ham need. They are heading in the wrong direction, even if slowly, and slip-ups cannot be allowed to become a full-on skid. West Ham have problems higher up than in the managerial seat, but finding someone to fill it is the only immediate concern.

EXCLUSIVE: Jim Carrey Lands a Buyer for His $35 Million Hawaii Home—One Month After Putting it on the Market

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Jim Carrey has found a buyer for his sprawling Hawaii waterfront retreat, less than one month after listing the tropical property for $35 million and after tying the knot with his longtime partner, Min Ah.

The 64-year-old "Ace Ventura" star married Ah in an intimate ceremony in Los Angeles, with a representative for the actor confirming the nuptials to People.

Fresh off their wedding, Carrey made another major life change by putting his lavish Maui estate on the market.

Now, Realtor.com® can exclusively reveal that the home is in the "pending" stage, meaning Carrey has found a buyer.

The actor purchased the waterfront property for $16.5 million in 2016, according to records. Its $35 million asking price means Carrey is hoping to more than double his investment after a decade of ownership.

Built in 2002, the three-bedroom, 4.5-bathroom residence spans 4,717 square feet and sits on nearly an acre along Keawakapu Beach.

Listed by Blake Hill of Hawaii Life, the property is being offered fully furnished and is described in the listing as a "bohemian luxury unicorn."

"Close to an acre on Keawakapu Beach, where South Kihei Road ends and Wailea begins. Roughly 170 feet along the water lets the homes stand far back behind a wide green lawn and a grove of tall coconut palms," the listing states.

Surrounded by lush tropical greenery, the estate was designed to embrace seamless indoor-outdoor living while taking full advantage of its oceanfront setting.

Inside, soaring ceilings and an expansive floor plan create an airy atmosphere, while coastal-inspired furnishings complement the home's enviable location.

"The great room rises into the trusses and opens fully to a covered stone lanai," the listing adds

The chef's kitchen features two large islands, white cabinetry, and high-end appliances, while the primary suite takes the home's relaxed aesthetic one step further with a swinging bed and floor-to-ceiling windows.

Its accompanying bathroom is equally luxurious, complete with a statement soaking tub and dual vanities.

The dining area continues the breezy beach aesthetic, with a large wooden table and multiple seating areas providing plenty of space for entertaining.

Outside, the amenities become even more resortlike.

"Between the homes, a freeform pool curves through natural rock, hiding a grotto and a slide, with a shallow ledge for lounging and an elevated spa," according to the listing.

The property was designed by architecture firm Ike Kligerman Barkley, with the listing emphasizing the rarity of its expansive oceanfront setting.

"This bohemian luxury property is truly a unicorn. Oceanfront land like this is uncommon in Maui. Designed by Ike Kligerman Barkley. Offered fully furnished and in excellent condition," the description states.

Carrey's decision to sell comes just after he and Ah marked a major milestone in their famously private relationship.

Their marriage followed a rare public appearance together at the César Awards in Paris in February, when Carrey accepted an honorary award and offered a sweet tribute to his partner from the stage.

While accepting the honor, the comedian thanked his daughter, Jane, and grandson, Jackson, before referring to Ah as his "sublime companion" and declaring his love for her.

The couple has otherwise kept their romance largely out of the spotlight, despite reportedly having been together for several years.

That desire for privacy appears to align with the quieter lifestyle Carrey has embraced away from Hollywood in recent years.

The "Truman Show" star has spent significant time in Hawaii, where his waterfront Maui residence provided a dramatically different backdrop from the Los Angeles lifestyle he maintained for decades.

Reports have painted a picture of a decidedly low-key existence for the actor on the island, with Carrey spending his time reading, writing, walking, painting, and sketching.

That makes his decision to part with the property particularly notable. It remains unclear whether Carrey plans to maintain a home in Hawaii after the sale or whether he will return to his California roots.

In 2022, Carrey revealed that he was "fairly serious" about stepping away from acting, explaining that he had become content with the life he had created outside the relentless pace of Hollywood.

"I really like my quiet life," he said at the time while discussing his passion for painting and his spiritual life.

Carrey had spoken about his affinity for solitude even earlier. In 2018, he described his Maui lifestyle as "an isolated life" while explaining that he enjoyed spending time alone.

His move toward a more private existence also coincided with the end of another major chapter in his real estate portfolio: his decadeslong ownership of an impressive Brentwood estate.

Carrey purchased the Los Angeles property for $3.8 million in 1994—the same year his career skyrocketed with the release of "Ace Ventura: Pet Detective," "The Mask," and "Dumb and Dumber."

Over nearly three decades of ownership, the 2-acre property became an amenity-packed private retreat.

Built in 1951, the ranch-style residence spans more than 10,000 square feet and features five bedrooms, six fireplaces, an Art Deco-style movie theater, and a circular sunroom.

After owning the property for nearly 30 years, Carrey decided it was time to move on. He initially listed the Brentwood estate for $28.9 million in February 2023, revealing at the time that he was no longer spending much time there.

Finding a buyer proved to be a lengthy process. The asking price was subsequently reduced to $26.5 million and then $23.95 million. In May 2024, Carrey lowered the price again to $21.9 million before trimming it to $19.75 million that September. The home ultimately sold for $17 million.

Mortgage rates are constantly changing, since a major part of how rates are set depends on reactions to new inflation reports, job numbers, Fed meetings, global news ... you name it. For example, even tiny changes in the bond market can shift mortgage pricing.

This week, the Nerds will be watching the October jobs report, which comes out on Friday, Nov. 6. If it shows that employment remained steady (or even improved), that will build a stronger case for the Federal Reserve to raise overnight borrowing rates again at their next meeting on Dec. 8-9.

While the Fed doesn’t directly set mortgage rates, it does set the federal funds rate, which is the fee that banks must pay to borrow from one another (which is how they fund mortgages). Lenders raise mortgage rates any time they think that the federal funds rate is about to go up.

Central bankers hiked the federal funds rate in September, so a relatively steady jobs report would suggest the labor market could withstand higher rates.

The Fed has a dual mandate to control inflation while supporting maximum employment, and raising the federal funds rate is their main tool for bringing inflation down. If they can work to lower inflation without hurting employment too badly, central bankers may feel more confident to act.

The September Personal Consumption Expenditures price index (PCE), the Fed’s preferred inflation gauge, was released last Thursday. The data shows inflation was 3.6% year-over-year in September, up from 3.4% in August.

September’s federal funds rate hike came in the middle of the month, so October’s inflation data will really capture its effect on inflation. The October PCE report will come out on Nov. 25.
