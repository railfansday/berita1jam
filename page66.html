HBO’s Harry Potter Officially Confirms 1 Original Movie Star Is Returning

Rowling​​​, the world will settle in for the ultimate Christmas present, as a brand-new, shiny adaptation of Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone debuts. Recently, HBO released the first full clip of the series, as the new students of Hogwarts celebrate Dumbledore (John Lithgow) and begin their first feast in the Great Hall.

Alas, the clip has proven far from satisfying for some fans, who have filed plenty of complaints about the show via social media. Just one name has survived from the original movie cast through to this new adaptation, as Warwick Davis, the man behind Charms Professor Flitwick and Goblin Griphook, returns.

And what was it like to be asked to return? Davis replied:

"To be asked to come back and play him again for the series was a huge honour, and I’m excited to retell these stories because they’re fantastic stories."

The Wachowskis Are Trying to Get The Matrix Back From Skydance

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Twenty-seven years have passed since The Matrix was released in cinemas, changing the entertainment landscape in perhaps more ways than its creators, Lana and Lilly Wachowski, could have envisioned. The Matrix was part of a three-movie deal that the Wachowskis landed with Warner Bros., and the rights to the franchise​​​​​​​ remained firmly in the hands of the studio, recently absorbed by Skydance in a much-discussed merger. But things may change in the future.

As revealed by Eriq Gardner in an article for Puck, the Wachowskis have formally notified Warner Bros. that they are exercising their copyright termination rights. This means that, as early as February 2029​​​​​​​, Warner Bros. could lose the rights to produce and distribute new Matrix movies, which would go back to the creators. With a fifth movie currently in development, helmed by Drew Goddard, a legal battle could have a profound impact on the franchise.

The Wachowskis are not involved with The Matrix 5, but, according to Gardner's undisclosed sources, they "have been encouraging of the newest sequel and have even increased their involvement lately." This means that, despite Lilly's statement in September 2026 that she was "kinda done" with The Matrix, the sisters are still involved in some way​​​​​​​, and their relationship with Warner Bros. isn't necessarily bad.

In fact, the motion to terminate the copyright doesn't mean that Warner Bros. and the Wachowskis will engage in a legal battle over The Matrix. The studio could simply negotiate a fresh grant of rights with the Wachowskis and keep making The Matrix movies. If the issue goes to court, the key will be proving which party can truly be considered the "creator" of the franchise. The commonly reported origin story of the franchise, also mentioned by Gardner in his article, is that a Warner Bros. executive, Lorenzo di Bonaventura, commissioned the Wachowskis to write and direct the movie, after being impressed by their earlier work on Assassins and Bound. Commissioned work​​​​​​​ is ineligible for copyright termination, but proving that in court will be another matter​​​​​​​.

Overall, the most interesting takeaway from this story is that, in 2029, the Wachowskis could start working on a reboot of the Matrix franchise, freed from the shackles of corporate oversight. Meanwhile, there have been no updates about The Matrix 5 in a long time, but speculation points to a 2029 release​​​​​​​. As Morpheus said, "I do not see coincidence, I see providence."

Astronomers Discover a Chilling Preview of Our Solar System’s Future

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Astronomers have discovered an extraordinary ring system surrounding a dead star, offering a possible preview of what awaits our Solar System billions of years from now. Using the European Southern Observatory’s Very Large Telescope (ESO’s VLT), researchers identified two separate rings of planetary debris with a distinct gap between them. The unusual structure may be maintained by two planetary bodies that survived the death of their star, including one potentially as large as Pluto or even the Moon.

“We have known for some time that planets may survive beyond the transformation of their central star, but this is the first example of a complex system with a double debris disc and potentially two surviving planetesimals,” says Nicola Gentille Fusillo, an astronomer at the Università degli Studi di Trieste in Italy and lead author of the study published in Nature Astronomy.

At the center of this remarkable system is J1328−1450, a white dwarf. These incredibly dense stellar remnants are what remain when stars similar to our Sun exhaust their nuclear fuel and reach the end of their lives.

When a Sun-like star approaches the end of its life, it swells dramatically before shedding its outer layers. During this transformation, nearby planets can be swallowed and destroyed. Other rocky objects may survive the initial upheaval, only to break apart later and create rotating discs of debris around the star’s remaining core.

Astronomers have observed such debris discs around white dwarfs before, but J1328−1450 stands out. Instead of a single ring of vaporized planetary material, it has two concentric rings of gas separated by a well-defined gap.

“The structure we see in this system is particularly intriguing because the two gaseous rings are remarkably stable and remain separated by a gap,” says co-author Felipe Lagos-Vilches, an astronomer at the University of Warwick in the UK and at the Universidad Técnica Federico Santa María in Chile.

“The system is a rare glimpse of the violent, chaotic process by which a planetary system meets its end, and it hints that dead stars can have disc structures far more complex than previously realized,” adds Gentile Fusillo, who is also affiliated with the INAF-Astronomical Observatory of Trieste.

What keeps the two rings separated? The researchers suspect that two surviving planet-like bodies, traveling along different orbits, are helping maintain the unusual arrangement.

The smaller object, associated with the inner ring, could be comparable to a typical asteroid, measuring just a few kilometers across. The larger body, associated with the outer ring, may be roughly the size of Pluto or even Earth’s Moon. Although their presence has not been directly confirmed, these objects could explain why the debris remains divided into two stable rings.

Until now, astronomers had identified debris discs around white dwarfs through a characteristic observational signature associated with a single, flat, rotating disc. However, observations of J1328−1450 revealed something entirely different.

Using the X-Shooter instrument installed on ESO’s VLT in Chile’s Atacama Desert, the researchers detected the distinctive signature of a double disc, showing that planetary debris around dead stars can form more complicated structures than previously recognized.

The rings also contain important clues about the worlds that once orbited the star. Astronomers detected calcium, oxygen, magnesium, and iron, elements commonly found in rocky planetary material throughout our Solar System.

Even more intriguing, the two rings have noticeably different chemical compositions. The inner ring contains abundant magnesium gas, while the outer ring has very little. This difference suggests that the debris feeding the rings may have come from two separate planets with distinct compositions.

Another possibility is that the material originated from different layers of the same planetary body. For example, one ring could contain debris from a planet’s crust, while the other holds material from its core.

The discovery may also reveal something unsettling about our own cosmic future. Billions of years from now, the Sun will undergo a similar transformation, expanding as it nears the end of its life before eventually becoming a white dwarf. Earth could be destroyed during this process, while other planetary bodies may survive and leave behind rings of debris.

The unusual system around J1328−1450 suggests that the aftermath of a star’s death can be far more complex than a simple cloud of shattered planetary remains. Even after a planetary system experiences catastrophic destruction, some of its original bodies may endure and continue shaping the wreckage.

“The system therefore offers a remarkable glimpse of what the future of planetary systems, including systems like our own, might look like,” concludes Lagos-Vilches.

The Premier League’s Key Match Incidents Panel has voted unanimously that Arsenal should have been awarded a penalty against Hull.

However, it could have been a much more comfortable afternoon had Arsenal been given a penalty in the first half, when it was still goalless.

The panel added: “Being in an offside position, in itself, is not an offence.

Harvard GSD students propose alternatives to Trump's arch

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Images by Jeremy Dvorak and Jake Geitner courtesy The Cultural Landscape Foundation

Landscape architecture students at Harvard’s Graduate School of Design have designed two proposals for Memorial Circle in Washington, DC, the site of US president Donald Trump‘s proposed triumphal arch.

Both designs feature expansive pools of water that cover the current vehicular roundabout at Memorial Circle, and emphasise connectivity to the city’s historic processional axis and straight sightlines to the Lincoln Memorial and Arlington Cemetery.

The students, Jeremy Dvorak and Jake Geitner, worked with Washington DC non profit organisation The Cultural Landscape Foundation (TCLF) on the designs. The aim was to engage the public in the design process, as the team views the government did not properly open the arch up for review.

“By recognising Memorial Circle’s implicit expressions of monumentality, this pair of counter proposals asserts design’s capacity to critically engage history, accommodate public life, and advocate on behalf of our shared cultural landscape,” said Dvorak and Geitner.

The project began after Dvork and Geitner attended a seminar at the GSD taught by TCLF founding partner Charles A Birnbaum.

“As graduate students in landscape architecture, we took Birnbaum’s course to ground our emerging practices in critical historical inquiry and public participation,” they said.

“The current administration’s assertion of authority over national memory presented an opportunity to put these convictions into practice.”

The first proposal treats Memorial Circle as “a void along the historic axis between Washington and Arlington”. The designers chose to fill the central roundabout circle, currently covered by grass, with a low-lying pool of water.

A natural stone perimeter surrounds the pool and is inscribed with words of “belonging and unity”.

It’s design is “restrained” according to the team, and primarily meant to be viewed from a vehicle while passing through the traffic circle.

The second proposal does away with the traffic circle entirely and instead inserts a straight thoroughfare to Arlington Memorial Bridge.

Two circular fountains would be located on either side of the road. Additionally, a large pool of water, mirroring the perimeter of the former traffic circle, would fill up the site during commemorative events when the bridge is closed.

It draws upon previous historical proposals for Memorial Circle and seeks to pay homage to the precedent of the site.

“[The fountain] geometries arise from the site’s historic proposals, combining concentric circles, intersecting alignments, and formal planting into a composition that acknowledges history whilst creating its own present identity,” said the designers.

“In this way the landscape becomes a physical record of its own change.”

Trump’s proposed arch was announced late last year with renderings by US studio Harrison Design. It features gilded statues and a neoclassical design and has been dubbed the “Arc de Trump” – a play on the Arc de Triomphe in Paris.

Construction on the arch was set to begin in September with the government barreling ahead, but it was halted in early November by Judge Tanya S Chutkan.

Trump has claimed the arch will serve as a “military complex” with snipers in an attempt to use national security as a pretext for construction.

Many take issue with the arch blocking views to adjacent Arlington Cemetery, including members of the Kennedy family, who filed a lawsuit to block construction, claiming that the arch would cast shadows over the grave of president John F Kennedy.

The US Commission of Fine Arts (CFA), which reviews projects in the capital city, approved the project in May.

The proposals also follow recent efforts by the legislature to change Section 106 of the National Historic Preservation Act, which requires federal agencies to consider the impact of proposed projects on historic properties.

Earlier in November, protesters gathered in Washington DC in opposition of the administration’s proposed rewrite of these protections. They are couched within the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act (BAAJA) bill recently introduced to Congress.

U.S. export controls and Nvidia’s leadership transition remain key watchpoints

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Nvidia’s solid third-quarter results will no doubt reassure Gulf investors that the U.S. chipmaking giant is on course to maintain a healthy growth trajectory despite the resignation of Jensen Huang.

Nvidia reported revenue of $116 billion for the three months to October, up 103% from a year ago, and above Wall Street estimates of $109 billion. It has beaten estimates in each of the last five quarters.

For the Gulf states, which are betting big on AI to help move their economies away from oil and rely on Nvidia’s advanced chips to achieve that, the company’s future success is critical.

Huang’s affair with a woman suspected of being a Chinese spy has heightened political pressure to tighten export controls amid long-running concerns in the U.S. that China’s access to advanced American AI chips could undermine national security.

It is also likely to sharpen scrutiny of Nvidia’s future dealings with the Gulf as part of a broader effort to prevent China from accessing banned American technology via intermediaries.

Such concerns have previously made securing Nvidia’s advanced chips an uphill battle for regional AI champions such as PIF-owned HUMAIN and Abu Dhabi-backed G42.

In July, the U.S. Department of Commerce loosened export restrictions on the UAE so that approved government agencies and entities—including its tech champion G42—can receive advanced computing hardware, including from Nvidia, under broader license exceptions. The new rule set no caps on how many chips or other sophisticated goods can be sold to those entities.

Democratic China hawks have frequently raised the alarm over the large-scale sales of AI chips to the Gulf region, arguing they could give China backdoor access to U.S. technology. Last May, Democratic senators demanded that AI chip deals with Saudi Arabia and the UAE contain adequate guardrails to prevent the leakage of sensitive technology to China.

Both Gulf countries maintain strong commercial ties to Chinese tech entities.

In September, Chinese tech giant Alibaba Cloud announced plans to ramp up its data center capacity and expand localized AI services across the UAE. Meanwhile, in Riyadh, Lenovo unveiled its first laptop made in Saudi Arabia at its sprawling 200,000-square-meter manufacturing facility, developed as part of a $2 billion partnership with Alat, a subsidiary of Saudi Arabia’s Public Investment Fund (PIF).

In its first policy response to the Nvidia scandal, on Monday the U.S. Commerce Department introduced an interim rule requiring licences for global transfers of AI chips and servers to companies in 22 embargoed countries, including China. The move aims to close a loophole that has allowed Chinese companies to lease Nvidia’s computing power from locations in other countries.

With Democrats holding majorities in both chambers of Congress from January next year, increased congressional scrutiny of Gulf AI deals is likely.

More broadly, the Gulf’s exposure to the volatility in AI stock prices has been further rattled by Jensen’s resignation amid a broader sell-off in AI tech stocks over the past few months, on the back of valuation and investment bubble concerns.

While not all Gulf funds disclose their individual investments, publicly available filings suggest that Gulf sovereign wealth funds, such as Mubadala, have relatively limited direct exposure to Nvidia shares compared to the scale of their investments in other tech firms.

According to the consultancy, Global SWF, Mubadala ranks among the top institutional backers of AI worldwide.

MGX, an AI and advanced tech investment firm backed by Mubadala, holds stakes and has participated in funding rounds for OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI, Databricks, GlobalFoundries, Isomorphic Labs, and SpaceX.

Saudi’s PIF-backed AI venture HUMAIN invested $3 billion in xAI’s $20 billion Series E round in February, shortly before it merged with SpaceX.

Global SWF estimates that Mubadala’s portfolio may have declined by $17.5 billion since the onset of the AI selloff at the end of June, largely driven by a more than 50% drop in chipmaker GlobalFoundries’ share price. PIF, meanwhile, is estimated to have suffered a $5.6 billion decline in the value of its AI-related holdings, largely attributable to its stake in SpaceX.

“Indirectly and strategically, Gulf wealth funds are exposed to the current market turbulence,” Sam Winter-Levy, a senior fellow in the Technology and International Affairs program at the Carnegie Endowment for International Peace, told me.

“MGX and other Gulf vehicles hold large stakes in AI labs, data center platforms, and other companies whose valuations depend on the AI boom—and Nvidia, of course, is central to that boom. The Gulf’s national AI strategies are built on Nvidia hardware. I think a prolonged loss of confidence in Nvidia would hit the Gulf relatively hard.”

In a research note published on Monday, London-headquartered Capital Economics warned that the ongoing shakeout in the AI sector is setting the stage for a larger downturn in the U.S. equity market over the next year.

With Nvidia considered a bellwether for the AI industry, its results should help to calm the markets and buoy investor sentiment ahead of Anthropic’s planned listing on Thursday.

Abu Dhabi-backed Masdar has completed its $950 million Lockhart Solar and Storage complex in California, marking another major step in the clean energy giant’s growing U.S. renewables push.

With 365-MW of solar generation and 173.7MW of battery storage, the project can produce electricity to meet the annual needs of about 350,000 homes and businesses.

Commercially, the project is backed by a hybrid of long-term power purchase agreements with California utilities and corporates.

The project was developed by Terra-Gen, which is owned jointly by Masdar and U.K.-headquartered global infrastructure manager, Igneo Infrastructure Partners.

Masdar operates a major part of its U.S. footprint through Terra-Gen, an independent U.S. renewables developer and operator, in which Masdar acquired a 50% stake in 2024 alongside Igneo.

Masdar has already invested around $3 billion in renewable energy projects across the U.S., with a portfolio reaching 6.3 GW across onshore wind, solar, and battery energy storage systems as of Sep. 2026.

It is targeting an expansion of its portfolio by as much as 25-GW by 2030, capitalising on rising power demand from data centers, industrial electrification, and expanding domestic manufacturing.

Globally, Masdar is also pressing ahead with its ambition to reach 100-GW of portfolio capacity by the end of the decade.

The Perfect 'Deadwood' Cast if the Show Were Made in 2026

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

It's been more than 20 years, and Deadwood still remains one of the greatest shows in HBO history. Created by David Milch, the three-season series is set in 1876 Deadwood, South Dakota, and follows the titular camp as it operates as a lawless sinkhole of crime, gambling, and rough-and-tumble survival. The town explodes from a muddy, chaotic tent camp into a bustling settlement driven by a massive gold strike that draws prospectors, outlaws, entrepreneurs, and working women.

Filled to the brim with dramatized figures from history, Deadwood is a masterful mix of Western idealism and the true grit of surviving in a changing moment in history. At its core, the series explores how people piece together rules, commerce, and community from pure chaos when formal government and laws are absent. The heart of the show centers on incredible characters, brought to life by an iconic ensemble. So, what would happen if Deadwood were made in 2026? Though the show is untouchable for devoted fans, this fantasy casting honors the project's heartbeat with a new ensemble of actors who capture the characters' essence.

The true heartbeat of Deadwood came from the heroic man with the badge, Seth Bullock. He arrives in the lawless mining camp as a former Montana marshal looking to start a new life. Alongside his friend and business partner, Sol Star (John Hawkes), he sets up a hardware and general store before becoming Deadwood's first sheriff. Loosely based on the real-life historical figure, much of Seth's story revolves around keeping Deadwood morally afloat. Timothy Olyphant brought him to life perfectly, launching him into an archetype that made him famous. Offering a soft-spoken, gentle demeanor, Olyphant subverted the traditional Western hero. With a physical presence and fixed glare, Olyphant's restraint made Seth's moments of explosion feel earned. He masked a deep internal rage, but he was still quite stubborn.

As one half of the dominating duo on Deadwood, selecting an actor who has the same essence as Olyphant is required. A brilliant selection would be Logan Lerman. His career evolution aligns perfectly with Seth's core tension. He went from boyish charm with a quiet side hiding impulses within. In projects like Hunters and Indignation, Lerman mastered the art of playing characters who look unassuming but harbor boiling, dark intensity beneath the surface. He carries a classic, honorable leading man quality that evokes a trustworthiness that would allow him to lead a camp of chaotic and unpredictable characters. Even early in his career, Lerman excelled at characters with heavy dramatic undertones, namely The Perks of Being a Wallflower, but as he has matured, he has built the gravitas to take on a hardened lawman.

There is no doubt that Al Swearengen is one of the greatest television characters of all time. Some might have felt he was a caricature compared to more grounded characters, but Swearengen's quirks and eccentricities made him genuinely Western. Swearengen is a criminal operator, pimp, and master manipulator who runs the most successful bar and brothel in Deadwood. He is foul-mouthed, violent, and cold-blooded, yet he has a complex code of pragmatism, fierce loyalty to his inner circle, and a driving urge to guide Deadwood toward survival. He's a brilliant antihero who begins the series as the established villain, only to be outdone as new faces arrive. Swearengen is a masterclass in character, thanks to Ian McShane's pitch-perfect performance. Though based on the real-life figure, McShane made him larger than life. He blended a terrifying ruthlessness with theatrical, Shakespearean depth. McShane delivered Milch's dense, rhythmic monologues with a commanding cadence.

With a Machiavellian presence, McShane could have Swearengen command a room atop a balcony, offering a cold calculation like a mob boss. To play Swearengen, an actor with the same power and mastery of the text is required. An actor who loves a good antihero and could reinvent brutal, eloquent, fierce loyalty is Rufus Sewell. He has built a career playing characters who operate in moral grey areas. From his chillingly calculated performance as John Smith in The Man in the High Castle to the charismatic yet deeply flawed Hal Wyler in The Diplomat, he brings a dark magnetism to the screen. Sewell has an imposing, intense gaze and powerful vocal delivery that can pivot from quiet threat to an explosive outburst. As an accomplished stage actor, Sewell has the linguistic dexterity to tackle Swearengen's text. Sewell could easily pull back the layers of the monstrous man to reveal the broken humanity underneath.

One of the more tragic and human characters in the series was Alma Garret. She arrived in the South Dakota gold-mining camp with her husband, Brom Garret (Timothy Omundson), to seek their fortune. After her husband's suspicious death, which was orchestrated indirectly through camp politics, she inherited a rich gold claim, establishing her own bank and independence, as she was not going to be pushed out by men. She struggled with and overcame an addiction to laudanum, had a complicated romance with Seth Bullock, but ultimately formed a unique family when she married prospector Whitney Ellsworth (Jim Beaver) and adopted a young orphan girl named Sofia Metz (Bree Seanna Wall). Bringing a quiet, nuanced strength to a complex frontier world, Molly Parker was anything but fragile. She shone by showing Alma's resilience, then shifting into an emotional palette comprised of fear and grief.

At her core, Alma was a brilliant character because she celebrated her determination, savviness, and sharpness. She had to mature into an independent woman quickly, yet still had an inherent youthful refinement. An actress who could capture that same emotional intelligence is Hailee Steinfeld. And wouldn't it be perfect since her rise to stardom came in a heavy Western, True Grit? Even at a young age, Steinfeld proved she could handle the demanding, rhythmically complex dialogue of high-end Westerns. Alma needs a duality of high-society and internal gruffness. Steinfeld has shown those traits in projects like Dickinson and The Edge of Seventeen, playing women constrained by societal environments but with a rebellious edge. Having previously played in male-heavy, ensemble-driven environments like the Marvel Cinematic Universe and Bumblebee, Steinfeld could capture Alma's core arc of navigating a camp run by aggressive, dangerous men.

Four icons and four completely different ways of walking into trouble. This is the one you would have been out there, why it fits, and what it would have cost you.

You have never explained yourself and you have never needed to, because people work out fairly quickly that you are the most capable person in the room without you ever having to mention it.

You are the funniest person in most rooms and the most loyal person in all of them, and you would be genuinely insulted if somebody suggested those two things were in any tension.

You never wanted this job, and you took it anyway because the alternative was standing there watching somebody less careful do it badly, which is the most Western reason there has ever been.

Nobody has ever successfully talked you out of something you had already decided was right, and a long line of grown adults has found that out the hard way.

There were certainly plenty of bad guys in Deadwood, but there was one man known for being beloved and ethical: Whitney Ellsworth. The outspoken prospector began his journey as a loquacious man; then, when he witnessed Dan Dority (W. Earl Brown) murder Brom Garret, he swooped in to help Alma Garret with her gold claim, ultimately finding a marriage arrangement that changed their relationship and formed a surrogate family in a camp of individuality. His fate was sadly sealed when he went toe-to-toe with George Hearst (Gerald McRaney), standing up for what was right. But in the Wild West, adversity can lead to death. Ellsworth's death remains one of the most devastating events in the entire story. Jim Beaver transformed the minor, unkempt prospector into an emotionally resonant hero. Beaver found a tenderness in Ellsworth that matched the working-class dignity that gave the camp warmth.

Ellsworth was created with great charisma and unpretentious decency. Whoever plays the part must be able to transform from heavy-drinking gold miner to beloved father figure. Shea Whigham would be a perfect fit, as he possesses the exact blend of working-class grit, underlying nobility, and tragic weariness required to build Ellsworth. Whigham has a history in Westerns, having previously appeared in American Primeval and Lawman: Bass Reeves. He wears the dirt, sweat, and moral ambiguity of the Old West naturally. Ellsworth is a tragicomic character, and Whigham has created a career playing deeply flawed men wrestling with guilt and searching for redemption. His performances in Boardwalk Empire and Lake George prove he can tackle an individual beaten down by life but still capable of good. Whigham's masterful resume in ensemble pieces is further proof that his generosity would be welcome in this profoundly brilliant series.

The camp was filled with a lot of eccentric personalities, often defined by their trade. As one of the most needed individuals in Deadwood, Dr. Amos "Doc" Cochran was the sole physician in the mining camp. A former Civil War battlefield doctor, Doc is deeply scarred by violence, suffering, and amputations witnessed during the war. He is honest, compassionate, and moral, treating anyone who needs help, regardless of their status, creed, or background. Doc had some wonderful moments during his time, including inventing a specialized leg brace attached to a boot for the character Jewel (Geri Jewell), but that was countered by a bout with tuberculosis and an affinity for grave robbing. Played by Brad Dourif, he balanced deep, raw empathy with a weary, nervous intensity that allowed Doc to feel richly human. Dourif brought a layered performance to the series, finding nuances with each character he shared a scene with. Whether being berated by Swearengen or having a delightfully playful bond with Jewel, Doc's presence was always spelled fun.

To take on Doc in a new iteration, you need an actor with the same decency. He must have instinctual intelligence, yet emotional honesty. A great option would be Ben Whishaw. He has the precise blend of raw vulnerability, sharp intellect, and world-weary cynicism to step into his iconic boots. As the sole medical authority in camp, Doc needs to be dependable, yet never let that go to his head. Whishaw showed that same energy in This Is Going to Hurt. His portrayal of a brilliant, utterly depleted, and stressed doctor managing a broken system perfectly mirrors Doc's overwhelmed, yet deeply dedicated bedside manner. Whishaw has played men with fractured psyches, including The Hollow Crown, which would allow him to tap into the trauma that Doc carries. While Dourif brought a wild-eyed, older eccentricity to the character, Whishaw would have a bit more youthful energy. That said, as Q in the James Bond franchise, his younger brilliance allowed him to go toe-to-toe with tyrants he's not intimidated by.

There was always someone offering kindness in Deadwood, and often, it was Sol Star. Arriving alongside Seth Bullock, he was a natural entrepreneur, and unlike many opportunistic figures, Sol served as a steady, practical, and compassionate moral conscience. Based on the real-life Jewish immigrant, John Hawkes brought a quiet, genuine, gentlemanly quality to the story. He navigated the subtle camp prejudices authentically. He may not have been the biggest personality on the series, but when he was on the screen, he brought brilliant intrigue. Hawkes played the perfect narrative foil throughout the show's run.

While he wasn't a visibly active player later in its run, Sol's grounded nature was crucial when times got tough. For a new iteration, we need an actor with a blend of quiet intensity, period-authentic gravitas, and empathetic nuance. Look no further than Ben Rosenfield. With an extensive pedigree in HBO period drama, his run on Boardwalk Empire as Willie Thompson proves he can deliver stylized, complex dialogue without losing the natural human emotion behind it. Where Bullock can be hot-headed, Sol is calculating and deeply pragmatic. Rosenfied had to navigate a similar dynamic as Willie. He's also shown a tender side while playing John Schlafly in Mrs. America. While excelling at characters who use intellect, restraint, and tactical patience, he would bring the right energy for a man who needs to be smart to survive a lawless land. As an actor from a Jewish family, Rosenfeld may bring a personal connection to Sol's identity and resilience.

What Deadwood did particularly well was never skimping on reality. If that meant violence, they showed it. If it meant showcasing "sinful" lives to survive, it was presented. In Deadwood at the time, women were relegated to some very unfortunate positions to survive, which included sex work. Perhaps the most prominent figure in that world was Trixie. She was a sharp-tongued prostitute at Swearengen's Gem Saloon. As his favorite girl, she shared a complex, volatile, and deeply codependent dynamic with him, marked by both abuse and unspoken affection. Over the course of the series, Trixie evolved, found her independence, and found love with Sol Star. Paula Malcomson made her a standout character with an impeccable performance. She smartly avoided the "hooker with a heart of gold" cliché by making her sharp, intelligent, and fiercely resilient. Her fight for her agency was what made her a profound character, and one to truly champion.

Growing from a traumatized young woman into a fervently protective moral compass gave her one of the most natural arcs of any character. Yet, no matter where she was in life, Trixie never lost her edge. To bring a similar ferocity and the same exact emotional depth, Dominique Fishback would be perfection. Fishback's greatest strength as an actress is mastering vulnerability wrapped in armor. Trixie's defining trait is her extreme defense mechanisms. When she launches into contextless, explosive tirades, it's meant to mask deep trauma. In The Deuce, Fishback played a part that mirrored much of this attitude. As Darlene, a 1970s sex worker, she navigated an incredibly harsh, predatory environment, yet refused to let others crush her. Able to take on darker parts, her Emmy-nominated performance in Swarm highlighted how she can lean into uncomfortable, deeply intense, psychologically rich moments. Fishback is one of the most transformative actresses of the generation. She can play tender, like in Judas and the Black Messiah, and gritty, like in Project Power, but her ability to tackle Milch's text was proven in her one-woman show Subverted. She can capture the rhythmic cadence and weight of the melodic Western text that would make her iteration of Trixie more powerful than ever before.

Few people were as strong an advocate for the women in the camp as Joanie Stubbs. She began her tenure as the savvy, high-level madam of the Bella Union gambling hall and saloon, working alongside and confiding in the manipulative Cy Tolliver (Powers Boothe). She eventually broke away from his control by opening up her own upscale brothel and schoolhouse, Chez Ami. Kindhearted and empathetic, Joanie carries deep emotional trauma from a severely abusive childhood when she was sold into servitude. During her time, she established a tender romantic and emotional bond with Calamity Jane (Robin Weigert). Brought to life by Kim Dickens, she ensured the hardened woman had a soul inside. She mastered the dense text, making it feel colloquial. Dickens' greatest strength was finding subtle humor, giving Joanie a bit of dry wit amidst the weight of the world.

The most important quality anyone playing Joanie needs is the ability to align with her complex, melancholic, and deeply resilient nature. Having quite a resume playing quiet, grounded, emotionally rich women, Kerry Condon would excel in the part. Whether playing the grieving Stacey Ehrmantraut in Better Call Saul or capturing the ticking-clock internal panic of Kay Summersby in Pressure, Condon excels at conveying a character's internal, unspoken pain. Joanie has a duality: an empathetic woman and a business powerhouse. That was on full display as Siobhan Suilleabhain in The Banshees of Inisherin, capturing the sharp-tongued, no-nonsense exhaustion while remaining morally centered. Having extensive experience of performing Shakespeare, she would ease into tackling the Western Shakespeare of Milch's text. Period pieces are also Condon's forte. From Octavia in the lavishly decadent historical drama Rome to the melancholic wilderness drama Train Dreams, Condon's ability to slip into Deadwood's period setting would make her an absolute MVP.

Part of the dynamics that made Deadwood a deliciously delectable story was watching the variety of individuals who called the camp home. The show began with a few new factions of characters arriving, shaking up Deadwood's natural order. One prominent trio was Charlie Utter, Calamity Jane, and the infamous Wild Bill Hickok (Keith Carradine). Honoring the history of the West, the show killed off Wild Bill quite early on, which came as a shock. In turn, it allowed Charlie to grow into a brilliant individual. He is the loyal, longtime friend and traveling companion who tries to manage his heavy drinking and gambling while staying solvent. He is deeply honest, morally uptight, and famously blunt, tough, and fearless in the face of danger. If there are scoundrels in camp, Charlie will sniff them out. Dayton Callie brought an impressive, gritty, world-weary loyalty that grounded the moral core. Callie kept Charlie as the ethical compass. Callie infused the character with a weathered physicality, bringing a slightly hunched posture, observant eyes, and the quiet dignity of a man who has survived hard miles.

When examining what Deadwood might look like should it be cast in 2026, one can't help but ponder who from the original cast could return in new parts. There are a few parts that he could tackle in his later career, but the most interesting would be his take on Charlie Utter. Hawkes now has the perfect blend of grit and gravitas for seasoned character parts. He fits the practical, dirt-under-the-fingernails frontiersman perfectly. Charlie needs to shift into comedic, pathetic, and dangerous aspects without ever losing his humanity, and he has to be capable of being pushed to violence. Hawkes can do that. From menacing, rugged Teardrop in Winter's Bone to the lonely, broken Hank Prior in True Detective: Night Country, Hawkes has range.

At first, it seemed as if Al Swearengen was meant to be the typical villain of the story. But then, when Cy Tolliver arrived, Swearengen became a richly complex protagonist because Tolliver was the real antagonist in Deadwood. Well, before George Hearst said, "Hold my beer." Tolliver is a former riverboat gambler, con man, and pimp who arrives to open the Bella Union, a direct rival of the Gem Saloon. He is a cold-blooded individual with a passionate desire to carve out his own share of the town's lucrative gold-rush wealth and vice trade. Tolliver is a deeply calculating individual whose inner motivations remain famously opaque. Powers Boothe's display of villainy was so menacing due to his imposing physical presence, chilling, cold-blooded intelligence, and trademark full-body sneer. He did lean into that mustache-twirling trope, but that's why he was so incredible when he went toe-to-toe with McShane's Swearengen.

Boothe had a mix of dark humor, calculating ambition, and an underlying fragility driven by obsession and power plays. He was one of the most Shakespearean figures in camp. As fascinating as Olyphant would be in the part, we'll give it to the master of bad guys, Danny Huston. Bitwise Asset Management is seeking to list a spot Zcash exchange-traded fund (ETF) on the Texas Stock Exchange (TXSE), according to a registration statement filed with the US Securities and Exchange Commission.

The S-1 filing names TXSE as the anticipated listing venue for the Bitwise Zcash ETF, which would trade under the ticker ZEC. The fund would hold Zcash directly and seek to reflect the value of its holdings, minus operating expenses and liabilities.

Coinbase Custody would safeguard the fund’s ZEC tokens, while Bitwise Investment Advisers would serve as its manager and sponsor.

The Bitwise fund has not begun trading, and its registration statement has yet to become effective. Its proposed annual sponsor fee also remains unspecified. The public filing also follows an Oct. 16 draft that identified NYSE Arca as the proposed exchange.

The filing comes as asset managers continue to expand their Zcash-linked investment products in the US.

Modern Warfare 4 increased SBMM in DMZ despite multiplayer backlash: Report

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Infinity Ward has reportedly tweaked Modern Warfare 4’s DMZ matchmaking to give player skill more weight when putting lobbies together.

Skill-based matchmaking (SBMM) has been a hot topic in Modern Warfare 4 since the beta. Players pushed back when Infinity Ward brought over Classic matchmaking from Black Ops 7, arguing that putting more emphasis on skill made matches feel too sweaty.

The debate carried on after launch, with multiplayer creative director Joe Cecot saying Infinity Ward considers how matchmaking affects different groups of players, while former Call of Duty producer Mark Rubin argued that public lobbies are more fun with a wider mix of skill levels.

Now, that same debate has reached DMZ, as SBMM has reportedly been cranked up in the mode.

“Infinity Ward recently updated matchmaking in MW4 DMZ to increase consideration of skill factor in match selection, per sources familiar with the change,” CharlieIntel reported on X.

Infinity Ward recently updated matchmaking in MW4 DMZ to increase consideration of skill factor in match selection, per sources familiar with the change pic.twitter.com/YIY7LIFZQT

In practice, that could mean seeing more opponents around your own skill level when you load into a DMZ run. How noticeable the change is is still unclear, as the devs haven’t revealed how much of a factor it was at launch.

That being said, it may have already had an impact on how long it takes to find a match, with streamer JGOD posting that he had to wait over 6 minutes to get into a game.

Never waited this long for a match in DMZ pic.twitter.com/VMV0sbVutH

We know that Call of Duty matchmaking takes many things into consideration when building lobbies, including ping and location, but the skill aspect is always the most divisive.

For less experienced squads, a tighter skill range could mean running into fewer veteran teams, giving them more of a fighting chance. Meanwhile, more experienced players could find themselves up against tougher squads more often, which they argue punishes them for playing well.
