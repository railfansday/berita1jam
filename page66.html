Vanguard S&P 500 ETF vs. Schwab U.S. Dividend ETF: How Much You’d Need to Collect $1,000 a Month From Each.

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

While VOO and SCHD both pay dividends, one produces three times as much income per dollar invested.

The Vanguard S&P 500 ETF (VOO -1.04%) is the world's largest exchange-traded fund (ETF) with nearly $1.1 trillion in assets under management (AUM). While the Schwab U.S. Dividend Equity ETF (SCHD -1.53%) is a fraction of VOO's size, with almost $123 billion in AUM, it's the largest ETF focused on dividend stocks.

Both of these top ETFs pay dividends, making them potential options for investors seeking to generate passive income. Here's a look at how much you'd need to invest in each one to generate around $1,000 of dividend income each month.

The Vanguard S&P 500 ETF tracks the S&P 500 Index, which measures the return of the 500 largest publicly traded U.S. companies. Many of these companies pay dividends, which VOO collects and then distributes to investors each quarter. For example, its top holding is Apple, which currently pays a quarterly dividend of $0.27 per share. However, while many of its holdings pay dividends, the ETF currently has a relatively low yield of around 1%, matching the S&P 500's dividend yield. That's because many of its holdings have lower dividend yields (Apple's is a meager 0.3%).

At the ETF's current yield of around 1%, you'd need to invest almost $1.2 million into VOO to generate $12,000 in dividend income each year, or around $1,000 per month. That's a hefty investment. On a positive note, the dividend income should grow. The S&P 500 has delivered mid-single-digit annualized dividend growth over the past three years.

The Schwab U.S. Dividend Equity ETF focuses specifically on dividend-paying stocks. It tracks an index (Dow Jones U.S. Dividend 100 Index) that screens companies based on several dividend-quality characteristics, including yield, five-year dividend growth rate, and financial strength. As a result, the fund holds 100 high-quality, high-yielding dividend growth stocks.

The ETF currently has a dividend yield of around 3%, based on its payments over the last 12 months. Thanks to its much higher yield, you can generate more income with less capital from SCHD. For example, you'd only need to invest around $400,000 to generate $12,000 of dividend income each year to hit the monthly target of $1,000.

Further, this ETF should deliver faster dividend growth in the future, due to its dual focus on yield and dividend growth. Over the last five years, its average holding has delivered more than 9% annual dividend growth. However, dividend growth has slowed considerably over the past year, with SCHD's payout rising only about 2% over the last 12 months.

VOO and SCHD both provide investors with passive dividend income. However, thanks to its currently higher yield, you'd need to invest one-third as much capital in SCHD to generate the same amount of dividend income. That makes it the better option for investors seeking to maximize their passive income.

Despite leaving Tottenham, Will Lankshear's career path so far bears similarities to the one Harry Kane took.

That is the opinion of former senior Tottenham scout, Bryan King, speaking exclusively to Football Insider.

The 21-year-old joined Middlesbrough from the Lilywhites in the summer transfer window, after a deal worth up to a possible £20million was agreed.

Lankshear has exploded into form since, scoring 15 goals in 16 games across all competitions so far this season and netting seven in just his last five Championship appearances.

The striker missed two games before the international break and Middlesbrough failed to win either, with the hope being that he returns to face Bristol City on Saturday.

Lankshear is already reportedly attracting interest from the Premier League, and could well become an extremely expensive mistake by Tottenham.

Harry Kane believes Will Lankshear is doing "extremely well" at Middlesbrough after calls for the striker to be in the most recent England squad 🏴󠁧󠁢󠁥󠁮󠁧󠁿 pic.twitter.com/uSTauAviEI

Like Kane, Lankshear was initially with Arsenal before eventually making the move to Tottenham in 2022.

He went on Championship loans to West Brom and Oxford United like Kane did to Leyton Orient, Millwall, Norwich City and Leicester City.

Yet when the Ballon d'Or winner was experiencing his first true breakthrough at the age of 21, he was still a Tottenham player.

"He went for an initial £10m, and it goes up to £20m. So he's a Middlesbrough player, Spurs have got no call on him," King told Football Insider.

"His path is clearly similar to Harry Kane's. Out on loan, just like Harry was, young, on the verge of the first team and not really getting into the first team.

"I'd like to see him do well because he impressed me when he came in to the Spurs team. I think it was Ange [Postecoglou] who gave him his chance.

"When you spend that money on front players, it's very difficult for a young player to come up. He was an Arsenal player first. Started at Arsenal. That's why I say it's a very similar path at that age to what Harry Kane did.

"I'm not sure he's going to be a new Harry Kane, but it's funny that he started at Arsenal, then he went to Sheffield United. I think they got him for about £2m."

Tottenham will be grateful to have a sell-on clause and a matching rights agreement as part of Lankshear's move to Middlesbrough, which provides them with some protection.

But if the 21-year-old is the new Kane, the decision to sell him will age poorly regardless.

"It's obviously amazing to be compared to someone like that," Lankshear said at the start of the season when asked about comparisons to England's all-time top scorer.

"But I think it's important also not to put too much pressure on young players and comparing them to other players.

"Obviously I've still got a long, long way before I get to that level. If I keep progressing and keep going the way I am, then who knows?"

CBA’s Comyn says US politics make Australia more attractive for compute

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The Commonwealth Bank chief, whose bank backs Anthropic, said US data centre delays could push AI demand to Australia.

Commonwealth Bank of Australia chief executive Matt Comyn said US political changes have made Australia a more attractive place to host AI computing power, Bloomberg reported. He spoke in Sydney on Wednesday at an AI conference run by the bank.

Democrats’ gains in this month’s US midterms have fuelled talk that data centre building could slow in parts of the US. Voters rejected data centre plans in all but one of 41 ballot measures, Al Jazeera reported.

“Particularly in a state like Texas, there’s a lot of forward compute capacity and so if that gets delayed, Australia is an even more attractive destination,” Comyn told Bloomberg.

Australia may also gain from new US limits on chip access in Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia, Bloomberg reported. The rules aim to stop Chinese firms renting Nvidia chips abroad.

Comyn’s view echoes former Macquarie chief Shemara Wikramanayake, who said earlier this month that Australia could sell computing power to the US, according to Bloomberg.

CBA, Australia’s biggest lender, invested in Anthropic in 2025. Comyn said the stake gives the bank access to Anthropic’s models and tools, and a sense of its product roadmap.

He declined to say what CBA would do with its stake when asked about Anthropic’s upcoming stock market listing.

SAP opens AI to on-prem customers, but cloud commitments could shut smaller firms out

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

User group says access terms favor enterprises that can migrate one ERP system at a time

SAP's commercial terms could put its latest AI tools out of reach for many customers, particularly smaller businesses running a single ERP system, the new chair of its German-speaking user group has warned.

Thomas Henzler, appointed chair of DSAG last month, told The Register that Joule Studio 2.0 – SAP's upgraded platform for developing and deploying AI agents to automate business tasks – is a leap forward.

Yet DSAG, which represents SAP users in Germany, Switzerland, and Austria, remains in talks with the vendor about the commercial conditions attached to access.

In July 2023, CEO Christian Klein told investors that SAP's "newest innovations and capabilities" will only be delivered in the public or private cloud using RISE with SAP, the vendor's lift-shift-and-transform program launched with partners and cloud providers in early 2021. Customers running ERP software on-premises, or hosting it with a hyperscaler outside RISE, will miss out, he said.

However, in May this year, SAP appeared to perform a U-turn. It said on-premises customers could access Joule 2.0 if they commit to a cloud "journey" through RISE with SAP, changing their support and commercial arrangements while migration projects are still underway.

Henzler told The Reg: "There is a gap between the customer's reality and SAP's strategy for the autonomous enterprise. A lot of customers are still planning to migrate to S/4HANA, and now the question is, 'what do we have to do to use SAP AI, and what does it mean regarding the cloud strategy?'

"Now it's a completely new strategy, a new approach, and a new underlying technology. The question is, what does it mean for existing projects, S/4HANA migrations, cloud migrations and so on? Is it necessary to rethink all the projects? Is there a big shift in the technology and architectures?"

The DSAG chair said the biggest challenge in connecting legacy on-prem systems, such as ECC, to the new Joule suite is the commercial model.

"Access requires a substantial RISE commitment and a minimum cloud-to-on-premise maintenance ratio. In practice, this creates a problem for customers with only one ECC system. To meet the cloud threshold, they have to convert their entire on-premises maintenance into a cloud subscription, which defeats the purpose of an on-premises connector. Consequently, the model only works for large enterprises with multiple SAP ERP systems that can, for example, migrate one system after the other to unlock AI for the rest," he said.

"In the end, to get the benefit you have to start a RISE with SAP migration. Therefore, the connector doesn't make sense for the majority of SME customers. From my perspective, this approach works only for large customers with multiple ERP systems, for example, if they take one system, move it to the cloud, and then another one. Then they can use AI, because they reach the needed ratio to use the on-prem connector. That is more or less a commercial approach. We need a solution for all customers, independent of the commercials."

The Register asked SAP to respond to Henzler's concerns. A spokesperson said: "The offering separates a customer's commercial commitment to cloud ERP from the timing of its operational migration. Customers that meet the applicable commercial and technical requirements can use supported AI capabilities with eligible ECC or on-premises SAP S/4HANA systems while modernizing their landscape. Eligibility is not determined by the number of ERP instances and can therefore also extend to single-instance customers."

There are thousands of SAP ERP users worldwide, including some of the world's largest businesses. Despite efforts to get them onto the latest iteration – S/4HANA – and in the cloud, where the vendor promised it could gather a greater "share of wallet" from customers, there has been a dogged resistance to these complex and time-consuming migrations.

Customers still running older ERP systems face pressure to embrace AI-supported automation. Whether SAP's terms help them do that – or simply help move them onto RISE – remains the question. ®

InfoQ Homepage News Vercel Releases UI SDK for Agents to Compose UI from Reusable Behaviors

Nov 18, 2026 2 min read

Vercel has released UI SDK, an open-source TypeScript library that treats keyboard navigation, focus, selection, popups, and drag and drop as reusable behaviors rather than details buried inside finished components. Vercel UI SDK is published on GitHub as vercel-labs/ui and is currently in beta.

The behaviors live in @ui-sdk/core. Vercel says it built more than 50 of them, including createPopup, createSelection, createTypeahead, createRovingFocus, createReorder, createResize, createViewport, createListbox, createChange, createFocusRecovery, and createVirtualizer. A developer, or more often an agent, combines those helpers inside definePrimitive, which declares a component's state, API, and slots once. Framework adapters then render the same primitive through createParts in React, Vue, Svelte, Solid, or as Web Components. The parts are unstyled and expose attributes and CSS variables. The same behaviors also back a starter library of more than 50 unstyled components, from Color Picker, Token Input, and Command to Calendar, Tree, and OTP Input, with the same part names in all five adapters.

UI SDK is meant to be used by agents, not only written by them. The ui-sdk skill, installed with npx skills add vercel-labs/ui --skill ui-sdk, points an agent at the Markdown docs shipped in the installed packages. At runtime, createAgent, imported from the matching framework package such as @ui-sdk/react/agent, exposes snapshot, act, and query so an agent can read component state and call actions through the component API, without screenshots. Apps can approve or refuse those steps, and change callbacks receive a source of "agent" and may cancel a change. registerTools can expose the same methods to a browser's built-in agent through WebMCP, when the browser provides document.modelContext.

Developers already have several headless libraries at this layer, including Radix UI, Base UI, React Aria, Ark UI and its Zag.js state machines, and Headless UI. shadcn/ui sits one level above those primitives as a copy-into-your-repo component kit. UI SDK aims lower, at composable behaviors shared across frameworks, and adds a first-class agent bridge. The changelog thanks the teams behind Radix, Base UI, React Aria, shadcn/ui, Nuxt UI, and Geist. Vercel has not said whether shadcn/ui will be rebuilt on UI SDK.

Rahim, a product engineer at Mux who built Vidstack, wrote after reading the source:

This is so genuinely cool. I love how it's been packaged up. So elegant @shadcn and @shuding! I'll be studying this for a while. 1. createAgent is an "agent bridge" which gives a snapshot, act, and query tool. It allows agents to interact with the actual UI components directly 2. Change callbacks know when the agent made the change (details.source === "agent"), and the app can refuse it with cancel (I love this)

A developer identified as Stefan looked at the repository and asked how the idea compares with existing headless libraries:

I tried looking at the repo a bit but I’m not sure I quite understand the value prop of this versus something like Base UI. Is the thought process that we should be thinking about UIs/components as combinations of behaviours?

UI SDK is in beta as 0.1.0-beta.1, licensed under the Apache License 2.0, and subject to Vercel's public beta terms; the APIs, documentation, and behavior may change before general availability. MEXC confirmed that its Global Card service will be discontinued on November 18, according to the exchange's announcement. The company cites service adjustments by its third-party card issuer. From the deadline, existing cards can no longer be used for transactions, and new card applications are suspended.

Users have two things to take care of before the service ends.

First, the remaining card balance has to be withdrawn: the Withdraw button on the Global Card page sends the funds to the Fiat account.

Second, subscriptions linked to the card should be cancelled or switched to another payment method. Unfortunately for cardholders, recurring payments will most likely fail if they stay on the old card.

If there's still a balance when the service ends, MEXC will credit it to the Spot account, so users will simply receive their funds back. It's important to keep in mind that pending card transactions need time to settle, which is why the balance snapshot comes on November 21, three days after the shutdown. The amount will be credited within three business days of the snapshot. Users do not need to take any action.

Refunds for previous card transactions will be credited to the Fiat account once MEXC receives them. Pending cashback will be credited automatically as scheduled. Elite VVIP users with zero-fee benefits will be refunded too, though the eligible fees return to their Spot accounts only before December 15.

Transaction history, card orders and cashback records stay available on the Global Card page for three months after the service ends, so it's worth saving anything needed before access ends.

Cryptocurrency services delegate certain types of services to third parties all the time, so witnessing a similar situation is more than a possibility here. Realistically, the shutdown is somewhat inconvenient rather than dangerous, as the balances are returned automatically. For users: stay vigilant and try not to put all your eggs in one basket.
