Man Utd & Palace shocking in 2026-27 Premier League wage bill vs. position comparison

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Chelsea, Tottenham Hotspur, Manchester United, Liverpool, Manchester City and Arsenal are all sitting in vastly contrasting places when you compare their wage bill rankings to their league positions after the first four games of the 2026-27 Premier League season.

Of course, it’s still early in the season. Ten matches can tell us only so much about how the season will eventually play out, and the table is still taking shape.

Still, there are already some big, eye-catching differences between wage outlay and performance.

Brighton lead the way. They are second in the Premier League despite having the 15th-highest wage bill, which stands at just £51million. That is a remarkable +13 difference between their league position and wage ranking.

Brentford are second in that regard. They are eighth while ranking 17th for wages, with a £49million annual bill, giving them a +9 difference. Hull City are fourth, sitting 13th despite having the 19th-highest wage bill at just £16million. Their +6 difference is another remarkable return for a newly-promoted side.

Leeds United have also made an impressive start. They occupy 9th place while sitting 14th in the wage rankings. Their annual wage bill is only £53million, giving them a +5 difference.

Ipswich are another side punching well above their wage bill. They sit 14th while ranking 18th for wages, with an annual wage bill of just £20million. That’s a +4 difference.

The traditional powerhouses have produced a mixed bag. Arsenal are top while having the third-highest wage bill in the division, at £154million. That puts them two places ahead of their wage position, while Manchester City sit third in the league and second in the wage rankings.

Chelsea are fifth with the seventh-highest wage bill, £97million, while top spenders Liverpool are fourth. The Reds have the highest wage bill in the Premier League at £177million but sit three places below that ranking.

The other ‘Big Six’ underachievers are not hard to spot. Manchester United are 18th with the fourth-highest wage bill of £152million, while Tottenham are 11th despite having the fifth-highest at £138million. Oof.

Villa are also slightly off the pace. They are 10th despite having the sixth-highest wage bill, with £98million going on salaries. That puts them four places below their wage ranking.

Gianluigi Donnarumma has set 'alarm bells' ringing at Man City - ex-coach

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Gianluigi Donnarumma has set alarm bells ringing with his recent run of disappointing performances for Manchester City.

The 27-year-old has long been viewed as one of the best goalkeepers in the world and is seen as a crucial part of Enzo Maresca's ambitious plans both immediately and for the future.

Maresca recently confirmed the Italy international was the only guarunteed starter on his teamsheet, but that decision has now sparked questions about his displays.

Former Man United chief scout and assistant manager Mick Brown warned Football Insider that Man City may no longer be able to rely on Donnarumma to get them out of trouble at the back.

This comes off the back of three straight defeats in the Premier League against Aston Villa, Brighton and Nottingham Forest, where the goalkeeper has struggled to impress.

Despite their winning start to the season, Donnarumma has kept just four clean sheets in 15 appearances, with 23 goals conceded, raising questions about his form.

Former Man City financial adviser Stefan Borson told Football Insider on 6 November that Donnarumma is becoming a "very expensive problem" at the Etihad Stadium.

His latest mistake came in Man City's five-goal thriller against RB Leipzig last week, while he did little to prevent goal-shy Nottingham Forest from scoring three.

That has now been flagged as a concern for Maresca, who has been keen to publicly back his goalkeeper, but could see that decision backfire if his form does not improve.

"Donnarumma's form is going to be a serious problem for Man City if it does not improve," Brown told Football Insider.

"Losing three Premier League games in a row is well below expectations for them, so they have to be looking at why things have gone wrong and where they can improve.

"The goalkeeper has made a number of mistakes this season, and that has set alarm bells ringing, he's gone from one of the best in the world to somebody they cannot rely on.

"Obviously they'll be hoping it's just a dip in form, but what if it's not? He keeps making these mistakes and I'm sure the manager will be having doubts.

"So it could be a big problem for Man City if he's not performing, but he's not the only one, they have to look at a few players in that squad who are not stepping up."

Given Donnarumma's struggled in recent weeks, there have been calls for backup goalkeeper Geronimo Rulli to come into the side, after his impressive Carabao Cup displays.

However, Man City have also been criticised for their decision to let James Trafford leave in the summer, when he joined Leeds United for a fee of around £40million.

Football Insider reported back in September that Trafford has quickly become one of the best goalkeepers in the Premier League and has shown his worth in this Leeds side.

The England international has gone from strength to strength at Elland Road, having pushed for a move away from Man City in search of regular first-team football.

As a result, Man City may be regretting having let him go with their own goalkeeper problems becoming clear amid growing doubts over Donnarumma's role.

gta vi takes over miami with six real-life cars built by west coast customs

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

from a pink-and-purple sports coupe to a police-liveried supercar, the fully drivable lineup turns fictional automotive excess into something with actual horsepower.

Rockstar Games has brought six vehicles from Grand Theft Auto VI out of the screen and onto the streets of Miami, enlisting Los Angeles-based West Coast Customs to transform fictional designs into fully drivable machines. The project arrives ahead of the game’s November 19 release, translating some of its most recognizable automotive creations into physical vehicles. From classic American lowriders to contemporary supercars, the collection recreates the exaggerated personalities that have long defined the franchise’s approach to car culture.

The six vehicles retain their fictional brand names, distinctive silhouettes and elaborate finishes, with modifications extending well beyond their exterior appearance. Each has been presented alongside its digital counterpart, revealing how closely the real builds follow their virtual references. Photographs also place the collection at recognizable Miami landmarks, including Herzog & de Meuron’s 1111 Lincoln Road parking structure and the Miami Marine Stadium. The locations connect the actual city with Vice City, Rockstar’s fictional interpretation of Florida’s coastal metropolis.

Among the six creations developed by Rockstar Games in collaboration with West Coast Customs, the Grotti Cheetah draws on the proportions of classic Italian supercars, with a low-slung body, sculpted side intakes and a naturally aspirated 4.9-liter V8 engine. The customization workshop finishes the vehicle with a gradient paint treatment, lowered suspension and custom leather upholstery. The Invetero Coquette takes a more contemporary direction, pairing a 6.2-liter V8 with a striking pink-and-purple camouflage exterior. Meanwhile, the Progen Emerus embraces track-inspired engineering, combining a twin-turbocharged 4.0-liter V8 with aerodynamic bodywork, an oversized rear wing and a bright orange interior.

American automotive culture provides the references for the Albany Buccaneer and Albany Manana. The former reinterprets a 1960s lowrider with candy-green metallic paint, gold wheels, air suspension and zebra-patterned interior details. The Manana follows a different tradition, adopting the dramatically elevated stance of a donk-style custom car, complete with oversized wheels, blue bodywork and gold accents. Completing the lineup is the Pegassi Tempesta, a V10-powered supercar dressed in the fictional Vice Beach Police Department’s black-and-white livery. Apparently, even law enforcement gets a supercar in Leonida.

The project reflects the close relationship between Grand Theft Auto’s visual identity and real-world automotive customization. Throughout the series, Rockstar Games has developed fictional manufacturers and vehicles that borrow recognizable proportions and styling cues from established brands while exaggerating their defining characteristics. Translating these designs into functional machines requires more than reproducing their shapes. Paint finishes, wheel proportions, suspension setups and interior materials become essential to preserving the identity of each vehicle beyond the screen.

The resulting collection also highlights the influence of Miami’s automotive culture on the fictional world of Vice City, from extravagant lowriders to brightly colored performance cars. By placing the vehicles in the same urban environment that inspires the game, Rockstar Games brings its digital references back to their physical origins. With Grand Theft Auto VI scheduled to launch on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S on November 19, the six builds offer an unusually tangible preview of its universe, where even the most improbable designs appear perfectly at home on the road.

Tribeca Prizewinner Darko Lungulov Wraps Production on Serbian Coming-of-Age Drama ‘1970’: ‘It Is a Story About Today’ (EXCLUSIVE)

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Tribeca prizewinner Darko Lungulov (“Here and There”) has wrapped production on “1970,” a coming-of-age drama set against the backdrop of President Nixon’s visit to the former Yugoslavia during the Vietnam War. The film will take part in the Agora Works in Progress strand at the Thessaloniki Film Festival before heading to Tallinn’s Baltic Event industry showcase later this month.

“1970” follows 13-year-old Draško, who — after being abandoned by his parents — runs away with the rebellious 16-year-old Eržika. Drifting from place to place, swept up amid a roiling climate of anti-war protests, rebellion, free love, wild rock concerts, drugs and decadent parties, his desperate journey home becomes an awakening of heartbreak, loss and freedom. Related Stories ‘Star Trek’ 60th Anniversary Celebration Hits Rome MIA Market With Iconic Franchise at 'Crossroads’ After Paramount-Warner Bros. Merger

The film is written and directed by Lungulov, who shares a producing credit with Snezana van Houwelingen. It’s produced by This and That Productions (Serbia), Papa Films (Serbia), Mirage Film (Hungary), Samsa Film (Luxembourg) and PomPom Film (Croatia), with financing and support from Film Center Serbia, the Hungarian National Film Institute, Film Fund Luxembourg, the Croatian Audiovisual Center, Film Fund Vojvodina and Creative Europe MEDIA.

Speaking to Variety in Thessaloniki, Lungulov explains that while his period drama is set more than 50 years in the past, “it is a story about today.”

“The film takes us to a time when young people believed they could change the world — something we seem to have forgotten,” the director says. Pointing to recent events in his native Serbia, where after two years of struggle, a student protest movement ousted longtime strongman Aleksandar Vučić last month, Lungulov says that “that possibility came incredibly to life again.”

The Belgrade-born director, who studied filmmaking in New York, is best known for “Here and There,” which won the Best NY Narrative Award at Tribeca in 2009 and was Serbia’s submission for the foreign-language Oscar that year. The film, which contrasted the adventures of an enterprising young Serbian in New York with the experiences of an aging New Yorker in Belgrade, was praised by Variety’s Ronnie Scheib as a “laid-back character study [that] accrues small epiphanies with patient care, its mordant Eastern European humor tempered by a gentler sense of postwar absurdity.” His 2014 follow-up, the tragicomedy “Monument to Michael Jackson,” premiered at Karlovy Vary.

Lungulov’s last film was the 2023 documentary “A Fleeting Dream,” which followed the rock band Yu Grupa — a long-running musical act that, coincidentally, was formed in 1970. His latest will also incorporate documentary elements, with “1970” utilizing a mix of “real and fake home movies, newly shot 16mm and digital footage,” he says. “It is a film based on my own memories, and I wanted to create the hero’s inner world, his memories and the atmosphere of 1970 through the film’s internal language.”

Mirroring the political climate of that time, with raucous anti-Vietnam War protests greeting Nixon’s visit to Yugoslvaia, Lungulov notes that his two young leads — Andrija Femić, who plays Draško, and Lilla Gregus, who plays Eržika — were both “very active in the student movement” that brought down Vučić. At the time of filming, Gregus was a 22-year-old student at the Academy of Arts in Novi Sad, the Serbian town whose railway station disaster in 2024 jumpstarted the protest movement.

On one occasion, Lungulov himself joined the young actor and her fellow students at a demonstration after a rehearsal for “1970.” It’s that echo of the past in the present day, he says, that makes his film so timely.

“I feel this story is about today — young people searching for their voice in a hopeless world and refusing to be broken by bullying authorities, which feels like our last hope in today’s scary times,” he says. “And the Serbian students brought that hope back to us in Serbia, and perhaps to the world.”

The Thessaloniki Film Festival runs Nov. 5 – 15.

House Republicans to elect a new leader as they look to rebuild from midterm defeat

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Rep. Jim Jordan, R-Ohio, left, and Rep. Steve Scalise, R-La., confer during a news conference at the Capitol on June 8, 2022. The two GOP leaders are competing to replace Mike Johnson as the top House Republican. J. Scott Applewhite/AP hide caption

Just one week after losing the majority in the midterm elections, House Republicans will meet behind closed doors on Tuesday to pick a minority leader for the next Congress.

The choice to succeed Mike Johnson, R-La., as the top House Republican will be between current House Majority Leader Steve Scalise, R-La., and Ohio Republican Jim Jordan, a co-founder of the ultra-conservative House Freedom Caucus.

The election — which will be conducted by secret ballot — represents a high-stakes decision for the party that will help determine which way it goes as it looks to regroup from a staggering defeat that saw Democrats flip more than 40 GOP seats en route to winning back the majority.

"I think what develops here will become a larger, external conversation about the direction of the Republican Party," said former House Speaker Pro Tempore Patrick McHenry, who held the gavel in an acting role during the tumultuous GOP speakership fight in 2023.

Republicans say they are ready to turn the page on their losses and start rebuilding to regain voters' trust. But first, they will have to decide between two candidates who would bring very different styles of leadership.

In Scalise, 61, House Republicans would be choosing a form of continuity — the No. 2 House Republican since 2023, and someone who has served in broader GOP leadership roles for more than 10 years. He's seen as more moderate than Jordan, and in a letter to colleagues announcing his candidacy, the close Trump ally said his approach to the job would be "conservative and consensus-driven."

"My number one goal as Republican Leader will be to win back the majority and I expect you to hold me accountable to that mission," Scalise wrote. "To succeed, we will need to be effective in both the oversight and legislative fights ahead, but we will also need to lay out a clear and optimistic vision for America."

"We have big tasks ahead, and tested leadership is needed now more than ever," he added.

In Jordan, 62, Republicans would be choosing a veteran of both the House Oversight and House Judiciary committees — two panels poised to play a central role in the new Congress as Democrats plan for a long list of investigations into President Trump and his administration.

A former college wrestling coach known for his pugilistic style, Jordan has pitched an aggressive posture to his colleagues, writing that "offense, not defense, wins elections."

"For too long, we have operated without a clear plan to survive the hour or the day in front of us. That will not cut it against an emboldened and energetic Democratic party," Jordan said in his letter to colleagues, taking a swipe at the House leadership team led by Johnson and Scalise.

"I am battle-tested on the oversight front and I know the Democrats' playbook," he added.

The contest is a rematch from 2023. Back then, Jordan and Scalise were vying to succeed Kevin McCarthy. After multiple candidates failed to secure enough votes, Republicans ultimately voted for Johnson.

Leslie Shedd, a Republican strategist, said the candidates will need to break through a process that can be like "family-on-family violence."

"It's really important … to lay out a very clear, substantive vision of how they plan to lead this conference out of what is now a desert," she said.

John Leganski, who was an aide to McCarthy, the former House Speaker, estimates more than 70% of the incoming House GOP conference was elected after Trump won his first term. As a result, they were largely made in his image or won because of it.

That means, Trump could wield a major influence over the direction of the House Republican conference as a result. So far, he's staying out of the race, though in 2023 he did endorse Jordan.

Leganski said that the size of Democrats' victory last week will limit leverage for Republicans under the eventual House Minority Leader when the new Congress is seated in January.

"I think whoever wins your job is to … number one, try to win back to the House," he said. "And number two, make life as difficult for the Democrats as possible, largely by voting no."
