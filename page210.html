A Fresh Look at Exes Will Smith and Sheree Zampino's Co-Parenting Bond

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

While Will Smith and Sheree Zampino got hitched and then divorced in the 90s, they've remained on good terms. See how they've continued their pursuit of happiness as co-parents.

Now this is a story all about how Will Smith and Sheree Zampino’s life got flipped, turned upside down.

And we'd like to take a minute, just sit right there, to tell you how The Fresh Prince of Bel-Air alum and the Hollywood Exes star went from married, to divorced, to co-parents who’ve remained on good terms. So good, in fact, that Smith and Zampino were recently spotted sharing a few laughs at the airport in Sydney, Australia, where he’d been filming his new movie Supermax.

Not that this is super shocking. After all, Smith’s wife Jada Pinkett Smith has noted that he and Zampino still go on trips together—not in a romantic way but in the sense that they’re still family as parents to 33-year-old son Trey.

"She and Will have their own relationship," the Girls Trip actress said on Sway’s Universe in 2018. "She’s gone on vacations with Will without me with Trey. Because I feel as though Trey needs to feel that dynamic between his two parents. That’s very important that he can feel that connection and where they do meet."

As for how they first met, it was back in the early 90s when Smith attended a taping of Different World. While the Men in Black star’s mission had originally been to impress Pinkett Smith, he ended up meeting Zampino.

"I didn’t meet Jada that night," he recalled in his 2021 memoir Will. "Sheree and I left together before the show was even over. We laughed all the way through dinner, all the way through that fall, and three months later, we were married."

Getting hitched in 1992, Smith and Zampino welcomed Trey later that year. And as their family grew, so did the actor’s career—with him landing the role in 1993’s Six Degrees of Separation shortly after they wed.

But ultimately, Smith and Zampino realized their pursuit of happiness wasn’t with each other. And on Valentine’s Day 1995, she filed for divorce.

Noting they tied the knot in their early 20s, "Marriage is not easy, especially at that age," Zampino said in a 2016 social media video. “Now, we’re trying to be parents, navigate through our marriage, I couldn’t reconcile all of that. Will was becoming bigger and bigger with his career. I’m becoming smaller and smaller and smaller. I didn’t have the tools. We didn’t have the tools to make it work. I left because I was unhappy. It’s really that simple.”

And Smith has been honest about the challenges he faced following their divorce.

"With Sheree and with Trey, that was a really difficult time," the Oscar winner noted on Pinkett Smith's Red Table Talk in 2020. "Divorce was the worst thing in my adult life. Divorce was the ultimate failure for me. I’ve been hurt a lot in my adult life, but I don’t think anything touches the failure of getting divorced from my 2-year-old son’s mother."

Smith went on to marry Pinkett Smith in 1997. They welcomed son Jaden the next year followed by daughter Willow in 2000. And along with Zampino and Trey, they learned how to become a blended family.

"We really had to figure it out along the way," Pinkett Smith shared on Red Table Talk in 2018. "For me, it really was just about maturity. Just not understanding the marital dynamic like, 'OK, well, divorce papers are sent, and people are over it, then this is done.' Guess what? It aint never done."

Because as she put it, "This woman is a part of this family. It’s not only taking on Trey, but Sheree was coming along too. It’s part of the package."

"There were times where we have bumped heads and it hasn't been pretty," Zampino admitted on Behind the Velvet Rope in 2022. “But listen, we get back on that horse and we press on. We do what we have to do. Because again, at the end of the day, it's about the kids. We have a responsibility to them."

And they have a desire to support each other too. Like how Zampino was the first guest on Pinkett Smith’s Red Table Talk and stood in Smith’s corner after he slapped Chris Rock at the 2020 Oscars.

"I hope people allow him to be human," she told DailyMail.com in 2022. "I really hope for that because I stand in support of him, we are on good terms."

Over the years, their family has gone through changes, including Zampino’s marriage and divorce from Terrell Fletcher as well as Smith and Pinkett’s separation.

"We are together," Pinkett Smith clarified on It’s Been a Minute in 2024, "but we are together in the way that works for us."

And it seems that Zampino and Smith will always be tied together too.  "I think that in terms of co-parenting, we’re more successful, a lot more successful actually, than in a marriage dynamic," she told Fox News in 2020. "We were put on this Earth to be co-parents. That’s what we did and that’s what we’re doing. We love each other and it’s been amazing. It’s been a blessing."

So no, Zampino didn’t agree with Smith calling their divorce a failure.

"I had an issue with that because I didn’t understand how we could do what we do as a blended family and do it as well as we do and as loving as we do and have an amazing son, and it’s looked at as a failure," she said on Red Table Talkin 2022. I reviewed the new iPad mini: It’s the best Apple tablet, with a price to match

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Key specs: 8.5-inch OLED display, A20 Pro chip, and just 5.4mm thin

What we loved: Incredible, lightweight design, strong performance, and a beautiful display

What we didn't love: The $699 starting price

The new iPad Mini is here — I'm looking right at its super-bright OLED display as I type this review — and it's beautiful. The lightest Apple tablet ever made, the iPad Mini has the same powerhouse A20 Pro chip as the iPhone 18 Pro Max, and even though it may not be the ideal device for intensive work, it can still support local AI workloads, not to mention the new Siri AI.

But why oh why does everything have to be so damn expensive in 2026? That's not a serious question — we know exactly who's hogging all the RAM, and putting Apple under pressure from memory makers. But for folks like me who have to count every dollar, the starting $699 price does hurt.

So, as much as I loved using the new iPad Mini (it really is a lot of fun), I'm not quite sure who to recommend this device to, unless you're in the top income tax bracket.

Let's cut to the chase: The iPad Mini is now my favorite tablet in the Apple lineup. It has an incredible display, excellent design, and strong performance. However, I'm not quite sure who should buy it.

I'm not the only reviewer who's a little confused by Apple's product lineup in 2026. At first, I thought the iPad Mini was the MacBook Neo of the iPad family. It's the smaller, less powerful model for everyday use. That makes intuitive sense, but it's also wrong.

The iPad Mini offers better performance in many respects than the iPad Air and a far superior display. And while the MacBook Neo is a budget laptop, the iPad Mini is definitely not a budget device. Its starting price is only $50 less than the iPad Air.

A slightly cheaper iPad Mini priced around $499 or $599 would make a lot more sense to me, and be much easier to recommend.

After testing the device, I can heartily recommend the iPad Mini to one-percenters looking for the best iPad for everyday use. But you can also get Amazon's new Alexa Pro tablets, and plenty of other good tablets, for well under $500, and most people will be perfectly fine without an OLED display or a chip that supports local AI models.

Given all that, I'm just not sure I'd pay $699 for the iPad Mini. Plus, the model I tested, which came with 512GB, the Nano-texture display, and the C2 cellular chip, would have set me back $1,249, almost twice as much — and that's definitely not in my budget.

Now, most people can skip the cellular and stick with WiFi only. The Nano-texture finish is also non-essential. And if you rely on iCloud, you can make do with 128GB of storage, bringing the price back down to $699. But that's still a big ask.

I praised the M4 iPad Air for its excellent design, but the new iPad Mini is somehow even thinner. I also found its more compact size to be pretty much perfect for streaming, browsing, gaming, and scrolling. It fits just right in your hands.

Apple made some other design changes as well. The new iPad Mini no longer has speaker holes at the bottom of the device, which will inevitably get filled up with gunk and lint. Instead, sound emanates from the device as part of a new Acoustic Display technology. And because the device is fully enclosed, it achieves IP67 water and dust resistance for extra durability. I haven't dunked my iPad Mini in the bathtub (yet), but it should withstand brief immersion in water.

I'm used to the larger iPad Air, and I instantly preferred the smaller, lighter design of the iPad Mini. I can see why the device has such devoted fans in the Apple community.

One design note: Apple moved the microphone and 12MP Center Stage camera to the side bezel of the device. Because I'm not used to it, I kept thinking it was a bit of schmutz on the screen and kept trying to wipe it off with my thumb. I got used to it, and so will you.

My only complaint about the design? It has Touch ID instead of Face ID.

Apple upgraded the iPad Mini with an OLED display, and it's fantastic. The clarity is so precise, and the brightness, color clarity, and deep blacks are instantly noticeable, especially when watching HDR content (the iPad Mini has a peak brightness of 1,200 nits with HDR).

I used the iPad Mini side by side with the iPad Air while watching my go-to monitor-testing video, and the difference was clear. Now, I have to call out that the iPad Mini display doesn't rise to the level of the iPad Pro.

The tandem OLED display on the latest M5 iPad Pro remains unrivaled in the tablet world. It's so good that the iPad Pro can act as a reference monitor for film editors, which is not something you can say about the Mini. The iPad Mini also lacks the ProMotion tech that gives the Pro its high, smooth refresh rate.

But these are minor quibbles that almost no one outside the tech or video-editing world really needs to care about.

To recap: The iPad Mini's OLED display absolutely rules.

I wouldn't call the Nano-texture finish (which adds $200 to the price) a must-have, but it is pretty phenomenal. It's also available on the iPad Pro and the new iPhone Duo, and it virtually eliminates reflections and glare thanks to microscopic glass-etching technology that's hard to wrap your head around.

Even when I shone a bright flashlight directly at the display, you could barely see it. And with the OLED and nano-texture combined, you can watch content even in direct sunlight. It's like a magic trick.

Instead of using a past-gen chip, the new iPad Mini gets the same super-powered chip as the new iPhone 18 Pro Max and iPhone Duo. And it's a damn fine chip. I put the iPad Mini through the Geekbench 7 benchmarking test, and it compares favorably to the iPad Air and iPad Pro, coming somewhere between the M4 chip in the latest iPad Air and the M5 chip in the iPad Pro.

Here are the single- and multi-core Geekbench 7 scores for each of Apple's tablets. (Note: I ran Geekbench 7 on the Mini and Air myself and used Geekbench's average for the 11-inch M5 iPad Pro.)

What does this mean for the average user? Single-core scores are a good measure of overall "zippiness" for everyday use, according to Mashable laptop reviewer Haley Henschel, while the multi-core score is a better measure of overall performance for complex, longer-running tasks.

The scores indicate that the iPad Mini can support multitasking as well as a laptop, which matches my experience. I made sure not to close any apps while testing the Mini, and it never slowed down. The A20 Pro chip can also support Apple Intelligence features such as visual intelligence and photo editing, as well as run powerful local models.

That being said, I'm not sure many people will be working much on the iPad Mini. If you're doing complex video editing, engineering, or photo retouching, the larger displays on the iPad Air and Pro are better suited to these tasks. The iPad Mini doesn't even support Magic Keyboard.

The new Acoustic Display technology on the iPad Mini works really well, which isn't a surprise. Apple doesn't really release anything half-baked. The speakers were surprisingly good, and the new Mini has new tweeters and woofers for music and streaming. The audio does end up sounding a little tinny, but that's to be expected.

Taking pictures with an iPad is very boomer-coded, and the 12MP Center Stage cameras are better suited to quick selfies and video calls than to true photography. And for FaceTime purposes, the cameras get the job done.

Only you can decide if the $699 iPad Mini fits into your budget. Apple products are sometimes luxury items and status symbols, and that's becoming even more true in the age of RAMageddon and higher prices.

If price were no object, the iPad Mini would be an easy recommendation. Setting price aside for a minute, it puts competitors to shame and offers better design and display than the iPad Air.

Michael Carrick is clinging onto his job by a thread as he takes charge of Manchester United’s meeting with Roma in the Champions League, desperate for a change of fortunes to save his skin.

United lost again on the weekend as they slumped to a 2-0 defeat at Chelsea, leaving them 17th in the Premier League, hovering above the relegation zone on goal difference.

After surviving initial club crisis talks at Carrington last week, every game now feels like it could be Carrick’s last unless he can manage to turn the tide, with United winless in nine games for the first time in 40 years.

United have just two more matches before the international break - with the visit of Aston Villa following on Sunday – which could bring about judgement day for Carrick.

Miguel Delaney was at Stamford Bridge as Michael Carrick slumped to yet another defeat - leaving Manchester United teetering above the relegation zone. Here’s his report:

As Michael Carrick walked into the press room at Stamford Bridge, with many waiting to hear whether he might actually muster some fire, there was also an eye on results elsewhere: a few permutations could have meant England’s most successful club dropped into the relegation zone at this stage of the season – now a quarter in – for the first time since 1986.

That alone is a stark reality, which would be a line in the sand in any other setting.

It barely needs to be said that moment in 1986 was of course when United appointed Sir Alex Ferguson. The rest is history, but this is the present and there is no sign of any salvation – from anywhere.

Devis Vasquez starts in between the sticks for Roma tonight. He is Roma’s third-choice keeper.

Roma have endured bad luck with goalkeepers this season, with number one Mile Svilar currently ruled out with a broken finger.

Pierluigi Gollini had so far deputised in the Belgian’s place, but he failed a late fitness test following a back injury ahead of tonight’s match, meaning Vasquez will play at Old Trafford.

We’re a few minutes away from line-ups being confirmed ahead of Manchester United’s Champions League clash with Roma tonight.

Gian Piero Gasperini is not getting “hung up” on Manchester United’s miserable form as the Roma boss attempts to mastermind a famous Old Trafford triumph.

Michael Carrick’s Red Devils limp into Tuesday’s Champions League encounter on the back of a nine-match winless streak that has included defeats away to Atletico Madrid and Como.

The 4-1 defeat at Cesc Fabregas’ ever-improving side a fortnight ago sent United into a tailspin that they are desperate to end against a Roma side buoyed by their unbeaten start to the Serie A.

Head coach Gasperini, who only has 17 outfield players to select from in England, said: “Obviously Manchester United is struggling somewhat in terms of results in the Premier League at the moment.

“But we can’t get distracted and too hung up on that situation because we know that Manchester United is a strong team, we know that they have top players in their squad.

“We know what the atmosphere is going to be like here as well.

“They make things very difficult for the opposition here at Old Trafford, so we’ll be playing tomorrow’s game as if we’re playing against Manchester United at the top of its form.”

Kobbie Mainoo called on Manchester United’s players to fight for under-fire manager Michael Carrick, the fans and themselves against Roma.

The Red Devils’ nine-match run without a victory in all competitions is their worst winless streak since 1988 and Saturday’s 2-0 loss at Chelsea means they began November above the drop zone on goal difference alone.

Carrick transformed results, mood and performances following his January appointment, with homegrown talent Mainoo’s fortunes changing starkly having been marginalised by predecessor Ruben Amorim.

“I’m very grateful to the boss for putting the trust in me as soon as he came in, and putting that belief in me,” Mainoo said.

“Giving me all the advice that he has, knowing he’s a player who’s been at this club and knows the history of this club and knows what it takes to win, I take those words to heart and I try and apply them every day in in training and in games.

“I feel like our way to repay that is to also be behind the manager on the pitch and off it, and fight for us and fight for us as a team.

“In these tough times you can’t expect the fans 100,000 per cent of the time to be happy and cheering when we’re not putting out results. We owe it to them to put the results out there for them to cheer again.”
