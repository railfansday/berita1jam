'Marshals' Brings One of Taylor Sheridan's Core Characters Back to the 'Yellowstone' Spin-Off

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Editor's Note: The following contains spoilers for Marshals Season 2, Episode 5.

As a Yellowstone spin-off, it never exactly comes as a surprise whenever Marshals nods to the events of the original Taylor Sheridan-made series. After all, Kayce Dutton (Luke Grimes) has a long Montana history that goes beyond his time with federal law enforcement. But sometimes, the CBS procedural goes a step further by using Kayce's past as a springboard for a larger idea. It may not always be in the form of an overarching plot, but in the case of this week's "Secret Weapon," he name-dropped a character who we thought the show forgot about after the first few episodes of last season: Wes Bentley's Jamie Dutton.

When trying to pull information about a bomb's location out of a runaway, Will McCoy (Keean Johnson, who previously appeared on Joe Pickett), Kayce compares him to his brother Jamie, noting that both were considered the "black sheep" of the family. In fact, Kayce takes the comparison a step further by adding that his brother was tormented by their older sister, Beth (Kelly Reilly), in a similar vein to how Will's older brother does him. Of course, all this is to figure out where Will's kin took serial bomber Rudy Carptener's (Braxton Hale) explosive device, which Derrek McCoy planned to use to take down the bankers who "stole" their family land. What’s especially interesting about Kayce's callback to his brother is that he uses Jamie's tragedy to encourage Will to do the right thing, deceptively noting that his own brother went and made his own life.

The show that claimed the most of your answers is the world you were built for. If two tied, both are shown — you're complicated enough to straddle two Sheridan universes.

You are a Dutton — or you might as well be. You understand that some things are worth protecting at any cost, and that the modern world's indifference to history, to land, to legacy, is not something you're willing to accept quietly. You lead from the front, you carry your family's weight without complaint, and when someone threatens what's yours, you don't escalate — you finish it. You're not cruel. But you are absolute. In Yellowstone's world, that combination of ferocity and loyalty doesn't make you a villain. It makes you the only thing standing between everything that matters and everyone who wants to take it.

You thrive in the chaos of high-stakes negotiation, where the money is enormous, the margins are thin, and the wrong word in the wrong room can cost everyone everything. You're a fixer — the person called when a situation is already on fire and needs someone with the nerve to walk into it. West Texas oil country rewards exactly what you are: sharp, adaptable, unsentimental, and absolutely clear-eyed about what people want and what they'll do to get it. You're not naive enough to think this world is fair. You're smart enough to be the one deciding who it's fair to.

You are a Dwight Manfredi — someone who has served their time, paid their dues, and arrived somewhere unexpected with nothing but their reputation and their wits. You adapt without losing yourself. You build loyalty through respect rather than fear, though you're not above reminding people that the two aren't mutually exclusive. Tulsa King is for people who are still standing when everyone assumed they'd be finished — who find, in an unfamiliar place, that they're more capable than the world gave them credit for. You don't need a throne. You build one, wherever you happen to land.

You carry the weight of a system that is broken by design, and you do it anyway — because someone has to, and because you're the only one positioned to do it without the whole thing collapsing. Mike McLusky's world is for people who are comfortable operating where there are no good options, only less catastrophic ones. You speak every language: law enforcement, criminal, political, human. That fluency makes you invaluable and it makes you a target. You've made your peace with both. Mayor of Kingstown belongs to people who understand that keeping the peace is not the same as being at peace — and who do the job regardless.

The whole thing was a clever but odd instance where Jamie, notoriously one of the worst on Yellowstone, actually did some good after death. Kayce seems to lament the fact that his brother died apart from the family, which is a new development, since we never got to see him grieve Jamie like he has others. Marshals killed off Monica (Kelsey Asbille) just before the events of the series, with Kayce's first season arc predominantly about dealing with her death. Likewise, the fifth and final season of Yellowstone spent time with Kayce (and Monica) as he grieved both his father, John Dutton (Kevin Costner), and his unborn son. But just as was the case with the premature demise of Kayce and Jamie's other brother, Lee (Dave Annable), all those years ago, Jamie's death has largely been swept under the rug by post-Yellowstone installments like Marshals and Dutton Ranch — except for one notable scene in the latter. While Kayce seems to know what happened to Jamie (as we can tell by the way he turns the tables on the young McCoy runaway later on), Marshals doesn’t press his feelings on the matter. Instead, he just buries it and presses forward.

Considering that Kayce now works in Montana-based law enforcement, and Harry Gifford (Brett Cullen) has previously expressed a professional (if not also personal) interest in the death of not just John Dutton, but also the disappearance of the state's attorney general, it's a bit odd that Marshals hasn't leaned more into Jamie's death. It seems like this would be a great way to have Kayce struggle to balance the sins of his family's past with the new path he's forging in the present, and it feels like too big a loose thread for someone in law enforcement not to be unraveling. Kayce and Jamie didn't end things between them on the best of terms, but throughout Yellowstone, it was always clear that Kayce cared for his older adopted brother. One has to wonder how Kayce really feels about Beth's homicide.

Between the death of the state's governor and the absence of its A.G., Montana — as explored in the Yellowstone Universe — has had a pretty tough break. It also didn't help that the government contracts with operators like Market Equities, which Jamie put together, fell through when Kayce and Thomas Rainwater (Gil Birmingham) struck a deal to preserve the Dutton land in Paradise Valley through the Broken Rock Reservation. With all of this in mind, Marshals could do something interesting here by leaning into what Jamie's disappearance (and John's death) has really meant for the state, and perhaps even pursue avenues of discovering what happened to him. Perhaps this ought to be the plot of a Dutton Ranch crossover! We can only hope...

Delta Air Lines Launches 4 New Nonstop Airbus A350 Routes: See Full List Now

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Delta Air Lines is preparing to expand its Airbus A350-900 network with four new route deployments between December 2026 and May 2027. The additions include new nonstop services to Manila and Riyadh, alongside the resumption of flights to Tel Aviv and an aircraft upgrade on its Athens route. The changes underline the growing importance of the A350 to Delta's international strategy.

The expansion comes as Delta continues investing heavily in long-haul aircraft. Its fleet of 42 A350s already serves destinations across Asia, Europe, Africa, South America, and Australia, while the larger Airbus A350-1000 is scheduled to arrive in 2027. The latest schedule changes offer a glimpse of how Delta intends to deploy this growing widebody fleet.

According to Cirium, an aviation analytics company, Delta has planned four additional A350-900 deployments, three of which originate at its Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International Airport (ATL) hub. However, these are not all entirely new routes: Athens' Eleftherios Venizelos International Airport (ATH) represents an equipment change, while Tel Aviv's Ben Gurion Airport (TLV) is a resumption of previously operated service.

The two genuinely new nonstop markets are Riyadh and Manila. Riyadh Airport (RUH) is particularly noteworthy, although its launch remains uncertain. Delta originally intended to begin the service in October but postponed it until March 2027, citing regional security developments. In its October 12 announcement, the airline emphasized its commitment to the Saudi Arabian market while prioritizing passenger and employee safety:

"Saudi Arabia remains an important market for Delta, and we are committed to serving the Kingdom for the long-term. We look forward to connecting Saudi Arabia to Delta's global network in March 2027."

Meanwhile, Los Angeles–Manila will launch on March 28, initially operating three times weekly before increasing to daily on June 7. The service marks Delta's return to the Philippines market after several years and will make it the only US airline operating nonstop between the two cities. Philippine Airlines already serves the market, providing established competition.

Atlanta and Los Angeles International Airport (LAX) will dominate Delta's A350 operations in June 2027, with 54 and 50 weekly departures, respectively. Together, they account for approximately 60% of the 175 weekly departures from Delta's six US hubs. Atlanta has the most geographically diverse network, while Los Angeles is especially important for flights across the Pacific.

The Pacific is particularly important to Delta's A350 strategy. Los Angeles will have nonstop A350 services to seven international destinations, including Hong Kong, Shanghai, Manila, Sydney, and Melbourne. Seattle provides another significant Asian gateway, while Detroit, Minneapolis, and Salt Lake City all contribute additional transpacific connectivity.

Delta's recent international expansion has increasingly relied on the aircraft's range and premium capacity. However, the A350's capabilities extend well beyond the Pacific. Atlanta to Johannesburg Airport (JNB) is Delta's longest scheduled service, covering approximately 8,450 miles (13,600 km).

Meanwhile, Cape Town International Airport (CPT) provides another important South African connection. Delta also deploys the aircraft to Santiago and selected European destinations, demonstrating its flexibility across different long-haul markets and its importance to the airline's global network.

Transpacific corridors dominate this list, although the very longest routes serve destinations in Africa.

According to its latest quarterly filing, Delta currently operates 42 Airbus A350-900s, with another 17 on firm order. The carrier also has purchase options for 20 additional widebodies, which can be converted to either A350-900s or Airbus A330-900s. Delta currently operates 39 of the latter, with firm orders for 16 more.

Delta is currently standardizing its A350-900 fleet around a premium-heavy 275-seat configuration, featuring 40 Delta One Suites, 40 Premium Select seats, 36 Comfort seats, and 159 Main Cabin seats. Fleetwide conversions are targeted for completion by the end of 2027. The next major development will be the arrival of Delta's first A350-1000s in early 2027.

The airline has ordered 20 of the larger variant, with eight scheduled for delivery during 2027 and 12 during 2028. These aircraft will introduce next-generation Delta One Suites, larger entertainment screens, and additional premium capacity, supporting the carrier's international growth ambitions.

Delta's January 2026 order for 15 additional A350-900s and 16 A330-900neos further reinforces this strategy. Once its current Airbus widebody commitments are fulfilled, Delta expects its A350 fleet to reach at least 79 aircraft, including the 20 A350-1000s. Alongside its future Boeing 787-10 fleet, these aircraft will provide greater flexibility as Delta expands internationally and gradually replaces older widebodies.

Jason Statham & Guy Ritchie Officially Reunite For New Netflix Action Crime Movie

Jason Statham and Guy Ritchie are now set to reunite at Netflix for their seventh movie together.

Action star Jason Statham and director Guy Ritchie's movie collaborations date back to 1998 with the crime comedy Lock, Stock and Two Smoking Barrels, later being followed by Snatch (2001), Revolver (2007), Wrath of Man (2021), Operation Fortune: Ruse du Guerre (2023), and the forthcoming thriller Viva La Madness. Now, for the first time, Ritchie and Statham are bringing their action prowess to Netflix for a revenge-focused action vehicle.

Netflix has now officially announced that Guy Ritchie and Jason Statham will re-team for Dogfight, a revenge thriller "with a stylish twist" set in the criminal underworld of London. While Netflix has confirmed Ritchie and Statham's involvement and teased "a cast full of colorful criminals and plenty of thrilling action," plot details for Dogfight are still being kept tightly under wraps.

However, the streaming service has revealed that the upcoming film will kick off production in early 2027 in the UK. Dogfight is written and directed by Guy Ritchie, who also serves as a producer alongside Jason Statham, Ivan Atkinson, and John Friedberg. The Netflix thriller is being produced in collaboration with Black Bear, Toff Guy Films, and Punch Palace.

Dogfight marks Guy Ritchie's second collaboration with Netflix after his hit crime thriller series The Gentlemen, which serves as a spinoff of Ritchie's 2019 movie of the same name. Ahead of its critically acclaimed sophomore outing's release this September, Netflix officially renewed The Gentlemen for season 3, which is already in production for a speedier turnaround.

As they prepare to begin filming on their seventh movie together early next year, Guy Ritchie and Jason Statham already have packed upcoming release slates. Harry Redknapp fires warning to West Ham fans over Scott Parker appointment

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Former West Ham player and manager Harry Redknapp has warned Hammers supporters that they may have to see an unattractive brand of football under Scott Parker due to the limitations of the playing squad.

The East London outfit sacked Nuno Espírito Santo on Tuesday morning following successive home defeats to Southampton and West Brom, which has left the Hammers eighth in the Championship table before their trip to Cardiff City on Wednesday evening.

The hierarchy have moved swiftly to find his successor, with former Hammers midfielder Scott Parker set to land the role.

Parker has guided all of Bournemouth, Fulham and Burnley back to the promised land, with his excellent CV in the second tier enough to convince those at the top of West Ham that he can register a fourth promotion at the London Stadium.

Questions have been raised about whether Parker is a long-term fit for the club should he get them back to the top-flight, but focus for now remains on getting the Hammers back towards the top end of the table, with the team’s confidence bound to have been dented after successive home defeats against fellow promotion rivals.

Meanwhile, Harry Redknapp, who has such a deep connection to West Ham after coming through the youth ranks as a player and going on to eventually manage the club, has commented on Parker being close to landing the job, and what the supporters can expect from him in the dugout.

Before a ball was kicked ahead of the new Championship season, many were touting West Ham to run away with the division and be convincing champions, in turn sealing an instant return to the Premier League.

But it’s been far from straight forward, with Hammers supporters deeply frustrated by the performances during their opening 13 games.

Fans have been worried for a while about the style of play and the team’s inability to start games quickly enough, and those problems came to roost against two promotion rivals in Southampton and West Brom over the last week, who put West Ham to the sword in front of their own supporters.

Such heavy defeats dropped West Ham to eighth in the table, which was enough to make the hierarchy believe that Nuno was not the man to lead the club forward.

The club confirmed his departure on their club website, which read: “West Ham United can confirm that men’s first-team Head Coach Nuno Espírito Santo has left his position with immediate effect.

“Following a thorough review of the start to the EFL Championship season, the Board of Directors believe that a change is necessary at this time in order to ensure that the Club’s objectives and expectations remain on track.

“Assistant Head Coach Rui Pedro Silva and Goalkeeper Coach Rui Barbosa have also departed.

“West Ham United would like to thank Nuno, Rui and Rui for their hard work and commitment during their time at the Club, and wish them all the very best for the future.

“The process of recruiting a new Head Coach is already underway.

“Mark Noble, Steve Potts and Gerard Prenderville will take temporary charge of squad preparations ahead of tomorrow evening’s EFL Championship fixture at Cardiff City, and the Club will make no further comment until the appointment of the new Head Coach is confirmed.”

In the next few hours, a report by the BBC revealed that West Ham were in advanced talks to name Scott Parker as Nuno’s successor, and that his appointment could be confirmed before Wednesday's Championship clash against the Bluebirds.

Speaking to Jeff Stelling and Andy Townsend live on TalkSPORT (From 11:35), Harry Redknapp, who managed 322 games for West Ham between 1994 and 2001, reacted to the developing news of Scott Parker closing in on the Hammers job.

And he fired over a warning to the fanbase about expectations over the style of play under Parker, acknowledging that while the supporters want to see an entertaining way of playing, the limitations in the playing squad may make it difficult for Parker to deliver such a front-footed approach.

Responding to Townsend’s question about Parker striking the right balance, Redknapp replied: “They want to see you play football for sure, if you can, but as you know, Andy, you need players that can play to play good football. They can all come in and have ideas about what we should be doing.

“West Ham was called the academy because they had incredible players like Jonny Burn, Alan Devonshire, (Tony) Cottie, (Frank) McAvennie, Billy Bonds, Frank Lampard, you go back to Bobby Moore, Geoff Hurst, Martin Peters, they’ve had incredible players, they haven’t got them players now, so whatever it takes to get out the division, then that’s what Scott’s got to really.

“If it means playing up to a big centre-forward and getting up the park, then that’s what you’ve got to do.

“They can all dream of a philosophy, but it’s not easy to implement unless you’ve got the players.”

Reaction to the news of Nuno Espírito Santo departing the London Stadium was largely positive, but there have been some mixed views about Scott Parker stepping into the hotseat.

Dave from West Ham TV is a fan of Parker coming in for the short-term, but believes in the long-term he will end up failing.

Dorset Hammer has shown some concern about the short amount of time Parker has stayed at clubs during his managerial career so far, pointing out that he hasn’t lasted longer than 18 months at any of his former employers.

West Ham fan Nathan is also not a fan of the Parker appointment, believing Kieran McKenna was a better and at the same time, still a realistic target.

Finally, another Hammers fan has backed Parker to come into the club and get them promoted, but says the club should then part ways given his poor track record in the top-flight.

There is gigantic pressure and expectation awaiting Scott Parker, and given the mixed reviews on social media to his arrival, it doesn’t appear his extensive time as a Hammers player will grant him much patience.

West Ham have to turn the tide quickly if they are to avoid slipping down the table further, as if that happens, there could be an increase of swirling toxicity that makes Parker’s job so much harder to succeed in.

There’s a lot of areas to fix for Parker, with organisation in the backline a priority, which has been on show with the Hammers conceding six goals in their last two home matches.

West Ham fans, do you back Scott Parker to turn the tide at the London Stadium? Can he be a long-term replacement for Nuno? Drop your thoughts in the comments below!

It may have to see Parker batten down the hatches and start grinding out results, although that is something that could grate with the supporters given their urge to see a positive style of play.

But what matters most is getting back to the Premier League. If the results turn because of the change, then many can’t complain, but if the style of play gets worse and the results don’t improve, then Parker may not be in his post for too long.

It’s such a big job for Parker to take on, with his credibility as a boss on the line following on from yet another short last stint at Turf Moor.

Forget Takeout: Customers Love These Frozen Mexican Foods

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

If you're lucky, you live just a short ride away from a half dozen quality Mexican restaurants serving bubbling bowls of pozole, punchy tacos, and sultry stews of slow-cooked meats. But not everyone lives in communities blessed with quality Mexican cooking. And that's a major bummer, because this regional cuisine is simply electric, balancing deep flavors with fresh herbs, warm spices, and piquant chiles. Mexican food plays with different textures and shapes, allowing you to compose bites that are simultaneously creamy and crisp, cool and spicy, savory and sweet.

Thankfully, you can enjoy decent Mexican and Tex-Mex-inspired food from the comfort of your couch. And all it takes is a trip to your local supermarket. First, let's preface this entire article by saying that a good restaurant will almost always outdo a frozen dupe. Still, there is something to be said for the convenience and cost savings of air-frying a chimichanga or microwaving a burrito for lunch. I turned to the internet to find frozen Mexican meals comparable to what you might get at a restaurant or fast-casual takeout spot. After sorting through hundreds of reviews and comments, I put together a list of the top customer-favorite Mexican appetizers, sides, and main meals available in the freezer aisle.

The foundation of a good tamale is corn, specifically corn that's been soaked in an alkaline solution (AKA nixtamalized), which transforms the dense starchy kernels into a pliable, dough-like carbohydrate called masa. Making masa (and tamales) requires time, skill, precision, and technique. But if you don't possess any of the above, then heating up a frozen tamale is your best option.

There are a surprising number of choices in this department, but customers are especially fond of the Trader Joe's versions. Like many Trader Joe's items, people gripe that these come in and out of stock with about as much predictability as a June weather forecast. However, the cheese and green chile and the chicken and cheese varieties are consistently the most reliable (and available). Tamales make a good side dish, especially if you want an alternative to potatoes or pasta, or, like one Redditor, you can steam one and bring it for lunch. A negative is that since they have to be reheated, the masa interior can get a tad dry. To remedy this, Trader Joe's shoppers recommend a side of salsa, hot sauce, guacamole, rice and beans, or a blanket of extra melted cheese.

As far as frozen Mexican entrées go, the burrito bowl can be controversial. Why? Unlike a tamale, chimichanga, or taquito, a burrito bowl doesn't require the same finesse, so customers generally expect a higher quality product. Surprisingly, many reliable brands struggle to execute what seems like a simple dish. But not Kevin's Natural Foods. If unfamiliar, Kevin's positions itself as a better-for-you frozen meal. Ingredients are clean, non-GMO, and gluten-free. These restrictions are far from a hindrance. On the contrary, the quality of Kevin's frozen meals, especially its burrito bowls, is top-notch.

At least, that's what the customers say. "Absolutely loaded with cheese, chicken in every bite, rice perfectly cooked, veggies still have a bit of a snap to them, and the chipotle sauce has a ton of flavor," one Redditor commented. Others shared a similar opinion. With Kevin's, there's a general appreciation for the quality and portion size. The brand doesn't skimp on proteins or portion size, and whatever ingredients are included are thoughtfully prepared — no mushy rice, sad veggies, or gloopy cheese. The result keeps customers happy.

Many culinary historians consider mole the national dish of Mexico. The sauce's dynamic flavors mirror the country's history: dark and complex, with an underlying sweetness and grounding depth. Mole requires dozens of Mexico's most prized indigenous ingredients — chocolate, chiles, nuts, seeds, spices — to create something that's, frankly, unmatched in any other cuisine.

Does Trader Joe's version capture that centuries of Mexican culinary tradition? That would be impossible. However, given its price and unbelievable convenience, it does a pretty good job of imitating the sweet-and-savory balance of a traditional mole. With just one package, you can make multiple meals. And while a traditional mole takes hours — if not days — to prepare, TJ's frozen chicken mole takes just minutes in the microwave.

The taste is bold, but the texture of the chicken — not overly mushy or stringy — makes it ideal for several applications. Customers say they use it to fill enchiladas and tacos, but you can use it for everything from pizza to nachos to an omelet.

Known for its better-for-you ingredient profile, Amy's has been supplying organic frozen entrées for decades. While some frozen tortilla-wrapped items suffer from a textural breakdown, the tortillas housing Amy's enchilada filling hold up well. All of Amy's frozen entrées are vegetarian-friendly, but where they lack in meat, they make up for it in texture, flavor, and composition. Amy's cheese enchiladas have been a hit with shoppers for decades, with the blend of cheeses, toasty corn tortillas, and savory tomato-based enchilada sauce, and they still earn online praise today.

"I find these to have an incredible depth of flavor for a frozen meal," began one Redditor's review. "I would be 100% happy if this was served to me in a restaurant." Others say that the product is the best in frozen food. And while there's no question that shoppers have enjoyed and appreciated the taste of Amy's foods for years, they are a bit disgruntled about the ever-increasing price point. And while that might be on the higher end of the price spectrum, anything in the $7 or $8 range for a frozen meal does seem a little steep. But they're still beloved, despite that.

The taquito was created as a thrifty way to use up leftover tortillas and filling. Little did its creators know that the fried, toasty snack would become widely beloved happy marriage of taste, form, and texture. Taquitos are almost always made from corn tortillas and filled with everything from chicken and cheese to beef, veggies, and beans. But the best part of a taquito is its tubular shape that's seemingly designed for dipping.

Frozen taquitos aren't new, and their basic composition hasn't changed much over the years. You'll find a plethora of options in the freezer aisle, but customers are most fond of El Monterey's Extra Crunchy Southwest Chicken variety. Fans say it's hard to eat just one... or two. Shoppers also love its unique exterior crust.

The only downside to El Monterey's taquitos is the quantity of filling. There's a collective sigh at how the interiors have grown increasingly barren over the years, an observation noticeable in the chicken taquitos, where the chicken and vegetables retain their individual shapes, unlike the beef and bean option, which is more of a paste. An easy solution is to prepare a few different dips — sour cream, guacamole, queso — to add extra moisture and unctuousness.

If a taquito and a burrito had a baby, it would be a chimichanga. It's deep-fried, which ups the richness but also gives the tortilla a crispy shell that's the ideal foil for any smooth refried bean or cheese filling. Deep frying at home doesn't require the same precision as making a chocolate soufflé, but it still means more work and cleanup than simply reheating on a hot frying pan. That's why a good frozen chimichanga is crucial if you suddenly get that itch for this fried Tex-Mex specialty. And, again, the El Monterey brand delivers.

Online, there was a lot of love for El Monterey, saying it has some of the best frozen burritos and chimichangas on the market. Unlike the taquitos, the chimichangas have a generous filling that, as one person put it, is comparable to the reference photo on the bag. The key to executing these properly is in the preparation. The chimichanga is inherently greasy, so a quick thaw in the microwave followed by a crisp-up on a hot pan usually does the trick. An air-fryer works, too. Some people just throw them in the deep fryer because, well, why not?

The burrito is the quintessential frozen Mexican food item, and over the decades, dozens — if not hundreds — of brands have battled each other for category supremacy. Many have faltered, but a handful have held their ground. Like Red's. Founded in 2009, Red's focuses on one thing: burritos. Sure, it'll try a breakfast sandwich or a "Snacker," but its product lineup is mostly just an array of tortilla-wrapped goodies.

Customers are big fans of the steak and cheddar burrito. While other cheaper, mass-market frozen burritos rely on mushy, indistinguishable fillings, Red's has made a name for itself by using higher-quality ingredients. Although shoppers are big fans of Red's entire line of burritos, they do gripe about the prices. Heating the burrito is another issue entirely, but one Red's expert has a foolproof method: Run it under water in the sink for a few seconds before microwaving. The extra moisture makes the tortilla a bit springier and more pliable. Wrapping it in a wet paper towel will have a similar effect.

The Mexican Style Street Corn Triangles from Tastiez are far from traditional Mexican cuisine. Instead, they are a frozen food product inspired by the classic Mexican street food esquites, which is like a bolder, saltier version of creamed corn. Another way to think of it is like elote, that yummy, cheese-dusted ear of grilled corn, stripped off the cob.

This product is a major departure from the original dish, but if you're craving an easy appetizer inspired by an authentic Mexican dish, it's not such a bad substitute. And, most importantly (at least for this article), customers have given these fried, corn-filled triangles their seal of approval. Whereas your average stuffed, fried snack oozes its insides after a hard bake or big bite, these stay pretty much intact. The crisp outside and gooey center make for a satisfying finger food, but it's the taste that brings it home, with reviewers gushing over its delicious street corn flavor. They come out best when deep-fried, but the air-fryer is an excellent alternative that still yields a crisp exterior.

Today, you can find the most elaborate culinary delights in your supermarket freezer. But while brands can execute everything from frozen kimbap to cioppino, many still struggle to nail a good frozen taco. What has customers complaining is the ratio of filling to shell: not enough of the former and too much of the latter, resulting in a product that's dry and brittle. Maybe tacos are difficult to seal, so manufacturers are forced to under fill? Whatever the reason, some brands miss the mark, while others, like Don Miguel, lead by example.

The Don Miguel Chicken Chipotle Mini Tacos have an active and passionate Reddit community that few items on this list can match. But why? What makes this 50-pack of frozen tacos from Costco so special? The price and crave-worthiness. However, while the consensus is generally positive, long-time customers have commented that the tacos are a bit spicier than they used to be. For this issue, the main workaround is a cooling condiment, like Greek yogurt or sour cream. Still, although people do love these things, it seems like the perfect mini taco has yet to be discovered.

The churro is one of the most globally recognized Mexican desserts. It's basically a twist or extruded wand of fried dough that's tossed in cinnamon sugar and served with, if you're lucky, a smooth chocolate sauce or caramel. Churros come in many shapes and sizes. Costco, famous for scaling everything to the max, sells a foot-long version in its food court for just $1.49. But if you want to scratch that churro itch at home, you can pick up a pack of Hola Churros.

No, these aren't as long as the ones you'll find in the food court, but some regular Costco customers think they're just smaller versions of the same make and model. But how do they taste? Overall, shoppers are pretty pleased. Are they as crisp and pleasantly oily as the ones you'll find at a street fair or Mexican restaurant? Probably not. But they do the job. The key is finding ways to deliver that extra punch of flavor. Although they come with individual packs of cinnamon sugar, some Redditors recommend drizzling chocolate on them. They're also good as a topping for an ice cream sundae. For heating, skip the microwave and go for the air-fryer or toaster oven. No matter how you make them, they are an unbelievably good value: around $14.49 for a box of 30.

When you see a chile relleno on the menu at a Mexican restaurant, it's worth getting. This dish is pretty special, integrating all the different tastes and flavors that make cuisine from this part of the world so interesting and seductive. It's essentially a cheese-stuffed pepper that's battered, fried, and served on top of a rich tomato sauce. It takes some work to prepare, but it's a real stunner when it hits the table.

Somehow the team at Trader Joe's has managed to capture all these dynamic tastes and textures in one convenient, easy-to-heat frozen package. The batter is tempura-style, somewhat spongy but not in a soggy way, providing heft and unctuousness to the otherwise bare, roasted pepper wrapper. The cheese is pleasantly rubbery, and the sauce is savory and delicious. On Reddit, customers are surprised by how good it tastes, even despite the soft, slightly mushy texture. Of all the foods on this list, this one had the most overwhelmingly positive score for flavor. The consensus is that you should reheat the peppers in the air-fryer, not the microwave. Overall, people think the flavor is fantastic, and for just $5.99, you really can't go wrong.

Although this brand of Trader Joe's frozen rice is called "Spanish-style," its flavor profile is decidedly Mexican, drawing on spices and ingredients indigenous to Central America. Tomato gives it its mellow orange color, and a savory spice blend and quick toasting add an umami undertone that makes it easy to pair with dishes from across the culinary landscape, from madras lentils to gyros.

On Reddit, Trader Joe's faithful say it's an excellent accompaniment to many of the store's frozen and refrigerated Mexican entrées, like the birria, mole, green chili and cheese tamales, and even the salad kits. Others use it as a quick and flavorful foundation to a dish. Its utility is what makes this product so special. With dozens of comments on one Reddit thread dedicated to the Spanish-style rice, you can find dozens of interesting and inventive use cases. And while it's marketed as a microwavable grain, some shoppers are convinced you get a better result when you toast it in a pan.

For example, the top taquito maker didn't necessarily deliver the best burrito. I wanted to present a variety, so I didn't repeat dishes. It was difficult to find the top flavor(s), so when there wasn't a clear consensus, I tended to go with the one with the most feedback and attention.

Ethereum Privacy Network Aztec Rolls Out Major Security and Speed Upgrade

Aztec, the privacy-focused Ethereum layer-2 network, has rolled out a major upgrade that fixes a critical vulnerability from its previous version, doubles the speed of private transactions, and ushers in a wave of applications built to keep user data hidden.

The Aztec Foundation said Tuesday that the upgrade, called Aztec Tierra and designated Alpha V6, delivers stronger security, formal verification of its proving system and faster deposits from Ethereum, alongside a refreshed brand.

Aztec is a decentralized layer-2 with more than 3,000 validators and fully programmable private smart contracts, and it pitches itself as having no privileged viewing keys or centralized sequencer, meaning no single operator can peer into or control user activity.

The upgrade resolves known vulnerabilities from earlier versions, including a critical issue discovered in V5, and adds extensive hardening. Notably, that includes a new AI-powered security testing harness and formal verification of core cryptographic components, a mathematical process for proving code behaves as intended.

The move reflects a broader scramble across crypto to harden code as AI tools make vulnerabilities easier to find.

On performance, Aztec said private transactions now generate client-side proofs in about one second on a standard device, twice as fast as before, while a new architecture cuts the time for Ethereum-to-Aztec messages from an average of two minutes to as little as 12 seconds, speeding up deposits and cross-chain activity.

“Privacy shouldn’t be something you have to request or trust someone else to protect,” said Zac Williamson, co-founder and CEO of the Aztec Foundation. “It should be built into the network.”

The upgrade arrives with a crop of private applications spanning payments, trading, AI and social.

Leading them is zk.money, a private payments and savings app built by Aztec Labs that the team revived this year. With Tierra’s faster messaging, users can deposit directly from any Ethereum wallet without complex bridging.

Others in the lineup include Galactica for private AI inference, Nemi for private perpetuals trading, a private browser wallet called Nulo, and Rolling Chat, a private message board based on Ethereum co-founder Vitalik Buterin’s own Aztec experiments.

The launch lands amid a renewed push for privacy on Ethereum. Buterin has laid out plans for a “cryptographic world computer” and pressed to slash the cost of quantum-safe privacy, while a separate team spun out a for-profit firm to drive institutional privacy adoption.

Aztec, which raised $17 million in an earlier funding round to build out its network, is betting that privacy becomes a default feature of Ethereum rather than an add-on.
