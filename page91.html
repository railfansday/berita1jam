Here’s What It Takes to Keep a Pool Running During the Winter, According to Experts

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Before you dive in, it’s important to understand the risks and expenses involved.

When most people think of backyard swimming pools, relaxing outdoors in the summertime immediately comes to mind. But some homeowners don’t want the swimming season to end when temperatures drop. Fans of cold-water swimming are starting to consider ways to enjoy their pools throughout the winter as a part of an at-home wellness experience.

“Ten years ago, most people didn’t think winter swims were possible, especially in colder climates,” says Karen Larson, cofounder of Soake Pools. “Times have changed because these days, they’re seeing neighbors, friends, and others do it and post the proof on social media.”

The increasing interest in cold-water experiences is part of a broader wellness trend. In American College of Sports Medicine's 2026 Worldwide Fitness Trends report, cold therapies, including cold plunges, cryotherapy, and cold showers, earned the 17th spot among fitness trends for the year, up from number 20 in its 2025 report.

If you’ve previously closed your swimming pool around Labor Day but are interested in making cold-water swimming a part of your daily wellness routine, there’s a lot to keep in mind. Not every existing pool is a good candidate for keeping open for winter swimming, and it’s important to understand the risks and expenses involved. But if you’re curious, here’s what to consider about keeping a pool open year-round.

Why is cold-water swimming becoming more common? There are numerous purported mental and wellness benefits, from athletic recovery to stress relief and better sleep. A 2025 systematic review and meta-analysis published in PLOS ONE found that cold-water immersion led to a reduction in stress for 12 hours afterward as well as improvements in sleep quality and overall quality of life.

“Winter swimming often creates a time where friends and family can come together without distractions,” adds Larson.

That said, while swimming during the colder months has its perks, there are also several risks to keep in mind. A 2020 review in the International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health found that cold-water immersion can trigger an immediate shock response, leading to involuntary gasping, hyperventilation, and a rapid increase in heart rate and blood pressure. These reactions can make it difficult to control your breathing and increase your risk of drowning. Cold temps can also impair your muscle strength and nerve function, making it challenging to swim safely.

It’s a good idea to acclimate to the cold water gradually and avoid prolonged exposure. You’ll also want to make sure there’s someone else home while you take your cold-water plunge in the pool.

In addition, if you have underlying conditions like heart disease, consult your doctor before all else, as the cold water may place additional strain on your heart.

If you’re trying to consider whether your pool is a good candidate to keep open during the colder months, these are the things to look into.

The cost of keeping a swimming pool open for winter swimming will depend on your location, size, desired water temperature, and the type of heater you use.

“Routine pool maintenance generally remains about the same year-round, although chemical consumption may decrease depending on how frequently the pool is used,” explains Jim Eisch, Jr., vice president of operations at Pinch A Penny Pool Patio Spa. “The biggest expense to consider in the winter is heating the pool.”

For this reason, prospective winter swimmers may want to consider an upgrade to their heating system. Instead of using pricey natural gas heating or a traditional heat pump, Eisch says that inverter technology is one of the most affordable ways to maintain a swimmable pool temperature year-round.

For example, if you live in Florida and have a 10,000-gallon swimming pool, Eisch estimates your annual heating costs will be $2,467 with a natural gas heater, $741 with a traditional heat pump, and $434 for a heat/cool pump that uses inverter technology.

As aforementioned, the goal with wintertime swimming is not to be soaking in bathwater-like temperatures while it’s freezing outside. For safety purposes, winter swims are quick, and the heating system is to keep your chlorinated pool water well above the 19-degree Fahrenheit freezing point.

Unsurprisingly, a standard 14-by-28-foot swimming pool is going to be one of the hardest to use effectively year-round, unless you live in a region with a mild wintertime climate.

“You can technically use a large pool year-round, but it’s difficult to cover all its surface area well enough to hold in the heat, so heating bills can climb fast,” says Larson.

Therefore, smaller sizes like a 10-by-16-foot cocktail pool or plunge pool are better candidates for keeping open. Dedicated winter swimmers should also invest in an easy on-and-off cover—or a motorized auto cover—for added protection.

Regular pool cleaning and maintenance are essential, and this upkeep over the winter is no exception.

“Check the filtration system regularly to make sure it’s circulating smoothly, monitor the water chemistry, and plan for power outages—because there’s a good chance they will happen,” Larson explains. For this reason, you should also invest in a generator that can keep the pump running if the power goes out.

Safe pool access is just as important as any other aspect of the pool’s design. That’s why a nonslip surface, excellent drainage, and ample lighting are key.

“Your design should also provide a clear path from the house to the water,” says Neha Gupta, interior designer and founder at NU Curations.

Especially in the wintertime when ice and snow are a factor in your region, make sure you have a safe and smooth exit plan for getting out of the water, bundled up, and into your warm home without any obstacles. And then, of course, remember to cover the pool back up to keep it protected and ready for your next plunge.

Melania Trump calls for lasting Ukraine truce as she hosts Russian and Ukrainian officials

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Melania Trump was awarded Ukraine's Order of Princess Olha, 1st Class for her "significant personal contribution to humanitarian support for Ukraine"

US First Lady Melania Trump called for a "more enduring" ceasefire in Ukraine as she welcomed Russian and Ukrainian delegations at the White House following last week's truce in the southern region of Kherson.

Hundreds of residents of the Russian-occupied Ukrainian town of Oleshky facing starvation and constant bombardment were able to evacuate during a six-day ceasefire, which both Moscow and Kyiv credited Trump with brokering.

"The lives of innocent people must transcend the division of war. I hope Oleshky inspires greater peace," she said.

The unusual White House event saw senior Ukrainian and Russian officials meet away from the setting of official US-brokered peace talks they have been holding without success since last year.

The delegations were made up of Ukraine's human rights ombudsman Dmytro Lubinets and his Russian counterpart Yana Lantratova, as well as Ukrainian MP Davyd Arakhamia and Kremlin envoy Kirill Dmitriev.

Melania Trump was credited by all for playing a key though unspecified role in the ceasefire, and all parties conveyed to her the gratitude of the Russian and Ukrainian presidents.

Melania Trump handed Dmitriev and Arakhamia "personal letters" for their respective presidents

The US first lady said she had "reviewed demining plans, aerial images, assessed roadways, and discussed evacuation routes, hospital access, vehicles, and medical resources" during the truce.

"Last week in Oleshky and neighbouring areas of Kherson, people experienced the simple joys of life for the first time since 2022," Trump said. "They walked down familiar streets without fear of explosions."

She thanked both Volodymyr Zelensky and Vladimir Putin, making no reference to the origins of the war, which began when the Russian president launched a full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022, and which has caused thousands of civilian casualties and the displacement of millions.

Dmitriev, a businessman and Kremlin special envoy who has previously represented Moscow during peace talks with Ukraine, thanked Trump for "saving and touching so many lives".

"Let us continue making a humanitarian contribution together; this is within our power," Dmitriev said, before gifting Melania Trump a framed drawing by an 11-year-old Oleshky evacuee.

He also praised Trump for her work on reuniting children separated from their families by the war. These include children allegedly abducted by Russia.

Arakhamia, a Ukrainian MP who heads Zelensky's party in parliament who has also been part of the Ukrainian negotiating team, said the Oleshky ceasefire had constituted the "biggest de-escalation since 2022".

"We are creating a momentum where both sides are learning theâ¦ mechanics of how to actually keep the peace when the peace comes," he added.

He acknowledged that the "political track" of negotiations to end the conflict was "complex" but said Ukrainians were asking Melania Trump "to keep going and find new missions and de-escalate more."

At the end of the event, Trump handed Dmitriev and Arakhamia two letters to be hand-delivered to their respective presidents.

The White House later said Melania Trump had invited her guests to view two historic artifacts which she had "personally selected as powerful symbols of peace".

One was an 18th Century medicine chest which was removed from the White House when the British set it on fire in 1812. "Once adversaries, the United States and Great Britain ultimately established a strong alliance," the White House said.

The second artifact was a cup gifted in 1899 to the French ambassador in recognition of his efforts to bring the Spanish-American war to an end.

Melania Trump has generally remained discreet on foreign policy matters, and neither Moscow nor Kyiv have elaborated on the nature or extent of her involvement in coordinating the delicate ceasefire - the most enduring and widespread of the war so far.

A source who works in the local government of the Kherson region told the BBC that work on the Oleshky evacuation had been ongoing for almost a year.

Oleshky evacuees arrived in Ukraine after a journey through southern Russia and Belarus

However, the source said, "at a certain point other individuals became involved, seemingly after most of the groundwork had already been laid and subsequently began taking credit for the progress that had been made."

It was difficult to determine exactly who had played what role and at which stage of the process, the source acknowledged, adding that "those who had been directly involved in the negotiations for nearly a year were eventually prohibited from communicating publicly about the matter."

Speaking after the meeting in the White House had taken place, President Zelensky said the success of the Oleshky ceasefire "had proved a very simple thing: when Putin wants the fighting to stop, it stops."

"The same applies to our people who are held in Russian captivity. When Putin wants, they go home."

A source close to American officials shared a similar assessment, implying to the BBC that despite the outward praise, "the person who made [the ceasefire] possible, whether we like it or not, is essentially Putin."

"And this was done in order to pass the ball to [Donald] Trump... so that it would have some positive effect for his midterm elections."

There has been no change whatsoever in Russia's position, Ukrainian political analyst Dmytro Levus told the BBC.

Moscow "agreed to it because there was pressure from the American side... In doing so, the Russians confirmed Zelenskyy's words that, when necessary, it turns out Russia can quickly stop firing and even maintain that ceasefire."

As the frontline region of Kherson experienced its first six days of peace in several years, the rest of Ukraine continued to come under Russian attack.

This week, several drone and missile attacks killed and injured civilians across the country. On Monday alone, more than 40 people were killed in a single Russian strike on an aid distribution centre in the region of Zaporizhzhia. More are thought to still be buried under the rubble.

Iran war hawk Pete Hegseth is facing the fallout from Republicans' midterms losses as high energy prices turned voters toward Democrats. US President Trump continues to support his embattled defense chief — for now.

Republicans in Washington continued to assess the damage on Thursday, after a resounding electoral defeat in the midterm elections two days prior saw Democrats take over Congress by large margins.

Republican Party insiders told US outlets CNN and The Washington Post that Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth is feeling the heat over criticism that the war in Iran, which he supports, cost his party the elections.

Trump on Wednesday acknowledged that the Iran war affected the midterms, saying that voters were frustrated how the 8-month-long conflict has cause gas and diesel prices to skyrocket.

"The Iran thing we had to do, and we continue to do it," Trump told reporters, but he ultimately defended the war effort and denied any plans to fire his defense secretary over it, saying Hegseth was "doing a very good job."

But US Vice President JD Vance has sought to distance himself from the war, questioning whether it was worth the cost.

"The ⁠reason ⁠we lost is the war," Vance said on X. "If you'd like to argue it was worth it, that losing the Senate for the next 6 years was worth it, then do so."

Hegseth has been a loud voice in Washington for the US attacking Iran, and has publicly chastised lawmakers and journalists for raising questions about the conflict's strategic objectives, cost and lack of congressional authorization.

The defense secretary has also been accused of not being forthright about the damage to US bases in the Middle East and the severity of troop casualties. He has also purged dozens of high ranking military officers, while denying promotions to others on dubious grounds.

Hegseth was in hot water even before the Iran war. In October 2025, a Signal group chat between himself and Vice President JD Vance discussing a military strike on Yemen in October 2025 accidentally included a journalist, and led to calls for Hegseth's dismissal.

According to CNN's reporting, multiple Republicans told the network on condition of anonymity that many in their party are now turning against the defense secretary.

Sources also told the Washington Post that the political situation at the Defense Department appears "very dynamic at the moment," with "knives out."

With Trump backing Hegseth for now, The Post reported that those close to the Trump administration have floated the notion that the president might dismiss General Dan Caine, the chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff, or Admiral Brad Cooper, who has overseen the war, instead of the embattled Hegseth.

Pressure is mounting in Capitol Hill, as Hegseth's critics from both sides of the aisle turn their focus on him.

CNN reported that House Republicans are expected to hold an impeachment vote to directly repudiate the defense secretary in a matter of days.

Republican Representative Thomas Massie of Kentucky, who has been a critic of Trump, told CNN he plans to trigger the impeachment, which multiple GOP sources say is expect to pass.

In September, Massie had said he would force a vote in the House on impeaching Hegseth, which led to House Speaker Mike Johnson sending Congress into recess until after the midterms.

On Thursday, Massie posted on X that his impeachment resolution is a "much gentler" version of what's coming when the Democrats take over Congress in January.

"They'll have hearings & trials," he wrote.

Chief Pentagon spokesman Sean Parnell said that Hegseth "will not back down or be deterred by politicians seeking to score partisan points or launch phony investigations where no wrongdoing exists."

"We will continue to drive President Trump's agenda at the Department of War — despite threats of sham witch hunts from Democrats — and Secretary Hegseth is 100% committed to leading the Department until the end of President Trump's term in office," Parnell added, using the administration's name for the Department of Defense.

Fresh off their sweeping wins, Democrats will have the power to launch investigations on Hegseth and even slash funding for the Defense Department and the Iran war.

"I'm going after Pete Hegseth. Not only is he incompetent and corrupt, he's undermining our national security and the safety of our troops," said Representative Jason Crow, a Democratic member of the House Armed Services Committee.

Representative Adam Smith, the top Democrat on the committee, said a top priority would be "to conduct vigorous oversight of the Department of Defense and the Trump Administration," including on "the illegal war against Iran."

In the Wake of the 2026 Midterms, Can We Reframe the Patriotic Song?

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

When Brandy Clark arrived on set to film the lyric video for her new single “American Roots,” her makeup artist had an idea. He wanted to paint her face and hands red, white, and blue, to riff on the song’s ideas of envisioning a loving, inclusive America where everyone, including immigrants, belong.

Clark was a little nervous at first, but it didn’t stop her from moving forward. “I’m so associated with the rainbow flag,” Clark, whose excellent new album The Bird is out in January, tells Rolling Stone. “But we deserve this. My fans deserve to see this.  And a lot of people in my family, my dad being one of them, served in the military for that flag. That’s all of our flag. MAGA does not own the flag.”

It’s a sentiment that a lot of Americans have been wrestling with as the Trump administration has worked to turn the flag into a symbol not of pride, but exclusion — and for country fans, one that was co-opted in a post 9/11 landscape where jingoistic songs passing as patriotism became radio gold. In the wake of the November midterms, however, when a blue wave saw Democratic gains in states once dismissed as lost causes, can artists change their relationship to patriotic songs?

Long before Tuesday’s elections, it’s something that Clark was thinking about with “American Roots,” along with others like Mickey Guyton, the War and Treaty, and many more country and roots artists interested in reframing exactly who gets to own the idea of patriotism. In a clip from Newport Folk Festival making the viral rounds this week, Joan Baez, alongside Mavis Staples, Nathaniel Rateliff, Brandi Carlile, and others, close the festival with their take on “America the Beautiful,” introduced with some potent words. “This song is something that we are taking back,” Baez said.

Part of this process, according to the War and Treaty’s Michael Trotter Jr., is divorcing patriotism from partisan (and often hateful, exclusionary) politics. “There are words we use against each other to create sides: ‘fake,’ and ‘woke,’ and ‘patriot,’” Crow said. “The word ‘patriotic’ and ‘patriot’ really is offensive to me. Because I feel very patriotic about my country, but I’m not a conservative. I’m a middle-of-the-road pragmatist. That approach has been a part of the country music playbook for decades now, and was on Clark’s mind when she started writing “American Roots,” a song that is gentle in its messaging: we’re all Americans, with equal rights to walk on our hallowed land, wherever you may come from originally. It hinges on a lyric in the bridge. “When you set foot onto that shore,” she sings, “it’s no more mine than it is yours.”

LIV Golf Shutting Down After Last-Ditch Financing Plan Fails

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Five years after it began its quest to upend a genteel sport with billions in Saudi Arabian money and brash style, LIV Golf is liquidating its operations.

The would-be rival to the PGA Tour, which was championed by major winner Phil Mickelson and run by another former player of note, Greg Norman, had financing in place to attempt a reboot after Saudi Arabia pulled its investment. But BC Partners Credit, which had promised $300 million for what was dubbed “LIV 2.0,” reversed course after seeing the difficulty the golf league was having recruiting players.

Executives who were leading the effort to shepherd LIV through Chapter 11 bankruptcy told a U.S. Bankruptcy Court judge in New Jersey on Thursday that they would pivot toward a full liquidation of the league. The Saudi Public Investment Fund‘s decision to back away from LIV, which had been a major component in the Mideast nation’s effort to promote itself on the global stage, proved to be an insurmountable hurdle. The league regularly drew controversy due to its Saudi backing, drawing protests at a Donald Trump-owned tournament site in New Jersey from families of 9/11 victims outraged by what they saw as the legitimizing of a state sponsor of the terrorist attacks.

Jon Rahm, one of the PGA veterans who accepted large paydays in order to go exclusive with LIV, has recently expressed a willingness to face penalties in order to return. Bryson DeChambeau, perhaps the most ardent promoter of LIV among current major championship contenders, faces an uncertain future after the dissolution of the league. The reluctance of Rahm, DeChambeau and others to sign onto LIV 2.0 sealed its fate.

LIV got its name from the Roman numerals for 54, which is the number of holes its three-day tournaments encompassed. Traditional PGA and USGA events span four days and 72 holes. Along with that departure from golf custom, LIV also allowed players to wear shorts and organized them into Formula 1-style teams with names like the Crushers. On the courses, loud music from DJ tents blared as players teed off, part of an intentional bid to disrupt the country-club feel and hushed tones of the PGA.

Max Justicz, the chief technology officer and cofounder of the startup Charge Robotics, used an OpenAI model to find a severe vulnerability that — if exploited — could allow hackers to take control of servers running Ubuntu, a widely-used open-source Linux based operating system.

Justicz, who has a hobbyist’s interest in cybersecurity and previously found a vulnerability in the same codebase, told TechCrunch that he found this issue using GPT 5.6 Sol, released in July, through the AI maker’s Daybreak Blue, a program that gives cybersecurity defenders access to powerful frontier models.

“I literally just aimed ChatGPT at the same body of code and said ‘I found this bug previously that was fixed several years ago, please find bugs as bad or worse,’ and it came back in 2 hours with this,” Justicz said. “It’s extremely convoluted and hard for a human to find, but it works!”

He reported the issue to the Debian Project, which maintains the software in question and issued a patch to fix the vulnerability. There has been no known evidence of exploitation. Still, the ease with which he found it suggests that we’ve only seen the tip of the iceberg when it comes to potential exploits that even novice hackers can utilize with the help of AI models.

“I can only imagine what one could do with more time and access to less neutered models. We should expect some extremely crazy bugs to be found and exploited over the next few months,” Justicz wrote in a social media thread discussing his finding. “This might be obvious to most folks here, but there is currently huge alpha in taking some code or binary someone has not audited yet and just aiming a powerful AI model at it.”

OpenAI appears to have noticed. Today, the AI giant announced a new model focused on cybersecurity, ChatGPT Cyber, would be made available to the highest-tier of users in the cybersecurity program, Daybreak Red, which allows offensive cybersecurity researchers tools for vulnerability hunting and development. Yesterday, OpenAI said that the same model had been used to scan and prepare patches for vulnerabilities in the Linux kernel, finding 948 potential issues. OpenAI also found 81 “novel” vulnerabilities in Firefox, the web browser.

“Our goal is to dramatically expand Daybreak, bringing advanced security research to organizations of every size, independent researchers, and open-source maintainers,” the frontier lab said in a statement.

OpenAI has insisted that there is a narrow window of time for the people who safeguard digital infrastructure to take action before AI models become even better at hacking and easier to access. For cyber defenders, however, it can feel like the frontier labs have unleashed a mountain of new work that outstrips their resources. Justicz said that he personally spent about $5,000 on subscriptions and tokens to find several exploits.

Besides Daybreak’s model access, OpenAI has also announced a $1 billion initiative to support cybersecurity professionals in the program who work on critical infrastructure like the electrical grid or water systems.

The Daybreak program has its own critiques. Justicz notes that the frontier lab appears to have recently “nerfed” Daybreak Blue to make it less useful at finding vulnerabilities. One one hand, it may be good to limit access from potential bad actors — Justicz said he just uploaded a photo of his drivers’ license to join the program. On the other, cybersecurity professionals say they have one hand tied if they can’t use frontier technology while bad actors can deploy open-weight models with abliterated guardrails.

Still, OpenAI says just “thousands” of cybersecurity professionals are participating in the Daybreak program, versus a far larger number of potential hackers.

It’s unclear if frontier labs are devoting enough of their resources to this issue. Eric Wallace, a researcher on OpenAI’s technical staff, said that “we are now allocating a significant amount of our frontier compute towards discovering and patching vulnerabilities in open source codebases, fully pro bono.”

But the Linux bug hunt cost $1.2 million, which is significantly less than the approximate $6 million token spend the company invested in finding a potential solution to a famous problem of fluid dynamics.

If there is a narrow window of time to patch the digital infrastructure the world depends on, more urgency may be required.

Liam Neeson's 2022 Flop Gets A Shot At Redemption With Fox's Dark Thriller

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Although one Liam Neeson action thriller from 2022 never came together quite right, Memory has gotten a shot at redemption with a dark new show from Fox that converts its story into a long-form format. Sometimes, even great adaptations can withstand a second go-around. When 1994’s Anne Rice adaptation Interview with the Vampire assembled an astounding cast including Tom Cruise, Brad Pitt, Antonio Banderas, and Stephen Rea, it seemed like the beguiling movie might never be outdone.

However, after a few decades of killer vampire shows like From Dusk Til Dawn: The Series and Buffy the Vampire Slayer, AMC took another shot at Interview with the Vampire with a longer series based on the same book that debuted in 2022. The result was a genuine masterpiece that made major stars of its leading men, Sam Reid and Jacob Anderson, as well as reintroducing Rice’s classic Gothic tragedy to a new generation of viewers.

Since the original movie was already perfectly good and its TV remake was even better, it stands to reason that a TV show remaking an already-flawed movie is even more understandable. Released in 2022, the Liam Neeson vehicle Memory was an action thriller from Just Play Dead director Martin Campbell. Based on the Jef Geeraerts novel De Zaak Alzheimer, the movie had a killer pitch, but squandered it with forgettable action sequences and a lack of depth. Fortunately, Fox’s Memory Of A Killer can now do justice to the novel.

Created by Ed Whitmore, Fox’s Memory Of A Killer stars Patrick Dempsey as the hitman Angelo Doyle. While Angelo’s brother has already lost his memory to Alzheimer’s disease, Angelo is only just starting to see the effects of the disease materialize in his own faltering memory. Angelo has managed to meticulously hide his secret criminal career from his family for years, but when a hit is put out on his pregnant daughter, his fading memory starts to blur his worlds together.

While Memory Of A Killer has a bigger cast of characters than 2022’s Memory, that is not the only difference between the two movies. In Memory, Neeson’s aging hitman lives in Mexico and is sent on one last job in El Paso, whereas in Memory Of A Killer, Dempsey’s Angelo is based in New York City. Angelo maintains an elaborate second life posing as a photocopier salesman, while Neeson’s lonely protagonist is a full-time contract killer.

Both Dempsey and Neeson's projects are based on 2003’s Belgian thriller The Alzheimer Case, directed by The Loft’s Erik Van Looy. However, while Memory is almost too straightforwardly similar to The Alzheimer Case, Memory Of A Killer changes a lot of plot details. These range from changing the setting to introducing the double life story, to adding Angelo’s backstory as a widower.

The end of Memory Of A Killer season 1 threw Angelo a curveball when one villain’s death only opened up another can of proverbial worms with the revelation of a mysterious document. Meanwhile, Angelo learns that his dementia is getting worse near the end of season 1, meaning it seems increasingly unlikely that Memory Of A Killer’s protagonist will be able to keep his work and personal lives separate as the show continues.

While Memory Of A Killer season 2’s release date has not yet been announced, the Fox show is expected to return early in 2027 as part of the network’s mid-season schedule. Deadline reports that season 2’s cast will introduce Soul Food’s Boris Kodjoe as Tre, the Fourth Musketeer of Angelo’s friend group, while Meta Golding will play Angelo’s childhood love Mel when Memory Of A Killer season 2 arrives next year.

macOS 27.3 Golden Gate public beta 1 now available, here’s everything new

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Following the release of macOS 27.3 developer beta 1, Apple is now rolling out the first test build for users enrolled in the public beta. Here’s what’s coming

Contrary to macOS 27.2, it looks like Apple had quite a bit in store for macOS 27.3 and its counterparts.

In addition to expanding Siri AI to four more languages, including Traditional Chinese, Norwegian, Turkish, and Vietnamese, the system also introduces several new Safari features, including Vertical Tabs, Adaptive Dark Mode for websites, and the ability to organize and manage tabs using Siri AI.

macOS 27.3 also adds native Markdown rendering to Quick Look, allowing users to preview .md files with proper formatting instead of raw Markdown syntax.

The update also expands Siri AI’s capabilities across Apple’s own apps, allowing users to perform more complex actions in apps like Messages, Music, Calendar, Photos, and Reminders, as well as manage files and folders.

Apple is also introducing new tools for third-party developers to create advanced Siri AI integrations using Markdown-based instructions, allowing the assistant to coordinate multistep actions across different apps.

Users who enjoyed the wallpaper introduced with the new touchscreen MacBook Pro will also find a new screensaver called Pro Lighteensaver, which features light moving along the metallic-looking lines that form the word “PRO.”

Earlier this week, Apple also confirmed that macOS 27.3 will introduce new checks for potentially harmful content shared through public iCloud links. Starting with the update, files and photos shared using the “Anyone with the link” option will be checked by Apple, which may remove flagged content or take action against accounts that violate its policies.

The release build for macOS 27.3 public beta 1 is 26C5035r, which is the same as the developer build.

If you’d like to join Apple’s free public beta program, visit beta.apple.com.
