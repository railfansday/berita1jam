French Cinema Is Thriving at Festivals but Facing an Uphill Battle in U.S. Theaters as Distributors Navigate a Shrinking Market

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

French cinema may be enjoying a golden run on the festival circuit and in the awards race, but getting those films into U.S. theaters, and persuading American audiences to buy tickets, has become an increasingly uphill battle.

That paradox was at the heart of a lively discussion at the American French Film Festival on Thursday, held in partnership with the CNC and Variety, where three veteran U.S. distributors painted a sobering picture of a market squeezed by shrinking theatrical opportunities. Related Stories CAA's Roeg Sutherland Says It's Too Soon to Predict Fallout From Netflix Firings, Thinks Recent Mergers Represent 'Opportunity' for Industry Why Netflix Fired Dan Lin

The panel brought together Robert Aronson, executive VP of Cohen Media Group; John Gerrans, co-president of Strand Releasing; and Paul Hudson, CEO of Outsider Pictures, who have collectively brought hundreds of international films to American audiences.

The challenges extend well beyond French cinema. With fewer distributors acquiring independent films and arthouse theaters increasingly turning to mainstream Hollywood fare, even critically acclaimed movies are struggling to secure meaningful theatrical runs.

The definition of a French-language box office hit has also changed dramatically along with the health of the special market for most foreign-language films. Jean-Pierre Jeunet’s cult film “Amélie” grossed approximately $33 million domestically a quarter-century ago. By comparison, Justine Triet’s Palme d’Or winner “Anatomy of a Fall,” one of the most successful French films of the last five years which was handled by Neon and won an Oscar, earned around $5 million in the U.S..

The most accessible French blockbusters aren’t necessarily easier to market in the U.S. Samuel Goldwyn, whose company released Pathé’s two-part “The Three Musketeers” and “The Count of Monte Cristo,” and recently acquired Antonin Baudry’s historical epic saga “De Gaulle,” told Variety in a separate interview following the panel that these films have far broader commercial potential than their limited theatrical exposure suggests.

“The only thing that makes it an arthouse movie is because it’s in a foreign language,” Goldwyn said of “De Gaulle,” adding that it could easily play on 1,000 screens if it were in English. He’s still managed to roll out the first opus of the WWII saga into theaters this weekend across key U.S. markets simultaneously with transactional VOD.

Yet while the pool of U.S. buyers has narrowed, competition remains fierce for the most coveted titles. Deep-pocketed players including Neon, Netflix, Mubi, A24 and Sony Pictures Classics continue to chase high-profile international films, particularly those from established auteurs. And even though Sony Pictures Classics, historically one of the most prolific U.S. buyers of French cinema, has become more selective, it’s still on the lookout for certain titles. The company just bought “Violette,” Jeunet’s latest film, and Louis Clichy’s animated feature “Iron Boy,” which won a prize at Cannes’ Un Certain Regard and was shortlisted as France’s Oscar submission alongside “De Gaulle.” The distributor also scored a solid theatrical run with Rebecca Zlotowski’s “A Private Life,” starring Jodie Foster, earlier this year.

But for smaller distributors, competing for films with the greatest commercial potential is often a losing game.

“We all see the same thing. So usually you go, boy, I could sell that. There’s something in here that I know I can sell. It’s got great reviews. It was in competition in Cannes or Berlin. And then Netflix buys it,” says Gerrans.

In any case, securing screens can prove even harder than winning a bidding war. Arthouse theaters that once championed foreign-language cinema are increasingly booking Hollywood releases, while others have shuttered altogether.

“We have to struggle to get into those theaters that used to be” devoted to arthouse films, Aronson said. Cohen Media Group owns the Landmark Theatres circuit, but that doesn’t guarantee bookings for its international releases. “They keep their own books and they have their own agenda and they need to be profitable,” said Aronson, whose recent acquisitions include László Nemes’ Cannes competition period drama “Moulin” and Géraldine Nakache’s “Think Good,” which played in Un Certain Regard.

The company has also picked up Marc Fitoussi’s “All About Corinne,” starring Isabelle Huppert as a struggling film extra who dreams of becoming an actress and befriends a movie star played by Sandrine Kiberlain. Aronson, who compared the comedy’s tone to the French hit series “Call My Agent” (which was partly directed by Fitoussi), said star power remains a crucial selling point.

Strand has acquired Ulrike Ottinger’s experimental German-language vampire movie “The Blood Countess,” also starring Huppert, and Gerrans said her involvement has made a significant difference.

“We’re able to book it because of her, and we’re getting a lot of festivals because of her,” Gerrans said. “So that one’s sort of booking itself, and that doesn’t happen too often.”

For less recognizable titles, the outlook is considerably tougher. Gerrans admitted that he sometimes consults exhibitors before committing to acquisitions, particularly when sales agents require a theatrical release.

“A lot of times we hold off and we ask some of the exhibitors, if I acquire this, will you play this film?” said Gerrans, whose upcoming French releases include “Flesh and Fuel” and “Daughters of Abraham.”

He recalled pitching a film he loved to a New York exhibitor, only to be turned down. “It’s a fantastic film. You’ll love it. I think the audience will love it. They just go, that storyline seems a little too small.”

Hudson, whose recent acquisitions include Cannes Un Certain Regard title “Congo Boy,” which earned Bradley Fiomona Dembeasset the best actor prize, described a similarly brutal scramble for screens.

“We’re all trying to get the IFC Film Center or the Quad or the Angelika to book the film in New York, and there’s, I don’t know, 45 of us with all eight films trying to get them into these” theaters, Hudson joked.

With major New York arthouse venues often booked months in advance, distributors sometimes resort to renting screens just to secure a theatrical launch.

And competition isn’t coming only from Hollywood blockbusters. Repertory screenings, television series playing in multiplexes and special events are also taking up valuable screen space.

“I understand arthouse theaters playing reissues,” Hudson said. “And young audiences rediscovering ‘Raise the Red Lantern.’ Amazing. But what about the new films? What about the new filmmakers? Where are the new ones going to get space to find room in the marketplace with all of this other stuff?”

The squeeze extends beyond theaters. With DVD revenues largely gone, TV sales collapsing and streaming licenses losing value, independent distributors have fewer ways to recoup their investments.

“We have a really hard time getting our films on the streaming platforms,” Aronson said. “Things have changed a lot in that market because they’re not that interested anymore in kind of single U.S.-only, after-theatrical streaming deals with independent distributors.”

Hudson was particularly scathing about Amazon Prime’s declining royalty rates. “They were paying 10 cents per hour viewed, which is awful. But then they cut the rate down to 1 cent per hour viewed. Then they cut the rate down to half a cent per hour viewed.”

He said he and other independent distributors have pulled titles from Amazon Prime and are looking to alternative outlets including Tubi, Roku, Pluto and YouTube.

Strand operates its own subscription streaming channel through Amazon, but Gerrans said “they could decide tomorrow to turn it off.”

With traditional distribution avenues under pressure, festivals are becoming an increasingly important way for films to reach audiences.

“Film festivals are kind of like another tier of distribution, another level of distribution,” Aronson said. “It’s a way most independent films get seen by anyone.”

Distributors are also looking to younger moviegoers, particularly through Letterboxd, which has built a passionate community of Gen Z cinephiles. But reaching that demographic without substantial marketing budgets remains a challenge.

“Letterboxd is a real opportunity for us to find a younger audience,” Hudson said, describing how a friend’s son has helped him navigate the platform and build a community around his films.

Yet enthusiasm for cinema doesn’t necessarily translate into ticket sales for contemporary French films.

“I can’t even get my teenage kids to go see my movies,” Gerrans said, citing Strand’s release of Agathe Riedinger’s “Wild Diamond,” about a 17-year-old aspiring reality television star. Despite a premise seemingly tailored to younger audiences, the film failed to take off.

Still, distributors are experimenting with ways to build word of mouth around films that might otherwise struggle to find an audience.

Gerrans is optimistic about “Flesh and Fuel,” which he described as “the gay trucker movie you’ve never seen before,” betting that its uplifting ending will resonate with moviegoers.

Hudson, meanwhile, plans an extended theatrical rollout for “Congo Boy,” stretching from Black History Month through Juneteenth, even if bookings remain scarce.

“I can do five months because nobody’s clamoring for this movie to go onto a subscription service. But I may only get seven bookings.”

French institutions including the CNC, Unifrance and Villa Albertine are also helping distributors promote French cinema through festivals, promotional events and talent travel.

“All that helps to generate awareness,” Hudson said. “We have no support from the government. I mean, everything we do is from our own pockets. Film’s commerce here, it’s not culture.”

The discussion was one of several panels bringing together French and American industry figures at the American French Film Festival, which is celebrating its 30th anniversary this year with a lineup of 60 films and series. The festival’s industry program also featured American filmmaker Randal Kleiser and French director Pierre Salvadori discussing artificial intelligence, as well as a panel moderated by Writers Guild of America West member Yvette Foy with French screenwriters Coralie Amedeo, Fanny Burdino, Abdel Raouf Dafri and Thomas Finkielkraut. The French delegation also included directors Baudry (“De Gaulle”), Laïla Marrakchi (“Strawberries”), Fitoussi (“All About Corinne”), Lukas Dhont (“Coward”) and actor-director Raphaël Quenard (“Le rêve américain”), among others.

Warriors rookie Yaxel Lendeborg surprises mom with new house in amazing moment

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Golden State Warriors rookie Yaxel Lendeborg has always been vocal about the love for his mother, Yissel Raposo. Together, they shed tears of joy when his name was called at the No. 11 pick in the 2026 NBA Draft to the Warriors in June.

Lendeborg has been vocal about the sacrifices that his mother made for him and one of his goals was to pay her back. He debuted a new YouTube channel showcasing "behind-the-scenes, life outside of basketball and everything in between" where a video released on Friday, Nov. 6 had a surprise reveal of a recently purchased house for his mother.

"On behalf of everybody that came to show up, welcome home," Lendeborg said to Raposo, moving her from New Jersey to California.

Raposo was speechless as happy tears trickled down her face and she took the keys out of a box Lendeborg handed her and she opened the door as they took a tour of her new home.

Lendeborg said it took him 2 to 3 months to finalize the house. To keep his mom from finding out, he and his sister would often disguise phone conversations around visiting Hawaii; instead, they were talking about how to get their mom to her new house without suspicion.

"There is no gesture big enough to properly say thank you for all the love, sacrifice, and support my mom has poured into me but this moment was pretty special and one I'll never forget," Lendeborg said in his video description. "She deserves it all. For me, it's bigger than basketball. It's about making sure the people who believed in me get to enjoy the blessings too."

Lendeborg has always discussed the bond that he and his mother share and how she's directly responsible for his achievements in basketball and finding early success in the NBA.

"I got here because of her," Lendeborg said in June after getting drafted. "She pushed a dream, forced me to go out there and step into the world and become a man. I had no choice but to go out there and do the best I could. So I vowed every day to be a better man, be a better person and just do the best I can for her."

Sharing the moments of success with his mom means everything to Lendeborg because of the sacrifices that she made for him and his sisters, having to work multiple jobs to ensure they have what they need. Raposo also battled Stage 4 appendix cancer, another thing that fuels Lendeborg.

"She put her whole life on hold for my life and I'm super proud of her for fighting so hard and I'm glad we get to share this moment together," he said in a June interview after being drafted.

Lendeborg has been a bright spot for the Warriors on the court, who are on a six-game winning streak as they face the Orlando Magic in a 5:30 p.m. PT (8:30 p.m. ET) tip-off on Saturday, Nov. 7 at Chase Center in San Francisco, California.

The rookie averages 21.0 points, 7.1 rebounds, and 4.1 assists per game through his first 9 games of the 2026–27 NBA season.

Echoes of Mirror's Edge, Dishonored and a dream set in Italy I once had can be found in Boars in Rome, an expressive parkour platformer set in dystopian Rome

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Years ago, I had this dream where I was parkouring through a greatly sunken version of Venice, no doubt sourced from the many hours I put into Assassin's Creed 2 as a teen who should have been studying for their GCSEs a lot more than they were. It's stuck with me after all this time mostly because of how much I wanted to capture that feeling again somehow, as the real Assassin's Creed didn't quite cut it. And lo and behold, there I was today browsing Bluesky when I came across Boars in Rome, a Mirror's Edge and Counter Strike Surf inspired parkour platformer set in a dystopian version of Rome.

Truthfully, witnessing the clip I've shared below was baffling to me. "This is what I dreamt of!" More or less, anyway, for the setting isn't quite the same; "You have been selected to start a new life off planet. Earth has been abandoned," explains the game's Steam page. "Machines keep working and nature claims what remains. Find out what happened while you were frozen in time by exploring the remnants of Rome where historical monuments mix with futurist megastructures."

Mirrors Edge x Counter Strike Surf where you parkour on anything! Be smooth n stylish in dystopian Rome. Or just jump around and explore, whatever works for you. It's called Boars in Rome! #PitchYaGame[image or embed]

Not all of it matches that dream of mine, some of it leans much more into brutalism than the typical, much older architecture you'd expect of Rome, but it's there nonetheless. In motion, Boars in Rome feels quite oneiric too. There is so much more control over the game's avatar than what my real body is capable of, or wants to do. You can run, jump, slide and wallrun, positioning it quite closely to VHOLUME mechanically. Where it differs is in its additives, movement tech like being able to swing on lampposts or vault over low down obstacles.

VHOLUME is more of a game about providing you with a simple toolkit and leaving you to push it to its maximum. Boars in Roam is a touch more expressive, and I'd dare to even call it an immersive sim. The levels you can romp around are linear, you have an end point to reach, only the journey is up to you. It certainly feels appropriate to put it in the im sim camp given that, at least the game's first level, calls to mind Viktor Antonov's work on Dishonored (and Half-Life 2, too, honestly).

As it currently stands, there's no release date for Boars in Rome, but there is a playtest you can sign up and gain immediate access to if you fancy a go yourself; you can do so on Steam right here, or at least pop it a wishlist.

Popular Dairy Alternatives Could Be Leaving You Short of Essential Nutrients

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Replacing even a modest amount of dairy with plant-based alternatives could significantly reduce essential nutrient intake, according to a new study from the University of Surrey. Researchers found that substituting just 20% to 35% of dairy products increased the estimated prevalence of inadequate iodine intake among adolescent girls from 54% to 65%. Among women ages 19 to 49, the figure rose from 32% to 42%.

The study also identified reductions in calcium, riboflavin, and vitamin B12 intake. However, researchers found that fortifying plant-based alternatives and other foods could help prevent these nutritional shortfalls.

In the first UK study of its kind, researchers from the University of Surrey and Queen’s University Belfast analyzed dietary data from more than 3,500 people, ages 18 months to 100 years. Using the National Diet and Nutrition Survey, they modeled how replacing milk, yogurt, and cheese with commercially available plant-based alternatives would affect intake of four essential micronutrients.

Dr Katie Nicol, a research fellow at the University of Surrey and co-author of the study, explained:

“With the growing need to address environmental sustainability, many dietary recommendations are focused on plant-based diets and the reduced intake of animal products.”

“With the rise in plant-based alternative foods, we need to know whether they are like-for-like replacements for the micronutrients that are found in dairy products. This is particularly important for vulnerable groups, including young children and adolescent females, who are more likely to be deficient in certain micronutrients, which could affect their overall health.”

Iodine was the nutrient most affected by dairy replacement. When researchers modeled completely replacing dairy with plant-based alternatives under current fortification levels, the estimated prevalence of inadequate iodine intake reached 80% among adolescent girls and 72% among adolescent boys. These figures reflect modeled dietary inadequacy, not confirmed clinical deficiencies.

Iodine is essential for producing thyroid hormones, which regulate metabolism and support growth and brain development. It is particularly important before and during pregnancy, when iodine requirements increase.

Dr Sarah Bath, associate professor at the University of Surrey and lead author, said:

“The UK is particularly vulnerable to iodine insufficiency due to a lack of an iodine fortification policy such as universal salt iodization, which many other countries have.”

“People may also be unaware of the importance of iodine, which is needed to make thyroid hormones to regulate our metabolism, support healthy growth, and brain development. Iodine is particularly important during pregnancy, when the requirement increases, but it is also essential prior to pregnancy to ensure that stores are optimized. People are also unaware that dairy products are a major source of iodine, particularly in the UK, and that plant-based alternatives, unless fortified, have a very low iodine content.”

The researchers found that mandatory fortification of plant-based dairy alternatives could prevent declines in micronutrient intake. However, even with fortified alternatives, an estimated 59% of adolescent girls would still have inadequate iodine intake. Adding iodine to bread reduced that figure to 37%, suggesting that combining both fortification strategies could be more effective.

Dr Nicol emphasized the importance of aligning nutrition policies with environmental goals:

“Nutrition policy must evolve in tandem with environmental targets; however, it is important that shifts towards more sustainable diets do not compromise on public health. Fortification of foods is one way that this could be achieved; however, government policy is needed to make this happen.”

Dr Anne Nugent, from Queen’s University Belfast, described how the team identified dairy-containing foods in the UK National Diet and Nutrition Survey and modeled replacing them with equal amounts of plant-based alternatives. The researchers accounted for differences in the nutritional content of available products and tested whether fortification could prevent deficiencies without causing excessive nutrient intake.

“During the analysis, we first identified all foods and recipes containing dairy produce (milk, cheese, and yogurt) in the national food survey for the UK, the National Diet and Nutrition Survey. Next, we conducted a series of replacement scenarios whereby we substituted the dairy present with an equal weight of plant-based dairy alternatives. As the nutrient composition of dairy alternatives available on our supermarket shelves can vary, we also applied a condition to make sure that every dairy alternative we had identified in our previous market survey had an equal chance of selection. The last step was to examine the impact on dietary intakes of key nutrients following either the reduction or removal of dairy from the diet, replacing it with the dairy alternatives as above, and asking whether a series of fortification options could mitigate against any shortfalls in intake while avoid situations of excess.”

Reference: “The impact of replacing dairy products with plant-based dairy alternatives on iodine and other micronutrients: a modelling study and evaluation of policy options to support population intake” by Katie Nicol, Anne P Nugent, Jayne V Woodside, Kathryn H Hart and Sarah C Bath, , The Lancet Planetary Health. There are several reports that Sassuolo are ready to fire Alberto Aquilani after five consecutive defeats. ‘I have to be judged on results and I have to question myself,’ said the coach.

Aquilani was appointed over the summer to replace Fabio Grosso and had also been in negotiations with Torino before agreeing terms with the Neroverdi.

“I haven’t spoken to them yet. I always felt their faith, but obviously I have to be judged on results, and I have to question myself. Many things have to change.”

In-N-Out’s billionaire owner vows iconic chain ‘will always be’ in California and says leaving the state would be the ‘dumbest business decision ever’

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Lynsi Snyder, president and owner of In-N-Out, moved her family to Tennessee recently but reaffirmed that the iconic burger chain will never abandon California.

In an interview with actor Terry Crews that In-N-Out posted online, she was asked how the company will retain its California DNA as it expands to more states around the country.

“This is where we started. It’s where we are now, and it’s where we will always be,” Snyder replied.

Her grandparents opened the first In-N-Out in Southern California in 1948, and the company has gradually expanded to other parts of the state—and to other parts of the country.

While far from being the biggest chain, it retains a cult following that has helped its expansion as people who moved out of California make pilgrimages when restaurants open in new states.

But Snyder, who grew up in Northern California, revealed last year that she and her family were moving to Tennessee, saying that raising kids was difficult in the state and that “doing business is not easy” there either.

That sparked fears In-N-Out might leave California, prompting the burger heiress—who is worth an estimated $7.3 billion—to clarify that her departure from the state doesn’t mean the company will too.

In a video posted to her Instagram page last year, Snyder told followers: “We’re not leaving California, or leaving our roots behind. Each one of our locations is here to stay.”

Still, there has been a corporate exodus from California to states like Texas, where Elon Musk has relocated the headquarters for Tesla and SpaceX.

In-N-Out has also clashed with state authorities, such as when it refused to comply with officials and request that customers must show proof of vaccination to make purchases during the pandemic.

And the company reluctantly raised prices after California imposed higher minimum wages for fast-food workers in 2024.

But during her latest interview, Snyder addressed the fears about In-N-Out, which maintains headquarters in California and Tennessee.

“There were rumors about ‘they’re gonna close all their California stores.’ I’m like what where did this even come from? That’s unimaginable. I can’t even imagine that — dumbest business decision ever,” she said.

Snyder added that In-N-Out continues to grow in California, with “lots of stores” slotted for the other cities in the state.

At the same time, the company has expanded to Nevada, Arizona, Utah, Texas, Oregon, Colorado, Idaho, Washington and Tennessee. Snyder suggested that Mississippi, Arkansas, and Oklahoma are under consideration, with New Mexico also in the works.

Although he only appears about once per season, his episodes serve as proof of his immense talent. The Bear has come to a close, with the final season wrapping up this past June, but it's still worth a binge to catch every awe-inspiring minute of Bernthal's performance.

NASA Awards Moon Base Transportation, Technology, Science Projects

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

NASA announced a suite of awards and mission updates on Saturday to expand the agency’s Moon Base exploration, science, and infrastructure capabilities. The investments will advance commercial robotic deliveries, a nuclear-powered rover named PROMISE, launch and orbital transfer services, and surface infrastructure as the agency works with American industry to establish a sustained presence on the Moon’s South Pole.

The agency shared the news at the MAX POWER aerospace expo and air show at NASA’s Kennedy Space Center in Florida.

“We are building the Moon Base mission by mission,” said NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman. “Today’s announcements expand nearly every part of that campaign, using the playbook that carried NASA through its greatest eras: fly often, learn, apply those lessons, and get more better and more capable with every mission. When our astronauts return to the Moon, they will arrive with the first elements of the Moon Base already waiting for them on the surface.”

“In the eight months since Ignition, we’ve made incredible strides in accomplishing the near impossible: establishing a permanent outpost on the lunar surface,” said Carlos García-Galán, manager, Moon Base Program. “We’ve consolidated numerous efforts into a streamlined program, evolving our human exploration architecture into an iterative and sustained campaign for Moon Base technology development and implementation. Most importantly, we’ve taken action, awarding new contracts and forging new partnerships to accelerate development.”

To strengthen the cargo lander network needed to support the Moon Base, NASA selected eight companies for the next phase of its CLPS (Commercial Lunar Payload Services) initiative. The companies are eligible to compete for future lunar surface delivery missions under the new CLPS 2 contract:

Under the CLPS 2 framework, the companies will provide lunar delivery services, including managing, building, and operating landers that transport NASA science, technology, and infrastructure payloads to the Moon.

The CLPS 2 framework is a 10-year, multiple-award, indefinite-delivery/indefinite-quantity master contract with a combined maximum value of $10 billion. NASA increased the value of the current CLPS 1.0 contract from $2.6 billion to $4.2 billion to support initial Phase One Moon Base objectives. The original CLPS contract will remain in place through November 2028, with an additional five-year period to complete awarded lunar deliveries through November 2033.

NASA also announced it is moving forward with development of PROMISE (Polar Rover for Observation, Mapping, and In-Situ Exploration). Carrying science instruments and technology demonstrations, the six-wheeled rover will land at the Moon’s South Pole to characterize the surface and subsurface, assess resource potential, and provide data to enable landing site selection, as well as future infrastructure and exploration activities. The nuclear-powered rover can operate during long lunar nights and within permanently shadowed regions thought to harbor ice, which is crucial for a sustained human presence on the Moon.

The agency’s Jet Propulsion Laboratory in Southern California, a division of Caltech, will manage the mission and build the rover from a combination of spare flight components and engineering test models for NASA’s Curiosity and Perseverance Mars rovers. Using proven hardware, software, and Mars rover operation approaches significantly reduces cost and enables integration and testing of the complex PROMISE rover on a shorter timeline. NASA has assessed the compatibility of the hardware with the lunar environment and determined it can tolerate the harsh conditions found near the Moon’s South Pole.

The PROMISE rover will carry a drill to extract regolith, or lunar dust, to characterize ice and other resources from approximately 3 feet (1 meter) below the surface, spectrometers for analyzing the chemical composition of surface material, temperature and radiation sensors, and visible-light cameras. It will demonstrate a next-generation, radiation-tolerant computing system, NASA’s High Performance Spaceflight Computing, and advanced autonomy sensors and algorithms. NASA is targeting late 2028 to launch PROMISE and planning an 18-month mission for the rover.

NASA awarded Blue Origin a $94 million base contract, with an option period worth an additional $140 million based on performance to deliver PROMISE to the lunar South Pole. The award expands the task order awarded in May 2026 to deliver two lunar terrain vehicles to the Moon. Blue Origin will use its Mark 1 lander to deliver and deploy the rover as part of the Moon Base’s first phase.

Delivering Moon Base infrastructure will require a robust transportation network between Earth and the Moon. Orbital Transfer Vehicles (OTVs) act like long-distance freight trucks, carrying large loads across space. Lunar landers handle the final delivery, bringing supplies, equipment, and infrastructure safely down to the Moon’s surface.

To demonstrate those capabilities, NASA awarded six task orders as part of the agency’s Venture-Class Acquisition of Dedicated and Rideshare (VADR) launch services contract. The awards were given to Arrow Science and Technology, Blue Origin, and Impulse Space to integrate and transport Moon Base cargo.

Three mission types will allow NASA flexibility to fly science and technology demonstrations when they are ready.

The first will include missions to reduce risks of future long-term human spaceflight for lunar exploration. The missions will verify that communication and navigation methods work properly during the journey from Earth orbit to the Moon, and OTV capability to support delivery of a CLPS lunar lander to a desired orbit. This mission type will deliver the CAPSTONE 02 spacecraft to deep space to test proximity maneuvers, navigation, and communications around the Moon.

The second category of missions will prepare the lunar South Pole for human exploration by sending a group of highly mobile drones to survey the surface before astronauts arrive, enabling safer, more informed human missions while demonstrating advanced robotic capabilities. Such missions will include the agency’s MoonFall drones, designed to scout areas beyond the reach of traditional rovers, as well as delivery of additional Moon Base assets necessary to support Phase One objectives.

To reduce risk for future missions, the third mission type will test navigation technologies and demonstrate operations in multiple cislunar orbits, between Earth and the Moon. This mission type will demonstrate commercial orbital transfer capabilities intended to increase the amount of cargo that future landers can deliver to the lunar surface.

The task orders have a value of approximately $1.3 billion combined and fall under the 10-year VADR indefinite-delivery/indefinite-quantity contract, which has a maximum total value of $5 billion across all contracts. The VADR contracts are managed by NASA’s Launch Services Program Office at the agency’s Kennedy Space Center as part of the agency’s Commercial Access to Space Program. By procuring multiple launch and in-space transportation services together, NASA provides industry with a more predictable set of mission opportunities while gaining access to a broader range of commercial capabilities for future lunar exploration.

Some missions will launch aboard Blue Origin’s New Glenn, Relativity Space’s Terran R, and SpaceX’s Falcon 9 rockets. In-space transportation spacecraft for these missions will be provided by Blue Origin, Firefly Aerospace, Impulse Space, and Quantum Space.

Complementing those in-space transportation demonstrations, NASA selected SpaceX for the Cislunar Communications Deployment Demonstration under an existing lunar logistics contract. Through a Specialized Delivery Mission task order, SpaceX will launch communications satellites aboard a Starship to cislunar space in 2028. The demonstration will help inform the development of future lunar communications networks that keep astronauts, mission controllers, and the Moon Base connected.

To extend infrastructure capabilities on the lunar surface, NASA plans to purchase commercial components for a Mini-Communications and Solar Tower (miniCAST), including solar arrays, batteries, and wireless power-transfer hardware. The agency awarded Honeybee Robotics, a Blue Origin company, for energy storage capability, Redwire for power generation, and Yank Technologies, Inc., for wireless power transfer in support of miniCAST.

The miniCAST will combine power and communications capabilities to support future Moon Base activities. Engineers at NASA’s Marshall Space Flight Center in Huntsville, Alabama, will assemble and test the systems before they are delivered to the lunar surface.

NASA expects work to begin within months, and the initial effort will involve components rather than a major integrated system. The agency also plans a separate solicitation for fully integrated power systems, which is anticipated in early 2027.

NASA intends to release a directed call under its Next Space Technologies for Exploration Partnerships, or NextSTEP-3 B, solicitation to help close architecture-driven, high-priority data gaps for Moon Base development. The effort will help NASA gather information it needs to plan future missions, reduce risk, and support technologies and infrastructure for sustained operations on the Moon’s South Pole. The work could include small spacecraft missions, payloads on future Moon Base flights, or the purchase of data collected by commercial partners.

As part of a Golden Age of innovation and exploration, NASA will send Artemis astronauts on increasingly difficult missions to the lunar South Pole to explore more of the Moon than ever before. In addition to providing economic benefits and advancing scientific discovery through its Moon missions, NASA will establish an enduring human presence on the lunar surface and build on this foundation for the first crewed mission to Mars.

The 6 Best Thriller Shows Released Since 2010

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Sir Gary Oldman in "Slow Horses," now streaming on Apple TV.

Based on the types of television shows still being produced, the thriller remains near the top. Encompassing a large spectrum of subgenres, the thriller provides audiences the chance to get their heart pumping from the safety of their own home. With network television and streaming platforms taking bigger and bolder risks, the thriller has been getting better and better. Since 2010, we’ve seen a rise in high-quality thrillers, and these six titles are proof.

From a masterpiece anthology series based on a ‘90s classic to a crime thriller lifted from an iconic novel series, these six shows got adrenaline rushing as people kept talking. While a plethora of other incredible thrillers have come out over the last decade and a half, they don’t hold a candle to these.

Who says comedies can’t be thrilling? In the mystery comedy hit Only Murders in the Building, three eccentric neighbors, Charles-Haden Savage (Steve Martin), Oliver Putnam (Martin Short), and Mabel Mora (Selena Gomez), live in their upscale New York City apartment building, the Arconia, and share an intense obsession with true crime podcasts. When a suspicious, gruesome death occurs inside their building, the trio teams up to investigate the truth using their knowledge of the genre, recording their own podcast to document the case. As they untangle a web of secrets and mounting clues, they quickly realize a ruthless killer might be living right among them. A cozy murder mystery series created by Martin and John Hoffman, Only Murders in the Building redefined the modern murder-mystery genre.

Instead of a grim, linear police procedural, the series pokes fun at and analyzes society's obsession with true-crime podcasts while still delivering layered, twist-filled whodunits. A genuine masterclass in performance, the legendary comedy duo elevate their costar to create a unique, multi-generational dynamic that grounds high-stakes apartment-building murders in genuine warmth and character-driven humor. The show has become a destination for some of Hollywood’s biggest names to join in the fun, including Meryl Streep, Paul Rudd, Tina Fey, Tea Leoni, Renée Zellweger, and many more. For the next season, they’ll be welcoming some of Britain’s best, including Olivia Colman, David Tennant, Nicola Coughlan, Jodie Whittaker, and Martin Freeman, among others. A brilliant escapist thriller, Only Murders in the Building is entertainment at its finest.

The Breaking Bad blueprint has been attempted to be replicated often since its final season. Very few shows found success. Ozark was one. Created by Bill Dubuque and Mark Williams, the four-season series follows Marty Byrde (Jason Bateman), a financial planner who is forced to move his family from Chicago to the Missouri Ozarks after a money-laundering scheme for a Mexican drug cartel goes dangerously wrong. To keep his family alive, Marty must wash $500 million in five years, dragging his ambitious wife Wendy and their children deeper into a dark world of local criminals, corporate corruption, and ruthless drug lords. As the family adapts to their new criminal reality, they evolve from desperate survivors into a formidable and calculating political powerhouse. Ozark became one of the defining crime thrillers of the 21st century due to its relentless tension, strong acting, and unique atmosphere.

Like Breaking Bad, Ozark strips away filler episodes and continually ramps up the stakes for Marty and Wendy Byrde (Laura Linney), as they navigate Mexican drug cartels, the FBI, and local criminals. Ozark uses the grim, blue-tinted, and menacing lakefront setting of the Missouri Ozarks to amplify its suffocating dread. The writing regularly defies traditional crime-genre tropes, refusing to give characters easy moral redemptions or predictable fates. The series boasted some sensational performances, perhaps none better than Julia Garner as Ruth Langmore. The range and depth she gave the morally troubled young woman became the heartbeat of the series. While some fans felt the series stumbled toward the end, Ozark remains one of Netflix’s greatest products. ​​​​​​​

Four criminal masterminds. One of them was always going to be yours. Here is who comes for you, why they picked you out of everyone available, and what they want from the encounter.

Your nemesis does not want to beat you. He wants to know you completely, and he is patient enough to wait for it. The frightening part is how good the conversation is.

Your nemesis is not after money, territory or revenge. He is after a demonstration, and you are the person he has chosen to prove his point on.

Your nemesis respects you, which sounds flattering until you work out what it means. He chose you because nobody else was worth his time.

Your nemesis has no personal interest in you whatsoever, and that is precisely what makes him unsurvivable. You are not a rival. You are a stop on the way.

Perhaps the very best spy thriller ever made, The Americans subverted traditional spy tropes. Created by Joe Weisberg, the series The Americans follows Philip and Elizabeth Jennings (Matthew Rhys and Keri Russell), two deep-cover Soviet KGB officers posing as a typical American married couple in the suburbs of Washington, D.C. While raising their unsuspecting children and managing the psychological complexities of their arranged relationship, they carry out high-stakes espionage right under the nose of their neighbor, an FBI counterintelligence agent. The Americans anchors high-stakes international espionage in the grueling, realistic intimacy of marriage, creating an agonizing, slow-burning tension that is deeply psychological. Running for six sensational seasons, The Americans features one of the most meticulously written, flawless final seasons and series finales in television history.

At its core, the show uses the extreme parameters of being KGB sleeper agents—lying, identity theft, seduction, and assassination—as a mirror for the complexities of a long-term relationship. Starting with an arranged marriage, it slowly morphs into genuine affection and love. The real suspense comes not just from whether they get caught by their FBI-agent neighbor, Stan Beeman (Noah Emmerich), but from whether their relationship can survive shifting ideological weight, mutual betrayals, and the profound psychological toll of their double lives. The Americans treats violence with grounding, bleak realism. Actions have permanent consequences; disposing of a body is a messy, bone-breaking chore, and close-quarters combat is exhausting and brutal. The show never lets the audience forget the immense physical and emotional cost of the characters' choices. The series pulls off the narrative magic trick of making Western audiences deeply empathize with Soviet operatives whose literal directive is to destabilize the United States. It’s all thanks to the sensational performances at top billing.​​​​​​​

One of the greatest films of the ‘90s is Fargo. Two decades later, it served as the basis for a brilliant anthology series, using the same DNA. Created by Noah Hawley, the five-season series set in the frozen Midwest features a different self-contained story that follows a different cast of ordinary citizens, ruthless criminals, and law enforcement officers as seemingly minor choices spiral into chaotic violence. With its unique blend of pulse-pounding suspense, dark humor, and philosophical depth, Fargo became a thematic masterpiece where the niceties of the North were the perfect juxtaposition for an examination of the nature of evil, systemic greed, luck, and the slippery definition of "a true story".

Fargo masterfully walks a tightrope between grim, white-knuckle neo-noir violence and absurd Midwestern dark comedy. The mundane politeness of small-town life sharply contrasts with chaotic, high-stakes criminality. Rather than serving up cheap thrills, Hawley uses the Coen brothers' tonal legacy to dig into the core themes: the chaotic intersection of ordinary life and brutal crime. The show consistently contrasts absolute, often personified malice against ordinary, kind-hearted Midwestern law enforcement officers trying to maintain a baseline of human decency. Morality tales are deeply steeped in black comedy and cosmic irony, highlighting how random coincidence or sheer human stupidity can dictate life-or-death consequences. By resetting the cast, setting, and decade each season, ranging from 1950s Kansas City to 2019 Minnesota, the show avoids the narrative fatigue that traps long-running serialized crime dramas. That means a top-tier revolving door of stars had fun in the freezing, including Billy Bob Thornton, Martin Freeman, Kirsten Dunst, Jesse Plemons, Carrie Coon, Ewan McGregor, Chris Rock, Jessie Buckley, Juno Temple, and Jon Hamm, among others. The show had five fabulous seasons, but die-hard fans would die for more! ​​​​​​​

Though it is the antithesis of polished spycraft like James Bond, Slow Horses is an espionage thriller at its finest. Based on Mick Herron’s novel series, the Will Smith-created series is a darkly comedic thriller that follows a group of disgraced British intelligence agents banished to a dumping-ground department called Slough House after career-ending mistakes. Under the leadership of their brilliant but notoriously abrasive and unkempt boss, Jackson Lamb (Gary Oldman), they are expected to endure dull paperwork but repeatedly find themselves entangled in dangerous conspiracies that threaten national security. Despite their "loser" status, the team must use their flawed skills to outmaneuver the mainstream MI5 operatives and save the day. Slow Horses subverts the exhausting, hyper-slick tropes of modern espionage in favor of working-class competency, pitch-black humor, and flawless pacing. Slow Horses has perfected a formula that keeps it operating at the absolute peak of the television landscape.

Unlike the untouchable spy thrillers of the era, Slow Horses platforms exiled duds and burnouts. Yet, the show avoids making them bumbling caricatures. They are deeply broken people dealing with real-world issues like addiction, divorce, and professional trauma, but when backed into a corner, their underlying spy training kicks in. Led by Oldman, Slow Horses is a tried-and-true ensemble series where each character has their own distinct identity. You love them for their flaws. Slow Horses masterfully balances pulse-pounding, high-stakes danger with laugh-out-loud workplace dark comedy. It treats the violence, institutional rot, and geopolitical threats with severe gravity, but lets the mundane reality of administrative purgatory provide a grounded, human contrast. Slow Horses operates with old-school efficiency. Perhaps the most genius decision the series has made is keeping each season at a tightly plotted six episodes. There are zero filler episodes, zero unnecessary flashbacks, and no narrative treading water. Each season, with one exception, adapts a single, self-contained book from Herron’s series, establishing a clear beginning, middle, and explosive end.

If there’s one crime thriller we wished wasn’t a one-and-done limited series wonder, it’s Mare of Easttown. Created by Brad Ingelsby, the gritty drama follows world-weary detective Mare Sheehan (Kate Winslet) as she investigates the brutal murder of a local teenage mother in a small, close-knit Pennsylvania town. As the investigation uncovers dark, long-hidden community secrets, she simultaneously struggles to keep her own personal life from shattering under the weight of divorce, custody battles, and the unresolved trauma of her son's suicide. A powerful story that moves beyond typical police procedural, Mare of Easttown subverts the traditional "whodunit" tropes by prioritizing devastating character realism over sensationalized twists. The series functions as a richly authentic study of grief, generational trauma, and community decay.

Unlike slick, stylized Hollywood thrillers, Mare of Easttown grounds its mystery in the harsh realities of the modern American Rust Belt, dealing heavily with the fallout of the opioid epidemic, poverty, and systemic despair. The series drops audiences into the lived-in DelCo part of Pennsylvania, ripe with pitch-perfect accents and localized texture. The show treats its setting with immense respect and gritty accuracy. It captures the claustrophobia of a small town where everyone is interconnected, everyone shares the same history, and every suspect is someone's cousin, childhood friend, or ex-lover. Winslet is at her finest, shedding her movie-star aesthetic to play a flawed, vape-smoking, unglamorous, exhausted, grieving mother and grandmother who happens to be a detective. Joined by a top-tier ensemble, every character feels like real people you'd meet in a struggling Pennsylvania town. A smartly plotted and tightly presented masterpiece, Mare of Easttown is a devastating, emotionally logical thriller.

Man United sink to new 40-year low after Nottingham Forest beat Man City

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

By Ben Knapton | 07 Nov 2026 22:41

Manchester United have sunk to a new 40-year low thanks to Nottingham Forest's shock victory over Manchester City on Saturday evening.

Ahead of the start of gameweek 10, the Red Devils were just about keeping their heads above water in the Premier League table, sitting outside the relegation zone on goal difference.

Michael Carrick's men led Crystal Palace by that metric, while sitting two points clear of rock-bottom Sunderland and one better off than Forest after nine rounds.

The Tricky Trees were unsurprisingly tipped to suffer defeat to Enzo Maresca's men at the City Ground, but in an unexpected turn of events, Forest claimed a 3-1 triumph on home soil.

Jair Cunha and Liam Delap propelled the Garibaldi into a 2-0 lead before Rayan Cherki pulled one back, but the Frenchman's response was futile as Delap struck his second of the game in the second half.

Forest's win propelled Oliver Glasner's men out of the Premier League drop zone, while simultaneously forcing Man United into the bottom three, two points adrift of 17th-placed Coventry City.

The Red Devils therefore find themselves in the bottom three after nine rounds for the first time since November 1986, when they were in 21st place after 15 games of the 1986-87 season.

November 1986 was the same month that Ron Atkinson was dismissed and Sir Alex Ferguson took over as head coach, and the latter ultimately led Man Utd to an 11th-placed finish in the First Division.

The 20-time English champions will rise to 16th place if they beat Aston Villa by any scoreline on Sunday, though, while victory by three goals or more will also lift them above Fulham into 15th spot.

Nevertheless, Carrick remains a man under major pressure after Man United's new 40-year worst, in spite of Tuesday's extraordinary 3-2 Champions League comeback triumph over Roma.

Man United always intended to get to the November international break before making any firm managerial decisions, so to say Carrick bought himself more time with Tuesday's fightback may be inaccurate.

However, another pitiful Premier League performance and result on Sunday could force the hierarchy to take action, especially with the current bookies' favourites available for hire immediately.

The odds on Eddie Howe replacing Carrick are now as low as 8/13 on some operators, while Julian Nagelsmann is the second-favourite to take charge at 11/4.

Antonio Conte and Unai Emery are not far behind, the latter's links with Old Trafford representing a fascinating sub-plot ahead of tomorrow's fixture.
