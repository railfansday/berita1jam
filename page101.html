Crimea bridge shut after Ukrainian strike, as Russia hits Kyiv crossings

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Images circulated on social media appeared to show part of the road section of the Crimea bridge has collapsed

A key bridge linking occupied Crimea and Russia is closed after a Ukrainian strike inflicted major damage on the prized Kremlin asset.

Images shared by Ukrainian officials showed a section of the Crimean Bridge - also known as the Kerch Bridge - had collapsed into the water.

Russian officials confirmed the structure had been damaged by drones, while Russia's deputy prime minister was due to chair an emergency meeting to discuss the situation.

Meanwhile, Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko said the Northern and Southern bridges, both important crossings over the Dnipro River, were closed after Russia targeted the Ukrainian capital overnight.

Former Ukrainian adviser Anton Gerashchenko shared images and video footage appearing to show extensive damage to the road section of the Crimea bridge, with remnants of the support arch and road section seen protruding from the water below.

The bridge, which has come under repeated attack by Ukrainian forces, was built by Russia after it invaded and annexed the peninsula in 2014, and was personally opened by Russian President Vladimir Putin in 2018.

While Ukraine has been trying to increase the cost to Russia of waging war economically, by hitting oil refineries and depots, striking the Crimea bridge is an attempt to increase the political cost on Putin.

The timing of the Crimea bridge strike comes at a significant moment, ahead of Putin meeting the leaders of China and the US. This strike could cloud his moment back in the spotlight.

While the long-term impact of the strike on was not immediately clear, the bridge has been used to move troops and supplies into Crimea and towards the front line.

The railway section of the bridge appears to still be intact but it has been damaged previously.

Russia's Investigative Committee confirmed structural damage, and said it had opened a "criminal case... on terrorism charges" after attacks by "Ukraine's armed formations".

The Crimea bridge (pictured in 2023) has been repeatedly targeted by Ukraine - one of the two distinctive white arches appears to have collapsed overnight

Monday night's strikes came a day after Ukraine began its most recent attempt to disable the Crimea bridge, in an attack which a Russian official said killed two and reportedly caused less significant structural damage.

Crimea's Moscow-backed governor Sergei Aksyonov confirmed on Telegram that traffic over the bridge had been suspended.

Zelensky had warned of major attacks in the days leading up to a meeting between Putin, Donald Trump and Xi Jinping in China's Shenzhen on Thursday.

Meanhile, loud explosions were heard in Kyiv just before midnight on Tuesday, with Klitschko saying shortly after that the city had suffered "damage to critical infrastructure systems" and disruption to heating and water.

The Northern Bridge (pictured in October) is closed after being targeted again by Russia

Some of Kyiv's above-ground subway services were damaged in the overnight attacks, officials said, after civilians were told to go to civil defence shelters.

Residents of Kyiv typically shelter in the city's underground stations during air raids.

Eight major bridges span the Dnipro River connecting the western and eastern parts of Kyiv, and an estimated three million residents rely on the bridges to travel across the city.

These latest Russian strikes will cause major disruption, with traffic already reportedly building in the capital.

Elsewhere, three people have been injured in a Russian missile attack in the Cherkast region, according to local officials.

The latest round of tit-for-tat strikes came after US, Ukrainian and European officials held talks on ways to facilitate "direct engagement" between Russia and Europe to end the war.

Following Monday's meeting, US envoy Jared Kushner, President Trump's son-in-law, said the US was committed to providing Ukraine with a "strong, credible security guarantees", as well as a path towards EU membership.

He added that Nato "will remain a critical pillar of European security", and that preparations are underway for a further multilateral meeting "in the near future".

Zelensky said that the meeting was "difficult", adding that more needed to be done to support Ukraine's defence against Russian ballistic missile strikes, which he said were being provided by North Korea.

The Ukrainian president has long called for European nations to be included in any peace talks, which he argues is necessary because of Kyiv's longer-term ambition to join the EU.

Meanwhile, Ukrainian officials said the final death toll from a Russian attack on the southern Ukrainian village of Kamyshuvakha earlier this month had reached 72.

Four people are still considered missing after the bombing, which Ukrainian officials said was one of the deadliest individual attacks since the start of the war in 2022.

Footage, from a camera on one of the killed police officers, showed residents going to an underground shelter after the initial attack on 2 November. Soon after a blast is heard and flames are seen as the shelter is hit.

Ukrainian police said of those killed were police officers, "staff of the gymnasium, utility workers and medical personnel".

Russia's launched its full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022. Approximately one-fifth of Ukraine is now under Russian occupation and Kyiv has refused Putin's demand to give up areas of Ukraine's eastern Donbas that remain under its control.

Judge Acquits MIT Brothers in $25M Ethereum MEV Case, Ending Prosecution

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Judge Acquits MIT Brothers in $25M Ethereum MEV Case, Ending Prosecution

A federal judge in Manhattan has acquitted Anton and James Peraire-Bueno, the MIT-educated brothers accused of exploiting Ethereum's transaction ordering system to take $25 million from automated trading bots.

U.S. District Judge Jessica Clarke granted the brothers' motion for a judgment of acquittal on Monday, clearing them of wire fraud, conspiracy to commit wire fraud and conspiracy to commit money laundering, according to her ruling. The decision closes the criminal case without a second trial.

While the brothers' scheme "may have frustrated the spirit" of MEV-Boost, software that lets Ethereum validators outsource the ordering of transactions to specialized block builders, that was not enough to make out fraud, Clarke said. In a footnote, she added that the prosecution "raises serious due process concerns," though she declined to rule on that question.

Because the money laundering charge relied on the proceeds of the alleged wire fraud, it fell with the fraud counts.

The case stemmed from an April 2023 exploit targeting so-called sandwich bots, automated traders that profit from maximal extractable value (MEV) by placing their own trades on either side of other users' transactions.

According to the ruling, the brothers placed bait transactions designed to lure specific bots, then exploited a bug in a relay's code to see the bots' bundled trades before committing to a block. They then swapped the bait transactions for trades that sold the tokens instead, leaving the bots with about $25 million in losses in roughly 12 seconds. Authorities arrested the pair in May 2024, saying they spent months planning the attack and took steps to hide the proceeds.

At trial in Manhattan last year, an insider testified that the brothers had allegedly planned the attack, and the defense had earlier sought to block evidence of their Google searches. Clarke declared a mistrial in November 2025, after the jury deadlocked.

The case was closely watched in the crypto industry. Clarke's ruling described it as a "first of its kind" prosecution, a label the government itself used in its indictment.

OpenAI bosses shrug off Anthropic’s IPO

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Finance chief Sarah Friar said OpenAI’s latest $1.25tn round was unplanned, sparked by an inaccurate news report.

OpenAI chief executive Sam Altman at The Grove, a Village Global event, in November 2022.

OpenAI’s top two executives said on Monday they are not worried about rival Anthropic going public first. Chief executive Sam Altman and finance chief Sarah Friar both spoke at Semafor’s Silicon Valley & The World event, as Brent Griffiths reported for Business Insider.

Asked whether OpenAI could end up like Lyft, which listed before Uber in 2019 but later fell far behind it, Altman brushed off the idea.

Anthropic made its S-1 filing public on 26 October and plans to list on Nasdaq under the ticker ANT. It is expected to debut this week.

Friar went further. Anthropic’s listing is good for the industry and good for OpenAI, she said, according to Semafor’s report. She added that OpenAI did not mind being second.

Friar also said OpenAI is closing a smaller funding round at a $1.25tn valuation, after raising $122bn in March. She described the round as unplanned and opportunistic.

It began, she said, after a well-known publication wrongly reported that OpenAI was raising money. Investors then contacted the company, Semafor reported.

Altman said journalists were more sceptical about AI than investors, who he described as bullish. He added that it was a good thing more investors wanted exposure to the AI boom.

He repeated that OpenAI will go public at some point, but warned that listing comes with costs.

“I think there are real downsides to being public,” Altman said, pointing to a volatile period for the industry and big decisions on safety.

OpenAI is still aiming for a 2027 listing. Friar told staff in August it would be public in 2027, or sooner if growth held.

The headline declared that Andy Burnham has urged caution on scrapping the 3pm Saturday TV blackout.

Ahead of this latest international break I was at the Fulham v Newcastle United game, the novelty of it being a rare 3pm kick-off for NUFC.

The BBC article that I was reading, stated: “The blackout originated in the 1960s when Burnley chairman Bob Lord encouraged the EFL to oppose television coverage of 3pm Saturday matches.”

I understand the sentiments expressed by Andy Burnham, as he doesn’t want to be seen as upsetting the lower league clubs who are dependent on match day receipts and the peripheral spending.

However, when you look at the amount of games in the Premier League played outside the Saturday 3pm kick-off, you begin to wonder.

The working man’s game where you could rock up to St James’ Park with a belly full of ale for a 3pm kick-off and pay at the turnstiles are a thing of the past and long gone.

Take a look at the Newcastle United fixtures so far this season and you will see Fulham really was novelty.

In these first five months of the 2026-27 Premier League season only three 3pm Saturday kick-offs for Newcastle United, home to Hull and away to Fulham, plus Ipswich away next month.

All of these Premier League games played outside of the 3pm Saturday slot is a boon for lower league clubs. Due to technology, I can watch Newcastle play at 3pm on a Saturday even when I don’t get a ticket for the match.

But the 3pm Saturday kick-off is such a rarity it has become an irrelevance.

As the Newcastle United games are now shown at an increasingly ridiculous range of times, that frees up Saturday afternoon for me and my mates to go down to Dulwich Hamlet on a Saturday afternoon and have a pint of Peckham pale ale on the terracing while watching live football in the Isthmian Premier League.

So as me and my lad fail to get home tickets for Newcastle United on a regular basis now (no need for tiny violins), we are down the Hamlet on a Saturday afternoon with hundreds of other like minded Premier League team supporting people, who simply want to see live football kicking off at a proper time.

Liverpool and Manchester United are both keeping tabs on AC Milan defender Koni De Winter as the Premier League giants assess potential additions to their back lines.
