Should You Buy Anthropic IPO Stock? Here’s What History Says

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Anthropic may begin trading as early as next week.

Investors have been sharply focused on initial public offerings this year, and the next such operation may be launched by AI lab Anthropic. Why are investors so interested in IPOs right now? They've offered the opportunity to get in on some of the most exciting companies in the high-growth artificial intelligence (AI) space. AI is booming now, and the market is expected to reach into the trillions by the end of the decade.

Cerebras Systems (CBRS +7.92%) launched its offering in May, the biggest operation in 2026 up to that moment. Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX -2.33%) a month later completed the largest IPO ever, raising more than $85 billion and entering the market with a value surpassing $2 trillion.

Now, Anthropic aims to price on Nov. 18 and begin trading on Nov. 19, CNBC reported, citing people familiar with the situation. Should you participate in the Anthropic IPO? Let's take a look at what history says.

Before we look at historical trends, though, we'll first take a quick look at the Anthropic story so far and what we know about the upcoming operation. Anthropic develops the Claude family of models, and its founders, led by chief Dario Amodei, originally worked at rival AI lab OpenAI.

The company has seen tremendous success in terms of demand and revenue, going from zero in sales three years ago to more than $17 billion in the third quarter of this year. And in the first nine months of the year, revenue soared 1,200% year over year to $33.2 billion. Enterprise adoption of Claude has been tremendous, with 11,900 customers accounting for $100,000 or more in revenue on a trailing 12-month basis. These customers use Claude for a variety of projects, from the simple to the complex, and Claude products may be applied to industry-specific tasks such as robotics or drug discovery.

This is positive, but Anthropic also involves some risk. It's not yet profitable, with a net loss of $104 billion in the first nine months of this year, and the AI lab pointed out in about 90 pages of its prospectus many potential problems that could arise. These include regulatory and litigation risk, and the idea that as AI models become more advanced, the possibility of doing harm grows too.

Anthropic this week said that it would offer 825 million shares at an IPO price between $70 and $78, according to a filing. People familiar with the situation told CNBC that Anthropic aims to allocate 20% of its IPO shares to retail investors -- as SpaceX did during its IPO.

This sets Anthropic and SpaceX apart from traditional IPOs, in which institutional investors generally have access to most of the shares, and retail investors get in on 5% to 10%. At the highest pricing, Anthropic could raise more than $64 billion, and the company may enter the market with a trillion-dollar valuation.

Should you participate in this IPO? Let's take a look back in time to see whether IPOs have delivered big returns in their early days of trading.

A look at the 10 biggest IPOs from 1999 through 2023, including companies such as Rivian and Coupang, shows that eight out of 10 fell in their first three months of trading. And this year's major IPOs followed that trend, with Cerebras declining 29% and SpaceX slipping 6% in their first three months on the market.

So, history shows us that buying a stock during an IPO isn't absolutely necessary to get in on a potentially big growth story. In fact, in many cases, investors would have been better off buying shares later on the dip. This means that, if you're on the fence regarding whether you should buy Anthropic right out of the gate, you don't have to rush to make a decision. If history is right, other opportunities to buy this AI growth player are likely to arise in the months and years ahead.

Dejan Kulusevski has opened up about the knee injury that kept him on the sidelines for over 18 months and revealed that he turned down a possible knee transplant treatment.

Kulusevski also explained how his recovery weighed heavily on his mind amid fears that he would never play football again.

The 26-year-old missed 526 days of playing time including the entirety of the 2025/26 season after suffering a patella injury during a defeat to Crystal Palace in May 2025.

He also missed the start of the current season but has since returned to action in substitute appearances against Coventry, Palace and Leeds United.

Speaking of his injury and the extent of his recovery, Kulusevski explored how he thought “it’s over, it’s over” when attempting to get back on the pitch and reignite his career before confessing to his mother that his treatment options may not work out.

Now the forward is back and fit enough to feature on a professional level and has been selected for Sweden’s Nations League games this month. Getting on the pitch against Bosnia and Herzegovina or Poland would see Kulusevski make his first international appearance in two years.

The Tottenham man also revealed why his recovery took as long as it did.

“The reason it took so long is that there are people who haven’t even recovered from injuries like this,” he told Swedish outlet Fotbollskanalen.

“I spent the whole summer talking to other doctors about having another operation. It would have been a knee transplant — swapping my knee for someone else’s.

“So I said no: ‘This is what we’ll do: if I’m in pain when I play, I’ll just have to play through the pain.’ I trained and tried to live like a normal person. I trusted that it would work out. So far, I’ve been right.”

Kulusevski went on to add that he was advised by doctors that a knee transplant had a “50/50” chance of setting his recovery back for an extended period of time.

The winger also spoke to former teammates, Zlatan Ibrahimovic and Ivan Perisic, who recommended a doctor in Serbia and Kulusevski praised the work and advise he received.

He added: “He [the doctor] was so sure it would work out. I needed someone by my side who would say to me: ‘Dejan, this is going to be fine.’ So he was important to me.”

Sweden are top of Group B4 in the Nations League and have the chance to secure promotion to League A.

Is the Crypto Market Walking Into a Bull Trap? Bitcoin ETF Records Five Straight Days of Outflow

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Ahmed Barakat is a journalist and copywriter based in Georgia with a growing focus on blockchain technology, DeFi, AI, privacy, digital assets, and fintech innovation.

The CryptoNews editorial team is composed of seasoned writers specializing in cryptocurrency and blockchain technology. Their expertise ensures comprehensive, accurate, and insightful content for...

Bitcoin’s latest recovery attempt is running into a serious problem: institutional investors are pulling money out of the market. U.S. spot Bitcoin ETF recorded five consecutive days of net outflows, shedding $1.44 billion between November 5 and November 11.

Bitcoin is now trading near $73,500, struggling to reclaim the $75,000 level. Bitcoin ETF outflow streak raises a bigger question: Is the crypto market walking into another bull trap?

The timing is particularly concerning. Bitcoin briefly reclaimed $80,000 on November 4 before reversing sharply the following day. Crypto has since struggled to regain momentum, leaving us questioning buyers’ decisions and whether we are stepping in too early.

The latest Bitcoin ETF withdrawals included $430.2 million on November 5, followed by another $411.9 million on November 6. Outflows continued on November 9, 10, and 11.

BlackRock’s iShares Bitcoin Trust (IBIT) accounted for most of the selling pressure. The fund alone recorded $269.7 million in net outflows yesterday.

The latest Bitcoin ETF outflow streak shows that institutional demand is struggling to support the market’s recovery. Five consecutive sessions of withdrawals have removed $1.44 billion from U.S. spot Bitcoin funds. BlackRock’s IBIT accounted for three-quarters of that amount.

However, institutional demand has not disappeared entirely. Morgan Stanley’s Bitcoin ETF attracted $9 million yesterday, offering a small counterpoint to the ETF-wide selling.

Glassnode’s latest Week 46 report adds another warning. Bitcoin has fallen below the short-term holder cost basis of $75,600. This leaves recent buyers sitting on unrealized losses.

The research firm also points to shrinking stablecoin supply and continued ETF selling. Both developments display that fresh capital is struggling to enter the market.

This creates an uncomfortable setup for anyone buying the latest dip. A short-lived recovery could attract retail investors before another wave of selling pushes prices lower.

Bitcoin’s derivatives market offers another piece of the puzzle. BTC futures open interest remains above $50 billion, while funding rates remain positive. This number somehow paints a clear picture: leveraged traders are still paying to maintain bullish positions.

However, leverage has already started coming out of the market. Open interest has declined, and much of the long-side leverage near current prices has been flushed.

That creates two possible outcomes. If spot demand returns, reduced leverage could help Bitcoin recover without triggering another major liquidation cascade. Alternatively, another failed breakout could leave remaining leveraged buyers exposed to further downside.

For now, $75,600 is the important recovery threshold. Reclaiming that level would put Bitcoin back above the short-term holder cost basis and improve market sentiment.

A grinding climb toward $80,000 would offer stronger evidence that buyers are regaining control, but losing the $72,000 area could expose BTC to another test of $70,000.

The bull trap argument also has a counterpoint. There is accumulation among two large wallet cohorts, while selling momentum has started weakening. This shows that some investors are already positioning for a recovery.

The market does not need another dramatic crash to trap investors. Sometimes, all it takes is a convincing bounce that runs out of buyers.

New warning sent to Fire Stick users as police 'trawl through bank accounts' to contact illegal EFL streamers

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Customers of jailbroken Amazon Fire TV Stick devices have received a huge new warning over such practices as authorities continue to push forward in their bid to tackle the issue, with the police now taking fresh action.

It's well documented, of course, that many people up and down the country purchase illegal Fire Stick devices in order to avoid forking out for controversially-priced subscription packages from the likes of Sky Sports and TNT Sports.

A central role of this illegal market is the ability to stream live sporting events through the chipped software, which often works out significantly cheaper than a £30 monthly Sky Sports subscription, for example.

Indeed, a fair share of 'dodgy' Fire Stick customers will just happen to be EFL supporters, too, as the devices often enable users to bypass the 3pm broadcasting blackout on Saturday afternoons - making it much easier to watch their team in action.

Many are far more content with paying between £30 and £100 for an illegal device as opposed to a legal subscription package, much to the ire of the authorities.

Ultimately, though, it's against the law, and recent years have seen big-hitting broadcasters and the authorities alike join forces in an effort to prevent the ever-growing popularity of jailbroken Fire Stick devices.

Those found guilty of infiltrating the devices with illegal software for distribution and monetary gain purposes run the risk of landing behind bars, but it's not only the sellers that can find themselves on the wrong side of the law and a new warning has now been sent to anybody considering buying a pirated Fire Stick.

The police have attempted various measures in their ongoing battle to crack down on illegal broadcasting distribution and consumption over the last couple of years, but they're now changing their strategy in a development that shows just how serious the issue really is.

Ex-detective Ray Wilding recently spoke on the BBC's Morning Live show to reveal a fresh strategy from the authorities which will increase their chances of taking action against users as well as distributors.

Wilding disclosed that police have now decided to begin tracing the details found in the bank accounts of sellers, which will show exactly who has been purchasing 'dodgy' Fire Stick devices.

He explained: "They’re going for the distributors. They’re going for the sellers and they are having a lot of success.

"But actually, once they’ve got the seller, they’re going through their accounts. They’re seeing who has been paying the money for these illegal things, and if they’ve been paying them for these sticks then people should possibly expect a conversation or two if the police want to know why they’ve been paying them money for this.

"It’s a crime. It’s not allowed. Owning the stick itself is not a crime, but using it for this purpose is.

"They (subscription service providers) are losing money to this, so they have been reaching out and are taking action against those people using them illegally as well."

Much of the authorities' efforts have revolved around taking root of the problem and arresting individuals who are hijacking the devices with illegal software to distribute to the public, which has resulted in jail sentences.

Now, however, they're becoming increasingly interested in those keeping the illegal industry afloat by knowingly purchasing such devices, and simply buying a 'dodgy' Fire Stick can still land you in serious trouble.

Wilding himself has warned of this by adding: "I wouldn’t say it’s something you should do.

"It is actually a crime and it can have a nasty fine, a huge fine and also a custodial sentence for users and sellers of these things."

It has been widely reported that penalties such as a hefty £50,000 fine can be imposed upon anybody found guilty of supplying or simply watching illegally-streamed software, and this new form of action will make it much easier to track down users.

The most significant legal action will always, quite naturally, be brought against the individuals chipping and distributing 'dodgy' Fire Sticks in the first place, but it's clear that the police are more intent than ever to clamp down on users and this is simply the latest effort to be made.

That's because, earlier this year, the Federation Against Copyright Theft (FACT) announced that contact had been made with more than a thousand individuals across the UK warning them to 'immediately cease using illegal TV streaming services' in order to avoid the possibility of prosecution under Section 11 of the Fraud Act 2006.

FACT's campaign emphasised that users are not anonymous, with chairman Kieron Sharp having previously warned individuals of "potential criminal liability" and "exposure to scams, viruses and harmful content."
