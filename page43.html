The headline declared that Andy Burnham has urged caution on scrapping the 3pm Saturday TV blackout.

Ahead of this latest international break I was at the Fulham v Newcastle United game, the novelty of it being a rare 3pm kick-off for NUFC.

The BBC article that I was reading, stated: “The blackout originated in the 1960s when Burnley chairman Bob Lord encouraged the EFL to oppose television coverage of 3pm Saturday matches.”

I understand the sentiments expressed by Andy Burnham, as he doesn’t want to be seen as upsetting the lower league clubs who are dependent on match day receipts and the peripheral spending.

However, when you look at the amount of games in the Premier League played outside the Saturday 3pm kick-off, you begin to wonder.

The working man’s game where you could rock up to St James’ Park with a belly full of ale for a 3pm kick-off and pay at the turnstiles are a thing of the past and long gone.

Take a look at the Newcastle United fixtures so far this season and you will see Fulham really was novelty.

In these first five months of the 2026-27 Premier League season only three 3pm Saturday kick-offs for Newcastle United, home to Hull and away to Fulham, plus Ipswich away next month.

All of these Premier League games played outside of the 3pm Saturday slot is a boon for lower league clubs. Due to technology, I can watch Newcastle play at 3pm on a Saturday even when I don’t get a ticket for the match.

But the 3pm Saturday kick-off is such a rarity it has become an irrelevance.

As the Newcastle United games are now shown at an increasingly ridiculous range of times, that frees up Saturday afternoon for me and my mates to go down to Dulwich Hamlet on a Saturday afternoon and have a pint of Peckham pale ale on the terracing while watching live football in the Isthmian Premier League.

So as me and my lad fail to get home tickets for Newcastle United on a regular basis now (no need for tiny violins), we are down the Hamlet on a Saturday afternoon with hundreds of other like minded Premier League team supporting people, who simply want to see live football kicking off at a proper time.

Liverpool and Manchester United are both keeping tabs on AC Milan defender Koni De Winter as the Premier League giants assess potential additions to their back lines.

The Belgium international has enjoyed an increasingly important role at San Siro under Ruben Amorim, with his versatility and performances attracting attention across England.

Liverpool are planning for the long-term future of their defence, while Man United are expected to prioritise another centre-back as Mauricio Pochettino begins reshaping his squad.

According to Tuttomercatoweb, Liverpool and Manchester United are among the Premier League clubs showing interest in De Winter, with Chelsea also following the 24-year-old.

However, Milan have no intention of sanctioning his departure in January unless they receive an extraordinary offer. De Winter is regarded as one of Amorim’s trusted defenders and remains an important part of the club’s long-term plans.

Interest from England is broader than just the traditional heavyweights. Scouts from Newcastle, Tottenham, Leeds and Brentford have also watched the Belgian this season.

A recent CaughtOffside report states that Liverpool and United have been impressed by his development, although neither club currently considers him an immediate priority.

De Winter himself is also believed to be happy at Milan and is not actively pushing to leave.

Liverpool’s interest is easy to understand. Virgil van Dijk is approaching the latter stages of his career, and the Reds need to continue identifying younger centre-backs before a major transition becomes unavoidable.

De Winter can operate in a traditional central partnership or on the right of a back three, giving him useful tactical flexibility.

United have an equally strong reason to monitor him. Pochettino is expected to strengthen at centre-back in January, and De Winter’s age, physical profile and experience at a major European club fit the type of signing United should be considering.

The obstacle is Milan. De Winter is contracted until 2030 and has started 10 of their opening 11 Serie A matches, leaving the Rossoneri under no pressure to negotiate.

For both Liverpool and United, continuing to scout him makes sense. But unless Milan’s position changes dramatically, a summer approach looks considerably more realistic than paying a January premium.

The Delta Reserve Card is worth it if you’re a frequent flyer with Delta, or if you want to earn elite status through credit card spending. Let’s take a look at all the details of the card.

Based on my latest miles & points valuations, I consider Delta SkyMiles to be worth 1.1 cents each. That means to me, the 125,000-mile bonus is worth $1,375. If you spend on the Delta Reserve Card, the good news is that there are no foreign transaction fees (Rates & Fees), so you can efficiently use the card abroad.

While Delta SkyMiles is far from my favorite mileage currency, it does have a high floor value, so there are good ways to redeem Delta SkyMiles.

Delta One (business class) awards can be a great option, especially on roundtrip awards rather than one way itineraries. Using SkyMiles for shorter hops on Delta can be great, too. I’ve seen award flights between New York (LGA) and Orlando (MCO) for less than 20,000 miles roundtrip.

Partner award flights can be an especially good option if you focus on flights that don’t touch the United States, since flights that do are more expensive and get you less value for your miles.

You can redeem SkyMiles for flights on SkyTeam partners, like Air France, China Airlines, and Korean Air. You can also redeem SkyMiles on some airlines that aren’t part of SkyTeam but still partner with Delta, like LATAM and WestJet.

One of the primary things that sets the Delta Reserve Credit Card apart from other Delta Amex cards is the lounge access perks that it offers. The card offers Delta Sky Club® access, Centurion Lounge access, and even several Sky Club passes. How does that work?

The Delta Reserve Card offers 15 Delta Sky Club visits each Medallion year for the primary cardmember. Each visit is defined as all entries within a 24-hour period, including at different airports. In other words, if you have a connecting itinerary, you can visit multiple Sky Clubs at different airports while using only one visit allotment.

It’s possible to unlock unlimited Sky Club visits after spending $75,000 in purchases on your card in a calendar year. Once all 15 visits have been used, eligible cardmembers may purchase additional Sky Club visits at a per visit rate of $50 per person using the card.

In addition to the primary cardmember getting Sky Club access when flying Delta the same day, they also receive four Sky Club guest passes annually.

These can be used by the primary cardmember to guest others into the lounge, allowing you to skip the $50 guesting fee four times annually.

The Delta Reserve Card also offers complimentary Centurion Lounge access when traveling on a same-day Delta flight booked with the card. The primary cardmember is entitled to access, and bringing additional guests costs $50 each.

Centurion Lounges, for the most part, offer a significantly improved food and beverage offering compared to most domestic airline lounges in the United States, so this is a nice option to have.

The Delta Reserve Credit Card has a good bonus, though if I were just looking for the best welcome bonus offer, I would go for the Gold or Platinum version of the card, since the cards have significantly lower annual fees.

The reason you want to get the Reserve version of the card is because of the valuable long term perks that it offers. Above I’ve talked about how the Reserve Card can help you earn status and can help you access lounges, though there are lots of perks beyond that. Let’s take a look at some of those benefits.

Every year upon renewal of the Delta Reserve Card, you receive a roundtrip companion certificate valid within the United States (including Alaska and Hawaii), and to Mexico, the Caribbean, and Central America. This can be used for fares ranging from first class to economy, so the flexibility offered by that is pretty awesome, and for many will more than justify the annual fee.

Just for having the Delta Reserve Card, you receive a $200 Delta Stays credit annually. The way this works, you receive up to $200 in statement credits per calendar year when you use your card to pay for a new Delta Stays prepaid hotel or vacation rental booking on the Delta Stays platform.

While I’m not usually a fan of using airline portals to book hotel stays and vacation rentals, this could prove worthwhile, as you can use it for a hotel stay costing right around $200, if you wanted to. If used right, the value here is considerable.

Just for having the Delta Reserve Card, you receive up to $240 in Resy credits annually (Enrollment required). The way this works, you can receive up to $20 in statement credits each month after using your card on eligible purchases at U.S. restaurants that offer reservations on Resy.com and the Resy app, purchases made directly on Resy.com or in the Resy app, and purchases made via Resy Pay.

Just for having the Delta Reserve Card, you receive up to $120 in rideshare credits annually (Enrollment required). The way this works, you can receive up to $10 in statement credits each month after using your card on U.S. rideshare purchases with select providers, including Uber and Lyft.

Delta has a Pay With Miles feature, where if you have a Delta co-branded credit card you can redeem miles for one cent each toward a Delta flight purchase (in increments of 5,000 miles per $50). That’s not the best deal in the world, though this is a feature that’s exclusively available to those with an eligible card.

Some people find this to be useful since they can redeem their miles toward flights, and then they still earn MQDs for taking those flights.

With the TakeOff 15 perk (exclusively for those with select Delta Amex cards), you can save 15% on the cost of a SkyMiles award ticket when traveling exclusively on Delta. Delta SkyMiles award pricing is dynamic, and generally you’ll find that each mile gets you a bit over a cent toward the cost of a ticket.

Saving 15% on these awards is valuable, and means that you’ll generally get somewhere around 1.2-1.3 cents of value per mile (or sometimes even more). In many cases this will end up being a better value than using the Pay With Miles option, so it’s a real perk of the card.

Having the Delta Reserve Card gets you priority for complimentary upgrades, assuming you have Medallion status. That’s because the card acts as a tiebreaker.

If you have the Reserve Card you get upgrade priority over other Medallion members within the same Medallion level and fare class. Given how competitive upgrades are, this can make a material difference in terms of whether you clear or not.

The Delta Reserve Card offers complimentary Delta First and Delta Comfort upgrades on eligible flights for non-elite members. You will be toward the bottom of the upgrade list, but there are lots of situations where this could help you score a good extra legroom seat, or in some cases even a first class seat.

Those with the Delta Reserve Card receive a Global Entry or TSA PreCheck credit. This benefit applies once every four years. This can save you big time both at security and immigration, and is something I highly recommend signing up for if eligible.

We Now Know What Robert Pattinson Was Doing When the James Bond Casting News Broke

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

All thanks to filmmaker Robert Eggers, who directed the star in 2019's 'The Lighthouse.'

It’s been a whirlwind 48 hours for Robert Pattinson.

The Hollywood Reporter heard from sources that the British star only found out he’d been tapped to succeed Daniel Craig as James Bond on Sunday morning, when Denis Villeneuve — who’s helming the next Bond movie — called him. Producer Amy Pascal was said to have informed the other candidates.

Within a day, the whole world knew too. Amazon MGM Studios, which bought the creative rights to the Bond franchise from producer siblings Michael G. Wilson and Barbara Broccoli last year, released the news via the character’s official social media accounts. Just before 7 a.m. PT on Monday, a tuxedo-wearing Pattinson emerged from the shadows, gun in hand. His announcement post has accrued over 1.2 million likes on X at the time of writing. Thanks to Robert Eggers, who directed Pattinson in his 2019 period horror-fantasy The Lighthouse, we now know what the next Bond was up to when that news became public and sent the internet into a tailspin. During an on-camera interview with IGN, Eggers was asked if, while Pattinson’s character killed a seagull during the making of The Lighthouse, he ever stopped to consider: “There goes the next James Bond.”

“No, but I’m not surprised that it panned out the way that it did,” replied the Nosferatu filmmaker. “And also, crazily enough, he was watching a screening of Werwulf when the news broke, and I was just texting with him about it this morning,” he said, laughing.

Werwulf, set for release on Dec. 25 in the U.S., stars Eggers regulars Aaron Taylor-Johnson, Lily-Rose Depp, Willem Dafoe and Ralph Ineson, and is set to chronicle a cursed farmer’s transformation into a monstrous creature in 13th-century England. Coincidentally, Taylor-Johnson was among the rumored Bond contenders for a long time.

So what did our new Bond think of Werwulf? “Oh, he loved it, of course,” added Eggers.

Pattinson, who this year alone has starred in The Drama, The Odyssey, Primetime and still has Dune 3 to come, said on Monday through an Amazon MGM Studios press release: “James Bond is one of the most legendary characters in cinema, and to be entrusted with 007 is the honor of a lifetime. I’m deeply grateful to Denis, Amy, David, Tanya and everyone at Amazon MGM Studios for their belief in me. To say this role is a dream come true is an understatement. I couldn’t be more excited about this partnership and the work ahead with this fantastic team.”

He won a months-long race for the role, beating fellow screen-testers Callum Turner, Josh O’Connor and Jack Lowden. Villeneuve, who recently worked with Pattinson on the final Dune outing, is directing a script from Peaky Blinders mastermind Steven Knight.

The 40-year-old actor is also already the face of DC Studio’s Batman. THR took a deep dive into why his casting is the smart but safe choice as the Bond franchise enters a new era. Fan reaction to the announcement is here — and some are suggesting Pattinson’s starring role in Twilight predicted his future Bond role.

In fact, Pattinson might have even manifested it. In 2012, during the height of his Twilight fame, the actor was asked in an interview if he could be playing 007 in 10 years’ time. “Yeah, definitely,” he replied, “but more like 20 years. […] I think there’d be nothing worse than, like, ‘Let’s get a fresh-faced Bond!’ That would be the worst idea in the world. After Daniel Craig, you have to have some baggage. It would be ridiculous to reinvent [him] as some young posh kid. Or, if [he] was, I’d have to get so tortured in the first few scenes. I’d have to do the first film with one arm or something.”

Dodge third-party sellers — Double-check the seller before buying. Marketplace scalpers frequently list units for jacked-up prices; make sure it says "Sold and shipped by [Retailer]" at the standard price.

Skip the web wars and head to physical stores — Online checkout bots grab website stock in under 15 seconds. Showing up in person is easily your most reliable path to securing one without paying scalper markups.

Stores like Walmart, Amazon, and Best Buy often hold onto popular gaming hardware to use as headline deals during Black Friday and Cyber Monday week.

With that said, Black Friday restocks are usually the hardest ones to catch. You will be fighting website crashes, gift shoppers, and automated bots all at once. If you can grab a console whenever stock appears, take it. Think of Black Friday as a backup plan, not your main bet.

Console hunting sucks right now, period. But between the surprise micro-drops and physical store restocks, you still have real shots at retail price — just be ready to move the second that stock goes live.
