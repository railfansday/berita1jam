Terry Dubrow Reveals Cadaveric Fat Transfers Are a "Hot New" Procedure

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Terry Dubrow revealed a shocking truth, stunning Jenny McCarthy Wahlberg during his appearance on her podcast when he revealed fat transfers from cadavers are a new cosmetic trend.

Warning: You won’t be able to unlearn the knowledge Terry Dubrow recently dropped.

And, well, his intel about how plastic surgery and cadavers are connected cuts pretty deep.

But if you’re still reading this, fine. We’ll allow the Botched star himself to explain.

“The new thing is cadaveric fat transfer,” Dubrow informed Jenny McCarthy Wahlberg on the Nov. 3 episode of her eponymous podcast. “Have you heard of this?”

In response, an aghast McCarthy Wahlberg asked, “Does that mean from cadavers?”

“Yes,” Dubrow affirmed, sparking the Two and a Half Men alum to make a disgusted noise, before he added, “That’s the hot new thing.”

For the particularly macabre, here’s how it works. AlloClae, the name brand for such an injectable, is a non-surgical body contouring solution made from donated human fat tissue (yes, we’re talking about cadavers). The injectable is comprised of non-living fat cells, extracellular matrix, triglycerides and connective tissues.

The way alloClae is processed, per its website, preserves the natural 3D structure of fat cells, allowing for immediate volume without the downtime and invasiveness that comes with surgery.

For those whose minds are still reeling with concerns, Dubrow told McCarthy Wahlberg, “It's been processed and radiated and stuff like that. But there's different kinds of cadaver fat. One of the kinds, there's no cells in it. It's just the matrix and you injected it and your own fat grows into it.”

The 68-year-old emphasized, “That's the hot thing right now.”

Of course, while the trend might be new in the world of plastic surgery, the use of cadavers has been a regular practice in dentistry. Allograft bone is processed, sterilized bone harvested from deceased donors and is used in procedures such as dental bone grating, socket and ridge preservation as well as periodontal defect repair.

Though cadaveric fat transfer was a new one for McCarthy Wahlberg, allograft bone is a concept she’s previously sunk her teeth into—literally.

“Do you know how many dead bodies are in my mouth from all of my mouth surgeries that I've had?” the 54-year-old said to Dubrow. “Like, I've eaten more cadavers in the last two years than I ever thought I would.”

Nevertheless, she added, “I don't think I want cadaver's fat.”

But while cadaveric fat transfers are relatively fresh out the box, so to speak, plastic surgery itself has long existed within Hollywood. Read on for what some of your favorite celebrities have said about their cosmetic procedures—and how much they paid for them.

"Then I had a facelift three-and-a-half years ago. I was left with one eye higher than the other. It took a year to put it all right."As for how much this all cost?"Well, I haven’t kept a running total," Mrs. O admitted, "but it must top more than a million."

Ebenezer’s Venmo and ‘How to Rob a Bank’ ATMs: Studios Are Now Promoting Movies by Just Giving Away Money

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Clipfarming, influencer screenings, and meme-bait popcorn buckets: promoting a movie in the internet age is often a full-court offensive to get noticed. In that spirit, why not just get down to business and resort to modern civilization’s oldest method for seeking attention: dangling some money in people’s faces?

That’s what the marketing teams behind Amazon MGM Studios‘ “How to Rob a Bank” and Skydance’s “Ebenezer” have tried in the weeks leading up to their shared November 13 release to North American theaters.

For the former ensemble heist thriller, a branded ATM was moved between multiplexes throughout the Los Angeles area over the course of four days. Those who learned of the campaign on the film‘s social media channels could gain clues about the machine’s location and a specified PIN number, which would spark payouts of a single one-hundred-dollar bill to those who bothered to seek out (or stumble upon) it. Related Stories What Are Local Movie Theaters Showing on Election Night? Don’t Forget, Oscar Watchers: ‘The Odyssey,’ ‘Project Hail Mary,’ and Other Big Guns Are Coming Back

“Ebenezer” took a more cheeky, trifling approach, promoting an official Venmo account for Johnny Depp’s Christmas crab apple that users could request funds from. Those who scrolled through the account’s transactions would see it shares the character’s penny-pinching spirit: the only completed transactions were those giving away one single cent. The Venmo account was only active for a few hours, though a Paramount Instagram post from last week teases followers to “check back” as “Ebenezer might be feeling generous on @venmo again.”

Several film news aggregation accounts like Culture Crave and DiscussingFilm reported that the account “crashed” due an overwhelming amount of requests, though Paramount declined to comment on if such a description was accurate. Venmo did not respond to a request asking if the company partnered with the studio on the sponsorship.

Much like that line, there is also a tackiness du jour to both money-mindset stunts, especially in a world where awards shows, professional sports and geopolitical events can hardly be watched without outlets asking you to bet — erh, predict — on them. A video posted to Paramount’s socials opens with a Venmo request for $500, asking Scrooge to help pay off a user’s student loans.

Not to mention the real inadvertent quirk: the account has been able to receive payments from users. Considering the scant number of one-cent payouts from the account and the number of influxes, it is almost certain that Paramount could have made money on the stunt, though an individual with knowledge of the account shared that all received funds were sent back to users via private transactions.

Krystyna Ursta, a 27-year-old actress, video editor, and Depp fan in Los Angeles, shared that she gave about $30 to the account and received a full refund a few days later — plus an extra penny and an invitation to a special screening of the film in New York on Monday. She will be attending a local press screening instead. Ursta also posted a video of herself reacting to the account, one of many fan responses that flooded in on social media.

“I kind of figured that they will probably return it,” Ursta wrote over text after being reached through her Instagram handle, which she included in her Venmo transactions. “I’m such a big fan. I was happy to support the film.”

The rather gimmick-less gimmick of waving around money also helps capture the attention of people that aren’t necessarily interested in seeing the movie, but are going through the average modern experience of thinking about one’s financial situation. Aggregation accounts were quick to post graphics reporting on both stunts, with captions like “HOW TO ROB A BANK ATM’S ARE BEING SET UP AROUND LOS ANGELES AND GIVING PEOPLE FREE MONEY AND IT COULDN’T HAPPEN AT A BETTER TIME” and “EBENEZER SCROOGE WILL SEND YOU REAL MONEY ON VENMO BUT THERE’S A CATCH SWIPE FOR MORE.”

There’s also people like Scott, a 35-year-old based out of Atlanta that couldn’t help but engage with the “Ebenezer” Venmo account after seeing it on X. He sent a penny, then received one in return. (A disclaimer: six Venmo users were reached for interview about their Ebenezer experience by sending them $0.01 with this reporter’s contact information in the caption; Scott was the only response that timed out before publication.)

“Seeing Ebenezer is now on Venmo was too funny and stupid to not engage with,” he wrote over text. “There’s something very boring dystopia about it: the idea of a fictional character from a 200-year-old Christmas story being commercialized on Venmo just shows how dumb Hollywood can. … It’s like McDonald’s Monopoly: take our slop and we might give your money.”

Also helpful for the studios is how little dangling the potential of money actually costs. Sans staffing fees, producing the “Ebenezer” Venmo account and its activity used a sum less than the coins between the couch cushions on the Paramount lot. Per the “How to Rob a Bank” promo’s website, each location’s prize pool gave away ten one-hundred-dollar bills and twenty twenty-dollar-bills ($1,400 total), plus some theater-supplied prizes like gift cards. An employee at the Regal Sherman Oaks that worked on the day of the on-site promotion said the ATM seemed to deal out the rewards in a random order, as opposed to $100 dollar bills for the first finders. Amazon MGM declined to comment, while Stellar Agency Inc., which sponsored and administered the promotion, did not respond to a request.

Both had much smaller overheads than a “Now You See Me: Now You Don’t” promotion than ran in April 2025. To launch the magician threequel’s first trailer, Lionsgate reportedly gave away roughly $250,000 to those that texted a mysterious phone number displaying in Times Square in the hours up to the drop. Like “Ebenezer” and “How to Rob a Bank,” it was a promotion that sparked a viral moment with plenty of user-generated content, each sharing the widely desired fantasy of receiving free money.

Neon handed out basic resources for an “I Love Boosters” promotion in May, where stars KeKe Palmer and LaKeith Stanfield took over a Shell gas station in West Pico to give out complimentary refills to the first 70 cars that arrived. The stunt played on then-peaking fuel prices, which had skyrocketed in the wake of the U.S.’ initial bombing of Iran in February and the subsequent blockade of the Strait of Hormuz.

In the months since, President Trump has promised $5,000 checks to every adult U.S. citizen if the Republican Party retains control of Congress in Tuesday’s midterm elections. Trump announced the idea, his approval rating was at a dismal 35 percent, per a CNN aggregate of various polls. Though the offer is unprecedented and dubious on its face, it has been covered extensively — and it cost nothing to float it.

Five Creative Ways to Spend Your FSA Funds Before the End of the Year

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

If you have a flexible spending account (FSA), you know the drill: Use the funds before the end of the year, or lose them. Unfortunately, chances are slim you can pack the rest of the year with doctor's appointments. Does that mean your hard-earned funds go to waste? Definitely not. You can spend your remaining FSA funds on other qualifying healthcare costs. And what counts as "qualifying" might surprise you—for instance, you can use your FSA or HSA funds to get yourself a sweet new fitness tracker or smartwatch.

As the end of the year approaches, here are all the ways you can spend any leftover money in your FSA balance.

Many over-the-counter medications and health items are FSA-eligible, including pain relievers, cold medicine, bandages, dental care products, menstrual products, and more. Scan your medicine cabinet and make a list of anything you need to stock up on.

FSA/HSA eligibility for smartwatches and other fitness wearables is real, but the main “catch" (as I’ve previously explained) is proving the device is in fact necessary, which requires you to obtain a Letter of Medical Necessity (LMN).

Screenless recovery trackers like Whoop, Amazfit Helio Strap, and the Oura Ring can usually be justified as HSA/FSA eligible around sleep quality, HRV-based stress monitoring, or recovery tracking tied to a chronic condition. Similarly, smartwatches can qualify the same way a basic tracker does, as long as you can link a relevant documented health concern. For instance, Coros offers FSA/HSA eligibility for all its watches and heart rate monitors through its partnership with Truemed. Amazfit similarly lets you filter by HSA/FSA eligibility for its smartwatches.

If you want to get creative with it, the official FSA store lets you filter by "surprisingly eligible." Similar to the section above, for some of these items, you'll want to brush up on how exactly to get an LMN to justify certain tech as FSA-eligible for you personally. In the meantime, go crazy and snag yourself Epsom salt for $8.99, Supergoop! sunscreen for $38.00, or perhaps a snazzy massage gun for $349.99.

Knee braces, wrist splints, compression socks, and other orthopedic items can help relieve chronic pain issues. Rather than wait for these issues to rear their heads down the line, use FSA money to offset the costs of these fixes now. If you use eyeglasses or contacts, consider using your FSA dollars to update or refresh your prescriptions. Don't forget to stock up on eye drops and lens solution, too.

If you have medical procedures scheduled for early next year, many healthcare providers will allow you to pre-pay the estimated costs this calendar year using FSA funds. This includes dental work, physical therapy, chiropractic visits, Lasik eye surgery, and more.

However you choose to spend your FSA funds, don’t let that money go to waste. After all, it came out of your paycheck. AC Milan, Inter: how much will the new Milan stadium bring in?

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

This is a major announcement for the future of AC Milan and Inter. While the two Milanese clubs are preparing the construction of their new stadium, intended to replace the historic San Siro, the name of the future venue is now known. Presented during a press conference organized at the Sforza Castle in Milan, the project officially revealed its main partner: the new stadium will be called “ Generali Stadium », named after the Italian insurance giant. A particularly lucrative commercial agreement since, according to the Italian press, the naming contract should bring in around 650 million euros over a period of twenty years, or an average of 32.5 million euros per season. La Gazzetta Dello Sport. A considerable financial windfall for the two Lombard giants, who are seeking to develop their revenues in order to compete with the greatest European powers. Generali will also become the official partner of the two clubs in the insurance sector.

Scheduled for the 2031-2032 season, the inauguration of this new enclosure will mark an important stage in the history of Milanese football. The future stadium will present an original architecture, with a hexagonal exterior structure and stands arranged in a bowl, raised on a podium. The Generali name will be widely visible on the facade of the building. However, a particularity is planned for European competitions: in accordance with UEFA rules concerning the commercial rights of speakers, the Generali Stadium will take the name ” Milan Stadium » during Champions League matches and other international competitions. An operation already applied to several large European stadiums, notably those of Juventus and Arsenal. With this partnership, Milan and Inter join the list of Italian clubs having sold the naming rights to their venue, like the Allianz Stadium in Turin or Atalanta’s New Balance Arena.

In ‘Elsinore,’ Billie Piper Found “Beautiful Chaos” With Andrew Scott — And a New Career Chapter

The decorated stage and screen star is a scene-stealer in the weepy Oscar contender, leading to the most attention she's received for a film role to date. The experience is inspiring her to forge a new path.

At the SCAD Savannah Film Festival, just ahead of Halloween weekend, Billie Piper held a masterclass for college students. “It was basically me just rattling on about being an actor — I hope some of it was useful, not sure,” she says the next day from a quiet library in the Southern city’s downtown. “Their line of questioning was so insightful and kind of ahead of their years. They were all there with the little moleskin books taking notes. I just didn’t expect that.”

She’d have little reason to. Despite her decades in the business, despite the Olivier Award win and five BAFTA nominations, the English native hasn’t really traveled the film-festival circuit before. She came to town as an honoree for the Oscar contender Elsinore, receiving the Marquee Award for her vivacious turn as the imperious real-life casting director Patsy Pollock. In spending time with the kids before the festivities kicked off, it dawned on her: “I actually thought: ‘God, I’ve done some really great jobs.’”

She arrives today with dark brown hair, a far cry from the striking blonde of Elsinore and her other most noted screen roles, and a tablet that she occasionally flashes open to reference movies she’s either seen recently or intends to soon watch. “It’s really fired me up about independent movies again,” she says of running around in support of Elsinore. (The film launched at Telluride, where star Andrew Scott emerged as an instant best-actor front-runner, and has since played all over the world.) “I felt like it was all dead, but it wasn’t dead. I just feel so jacked about all the movies I’m seeing.”

An intensely chameleonic actress unafraid to go big, Piper has never had the largest footprint in the feature space. “Often the films that I read weren’t that good for women,” she says. We may be at something of a turning point. Already, she’s received her first career British Independent Film Award nomination for Elsinore, competing in a supporting category alongside returning faces like Claire Foy and Tom Courtenay.

After breaking out as a teen pop star, the now-44-year-old has always followed the best work. She made her name onscreen by leading series like Doctor Who and Secret Diary of a Callgirl, then as a major stage actor in West End productions of The Effect and Yerma. For the latter revival of Federico García Lorca’s classic, she remains the only actor to have won every official Best Actress prize currently awarded for a single UK theater performance. She wowed again in HBO Max’s I Hate Suzie, a brilliant panic attack of a dark comedy about a moderately famous actress thrown into turmoil when nude photos on her phone leak online.

For one reason or another, these projects didn’t land with as much force in the US as back home. Rest assured, Piper’s BAFTA-nominated performance is every bit as ferociously compelling — and revealing — as what her peers delivered in those Emmy-winning smashes.

For a good chunk of American audiences, at least, Suzie remains a hidden streaming gem waiting to be discovered. Piper seems comfortable in that zone.

Elsinore dramatizes the heartbreaking, inspiring final act of Ian Charleson, a gay actor not out publicly who was known for his starring role in Chariots of Fire. Charleson decided, shortly after being diagnosed with AIDS, to play Hamlet at the National Theatre while fighting for his life — and somehow pulled it off. Scott’s turn as the late great dominates the movie, but Piper becomes its sneaky heart. Patsy, Charleson’s best friend, emerges as his caretaker and as an anchor in their defiant chosen family.

“Their relationship was beautiful chaos,” Piper says. “They partied hard. He was a big figure in her daughter’s life. She was a single mother working in a male-dominated industry.” When it came to Charleson’s controversial choice to play Hamlet — his doctor, portrayed by Olivia Colman, was initially firmly against it — Patsy simply got it. So did Piper. “It makes complete sense to want to play one of, if not the most challenging parts at your most vulnerable point in your life as you approach your death,” Piper says. “Patsy understood that about him. I understand that about what he chose to do as well. I think a lot of actors get it, to be honest.”

Elsinore is a tearjerker, no doubt, but it’s stuffed with joy. Piper brings plenty of levity with her glamorous, haughty embodiment of an old showbusiness powerhouse. She is clearly having a ball. “I really miss people like Patsy in our industry — the larger-than-life characters — because everything’s so serious and there’s no money now,” Piper says. “It was fun to pull from the characters I knew in my early career. There were agents I had who were cut from a different cloth. It wasn’t all terribly kind, but it was unlike anybody I’d grown up with — the flamboyance, the drama, the quick shifts in tone and mood.”

Her chemistry with Scott is undeniable. The two veterans of the London theater scene previously worked together on Lena Dunham’s critically acclaimed Catherine Called Birdy, including a birthing scene for the ages. Like Piper, Scott is plenty decorated back home, but his frequent co-star finds him unusually playful to work with.

“With that skillset, you expect someone who’s a bit of a dick, but he’s not remotely a dick — he is warm, he is generous, he is informal. The work feels casual,” Piper says of Scott. “He takes it seriously, of course — he knows what he wants and knows what he likes — but it’s not the only thing that’s happening in his life, which I think is healthy.”

Piper can relate. She’s a mother of three and values the separation of the personal and professional. “I do my work, then I go home and am a parent — and so there’s no real processing of what I’ve done in the day, which is often really thrilling and rare and something I’d wanted to do since I was a child,” she says. Take, for instance, the job she’s just come back from: Wednesday season three. Piper grew up on Tim Burton and was now surrounded by his and his team’s singular designs. She got to act opposite the likes of Winona Ryder. And she was gone for nine months, a very long time. Too long, maybe.

“I haven’t worked on a nine-month shoot since Doctor Who, and I left that show for a reason,” she says. “The gratitude is there, but also it’s not without a cost.” Now, of course, she’s even further away, in the American south: “I’m doing a lot of being away. I feel terrible and also excited.”

Some viewers read into the whirring, control-obsessed anxiety of Piper’s I Hate Suzie tour-de-force as a thinly veiled commentary on her own struggles as an artist in the public eye. (Suzie’s career beginnings mirrored Piper’s to some degree.) The show was fiction, of course, but Piper’s guttural performance came from somewhere true.

“It’s really fun to play,” Piper says now, “but it’s just no way to live your life.”

The lesson took some learning. “I need things to be beyond good for me to take it seriously, to take myself seriously — it’s really got nothing to do with anyone else, but with insane levels of achievement,” Piper says. “It’s dysfunctional. I’ve been like that since I was a kid.” She believed the work should feel painful, that it should take everything out of her. She credits Yerma, which she performed in London and New York from 2016-2018 under the direction of Simon Stone — who so happens to be the director of Elsinore — with ushering in a new way forward in that regard.

“Simon completely changed my career for me — completely changed the way I looked at acting,” Piper says. “That’s a nice thing to have in a career that has been going on for a long time, to suddenly work with someone that completely rips up the rule book.”

Brick by brick, Piper is building a thrilling new chapter on that experience. Elsinore has proven a cozy fit. Not just as an awards-bound prestige movie offering her a meaty supporting role — also in the way it’s welcoming her into spaces she’s never even thought much of before. Her main goal this awards season? To hang out with Club Kid writer-director-star Jordan Firstman. “I’ve watched Rotting in the Sun about five times. It’s so funny and so free,” she says, referring to Sebastián Silva’s graphic meta comedy that starred Firstman as a vapid version of himself. “Now I just want to be his best mate.”

She’s written a film that she describes as “romantic horror,” which she’s planning to direct. (Her feature debut, Rare Beasts, premiered at Venice in 2019.) She knows it will be difficult to get off the ground. But she’s been feeling inspired by what she’s seeing. “It’s really useful going into a process of trying to get a film made that may never happen, may never see the light of day — it’s energized my passion,” she says.

Same goes for the students with whom she spent the previous afternoon.

“Even though we live in this difficult day and age, even though it might feel more compromised and frightening in terms of a career choice, they still want to pursue this,” Piper says. “There is still this appetite for the arts. I find it quite moving.”

Ethiopian Authorities Suspend Independent Newspaper

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Click to expand Image A man reads the Ethiopian Reporter in Addis Ababa on December 11, 2019. © 2019 Eduardo Soteras/AFP via Getty Images

In what has become the new normal, the Ethiopian government has silenced yet another independent media outlet.

The Ethiopian Media Authority announced in a November 2 Facebook post that it is indefinitely suspending the print and online license of the Reporter, one of Ethiopia’s oldest media institutions. Publishing in English and Amharic, the Reporter is also the country’s last privately owned Amharic newspaper.

Stating that the suspension would take effect immediately, the Media Authority accused the Reporter of violating the country’s laws and media ethics by repeatedly publishing content that was against “national interests.” The authority added that the outlet published “distorted content that negatively affects foreign investment, tourism, and diplomacy.”

The action is part of the government’s ongoing assault on the media and civic space in the country and points toward increasing use of vague claims about “national interests” to shut down any critical reporting.

Since 2025, Ethiopian authorities have banned numerous media outlets, deported foreign correspondents, and targeted local journalists through arbitrary arrests, enforced disappearances, and office raids. In October, undercover police detained journalists Samuel Getachew and Amanuel Gebremedhin, charging them with reporting news that would cause disruptions in Addis Ababa, the capital. The two were jailed for eight days.

The Reporter, one of the few remaining independent outlets in the country, hasn’t been spared. Authorities have arrested its journalists, while the outlet itself has been under government pressure. Days before the suspension, the Reporter published an editorial warning that Ethiopia’s privately owned press was “navigating one of its most precarious periods in history” and calling for immediate reforms to prevent its total collapse.

The Ethiopian government should heed the warning; the country’s concerned partners should urge the government to immediately reverse course.

For a non-peak period, a first bag in economy costs $50 if paid for more than a day ahead of the flight and $60 if paid inside that 24-hour window, according to CNBC. During peak periods such as the holidays and summer, the fee is $55 in advance and $70 within 24 hours, up from $49 for the advance fee.

Customers who carry JetBlue's co-branded credit card or have elite frequent flyer status are exempt from the fees, according to CNBC. JetBlue Mint and BlueFirst passengers also receive a minimum of one complimentary checked bag, according to Thrifty Traveler.

"We're adjusting checked bag fees to help offset rising fuel and operating costs," JetBlue said in a statement, according to CNBC. "This helps us keep our base fares competitive while giving customers the choice to pay for a checked bag only if they need one."

Dominic Fike Might Be Growing Up, But He’s Angsty as Ever

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Across How to Quit Smoking, it’s clear that Dominic Fike has mastered the art of a distinctively Dominic Fike sound. It’s in his distorted, sunburned guitars, in the quake of his voice as he sing-raps, in all of the Florida nostalgia baked into his lyrics. This is his third album, but it doesn’t feel so far removed from the 22-year-old kid who sparked a label war after uploading low-fi songs to SoundCloud in the late 2010s — and who went wildly viral for hits like “3 Nights” and “Babydoll,” the latter of which is still skyrocketing up the charts. The angst and anguish and the ability to come up with a damn good hook are still there, even if the pressures and the stress of adulthood have taken on aching new heights.

How to Quit Smoking finds Fike at 30, now a dad and still figuring a lot of things out. Throughout his past albums, he’s detailed pretty clearly what he’s gone through: Fike recorded his first EP while under house arrest for battery of a police officer; when it started blowing up, he was in jail for violating his parole. Once Columbia won the label bidding war and signed Fike for a reported $4 million, he used a lot of that money to bail his mom out of prison, where she was serving time on drug charges. (“Everybody looking at me like I’m not invested/Bitch, you know how hard I was stressing?” he raps on “How Much Is Weed,” the jittery track that opened 2023’s excellent Sunburn.)

He’s outlined his journey with sobriety and addiction in his music, all while dealing with loneliness and some highly public breakups. His lyrics dwell on the things that he misses or wishes had gone differently, and How to Quit Smoking circles the same existential anxieties as Fike tries to kick bad habits and own up to mistakes. “I don’t even go up the street for a cup of ice,” he sings on the surprisingly sunny “Pangea.” “If I fuck it up, I’m going back to the hood.” On the dreamy “Cabby,” with Emma Ogier and Love Spells, he admits, “I’m not sanctified, I’m not Jesus Christ/But if this wine was water, I’d feel fit to drive.” It’s especially thrilling to see him pull in different guests across the album; Ogier also appears on the bouncy “Small Town” with Gabriel Jacoby; Grace Ives hops onto the smoky “Honeylove.” Editor’s picks The 250 Greatest Albums of the 21st Century So Far The 100 Best TV Episodes of All Time The 500 Greatest Albums of All Time 100 Best Movies of the 21st Century

There’s a void as he tries to give up his vices, and Fike is trying to find anything that’ll fill it: past memories, late-night drives, relationships he knows didn’t work out. He’s struggling on “Casting Call,” trying to reconnect with anyone who can help him escape for a while. “I’m sobrie, I’m sobri- I’m sober for right now,” he stammers on the song, suggesting he’s anything but — yet he doesn’t go any further into his turmoil.
