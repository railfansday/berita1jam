FAA pauses installation of new routers after ground stop

📅 2026-11-17  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The Federal Aviation Administration said Tuesday it would pause the installation of new routers after problems with the technology led the agency to issue a rare nationwide ground stop on Friday.

Tens of thousands of flights were affected by the ground stop, which halted departing flights for more than four hours. It was the longest halt to U.S. flight departures since 9/11.

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said Sunday that an overloaded router installed in southern Arkansas as part of an upgrade to a system overseen by L3Harris Technologies appeared to overwhelm systems that feed radar and other data for air traffic control.

The Wall Street Journal first reported the news about the pause.

The FAA and Department of Transportation have said L3Harris was working to convert the communications network from copper wires to fiber optic.

Democratic members of the Senate Committee on Commerce, Science and Transportation this week asked the FAA and the Department of Transportation for clarity on what went wrong.

"Identifying the device that failed does not establish why it failed," the senators wrote in a statement. "Modernization cannot come at the expense of operational safety."

The agencies have said they are working to figure out the root cause of the issue and have reiterated that there is no indication of a cyberattack or outside threat.

The senators also asked for a briefing with the FAA, L3Harris and Juniper Networks, which is making the new devices, by Monday. They asked the companies to lay out why the operational systems lacked sufficient redundancy and answer other questions about what led to the outage.

CNBC has reached out to L3Harris and Juniper Networks for comment.

Duffy on Friday called for Congress to approve $50 billion in funding for upgrades to the aging U.S. air traffic control system, more than he has previously asked for. Democratic lawmakers pushed back on the request, citing unspent money from existing funding, which includes Congress' approval last year of $12.5 billion for airport and air traffic control improvements over five years.

Aging equipment and a shortage of air traffic controllers have for years angered airline executives, who have complained about the cost of resulting disruptions.

— CNBC's Phil LeBeau and Leslie Josephs contributed to this report.

Hospitals’ operations took another dip in September amid a slew of ongoing headwinds including weakened payer mix and rising costs, healthcare advisory firm Kaufman Hall reported alongside warnings of a “historically large portion” of the nation’s hospitals running on negative or near-zero operating margins.

The firm’s latest monthly benchmark report, released Tuesday, showed a calendar year-to-date operating margin index of 1.4% and a single-month operating margin index of 2.4% in September, the latter of which was a decline from August’s 3.0% operating margin (all including health system allocations for shared services costs).

The performance decline wasn’t for want of patients. Daily discharges and adjusted discharges both rose 3% from August to September, and are generally above the numbers from last year. Operating room minutes per calendar day also rose 6% from August—an encouraging sign for hospitals whose surgical slowdowns had hamstrung acuity—but remain 1% below September of last year.

The increased foot traffic also grew revenues, with daily net operating revenue rising 4% from August and 6% year over year while gross operating revenue rose 4% month to month and 8% year over year. Much of the increase came on the outpatient side, where daily revenue increased 5% from August and 10% compared to September 2025. Net patient service revenue per adjusted discharge was more mixed, rising 2% from August but remaining 1% below September 2025.

However, hospitals’ margins are being challenged by persistent bad debt and charity care burdens, Kaufman Hall’s data show. Daily bad debt and charity care may have remained flat from August, but is 17% higher than September 2025 and 16% higher when comparing both years’ first nine months. As a percentage of gross operating revenue, they’re 6% higher than Sept. 2025 and 8% higher year to date.

On the spending side, daily total expense rose 3% from August and is 5% higher than September 2025, primarily reflecting the heightened volumes even as hospitals held their average length of stay flat month over month or 1% shorter year over year. The expenses were more pronounced on the non-labor side, where increases on a per adjusted discharge basis were higher than labor’s per adjusted discharge decline.

"Hospitals continue to operate in an environment marked by significant financial variability," Erik Swanson, managing director and Data and Analytics group leader at Kaufman Hall, said in a release. “Many organizations continue to face mounting pressure from uncompensated care and rising expenses, underscoring the importance of proactive operational and financial strategies that build long-term resilience."

Another amplifying market trend is the “bifurcation of hospital performance” often highlighted by the firm and others that monitor multi-organization financial trends, such as credit rating agencies.

"The margin gap between higher and lower-performing hospitals has widened to 19 percentage points, with a historically large portion of hospitals operating at negative or very thin margins,” the report reads. “Even slight revenue reductions threaten organizations, which face increasingly difficult decisions on future operations, care delivery, and expense control.”

The firm encouraged hospitals to take “deliberate, proactive steps” to strengthen their financial situations, particularly as macro reimbursement trends stiffen and the site of care delivery shifts from the hospital to outpatient settings.

Kaufman Hall’s hospital reports pull data from more than 1,300 nationwide hospitals as collected by parent company Strata Decision Technology.

Accompanying the monthly hospital benchmark was also Kaufman Hall’s quarterly numbers on more than 200,000 employed physicians and advanced practice providers, which painted a much rosier picture for health system and hospital employers.

It showed, for the first time in five years of data collection, that healthcare employers’ investment/subsidy per provider and per physician has declined year over year (by 1%), and “across every cohort, including primary care, medical specialty, surgical specialty and hospital-based specialty.”

Kaufman Hall attributed the rare turnaround to revenue growth and sustained productivity increases that together outpaced expenses.

"Medical groups made meaningful performance improvements this quarter, achieving gains in revenue, productivity and thoughtful workforce management," Matthew Bates, managing director and Physician Enterprise Service Line leader at Kaufman Hall, said in a release. "The growing utilization of [advanced practice providers] and ongoing focus on operational and team effectiveness are helping organizations manage financial pressures while increasing access to care.”

An Idaho Fish and Game commissioner just made a deal to avoid poaching and trespassing charges related to an illegal elk hunt last season.

His guilty pleas are for hunting without a tag and shooting across the road, according to local news outlets. The dismissed charges include two for poaching, trespass, motorized aid and failure to retrieve game. Harshbarger was appointed to the commission in 2024. His sentencing for illegal activities in 2025 is scheduled for Dec. 9. With the new plea deal and the number of charges reduced, the fine is expected to be $500 plus a one-year loss of hunting privileges.

Cerebras Puts Disaggregated Inference Into Production With GPUs

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Cerebras Systems reported on November 17, 2026, that its production disaggregated inference deployment, which assigns prompt processing to AMD Instinct GPUs and response generation to CS-3 systems, delivered five times the throughput per megawatt of a CS-3-only configuration without reducing token-generation speed.

The result applies to Cerebras Inference Cloud. The measurement came from a shared GPT-OSS-120B endpoint on November 14, 2026, comparing throughput per megawatt before and after disaggregation was enabled and normalizing the aggregated configuration to a 1× baseline. Shifting prompt work to the GPUs leaves the CS-3 systems’ serving capacity available for generating responses, expanding the fast-inference capacity Cerebras can offer through its cloud.

The production disclosure follows Cerebras’ October 1, 2026 explanation of disaggregated inference, which reported a 5× gain in early tests with the same wafer-scale-engine footprint. The new announcement moves beyond that early result by supplying a production measurement and a deployment package for infrastructure partners.

Each inference request has two principal stages. Prefill processes the input prompt and creates intermediate model state; decode uses that state to produce output tokens one at a time, feeding each new token back into the model. Because every decode step reads model data from memory again, memory bandwidth is central to interactive generation speed. Prefill instead performs more computation across the prompt in parallel, making it a better match for accelerators suited to that compute load.

Cerebras’ wafer-scale systems place model data in on-chip SRAM rather than relying primarily on off-chip high-bandwidth memory. Cerebras describes its wafer-scale chip as carrying tens of gigabytes of SRAM and tens of petabytes per second of memory bandwidth—350× the SRAM capacity and 5,400× the memory bandwidth of a typical GB300 GPU. That capacity allows more concurrent requests and model layers to remain directly on the chip for rapid decode.

The economic target is more customer-serving capacity and revenue potential from the same power budget in power-constrained data centers. Cerebras cites Muse, Copilot and Codex as examples of rapidly scaling agentic applications that require highly interactive responses, so the capacity increase must preserve generation speed to be useful for those workloads.

Infrastructure partners can use a Cerebras-provided reference architecture to co-locate their chosen prefill accelerators and Cerebras systems on a unified network. They then have two integration routes: an end-to-end software stack intended to shorten time to market, or a decode-only SDK for partners that want more control.

The full stack routes requests between prefill and decode pools and manages replica capacity and health. Its prefill engine can run open-source inference engines such as vLLM or SGLang, while Cerebras supplies the decode engine. KV Portal transfers intermediate model state between the two engines over RDMA, including the key-value caches that preserve request-specific context after prefill.

With the decode-only option, partners retain their own inference framework and prefill engine while using Cerebras’ decode engine and KV Portal. The SDK also includes a mock decode replica and a reference integration with an open-source disaggregated inference framework and prefill engine. Amazon Web Services, Fireworks AI, Gimlet Labs and General Compute are named as early-access partners, and Cerebras is expanding access to additional partners.

Cerebras plans to deploy disaggregated inference broadly across hundreds of megawatts in its own inference cloud and partner deployments by the end of 2027. That is a deployment target, not capacity already operating.

The announcement therefore combines a measured cloud configuration with a software path for outside operators. The central scaling question is whether the throughput advantage measured on one shared endpoint can be reproduced across partners’ chosen accelerators, frameworks and deployment sizes as Cerebras pursues that broader rollout.

Vertiv Expands 800 VDC Portfolio for AI Compute Pods and Data Halls

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Vertiv on November 17, 2026 introduced the PowerDirect 9000 and PowerNexus MV SST, extending 800-volt direct-current (800 VDC) power beyond individual racks to AI compute pods and data halls. The PowerNexus is a 5 MW solid-state transformer that converts medium-voltage alternating current (AC) to 800 VDC in one stage.

The additions respond to rising AI power requirements by giving operators three places to set the AC-to-DC conversion boundary: beside an IT rack, ahead of a pod, or at the data hall’s medium-voltage connection. Vertiv expects AC and DC architectures to coexist, with no single adoption timetable. The accompanying architecture portfolio combines conversion, storage, distribution, protection, controls and monitoring, allowing 800 VDC to be introduced without committing an entire facility to one design.

The rack-level option remains the PowerDirect 5000, introduced in May 2026 for deployments that retain upstream AC infrastructure. It sits beside an IT rack and accepts 480 VAC, with 800 VDC or ±400 VDC output; Vertiv’s rating puts it at up to 800 kW, with battery backup designed for at least 50 seconds of hold-up. Moving conversion and storage outside the IT rack frees 8 to 16 rack units for compute, while using one power rack for one or two IT racks helps isolate faults and simplify maintenance.

Inside the compute rack, the 1U PowerDirect 3000 shelf converts 800 VDC to 54 VDC for compute trays. The 90 kW unit delivers up to 98.5% peak efficiency and 180% pulse power for 0.5 milliseconds in Vertiv’s specifications. The same shelf connects to the pod-level design, creating a common final conversion stage at the rack.

For a row or pod, the new PowerDirect 9000 consolidates conversion into one system ahead of the racks. The power center converts 480 VAC to 800 VDC, is rated by Vertiv at 1.5 MW and scales in 60 kW increments through 27 hot-pluggable power-supply units. PowerBar Track DC busway carries power overhead to the shelves, with a metered, breaker-protected drop at every rack.

EnergyCore Li5 battery cabinets connect directly to the DC bus. Each cabinet is rated by Vertiv at 200 kW of continuous backup and up to 98% conversion efficiency, with as many as six operating in parallel. Consolidating conversion, backup and distribution removes the need for a power rack beside every IT rack, recovers row space and supports mixed AC and DC loads, including colocation sites with different tenants, workloads and GPU generations.

At data-hall level, the PowerNexus’s outdoor, liquid-cooled container is rated by Vertiv at up to 98% AC-to-DC efficiency. It eliminates two to three intermediate conversion steps in conventional power trains, provides galvanic isolation between input and output, and can support a 4.8 MW super-pod reference architecture without chaining. Moving conversion outdoors can recover indoor space for compute, while fewer stages help reduce cumulative electrical losses.

When paired with external energy storage through a DC/DC converter on the 800 VDC bus, the system can ride through electrical faults and smooth AI workload fluctuations, according to Vertiv. Bidirectional power flow can improve grid power quality and provide active power support, while battery communication ports coordinate storage with rapid load changes. The containerized design also supports faster deployment and standardized replication across campuses.

Safety features across the portfolio include touch-proof busbars, high-voltage isolation, midpoint grounding, ground-fault detection and arc-flash prevention. The accompanying service offerings are purpose-built for DC operating environments, rather than adapted from legacy AC procedures, according to Vertiv.

Vertiv is showcasing the portfolio at SC26 from November 17 to 22, 2026, with pilot programs, technical validation and customer-specific collaborative development underway. These options let operators match the conversion boundary to the facility: retain AC infrastructure during a rack retrofit, consolidate power around a pod, or move medium-voltage conversion outdoors for a campus. The choice extends beyond voltage, because each architecture also determines where backup, fault isolation and conversion losses are managed.

Uncontacted Indigenous tribe reaches out for help in the Brazilian Amazon

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Published Nov 17, 2026 2:08 PM EST

Around the world, the number of uncontacted Indigenous tribes continues to dwindle. Indigenous rights advocates estimate that half of the nearly 200 communities still existing in comparative isolation could disappear within a decade, without proper safeguards against climate change, land loss, fame-seeking opportunists, and illegal industrial encroachments. Unfortunately, the situation has apparently grown so dire that some Indigenous groups are reaching out for help—and exposing themselves to potentially deadly foreign diseases in the process.

On November 17, the nonprofit advocacy group Survival International reported a recent incident in which 20 members of the Awá people intentionally contacted local government officials in the Brazilian Amazon. Although proficient hunter-gatherers, the group requested yams before taking them back into the rainforest.

“This is a troubling indication of how the continued invasion of uncontacted peoples’ forests puts otherwise self-sufficient Indigenous people in real danger,” Survival International director Caroline Pearce said in a statement.

The Awá are one of the world’s last uncontacted Indigenous tribes, whose ties to the Brazilian rainforest stretch back centuries. They faced their first attacks from European settlers during the 1800s, which eventually forced them to adopt a nomadic lifestyle to avoid future encounters. Brazil finally supplied the Awá with settlement areas during the mid-1980s, but it still took years before the government properly invested in demarcated land. In the meantime, numerous deadly interactions with loggers, farmers, and other illegal intruders had reduced the Awá community to barely 300 people. Of those, advocates estimate only about one-third of the hunter-gatherers remain completely isolated from the outside world.

“The Brazilian government must commit the resources necessary to protect [the region of] Arariboia from the loggers and ranchers who are razing it, in flagrant defiance of the law,” Pearce added.

In addition to the advocacy work by Survival International, the Awá are supported by a team of Indigenous members of the Guajajara community known as the Forest Guardians. These volunteers have spent years pushing back against illegal cattle ranchers and loggers attempting to claim land in Arariboia, often dying as a result of their efforts.

“The Forest Guardians have always worked to protect the Awá against encroachment, even at the cost of losing warriors in battle,” the organization said. “We demand immediate action from the relevant authorities…to ensure what the Awá believe is best for them, safeguarding their lives, health, safety and dignity. HeyGen Launches Avatar 6 with Script-Aware Gestures and 2K Video

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

HeyGen introduced Avatar 6, its latest audio-driven avatar model, in a technical report published November 17, 2026. The model generates video of a specific person from a single photograph, with an optional short identity recording, and HeyGen reports native 2K output, no fixed duration limit and availability in its web app, mobile app and API from mid-November 2026.

Avatar 6 is designed to reproduce a person’s appearance, manner of speaking and habits of motion together. Its in-house system combines the generator, a super-resolution stage that restores details such as skin texture and hair, a motion planner and HeyGen Voice for speech in the speaker’s own voice. Customers can choose Avatar 6 Quality or Avatar 6 Turbo, the latter using HeyGen’s accelerated inference stack for lower latency.

On the product page, HeyGen says Avatar 6 uses a script’s meaning to decide where pointing, counting or directional movement fits. Users can separately specify mood, facial expression or hand movement for finer control. Written motion prompts can attach an action to a particular phrase, such as waving during a greeting or counting on the fingers through a list. The motion planner times those actions to the relevant words while preserving the subject’s characteristic movement between them.

HeyGen’s demonstrations include continuous videos lasting 25 to 28 minutes, each generated from one audio track without loops, so the running time follows the speech. The same framework also produces two speakers in one frame; one demonstration is a two-minute host-and-guest podcast that alternates wide shots with closer views of each person.

Avatar 6’s recorded-video editor can replace words or add a gesture while regenerating only the edited span, with new speech synthesized in the person’s voice. In HeyGen’s 20-edit comparison, 34.4% of frames changed on average, while MiniMax H3-max, Wan 3.0, Seedance 2.5 and HappyHorse Edit altered 99.7% to 100%. That preservation of the surrounding take is useful for correcting a finished presenter recording without recreating the entire shot.

GitHub reported on November 17, 2026, that a security pilot with Microsoft produced 55 fixes across six open source projects as of November 15, 2026. The Codename MDASH trial filtered 9,791 possible issues into 99 private reports, each checked by researchers and accompanied by a proof of concept and suggested fix.

Fourteen of the fixes had already been included in releases. The projects were AutoGPT, Caddy, Express, FastMCP, ImageMagick and Netty. Maintainers decided which findings to fix, how to fix them and when to ship, with reports and patches staying on GitHub.

The design addresses a maintenance problem created by cheaper AI scanning: duplicates and reports that are not actual vulnerabilities already consume maintainer time, while remediation still requires human judgment. GitHub wants researchers to consult maintainers before pointing another model at their projects and to submit patches alongside any findings.

Microsoft’s Codename MDASH system scanned each project with its MAI Cyber model and suggested fixes. A GitHub Security Lab AI triage agent, built on tailored Copilot skills, removed duplicates, checked validity and focused on high-impact bugs. Seven Microsoft FORGE Lab researchers then manually checked every remaining finding before reports went to maintainers through GitHub private vulnerability reporting.

The first fixes shipped about a week after the initial scan, according to GitHub. Caddy’s maintainers fixed all 22 reports they received in a week or less. ImageMagick, maintained by two people, also received 22 reports; in one case, its maintainers pushed a fix in 15 minutes after a summary identified the exact lines of code and showed that an apparently familiar issue was different.

At AutoGPT, a report arrived three days after maintainers shipped a broken check and pointed to the right line. They took the fix further than the suggested patch, wrote it within a day and had it in production less than a week later. Across the pilot, maintainers used some suggested fixes as written and adapted others.

A Netty maintainer described the remaining constraint: “Finding vulnerabilities is no longer our bottleneck. Fixing them is the hard part, and that depends on maintainers.”

GitHub described the project sample as small, the conclusions as early lessons and the manual review as not yet scalable; researchers spent about two days per project checking findings by hand. Low-quality reports still reach maintainers every day.

GitHub also identified private-fork CI/CD testing and maintainer access to cyber-capable models as remaining needs. The tests would verify fixes on private forks, and model access would help maintainers verify a proof of concept before and after remediation. Its recommended standard for AI-generated reports is to check each finding against the project’s existing knowledge, threat model and security policy, report privately, include a suggested fix and let maintainers set the pace.

GitHub plans to bring these lessons into private vulnerability reporting and security advisories. It will offer AI security scans with findings checked before private reporting, plus GitHub Copilot Max, to all alumni projects of the GitHub Secure Open Source Fund and extend the support to future Fund sessions. GitHub and Microsoft are also working to expand researcher review while preserving those checks.

For AI-assisted vulnerability disclosure, the pilot’s trade-off is concrete: automation can reduce a flood of raw findings to a manageable private queue, while expert review and maintainer control determine whether those reports lead to patches. Expansion hinges on preserving that standard without returning the triage burden to maintainers.

How reverse mortgage LOs can help borrowers to self-service

Reverse mortgage servicers are investing in borrower portals, mobile apps and smarter phone systems to reduce call volumes. Celink is launching ReverseLink soon, while Onity is expanding digital draw requests and emphasizing closing checklist steps for loan officers.  AI Summary

Reverse mortgage servicers are investing in portals, mobile applications and smarter phone systems so borrowers can manage their loans without contacting a human customer service representative. Servicing executives say that loan officers can play a key role in whether these tools actually work.

Gail Balettie, senior vice president of client satisfaction at Celink, and Ryan McIntire, director of reverse mortgage servicing controls at Onity Mortgage, outlined their companies’ self-service offerings during a panel discussion moderated by Chris Downey of CrossCountry Mortgage at last week’s National Reverse Mortgage Lenders Association (NRMLA) Annual Meeting & Expo.

“In today’s world, where we are conditioned to some extent to be able to go online, get immediate satisfaction, to do things without talking to somebody, the more we can do that, the better we can reach our customers,” McIntire said.

Balettie announced at NRMLA Annual that her company’s mobile app, ReverseLink, will be launching “in the next few weeks.” Its design is similar to Celink’s website and borrowers will receive an invitation to download it. The company is the largest independent subservicer of reverse mortgages and has an estimated market share of about 75% across federally insured Home Equity Conversion Mortgages (HECMs) and proprietary loans.

McIntire told the audience that Onity‘s servicing app is already available on iPhone and Android devices and mirrors its borrower portal. He stressed the importance of providing familiar technology to senior homeowners so “they don’t feel like they’re having to learn something all over again.”

Celink’s soon-to-be-released app will allow clients to accomplish multiple tasks, Balettie said. They’ll be able to request payoff quotes, make voluntary payments, upload forms and download up to two years of statements. The app automatically generates confirmation that an uploaded form has been received, cutting down the number of calls needed for this purpose.

“They can request a line-of-credit draw, and if they do so, they’re going to get their funds a day or two faster than the typical five-day turn time, because it comes in an automated fashion,” she added.

ReverseLink also has a number of FAQs and educational videos, covering subjects like how to manage the account online, what happens to the reverse mortgage when the borrower dies, how to shop for insurance coverage and how to file a claim, and how to find help with small home repairs.

Onity’s portal ties into the company’s system of record, McIntire said. It shows three months of transactions and includes an introduction package that’s tailored to the structure of the loan — for example, any funds set aside for required repairs. Onity is also in the process of building fully digital draw request forms. Today, borrowers have to download and complete these forms before returning it via email or fax, but the goal is to process e-signed draw requests within a few business days, McIntire said.

“Obviously, information that’s available to the borrower via this site is very sensitive. We obviously, as servicers, have to be very cognizant and mindful of fraud. So it does have multifactor authentication that just helps provide that additional sense of security,” he added.

Borrowers commonly contact their loan officer, rather than their servicer, when they have questions about their loan. Balettie said that LOs can take an “ABCD” role to approach to providing assistance in this area.

Making sure a servicer has the correct contact information for a borrower may seem obvious, but it doesn’t happen with perfect uniformity. “We get about 70% of our loans that board with an email address. I want to see that be 100%,” Balettie said. “I don’t know very many people who don’t have an email address in this day and age.”

Balettie sought to distinguish between the alternate contact on the loan application and an authorized third party. The latter may discuss the account but cannot change contact information or request a draw or payoff. Conversely, servicers use the alternate contact only when they cannot reach the borrower, she said.

In response to an audience question, Balettie said that loan officers are welcome to call a servicer on their client’s behalf, but to receive a relevant response, they must be listed as the authorized third party. This is especially important for brokers, who lose access to information once a loan is sold. “We have privacy laws we have to follow,” she added.

A power of attorney can act for a living borrower, but legal authority ends at death, the panelists noted. Downey urged originators to execute the authorized party form at closing and warned that without it, payoffs for a borrower “can be a nightmare.” Having this information on file removes the need to hire an attorney to search for heirs after the borrower dies.

Balettie said originators should collect a canceled check and the direct deposit form at closing. Borrowers without a check can provide a redacted bank statement or bank letter with their account and routing numbers. Due to security concerns, servicers don’t allow bank details to be added online, but they’ll accept faxed or emailed documents, and closing is the best time to gather them.

Securing Your Agents: A Hands-On Guide to Agent Anomaly Detection

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

As highlighted in our announcement post, we said that each new model generation lets agents do more on their own. However, the more an agent can reach, the more one bad decision can cost. You've probably seen a few of these in the news lately:

Because these events produced no system crashes or logging errors, the teams behind these agents had no way to know until the damage occurred.

Agent Anomaly Detection on the Gemini Platform watches for exactly these issues. It reads the traces and logs your agents already produce and flags sessions where an agent goes outside the job it was built for.

Before you point it at your production agents, you'll probably want to see it work in a test environment. In this hands-on guide, we’ll walk you step-by-step through setting up Agent Anomaly Detection and using it to capture your very first agent behavior anomaly. By the end of this post, you'll have:

To get started, open the companion notebook Detect anomalous agent behavior with Agent Anomaly Detection in a separate tab (available on Google Colab or GitHub). As you read through this guide, it will prompt you when to execute notebook cells or interact directly with the Google Cloud console.

If a step can be done in the UI console, this post shows that path, with screenshots. The notebook does the same thing through the Agent Anomaly Detection API, so by the end you'll also have the code to automate enablement and pull findings into your own tooling.

2. A place to run the notebook: Google Colab, Colab Enterprise, or a local Jupyter environment. The notebook installs the latest ADK for Python itself. Agent Anomaly Detection is compatible with agents running ADK 1.2+, though ADK 2.0+ is preferred for best results.

3. A Cloud Logging bucket and an Observability dataset in either US or EU multi-region. (Both resources need to be in the same multi-region)

4. In the notebook: run the Get started cells. They install the packages, authenticate you, take your project ID, and turn on the four APIs that Agent Anomaly Detection needs.

First, let’s create a Customer Support Agent called “anomaly-detection-refund-demo”. Our example agent is responsible for helping customers check their order history and request refunds. However, it has a deliberate flaw: the agent has access to an overprivileged fetch_records tool which can read any table, including a `payroll` table containing sensitive employee data. All three parts of the lethal trifecta are here: the agent can read private data, it takes input from people it can't vet, and it sends what it finds back in its reply. Tool permissions and guardrails should stop most of this, but they only block what someone thought to write down. Agent Anomaly Detection flags the sessions that get past them, usually within a few minutes.

The notebook has the full agent. The part that matters is the tool, which never checks the table name against an allowlist:

All data is canned and in memory, so nothing real is read or refunded. This tool is intended to be over-permissive to illustrate AAD functionality. Don't copy this tool into production.

The Step 1 cells deploy the agent to Agent Runtime. Two details in the deploy cell are what make the agent visible to Agent Anomaly Detection. First, it sets three environment variables on the deployment, which turn on telemetry and write prompt and response content to Cloud Logging, where detection looks for it:

Second, it sends the agent one test message right after deploying. Agent Anomaly Detection discovers agents through their telemetry, so an agent that has never served a request does not appear under Monitored agents.

The deployment takes about 5 to 10 minutes and prints the agent's resource name and the reply to the test message:

For more information on how to create and deploy an agent, take a look at these guides: Creating an Agent and Deploying an Agent.

In the console: The sample code we provide already enables logging and telemetry when deploying the agent. Verify this by navigating to the Agent Deployments page (Gemini Platform > Agents > Deployments) and clicking “Configure” next to the recently created anomaly-detection-refund-demo agent to open the Observability Service Configuration.

Great! You now have a working agent that is successfully configured and ready to be monitored by Agent Anomaly Detection.

Now let’s set up Agent Anomaly Detection to monitor your agent’s traffic.

In the console: navigate to the Agent Platform > Agents > Audit page and click “Enable”.

Choose your agent’s Logging Bucket and Observability Dataset, making sure they are in the same region (us-multi-region or eu-multi-region) enable “Automatically monitor new agents”, then click “Agree and continue” and Agent Anomaly Detection will be set up in your project.

If you'd rather do this in code, the first Step 2 cell in the notebook creates the same configuration through the API, using the default _Default bucket and _Trace dataset with automatic monitoring on.

Setup takes around 30 minutes. Once it’s done loading you’re ready to proceed to the next step! Because you enabled automatic monitoring and the agent already served a request in Step 1, setup picks it up right away. Check that it shows as Active under Settings > Monitored agents before you continue; sessions that start earlier aren't analyzed.

Now that the agent is being monitored, let's see Agent Anomaly Detection in action! The Step 3 cells send one normal conversation and one anomalous conversation, each in its own session. The normal conversation is a customer listing their orders and getting a refund for a broken lamp. The anomalous conversation simulates a user who finds the fetch_records flaw and uses it to pull payroll data:

Notice that the user brings almost nothing: a customer ID and one first name. Asking for a table that doesn't exist makes the agent reveal the existing tables, which is how they learn there is a payroll table within reach.

The notebook prints both transcripts with the agent's tool calls, and the session IDs you'll look up next.

Anomaly Detection will scan the sessions within 5 minutes. In the landing page, you should see that a couple sessions have been processed and one anomalous session detected.

Let’s take a look at the anomalous session in more detail. Click the session id to open the detail view or paste the anomalous session id into the search bar.

In our run, the top finding was Tool misuse at High severity, with 95% probability. Your detectors and severities may differ, because the models behind the analysis are non-deterministic. The recommended solution is to restrict the `fetch_records` tool to read from specific tables.

Click an invocation in the Session pane to see its analysis in Details. Each invocation is Medium on its own, but together they make the session High.

To stay informed without watching the console, connect Agent Anomaly Detection to your existing alerting workflows.

In Cloud Monitoring: Agent Anomaly Detection publishes two metrics: Detected events, which counts anomalies by detector and severity, and Monitored agents, which tracks your detection coverage. To view these metrics, click the arrow next to "View in Cloud Monitoring" on the Anomaly detection page and select a metric to open it in Metrics Explorer.

To be notified of new anomalies, go to Monitoring > Alerting, click "Create policy", and select the Detected events metric. Leave the filters empty so the alert covers every anomaly, including the demo's, then set the rolling window to 5 minutes with the delta function, and set the threshold to above 0. Once you know what's normal for your agents, add a severity filter (for example High or Critical) so you're only alerted on the findings that matter. A metric-absence policy on Monitored agents will also alert you if monitoring stops.

In Security Command Center: Each anomaly is also published as an AGENT_SESSION_ANOMALY finding in the Security Command Center if it is enabled for your organization. Click "View in Security Command Center" to triage these findings alongside your other threats, use "Set security marks" to tag and track them, and set up notifications for high-severity findings.

You’ve now seen Agent Anomaly Detection in action: it successfully flagged the session where our agent went off-script to read payroll records, while evaluating the benign session, and correctly not flagging an anomaly. The finding told you why it was flagged and how to fix the underlying issue, and alerts in Cloud Monitoring and Security Command Center ensure you are notified of future anomalies.
