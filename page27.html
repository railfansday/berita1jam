Terrific fantasy RPG Queen's Domain gets first proper update, adding a new throwing weapon and options to make puzzles "less frustrating"

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Extremely good King's Field-style RPG Queen's Domain – or is it a Metroid Prime homage? Or a stealth Morrowind-up? We hacks simply cannot decide – has received its first proper patch, adding a hitherto "missing" throwing weapon, the Grave Knife, alongside a bunch of quality-of-life fixes.

They've stirred in the ability to disable screenshake, alongside some new icons to make status effects more obvious. They've also introduced "options to make some late game puzzles less frustrating", though they don't specify which. How frustrating! Still, it's a tidy overhaul for a game that has rapidly become one of my 2026 favourites.

The big mystery with the update is what the new Grave Knife does. You'll find it inside the Tower of Knowledge, once you've managed to unlock passage. As for those overly aggravating late game puzzles – I find the toughest part of puzzling in Queen's Domain is often just spotting switches and key objects, so perhaps it's something to do with that?

True, there's a large selection of skeleton tableaus who comically point the way to your next objectives, but the game's pixel aesthetics can be rather dense. I very much approve of this on the whole. I like how organic and tumbledown the environment feels - the repeated delight of realising that, yes, you can get over there, and wow, there's a whole other level down here.

Queen's Domain has earned a lot of happy reactions, and is a solid commercial hit. Developers Freshly Baked say it has sold over 100,000 copies, with Vertical Slice reporting that they've already broken even. Glad tidings indeed. Find the full patch notes below, via Steam.

British Airways $710 Million Tax Fight Could Leave Members With Less Valuable Points

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

British Airways’ parent has €638 million tied up in a fight over taxing Avios. The government wants 20% value added tax when the points are issued. For members, that creates a risk of fewer points for their spending, weaker bonuses or more expensive redemptions (even if the tax authority never sends them a bill). IAG’s November 6 financial results update a dispute with the British tax authority over how its loyalty business should have been taxed going back to March 2018. While IAG is the legal taxpayer, a tax on the business selling your rewards can reduce what those rewards deliver. And VAT is something ultimately passed onto the consumer but collected throughout the value chain. What Britain Wants To Tax IAG historically accounted for value added tax based on what members redeemed their Avios for. Most redemptions were flights attracting a zero rate. The tax authority’s position is that IAG Loyalty is being paid to develop, administer and maintain a loyalty program. On that interpretation, the issuance of Avios attracts 20% tax regardless of what the member eventually books. IAG’s more detailed half-year disclosure says its treatment relied on an earlier ruling from the same tax authority and existing case law. It’s challenging the assessment and whether the government can reverse that treatment retrospectively. And the November filing identifies two amounts: €516 million of historical payments, net of input-tax recoveries, recorded as an asset IAG expects to recover if it wins. €122 million of subsequent payments disclosed as a contingent asset. Some of these amounts reduce the revenue IAG ultimately recognizes from Avios. Together that’s €638 million (US$710 million). IAG expects a tax tribunal hearing during the remainder of 2026 and a decision in 2027. Members Can Pay Without Receiving A Tax Bill Suppose a bank buys Avios to give customers for using its credit card. If supplying those Avios becomes more expensive, several things can happen: IAG receives less after tax. Existing contracts may prevent it from immediately charging partners more, leaving shareholders with lower profits. The bank pays more. It can absorb that cost, buy fewer Avios, reduce earning or initial bonuses, raise card fees, or steer spending toward a different rewards product. The member gets less from the points. Changes to award prices, cash charges or redemption availability can lower the cost of honoring rewards—but also make the currency less attractive to collect. IAG’s disclosure flags payments it doesn’t consider recoverable from commercial partners. That leaves IAG holding those costs initially. But it forces a reevaluation prospectively of those agreements, or revaluing the points themselves. In other words taxing the creation of rewards makes it more expensive to offer the same rewards. Mikel Arteta insists that Arsenal would have no issue with William Saliba being called up to the France squad.

Saliba has not played a single minute of football for either club or country so far this season, having been on the sidelines with a back injury since he was forced off in the World Cup semi-final defeat by Spain in July.

The centre-back has stepped up his recovery in recent weeks and on Wednesday made his first return to Arsenal’s matchday squad.

Dolly Parton's Estate Drops Lawsuit Against Her Nephew Bryan Seaver

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

More than a month after Dolly Parton's estate She's Alive LLC filed a lawsuit against her nephew Bryan Seaver, the company run by her longtime manager Danny Nozell requested to dismiss the case.

Dolly Parton's estate is clocking out of the family drama.

More than a month after nephew Bryan Seaver was hit with a lawsuit from She's Alive LLC—a company run by Dolly's longtime manager Danny Nozell, established to protect her businesses after her August death—the organization filed to dismiss the suit, according to court documents obtained by The New York Times and People.

The Nov. 5 notice of voluntary dismissal with the Chancery Court of Tennessee was filed without prejudice, so her estate could refile if it chooses.

Following the dismissal filing, Seaver's attorney Dominic J. Leonardo emphasized that his client's love for Parton—who tapped her nephew to announce her death at age 80—was there each step of the way.

"Mr. Seaver cares deeply about his Aunt Dolly, her music, her family, and the incredible legacy she left behind," he said in a statement to TMZ. "He has made a career out of protecting his aunt while she was alive and remains committed to Dolly and his entire family."

"Mr. Seaver is grateful to put this misunderstanding behind him," the message continued, "so that the entire family can continue to grieve peacefully and ensure that the bright light of Dolly's legacy remains entirely untouched."

E! News has reached out to Leonardo for comment and has not yet heard back.

Seaver—who was fired from his longtime role shortly before the lawsuit and an additional temporary restraining order was filed and granted—was accused of embarking on an "obscene, deliberate, and escalating campaign of threats" before and after Parton's death to get money from her estate, according to the September lawsuit obtained by E! News.

In one alleged instance detailed in the suit, her former head of security threatened to ruin a Dollywood business relationship. The message purportedly read, "I'm going to destroy the entire brand if you don't get these [Dollywood business partners] out of our bit."

Another exchange allegedly sent after the "Jolene" singer's death appeared to show Seaver threatening to start a podcast "dedicated to ruining Dolly's brand partnerships and telling the violations" people "have done on us."

"It's going to be great," Seaver purportedly wrote to Nozell. "Or f--king pay me."

Meanwhile, the temporary restraining order was granted, in which he was barred from contacting and coming within 1,000 feet of She's Alive employees and business partners, as well as entering company properties or interfering with business relationships

In response to the request made by She's Alive to extend the restraining order in October, Seaver vehemently denied the accusations against him, though he acknowledged using harsh language in his interactions.

"They were sent in the course of private conversations between two friends," he argued in the October affidavit obtained by CBS News, "who were both frustrated, grieving, and discussing strategy following Ms. Parton's illness and death."

He alleged the messages were not threats but rather a "private warning” about potential associates wanting to harm Parton's brand.

"I have declined to speak with the media about this case and have asked my attorneys to handle all media communications," Seaver concluded in the doc. "I care about Ms. Parton, her music, her legacy, and her family. I do not want this dispute to harm her legacy."

What To Do If The Unlearn Button Is Missing In WoW: Forever

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

World of Warcraft: Forever lets you learn up to two primary professions at a time, but you aren't stuck with your first choice. Maybe you picked Mining and Herbalism to earn gold selling materials at the auction house while leveling, only to decide later you'd rather craft your own gear or potions.

Fortunately, you can unlearn a profession to make room for a new one. Before switching, though, it's worth knowing where to find the option and what happens to your progress. We also encountered an issue with the unlearn button, but found a workaround that fixed it for us.

You don't need to visit a trainer to unlearn a profession.

Once you confirm, that profession is removed, and its slot becomes available. You can then visit a profession trainer to learn a new profession.

However, unlearning a profession resets your skill progress and removes the recipes you've unlocked. If you take up that profession again later, your previous progress won't return automatically, and you'll have to relearn its recipes. This applies to both purchased and looted recipes.

Only primary professions count toward the two-profession limit. Cooking, Fishing, and First Aid are secondary professions, and you can learn all three without using either primary slot.

A World of Warcraft Forever player has taken matters into their own hands after the flood of new Olympus guilds overseen by Asmongold.

We encountered an issue where the red X appeared as a blank space in the Professions window. The Unlearn tooltip appeared next to the blank space when we hovered over it, but we couldn't click the button. Changing the UI Scale in our game fixed the issue and let us unlearn professions again.

Unlearning a profession in World of Warcraft: Forever only takes a few steps, and adjusting the UI Scale may help if the red X isn't appearing. Just double-check your choice before confirming. You can always learn the profession again, but your previous skill progress and recipes won't automatically return.
