The Mauricio Pochettino blueprint and one non-negotiable to fix broken Man United

With empathetic man-management, a commitment to attacking football and hard-running, and a desire to be involved in recruitment, Pochettino has arrived at Old Trafford with a plan, writes Richard Jolly

Want to bookmark your favourite articles and stories to read or reference later? Start your Independent Membership today.

Mauricio Pochettino is the manager Manchester United could, time and again, have appointed in the past. He marked his eventual arrival at Old Trafford by trying to shrug it off; theirs more than his. Pochettino made it to the last two in 2022, when Erik ten Hag was instead chosen, and may have seemed United’s second choice in 2016 and 2019, when Jose Mourinho and Ole Gunnar Solskjaer were preferred. But by pivoting to the future was a way of avoiding the issue of why none of them – nor David Moyes, Louis van Gaal, Ralf Rangnick, Ruben Amorim or Michael Carrick – has truly succeeded.

Pochettino’s plan to fix United may draw on a more distant past, the identity he described that seemed to stem more from Sir Alex Ferguson than his eight immediate successors, but reliant heavily on a positivity about the future. “I don't think too much in the past. I think if we want to create something different and positive and the people feel proud about that, it’s to build our own way.”

His first target, he smiled, was to sleep; unsurprising given that he had a transatlantic night flight after his final game in charge of the United States, in Cincinnati, on Tuesday. A coach who long coveted the United job is relishing a return to club football, away from the stop-start nature of the international game. “I think we are animals of the grass,” he said.

As United are found in the relegation zone, without a league win since August, the temptation is to say his new charges might not be. But Pochettino is seeking to bring empathetic man-management, drawing on his record of making footballers better and fashion change; the hint was that his football will be different from Carrick’s even if a mid-season appointment facing a packed fixture list may have to compromise at first. “Our job is to adapt,” he said.

But it was notable that he said: “We arrived with the idea to create something from scratch, from our own ideas and philosophy, in the way that we love to work. Like always, we need to improve our players.”

And always Pochettino teams have run, normally further and faster than most. Maybe it was unsurprising, then, that he said: “I think the training session was a little bit longer than it normally is in [matchday] minus two.” Yet the United team he inherited have tended to be outrun; only Sunday’s opponents Liverpool have covered less distance in the Premier League this season.

Pochettino argued the statistics do not tell the whole story. “We cannot talk about things without context, and data is about context,” he said. “If you don't put context on the data, I think it's very dangerous to talk and I think it's easy to manipulate the data, but it's true that that sometimes data shows something that you can see on the field. But we are going to be very focused on trying to improve our data, our numbers in every single area of the field.”

Other figures that need an upgrade are the numbers of wins, a mere one, the defensive record, the third worst, the league position of 18th and the numbers that have contributed to United’s worst league start in four decades. If all of that is an indictment of an underachieving squad, Pochettino countered: “The commitment from all the players that we were chatting with was amazing from day one.” Whether all his predecessors would concur with that is another matter, but Pochettino spent three hours in his office on Thursday, talking to them individually. One decision has been made already: he will retain Bruno Fernandes as captain.

“The important thing is to find a way, through our experience, to create that platform for them to express their talent,” Pochettino said. There have been times in the last 13 years when the United players have appeared intimidated rather than liberated. The burden of playing for the club has been great. Pochettino wants to turn the pressure and expectation into a positive. “We need to transform that pressure into energy,” he added.

And he wants them to attack. Carrick bowed out with two games that felt throwbacks, the 3-2 win over Roma and the 4-4 draw with Aston Villa. In the seven goals they scored in five days, Pochettino may have something to build on. “We found a group of players that we proposed to do the football that we feel: being brave, being Manchester United,” he said.

One of the questions of the post-Ferguson era is what being Manchester United really is now. Pochettino has his answer. “I think the culture of the club and the identity of the club is very clear,” he said. “It's an historic club with amazing years competing and being one of the best clubs in the world. It's difficult to find another club the size of club that is Manchester United.”

Certainly there is not one of a similar size in a relegation zone. That is attributable in part to bad recruitment. If hiring Pochettino may change that, a manager with a two-and-a-half year contract wants an input on transfers. “In the future, I would like to be involved in all the main decisions that will happen on the squad,” he said.

That seems crucial in his long-term plan. The short-term part starts at Anfield on Sunday for a manager who is short of sleep but high on optimism.

SmileBasic 4, one of the key pieces of software being used to jailbreak Switch 2 consoles, is back on the Nintendo eShop. While the console's firmware has long since been updated to stop known jailbreak exploits, SmileBasic 4 itself has now also been patched.

SmileBasic is a line of programming tools which developer SmileBoom has released for various Nintendo platforms, allowing users to code their own simple programs and games. It's also opened multiple backdoors for jailbreak exploits over the years, first on 3DS, and then on Switch 2.

Technically, SmileBasic 4 is a Switch 1 game, but AI-backed hackers found a way to use it in order to jailbreak Switch 2 consoles, allowing the machines to run code not approved by Nintendo. That would allow users to, in theory, run homebrew applications, but as far as I can tell, the exploit had not yet been extended to allow people to play pirated games.

Nonetheless, Nintendo acted quickly to delist SmileBasic 4 from the eShop and to update the console's firmware to address the exploit at a system level. That's a necessary step, too, as SmileBasic 4 was not the only application that could be used to exploit it. Notably, Civilization 7, a game that is available on cartridge and thus impervious to delistings and software updates, could also be used for the jailbreak.

Even so, it seems that SmileBasic 4 itself has been updated to ensure that a similar exploit never happens again. As Twitter user MarioMasta64 notes, the software itself has gotten a new update post-reinstatement aimed at closing the hole used for the jailbreak. The application's demo – which could also be used to exploit it – has also been updated, but it has not yet been relisted on the eShop.

Every time news comes out about the French video game publisher/developer Ubisoft, it’s either something gamers are pleasantly surprised by or something entirely negative that doesn’t come as a shock.

It was nice to learn that studio franchises like Assassin’s Creed, The Division and Rayman were either getting content expansions or new series installments altogether. But it was equally disappointing to learn about the 2026 cancellation of several in-development projects, including the troubled remake of Prince of Persia: The Sands of Time. Gamers were ecstatic to hear that Ubisoft halted any future development in NFTs and Web3 gaming. But now they’ll probably be less than enthused all over again, now that Ubisoft is shifting its next big tech focus to generative AI.

After releasing its latest earnings report, Ubisoft has announced its intentions to go all-in on incorporating generative AI technologies into video game development with a new studio. But with that news comes the company’s latest round of layoffs, which resulted in hundreds of its studio’s game developers losing their roles at Ubisoft.

With both events happening so close to each other, it’s easy to see why gamers are questioning Ubisoft’s latest moves.

Ubisoft made it known that they’re making a big move towards playable generative AI experiences when it highlighted the new in-house studio behind its efforts in its most recent earnings report.

“Motherbrain, the team behind Teammates, the Group’s first playable generative AI experience, has been established as a dedicated subsidiary developing proprietary technology for new forms of gameplay. Its current focus is on new player experiences, smart NPCs with memory and AI game masters that manage large-scale systemic worlds,” the company stated. “It aims to serve creators globally, both within Ubisoft’s Creative House ecosystem and beyond, and form partnerships with selected technology actors.”

Ubisoft CEO Yves Guillemot commented on the creation of Motherbrain, noting that it is “the team behind our first playable generative AI experience Teammates, as a dedicated subsidiary developing proprietary technologies for gameplay to empower creators and open up new possibilities for richer, more immersive and engaging player experiences.”

Prediction: This Is What Will Happen to Micron’s Stock Price in December

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The memory specialist has taken a beating in recent months, but it could get a nice shot in the arm when it releases its quarterly results next month.

By Harsh Chauhan – Nov 20, 2026 at 12:18PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Micron Technology ›

Micron Technology (MU -12.71%) has made investors significantly richer in 2026, with shares of the memory specialist up 123% as of this writing. However, the stock has run into rough weather in the second half of the year.

Micron stock has lost 32% of its value since the beginning of July. Micron has been affected by concerns about rising competition from China and a pullback in artificial intelligence (AI) infrastructure spending due to higher interest rates, which could constrain funding. That's why Micron investors will now be eagerly awaiting Micron's fiscal 2027 first-quarter report, which is expected in the second half of December.

I will take a closer look at the state of the memory market to determine whether this high-flying AI stock can regain its mojo.

Market research firm Counterpoint Research notes that the memory supercycle continued in the third quarter of 2026. The dynamic random-access memory (DRAM) market's revenue increased by an incredible 5x over the prior-year period to $200 billion. A massive price increase drove this phenomenal jump, while bit shipments increased by only 4% year over year.

It is worth noting that Micron's fiscal Q1 ends in early December, so calendar Q3 overlaps with two months of its fiscal Q1.

But even if that's the case, Micron's DRAM business is on track to surge by a significant margin over the $10.8 billion in revenue it generated from this segment in the first quarter of fiscal 2026. Micron has called for $61.5 billion in revenue for the current quarter, implying a 4.5x increase over last year. The company derives nearly three-fourths of its revenue from the DRAM segment, suggesting it is on track to exceed $40 billion in DRAM revenue.

Also, the company is forecasting an increase of almost 8x in earnings per share from the year-ago period. Memory prices are still growing exponentially, as the limited supply of these chips forces buyers to compete for available capacity, thereby putting Micron in a position to command higher prices. As a result, the company should be able to achieve the phenomenal increase in earnings per share (EPS) that it expects.

However, a lot will depend on the company's guidance for the stock to accelerate again. There is good news for investors on this front as well.
