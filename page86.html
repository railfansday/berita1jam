Ainstein Opens Kansas Production Facility to Meet Growing Global Demand for Advanced Radar Sensors

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

New Lenexa facility adds domestic manufacturing capacity for 44,000 radars annually.

LENEXA, Kan., Nov. 16, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Ainstein, a global radar sensor and solutions company, opened its new manufacturing facility in Lenexa, Kansas, on Friday with a ribbon-cutting ceremony. The expansion marks a significant milestone in the company's growth strategy and brings new production capacity to Kansas, where Ainstein was founded in 2015.

As demand for radar sensing grows across autonomous vehicles, unmanned systems, and commercial applications, customers require higher capacity, shorter lead times, a more resilient supply chain, and the ability to scale. Ainstein built the Lenexa facility to meet that demand, adding capacity for 44,000 radar sensors a year as the first of many steps in its strategic investment in long-term production capabilities.

Ainstein develops advanced radar sensors for aerospace, specialty and commercial vehicle, professional sports, and emerging technology markets. With over 150,000 sensors deployed worldwide, its radars provide safety and autonomy functions for unmanned aircraft, construction vehicles, agricultural equipment, and commercial fleets.

"We started Ainstein in Lawrence eleven years ago with a small team of engineers and a conviction about what radar could do," said Dr. Zongbo Wang, Founder and CEO of Ainstein. "Today our radars support autonomy in the air and on the ground in over 40 countries. This facility is the next chapter in Ainstein's growth strategy. It allows us to continue expanding our production to meet rising global demand and the long-term commitments we have made to our customers, and we are proud to be doing that in Kansas, where it all started."

The new facility adds a high-tech sensor manufacturer to the state's aerospace and manufacturing base. It also strengthens domestic supply for critical systems at a time when unmanned aircraft programs increasingly require locally produced sensors at volume. The added U.S. capacity gives Ainstein more room to support customer program and fleet growth while reinforcing its commitment to high-volume manufacturing and investment in Kansas.

The ribbon-cutting ceremony on November 13 welcomed employees, board members, business leaders, community representatives, and partners to the new facility for remarks and tours.

For more information about Ainstein's radar technology, engineering capabilities, and partnership opportunities, visit https://ainstein.ai.

Founded in Lawrence, Kansas, in 2015, Ainstein is a global leader in millimeter-wave radar sensing solutions for aerospace, automotive, and industrial markets. Combining deep radar engineering expertise, application-specific development, and dedicated technical support, Ainstein delivers advanced sensing technologies that help OEMs, government contractors, and systems integrators improve safety, efficiency, and autonomy across their products and platforms. Backed by Doosan Bobcat, Ainstein is headquartered in Lawrence, Kansas, with a production facility in Lenexa, Kansas.

Sandisk Is Splitting Its Stock 8-for-1. Here’s the Number I’d Check Before Buying.

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Sandisk’s profit margin matters more to the investment case than its new share count.

A stock split always generates buzz. So, when Sandisk (SNDK -6.90%) recently announced an 8-for-1 stock split, investors cheered.

Yet while stock splits can make headlines, investors may want to focus on another figure -- its gross margin -- rather than its new, lower stock price. Here's why.

First, let's talk about stock splits. While they may seem exotic, they don't actually change anything fundamental about the business. The best analogy is culinary: If you take a whole, uncut pizza and cut it into eight slices, you've done nothing to change how much food you have. You've simply divided it up.

Stock splits work the same way. Fundamentals, like revenue, net income, and free cash flow, do not change when a company splits its shares. When Sandisk shares split on Nov. 20, no one receives anything for free; they will simply see what they already own split according to the 8-for-1 principle.

For example, say you own 10 shares of Sandisk today. As of Nov. 13, each share trades for around $858, for a total market value of $8,580. After the split, someone who owned 10 shares will own 80 shares, and each share will drop to $107.25 (assuming the price stays steady between now and then). The total market value of 80 shares at $107.25 per share? $8,580, the same as before.

Granted, the price of Sandisk shares will fluctuate between now and Nov. 20, but the principle remains the same. The market value of shares going into Nov. 20 will still equal the market value after the split; the only thing that will change is the share price.

Rather than focusing on the stock split, I think investors should take a closer look at Sandisk's gross margins. Gross margin is the profit remaining after deducting the cost of making a product.

Imagine Sandisk sells a memory chip for $100. If it took $15 to physically assemble the product, its gross margin on that product would be 85%. Bear in mind that the company doesn't get to keep the full $85 – it still has administrative and research costs, not to mention taxes.

However, gross margins are useful because they strip away these costs, allowing investors to see how profitable a business is without them. Sandisk's gross margins exploded since 2024, increasing from near 10% to around 85%.

SNDK Gross Profit Margin (Quarterly) data by YCharts

However, the big question is whether the company can maintain these incredible margins, or whether those margins will come crashing down.

For Sandisk, its gross margins are driven more by the price customers are willing to pay than by the input costs for its products. Therefore, it may be helpful to think of Sandisk's business like a hotel. The same rooms are far more profitable at $400 per night than at $150 per night, with many of the hotel's costs remaining steady.

Similarly, the supply-and-demand dynamics of the memory market have an enormous effect on Sandisk's gross margins. If memory demand were to pull back, perhaps as a result of slowing AI infrastructure investment, Sandisk's gross margins could tumble. On the other hand, if the company's long-term memory contracts prove durable, Sandisk's gross margins may remain resilient.

Stock splits alone do not make a stock an attractive investment. The split changes the unit size; profits determine the investment's appeal.

As a result, Sandisk must maintain its impressive gross margins in future quarters in order to justify its appeal. The stock already trades at a price-to-sales (P/S) ratio of 4.6. That's down significantly from its peak of over 25 in June, but it remains far above its 2025 average multiple of around 1.7.

The market has already priced a major step into Sandisk's profitability. If that fall doesn't materialize, the stock could be primed for a move higher. That's why I'm intrigued by Sandisk's gross margins, not its upcoming stock split.

Why scammers try to keep you on the phone (and how to spot their tactics)

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

If something feels off, hang up and call the company back on their official number.

That unexpected call from someone claiming to be from your bank can be worrisome, especially if they're calling to alert you about a suspicious money transfer. Your savings are at risk, but fret not, the caller who somehow got hold of your number says they can stop it — as long as you stay on the line.

But more than the emergency itself, their instruction to stay on call is the real catch. Scammers are counting on you to not hang up, as that valuable time helps them earn your trust. It then leads to turning up the pressure, which eventually leads you toward a decision you otherwise wouldn't take. That's when they might ask for a verification code, or straight up ask you to make a transfer. Keeping you occupied just long enough is the plan, so you don't end up calling the bank for a second opinion.

Dedicated scammers are known to stay on the line as the victims login to the accounts to transfer funds, buy gift cards or make a visit to the nearby ATM to withdraw cash. Some even go as far as to school you on what to tell bank employees when questioned about that odd transaction. When the person "helping" you protect your savings is deterring you from speaking to anyone else, that's a pretty obvious sign that something is off.

One possible way in could be a compromised bank account. A creative scammer could inform you about open accounts under your name or even scare you with an arrest warrant. The plan is to make you feel that following their instructions is the only way out, so hanging up doesn't feel like a plausible option.

Sometimes, the whole script includes more than just one scam artist. Back in August 2026, the FBI reported that scammers posing as bank employees told their victims that their information had been used to open an account to purchase illegal firearms. The scammers then transferred the victims to someone posing as an FBI agent, but disconnected after the victims were encouraged to verify the number.

Shortly after, they received a call displaying the FBI's Boston office number, which had been spoofed. These "FBI agents" could then ask the victims to continue the conversations using an encrypted messaging app. The FBI didn't specify how many people were targeted by this scheme.

But the threatening villain persona is not the only way these scammers are out to get you. Some of them act professional, stay patient and even go a step further to sound sympathetic. Now, when these friendly voices start to discourage you from reaching out to the organization directly or getting a second opinion, that should get your alarm bells ringing. After all, con artists don't want all of their efforts to result in failure.

Once the small talk is done, call handlers may switch to a dictating tone in order to get the outcome they want. This is a clear warning sign. When the caller demands you stay connected or instructs you to keep the conversation private, be sure to trust your instincts and end the call.

When they ask you to move funds to a "safer" account, it's time to hang up. Any request for passwords or even one-time verification codes should also be met with a call termination. Since scammers are often savvy enough to spoof authentic phone numbers, caller ID isn't all that reliable either.

Once you hang up or end that text conversation, reach out to the organization, but only using its official app or the number on your bank card or statement. Steer clear of contact numbers that the caller gives you, and trusting the customer-service numbers from sponsored search results isn't safe either. If it's a bit late to heed this advice and you've already shared sensitive information or worse transferred money to the scammer, contact your bank or payment provider right away.

Did you know your phone can filter out some of these unwanted calls? On iPhones running iOS 26 or newer, head to Settings > Apps > Phone > Screen Unknown Callers to enable extra incoming call checks, which include silencing unsaved numbers or asking callers to state who they are before they're connected.

If you own a supported Samsung Galaxy phone, try out spam filtering under Phone > Settings > Caller ID and spam protection, and Google Pixel owners can set up Call Screen using the Phone app's settings on supported devices in eligible regions. The downside is that legitimate callers can sometimes get caught by these filters.

Companies like Meta, Microsoft and Starlink have already worked to help take down over a million scam accounts. If you want to help save other unsuspecting consumers, it's best to report suspected scams to the FTC.

LinkedIn Leads Brand Engagement at 5.8% as Engagement Falls on YouTube, TikTok and Facebook, Vista Social Study Finds

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

NEW YORK, Nov. 16, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Vista Social today released its Social Media Benchmarks by Industry, 2027 Report, revealing a significant downturn in organic engagement across nearly all major social networks. The study, which tracked more than 54,000 brand profiles, found that median engagement rates fell year-over-year even as posting frequencies remained steady.

The complete 2027 report and industry-specific data are available at https://vistasocial.com/research/social-media-benchmarks/.

The findings indicate that reach is becoming increasingly difficult to secure for corporate accounts. Despite the decline in audience interaction, the study noted that brands on Instagram maintained a median of 4.3 posts per week in both 2025 and 2026, suggesting that many organizations are struggling to adapt their content strategies to shifting algorithm priorities.

The report also identified a retention crisis on X, where 42% of brand profiles lost followers between August and October 2026. This compares to one in five profiles on Facebook and approximately one in 100 on LinkedIn.

"Most benchmarks people quote come from a few hundred big brands, or from surveys of what marketers say they do," said Vitaly Veksler, CEO of Vista Social. "This report shows what tens of thousands of brands actually published and how their audiences responded. Reach is getting harder on most networks, and the brands doing best are not the ones with the biggest audiences or the most posts."

The data was anonymized and aggregated from profiles connected to the Vista Social platform via official network APIs. To ensure statistical integrity, no segment was published unless it included at least 50 profiles from at least 10 distinct accounts.

Probably Stolen aquaculture guide: All fish types and how to breed them

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Food  |  Read Source →

With meat being a hard commodity in Probably Stolen, very few specializations can give you a net positive like aquaculture.

But as you will quickly find out after having the tank installed, getting the right tools is not even half the problem. Here’s how aquaculture works in Probably Stolen and how to make plenty of credits fast with it.

Unlockable by purchasing its upgrade pack on Wild’s Favors or picking the Aquafarmer specialization before starting a run, aquaculture revolves around raising and then scrapping fish for both meat and other related items. To start, you need a few things: a fully powered-up Recirculating Aquaculture System, water, Fertilized Roe (or a few non-breedable fish), fish food of any type, and a handy knife (or Meat Cleaver).

You can access all starting items and fish from day one, with non-breedable fish (like eels and the sturgeon) requiring a second permit from Wild’s Favors. Some fish can also only grow in clear water and demand larger tanks, which makes getting both a purifier and a water source a must. You can farm five different types of fish in Probably Stolen, with their value and requirements varying by rarity. Tilapia, Graft Tilapia, and Gutter Tilapia are acquirable by acquiring fertilized roe and then placing it in a system. The variants are also the only types of fish in the game that can be bred. Here’s an overview of each type’s unique features:

Even if omnivorous, make sure to balance their diet, as any Tilapia that consumes over 25 percent Gutterflow Pellets will have its meat turn inedible. Because of that, we also don’t recommend adding the latter to your dispensers and only using it as a last-ditch effort.

Acquirable after getting the Aquaculture II Favor, both the Eel and Electric Eel are premium carnivore fish, whose meat can be sold for higher amounts once fully matured. That also means that the usual Fish Feed won’t do the job here. Instead, you need to give them meat.

Electric Eels can also power up their Aquaculture Systems and all surrounding machines after reaching adolescence, making it an ideal pick when looking to hook up multiple farms.

The most expensive fish in Probably Stolen, the Sturgeon gets the title for a reason. After all, they can also provide you with caviar after reaching adulthood. You will need to use a Surgery Kit to extract it, however.

Only possible with the tilapia variants, you can breed fish in Probably Stolen by keeping at least one adult female Tilapia, Graft Tilapia, or Gutter Tilapia in an Aquaculture System. They will then have a small chance of laying eggs during the night. Now, simply move the roe into a tank with a matching fish, who will fertilize it within the next 24 hours.
