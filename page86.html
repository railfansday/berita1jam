Giants get first win in Philadelphia since 2013

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Sunday’s performance in Philadelphia wasn’t a work of art for the Giants, but it was more than good enough to end their long losing streak in the City of Brotherly Love.

Tight end Isaiah Likely’s touchdown catch in the third quarter gave the Giants their first lead since the first quarter and they expanded it to 19-11 before punting the ball back to the Eagles with 90 seconds left to play in the game. Joe Milton picked up first downs on completions to Hollywood Brown and Dallas Goedert, but rookie wideout Makai Lemon dropped a pass that would have moved them into Giants territory and running back Elijah Mitchell didn’t get out of bounds on a short catch on the next play.

Milton spiked the ball with six seconds left to play and the Eagles on their own 44-yard-line for one final heave. Milton famously uncorked a 70-yard throw at the Combine, but he never got a chance to throw the ball because he was sacked by defensive end Darius Alexander.

That was one of seven Giants sacks on the afternoon, including three from first-round pick Arvell Reese. It was Reese’s first road game against the Eagles and his 1-0 record in Philly is a big change from recent history. The Giants had lost 13 straight games in Philadelphia and the route to ending that skid featured some strange twists and turns.

Likely’s touchdown catch came on a throw from Jameis Winston, who replaced J.J. McCarthy after McCarthy hurt his hip in the third quarter. Winston played one full series and threw for 71 yards on his way to the touchdown, but McCarthy returned just before the end of the quarter and led the offense for the rest of the proceedings.

McCarthy was sacked five times, but was 12-of-19 for 127 yards when he had time to throw and he ran for 22 yards while evading pressure over the course of the afternoon. The Giants couldn’t find any other success on the ground and Sunday’s 243 yards of offense didn’t make a strong case for the Giants sitting out the trade deadline if they have a chance to acquire any kind of help for their backfield.

Wide receiver Malik Nabers had five catches for 43 yards, but dropped a pass in the end zone that would have iced the win and his body language was that of an unhappy player throughout the game. The win should make it easier to avoid it being a focal point for the team after the game, but Nabers will likely have to answer some questions about what was bugging him.

They’ll be back on the field Thursday night against the Commanders and another win would move them to 6-2-1 with a 3-0-1 record in the division. That would make them even heavier favorites to win an NFC East that is theirs for the taking.

The Eagles spent a lot of time in that position in recent years, but injuries have gutted the roster and Milton didn’t provide any significant upgrade on the offensive production that the team got with Tanner McKee at quarterback. The Eagles will have a bye week to determine which way they want to go in the future, but the 2-7 team should really only have eyes on 2027 at this point.

Trump says renewed Iran strikes ‘highly unlikely’ if talks progress but warns he could still strike

📅 2026-11-08  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

President Donald Trump said he is "highly unlikely" to restart open war with Iran, but warned he could still strike the country as Washington tries to reach a deal with Tehran to end the conflict.

The now more than eight-month-old war, which stoked inflation and sent oil prices surging, has seen repeated bursts of fighting separated by ceasefires and peace talks for months. It was widely speculated that Trump might restart open conflict after the midterm elections, where his Republican Party lost badly to Democrats.

Trump's comments came during an interview with CNN, where he said restarting the war is unlikely as long as talks to constrain Iran's nuclear program and reopen the Strait of Hormuz make progress.

"It depends on whether or not they sign a very strong agreement," Trump said. "The answer is highly unlikely it's going to be necessary. They're a failing nation."

Trump, however, left the door open for additional strikes if he deems it necessary. The president reiterated his threats to strike bridges and power plants in Iran if the war restarts.

"I've got three aircraft carriers here. The other one just arrived. We have three. They're fully locked and loaded," the president said. "We have 38 bridges in our site that we can knock down in literally one hour."

An end to the war and reopening of the Strait of Hormuz would likely ease oil prices, though finding a truce has proven difficult. All previous attempts to end the war and reopen the strait have been met with disappointment.

Trump is likely to come under increased pressure to end the conflict, however, now that Democrats have won control of both the House and the Senate. Some Republicans are also likely to be eager for an end to the war, given the role high gas prices played in their drubbing at the ballot box.

The president has demanded Iran fully open the Strait of Hormuz and agree to never develop a nuclear weapon as part of any deal.

Later in the day on Truth Social, Trump posted that "Task Force Iron Gate is in full force and effect in the Islamic Republic of Iran."

"Coupled with history's greatest Naval Blockade, very little gets into, or out of, Iran!" he said.

Central Command announced Task Force Iron Gate on Saturday, describing it as an initiative aimed at "preventing the movement of advanced weapons systems, dual-use technologies, sanctioned materials, and illegal revenue streams across Iran's land borders and the Caspian Sea."

The U.S. has maintained a naval blockade of Iranian ports for months.

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang has admitted to having an affair with a woman who is allegedly a Chinese spy. The apology came in a letter to Nvidia staff, which we have added further below. Nvidia released a statement from the company's board of directors late on Sunday to address the report that U.S. intelligence believes Jensen Huang had an affair with a Chinese spy. While Nvidia's board says it has no reason to believe that sensitive corporate information or U.S. national security was compromised, the company intends to examine all available information to determine what happened. The BoD claims that it is fully confident in Jensen Huang and his leadership team. Huang says he fully supports the investigation.

"The Board prioritizes U.S. national and economic security, which is paramount to the interests of Nvidia and its shareholders. A recent report claimed that the U.S. intelligence agencies believe that Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang had a romantic relationship with Alice Huang, also known as Huang Le, a former executive at Singapore-based Megaspeed who was allegedly recruited by China's Ministry of State Security (MSS) to gather information and help Chinese companies obtain restricted AI GPUs. Megaspeed has been on the radar for smuggling restricted hardware for China since at least 2025: Megaspeed has been accused of smuggling $2 billion worth of AI chips into China via its subsidiary Speedmatrix.

Nvidia has strenuously rejected suggestions of improper dealings, saying that its CEO regularly meets thousands of customers and that these interactions do not involve sensitive corporate information or national security secrets. Chinese officials have also dismissed the espionage allegations as unfounded. The suspected intelligence operation has not been independently recognized as a fact.

"I owe my family and all of you an apology," Huang wrote in his X post. "I was unfaithful to my wife. My actions hurt her and our children. There is no excuse for what I did. I am deeply sorry. Throughout my life, my family has given me love and support. They deserve my honesty and loyalty. Instead, I let them down and damaged the life we built together. I take full responsibility for what I did and the hurt I caused, and I accept the criticism that comes with it. To my wife and family: I love you. I know an apology cannot undo what I have done. I hope our family will heal and that, over time, I can earn back your trust."

In June 2018, Intel chief executive Brian Krzanich resigned after an internal investigation, assisted by external legal counsel, confirmed that he had engaged in a consensual romantic relationship with an Intel employee, which violated the company's non-fraternization policy applicable to all managers. Krzanich, who had led Intel since 2013, also stepped down from the company's board, while then-chief financial officer Robert Swan took over as interim CEO. For now, it remains unclear if Jensen will meet a similar fate and lose control of the company he founded.

Recap: Lions’ defense dominates in 19-9 win over Dolphins

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The Detroit Lions offense has failed to score more than 20 points in three straight weeks, but the defense has carried them to wins in all three of those games.

It was the same story in the Lions' Week 9 win over the Miami Dolphins. The offense was disjointed and had to overcome some poor offensive line play early. And while they eventually were able to find some success late, Detroit's defensive performance is what really stood out.

The Lions held the Dolphins to just 77 yards rushing and 3.7 yards per carry. Miami quarterback Malik Willis had just 128 passing yards. And, most importantly, the Lions held the Dolphins out of the end zone completely for all four quarters.

It was an inspired performance, particularly without the heartbeat of the defense, Jack Campbell. The Lions clearly dedicated the game to him, sporting No. 46 jerseys while walking into the stadium. And while Campbell vowed this week to eventually continue his NFL career, Detroit proved on Sunday that they can operate without him for now.

Here's a full recap of the Lions' 19-9 win over the Dolphins.

The Lions got the ball first and had great field position after the kickoff landed before the landing zone. Interestingly, it was Miles Frazier at left guard to start the game in place of the (presumably) healthy Christian Mahogany. After picking up 6 yards on the first two plays, Jared Goff and the Lions faced an quick third-and-4. Goff found Amon-Ra St. Brown for a nice gain of 16 yards between Dolphins' zone coverage defenders. Detroit failed to pick up the next third-and-short when Goff's arm was hit by Dolphins defender Josh Uche.

That set up a fourth-and-3 from the Dolphins' 31-yard line. Detroit went for it, and Goff avoided pressure to find Isaac TeSlaa for 8 to keep the drive alive. Then Jahmyr Gibbs took a wildcat snap, faked a pitch to Goff, and took it for 16 yards down to the Dolphins' 6-yard line.

But on third-and-goal, Goff was sacked by Uche, forcing the Lions to settled for a chip-shot field goal. 3-0 Lions.

Detroit managed to get a quick three-and-out on defense after two rushes went for 0 yards, and Malik Willis' third-down pass was too far for his receiver.

But the Lions quickly gave the ball back to Miami. After two successful runs, Goff's arm was hit again by Uche while throwing, and the ball went right to Dolphins defensive back Chris Johnson.

Miami moved into scoring position with a leaping grab from Caleb Douglas for 20 yards down to the Lions' 20-yard line. In the red zone, the Dolphins faced a third-and-11 and they tried to catch Detroit sleeping with a draw play. But Aidan Hutchinson sniffed it out for a short gain, and the Dolphins settled for a field goal to tie it up. 3-3 tie.

The Lions offense gained just 3 yards on their first two plays to set up a big third-and-7 to start the second quarter.

On the first play, Goff went deep to Jameson Williams, but the receiver bobbled a very catchable ball and the Lions were forced to punt…. or were they?!? Jack Fox drilled a pass to Tay Martin for the fake punt conversion and 11 yards.

Detroit had to dig themselves out of hole, though, as Frazier was called for a holding penalty on the next play. While an 11-yard pass to St. Brown helped, a failed screen set up a third-and-9. And on the play, the Dolphins sent an aggressive blitz and Miami picked up their third sack of the game. This time, the Lions did actually punt the ball away.

The Dolphins were pinned at their own 5-yard line, but a play-action pass to Greg Dulcich for 11 yards got Miami out of the jam. That said, Hutchinson drew a holding penalty that eventually set up a third-and-18 for the Dolphins. Pressure from the Lions forced Willis to throw it away.

Despite a holding penalty on the Lions on the punt, they still got great field position due to a shank from Dolphins punter Bradley Pinion. Detroit started on their own 43-yard line, hoping to put some of the offensive line issues behind them. The drive started promisingly with an 11-yard pass to Sam LaPorta and a 10-yard run from Gibbs. Two plays later, Goff hit St. Brown for another 14 yards.

However, the drive stalled from there. A toss to Gibbs lost 3 yards. Then another Gibbs run lost another 3 yards. Goff scrambled on third down to make the field goal a little shorter, and Bates drilled a 40-yard field goal to make it 6-3 Lions with 3:30 left in the half.

Miami responded before the half ended. A reverse to Kevin Coleman earned 16 yards and put the Dolphins in Lions territory. Ollie Gordon then got Miami into field goal range with a pair of draws that went for 9 and 11 yards. Eventually, Miami drove down to the Lions' 10-yard line and had a shot at the end zone. But Hutchinson drew another holding penalty, forcing the Dolphins to kick a game-tying field goal, but former Lions Zane Gonzalez missed a 38-yard field goal to keep it 6-3 Lions at halftime.

Miami started with the ball and picked up a quick first down thanks to a pass interference penalty on Derrick Barnes. He made amends with a tackle for loss on the next play, helping set up a third-and-11 for the Dolphins. Willis tried to force a ball into a tight window, but safety Chuck Clark picked it off and returned it all the way to the Dolphins 26-yard line.

Gibbs gave the Lions a new set of downs inside the red zone with a third-and-1 conversion with a 3-yard gain on a toss. However, the Lions had to settle for another field goal when a second down Gibbs run went for no gain and Sam LaPorta committed a false start to set up a third-and-11. Goff turfed a pass to Gibbs, and Bates was good from 33. 9-3 Lions.

Bates' ensuing kickoff was well short of the landing zone, setting the Dolphins up at the 40-yard line. Miami turned that into points. Detroit drew another holding penalty—Skyler Gill-Howard this time. And it forced Miami to settle for another field goal attempt. This time Gonzalez was good from 52 yards. 9-6 Lions.

The Lions obviously went for it on fourth-and-1, and Detroit dialed up a well-timed pitch to Gibbs, who beat everyone to the outside for an 8-yard touchdown. 16-6 Lions.

Miami faced a third-and-3 at the start of the fourth quarter.

The Dolphins failed to convert after Malcolm Rodriguez punched out the ball on what would've been a converted first down pass. But Miami pulled off a fake punt of their own, and it was even more successful than Detroit's, gaining 46 yards.

Then, on a third-and-5 inside the red zone, Willis found Malik Washington for 7 yards to move the chain. Alim McNeill tallied a sack to put the Dolphins in a tough third-and-goal situation from the 13.

Derrick Moore chased down Willis on third down, forcing yet another field goal from the Dolphins. 16-9 Lions.

Detroit avoided a third-and-out when an attempt to St. Brown drew a 17-yard pass interference penalty. That would be all the Lions could muster, though. A holding penalty on Blake Miller pushed the Lions back, but Detroit dug out of it with a 17-yard screen to St. Brown on a third-and-8.

Detroit would stall out from there, as Goff overthrew St. Brown on third-and-7. Bates' 43-yard field goal was good, to give Detroit a two-score lead with 4:28 left. 19-9 Lions.

Starting at their own 35-yard line, the Dolphins had to score twice and score quickly. But Detroit's defensive line took over. Hutchinson forced a throw away on first down, Moore got a sack on second down, and on fourth-and-12, the Dolphins dropped a potential conversion. Detroit took over at Miami's 33 with 3:02 left and the game in hand.

The Lions offense managed to earn a couple of first downs and bleed the rest of the clock to move to 6-2 on the season at the midway point of the year.

Arnav’s Analysis: Takeaways From Rutgers’ 31-10 Loss At Wisconsin

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

After a first half in which Rutgers hung with Wisconsin, tying the score at 10 before a late Badgers field goal, the Scarlet Knights ran out of steam and did not score once in the second half. It was a stronger performance from AJ Surace, going 14-for-24 for 161 yards and a touchdown of 30 yards to Dyzier Carter, but that was not enough to overcome all the injuries, lack of scoring, and defensive struggles for the Knights.

Defensively, Rutgers held steady for the first half, but the same flaws plagued RU in the second half with wide-open scores, missed tackles, and allowing open throws for the freshman quarterback Ryan Hopkins. Antwan Raymond and Clay Thevenin were held under 100 combined yards despite carrying the football 25 times, and the offense suffered without their top lineman, quarterback, and wide receiver.

Here are my takeaways from another brutal defeat, Rutgers' seventh of this dismal season.

With the Rutgers defense largely holding Wisconsin in check other than the early touchdown, the offense was able to hold serve, and tight end Logan Blake had a nifty catch in triple coverage, having to rip the ball away from Badgers defenders for a first down. Clay Thevenin had three straight carries before Surace found Elias Coke for an 11-yard gain.

After two plays for no gain, Surace found Coke again for a seven-yard pass on third down, and Jack Scullion knocked through a 45-yard field goal to put Rutgers on the board 7-3. The offensive highlight of the day came on the next series, in which three straight run plays led to a beautiful deep ball for Dyzier Carter, who scored his first career touchdown.

However, the second half, the offense completely sputtered, as the opening drive saw a 15-yard pass to Ben Black III and ten yard pass to Dyzier Carter, but resulted in a punt despite getting across midfield. The next couple drives were worse, as the Knights gained eight yards in a three-and-out and then gaining eight net yards with one first down.

With the Badgers cruising down the field for touchdowns, Rutgers did not have the firepower to match any of the scores, and two sacks on Surace led to an incompletion intended for Black on a desparation fourth down heave.

Even a Wisconsin fumble recovered by Rutgers was not enough, as the AJ Surace-led offense was unable to generate any scoring drives or points against the staunch Badgers' front. Cooper Catalano had 14 total tackles and 2.5 per loss, while five different Badgers got home for sacks against a Rutgers offensive line missing star left guard Kwabena Asamoah and right tackle Tyler Needham.

Although at times Antwan Raymond and Clay Thevenin were able to move the ball on the ground, Raymond was limited to 56 yards on 15 carries (3.7 yards per carry), while Thevenin had 10 rushes for 40 yards (four yards per carry). Aiden Gibson and Ja'Shon Benjamin's late carries were negated by yardage lost on sacks by AJ Surace, for a team rushing total of just 94 yards. Surace was briefly replaced by Ashenfelder after an injury saw him leave the field, but he was able to quickly return to the field to finish the drive.

Through the air, Surace was sorely missing KJ Duff, as Black III led the team with six catches for 70 yards. Carter had the lone touchdown and finished with 40 yards on two catches, while Raymond added 21 yards with a reception. Elias Coke and Logan Blake rounded out the receiving column with two catches each for 18 and 14 yards respectively, while Thevenin lost two yards as the receivers were held to 161 total yards.

After both teams exchanged punts on their opening drives, Wisconsin drove down the field with the all too familiar chunk plays through the air: 15-yard pass, 9-yard pass, 18-yard run, and a 21-yard pass from Ryan Hopkins to Eugene Hilton Jr. to take an early 7-0 lead before the Scarlet Knights answered with a field goal.

The defense then held steady, allowing one field goal after a Rutgers three-and-out and punt from their own seven yard line set the Badgers up in plus territory. After the lone Knights' touchdown, they allowed another field goal, spurred by a 26-yard pass to Shamar Rigby and a pass interference on Devonte Anderson.

In the second half, it was an entirely different story, as the Rutgers offense sputtering resulted in repeated scoring drives for Wisconsin. Three separate plays went for 16 yards as the Badgers went up 16-10, then UW made Rutgers pay for a targeting call on Tariq Hayer as Hopkins connected with Ryan Schwendeman over the middle.

The game was largely decided on the next sequence with another Rutgers punt giving way to a 66-yard touchdown pass from Hopkins to tight end Jacob Harris, who ran the ball 47 yards up the right sideline while breaking and evading tackles. Rigby and Harris both crossed 70 yards in four and two plays, respectively.

For the Scarlet Knights, Zaire Angoy had a sack and 1.5 tackles for loss, while Hayer led the team in tackles with seven. Angoy and Kromah combined for a three-yard loss play on Darrion Dupree which set up a field goal, while DJ Allen had Rutgers' other sack.

The rush defense held up relatively well, holding the Badgers' run game to 122 total yards. Hilton led Wisconsin with 39 yards on three carries, while lead back Abu Sama had 35 yards on nine carries. The play of the defensive line has certainly improved, as the Badgers were held to just 4.2 yards per carry.

However, they made a true freshman quarterback making his first start look like a seasoned veteran, as Ryan Hopkins went 18-24 for 241 yards, three touchdowns, and no interceptions. Many of the throws were for over ten yards as the defensive gave up big and intermediate plays in space, as par for the course.

In total, Rutgers allowed 31 points, a near-perfect game from Hopkins, and tons of chunk plays all afternoon as Wisconsin turned a 13-10 halftime lead into a 31-10 victory to clinch bowl eligibility.

Next up for the Scarlet Knights is a Nebraska team that has watched a 5-0 start evaporate into four straight losses. After leading Indiana 17-6, the Cornhuskers fell 20-17 to the Hoosiers, then fell 17-14 at Oregon at the hands of Dylan Raiola and then a stunning defeat to Washington after leading most of the way.

Last week, Nebraska scored the opening touchdown, but the Illini scored three straight, and the Huskers were out of answers after Anthony Colandrea found Jacory Barney Jr. for a long score to cut the lead to eight. It was an upset victory for the Illini, and it sends Nebraska to New Jersey completely reeling.

However, the Scarlet Knights have looked all out of sorts, and are without Asamoah the rest of the season while Duff and Lonergan are uncertain for next week and beyond. Rutgers has never defeated Nebraska despite a few close calls, including the last two meetings which were decided by one point and one touchdown, respectively.

It will be hard to envision Rutgers finding a way to win, with the state of their offense being injured and defense struggling deeply, particularly through the air. However, the month of November continues to get easier, as the Scarlet Knights will have a chance to potentially pick off either Nebraska, Penn State, and Michigan State as the team looks to close out 2026 on a brighter note.

Le prestazioni di Ismaelo Ganiou non stanno passando inosservate. Il difensore classe 2005 del Lens, protagonista di un'importante crescita nel campionato francese, sarebbe finito nel mirino di alcuni dei principali club di Premier League e Bundesliga. Secondo quanto riferito dall'esperto di mercato Ekrem Konur, Manchester City, Tottenham, Borussia Dortmund e Lipsia starebbero seguendo con attenzione il ventunenne, che si è ritagliato uno spazio nella difesa della formazione francese. A suscitare interesse è soprattutto la sua duttilità tattica: Ganiou può infatti essere impiegato sia come difensore centrale sia come terzino, una caratteristica che amplia le soluzioni a disposizione dell'allenatore.

La concorrenza per il giovane difensore potrebbe dunque diventare particolarmente intensa. Da una parte ci sono Manchester City e Tottenham, interessate a monitorare uno dei profili emergenti della Ligue 1; dall'altra Borussia Dortmund e Lipsia, società attente ai giovani talenti da valorizzare.
