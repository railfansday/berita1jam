Ramayana Ending Explained: What Happens To Rama & How It Sets Up Part 2

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

The following contains spoilers for Ramayana: Rise of a LegendRamayana: Rise of a Legend is one of the year's big cinematic epics, with an ending that directly sets up the sequel in more than just one way. Based on the Hindu myth of the same name, Ramayana focuses on the epic battle that brews between the noble Rama and the dangerous Ravana. The first film takes its time laying out the world and setting up the conflict, with the pair not even meeting in the film.

Instead, the movie explores both characters' strengths and weaknesses to underscore what separates them on a deeply moral and human level. The film is uninterested in doing a typical 'good vs. evil' narrative, instead adding little touches of nuance to characters like Ravana, who are portrayed almost heroically in the cold open of the film as he leads his people to freedom.

Ramayana is a foundational morality tale that has endured for ages, with those same core themes present in the blockbuster film that's dividing critics. The movie is very much a part 1, leaving the stage open for next year's sequel. However, there's a lot more going on in the film's ending than just a tease for the follow-up.

Ramayana: Rise of a Legend ends on a big cliffhanger, with Rama set to venture across the realms to confront Ravana in a bid to save his wife Sita. For much of Ramayana, Rama and Ravana operate outside of one another's lives. Even when Ravana learns that Rama has been born and could pose a risk to him, he chooses restraint and patience rather than wage war.

However, the discovery that Rama and his family had defined his sister and subsequently wiped out her demon army prompts him to target the prince, capturing Sita and killing Jatayu when he tries to intervene. For the most part, the first film is laying the groundwork for the world and the characters, establishing Rama's might and showcasing his gradual growth into a man capable of fighting Ravana.

The film concludes with a deliberate tease of the next movie, with Rama learning a bit about Ravana's champions and family members that he'll have to overcome to reach the enmy king. Coupled with a furious Rama's final vow to kill Ravana and save Sita, the finale of the film is a very overt tease for the sequel, which is poised to be a far more direct and action-packed affair.

One of the interesting wrinkles of the film is the way Ramayana doesn't simply make Ravana a villain for Rama to overcome. The film takes pains to portray the character as a multilayered person, whose origin story centers around his efforts to life his people out of exile and back to the surface world. He is, in many ways, the protagonist of his own story instead of just an antagonist.

This plays into the movie's focus on the balance between order and chaos, but quietly inverts them in effective ways. Rama, as the mortal avatar of Vishnu, is tied inherently to order. However, he's also a chaotic whirlwind in battle and resists expectations to stay true to his heart. Conversely, the chaos-bringing Ravana is shown as a king, commanding a system built up in the wake of his victories.

The film isn't necessarily about good vs. evil, even when Ravana begins crossing serious moral lines within the narrative. It's a story of two forces that circle one another and contrast against each other. While Rama is content to abdicate his throne and is quick to rage when Sita is threatened, Ravana proves deceptively cruel and patient when it comes to ensuring his rule.

The thing to remember about Ramayana is that to millions of people around the world, the tale of the Ramayana is just as much a religious text as it is a mythic epic. The central story of the film is rooted in the morals and messages laid out in the original tale. Ramayana has inspired people for ages, underlining the importance of honor, loyalty, and humility through Rama's arc.

Ravana, by contrast, showcases the power that can be amassed, while laying the groundwork for his defeat. His overwhelming confidence leaves him open to being defeated in the follow-up. Ramayana: Rise of a Legend is still about those things, reinforcing its perspective on the danger of pride and hostility by having other characters like Shurpanakha and her husband Vidyutjihva suffer for theirs.

Ramayana is in many ways a morality play, highlighting the inner strength that comes from Rama and Sita that provides them with a happiness and peace that Ravana and his people have never known. Despite the power they've gained, there's still an aura of chaos in their perspective on the world that will invite chaos to their door, with a vengeful Rama in the follow-up.

Is Appian a Buy After Its Latest Earnings Report?

Appian's AI momentum accelerated in the third quarter.

Appian (APPN +8.45%) came into its third-quarter earnings report in the midst of its strongest rally since the pandemic, up more than 150% from its lows this year.

The process automation software specialist has seen its growth and profitability ramp up in the AI era as the company has incorporated AI across its software platform, and Appian AI, its AI-powered app-building software, is seeing explosive growth.

Customer AI usage jumped 50% sequentially in the third quarter, showing its base of government and enterprise customers are finding value in the company's AI tools. For example, Appian AI helped customers conduct audits three times faster, perform underwriting reviews 75% faster, and run investment processes with 34% greater efficiency.

As a result of its AI growth, overall revenue jumped 25% to 234.6 million, well ahead of the consensus at $217 million. Revenue growth benefited in part from a surge in on-premises revenue, driven by the busiest quarter in the public sector. Cloud subscription revenue, which Appian generally considers its core focus, was up 20% in the quarter to $136.4 million, and total subscription revenue was up 28% to $187.8 million.

On the bottom line, adjusted earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization (EBITDA) rose from $32.2 million to $43.4 million, and adjusted earnings per share jumped from $0.32 to $0.51, well ahead of the consensus at $0.33.

Appian's guidance called for its momentum to continue into the fourth quarter with cloud subscription revenue guidance of $143 million-$145 million, representing 22%-24% growth, and total revenue guidance of $233 million-$237 million, representing growth of 15%-17%. On the bottom line, it sees adjusted earnings per share of $0.30-$0.34, up from $0.15 in the quarter a year ago. For the full year, adjusted earnings per share are expected to double to $1.20-$1.24.

Appian stock was trading up 4% Thursday afternoon after jumping nearly 20% in the premarket session, though the rout in the broader stock market erased some of those gains.

Only a few years ago, Appian was struggling in the post-pandemic era, and as recently as 2024, the company was losing money on an EBITDA basis.

However, Appian has reinvented itself, streamlining its sales force and reorganizing its go-to-market strategy to be more efficient, and those efforts paid off in improved profitability.

In the AI era, the company also sees itself as having established a competitive advantage through its years of work with process automation before AI and its established trust with organizations like government agencies and multinational banks, which have mission-critical applications and prioritize reliability.

Speaking in an interview with The Motley Fool, Appian CEO Matt Calkins commented that its competition has actually decreased in the AI era because the company is now competing at a higher tier. He explained, saying, "The name of the game is reliability because we're talking at the top of the organization and above all they want AI connected to their most important work in a reliable way."

Calkins also said that Appian had done a good job of predicting the evolution of the process automation market, saying that AI had changed the market it's competing in, and added, "I think we've done a number of things right in the last few years, but what I think in retrospect we've done is to see the obvious a little bit in advance."

Above all, Appian is meeting the moment by providing a comprehensive platform built on its data fabric, a top-rated orchestration layer, governance protocols to ensure safety, and an efficiency layer that ensures tokens are used in a cost-effective manner.

On the earnings call, CFO Serge Tanjga said, "We all show up here for work knowing that we should be a much bigger company in a few years," showing strong confidence in the business’s direction.

Appian has the momentum, and AI is providing a massive opportunity. The company expects margins to continue to expand as it grows, meaning 2027 should be another year of strong profit growth. Given that, Appian stock looks like a good bet to keep moving higher.

Investors are savoring the glazed doughnut seller's improved profitability.

Shares of Krispy Kreme (DNUT +30.68%) surged more than 30% on Thursday after the doughnut maker boosted its full-year sales forecast.

Krispy Kreme's net revenue declined 12.4% year over year to $328.8 million in its fiscal third quarter, which ended on Sept. 27.

The decline reflects the doughnut shop operator's shift toward a less capital-intensive, franchise-based business model. Roughly a quarter of the chain's systemwide sales -- the total dollar value of all Krispy Kreme product sales -- came from franchise-operated stores in fiscal 2025. That figure rose to 42% in the third quarter.

Management is targeting 50% of systemwide sales generated by franchisees by fiscal 2027. Third-quarter systemwide sales increased 1% to $490.2 million.

Refranchising is a key part of Krispy Kreme's turnaround strategy. Selling company-operated stores to franchisees is helping Krispy Kreme pay down debt and improve its profit margins and cash flow.

The company's Q3 adjusted net income rose sevenfold to $9.9 million, or $0.06 per share.

Looking ahead, Krispy Kreme lifted its full-year net revenue target range to $1.35 billion to $1.4 billion, up from a prior forecast of $1.25 billion to $1.35 billion.

Systemwide sales are projected to rise 2% to 4% to $1.96 billion, driven by at least 100 franchised store openings. The company also expects to generate free cash flow of over $15 million.

"Our results demonstrate the success of the actions we are taking to drive sustainable, profitable growth," CEO Josh Charlesworth said.
