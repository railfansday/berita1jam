Liverpool Hold Talks Over Man Utd and Tottenham Target Alejandro Balde

Liverpool, Manchester United, Tottenham Hotspur and Aston Villa are among the teams who have held talks with super agent Jorge Mendes over a move for Barcelona left-back Alejandro Balde, according to a report.

Mendes offering Liverpool, Man United, Tottenham and Aston Villa the chance to sign the Spanish star suggests all four Premier League sides are considering signing a new left-back in 2027, with the 23-year-old prioritising a move to the Premier League if he does leave the Camp Nou.

The Red Devils have long been linked with a move for Balde, including in the summer when they were looking to bring in competition for Luke Shaw.

After playing all 38 of the club's Premier League matches last term, injury problems have hit the Englishman again and the latest suggestion is that he's likely to leave Old Trafford at the end of the season when his contract expires.

Balde and Newcastle United left-back Lewis Hall were both mentioned as primary targets for United over the summer by Fabrizio Romano, but neither move materialised as they focused on bringing in three new central midfield players instead.

Meanwhile, the jury is out on Liverpool left-back duo Milos Kerkez and Kostas Tsimikas following the summer exit of Andy Robertson, with pundit Alan Shearer claiming this week that the Scotland international is better than both players who remained at Anfield.

It would be no surprise if Iraola looks to strengthen at left-back in 2027, but Tottenham increasing their interest would be somewhat of a surprise given they have Robertson, Ben Davies and Souza in their ranks, with Pedro Porro and Micky van de Ven also able to play there if needed.

Clearly, Unai Emery may also feel that he needs some added competition for Ian Maatsen in that position too.

According to Sport, Mendes has already started working behind the scenes to get Balde a move away from the Catalan giants and is in contact with multiple clubs, with the player's preference over move to England confirmed.

The report adds that while there have been talks, United, Liverpool and Villa are yet to make a decision on whether they will advance a January move for Balde, while any move from Spurs could depend on departures.

Iraola faces a decision on what he is going to do in the left-back position, with many fans remaining unconvinced over Kerkez, while Tsimikas is looking likely to leave Merseyside at the end of the campaign when his contract expires.

If the former Bournemouth boss does decide to ramp up his interest in Balde, he could hold a big advantage over some of his Premier League rivals for the signature of the 23-year-old, who is said to be valued at around £36m.

Firstly, the fact that both Iraola and the player are Spanish is a boost, both in terms of communication and for him settling in England if he did make the switch.

Liverpool and Barcelona also have a recent existing relationship when it comes to transfer negotiations, following the loan addition of Ronald Araujo in the summer.

Araujo has become a key member of the Reds' backline, with his agent issuing a fresh update amid a £47m decision over his loan, and Balde would fancy his chances of usurping Kerkez in the XI at Anfield, but there would be more uncertainty over whether he would definitely start at United when Shaw is fit.

The Premier League clubs should have questions too, about a player who was once seen as a generational talent is now Barcelona's third-choice left-back.

The Spain international clearly has something about him, but injury issues have blighted him and he has a lot to prove as a result when it comes to his next move.

Of course, if an English club is able to bring the 23-year-old in on a temporary basis until the end of the season, it could be a no-brainer, as Balde looks to show he still has a future among the European elite.

Not playing at Man City 'horrendous' - Trafford

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

'I had to play regularly for my mental wellbeing' - Trafford

James Trafford says not playing for Manchester City last season was "horrendous" before he moved to Leeds United in the summer in search of first-team football.

City re-signed academy graduate Trafford in July 2025, two years after he had left the club to join Championship side Burnley.

But less than six weeks after his arrival, Italy international Gianluigi Donnarumma arrived from Paris St-Germain.

The 27-year-old, who helped the French club win the Champions League just weeks earlier, immediately became City's first-choice goalkeeper, leaving Trafford short of playing time.

After starting the first three matches of the campaign, 24-year-old Trafford made just one further Premier League start in the 35 games that followed Donnarumma's arrival.

Speaking on Football Daily's The Football Interview, Trafford reflected on the difficulties he encountered during a season on the fringes of Pep Guardiola's squad.

"What a horrendous, horrendous feeling. Not playing," said Trafford.

"You are ringing a lot of people and just venting. Like most people, you have a [bad] day at work and you ring someone up.

"I ring my dad, I ring my mum, I ring my missus, I ring my mates. I like ringing my mates and we can just have a good talk about it all. It's quite funny."

Despite his limited playing time, Trafford believes he still gained plenty from his final season at City, having helped the club win the League Cup and FA Cup after starting both finals at Wembley.

"Going into any season, I always have the highest of hopes and always the belief that it will go the best way it could," he added.

"Unfortunately, we didn't win all four trophies. It didn't go how I expected it to go, but it happens.

"In the moment, I just tried to make the best of it as I could. Looking back, I don't think it was a disappointing season in the end because I learned so much, I improved so much and I won two trophies in one year.

"There were moments when I was very down, regretting things and feeling disappointed, but looking back, I'm very thankful for the experience. What an incredible year."

Trafford was part of England's squad for the 2026 World Cup and, although he did not get game time, being part of Thomas Tuchel's squad was an "incredible experience" that he says he will not forget.

"I didn't play, but it is what it is," said Trafford. "I didn't expect to play when I got called up. I knew what my role was, but you just take as much experience as possible, learn from it and keep trying to win the day, because the more days you win, the better position you're in."

Next time around, though, Trafford hopes to be England's starting goalkeeper and believes that if he continues his impressive performances with Leeds, that ambition is entirely achievable.

"It's my aim and it's something I've always dreamed of," said Trafford.

"I always believe that one day I could be. Every day I try to keep improving to put myself in the best position to be England's number one one day.

"I think everyone in the goalkeeping department with the national team wants to be number one because it's an amazing position to be in."

'Everyone's got an opinion on goalkeeping'

The former Bolton Wanderers and Accrington Stanley loanee has started all nine of Leeds' Premier League matches this season, keeping three clean sheets, with only Brighton (seven) and Arsenal (five) having conceded fewer goals than Daniel Farke's side (eight).

His performances have helped Leeds to 12th in the table before this weekend's fixtures - just two points off the European places - and he says the club are only going to get better.

"I just think it's an incredible club," he said. "You look at the fanbase, everyone here - it's an incredible place. It's just somewhere that's only going and getting better.

"We'd love to get back into Europe. I think every club you ask would like to get into Europe, so the main goal is just to keep on improving."

Plymouth Argyle will get the opportunity to freshen up their ranks this winter with some reinforcements. PAFC are sat in 5th place in the League One table after their first 14 fixtures as they eye promotion back to the Championship.

The Pilgrims should target a move for Walsall striker Aaron Pressley to add more competition and depth to their attacking department. At the age of 25, he has already picked up a decent amount of EFL experience and he has potential to grow and develop further down the line.

Pressley, who is an ex-Scotland youth international, is a key player for the Saddlers and has proved in League Two that he knows where the back of the net is. He has made 15 appearances in all competitions for Lee Grant‘s side in this campaign, 12 of which have come in the league, and he has scored 10 goals already.

Plymouth Argyle are in the play-offs along with Oxford United, Luton Town and Reading as they chase down Huddersfield Town and Stockport County. They take on non-league Alvechurch at home in the FA Cup this weekend.

Plymouth Argyle sold key attacker Lorent Tolaj to Bristol City in the summer. PAFC have since missed his presence and goal scoring touch.

Welshman Will Evans was brought in as his replacement from Mansfield Town. However, he has only scored three times and hasn’t been prolific in Devon.

The Pilgrims are the lowest scorers in the top six and need to be more ruthless in front of goal. Therefore, signing another forward in January has to be a priority.

Pressley fits the bill for Plymouth Argyle and will be full of confidence at the moment. He is under contract at Walsall until June 2028 meaning he still has just under two years left to run down.

Standing at 6ft 4inc, Pressley is solid in the air and would give Plymouth Argyle another dimension going forward. He would be useful for PAFC to throw on if they needed a goal.

The Edinburgh-born man moved down to England from Hearts as a youngster and had spells at Aston Villa and Brentford in the Premier League. He has since had stints at AFC Wimbledon, Accrington Stanley, Stevenage and Barrow.

His move to Walsall in 2025 has worked out well for all parties and he is one of their most prized assets. The Saddlers could lose him soon though and it wouldn’t be a surprise to see other teams sniffing around him.

Agent Distraction

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

What is easy for humans to skim past in code, agents see as a call to action. They do not know better, latching on to that which is littered across the files read during the planning and research phase of the coding journey.

As agents' agency through tools and skills grows the ability for side-quests scales proportionally. Obsolete specs, long resolved bugs, and lagging documentation no longer rest in long forgotten corners of the codebase, but rather are unintentionally ingested as context indirecting and misdirecting the next wave of coders - the agents. This indirection is not free: in the best case it only costs tokens, steps, and time - in the worst case it distracts and grounds the agent on falsehoods leading to incorrect logic and decisions during implementation.

Measuring how often agents fall into these traps across millions of monthly sessions is impossible with manual human review - we need an objective signal derivable from the code itself. Inline TODO(bug/...) comments provide the perfect proxy. TODOs tend to be left behind long after the underlying bug is resolved or abandoned, while the linked bug ticket acts as the canonical record. By cross-referencing every TODO an agent encounters against the TODO's bug status and the user's prompt, we can automatically separate valid work from wasted detours at scale.

Controlling for developer, codebase complexity, task type, model tier, number of files read, initial prompt length, and the step where the bug reference appeared, using a multi-way fixed-effects regression with developer-clustered standard errors, we see that unprompted bug detours add substantial overhead (all stat sig p<0.0001). AI Overviews: Why Brand Mentions Matter More Than Backlinks

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Posted on Nov 6 • Originally published at blog.geosearchleads.com

Backlinks remain an important part of the web's discovery and authority ecosystem, but they may not be the strongest off-site signal associated with a brand appearing in Google AI Overviews. An Ahrefs analysis of 75,000 brands found that branded web mentions had a substantially stronger correlation with AI Overview visibility than raw backlink counts.

The finding does not mean links no longer matter, nor does it establish that mentions cause AI citations. It does, however, give brands a clearer indication of what may distinguish companies that are repeatedly surfaced in AI-generated answers: being discussed, described and corroborated across the web.

According to Ahrefs' analysis of AI Overview brand visibility factors, branded web mentions correlated with AI Overview mentions at 0.664. Raw backlink counts showed a correlation of 0.218. In practical terms, the association for web mentions was roughly three times stronger in this dataset.

Ahrefs compared several off-site brand signals with the number of AI Overview mentions observed for the brands in its dataset. Branded web mentions were the strongest reported factor among those highlighted. Branded anchor text and branded search volume also showed stronger relationships than raw backlink totals.

The paid-brand metrics were weakly correlated with AI Overview mentions, at 0.216 for branded ad traffic and 0.215 for branded ad cost. That is a useful distinction for teams evaluating whether paid brand activity can substitute for broader recognition. The analysis suggests that ad spend alone is not reliably associated with AI Overview inclusion.

The distribution in the data was also uneven. About 26% of brands recorded no AI Overview mentions. Meanwhile, brands in the top quartile for web mentions averaged 169 AI Overview mentions, compared with 14 for the next tier. That gap points to a concentrated visibility pattern in which widely discussed brands can have a disproportionate presence.

A backlink can indicate that one page references another, but its meaning varies widely. Links may be navigational, promotional, syndicated or only loosely related to the subject a user asks about. A branded mention, especially in a relevant editorial context, can more directly connect a company to a category, product type, problem or expertise.

That distinction matters for generative search. AI-generated answers need to synthesize claims from material they can identify and compare. A brand that appears in independent reviews, editorial comparisons, specialist publications and relevant community discussions leaves more evidence that it is associated with a particular topic.

This is not an argument to abandon link earning. High-quality editorial coverage can produce both a citation-worthy mention and a valuable link. The practical change is to stop treating the link itself as the sole outcome. Teams should assess whether coverage accurately explains what the brand does, where it fits, and which claims can be independently verified.

Third-party recognition is only one side of the equation. A brand still needs clear source material on its own site. If product pages, service pages and documentation are vague, inconsistent or difficult to parse, external mentions cannot fully solve the problem.

Start by reviewing whether important pages provide direct, supportable answers to the questions a buyer or researcher would ask. Useful content often includes clearly stated capabilities, specifications, limitations, definitions, pricing or process information where applicable, and evidence behind material claims.

The goal is not to create pages solely for an AI system. It is to make first-party information specific enough for people, search engines and answer systems to understand. Consistent terminology across pages also helps reinforce the entities and topics a business wants to be associated with.

Traditional digital PR is often evaluated by placements and referring domains. Those remain useful measures, but the Ahrefs findings suggest the brief should include a second question: will this coverage establish a meaningful association between the brand and a relevant topic?

Original research, proprietary data, expert commentary and useful tools can give publishers a concrete reason to mention a company in context. A generic announcement may generate a short-lived burst of attention, while a credible data point or distinctive expert perspective can be cited in coverage that explains a category.

This approach should not become an attempt to manufacture mentions. Repetitive, low-quality placements may add noise without building the trust signals that independent coverage is meant to provide.

The strongest off-site footprint is not limited to press coverage. Relevant independent reviews, editorial comparisons and niche discussions can help demonstrate that a brand is recognized beyond its own marketing materials.

For ecommerce businesses, that may mean reputable product reviews and comparisons that accurately describe the offering. For software companies, it may include analyst-style coverage, practitioner reviews or discussions in specialist communities. For agencies and professional services firms, expert commentary, case-based editorial coverage and credible industry citations may be more relevant.

Quality and relevance matter more than a simple mention total. The research reports correlations, not a formula for earning AI Overview inclusion. A large number of irrelevant mentions should not be treated as an equivalent substitute for trusted, topically aligned references.

A useful audit combines first-party clarity with an assessment of how the wider web describes the brand.

Map priority topics and questions. Identify the products, services, use cases and category questions for which AI Overview visibility would be meaningful. Check whether your site has a clear, factual page supporting each topic.

Review third-party topic associations. Collect relevant editorial coverage, reviews, comparisons and specialist discussions. Look for whether they name the brand, describe it accurately and connect it to the intended category or use case.

Separate mentions from links in reporting. Track referring domains and backlinks, but also monitor unlinked mentions, branded anchors and the context of coverage. A link report alone may miss the signals most closely associated with AI Overview visibility in the Ahrefs dataset.

Monitor the answers themselves. Search results can vary by query and change over time. Record whether relevant AI Overviews appear for priority queries, which brands are cited or mentioned, and whether the underlying sources reveal content gaps or third-party validation opportunities.

The most useful next step is not chasing a single metric, but building a repeatable view of where your site explains key topics well and where independent web evidence is still limited.

Do backlinks still matter for AI Overview visibility?

Yes, backlinks can still support search visibility and discovery. However, Ahrefs' analysis found raw backlink counts had a weaker correlation with AI Overview mentions, at 0.218, than branded web mentions, branded anchors and branded search volume.

Do brand mentions cause a company to appear in AI Overviews?

No. The Ahrefs findings show correlation, not causation. Brand mentions may be associated with other factors, such as genuine market recognition, strong content or broader editorial coverage.

Can paid search activity improve AI Overview visibility?

The analysis found weak correlations for branded ad traffic, at 0.216, and branded ad cost, at 0.215. The data does not support treating paid brand activity as a reliable route to AI Overview mentions.

What types of third-party coverage are most useful?

Relevant independent coverage is likely more useful than generic publicity. Examples discussed in the research include reviews, editorial comparisons and niche discussions that accurately corroborate a brand's capabilities or topical relevance.

The Ahrefs study shifts the AI Overview conversation beyond link volume. Brands seeking stronger visibility should continue earning quality links, but pair that work with extractable first-party facts and credible third-party evidence that consistently associates them with the topics they want to be known for.

Looking a word up, and looking a claim up

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Looking a word up, and looking a claim up

Two things I do every week, and they look like the same activity right up until the moment you try to automate them.

In one case I have a word and want to know what it means. In the other I have a claim and want to know whether it holds up. The first is a lookup. The second is a comparison, and it needs a different kind of reference.

Knowing a word and not knowing it are different problems, and the second one is not solved by a dictionary.

A dictionary tells you the accepted meaning. What you usually need is narrower: what the word means in the specific community you are in, where it came from, and whether using it sounds right or sounds like a joke.

Taboords is that kind of reference: definitions of gírias, siglas, relationship terms, psychology, subcultures and Japanese culture, with the origin and the register next to each entry, at https://taboords.com.

The origin is the part that earns the lookup. A word with a documented origin is a word you can use with confidence, because you know the context it came from.

The other case is harder to recognise because it feels like reading. Something tells you a supplement works, or that a training method is better, or that a number means something, and you want to check it before you spend money on it.

The problem is that marketing copy and evidence look identical when you read them in a hurry. The claim is always confident. The difference is whether anybody lined several of them up against the same criteria and published the result.

Treino is that kind of base: supplement comparisons, exercise references and calculators, arranged for practical choices rather than for browsing, at https://treinando.org.

There is one practical way to tell the two apart, and it is about how you leave the page.

After a word lookup you leave with a word. After a claim lookup you should leave with a number and a reason, because the useful output is not the verdict but the criteria that produced it.

Neither of these is a substitute for thinking. But a reference that gives you the criteria is doing half the work for you, and that is most of what people actually need from a lookup site.
