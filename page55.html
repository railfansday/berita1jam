Ultraprocessed Foods Alter Food-Reward Brain Activity

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

A controlled feeding trial reveals that just two weeks on a diet high in ultraprocessed foods alters brain reward responses in emerging adults ages 18 to 21, while leaving adults ages 22 to 25 unaffected. The fMRI study shows blunted activation in the developing orbitofrontal cortex, a key region for food valuation and self-control, even when diets are strictly matched for calories, sugar, fat, and nutrients.

Ultraprocessed foods, products formulated with industrial processing methods and ingredients rarely found in a home kitchen, account for more than half of the calories consumed by young adults in the United States. While extensive research links these foods to obesity, metabolic syndrome, and cardiovascular disease, neuroscientists are increasingly uncovering how industrial processing directly interacts with the developing human brain.

In a controlled feeding study published this month in the journal Obesity, researchers at Virginia Tech’s Fralin Biomedical Research Institute at VTC and Department of Human Nutrition, Foods, and Exercise found that emerging adults ages 18 to 21 exhibit distinct neural changes in food-reward circuitry after consuming an ultraprocessed diet for just two weeks.

Led by first author Dr. Emma Leslie alongside neuroscientist Dr. Alex DiFeliceantonio and registered dietitian Dr. Brenda Davy, the findings complement earlier results from the same trial showing that 18- to 21-year-olds consumed excess calories at a buffet meal after eating ultraprocessed foods, even when they were no longer hungry.

To separate the effects of industrial food processing from basic nutritional content, the researchers recruited 22 healthy adults ages 18 to 25 (including seven participants ages 21 or younger) for a randomized crossover feeding trial. Each participant followed two strictly controlled diets for two weeks apiece, separated by a four-week washout period:

Critically, both diets were engineered to have identical macronutrient profiles (50% carbohydrates, 35% fat, 15% protein), matching levels of fiber, added sugars, saturated fat, sodium, energy density, and overall nutritional quality, calibrated to keep each participant’s body weight stable.

Before and after each two-week dietary phase, participants underwent functional MRI (fMRI) brain scans while receiving small sips of an ultraprocessed milkshake or a tasteless control solution through a specialized delivery tube.

When the researchers analyzed the neuroimaging data, they discovered a striking developmental divide:

“Among the younger participants, consuming a diet high in ultraprocessed foods for just two weeks was associated with decreased brain response to a rewarding food — a milkshake — in the orbitofrontal cortex,” explained Leslie, who conducted the work as a doctoral student at Virginia Tech and is now a postdoctoral associate at the University of Michigan.

Located just behind the eyes, the orbitofrontal cortex is one of the final regions of the human brain to finish maturing, continuing to remodel into the mid- to late 20s. It plays a central role in evaluating food rewards, regulating cravings, and exerting executive self-control to stop eating.

Because the two diets were matched across nutritional metrics, the findings point toward non-nutritive properties of ultraprocessed foods, such as chemical additives, emulsifiers, texture alterations, or compounds generated during industrial processing, as potential drivers of altered brain signaling.

“This study is important because you have this change in an area of the brain known to govern self-control and potentially put a stop to eating,” said DiFeliceantonio, associate professor at the Fralin Biomedical Research Institute and interim co-director of its Center for Health Behaviors Research. “This is giving us insight into how ultraprocessed foods are affecting the bodies and brains of younger people, who might be especially vulnerable to their effects. And that’s important to know for things like dietary guidelines, doctor recommendations, and policy decisions.”

While the researchers note that the sample size was small and the age-based findings were exploratory, and did not directly correlate individual fMRI shifts with buffet calorie intake, the results highlight adolescence and early adulthood as critical windows of neurodevelopmental sensitivity.

Next, DiFeliceantonio and Davy plan to investigate whether adolescents under 18, who consume the highest proportion of ultraprocessed foods of any U.S. demographic, show even stronger neural sensitivity, and whether brain responses recover once individuals return to whole-food diets.

How do ultraprocessed foods affect the brains of young adults?

A Virginia Tech study found that after eating a diet high in ultraprocessed foods for just two weeks, young adults ages 18 to 21 showed decreased brain activity in the orbitofrontal cortex, a region involved in food reward, valuation, and self-control, when tasting a milkshake. Adults ages 22 to 25 did not show these brain changes.

Was the brain effect simply caused by extra sugar, fat, or calories?

No. Both the ultraprocessed diet and the unprocessed comparison diet contained the exact same amounts of calories, carbohydrates, fat, protein, added sugars, saturated fat, fiber, and sodium. This suggests that industrial food processing or additives themselves, rather than basic nutrients, drove the brain changes.

Why are 18- to 21-year-olds more sensitive to ultraprocessed foods than slightly older adults?

The orbitofrontal cortex is one of the last brain regions to finish developing, continuing to mature into the mid- to late 20s. Because this self-control and reward-evaluation circuit is still actively wiring in 18- to 21-year-olds, it appears more sensitive to dietary shifts.

Funding: The research was supported by a grant to DiFeliceantonio and Davy from the Eunice Kennedy Shriver National Institute of Child Health and Human Development, part of the National Institutes of Health. Leslie was also supported with an exercise health sciences graduate fellowship from Dr. Ray A. Gaskins.

The average American consumes 55% of daily energy from ultraprocessed foods (UPF), created through industrial processes and additives not used at home. We investigated if a high-UPF diet alters brain response to milkshake compared with a diet free from UPF in emerging adults, who are in a critical brain development period and typically consume high amounts of UPF.

In a randomized controlled crossover trial participants aged 18–25 years completed two 2-week controlled feeding periods including a UPF (81% UPF) and non-UPF (0% UPF) diet. Before and after each diet intervention, participants consumed milkshake concomitant with functional magnetic resonance imaging.

In the entire cohort, there were no differences between diet conditions in brain response. An exploratory analysis revealed orbitofrontal cortex response to milkshake decreased after the UPF diet and increased following the non-UPF diet in late adolescents (18–21 years) but not young adults (22–25 years). In all participants, habitual UPF intake (g) was positively associated with orbitofrontal cortex response to milkshake, independent of diet condition.

An acute UPF dietary intervention may only alter brain response in late adolescents. Further work is needed to determine potential vulnerability of late adolescents to changes in dietary UPF on brain response to rewards.

GTA 6 won't let you use cheats until you complete the main story, and locks progression when you do

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Here's a twist: you can't use cheats in Grand Theft Auto 6 until you complete the entire game's main story.

That's what we discovered from our tests at least, after loading in saves at various points through the game to be sure, and it's also both a first for the series and a departure even from how developer Rockstar handled cheats in Red Dead Redemption 2. In that game,approximately half of the cheats were available from the off, and the other half required discovering specific newspapers from around the game's world to unlock.

In GTA 6, you'll again need to do some legwork. This time it appears that all of the game's cheats not only require you to finish the story, but also complete various tasks or challenges to unlock them.

The way it works is simple enough. Once you finish the main story, the "Cheats" tab - previously hidden - quietly appears at the far end of the Progress menu, accessible when you open the in-game map and scroll with the bumper buttons. From there, you can view various sub-sections of cheats, sorted by category, and a list of 10 or so cheats for each.

With each cheat there'll be a short explanation of what it does, and what's required to unlock it and, once unlocked, guidance on how to perform it. This is done via whipping out your phone, holding down the shoulder buttons and using various D-Pad inputs - much the same as GTA 5.

There's also another caveat to bear in mind: when you use a cheat, you'll see a notification warning you that the game can't be saved while it's active, and that Trophies and Achievements are also disabled until you end that session. This makes sense, once you understand the nature of GTA 6's story - there is a cheat for giving yourself a load of money, for instance; another departure from GTA 5 where that popular cheat was removed - but given you'll need certain amounts of cash at various points of the story, enabling you to shortcut that would disrupt the way the narrative unfolds.

We'll have a guide from yours truly to all the game's cheats live on Eurogamer in the very near future, as the game releases here in the UK. In the meantime, if you find yourself on the fence about this one, be sure to give our GTA 6 review a look. As I put it there, giving the game five stars: it is "the most believable and unbelievable video game world ever made".

Judge found guilty of drunk driving but without certainty of elevated blood alcohol level

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

A judge in Michigan was found guilty Monday of drunk driving after he rear-ended a public transit bus, but a district court judge ruled that prosecutors failed to prove beyond a reasonable doubt that his blood alcohol level met the state’s threshold for intoxication. (Image from Shutterstock)

A judge in Michigan was found guilty Monday of drunk driving after he rear-ended a public transit bus, but a district court judge ruled that prosecutors failed to prove beyond a reasonable doubt that his blood alcohol level met the state’s threshold for intoxication.

Warren 37th District Court Chief Judge John M. Chmura, 63, was found guilty of intoxication while driving his Ford Fusion into a parked bus that was displaying hazard lights March 25. The court rejected the charge of Chmura driving with an elevated high blood alcohol content of 0.17 or greater, according to coverage on Law360.

Novi 52nd District Court Judge David Law noted that Chmura’s judgment had been affected, but he questioned the blood alcohol concentration at the time of the crash.

“This court may think it knows the answer to whether or not this was a high BAC case,” Law said. “But thinking it knows is not enough. It needs to be proven beyond a reasonable doubt.”

A bus driver sitting inside the bus waiting to resume his route was uninjured in the crash. Sterling Heights, Michigan, police officer Jonathan Journeau testified that Chmura smelled of alcohol, slurred his speech and couldn’t maintain his balance.

Chmura’s attorney, David J. Kramer of the David J. Kramer Law Firm, argued that prosecutors could not establish the higher offense because Journeau failed to determine when the judge’s final alcoholic beverage was consumed.

Would Ukraine trade land for NATO membership or protection?

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

The US is exploring whether Ukraine would cede parts of Donetsk province still under its control in return for NATO membership or protection. Experts warn the idea is risky.

The US has asked Ukraine whether it would be willing to cede Donetsk province to Russia in return for NATO membership. Together, Donetsk and Luhansk constitute the so-called Donbas region. Russia currently controls most of Luhansk province, and large swathes of Donetsk, though Ukraine continues holding a number of major cities there, including Kramatorsk and Sloviansk.

So far, no concrete proposal had been put forward, according to Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy. He said he would publicly discuss the matter once the US and NATO member states had agreed on the plan, stressing that he could not make any such decision "on his own." Zelenskyy also said any potential agreement would go ahead "without recognizing Russia's occupation of these territories."

The Ukrainian leader said the US was talking about guarantees similar to those enshrined in Articles 4 and 5 of the North Atlantic Treaty, though did not envision US troops on Ukrainian soil. Article 4 provides for consultations among member states in the event of imminent threats, while Article 5 stipulates that an attack on one state is considered an attack on all. The US, however, has said it pictures bilateral guarantees for Ukraine similar to those established with Israel and certain Middle Eastern countries.

Russia has yet to comment on these proposals, with President Putin's spokesperson Dmitry Peskov stating that "we maintain our position that, in the interest of the peace process, it is not appropriate to disclose details of the talks between us and the Americans."

Experts interviewed by DW are skeptical of the land-for-security-guarantees idea, objecting that the US cannot uphold such an arrangement on its own.

"It seems to me that this is what happens to normal diplomacy when it's been run by real estate agents who are used to selling people something that doesn't really exist," Oleksandr Kraiev of the North America-Israel Research Program at Ukraine's Ukrainian Prism think tank tells DW. He says that at least half of NATO's member states have reservations about Ukraine's accession.

"We'll just end up with a second Budapest Memorandum, it'll just carry a different name," Kraiev told DW. The original 1994 Budapest Memorandum stipulated that Russia, the US and UK respect Ukraine's independence and sovereignty in its existing borders and refrain from the threat or use of force to change its territorial integrity. Ukraine in return committed to giving up all nuclear weapons.

The Budapest Memorandum, however, did not contain any commitment by the UK or US to defend Ukraine in the event of an attack.

Most experts interviewed by DW are critical of the idea of holding a referendum to let ordinary Ukrainians decide whether their country should hand over full control of Donetsk province in exchange for NATO membership.

This is because ceding territory would not only violate Ukraine's constitution and harm its national interests, but would also undermine a world order "in which borders cannot be redrawn by force," Oleksandr Khara of Ukraine's Centre for Defence Strategies told DW. "It doesn't matter whether the citizens of Ukraine vote for or against such a proposal," Khara said, adding that any such referendum would set "a very dangerous precedent."

A survey by the Kyiv International Institute of Sociology (KISS) conducted between October 8 and 16 found that 61% of respondents categorically reject withdrawing troops from Ukrainian-controlled parts of the Donbas, such as cities like Kramatorsk and Sloviansk, even in exchange for US and European security guarantees. 35% of respondents said they would begrudgingly accept a land-for-security deal.

Defense analyst Khara doubts a bilateral agreement between Ukraine and the US, akin to those established between the US and Israel, would guarantee Kyiv any more support than it presently receives.

"This concerns financial support, joint weapons production and development, and the training of Ukrainian troops — everything that falls below the threshold of collective security," Khara told DW. "It is good that these commitments — security agreements, political declarations, and the allocation of funds in the budgets of the countries supporting us — are set out in writing, at least for the next five years." Yet to prevent another war, Khara believes that ideally, Ukraine needs "a nuclear shield," specifically, a "strategic one from the US" and a "tactical one from Europe."

Ukrainian experts already floated to the idea of an alternative security arrangement to NATO members in December 2023, says Sergiy Solodkyy of Ukraine's New Europe research center. The arrangement would see Ukraine join the alliance within its internationally recognized borders, though specific protocols would stipulate that only territories under Kyiv's control would be defended.

This arrangement could be modeled on the German case, where West Germany became a member of NATO first, while East Germany joined the alliance only after German reunification.

West Germany never gave up on reunification, Solodkyy told DW. "That means we, too, should not give up on our territory or recognize the border." That is why he says the land-for-security deal proposed to Ukraine is unacceptable.

"This is a trap that Russia and the US negotiators are trying to lure the Ukrainian side into," Solodkyy told DW.

What Watch Will Robert Pattinson Wear as James Bond? It's Complicated.

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Timepieces are almost as much a part of 007’s wardrobe as his dinner jackets.

Perhaps you've heard? There's a new James Bond in town!

Robert Pattinson has been announced as the next 007, and while most of the speculation has understandably focused on how the former vampire/current Batman might approach Britain's foremost secret agent, watch aficionados are asking another question: What will he wear on his wrist?

Watches, after all, are almost as much a part of Bond's wardrobe as his dinner jackets, Aston Martins, and vodka martinis. Sean Connery famously wore a Rolex Submariner, Roger Moore experimented with Seiko's digital watches, and Omega has been 007's watch of choice since Pierce Brosnan strapped on a trusty Seamaster in GoldenEye in 1995. That brand remains, of course, a paid partner of the franchise.

Yesterday's reveal showed no watch on the newly anointed 007's wrist, and Pattinson's own taste in watches is rather different from the rugged, sporty timepieces traditionally associated with Bond.

In fact, he doesn't seem particularly keen on big watches at all.

"Whenever a watch gets too thick and it keeps your cuff up, I don't really understand the appeal," he told one interviewer as recently September. "A lot of guys really like that, but I don't think I can really pull that off. I need to only have a half reveal."

And when asked what he looks for in a watch, he was even more specific.

"I definitely like really thin watches. That's the main thing. In general, I would probably prefer a leather strap."

Which is quite a contrast with the substantial divers we've become accustomed to seeing on Bond's wrist.

The fact is, until recently, Pattinson wasn't much of a watch enthusiast at all. That changed at this year's Oscars, when he wore a then-unreleased rose-gold Jaeger-LeCoultre Master Control Chronomètre Perpetual Calendar. Other actors apparently began asking him about it at the afterparty, and something clicked.

He subsequently became an ambassador for Jaeger-LeCoultre, although his relationship with the brand goes back much further. As a teenager, he borrowed his mother's Reverso to impress his schoolmates. More recently, he's revealed that Bruce Wayne will wear a Reverso in the next Batman film.

On screen, Pattinson's watch choices have been interesting. He wore a Chanel J12 in David Cronenberg's Cosmopolis, while Christopher Nolan's Tenet, in which he played a mysterious secret agent, featured a Bond-adjacent Omega De Ville.

Meanwhile, the announcement has prompted some entertaining activity on Instagram. Within hours of Pattinson being named Bond, Jaeger-LeCoultre posted pictures of its new ambassador wearing the Master Control Chronomètre Perpetual Calendar. Omega, meanwhile, posted an image of its James Bond Seamaster. And today, Jaeger-LeCoultre has been back with more Pattinson pictures.

It's a little social-media tit-for-tat, although there's no reason the two relationships can't coexist. For now, at least, there's a straightforward distinction: Pattinson can wear whatever he likes. Bond has his own watch.

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Giorgio Chiellini says that his overall assessment of the Italy national team over the last two months is positive despite ending the calendar year on a sour note with a 4-1 defeat against Belgium, and insists that the light at the end of the tunnel is starting to become visible.

Juventus’s Chief Club Affairs Officer spoke to reporters at the inaugural event for the Juventus Official Fan Club European Union in Belgium on Wednesday.

Chiellini shared his thoughts on the Italy national team since the return of Roberto Mancini as head coach and said that it is the nation’s ‘duty’ to support the Azzurri through their transitional period ‘and the people who are making it happen’.

Italy have won three, drawn one and lost twice since Mancini returned as national team head coach over the summer. Both defeats came against Belgium, in their first and final matches of the Nations League group stage campaign.

Italy had sat at the top of their Nations League group heading into the final round of group stage fixtures thanks to a 3-1 win at home against France last week, but a 4-1 defeat in Brussels saw them overtaken by both Belgium and Les Bleus on Sunday night.

“Of course I wanted the last few matches. I think it’s the start of a process, and one that has moved quicker than expected,” Chiellini told reporters on Wednesday (via Mediaset).

“Since the first defeat in Rome, there has been significant growth. It’s not easy to get all games right at this level when they’re just a few days apart.

“So, I believe the assessment of these last two months in this tournament is positive and the light at the end of the tunnel is visible.”

Italy have picked up 10 points from their last five matches against Belgium, France (twice) and Turkey (twice). That was not quite enough for a spot in the Nations League quarter-finals, but was enough to avoid relegation to Nations League League B.

“There’s still a long road ahead and it won’t be easy, it takes patience, strength and a lot of willpower, but I believe it is our duty to support this restart and the people who are making it happen,” Chiellini concluded.

One of the beautiful things about traveling around the world is the chance to experience hospitality in different ways in different countries. I listen to music during the day as I work on this blog and on my other work. Some people require absolute silence to work, but I like music, specially Christian hymns. I primarily use YouTube and of course the algorithm gets to know you. There’s no music I like more than a powerful organ playing a beloved hymn. Lately, the algorithm has been suggesting a very gifted organist named Lauma Akmene, a Latvian-born musician who is the organist, conductor, and music director at St. Peter’s Episcopal Church in Salisbury, Maryland.

As I listened to her music all morning, one of her YouTube shorts noted that she would be performing a classical concert in Los Angeles, which immediately piqued my interest.

I obtained ticket and found the concert would be held at a place called Stan’s Musical Parlor, which was actually a beautiful 1926 home in LA’s posh Lafayette Square neighborhood. Not knowing quite what to expect, I showed up and was warmly greeted by Stanley Harris himself, who I came to find out has opened his house since 1986 to showcase musical talents. Dr. Harris is a retired psychiatrist and was a longtime clinician and faculty member at the University of Southern California.

Stan has a Johannus-BachMen-Hauptwerk organ (including Hauptwerk virtual pipe organs) in his living room. Essentially, this digital organ can be set to emulate sounds from many of famous pipe organs around the world.

The concert began at 8:00 pm and was simply fabulous, with Lauma playing several classic and neo-classic works.

One of the guests mentioned that he was hosting Lauma the following evening at his home in Malibu, and invited me to attend. I had to be in Palm Springs for work that day, but I drove from Palms Springs all the way to Malibu via Pacific Coast Highway (took about three hours!) and made it just in time for the concert, another fabulous evening of food, fellowship, and great music.

Already, I’ve made friends through these two events, who I am staying in touch with, and will go to more of these concerts. Community is beautiful thing, as is hospitality, and all in my backyard, one of the many reasons I love Los Angeles and Southern California so much.

Truth be told, I almost did not go to either event. I was tired and probably could have used a couple extra hours of sleep. But I’m so glad I did go and I’m thankful to YouTube for suggesting Lauma and making these two possible in the first place.

Housing-focused data and analytics company HouseCanary announced today that Weichert, Realtors® has joined the Google Listings Program as the tech giant continues to expand its latest foray into residential listing.

The active listings for Weichert’s company-owned offices in its five-state footprint of New Jersey, New York, Pennsylvania, Virginia and Maryland are now live and available for display in Google mobile search results in live markets, according to a release from HouseCanary.

Weichert is only the latest to join the program, following three others that included REMAX Advantage, Berkshire Hathaway HomeServices Florida Properties Group and ENRG Realty.

According to HouseCanary, these listings are now among those that appear in a carousel at the top of Google mobile search results. Each listing shows basic property details and listing brokerage attribution, and consumers can tap through to a detail page with more information about the home, the listing agent’s name and a click-to-contact button.

“Weichert is one of the most recognized names in residential real estate, and its decision to join reflects how the US’s largest brokerages are recognizing the value of getting as much exposure for their listings as possible,” said Chris Rediger, CEO of HouseCanary. “We are thrilled to have Weichert, Realtors in the Google Listings Program.”

HouseCanary also connects directly with brokerage companies, such as Weichert, Realtors®, to bring their listings into the program, via a participating MLS.

“Joining the Google Listings Program is an exciting step forward toward maximizing visibility for Weichert listings and delivering high-intent leads to our agents,” said Joseph McDonald, president of residential sales and real estate services for Weichert, Realtors®. “The initiative creates a streamlined home search experience for consumers and a direct path for buyers to connect with Weichert agents. Kia went from this one quirky EV 40 years ago to an entire electric lineup

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The Kia Besta EV hit the roads in 1986 as the brand’s first fully electric vehicle. 40 years later, Kia continues to “lead the pack” with a full lineup of EVs ranging from entry-level cars and SUVs to three-row models, and just about everything in between.

Kia wanted to design a new vehicle for the 1986 Asian Games, which were held in Seoul, South Korea, one that did not emit any toxic fumes in front of runners.

So, it created the Besta EV. The Besta van was initially developed 40 years ago by Kia’s Central Research Institute as the successor to the popular Bongo van, which is still sold in Korea and other markets today.

Unlike the Bongo, it was developed using Kia’s in-house tech rather than sourced from another automaker and rebadged as its own.

Kia swapped the 2.0L four-cylinder diesel engine for a 35 kWh battery and electric motor, which provided a range of more than 26 miles (42 km) on a single charge.

While the Besta EV was never actually sold to the public, it laid the groundwork and offered an early glimpse at Kia’s shift to electrification.

The first mass-produced Korean EV certified for commercial sale was the Kia Pride EV in 1993. In 2011, the Ray EV became Kia’s first series-production electric car.

Kia expanded its electrification ambitions to Europe and the UK in 2014 with the first-generation Soul EV, the beloved boxy, funky crossover that was discontinued after the 2025 model year.

The Korean automaker has come a long way since the Besta EV, with a full lineup of electric vehicles ranging from compact cars to mid-size and three-row SUVs, vans, and just about everything in between.

Kia now offers an electric option for nearly everyone with seven-passenger EVs on sale in the UK and Europe, including the EV2, EV3, EV4, EV5, EV6, EV9, and PV5 Passenger van.

With its first software-defined vehicle (SDV) and a new B-segment electric hatchback set to launch by the end of 2027, Kia has no plans to slow down. On top of this, Kia will expand its PBV lineup with the PV7 next year, a larger sibling to the PV5.

Four decades after the Besta EV arrived, Kia said it continues to “lead the pack today” with one of the most diverse all-electric EV lineups of any brand.

Through October, Kia has sold over 135,000 EVs in the UK, with fully electric vehicles now accounting for over 37% of total sales.

Thanks to its new EV lineup, Kia became “Europe’s fastest-growing top 10 passenger-car brand.” According to the European Automobile Manufacturers’ Association, Kia registered 377,078 vehicles between January and August 2026, up 9% compared to the same period last year.

Sjoerd Knipping, Chief Operating Officer of Kia Europe, said the growth is thanks to having “one of the broadest and most advanced electrified line-ups in Europe.”

In the US, Kia launched the compact EV3 (check out our review of it) this year, starting at just $29,890. It joins the EV6, EV9, and Niro EV in Kia’s US electric vehicle lineup.
