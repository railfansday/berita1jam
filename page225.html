Tesla wins trade zone status for Texas Megapacks, but China tariffs stay

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Tesla’s Megapack factory in Brookshire, Texas, is now officially a foreign-trade zone, a US customs program that lets manufacturers defer duties on imported parts. The FTZ Board’s approval, published in the Federal Register on Monday, runs for 14 months.

It’s easy to read this as Tesla getting a pass on tariffs for the Chinese LFP cells inside its Megapacks. Tesla’s own application says otherwise: parts hit by Section 301, 232, or 122 tariffs still pay them.

Tesla filed the request on May 20 for Subzone 84AM, part of FTZ 84 in the Houston area. That’s the factory where Tesla started Megapack 3 production in August, with a planned capacity of 50 GWh per year.

The application lists roughly 80 imported components Tesla wants to bring in under zone procedures, from coolant pumps and transformers to busbars, wiring harnesses, circuit boards and battery cells. Duty rates on those parts go up to 6.5%, while finished Megapacks and Bento inverters range from duty-free to 3.4%.

That gap is the usual reason companies set up an FTZ. A manufacturer in a zone can normally elect to pay the finished product’s lower rate instead of the rate on each imported part.

But the application spells out the limit. Components subject to duties under Section 122 of the Trade Act, Section 232 of the Trade Expansion Act, or Section 301 “require subject merchandise to be admitted to FTZs in privileged foreign status,” according to the notice.

Privileged foreign status locks in a part’s own classification and tariff treatment when it enters the zone. So a Chinese LFP cell that ends up in a Megapack sold in the US still pays the Section 301 tariff on Chinese non-EV lithium-ion batteries, which jumped from 7.5% to 25% on January 1, 2026. Tesla just pays it later, when the finished Megapack leaves the zone.

The approval came with the same wording the FTZ Board has used all year. Production was “authorized on a limited basis,” and “further subject to a fourteen-month time period.”

Tesla got an identical decision for Megapack and inverter production at Fremont, Livermore and Lathrop in California this summer, with a one-year limit instead. Eastman Chemical, battery pack maker Rose Batteries, and chipmaker Qorvo all received similar time-limited, “limited basis” approvals in 2026.

The 14-month clock puts the expiry around the end of 2027. That’s roughly when LG Energy Solution is due to start supplying Tesla with LFP cells from Michigan under a $4.3 billion contract for Megapack 3, with mass production expected in the second half of 2027.

Tesla hasn’t hidden the issue. When tariffs escalated in April 2025, CFO Vaibhav Taneja said the impact on energy storage would be “outsized since we source lithium iron phosphate (LFP) battery cells from China.” We wrote at the time that Tesla’s only growing business was kneecapped by Trump’s tariffs.

Tesla has since brought up its own LFP cell factory in Nevada, but Taneja said it would only cover a fraction of Tesla’s installed capacity. And Brookshire alone is designed for 50 GWh a year, on top of the 40 GWh Lathrop factory in California.

5 iPhone features you should be using after updating to iOS 27

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

iOS 27 just made your iPhone a lot more capable.

The iPhone is easy to recommend to anyone looking for great smartphone hardware and long-term software support. The previous few iOS releases, however, have been pretty janky. With iOS 27, Apple therefore shifted its focus to ironing out bugs and improving overall stability rather than cramming in another round of flashy new features, and a good chunk of the WWDC 2026 keynote covered improvements such as snappier app launch times, faster AirDrop transfers and better memory management.

Apple's commitment to software updates also means iOS 27 compatibility extends all the way back to the iPhone 11, which is great news since iOS 26 would have been a terrible final major update to be stuck on. If you haven't already updated your iPhone, you can do so by navigating to Settings > General > Software Update.

Not much has changed visually, which makes sense since Apple only recently overhauled its design language with Liquid Glass. There is now a system-wide transparency slider, though, which many users had been asking about. Reports about battery life are mixed — some say they're getting less screen-on time than before, while others haven't had issues. Personally, iOS 27 has been such a blessing performance-wise on my aging iPhone 15 Pro Max that I'm willing to put up with slightly worse battery life.

Of course, stability upgrades aren't the only thing Apple introduced with iOS 27. The update also brings a handful of useful tidbits, and here are five features you should definitely check out after updating your iPhone.

Apple Intelligence has had a rocky start. After all, it took Apple two whole iOS generations to deliver on its promises of a more capable Siri. Most of the Apple Intelligence suite has been pretty underwhelming, unless you're one of the five people who enjoy generating custom emojis using Genmoji, but iOS 27 changes that. The Shortcuts app has received a major upgrade, and Apple Intelligence might finally have a genuinely useful feature to show for itself.

You've been able to create pretty complex shortcuts and automations on iOS for a while now, and you can even import shortcuts that others have created and shared. iOS 27 now lets you create shortcuts using natural language prompts, which Apple Intelligence handles. When you launch the app and tap the + button, you can start describing the shortcut you want.

In my testing, the integration works pretty well and usually creates straightforward shortcuts without issues. That said, it pieces together the wrong actions sometimes and ends up with a shortcut that doesn't do exactly what you want. Fortunately, you can still manually tweak shortcuts created using Apple Intelligence by tapping Edit.

You can also use Apple Intelligence actions within your shortcuts. Apple currently offers three AI models, including an on-device model for faster processing. You can create shortcuts for actions involving information extraction, summarization or generation of text and documents. The Cloud and Cloud Pro models do have a daily rate limit, though.

The Wallet app on the iPhone stores your payment cards, transit passes, event and cinema tickets, digital keys and more. Until now, users could only add digital passes and credentials if the issuer supported Apple Wallet and provided a compatible pass. While several companies and services offered this convenience, plenty of issuers didn't support it. As a collector, I was a bit disheartened to see my local IMAX theater stop offering Apple Wallet passes for movie tickets.

With iOS 27, you can create custom passes and add them to Apple Wallet. This is extremely handy for those with loyalty cards or gym memberships that don't offer native Apple Wallet support. There are two ways you can create a pass in Apple Wallet, and Apple Intelligence comes in handy again here. On supported iPhone models, you can open an image (or screenshot) of a ticket or membership card with a barcode, swipe up and tap on Add Pass to Wallet.

If this option doesn't show up, you can also open the Wallet app, tap the + icon at the top and select Create a Pass. Tap on Continue and take a photo of the ticket or ID card. The Wallet app will then use Visual Intelligence to extract relevant information from the image and create a pass. If you want to change the pass' details or visuals, tap Edit.

For years, Apple's virtual assistant, Siri, has lagged behind competitors like Google Assistant. Slow responses are forgivable, but Siri would often struggle with even the most basic requests. Apple first promised a major Siri overhaul with iOS 18's Apple Intelligence suite, but it's taken over two years to come to fruition. iOS 27 comes with Siri AI, and users with a compatible iPhone can join the waitlist by navigating to Settings > Siri. The AI-powered virtual assistant is still in beta, but things are already starting to look promising.

Siri now understands and fulfills your requests noticeably better, with a huge improvement in how it handles follow-up queries. Though it still takes a hot second to process most requests, its responses are more relevant, and it doesn't hit you with the "Here's what I found on the web" reply as often. What's more impressive about Siri AI, though, is the fact that it now understands personal context.

It can pull information from your messages, emails, photos and more to answer questions or perform tasks that previously required you to manually dig through different apps. Third-party app support is still limited, but if you mainly use iMessage and the default Mail app on your iPhone, you'll likely find Siri to be one of iOS 27's biggest improvements. The Camera app has a new Siri mode as well, which taps into Visual Intelligence to answer questions about whatever you're pointing your camera at.

The AirPods are some of the most popular wireless earbuds on the market, and they work best when paired with an iPhone, iPad or Mac. Most modern AirPods models support Spatial Audio with head tracking, and several music streaming services like Apple Music and Tidal let you enjoy Dolby Atmos content. Sound quality is also widely regarded as one of their strongest selling points, particularly on the higher-end Pro models like the AirPods Pro 3 that we reviewed. With iOS 27, though, you can finally tweak the sound to your liking using a built-in equalizer.

Apps like Apple Music have long allowed you to apply EQ presets, but the new AirPods equalizer works system-wide. The feature is available on AirPods 4 and newer, AirPods Pro 2 and newer and the AirPods Max 2. Connect your AirPods to your iPhone, launch the Settings app, tap on the AirPods settings at the top of the screen and navigate to Audio & Routing > Equalizer.

The Recommended option is selected by default, which delivers the balanced sound profile you've grown used to. If you tap on Custom, you can manually adjust the lows, mids and highs to your liking. If you prefer thumpy bass, increase the lows slider, but if you want vocals to sound clearer, try bumping up the mids or highs instead. Though Apple is only offering a 3-band equalizer, it's still a welcome addition. My AirPods Pro 2 already deliver punchy lows, but as a basshead, I appreciate having the option to crank up the bass even further.

Apple's latest mobile OS also has several additions that make browsing more convenient. For starters, Safari now lets you set up alerts for webpage changes, so you don't have to check yourself. This is useful if you're tracking a product's price, waiting for tickets to go on sale or keeping an eye on when an out-of-stock item becomes available again.

On your iPhone, launch Safari, head to a webpage you want to track, tap on the icon with three horizontal lines in the address bar and select Notify Me. Since this feature uses Apple Intelligence, you can describe what exactly you want Safari to monitor. You can then choose how frequently Safari checks the webpage for changes.

Safari also uses Apple Intelligence to organize your active tabs into relevant categories. For instance, if you have a couple of tabs open for an upcoming event, Safari can group tabs related to ticket purchases, venue directions or parking information into a single category. To set the feature up, enter the tab overview in Safari, tap on the sort button and select the Automatically Create Topics option.

Apple Intelligence also lets you create custom Safari extensions. Tap on the icon with the three horizontal lines in Safari and select Describe Extension. Apple lists a few examples, but you can ask Apple Intelligence to build an extension that performs a specific task. The few extensions I tried creating were surprisingly good at doing what I asked, though your mileage may vary.

Election Day is unfolding across America after a surge in early voting for the midterms

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The final day of voting in this year’s midterm elections arrived Tuesday against a backdrop of uncertainty after a year in which President Donald Trump has tried to interfere in how states run their elections and sown doubts about the outcome should Republicans lose.

Millions of Americans already have cast their ballots, with early voting turnout on pace to eclipse that of the last midterm elections. In contrast to the president’s continued rhetoric about election cheating, voting so far has unfolded with relatively few problems.

Trump has spent months trying to use the power of the presidency to put his thumb on the scale of this year’s elections, which will determine whether his party keeps control of Congress.

His Justice and Homeland Security departments have been involved in a massive hunt for voters who are not U.S. citizens, at one point engaging in a made-for-the-cameras predawn operation that led to more than two dozen arrests in California. The Department of Justice was planning its largest-ever effort to monitor Election Day polling places, countered by an even larger effort among Democrats and allied groups, and at the president’s urging had sued to block certification of Alaska’s results if the outcome of the Senate race favored Democrats.

At the same time, Trump’s constant false claims about widespread voter fraud led election officials and Democratic groups across the country to plan for the possibility of immigration agents at voting sites or federal officers seizing voting machines. His administration has insisted that no armed federal officials would be guarding the polls on Election Day.

Courts have blocked the Trump administration’s efforts to influence elections

In the run-up to Tuesday, none of the steps Democrats most feared had come to pass, and the Republican administration’s legal maneuvers had been largely blocked by the courts.

One of the Trump administration’s most high-profile attempts to interfere in state elections was turned away Monday night by the U.S. Supreme Court, which rejected its push to force California to set aside thousands of ballots as part of its search for noncitizen voters. In another Monday night ruling, a federal judge said the Trump administration must stop sending voter lists of alleged noncitizens to election officials.

Even the administration’s own record of arrests and charges has demonstrated what research and state investigations have made clear for years — that instances of noncitizens casting ballots are extremely rare.

Monday’s rulings followed another defeat for the president at the Supreme Court less than two months ago, when it rejected his attempt to limit mail balloting through changes at the Postal Service. That directive was included in one of two executive orders Trump has signed during his second term that attempted to federalize certain aspects of elections, even though the Constitution gives the states, and sometimes Congress, the authority to set election rules.

Voting begins smoothly with some weather disruptions in the Southwest

The first polling sites opened at 6 a.m. EST Tuesday in a handful of states — Connecticut, Indiana, Kentucky, Louisiana, Maine, Missouri, New Jersey, New York and Virginia.

Heavy rains and flash flooding in a swath of the Southwest were expected to make it harder for some voters to reach polling places in parts of Colorado, Arizona, Utah and New Mexico. Election administrators in the Navajo Nation announced changes to several of its polling locations, and in Utah’s rural San Juan County, voters were being allowed to vote electronically on Tuesday because some of them may not be able to reach polling places.

Election administrators and voting rights advocates have been advising voters casting late absentee ballots to use drop boxes or deliver them to polling places in person rather than mail them. The advice came after concerns emerged that recent changes in U.S. Postal Service operations could result in postmarking delays that would mean mail ballots could not be counted. Late-arriving ballots or late postmarks disenfranchised tens of thousands of voters in California, Oregon and Washington, which mail ballots to all voters, during this year’s primaries.

Trump hasn’t committed to accepting the election results

The most significant disruptions ahead of Election Day were the Trump administration’s attempts to interfere. Voting rights groups, Democrats and the Justice Department have been exchanging a flurry of legal filings over alleged noncitizen voters, federal election monitors, federal intimidation of election officials and other concerns. They reveal how Trump’s attempts to expand federal control over state-run elections — combined with high-stakes races — are resulting in court battles that could extend past Election Day into ballot counting and certification of the results.

Election law experts said Trump’s lawsuits go beyond historic norms, but they said the administration’s legal arguments were unlikely to convince the courts.

Trump also has ramped up his own equivocations about whether the election will be legitimate, as he has in the past. A week before Election Day, when a reporter asked him if he would accept the results, he offered a qualified answer.

“I will if they are honest,” he said.

The Case For Pre-Breach Intelligence: Why Buying Access Before Attackers Do Beats Cleaning Up

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Austin Berglas is Managing Director and Head of Cyber Operations at Nardello & Co. and the former head of FBI New York Cyber.

​For decades, enterprise security spending has followed one pattern: Detect the intrusion, contain it, investigate and recover. The breach occurs, and the security team responds as fast as it can.

That approach is expensive and increasingly avoidable.

IBM’s 2025 Cost of a Data Breach Report put the global average cost of a breach at $4.44 million and the average time to identify and contain at 241 days. Yes, this was a nine-year low, yet it still amounts to nearly eight months in which an attacker can sit inside a network, expand access and live off the land to conduct a larger-scale attack.

This is the limitation of post-breach incident response: It’s reactive. A SIEM can only correlate logs that exist, and an EDR platform can only flag malware after it’s been deployed. The industry has put enormous effort into faster detection and response and far less effort into stopping the incident from happening in the first place.

Most modern intrusions don’t begin with a zero-day exploit or custom malware. They begin with a transaction. Initial access brokers are criminals who specialize in breaching a network and selling that foothold to the highest bidder. These brokers supply a large share of the initial access used by ransomware crews and other individuals and groups conducting cyber-enabled crimes.

A gbhackers report noted that ​Rapid7 researchers tracked initial access listings across five major underground forums and found the average base price “rose from $2,726 in 2024 to $113,275 in the second half of 2025.” Additionally, Rapid7’s earlier dataset showed that more than 70% of listings “included some degree of privilege,” which means buyers were getting more than just a low-level login—they were purchasing a foothold from which to move laterally and expand access.​​​​​

Once access is listed, it doesn’t sit there for long. KELA Cyber research from 2022 found that access was often sold within one to three days of listing and that victims were listed on ransomware leak sites an average of 23 to 36 days later. This is a fairly significant window to conduct an attack.​

By the time the tech stack flags unusual activity, the access has already changed hands on a criminal forum. The attack that eventually triggers an alert is frequently the second or third step in a chain that began with a transaction on some dark web forum, completely unknown to the security team.

Pre-breach intelligence inverts the model. Instead of waiting for, and reacting to, a compromise, it involves direct monitoring of and, in some cases, legally acquiring access to the criminal marketplaces where access and credentials are bought and sold. In this way, an organization can learn if access to its own network is for sale before an attacker completes the purchase and exploits it.​

This changes what “detection” means. Pre-breach intelligence tells you that a specific method of access to your organization’s network is actively being marketed to buyers who intend to use it for a variety of crimes, such as ransomware, fraud or corporate espionage, while there’s still time to rotate credentials, patch the stolen access route and notify law enforcement before anything nefarious happens.​

Pre-breach intelligence is also significant for third-party risk. Vendors and partners are often the weakest link in an organization’s security posture and are frequently the last to know when their own access has been compromised. Monitoring the marketplaces where access to those vendors is sold can surface a third-party compromise weeks before the vendor discloses it or it becomes public.

None of this replaces traditional security operations and best practices. Endpoint detection, patch management and a competent incident response plan are still nonnegotiable. Pre-breach intelligence adds a layer that sits earlier in the attack chain. This is the difference between finding out your house key was copied and finding out someone already used it to get inside.

Insurers have started to take the approach seriously. In March 2025, Reinsurance News reported that AXA XL’s specialty risk division had partnered with DarkwebIQ, a pre-attack intelligence provider that intercepts access sales by infiltrating forums, marketplaces and private channels.​

Post-breach response has dominated budgets because its value is easy to explain. Here was the incident, here’s the cost and here’s what we spent fixing it. Prevention is a harder sell internally because success looks like nothing is happening. However, given how long it can take to remediate and recover from a data breach, and the significant cost associated, nothing happening is exactly the outcome worth paying for.

An organization that intercepts access before an attacker uses it avoids the costs that follow a breach. This can include months of containment, legal fees, incident response engagements, remediation and recovery, regulatory exposure and reputational damage.

Security leaders should ask whether access to their network is for sale today. If their current tools, vendors or partners can’t answer that, there’s no question that they’ll learn about their exposure on the attacker’s timeline rather than their own.​

The phenomenon of a Full Moon arises when our planet, Earth, is precisely sandwiched between the Sun and the Moon. The phases of the Moon are: New Moon, waxing crescent, First Quarter, waxing gibbous, Full Moon, waning gibbous, Last Quarter, and waning crescent. A cycle starting from one Full Moon to its next counterpart, termed the synodic month or lunar month, lasts about 29.5 days.

Though a Full Moon only occurs during the exact moment when Earth, Moon, and Sun form a perfect alignment, to our eyes, the Moon seems Full for around three days.

There are a wide variety of specialized names used to identify distinct types or timings of Full Moons. These names primarily trace back to a blend of cultural, agricultural, and natural observations about the Moon, aimed at allowing humans to not only predict seasonal changes, but also track the passage of time.

For instance, almost every month’s Full Moon boasts a name sourced from Native American, Colonial American, or other North American traditions, with their titles mirroring seasonal shifts and nature’s events.

Wolf Moon (January): Inspired by the cries of hungry wolves.

Snow Moon (February): A nod to the month’s often heavy snowfall.

Worm Moon (March): Named after the earthworms that signal thawing grounds.

Pink Moon (April): In honor of the blossoming pink wildflowers.

Flower Moon (May): Celebrating the bloom of flowers.

Strawberry Moon (June): Marks the prime strawberry harvest season.

Buck Moon (July): Recognizing the new antlers on bucks.

Sturgeon Moon (August): Named after the abundant sturgeon fish.

Corn Moon (September): Signifying the corn harvesting period.

Hunter’s Moon (October): Commemorating the hunting season preceding winter.

Beaver Moon (November): Reflects the time when beavers are busy building their winter dams.

Cold Moon (December): Evocative of winter’s chill.

In addition, there are a few additional names for Full Moons that commonly make their way into public conversations and news.

Super Moon: This term is reserved for a Full Moon that aligns with the lunar perigee, which is the Moon’s nearest point to Earth in its orbit. This proximity renders the Full Moon unusually large and luminous. For a Full Moon to earn the Super Moon tag, it should be within approximately 90 percent of its closest distance to Earth.

Blue Moon: A Blue Moon is the second Full Moon in a month that experiences two Full Moons. This phenomenon graces our skies roughly every 2.7 years. Though the term suggests a color, Blue Moons aren’t truly blue. Very occasionally, atmospheric conditions such as recent volcanic eruptions might lend the Moon a slightly blueish tint, but this hue isn’t tied to the term.

Black Moon: A Black Moon is a recent, unofficial term that can describe one of two situations. Either the third of four New Moons in a single astronomical season or the second of two New Moons in a month is a Black Moon. As its name suggests, the Black Moon is completely dark, as it is a New Moon that occurs when the Moon is between the Sun and Earth. The New Moon cannot be observed except during a total solar eclipse.

Harvest Moon: Occurring closest to the autumnal equinox, typically in September, the Harvest Moon is often renowned for a distinct orange tint it might display. This Full Moon rises close to sunset and sets near sunrise, providing extended hours of bright moonlight. Historically, this was invaluable to farmers gathering their produce.

What is the difference between a Full Moon and a New Moon? A Full Moon is witnessed when Earth is between the Sun and the Moon, making the entire Moon’s face visible. Conversely, during a New Moon, the Moon lies between Earth and the Sun, shrouding its Earth-facing side in darkness.

How does the Full Moon influence tides? The Moon’s gravitational tug causes Earth’s waters to bulge, birthing tides. During both Full and New Moons, the Sun, Earth, and Moon are in alignment, generating “spring tides.” These tides can swing exceptionally high or low due to the combined gravitational influences of the Sun and Moon.
