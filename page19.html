Magic: The Gathering Officially Breaks A 34-Year Tradition In 2027

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Wizards of the Coast's Universes Beyond initiative for Magic: The Gathering has brought some hugely popular franchises into the fold this year, including Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles, Marvel, The Hobbit, and most recently, Star Trek. While these crossovers have introduced MTG to new audiences, they've also become a major point of contention among longtime players, many of whom feel the game's original worlds are being pushed aside.

Much to the chagrin of seasoned MTG players, of the seven major sets released in 2026, just three have been traditional in-universe releases. With Star Trek officially out on shelves now, however, Magic: The Gathering is ready to return to its own mythology in a big way. Nauctis: The Sunken Realm, arriving in February 2027, is taking players underwater for an aquatic-themed ocean-based chapter in the franchise, with dedicated players breathing a sigh of relief after the underwhelming Universes Beyond sets of late.

However, WOTC has confirmed that Nauctis will be ditching one of MTG's most iconic combat mechanics, and the decision could prove pretty controversial.

For the first time in decades, Magic: The Gathering is set to release a major expansion without one of its defining mechanics. Flying, which has been a staple of MTG since the game's debut in 1993, will be completely absent from Nauctis: The Sunken Realm. The 2027 release will mark just the second time in Magic's 34-year history that an entire set has omitted flying, following 1999's Portal Three Kingdoms, which replaced it with a similar horsemanship mechanic.

The decision makes sense and isn't all that surprising given Nauctis' entirely underwater setting, although it is definitely a significant departure from traditional gameplay. In its official November 13 announcement, Wizards of the Coast explained:

"Game Design reimagined what evasion looks like in a world without flying."

MTG head designer Mark Rosewater also confirmed the change during MagicCon Atlanta, explaining that it would be somewhat ridiculous to have enormous underwater monsters defeated by something as simple as a seagull. Instead, Nauctis will introduce a new mechanic called Dive, providing an alternative approach to evasion without relying on flying and effectively giving players a viable replacement for the mechanic.

Importantly, flying isn't being permanently removed from MTG altogether, and existing cards featuring the keyword will still be playable in their respective formats.

Beyond this new gameplay change, Nauctis: The Sunken Realm will introduce an entirely new underwater plane filled with ancient whale gods, terrifying sea monsters, sprawling merfolk civilizations, adorable seal monks (not to be confused with monk seals), and more. Wizards has also confirmed four Commander decks for the upcoming release:

Each Commander deck will feature 100 cards, including new legendary commanders, and will retail for roughly $50. Premium versions with traditional foil cards and borderless commanders will also be available for about $80.

Nauctis: The Sunken Realm officially launches February 5, 2027, with pre-release events beginning January 29 and an MTG Arena release scheduled for February 2. While the absence of flying could frustrate some longtime players, the opportunity to explore an entirely original underwater world is certainly exciting, especially following Magic: The Gathering's recent emphasis on Universes Beyond crossovers.

Mauricio Pochettino's Man Utd appointment brings Middlesbrough transfer clause into focus

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

After an eye-catching summer of transfer business, it has been an excellent start to the season for Middlesbrough in the Championship.

Middlesbrough were beaten by Hull City in last season's play-off final, but as they look to bounce back from their Wembley heartbreak, Kim Hellberg's men head into the November international break sitting second in the Championship table.

After missing out on promotion in agonising fashion last season, Middlesbrough owner Steve Gibson provided Hellberg with strong backing in the transfer market this summer, and the ambitious rebuild has certainly had the desired effect so far.

While Will Lankshear was Boro's marquee summer signing, with the striker joining from Tottenham Hotspur for an initial fee of around £10 million, plus a further £10 million in add-ons, he was far from their only expensive addition.

Another Boro transfer deal that made headlines in the summer was the signing of goalkeeper Radek Vitek from Manchester United for an initial fee of £7 million, which could rise to as much as £14 million if add-ons are met.

Vitek spent last season on loan in the Championship with Bristol City, keeping 12 clean sheets in 41 appearances for the Robins, and after earning rave reviews for his performances at Ashton Gate, Boro spent a hefty fee on his services in a deal that was seen as a major coup for the Teesside outfit.

While Vitek was named in the Manchester United matchday squad on a number of occasions, he never made a senior appearance during his time at the club, spending time out on loan with Accrington Stanley, Blau-Weiss Linz and Bristol City.

However, the Red Devils clearly view Vitek as a potential future star as they included a buy-back clause in the deal to sell him to Middlesbrough, as well as a 35% sell-on clause and matching rights.

Ruben Amorim was in charge of United when Vitek was sent out on loan to Bristol City last summer, and ex-Boro boss Michael Carrick was at the helm when the decision was made to offload him on a permanent basis in August, but there has been yet another change in the dugout at Old Trafford this week.

The Red Devils parted company with Carrick on Thursday after a nightmare start to the season that left them in the Premier League relegation zone, and they did not take long to unveil his replacement, with former Tottenham Hotspur and Chelsea manager Mauricio Pochettino being named as the club's new head coach.

Pochettino will remain in his role with the United States national team for their upcoming double header against Haiti before flying over to Manchester next week to take up his new post, and his first game will be a daunting trip to face Liverpool at Anfield next Sunday.

There is no doubt that Pochettino has plenty of work to do to improve United's fortunes on the pitch, but the Argentine will also surely be keen to be involved in recruitment moving forward, which could have implications for Boro.

Manchester United are currently without a goalkeeping coach after Craig Mawson's departure this week, and it seems likely that he will be replaced by Toni Jimenez, who has worked with Pochettino for much of his managerial career.

One of Pochettino's first tasks at Old Trafford will be to tighten up the Red Devils' leaky defence, and he has a decision to make about whether to stick with current first choice goalkeeper Senne Lammens, who has had a tough start to the season.

Lammens starred in his debut campaign at United last season after his £18.2 million move from Royal Antwerp, but he has not looked like the same player since his high-profile mistake for Belgium against Spain at the World Cup in the summer, making a string of errors in recent weeks.

With the Red Devils' other senior goalkeepers, Karl Darlow and Tom Heaton, unlikely to be viewed as long-term successors to Lammens, Pochettino could enter the transfer market for a new shot-stopper if he is unconvinced by the 24-year-old, which could lead him to consider activating the buy-back option for Vitek.

However, the United hierarchy may not be having too many regrets about their decision to sell Vitek at this point after a shaky start to his Middlesbrough career, and it would be a surprise to see them bring him back to Old Trafford any time soon.

Vitek has been beaten at his near post on a number of occasions since his arrival at the Riverside Stadium, while he made another costly blunder in Boro's 2-0 defeat at Wrexham last week as he somehow allowed George Thomason's strike to go through him for the Red Dragons' opener.

Scotland got back on the winning trail with a 15-12 victory over Australia at Murrayfield.

After the disappointment of losing heavily to New Zealand eight days ago, head coach Gregor Townsend will have been pleased to see his team recover - although the triumph was far from swashbuckling.

Mark Atkinson was at Murrayfield to cover proceedings for The Scotsman and picks out some talking points from the match:

Winning was clearly the most important thing for Scotland, which is why with eight minutes to go, Finn Russell took the three points from a penalty rather than going for touch to push for a try.

It moved Scotland ten points clear and left Australia with too much to do, even with Max Jorgensen’s late try to keep the game very close.

However, Scotland’s inability to rack up the points is likely to harm them in the wider scale of the Nations Championship.

With one round of fixtures remaining before finals weekend, Scotland sit fourth in the standings on 15 points with a points difference of -8. After their historic win over New Zealand, fifth-placed Wales also have 15 points but with a points difference of -28. They face Australia next, which is winnable.

Scotland take on whipping boys Japan at Murrayfield on Saturday and need to win comprehensively with a bonus point to put pressure on third-placed Ireland and second-placed England, who are on 20 points and have points differences of +57 and +134 respectively.

Ireland take on South Africa and England face New Zealand, where a solitary bonus point will ensure either of them a top-three finish - and given England’s point difference, it would take something special to overhaul them. Scotland's best hope is probably a big Springbok win.

The chances are Scotland will finish fourth - which would mean a play-off against either Argentina or Australia.

Scotland did not play with much verve or pizzazz and were lamentably wasteful at the start of the second half when Billy Pollard was sent to the sin bin. Rather than adding to the scoreboard, they gave away two cheap penalties and lost their own lineout in a promising position.

Playmaker Russell failed to ignite the game and was patchy with some of his kicking, while the ball very rarely made it out to the wide players.

It wasn’t a game that got the pulses racing and Scotland will likely pay the price.

While both teams will rue not scoring a bucketload of points, there will be some satisfaction from the defensive effort given what happened the previous weekend.

Scotland shipped 43 points to the All Blacks and Australia were on the end of 45 points at Twickenham. It was evident that both sides were intent on keeping the door shut.

While imagination was at a premium, there were a lot of good tackles and discipline when pressure was on. Australia boss Les Kiss referenced it afterwards and Townsend will be pleased with the way his team held firm in difficult conditions for the majority of the final 20 minutes.

Darcy Graham’s tackle right at the end on Tom Wright ensured the win for Scotland and must be applauded for his work at the death.

Scotland had to turn to their bench before and after kick-off, and the replacements did themselves no harm.

Tom Jordan was a last-minute sub for captain Sione Tuipulotu, who failed a late fitness test due to a knee injury. The Bristol Bears man is a versatile fellow and while he’s more often than not been used at 10 or 15 for Scotland, he was asked to partner Huw Jones at centre.

This wasn’t a game for creativity, but Jordan did have some nice offloads and he defended well. If Tuipulotu doesn’t make next week against Japan, Jordan has probably kept himself ahead of Stafford McDowall.

In the pack, Scott Cummings pulled up on 25 minutes and the lock was replaced by his Glasgow Warriors club-mate Max Williamson, who made his presence felt with some big hits and powerful work at the breakdown.

Williamson has found himself behind Cummings and another Glasgow man in Gregor Brown, but this was a performance that will give Townsend food for thought for the rest of the year.

Blair Kinghorn used to be a nailed-on starter for Scotland, but his own form at Toulouse, niggling injuries and the emergence of Kyle Rowe at Glasgow Warriors has put his position in jeopardy.

Townsend gave Kinghorn the whole match at full-back and he served up a reminder of why he can make a difference in the Test arena.

He was secure under the high ball, ran with purpose and broke the line on several occasions. He also put in an excellent tackle on Jorgensen at a crucial point in the game.

Many feel Rowe was unfortunate not to feature against Australia, but Kinghorn has that little bit extra physicality and oomph that sets him apart.

We are also in the midst of an international break in football and that allowed a couple of Rangers players to take in this match.

Scotland striker Lawrence Shankland and Australia midfielder Cammy Devlin are both currently injured, so they were able to watch on in the Murrayfield stands without any commitments - and not far from their old stomping ground.

The ex-Hearts duo seemed to be enjoying the game and Shankland will no doubt have liked Scotland landing the bragging rights over the Wallabies.

Homophobia on the rise in football - what is your club doing about it?

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Rainbow laces were a common sight for several years across football and other sports

Warning: this article contains offensive language

Homophobia and transphobia are on the rise in English football, with new data revealing record numbers of discrimination reports in the game - so what is the Premier League doing about it?

Last season, with little fanfare, the league introduced a new LGBTQ+ celebratory activation in February, Premier League With Pride.

The lowkey launch contrasts with the landscape of a few years ago, when England internationals like Jordan Henderson and Conor Coady were proudly proclaiming their allyship.

"The one thing it [Premier League With Pride last season] was lacking was engagement from players," said Jon Holmes, founder and lead of news organisation and campaign group Sports Media LGBT.

"Not only would you have the rainbow armbands, but clearer allyship from players - that was more around the late 2010s. That has been in decline."

A report last month from anti-discrimination charity Kick It Out says they received a new high of 1,744 discrimination reports during the 2025-26 season, a 25% increase on the previous campaign. In this, reports of homophobia rose 46% to 203.

Kick It Out said it had received 142 reports of discriminatory mass chanting during the season, a 41% increase on 2024-25 and the highest number recorded. These reports included increases in homophobic and transphobic chants.

A prime example is the 'rent boy' chant predominantly aimed at Chelsea players and fans, for which incidents had dropped since it was criminalised in 2022. However, the combined 24 reports of homophobic chanting in 2025-26 season were the most in three years.

According to Kick It Out,, external many of the incidents over the past few seasons can be linked to domestic and world events, such as the Supreme Court ruling on gender leading to the FA's ban on transgender women in the women's game, which it says led to a spike in transphobic abuse.

The first Premier League With Pride event included rainbow branding at grounds - but nothing involving players

BBC Sport asked all 20 Premier League clubs what diversity policies they have regarding the LGBTQ+ community, and how much of their budget is dedicated to such schemes.

Nine clubs responded to the BBC request. None said how much money they invest in these areas.

A Nottingham Forest spokesperson told BBC Sport: "The club commits significant funding to this important area, and has an Equality, Diversity and Inclusion lead whose role is dedicated to ensuring inclusion is at the heart of everything we do."

Some clubs do visibly engage with their local LGBTQ+ community. Leeds United have been a sponsor of the city's Pride celebrations since 2018. Luke Ayling, then Leeds captain, marched in the Pride parade in 2022.

Brighton are annually represented in their city's extensive Pride events, as are the Manchester clubs.

Manchester City host an annual Progress flag-raising ceremony, while Manchester United said staff marched in the city centre parade. They also ran a flag competition with LGBTQ+ supporters' club Rainbow Devils, with the winning design displayed at Old Trafford for all matchdays.

Tottenham said that at the start of 2024, Spurs in the Community helped launch a weekly football session for members of the LGBTQ+ community.

And for the first time this season, all 20 current Premier League clubs have dedicated fan groups for LGBTQ+ supporters.

"It feels like there is a renaissance in the LGBT fan movement," said Holmes. "There is a rise in homophobia, but it does seem like the authorities are sitting up and taking notice. We can feel a degree of optimism."

But it is difficult for Premier League teams to encourage LGBTQ+ allyship and diversity, when representation within clubs is a mixed bag at best.

According to Kick It Out, which gathered data on the senior management teams from 17 out of the 20 current Premier League clubs, just 0.3% of people in those roles openly identify as LGBTQ+ - well below the UK population average according to latest national census data, with 4% of the British population identifying as LGBTQ+.

At a senior coaching level, from 11 clubs which responded there is no-one from the LGBTQ+ community.

Kick It Out says, comparing the dataset to relevant census data, that if true diversity in football's workforce were to be achieved, over 100 more people from the LGBTQ+ community would have to be employed at Premier League clubs.

The Premier League previously had a high profile association with LGBTQ+ charity Stonewall's Rainbow Laces campaign - but this was ended before the 2025-26 season

Rainbow Laces started in 2014-15 as a collaboration between Stonewall, Paddy Power and the Premier League. For two game weeks, usually around the end of November, players would wear rainbow-coloured laces or armbands, and the colourful branding would be seen in grounds and on broadcast idents.

Holmes said by the end it was doing more harm than good.

"I think the novelty of Rainbow Laces wore off," he told BBC Sport. "It also became more newsworthy to talk about the controversies; it became more difficult for players to engage without difficult questions.

"Also, club media departments are more risk averse, given growing toxicity on social media.

"It has been misconstrued that players were forced to engage, I think it was because Rainbow Laces became de rigueur, and it was perceived that captains would need to lead with the armband and allyship."

Rainbow armbands were introduced in 2017 for captains, having first been worn by MLS club Orlando City following the mass shooting at Pulse, an LGBTQ+ nightclub, in the city in 2016.

But the Premier League held its final Rainbow Laces campaign from 29 November to 5 December, 2024, and it was surrounded by controversy.

Marc Guehi, then Crystal Palace captain, wrote 'I Love Jesus' on his armband in what was widely viewed as a protest against wearing the armband (which was not mandatory).

Ipswich captain Sam Morsy refused to wear the armband, while Manchester United scrapped plans for a Rainbow Laces-themed jacket during the pre-match ceremonies following an objection by Noussair Mazraoui.

Simon Blake, CEO of Stonewall, admitted to BBC Sport that by the end of its 12-year lifespan in the Premier League, Rainbow Laces in that form had run its course.

"When it started, getting a few people to wear the laces was success," he said. "When it ended, when a few people didn't wear them there was criticism.

"If a campaign no longer has a positive vibe, perhaps it is time to do something different.

"Everything must evolve and change, but visibility still matters, it is important not to throw the baby out with the bathwater."

Stonewall held its first ever Rainbow Laces Day on 8 October this year, to encourage LGBTQ+ people to engage with sport. It had no connection with the Premier League.

Marc Guehi and Crystal Palace did not face any action after he wrote on his rainbow armband

It is notable that the players most ardently against Rainbow Laces were also vocal in their religious beliefs.

Guehi has spoken of the importance in his life of his faith and was one of several devout Christian players in the Crystal Palace dressing room. Both Morsy and Mazraoui are practicing Muslims.

Both Holmes and Blake acknowledge that this may have had an impact on Rainbow Laces.

"The influence of religion is clearly quite significant, but I think more understanding is coming in too," said Holmes.

"We have seen many parts of life less willing to engage with Pride, and football reflects that. The nature of LGBT campaigns is peaks and troughs, and we are in a trough."

"We don't have any specific data about what people's beliefs and views are, but we are in a world where people have different views, even within religions themselves," added Blake.

"The important thing is about behaviours, and we must learn to live with people who have these different beliefs and respect them.

"I don't want to speculate, as it would be too easy to assume people of faith would not be inclusive, I know from my work across 30 years that is not true; I have worked with many religious people who are LGBTQ+ supportive. Freedom of belief is important."

The current picture is a difficult one for gay and trans people in football at all levels.

The grassroots game is where the bulk of the increase in abuse has occurred, says Kick It Out, which claims it received 140% more reports last year than in the 2024-25 season. Three quarters of these reports are from senior grassroots games, where the players are over 18.

Away from football, research by Stonewall published in October found 11% of LGBTQ+ people in the UK avoid the gym, exercise classes or sports groups for fear of discrimination. Among trans people, the figure rises to 29%.

It said 15% of LGBTQ+ people who had been to a live sporting event, or watched sport in a bar, in the past year had experienced discrimination there. This rose to 26% for trans people.

"What we know is that there is a lot of work going on around equality and diversity; we also know homophobia and transphobia continue to be rife," Blake said.

"We are in a world, whether at Premier League or grassroots football, where views that may have been perceived to be unacceptable are now being expressed freely. That influences peoples' relationship with sport."

Premier League With Pride will return in 2026-27.

"The matchday activations take place in February in line with LGBTQ+ History Month, when the league and clubs will showcase their commitment to LGBTQ+ inclusion," a Premier League spokesperson told BBC Sport.

"There will be two match rounds dedicated to Premier League With Pride. This means all 20 clubs have a home game during that period, providing an opportunity to highlight and celebrate the LGBTQ+ community in football and showcase the work undertaken to make the game more inclusive.

"There is no centralised activation lined up for November."
