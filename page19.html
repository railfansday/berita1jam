Andy Taylor, Former Duran Duran Guitarist, Dead at 65

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

The rocker had been battling prostate cancer for about eight years.

Andy Taylor, guitarist and former member of Duran Duran, has died after a yearslong battle with prostate cancer. He was 65 years old.

The musician’s former bandmates shared the news Wednesday (Nov. 25) on their website and on Instagram, announcing that Taylor had passed away two days prior. “Andy was there at the very beginning of our story,” the group wrote. “From the moment he arrived in Birmingham from the North East with a guitar and an unshakeable belief in what we could become, he brought an indelible fire and swagger to our music.”

Duran Duran continued, “His playing drove the songs that took us around the world, and with a dry wit that was matched only by his wonderfully unsentimental, no-nonsense outlook on life he kept us grounded and gave the band its edge through the extraordinary years that followed.”

Still in its early years, the band added Taylor to the lineup in 1980. He would leave in 1986 before rejoining in 2001, playing guitar on Duran Duran’s 2004 album Astronaut (which reached No. 17 on the Billboard 200) before leaving again in 2006. In 2023, he’d contribute guitar to Halloween-themed LP Danse Macabre.

During his time in the group, Duran Duran scored 13 hits on the Billboard Hot 100, including two No. 1s: “The Reflex” in 1984 and “A View to a Kill” in 1985. The group’s blockbuster Rio album (which housed “Hungry Like the Wolf”) hit the Billboard 200 in 1982 and remained on the chart a staggering 128 weeks. As a soloist, he scored two entries on the chart: “Take It Easy” (No. 24 in 1986) and “When the Rain Comes Down” (No. 73 in 1986).

Taylor’s wife, Tracey, also shared a statement Wednesday. “To the world, Andy was a brilliant guitarist, but to us, he was the most wonderful husband, a loving dad and the proudest grandfather,” she wrote. “He was the rock of our family. For all the incredible experiences music brought him, family always came first.”

His cancer diagnosis was first revealed in 2022, when frontman Simon LeBon told fans at Duran Duran’s induction into the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame — from which Taylor was absent — that he’d already been battling the illness for four years at that point. In 2023, Taylor shared that he’d started taking cancer drug Lutetium-177, which he said extended his life by five years.

“Andy faced the last few years with so much courage and determination,” Tracey added in her statement. “He never gave up, fighting until the very end, never losing his love of life. The kindness and support shown to him by fans from all over the world throughout his illness brought him immense comfort and strength, and for that we will always be grateful.”

Andy is also survived by his children — Georgina, Isabella, Bethany and Andrew — and grandson Albie.

Arsenal Thought They Signed a 'Phenom' in Miyaichi, but things went wrong

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Back in 2011, Arsenal had built up a reputation for identifying some of the most talented players, whether it was Nicolas Anelka, Aaron Ramsey or Theo Walcott. The Gunners did some wise business, and they thought they had done the same with Ryo Miyaichi when they plucked from obscurity in 2011, offering him a trial.

Arséne Wenger was impressed by Miyachi when he had a trial with the club, and he offered the Japanese youngster a professional contract at the end of January of 2011.

The Frenchman said at the time: "We’re delighted that Ryō Miyaichi has joined us. He trialed with us in the summer and has raw ability which has attracted many clubs around the world". Some believed at the time that he could be the 'next Lionel Messi'.

"Ryo has natural technical ability. He has good balance and phenomenal pace, and his passing and crossing is consistently of a high quality. He is a very exciting player." - Wenger on Miyaichi in 2011

Miyaichi was an ultra-skillful player who quickly impressed in the youth ranks, both for club and country, and he was on course to become a star. In fact, during his first senior spell in professional football, on loan at Feyenoord, he was nicknamed 'Ryodinho' by the Dutch media, which only sparked excitement among Arsenal fans.

"I like his pace, his mobility and his movement off the ball. I like players who can move when others have the ball. There is also his attitude, commitment and desire. That is why I decided to keep him." - Wenger on Miyaichi in 2011

He went on to spend time at Bolton Wanderers and then Wigan Athletic, where he suffered a long-term ankle injury that saw him miss their successful FA Cup campaign.

By this point, Miyaichi had managed just one senior league appearance for Arsenal, and after a loan spell with Twente back in the Netherlands, he was released by the Gunners. Miyaichi later reflected: "Back then, I didn't really think of myself as young. There were so many world-class players around my age, and I was always feeling pressed."

The Japan international found a new permanent home at St Pauli, but his debut was significantly delayed by an ACL rupture, and after returning to action, he ruptured the ACL in his other leg in a horrible run of luck.

Remarkably, the German side still renewed the winger's contract. Sporting chief Andreas Rettig explained: “Ryo has unbelievable bad luck, and we do not want to leave him alone. [We want to] show that we are behind him. We hope that this decision will help Ryo to return to his old level of performance.”

Reflecting on his career at a later date, Miyaichi went on to say: "At one point, I seriously thought about quitting soccer.

"I have been away from the team for long periods and have caused trouble time and again. I have also disappointed many people. That is why I thought about quitting."

He went on to enjoy a couple of seasons of full fitness before being released in 2021 after racking up 77 league appearances. It was at that stage that he returned to Japan, joining Yokohoma F. Marinos, where he has been since, racking up 104 league appearances and scoring eight times. Back in 2022, he actually added three more Japan caps to his name, which must have been nice for him after all the injury troubles that followed his first two caps in 2012.

At 33 years of age, Miyaichi is still playing with Yokohoma F. Marinos, and he won the JLeague with them back in 2022, adding to the two FA Cups he technically won between Wigan in 2013 and Arsenal in 2014.

Speaking back in June on why he wants to keep playing, Miyaichi said: "I think having fun is the most important thing. It's what fuels your growth. I still have the desire to keep improving and still feel the same way I always did. Showing people something that makes them think is cool on the pitch is what I'm there for."

Miyaichi's never-ending battles with injury must have made for a tough time, but fueled by his love for the game, he never gave up, and perseverance has allowed him to keep playing at the age of 33. Things could have gone a very different way for the winger, even if his biggest hopes were not fulfilled.

NBA announces $11 billion investment plan to develop, modernize arenas for NBA Europe

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Is European basketball ready for modern American-style NBA arenas? With adjacent entertainment districts around them?

The NBA announced plans Wednesday for a €10 billion (nearly $11 million at current exchange rates) initiative to pay for "the development and renovation of basketball arenas – and the sports and entertainment districts around them – across Europe." To make this happen, the NBA assembled a group of heavy-hitter global investment firms including Apollo Sports Capital; Ares Management; Arctos, a business of KKR; Blackstone; HPS Investment Partners, a part of BlackRock; J.P. Morgan; Sixth Street and Stonepeak, among others.

"We've seen firsthand how state-of-the-art arenas and the related developments around them can transform basketball teams and their surrounding communities," NBA Commissioner Adam Silver said in a statement announcing the initiative. "As we prepare to launch a new league in Europe alongside FIBA, we see a significant opportunity to help clubs develop facilities which both build on the unique traditions that define European basketball while also elevating the fan experience and attracting many more live events."

This is about arenas for NBA Europe teams, the league the NBA and partner FIBA plan to at least soft launch next October. That said, this initiative is entirely separate from the team investment process.

League officials have been open from the start that they see opportunity in Europe, not just because of the fan base, but because the arena infrastructure there does not maximize profitability — the NBA believes European clubs are leaving money on the table. Part of the plan to make NBA Europe profitable is to change that.

In its announcement, the NBA put it this way: "The investors will look to partner with participating clubs, ownership groups, local developers and other service providers to develop world-class arenas that improve the fan experience and, where appropriate, mixed-use districts around them. Depending on local needs and opportunities, these projects could include housing, workplaces, restaurants, retail and public spaces, helping to revitalize communities where people live, work and play. "

The NBA has long championed the idea that new world-class arenas drive economic growth in the cities where they are placed, not only through ticket sales and jobs created, but also by revitalizing parts of a city if there are housing and entertainment districts (with restaurants, theaters and more) around them. However, multiple studies suggest this is more a redistribution of wealth and spending from other parts of a city to the new district.

Europe does not have many arenas like the ones NBA teams often play in. The target cities for NBA Europe include Paris, Rome, London, Manchester, Lyon, Madrid, Barcelona, Athens, Berlin and more. However, those cities have not been finalized as the bidding process continues.

US Forest Service Releases Bigfoot Reports That Read Like Something Out Of Control

One of my favorite video games is Control. I also like this year’s Control Resonant, but not quite as much. And one of the best parts of those games is the government reports and files documenting creepy happenings and events in the game’s universe. Meanwhile, here in real life, the United States Forest Service has recently released some files about Bigfoot, and I was very pleased to see that feel like they fell right out of Control.

As reported by The New York Times, last month the USFS published a few documents and reports related to Bigfoot as part of a Freedom of Information request. One file is just a clipping of a newspaper article from a Montana newspaper in the ’80s about big, strange footprints found in the snow. The other two are far more interesting.

First, a 1982 report written by someone who was sent out to study alleged Bigfoot tracks found in Mill Creek, Oregon. Look at this and tell me it doesn’t look and read like something you’d pick up in Control.

The report goes over, in a lot of detail, the footprints that were spotted. Ultimately, the report’s conclusion was that these tracks were fake, with the writer citing inconsistencies with gait pattern, signs of tampering with leaves, and other pieces of evidence that led them to conclude the tracks were “made by something other than a 300-pound plus two-footed being.”

The other piece of Bigfoot documentation released by the USFS is what appears to be a short document written by someone who really thought Petty Creek, Montana was special and historically important. One reason it was historically important: Bigfoot, baby. Here’s the bit about a Bigfoot sighting that allegedly happened in the ’40s.

Another type creature that walked on its hind legs, that altho there wasn’t actual sightings, tracks, a strong odor, and a grunting sound were observed. The first time was when four of us went in to pack out a six point bull elk on our backs and on arriving where the elk was supposed to be laying we found the intrails only and in the snow coming to and leading away from the area was a large set of tracks. At first we guessed it was a very large bear except bears don’t walk on their hind legs only for short distances and ordinarily would drag a carcass this large.

Whatever it was had picked up the elk whole and clear of the ground. We started following the tracks and after about one half mile said creature had stepped across a 3 ft. diameter log that was laying on the ground and on top of the log in the snow we could see where the horns had drug. At this point we held a con- ference and decided that we were not particularly anxious to catch up to whatever it was and tho we didn’t confess it at this time, in later conversations we found out that we all had a very uneasy feeling and could feel chills running up and down our backs.

This event took place in 1949 in the South fork of Petty cr. about where the present Garden point road goes. The following year when W.W. Schmitz was hunting in the upper Bills creek area he came across the same type of tracks and smelled a strong odor and heard a grunting type noise. There will be many other folks who want to play the game and haven't had a chance to jump in yet. One thing they should note is that, with a hefty install size, GTA 6 will take up a significant chunk of their PS5 or Xbox Series X/S storage space.

You might be wondering whether you can install GTA 6 on an external hard drive to avoid having to delete other games and data from your console. You can move GTA 6 to your external storage when you're not playing it. However, to run the game locally, you'll need to have it installed on your system's built-in storage, an SDD you've installed in your PS5 or a compatible NVMe expansion card that's plugged into your Xbox.

As with many other recent games, GTA 6 needs to take advantage of the fast-loading capabilities of the PS5 and Xbox Series X/S. The consoles are unable to load data for these titles quickly enough from an external hard drive over a USB connection. For the same reason, disc-based PS5 and Xbox Series X games are installed onto system storage before you can play them. The PS5 can run PS4 games from external storage, though. Likewise, an Xbox Series X/S can run older Xbox One and Xbox 360 titles directly from an external hard drive.

How to Maneuver Generational Differences Over the Holidays

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

With the holidays yet again upon us, people will be setting their holiday gathering plans and preparing for an onslaught of multi-generational togetherness. When coming together for the holidays, the goal isn’t necessarily for everyone to think or celebrate alike; it’s to celebrate harmony and happiness while making room for the differences that you know may come with getting so many people of varying ages together in one room.

Here, we discuss how to make room at one’s table for generational differences while protecting the deep thread of connection that makes everyone family.

We’ve all experienced it. The sideways glances at the child who won’t eat their turkey. The sighs and stories about “Back in my day…” The admonishment to the teenager to “put your phone away.” The mom in the middle of it all who just wants a group photo of everyone before they dig in.

These are the sights and sounds of most holiday family gatherings. The younger generation has taken over the planning and organization of events, while the older generation sits in the wake of it all, wondering why anything had to change.

Even though the holidays bring together people who love one another, they can also bring friction. According to a 2023 American Psychological Association poll, 89% of U.S. adults reported holiday-related stress, and 22% identified the experience or anticipation of family conflict as a source of that stress.

However, generational differences don’t have to derail a holiday gathering. Once we learn to stop anticipating conflict and start remembering the reason we get together in the first place, we can begin looking forward to the fun stories, skill sharing, and memory-making that make the holidays so special.

It can be easy to get immediately annoyed when Grandpa starts talking about how they used to discipline kids or Great Aunt Margaret says that your stuffing was made incorrectly. Try to reframe statements before irritation or misunderstandings get out of control.

For example, instead of getting bothered when an older member of the family admonishes a child for being on their device, think about why they may be mentioning it. Perhaps they want to spend more time talking with the child, or maybe there are some cultural expectations around the holidays at play.

Researchers found that interactions and education can reduce or even eliminate ageism, which can be at play during multi-generational events. By teaching the younger generation about aging and helping them connect with older people, you can eliminate miscommunication, fear, and negative feelings.

Laying out expectations and boundaries can also be an effective way to help the holiday get-together go more smoothly between the generations. Prior to the event, invite the older generations into a conversation about plans. This way, there will be less chance of surprise or disappointment, and the older generation family members will feel like they are contributing.

Take care to ask about meaningful traditions in the family and choose a few to carry on while leaving room for something new. Discuss important topics like gift budgets, transportation, locations, and food options before the holidays so that there is less room for conflict.

Boundaries can sometimes sound like a dirty word to older generations, but they can still be important for intergenerational harmony during the holidays. First, distinguish between what you are willing to compromise on to keep the peace and a personal boundary that absolutely must be respected.

For example, letting Grandma tell stories about her childhood on the farm may not necessarily hurt anyone, but comments about your body, children declining a hug from a relative, or politics may be in need of some boundary-setting intervention. If boundaries that are important to you are repeatedly ignored, have a place you can go to take a break or a code word with your partner to signal that you need some help getting out of a conversation.

When you get different generations of family together for the holiday, there is bound to be some friction. We live in increasingly divided times, and people may feel more comfortable around their family letting conflicting opinions fly. Holiday hosts should aim to connect the generations instead of steeling themselves for inevitable discourse.

The ultimate goal isn’t to resolve all differences and agree every step of the way (that’s likely impossible). Instead, families should make room for stories, laughter, and the kind of connection that’s only possible between people who know one another as well as family members do.
