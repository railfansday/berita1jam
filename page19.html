Dinosaurs May Have Eaten Far More Fruit Than Scientists Ever Thought

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

An exceptionally preserved 74-million-year-old forest in New Mexico is revealing unexpected details about ancient plant life.

Dinosaurs may have been eating a much wider variety of fruits than scientists once believed. Fossils from a 74-million-year-old forest in New Mexico show that some flowering plants were already producing surprisingly large fruits millions of years before the asteroid impact that ended the age of non-avian dinosaurs.

The findings, published in the journal Science, challenge the longstanding theory that larger fruits and more complex seed dispersal strategies evolved primarily after the extinction event 66 million years ago. They also suggest that dinosaurs and other prehistoric animals may have helped spread the seeds of flowering plants much earlier than previously thought.

The research centers on an exceptionally preserved fossil forest near Truth or Consequences, New Mexico. James Saulsbury, a postdoctoral researcher at the University of Kansas Biodiversity Institute and Natural History Museum, helped collect, classify, and analyze the plant fossils discovered there.

“This paper focuses on a fossil forest in New Mexico and the preserved fruits, seeds and other reproductive structures of the plants that lived there,” Saulsbury said. “These fossils change how we understand the way flowering plants became modern in terms of their reproductive ecology.”

The forest was buried beneath volcanic ash that eventually hardened into rock. The preserved deposit stretches more than a kilometer (about 0.6 miles), capturing plants from approximately the same period and retaining evidence of how they were arranged across the ancient landscape.

“The New Mexico fossil site is exceptional,” Saulsbury said. “My co-authors have referred to it as a ‘botanical Pompeii’ because of its remarkable preservation. The site contains a layer of volcanic ash preserved as rock, known as tuff, that stretches for more than a kilometer. The deposits formed at essentially the same time, preserving many plants where they lived and died. It provides a snapshot of a forest from about 74 million years ago and even preserves aspects of its spatial arrangement.”

Flowering plants first appeared approximately 136 million years ago, but researchers have traditionally believed that their reproductive strategies remained relatively simple throughout much of the Cretaceous Period. Early flowering plants generally produced small seeds and fruits, with wind and other non-animal mechanisms thought to play major roles in seed dispersal.

“For much of their early history, they were relatively small, weedy plants that did not dominate forest canopies,” Saulsbury said. “Although they gradually diversified and occupied more ecological niches, it was thought their seeds and dispersal structures remained small and were primarily dispersed by wind or without fleshy fruit.”

Many early flowering plant seeds were thought to have ranged from dust-sized particles to roughly the size of poppy seeds. By contrast, the New Mexico fossils include fruits approximately as large as modern grapes. Such fruits are much larger than those previously expected from flowering plants of that age.

“The prevailing view has been that this more diverse reproductive ecology emerged only after the end-Cretaceous extinction,” Saulsbury said. “Our study suggests that transition began much earlier. The largest fruits in our fossil flora are about the size of a grape. That may not sound especially large today, but it is remarkably large for flowering plants of that period.”

The team examined hundreds of fossilized seeds, sorting them according to their physical characteristics. Because fossil material often lacks the features necessary to identify an exact species, researchers organized specimens into groups based on their shapes and structures.

“We sorted hundreds of fossil seeds into what we call morphotypes,” Saulsbury said. “These are groupings based on form rather than confirmed species because it is often impossible to determine whether similar-looking fossils represent one species or several.”

The fossil assemblage contains an unusual diversity of fruits, seeds, and reproductive structures. Although the exceptional preservation helped researchers recover this material, Saulsbury believes the forest itself differed from many other known Cretaceous environments.

“However, we don’t believe these findings are simply the result of preservation bias,” he said. “It appears to represent a genuinely unusual forest.”

Fruit size can provide information about the environments in which plants evolved and how their seeds may have spread. Today, many flowering plants produce fleshy fruits that attract animals, which can transport seeds away from the parent plant after eating the fruit.

“Large fruits tend to occur in moist environments with densely packed forests,” Saulsbury said. “Our findings indicate that modern-looking forest structure and reproductive ecology were developing earlier than previously believed. The diversification of flowering plant reproductive strategies did not have to wait until after the end-of-Cretaceous extinction.”

The discovery also complicates an established explanation for the evolution of fruit-bearing plants. Scientists had proposed that large, fleshy fruits became more widespread as mammals diversified following the extinction of non-avian dinosaurs, creating new opportunities for plants to disperse their seeds through animals.

“As mammals expanded into newly available ecological niches, flowering plants were thought to have diversified their seed dispersal strategies in response,” Saulsbury said. “Our findings suggest that story began earlier. If diverse, large fruits already existed in Cretaceous forests, they were likely being dispersed not only by early mammals but also by large dinosaurs and other animals.”

Reference: “Diversification of angiosperm reproductive strategies predated the end-Cretaceous extinction” by Jaemin Lee, Dori L. Contreras, James G. Saulsbury, Garland R. Upchurch and Cindy V. Looy, 25 June 2026, Science. DOI: 10.1126/science.adw9457

Meta introduces Muse Ember to power cost-efficient AI agents

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Meta Platforms Inc. today introduced Muse Ember, an entry-level artificial intelligence model that costs significantly less than the competition.

Notably, it’s the first AI that the company trained using Muse Wonder. The latter algorithm is the Facebook parent’s flagship large language model. Meta didn’t specify which part of the workflow it automated with Muse Wonder.

One possibility is that the company used it to perform post-training, the task of honing a model’s capabilities by training it on additional data. Thinking Machines Labs Inc. recently demonstrated that even a relatively small LLM can run post-training runs end-to-end. Anthropic PBC, for its part, last week revealed that its AI models could soon gain the ability to significantly accelerate machine learning research.

Applications often use frontier LLMs such as Muse Wonder as part of AI ensembles. When a user enters a request, the frontier LLM splits it into smaller tasks and assigns them to agents powered by a lower-cost model. Meta says Muse Ember is specifically optimized to play the latter role.

The company evaluated the model using the Artificial Analysis Index, which aggregates nine AI benchmarks spanning use cases such as coding and financial analysis. Meta says that Muse Ember matched the score of Anthropic’s entry-level Claude Haiku 5.5 model at about a third the price. It also outperformed OpenAI Group PBC’s comparable GPT-6 Luna algorithm.

The same internal evaluation saw Meta test Muse Ember and the two rival models across nine other benchmarks. All three algorithms were in “max” mode, which means they used the maximum amount of hardware at their disposal instead of prioritizing cost-efficiency. Muse Ember outperformed Haiku and Luna across seven of the benchmarks.

The area where the model appears to have the biggest edge is business task automation. Whereas most of Muse Ember’s benchmark results were only a few percentage points better than those of Haiku and Luna, it clocked a 17% lead on AutomationBench-AA. It’s a collection of 657 tasks that span popular cloud applications such as Gmail and Salesforce.

Muse Ember has a context window of 1 million tokens, which enables it to ingest lengthy business documents. Users can also include images, audio and video in their prompts. The model generates responses up to 512,000 tokens in length.

Muse Ember is available through Meta’s Model API for 10 cents per million input tokens and 50 cents per million output tokens. A built-in cache can reuse frequently used prompts to avoid duplicate processing. When the feature is active, Muse Ember’s pricing drops to 0.4 cents per million input tokens.

Australian Prime Minister Anthony Albanese released 18 recommendations on November 10, 2026, from a rapid review into an OpenAI agent’s unauthorized access to a Services Australia portal, the Department of the Prime Minister and Cabinet said. The package covers AI-developer reporting, possible criminal offenses and government cyber defenses.

The recommendations are intended to prepare Australia for AI incidents of greater scale, complexity and consequence. Their status varies: some direct agencies to act, while others ask the government or named departments to consider, explore or review possible measures.

Recommendation 3 asks the Australian Government to consider mandatory AI safety reporting within set time limits, expressly including misaligned behavior and cyber incidents involving AI agents. Recommendation 4 separately asks officials to explore support for timely reporting of AI cyber or safety incidents and near misses, including possible protections for AI developers and others with relevant knowledge who disclose incidents or vulnerabilities. Together, they put reporting deadlines, near misses and disclosure protections on the policy agenda without imposing a new reporting duty now.

The Attorney-General’s Department, consulting the Australian Federal Police and Department of Home Affairs, is asked to consider criminal penalties for egregious regulatory non-compliance. Another option is a standalone failure-to-prevent offense covering cases where a company develops a model that engages in harmful conduct and fails to take reasonable preventive steps. The recommendation explores those possibilities; it does not itself create an offense.

Internationally, the Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade, the Australian Signals Directorate (ASD) and the Department of Industry, Science and Resources, including the Australian AI Safety Institute, are asked to continue work on consistent expectations for safety, incident reporting, evaluations, safeguards and transparency. Other recommendations call for engagement with OpenAI’s Australian Taskforce and similar initiatives, possible personnel exchanges and technical placements, and encouragement of frontier firms’ Australian research, training and safety activities. The government is also asked to consider the AI Safety Institute’s future capacity for technical research, evaluation and rapid advice during crisis coordination.

The Protective Security Board is asked to issue guidance clarifying agencies’ obligations and escalation expectations when a reported vulnerability or suspected cyber incident has not yet been technically validated. Commonwealth agencies are told to review vulnerability-disclosure and incident-management procedures so unusual incidents reach ASD, Home Affairs and senior decision-makers promptly. That focus addresses the notification chain described by ministers: OpenAI’s first notice went to Services Australia’s public vulnerability-disclosure inbox on September 10, 2026, and the agency alerted ASD on September 15, 2026, after checking the report.

Formal liaison arrangements are proposed among ASD’s Australian Cyber Security Centre, the National Office of Cyber Security and the AI Safety Institute to improve information sharing and identify incidents with broader national-interest implications. The National Office of Cyber Security would decide whether arrangements to manage an incident’s broader consequences are required. Recommendation 13 adds a dated step: agencies responsible for national crisis plans are asked to assess AI’s effects on their hazards and response arrangements and write to Crisis Arrangements Committee chairs with outcomes and proposed changes by the end of 2026.

Resilience measures call for higher staff AI literacy, renewed focus on fundamental cyber-security practices, including ASD’s Essentials, consolidation of government digital systems, and shutdown or segregation of systems that do not need internet connections. Agencies are also asked to work with ASD to identify and assess responsible uses of AI-enabled monitoring, penetration testing, threat detection and incident response. It's built to directly support individuals who may not have a PCP on file.

HealthTap reaches out to those members proactively, the company said, and helps them schedule an initial virtual visit with a provider who stays connected for preventive services, chronic condition management and medication concerns.

"The angle that we're taking is: wouldn't it be cool if, in fact, your virtual primary care physician was just like a bricks and mortar primary care physician?" said Rick Cochran, senior vice president of health plans at HealthTap, in an interview with Fierce Healthcare.

In an ideal scenario, a patient who has a consistent primary care physician who they see over the course of multiple years and who they come to trust, he said. HealthTap is looking to build that experience with virtual primary care docs, and reach patients who are disconnected from the health system with critical services.

Cochran said that the team, at times, receives feedback about concern that a program like this could upset in-network practices. Infant Bleeding Cases Surge as More Parents Refuse Vitamin K Shots for Newborns

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Follow Healthline on our socials for all things health.

Two large studies published on November 5 link declining newborn vitamin K shot rates to a sharp increase in infant bleeding cases, including potentially fatal brain hemorrhages.

A research letter, published in JAMA Pediatrics, analyzed data on approximately 11 million infants from 2017 to 2025 and found that vitamin K administration declined from 97.7% to 95%, while cases of vitamin K deficiency bleeding (VKDB) nearly quadrupled.

A second study published in JAMA, based on data from 5.2 million U.S. infants, found that refusal rates more than doubled, rising from 3.1% in 2017 to 6.8% in 2025. Infants who did not receive the shot had a 47% higher risk of bleeding and more than twice the risk of intracranial bleeding.

Additionally, new data from Truveta suggest the trend is continuing: refusals of vitamin K shots increased by 57% between January and June 2026, rising from 5.2% to 8.1% of newborns.

Vitamin K is essential for blood clotting, and newborns naturally have very low levels. Without the preventive injection, infants are at risk for VKDB, which can cause sudden bleeding in the brain, gastrointestinal tract, and other organs. Late-onset VKDB can occur up to six months after birth, often without warning signs, and may be fatal.

The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) and American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) continue to recommend an intramuscular vitamin K injection for all newborns, ideally within 6 hours of birth.

The shot is not a vaccine; it is a nutrient injection that helps prevent dangerous bleeding. A single dose reduces the risk of late-onset VKDB by approximately 98%.

Experts not involved in the studies attribute the rise in refusals partly to broader vaccine hesitancy and mixed public health messaging.

“The decision to vaccinate a newborn or allow a vitamin K injection at birth is complex, with multiple factors playing a role in the ultimate decision,” said Graham Tse, MD, a pediatrician and chief medical officer of MemorialCare Miller Children’s & Women’s Hospital in Long Beach, CA.

Some of these factors, Tse said, include conflicting messages from federal health agencies such as the Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) and the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), both overseen by HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy, Jr., an outspoken critic of the childhood immunization schedule.

The vitamin K shot is not a vaccine, yet RFK Jr. has declined to reassure parents about its safety, despite the CDC’s current recommendation to administer it at birth.

Additionally, growing medical distrust among parents has led to refusal of routine newborn care, such as the vitamin K shot, Reuters reports.

Healthline spoke with Tse to learn more about the risks of refusing the vitamin K shot and what new or expectant parents should know about this routine newborn treatment.

This interview has been lightly edited and condensed for clarity and length.

Tse: The decision to vaccinate a newborn or allow a vitamin K injection at birth is complex, with multiple factors playing a role in the ultimate decision.

A contributing factor to the decline in vitamin K injection at birth, as well as in newborn hepatitis B vaccination, is the conflicting messages from the Department of Health and Human Services (HHS), and the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC).

Recommendations from these entities and others are conflicting or do not align with established, research-based, and proven recommendations from organizations such as the American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) and the Infectious Diseases Society of America.

When there are conflicting recommendations, it creates confusion and doubt among parents about what the best or right choice is for their child.

As well, there is so much misinformation on the internet, among influencers, and on social media that it is difficult to know what information is accurate, well-researched, and proven, versus what may be just someone’s opinion or belief.

Some are mistaking the vitamin K injection at birth for a vaccination, which it is not. Ultimately, all these factors are contributing to parents declining or delaying vitamin K injections at birth.

Tse: Declining the newborn vitamin K injection puts the newborn at significant risk of serious and life threatening complications from vitamin K-deficient bleeding (VKDB). This was previously referred to as hemorrhagic disease of the newborn.

VKDB can be as mild as bruising of the skin but can be as serious as bleeding into the brain, and newborns and infants can die from VKDB.

Declining vitamin K injection at birth is significant because of the serious and potentially life threatening consequences of deficiency of Vitamin K and not being able to clot the blood.

The vitamin K injection at birth is highly effective at preventing VKDB.

Tse: If a neonate or infant has vitamin K deficiency, the ability to clot their blood and stop bleeding is highly reduced.

All newborns are at risk of low vitamin K due to low body stores, low vitamin K content of breast milk, reduced ability of the gut to mobilize vitamin K, and poor transfer of vitamin K across the placenta.

When the blood cannot clot, bleeding from minor or minimal trauma can occur, and even bleeding without injury or trauma may occur. This can include bruises, bleeding from the mouth or gastrointestinal tract, bleeding from cuts or circumcision.

Bleeding can occur anywhere in the body, including in the brain, which can be catastrophic.

VKDB can occur from birth and through several months of life. Bleeding in the brain from VKDB, one of the most serious consequences, can occur at any time from birth to up to 6 months or so and can occur unexpectedly and suddenly. Vitamin K given at birth can prevent this risk of bleeding.

Tse: Vitamin K is a necessary vitamin that all humans need. Vitamin K is not a vaccination.

Newborns need vitamin K to prevent serious, significant, and often catastrophic bleeding during their first several months of life.

Vitamin K has been given to newborns in the United States for close to 70 years, is safe, is studied, and is proven to help prevent vitamin K-deficient bleeding.

I urge new parents to allow their newborns to receive the vitamin K injection at birth — it protects them, keeps them healthy, is proven safe, and is recommended.

Plant-Based Milk Alternatives May Not Give You Enough Iodine. Here’s Why That Matters

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Follow Healthline on our socials for all things health.

Replacing dairy with plant-based alternatives (PBAs), even partially, could substantially lower iodine intake, especially if those alternatives aren’t fortified, according to a recent study published in the Lancet Planetary Health.

The United Kingdom-based researchers found that only 12% of UK plant-based milk, yogurt, and cheese products analyzed were fortified with iodine. This compares with 61% for calcium and 46% for vitamin B12.

In the United States, the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) provides guidance on the nutritional content of PBAs, but fortification with iodine is voluntary.

The study found that replacing as little as 20% of dairy intake with plant-based alternatives could increase the risk of inadequate iodine intake, particularly among adolescent girls and women of reproductive age.

“People often worry about a decline in calcium intake when they replace some of their dairy with plant-based alternatives. As a result, iodine is frequently overlooked. This study serves as a valuable reminder of iodine’s significance as a nutrient,” said Victoria Finn, MD, board certified endocrinologist at Medical Offices of Manhattan, who wasn’t involved in the study.

The study included analysis of UK plant-based milk, cheese, and yogurt products, as well as dietary data from 3,500 people.

The researchers used statistical modeling to predict how swapping dairy for PBAs might affect iodine and other nutrients.

The people who are most vulnerable to iodine inadequacy are adolescent females and people at reproductive age, especially those who are pregnant or who plan to become pregnant.

The researchers estimated that replacing 20% to 35% of dairy intake with PBAs would increase the proportion of people with inadequate iodine intake:

When the researchers modeled the replacement of all dairy products with PBAs, the proportion of adolescent females with inadequate iodine intake rose to 80%. Among females ages 19 to 49, that proportion remained relatively stable as replacement rates increased.

It is important to keep in mind that the study used dietary modeling rather than direct measurement of iodine status.

Iodine is important for thyroid hormone production, metabolism, and both fetal and child brain development.

“Your thyroid gland requires iodine to produce thyroid hormones, which play a crucial role in regulating metabolism and supporting normal growth and development,” said Finn.

She added that if you consistently consume too little iodine over an extended period, the thyroid gland may enlarge and eventually struggle to produce enough hormones. This condition is known as hypothyroidism, which can lead to various symptoms such as lethargy, sleepiness, weight gain, dry skin, and more.

“Hypothyroidism can be particularly dangerous during pregnancy, as the baby’s brain, nervous system, and organs rely heavily on the mother’s thyroid hormone during the early stages of development,” Finn added.

In the United Kingdom, dairy is a major iodine source due to cattle feed supplementation, not because dairy is naturally high in iodine.

In the United States, dairy is also an important source of iodine for the same reasons.

However, the risk profile for iodine deficiency in the U.S. may differ from the U.K., as iodized salt is also a significant source of iodine and is widely available in the U.S., but not in the U.K.

According to the National Institutes of Health (NIH), the recommended daily intake of iodine depends on your age.

Plant-based or plant-forward diets have grown in popularity, in part due to the health benefits they offer. For example, consuming less meat can reduce your risk of heart disease, type 2 diabetes, obesity, and high cholesterol.

However, as this study suggests, it may be possible to reduce your iodine intake when transitioning to a plant-based diet. There are ways you can protect your iodine intake when choosing a more plant-forward diet, though.

Check the labels on plant-based dairy alternatives to find those fortified with iodine. You can also read the nutrition label. Iodine may be listed as potassium iodide or potassium iodate.

Seafood, eggs, and iodized salt are all good sources of iodine.

You can also discuss iodine supplements with your healthcare professional.

“Iodine supplements should be taken with caution, especially without checking your thyroid levels,” said Finn. “Additionally, be cautious with kelp supplements, as their iodine content can vary, and excessive iodine intake can disrupt thyroid function.”
