Bitcoin Tanks Ahead of Anthropic’s IPO. Should We Really Blame AI?

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Bitcoin Tanks Ahead of Anthropic’s IPO. Should We Really Blame AI?

Bitcoin fell toward $63,000 Wednesday, extending a six-week retreat just as AI developer Anthropic prepared to price one of Wall Street’s biggest initial public offerings.

The timing has revived an attractive theory: investors are selling crypto to buy AI shares.

Right now Bitcoin is about to touch $62,000, down 3.8% on today’s session. Anthropic expects to price shares Wednesday at an indicated $70 to $78, ahead of Thursday’s Nasdaq debut under ANT, potentially valuing the company at $1.4 trillion, according to its IPO prospectus.

Kraken chief economist Thomas Perfumo told MarketWatch the IPO “could also be drawing capital out of risk assets” as investors position for the sale.

That’s a theory Binance founder Changpeng Zhao shared, flagging the listing as one possible factor in Bitcoin’s decline in an X post Sunday.

No one knows for sure. Correlation does not prove causation. Here are some potential reasons.

There is a tempting precedent. Bitcoin fell 19% in the 50 days before SpaceX listed in June and 23% in the equivalent period before Anthropic. But the comparison misses an important difference: AI stocks rallied before SpaceX; this time they have been falling alongside crypto.

“This time, the sell-off has been led by the AI trade itself,” Block Scholes research analyst Thahbib Rahman wrote in comments shared with Decrypt. Over the prior 50 days, Nvidia fell 32% and the Nasdaq dropped 11%, unlike their gains ahead of SpaceX’s IPO.

Bitcoin’s 30-day correlation with Nvidia rose from 0.20 to 0.56, while its correlation with gold fell from 0.63 to 0.39. “Liquidity is ebbing and flowing out of crypto and equities together once more,” Rahman wrote.

Bitcoin’s daily chart looks weak without any IPO theory. The relative strength index reads 21.8, well below the 30 threshold commonly associated with oversold markets, while an average directional index of 38.7 indicates a strong trend—in this case, downward.

The 50-day exponential moving average is also below its 200-day counterpart, a bearish configuration associated with the so-called death cross. The pattern historically signals deteriorating momentum and the possibility of further losses, although it is a lagging indicator and does not necessarily predict where prices will go next.

Also, oversold does not necessarily mean the selling has ended.

The immediate floor is around $62,000, close to Sunday’s low. Glassnode data show buyers absorbed 66,000 BTC between $62,000 and $66,000 during heavy selling, but stablecoin supply has shrunk 4.2% over 90 days. Below $62,000, the next psychological level is $60,000; resistance sits near $65,500, $69,300 and $73,700.

More tangible selling pressure has already been recorded. U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs lost $239.9 million Tuesday, including $209.5 million from BlackRock’s IBIT. Nearly $2 billion in leveraged crypto bets were liquidated during the weekend rout, amplifying losses as exchanges forcibly closed positions.

Hilbert Group portfolio manager Jesse Marre called the selloff “simply a stop loss run,” arguing that derivatives traders had already capitulated. Rising Treasury yields, a stronger dollar, and renewed expectations of Federal Reserve rate hikes also make speculative assets less attractive.

In other words, the dip is more natural market moves than money switching markets.

Nvidia forecast $130 billion in fourth-quarter sales, beating expectations after Jensen Huang’s abrupt departure. Yet markets remained split: global shares slipped 0.1%, while the Nasdaq gained 0.35% early and CoreWeave dropped 5.4%.

BitMEX co-founder Arthur Hayes sees the IPO less as a drain than a barometer. “Bitcoin’s going to go up too” if Anthropic soars, he said, while a weak debut could deepen pressure across risk assets.

Anthropic is scheduled to price Wednesday and start trading Thursday. Before that debut, Bitcoin must hold the same $62,000 area buyers defended during Sunday’s liquidation wave.

Switch 2 Is Getting an All-new Gamecube Controller

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

8BitDo has a wireless take on a beloved gamepad

Super Smash Bros. fans, this one’s for you. Peripheral maker 8BitDo just revealed the GC Bluetooth Gamepad, a wireless spin on the GameCube controller meant for Nintendp Switch, Switch 2, and Windows PC. It’s available in the GameCube’s iconic purple-and-orange color palette and features the "classic GC design and iconic button layout," according to its product description. The controller can connect wirelessly via Bluetooth 5.0 or a 2.4 GHz adapter, and has wired support via a USB-C cable. All three connection types work with the original Switch and Switch 2, while only wired and 2.4 GHz wireless connections work on PC. The controller goes for $49.99 and ships on Dec. 10, 2026.

Nintendo released its own wireless GameCube controller for the Switch 2 last year. It was closely modeled after the original gamepad, with a few small changes, like additional buttons for better compatibility with the Switch 2.

The most obvious reason that both Nintendo and 8BitDo created modern versions of the GameCube controller is that the Switch 2 supports certain GameCube games through a Nintendo Switch Online subscription. Specifically, the service has beloved titles like The Legend of Zelda: The Wind Waker and F-Zero GX. You can even play Wario World on there, for some reason.

Beyond that, GameCube controllers have remained surprisingly relevant over the years thanks to Super Smash Bros. fans. The controller is still popular because many players first got into the series through Super Smash Bros. Melee on the GameCube, which maintains an active tournament circuit. Nintendo even officially re-released the original wired GameCube controller for the Wii U as a tie-in for Smash 4. The company later released a USB adapter that lets the original GameCube controller work with the original Switch when playing games like Super Smash Bros. Ultimate.

Another reason many players likely want a modern wireless GameCube controller is to play emulated games on PC. While reviews described Nintendo’s GameCube controller for the Switch 2 as faithful to the original, players found it wasn’t compatible with the original Switch or Windows by default, limiting its use cases. 8BitDo’s version of the gamepad works with both of these platforms. Additionally, the 8BitDo version costs $20 less than Nintendo’s official release. We’ll find out whether the third-party peripheral matches the feel of the beloved GameCube controller when it launches next month.

The story of Augustin is even more interesting, from a player who signed on loan from RB Leipzig, to one who ended up costing the Whites around £40m, despite playing only 48 minutes of football for them.

CoreWeave and Nebius: 2 AI Neocloud Stocks Due for a Rebound

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Neocloud stocks have sold off heavily in the wake of the Nvidia transition.

By Keithen Drury – Nov 18, 2026 at 2:15PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Nebius Group ›

The neocloud industry has gotten some bad headlines recently. While not as damaging as some of the headlines surrounding former Nvidia (NVDA -2.37%) CEO Jensen Huang, the secondary effects are harming neocloud stocks like CoreWeave (CRWV -4.30%) and Nebius (NBIS +2.11%). Both of these companies operate in this segment and may have seen the ground shift from underneath them.

But does that warrant the huge sell-off they've experienced? Let's take a look at these two, and see if now is the perfect buying opportunity.

The neocloud business is a fairly new one, and it was brought about by the massive investments in AI infrastructure. Essentially, the biggest AI hyperscalers couldn't get enough computing capacity online fast enough, so other companies decided to form and start building their own AI data centers, then rent those out to the AI hyperscalers. This has been a fast-growing business, as the hyperscalers are basically willing to pay a huge sum of money for access to more computing power.

The biggest problem with this is who is funding these data centers. These upstarts don't generate ample cash flows to build data centers, like some of the other major cloud computing providers. Instead, they must seek outside funding.

This can come from multiple sources, including issuing debt and selling shares in the company. One of the biggest backers of these two was Nvidia, as it saw CoreWeave and Nebius as two of the best operators in this space. Nvidia became a major shareholder of both companies as a result, but that relationship may be coming to an end.

With Huang out as CEO and Colette Kress, who comes from the CFO role, assuming the interim CEO role, these deals may be on hold. CoreWeave and Nebius could lose one of their major financiers and have to turn to the debt markets to raise funds.

That could be an issue, as CoreWeave recently saw some of its debt get priced at "distressed" levels, which means there's a low likelihood of it repaying its commitments. If CoreWeave were to default on its debt, that would leave the neocloud industry with a black eye. Funding would likely dry up for every company involved in this space, as lenders worry about the next firm to go belly-up.

There is huge risk in this industry right now, and the stocks are being priced accordingly. But with high risk comes high potential reward, and these stocks could rebound with a vengeance if the market changes its perception.

Both CoreWeave and Nebius are growing rapidly, but the market really only cares whether these two can pay off their debts and grow sustainably. Because neither of these companies is profitable, judging them on projected sales is the best measure.

CRWV PS Ratio (Forward 1y) data by YCharts

CoreWeave trades for a forward price-to-sales ratio of just 1, while Nebius is a bit more expensive at around 3. The price-to-sales ratio isn't as commonly used as the price-to-earnings ratio because it doesn't account for profit margins, but with these two producing no profits, it's the best tool.

To put it in perspective how cheap these two stocks are, if they could turn a 20% profit margin (which they're a long way from doing), that would price CoreWeave at 5 times forward earnings and Nebius at about 14. Those are cheap price tags for the growth these two are putting up.

CRWV Revenue (Quarterly YoY Growth) data by YCharts

But once again, the problem isn't growth; it's whether they can reach profitability. If they can, there's huge upside here. If they can't, then investors should stay away.

I think the market has gotten too bearish on these companies, pricing in virtually no chance of success. If these two can continue with the status quo, they could be due for a huge rebound.

I'm a cautious buyer here, and investors need to keep sizing to a minimum, as the risk is high.

Is Mauricio Pochettino’s job at Manchester United the toughest in the Premier League?

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Shumail Sajid writes about Manchester United for Read Man Utd and Crystal Palace for Read Crystal Palace, with occasional pieces on other Premier League clubs. He has also written for FanSided's Red Devils Dispatch, Yardbarker, Anfield Central and The Sports Side.

Manchester United hired Mauricio Pochettino as their next manager to replace Michael Carrick, who managed just one win in 10 games.

His appointment makes him Manchester United’s eighth permanent manager since Sir Alex Ferguson retired in 2013.

Pochettino brings considerable Premier League experience after previous spells with Southampton, Tottenham and Chelsea. He has also managed Paris Saint-Germain before the USA job.

As he faces Liverpool for his first game on Sunday, the question arises: is he facing the toughest job in the Premier League?

The question was posed on BBC Radio about who is going to have more pressure, Pochettino or Thomas Frank at Crystal Palace.

According to former Premier League defender Nedum Onuoha, the expectations surrounding Manchester United will make the Argentine’s job considerably harder.

Pochettino is preparing for his first game in charge of Manchester United, with Liverpool waiting at Anfield on November 22. However, the challenge facing the new manager goes beyond getting results on the pitch.

Onuoha believes Pochettino will have to deal with constant discussion and criticism about his work, much of it coming from former United players who are now in the media.

Choosing between Pochettino and Frank, Onuoha said, “Probably Pochettino, because every former Manchester United player works in the media, so there will always be discussion around what he is doing.

“Whereas Frank can just enjoy playing his football at Crystal Palace. If he does well then it is great, if he doesn’t do well then it is still OK.

“But from Pochettino’s perspective, if Manchester United finish the season 15th in the Premier League, whether we like it or not, there will be some criticism of him because he’s had 28 games to do something.”

The comments just highlight the difference in expectations at the two clubs. Frank may have more freedom to work without the same level of attention, while Pochettino will be judged from day one against the standards historically associated with Manchester United. That is one big problem for Pochettino.

Rory Smith from The Observer highlighted another glaring problem at United.  Highlighting a major problem, Smith said, “there is a cycle element to Manchester United whereby a manager comes in, makes things a bit better, and then within a year to 18 months they aren’t challenging for titles again because the Premier League has changed since they were a dominant force.”

Pochettino’s first taste of action as a United manager will be against Liverpool at Anfield on November 22. That opening fixture will give him an immediate test, but the bigger task will be to get back United to winning ways, step by step.

Will the Manchester United job be without pressure for Mauricio Pochettino? Absolutely Not.

Meta is finally addressing a particularly annoying quirk I'm confident has affected just about all of Instagram's 3 billion users at one point or another. More than a decade after Stories became a central part of the photo-sharing app, the company is giving users the ability to edit their posts after they've been shared.

Want to fix an embarrassing typo? Add or change the music? Write a different caption altogether? All that is now possible without the need to delete a post, Instagram head Adam Mosseri shared. With the change, Instagram users have a 15-minute window to tweak their Stories while preserving views, likes and replies.

Deleting and re-uploading a Story might not seem like a huge inconvenience, but it can be frustrating when it happens within a sequence of posts. Since Stories slides appear reverse chronologically, there's no way to delete and re-share a post and have it appear in the same place if there are other images immediately before and after. It's even worse if you're a professional creator or social media manager and need to worry about a do-over affecting your metrics. So while the 15-minute editing window only gives a little additional flexibility, it does give you the chance to catch errors that have already been posted.

It's not clear why such a seemingly obvious change took so long for Instagram to implement. Meta has long allowed users to edit regular Instagram posts after the fact. "A lot of people have been asking for this, so I'm excited to get this one out there," Mosseri wrote.

The feature is rolling out now. Once editing is available, you'll be able to tweak Stories from the "more" menu and selecting "edit story." Posts that have been edited will include an "edited" label when others view them.

Instagram is also tweaking a few aspects of the Stories composer too. The app is updating its available background color options to include gradients and other effects. It's also making it easier to share and format several images at once.

Mortgage Applications Dip as Rates Hit Three-Week High

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

House keys with a house shaped keychain resting on a mortgage application form

Mortgage applications dipped slightly this week, in keeping with the average 30-year mortgage rate rising to a three-week high.

The latest Market Composite Index from the Mortgage Bankers Association (MBA)—its measure of mortgage loan activity volume and includes purchases and refinances—shows mortgage application activity decreasing 2.3% on a seasonally adjusted basis after last week’s 6.2% increase. On an unadjusted basis, the Index decreased 5% compared with the previous week.

This week’s decline follows weeks of fluctuations as the 30-year fixed mortgage rate still solidly remains over 7%–last week decreasing to 7.11% according to Freddie Mac but currently sitting at 7.46%, according to Mortgage News Daily. Freddie Mac’s next rate report will be released Thursday.

“Mortgage applications dipped as mortgage rates moved higher, with the 30-year fixed mortgage rate up to its highest level in three weeks at 7.35 percent,” said Joel Kan, CMB, MBA’s vice president and deputy chief economist. “Purchase applications were down 18 percent from a year ago, as renewed affordability challenges from elevated mortgage rates have pushed prospective homebuyers to the sidelines.”

MBA’s Refinance Index decreased 2% from the previous week and was 54% lower than the same week one year ago. MBA’s seasonally adjusted Purchase Index–its weekly measurement of nationwide home loan applications based on a sample of about 75% of U.S. mortgage activity–decreased 3% from one week earlier. The unadjusted Purchase Index decreased 7% compared with the previous week and was 18% lower than the same week one year ago, according to the report.

The refinance share of mortgage activity saw an increase this week–to 41.1% from 39.8% the previous week, with Kan pointing to a rise in FHA refinances as most notable.

“Overall refinance activity was down over the week, but FHA refinances increased almost 7 percent,” Kan stated. “The FHA rate was around 30 basis points below the conforming rate, which supported refinance opportunities for some homeowners.”

The FHA share of total applications increased to 16.1% from 15.5% the week prior, with MBA listing the VA share of total applications increasing to 12.8% from 12.4% the week prior. The USDA share of total applications remained unchanged at 0.5 percent from the week prior.

Kan also pointed to additional macroeconomic indicators as potentially weighing on home-purchase application activity into the new year.

“Treasury yields rose last week amid data showing inflation accelerated in October and Fed officials acknowledging that additional rate hikes may be needed in the coming months,” he said.

Dopo vent'anni da professionista, Juan Mata ha deciso di mettere fine alla propria carriera da calciatore a 38 anni. Lo spagnolo, senza squadra dallo scorso settembre, ha comunicato la decisione attraverso un video accompagnato da una frase particolarmente emozionante: "A volte amare significa lasciar andare... Grazie, calcio".

Il centrocampista ha scelto lo spogliatoio del Juventud Estadio, a Oviedo, per annunciare il proprio addio. Il momento più importante della sua carriera è stato sicuramente il trionfo al Mondiale di Sudafrica 2010 con la nazionale spagnola. Mata, tuttavia, scese in campo soltanto per 20 minuti nella partita contro l'Honduras.

For a while there, it seemed like Lenovo had given up on selling handheld gaming PCs, hiking the price of its Legion Go 2 and Legion Go S even higher than other RAM-starved competitors. A decent one can easily cost $1,000 if not $2,000 now. But it looks like Lenovo might be about to announce a relatively cheap handheld once again— the Legion Go Beam.

Windows Latest has leaked images of the handheld, and there’s no question it’s designed to be a value alternative to even the company’s least expensive Legion Go S variant. While it’s got the same big stippled grips and triggers for comfort, there’s only one USB-C port on the top instead of two, a comparatively cheap-looking D-pad, no touchpad, and only a single tiny intake and exhaust for its fan.

There’s no SteamOS logo where that would go, so it likely runs Windows instead… but then there’s that name, “Beam.” Perhaps you’ll “beam” your games to it because it’s a streaming and cloud gaming handheld, instead of one that primarily plays games on its own chip? Maybe that’s why it doesn’t need as much airflow as previous handhelds?

Perhaps there’s a partnership with Xbox Cloud Gaming specifically, and that’s why it’s all lit up in Xbox green?

But it might just be green because it looks nice with white, which has been the “value” color for handhelds so far (see: ROG Ally, Xbox Ally, and the Windows-based Legion Go S). And it might be “Beam” because there’s a literal beam of light on the bottom, even though the first thing I do with most handhelds is turn any and all distracting RGB lighting off. Or maybe it’s “Beam” just because it sounds like a friendly, fun, value-oriented name, instead of something “S” or “Pro.”

I won’t begin to speculate on the Legion Go Beam’s price, except to say that “cheap” may be relative. The Legion Go S was originally supposed to cost $499, but wound up costing far more except on the occasional sale. The Legion Go 2 was supposed to start at $1,099 but wound up costing $1,500 and up after RAMageddon. Streaming-first handhelds have been far cheaper, though, like the $249 Sony PlayStation Portal.

Lenovo has a history of announcing its new gaming devices at CES in January each year, including previous Legion Go handhelds, so I’m expecting to see an official announcement of the Lenovo Legion Go Beam then. Even if so, that doesn’t mean it’ll be available in January — there’s often a many-month lag.
