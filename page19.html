New warning sent to Fire Stick users as police 'trawl through bank accounts' to contact illegal EFL streamers

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Customers of jailbroken Amazon Fire TV Stick devices have received a huge new warning over such practices as authorities continue to push forward in their bid to tackle the issue, with the police now taking fresh action.

It's well documented, of course, that many people up and down the country purchase illegal Fire Stick devices in order to avoid forking out for controversially-priced subscription packages from the likes of Sky Sports and TNT Sports.

A central role of this illegal market is the ability to stream live sporting events through the chipped software, which often works out significantly cheaper than a £30 monthly Sky Sports subscription, for example.

Indeed, a fair share of 'dodgy' Fire Stick customers will just happen to be EFL supporters, too, as the devices often enable users to bypass the 3pm broadcasting blackout on Saturday afternoons - making it much easier to watch their team in action.

Many are far more content with paying between £30 and £100 for an illegal device as opposed to a legal subscription package, much to the ire of the authorities.

Ultimately, though, it's against the law, and recent years have seen big-hitting broadcasters and the authorities alike join forces in an effort to prevent the ever-growing popularity of jailbroken Fire Stick devices.

Those found guilty of infiltrating the devices with illegal software for distribution and monetary gain purposes run the risk of landing behind bars, but it's not only the sellers that can find themselves on the wrong side of the law and a new warning has now been sent to anybody considering buying a pirated Fire Stick.

The police have attempted various measures in their ongoing battle to crack down on illegal broadcasting distribution and consumption over the last couple of years, but they're now changing their strategy in a development that shows just how serious the issue really is.

Ex-detective Ray Wilding recently spoke on the BBC's Morning Live show to reveal a fresh strategy from the authorities which will increase their chances of taking action against users as well as distributors.

Wilding disclosed that police have now decided to begin tracing the details found in the bank accounts of sellers, which will show exactly who has been purchasing 'dodgy' Fire Stick devices.

He explained: "They’re going for the distributors. They’re going for the sellers and they are having a lot of success.

"But actually, once they’ve got the seller, they’re going through their accounts. They’re seeing who has been paying the money for these illegal things, and if they’ve been paying them for these sticks then people should possibly expect a conversation or two if the police want to know why they’ve been paying them money for this.

"It’s a crime. It’s not allowed. Owning the stick itself is not a crime, but using it for this purpose is.

"They (subscription service providers) are losing money to this, so they have been reaching out and are taking action against those people using them illegally as well."

Much of the authorities' efforts have revolved around taking root of the problem and arresting individuals who are hijacking the devices with illegal software to distribute to the public, which has resulted in jail sentences.

Now, however, they're becoming increasingly interested in those keeping the illegal industry afloat by knowingly purchasing such devices, and simply buying a 'dodgy' Fire Stick can still land you in serious trouble.

Wilding himself has warned of this by adding: "I wouldn’t say it’s something you should do.

"It is actually a crime and it can have a nasty fine, a huge fine and also a custodial sentence for users and sellers of these things."

The most significant legal action will always, quite naturally, be brought against the individuals chipping and distributing 'dodgy' Fire Sticks in the first place, but it's clear that the police are more intent than ever to clamp down on users and this is simply the latest effort to be made.

That's because, earlier this year, the Federation Against Copyright Theft (FACT) announced that contact had been made with more than a thousand individuals across the UK warning them to 'immediately cease using illegal TV streaming services' in order to avoid the possibility of prosecution under Section 11 of the Fraud Act 2006.

FACT's campaign emphasised that users are not anonymous, with chairman Kieron Sharp having previously warned individuals of "potential criminal liability" and "exposure to scams, viruses and harmful content."

Netflix to Release Documentary ‘Russell Brand: Open Secret’

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Netflix will release a new documentary, Russell Brand: Open Secret, on Nov. 13. The film, directed by Olivia Isaacs, “charts the story of Russell Brand’s meteoric rise and steep fall following allegations of sexual offenses.”

According to a press release, Open Secret features inside voices and archive footage that offer insight into Brand’s life and the investigation run by U.K. publications the Times and the Sunday Times and their reporters Rosamund Urwin and Charlotte Wace. A short teaser for the film calls Brand a “shapeshifter” and a “grifter,” and explains that the journalists initially didn’t realize their investigation would lead to criminal charges.

Brand was found guilty of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault on Nov. 5 following a trial in London. He was found not guilty on two additional counts of rape, as well as one count of attempted indecent assault. The allegations against him were tied to claims made by six women between 1999 and 2009. The actor and comedian was sentenced to 10 years in prison.

Police began investigating Brand in 2023 after multiple women came forward with allegations of assault and abuse in a Channel 4 documentary and the Sunday Times report. Prosecutors first filed five charges against Brand in April 2025, while two additional charges were brought in December.

Following the trial, Brand’s attorney Ian Winter confronted Urwin outside the courtroom, telling her “I hope you are pleased with yourself.” Urwin responded, “I’m pleased justice has been done,” to which Winter replied, “Well, it wasn’t. You fucked it.” Winter later confirmed he had referred himself to the Bar Standards Board.

Urwin explained on a Times podcast that she approached Winter after the verdict because her name had been mentioned during the trial. “I thought he’s talked about me a lot, I’ll go and introduce myself and that is a legitimate thing to do,” she said. “I was surprised to be confronted and spoken to in that way.”

The bulk of Brand’s sentence is tied to the rape charge, for which the judge sentenced him to nine years in prison. Meanwhile, each of the three assault charges carried their own penalty: nine months, 18 months, and eight months. Blockchain infrastructure provider Chainstack revealed it experienced an AI-powered security breach in late October that exposed customer names, emails, and password hashes.

In an updated blog post on Tuesday, Chainstack said the incident has been contained and that customer-facing services continued to operate normally throughout the breach.

The company noted that an attacker first gained access to a development environment on Oct. 26, and reached parts of the production environment on Oct. 29. The firm then detected the intrusion and cut the access paths on Oct. 31. It later finalized the investigation on Nov. 2.

According to the post, the attacker accessed the system via an internet-exposed Grafana instance in a development cluster that was deployed for testing with its default administrator credentials and without an additional layer of protection.

The attacker then obtained machine credentials that reached production, and used them to access the database network. Chainstack said the attacker used the ARTEX AI agent framework to carry out the breach.

The Block has reached out to Chainstack for further comment.

Following the incident, Chainstack said it is currently treating node credentials as exposed and notifying customers to rotate them and change passwords. The company added that customer nodes, console, and API were not modified. Self-service rotation for node credentials is currently available in the console without downtime, per the post.

Chainstack also warned customers to watch for phishing attempts. "Chainstack will never ask for passwords, tokens, or keys," the team said.

The incident comes amid a wave of cyberattacks and security breaches as AI tools become increasingly capable. Earlier this week, crosschain interoperability protocol Across suffered a domain takeover, and later restored control of its website and resumed normal operations.

The crypto market is struggling to regain momentum as Bitcoin and other digital assets face continued pressure. Slowing US liquidity and regulatory uncertainty are two major concerns weighing on the market, according to Real Vision founder Raoul Pal and investor Kevin O’Leary.

While Pal believes tighter financial conditions are limiting money flowing into crypto, O’Leary says the lack of clear US regulations is preventing businesses from adopting blockchain technology on a wider scale.

Raoul Pal believes slowing US liquidity is one of the main reasons behind the crypto market’s weak performance.

Pal said US liquidity has remained largely flat since July 2026. A stronger dollar, rising interest rates and a flatter yield curve have slowed money creation through the banking system.

He explained that crypto needs increasing liquidity to support price growth, but current conditions are not providing enough support.

“Markets have gone nowhere. Narrow liquidity, so this is total liquidity including the banking system loans and leases. It’s not growing as fast,” Pal said.

Kevin O’Leary points to regulatory uncertainty in the US as another reason the broader crypto market is struggling.

Resident Evil 1 Remake Gets Official Update From Capcom

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Capcom has officially provided an update regarding the remake of the first Resident Evil game. The developer/publisher has been steadily remaking the classics into modernized versions, more similar to what the franchise looks like today. That started back in 2019, with the release of Resident Evil 2 Remake, but we’re now looking at a new Resident Evil Remake collection for 2027. The company recently announced that it is also working on the remake of one of the most impactful entries in the series, but until now, talks about a remake of Resident Evil 1 hadn’t occurred.

As part of a special Nikkei Entertainment issue focused mainly on the 30th anniversary of Resident Evil, according to a report by Games Radar, series producer Jun Takeuchi provided some information about the remake of Resident Evil 1. The developer did not confirm or deny the long-standing rumors about the remake, but he did explain why Capcom did not choose the first entry in the series as the first remake the series would handle, and it has everything to do with how different the first game is from the second and all other installments later on.

According to Takeuchi, Resident Evil 1 wasn’t chosen as the first option for the remakes at Capcom “because it’s such a stoic game.” The producer details that the first entry in the franchise has its horror fueled by that same “stoicism,” and that the company was concerned that a remake of that game would “not resonate with today’s game fans.” The rumored remake of Resident Evil 1 would be, indeed, drastically different from Resident Evil 2 Remake, on all fronts.

What Takeuchi seems to describe is how much the first game’s horror is built around a continuously tense atmosphere and ambiance. The sequel’s horror comes mostly from a large stalking presence. Moreover, the series sees a huge pivot. While the first game is about surviving without getting caught, the franchise has constantly evolved into something a lot more action-oriented, where players have tons of different tools at their disposal to deal with the infected. Those who played the latest core Resident Evil games would see something completely different in a potential Resident Evil 1 Remake.

If a fan’s first contact with Resident Evil came with Resident Evil 5, Resident Evil 6, or Resident Evil 7: Biohazard, they’d likely expect a bit more adrenaline and action, and Resident Evil 1 is more about getting haunted by your surroundings. This isn’t a confirmation that the first entry won’t get a remake, but an explanation as to why it wasn’t Capcom’s starting point. The rumors about a Resident Evil 1 Remake are strong, but there is still something officially announced before that: the upcoming Resident Evil: Code Veronica Remake.

Given how successful the remake installments have proven to be, despite the well-deserved criticisms of Resident Evil 3 Remake, it wouldn’t be a surprise if Capcom were to continue making remakes of the series. After the Code Veronica remake, the most logical step would be to remake the oldest entry in the Resident Evil series, and if the rumors are to be believed, it’s already an unannounced reality we just have to wait for.
