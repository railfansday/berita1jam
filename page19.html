Robert Pattinson was just announced as the next James Bond, and the internet has spent the days since joking that no Hollywood role is safe from him. I thought those jokes had about run their course until Ralph Fiennes weighed in. The actor who terrified an entire generation of Harry Potter fans as Lord Voldemort was asked who should take over the role in HBO's upcoming television series, and his answer was so dry that I almost missed how funny it was.

Fiennes was speaking with the Associated Press at the New York premiere of The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping on Nov. 16. The conversation turned to the new book-to-screen adaptation of the Harry Potter novels and whether he'd consider returning as He Who Must Not Be Named. Fiennes indicated he'd had his fill of the character. As for who should put on the robes next, he offered one name:

Maybe they'll offer it to Robert Pattinson.

I love how little effort went into that joke. The Red Dragon veteran actor didn't need to explain it, either. Following Pattinson's Bond casting, social media has been having a field day with the idea that he's taking every acting job. One popular variation suggests that artificial intelligence isn't coming for anyone's job because Pattinson will get there first. The OG Potter villain apparently decided to throw one more highly coveted part onto the pile.

Of course, there's another reason his suggestion is particularly good, and it involves a Harry Potter movie I still have a hard time watching without getting a little upset. Pattinson played Cedric Diggory in 2005's Harry Potter and the Goblet of Fire, the same film that introduced Fiennes as the fully resurrected Voldemort. Cedric made it through the Triwizard Tournament only to be murdered by Peter Pettigrew on Voldemort's orders. It's a miserable ending for one of the story's most likable characters.

So imagine the actor who played Cedric now taking over as the man who ordered his death. I'd almost pay to see Pattinson in the makeup just for that bit of full-circle casting, although I'm pretty sure Fiennes was having a laugh rather than making a serious recommendation.

The two actors also have a second franchise connection thanks to Bond. Fiennes played M opposite Daniel Craig in Skyfall, Spectre and No Time to Die, so he's watched a different actor occupy the 007 role that Pattinson is now inheriting. The Tenet actor has already spent plenty of time bouncing between massive franchises, from Twilight to The Batman. It's getting hard to name an iconic fictional universe he hasn't visited.

The actual casting of Voldemort remains a mystery. Fiennes previously said he was all for Cillian Murphy playing the Dark Lord, and there's been no shortage of rumors about who might portray the villain. He's also offered the eventual actor some practical advice on navigating Voldemort's robes. Whoever gets the job has quite a performance to follow.

I'm excited to see who HBO chooses, but Fiennes has made me realize there's one more possibility I hadn't considered. If Pattinson somehow lands the role, he'll have played Cedric Diggory and the wizard who had him killed. At this rate, I'd hesitate to rule out anything.

Lufthansa Turns Airplane Etiquette Into Clothing

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Lufthansa is turning airplane etiquette into a clothing collection, including a new shirt reminding passengers that there is no need to stand up immediately after landing. I like the idea and agree with much of the advice, though standing up is not necessarily the problem…pushing past everyone else is.

Lufthansa has announced that four items from its Travel Etiquette Collection will go on sale through the WorldShop beginning November 27, 2026.

The collection debuted in August and was not originally intended for sale. Lufthansa says the response prompted a retail edition, including a new shirt based on the most popular suggestion from its social media community.

Each design is limited to 250 individually numbered pieces. Lufthansa will donate its licensing proceeds to help alliance, the Lufthansa Group’s aid organization (that is the licensing revenue, rather than necessarily the entire purchase price).

Prices were not included in the announcement.

First off, I love these…simple yet stylish.

As to the messaging itself, I understand why the shirt appeals to people. We have all watched passengers spring up as though standing in the aisle will somehow make the jet bridge attach faster.

And if the aircraft is still moving or the seatbelt sign remains illuminated, it should go without saying that you stay seated.

But once the aircraft is parked and passengers are permitted to get up, I have no objection to someone standing. After several hours in an economy class seat, stretching your legs is perfectly understandable. An aisle passenger can also retrieve a bag and be ready to leave when the rows ahead have cleared.

The problem is the person who charges forward from row 28 and wedges himself beside row 19, apparently convinced that everyone else is sitting there for his convenience.

Stand if you want. Just wait your turn to leave.

(The only exception I would make are the circumstances in which the crew asks passengers to remain seated so travelers with tight connections can get off first. That requires everyone to cooperate, including those of us who might otherwise prefer to stand…but I also have a problem with the “free riders” in those situations…another issue for another day.)

My favorite in this collection is the “Recline Responsibly” message, which strikes me as common sense, but something so many passengers are so rude about.

If an airline installs a reclining seat, passengers should generally be able to use that feature. But look behind you first and recline slowly. Giving the person behind you a moment to adjust a laptop or move a drink costs you very little.

During meal service, the seat should be upright, but other than that, recline away…just gently.

We spend so much time arguing about whether reclining is acceptable that we sometimes overlook how much the manner of doing it matters. A slow adjustment is very different from throwing your seat back without warning.

Lufthansa will begin selling four limited-edition etiquette items on November 27. I like the collection, particularly the reminder to recline responsibly. But I can’t say I’ll be up late waiting to buy it…how about you?

The Tech Sell-Off Is Creating the Best Buying Window in 2 Years. Here’s What I’m Buying.

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The recent tech rout didn't care which businesses were great and which weren't, and that's exactly why I'm buying two of the best.

By Micah Zimmerman – Nov 25, 2026 at 8:38AM EST

If you've checked your portfolio lately, I'm sorry. I looked at mine, too.

The Nasdaq had its worst day since April 2025 on Nov. 5, and things got worse after Nvidia (NVDA -5.86%) said on Nov. 12 that Jensen Huang had resigned as CEO. Not even Nvidia's better-than-expected earnings could stop the slide. By Nov. 20, all three major averages were in correction, the Nasdaq was negative for 2026, and the Nasdaq and Dow had just logged their worst week in more than six years.

Then the scenery changed. Nvidia's board unanimously reinstated Huang, who said, "I can't wait to get back to work." Nvidia shares jumped 31% for their best day since 2003, and the Nasdaq posted its best day since April 2025. The relief faded fast, as Nvidia gave back about 7% in the next session and pulled other chip stocks down with it.

Huang's return answered one question, not the bigger one: Is AI spending slowing? Democratic midterm wins and signs that big tech may rein in spending have raised doubts about the AI build-out, and traders have been bracing for the Federal Reserve to raise interest rates in December. Nvidia's board also says its investigation will proceed to conclusion.

I can't call the bottom, because market timing rarely works. But in the latest sell-off, the market sold the great businesses right along with the shaky ones. Here are the two I'm buying.

ASML (ASML -3.45%) is the only company in the world that makes extreme ultraviolet lithography machines, the tools Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing uses to print the most advanced chips. That's about as wide an economic moat as you'll find in tech.

ASML got whipsawed with the rest of the chip group, once dropping 5.8% in a single session as chip stocks sold off across Asia and Europe. Even after the bounce, shares closed Nov. 23 about 31% below their 52-week high.

The selling assumes chipmakers will pull back. ASML's customers say otherwise. "Our customers continue to increase their short- and medium-term equipment demand forecasts with us," CEO Christophe Fouquet said last month, adding that "some customers are expressing an interest in placing orders with us for 2029 and even 2030." He added, "our supply as currently planned for both 2027 and 2028 will not fully meet the demand of our customers."

China is the risk I'm watching. In mid-November, the Commerce Department moved to close a loophole that let Chinese firms rent Nvidia computing power abroad, and ASML still expects China to be about 20% of sales.

Shopify (SHOP -1.48%) had nothing to do with Nvidia's drama, yet it fell 4% in a single session during the sell-off. Even after the rebound, it closed Nov. 23 about 26% below its 52-week high. With a P/E ratio near 80, it's exactly the kind of stock that gets hit when rate-hike fears grow.

The business looks nothing like the stock. Revenue grew 32% last quarter, and CFO Jeff Hoffmeister said, "We extended our track record of consistent execution, with 32% revenue growth and a 22% free cash flow margin." Its guidance calls for revenue growth in the "low thirties" this quarter, and President Harley Finkelstein said, "I do think this is the first holiday season where AI-assisted shopping capabilities will become mainstream."

Recent shopper news has been encouraging. Walmart (WMT +2.11%) raised its full-year outlook. Target (TGT +4.68%) posted its best sales growth in more than five years. The test comes soon: Shopify usually reports its merchants' Black Friday-Cyber Monday sales the Tuesday after Cyber Monday. A weak number could push the stock lower. Still, as Finkelstein put it after Shopify's earnings-day drop, "we're going to keep building for the long term and ignore the noise day to day."

That's my plan, too. In recent weeks, the market panicked over one set of headlines and cheered another. ASML and Shopify kept growing through both, and I'll take their shares at a discount.

Mike McCarthy may have tipped his hand more than a week before he moved on from Aaron Rodgers in an absolute stunner.

Longtime NFL reporter Charles Robinson re-posted a clip after the news broke of Rodgers' benching of Mike Tirico and Cris Collinsworth discussing backup Mason Rudolph after he replaced Rodgers at the end of the team's blowout "Sunday Night Football" loss to the Bengals on Nov. 15.

"Kind of an interesting deal in Pittsburgh as we talked to Mike McCarthy last night," Tirico said late in the fourth quarter. "They're a roster with four quarterbacks, people were looking for people at the trade deadline. No real movement of the quarterbacks here. Aaron says it's his last year, Mason Rudolph has been around the Steelers facility for quite some time, Will Howard, Drew Allar, Penn Stater, drafted. Mike McCarthy said, 'I've got some pretty good quarterbacks here.'"

Collinsworth followed: "He even brought up the idea that hey, everybody assumes we're going to take a quarterback with Aaron retiring. We've got pretty good quarterbacks in here, as if, 'I don't know about that. I want one of these young quarterbacks or do I want to take one of these guys that we've had a full year to develop here and let them play?' If it falls to them in the right way, they probably will. If not, I think they just roll out Mason Rudolph. They've been impressed."

Robinson initially posted the clip stating that it shows why teams should not let coaches dictate teams' quarterback plans and why he believes McCarthy said something of note in the production meeting, citing that the 31-year-old Rudolph is not a viable long-term option.

Tuesday's news made that segment raise even more eyebrows.

"Absolutely time to revisit this #Steelers clip. Given what just happened with Rodgers, I refuse to believe Mike McCarthy didn't say something in the production meeting," he tweeted.

"There's no way. The Rudolph stuff is wildly on the nose of where we are right now with Rodgers getting benched."

Production meetings happen between coaches and announcers before games, providing a mix of on- and off-the-record details to help the broadcasters prepare for the game.

That Tirico and Collinsworth shared the Rudolph discussion indicated that McCarthy did not mind it getting out there, and it's certainly notable that Rudolph is now starting Friday.

Presumed first-rounders like Arch Manning and Dante Moore have only seen their stock fall, and the Steelers currently own the 21st pick in the 2027 NFL Draft.

There are also questions about the viability of Howard, a 2025 sixth-rounder, but Allar provides a potentially intriguing option.

As Murphy prepares to close a nearly four-decade career at Coca-Cola, his advice is a reminder that career growth isn’t necessarily about having a perfect plan. It’s about being willing to take on challenges, doing today’s work well and building relationships along the way.

AMD Stock Would Need to Rise About 57% to Get Back to Its Record. History Says How Long Past Comebacks Took.

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The chipmaker has come back from drops this big six times -- at very different paces.

By Daniel Sparks – Nov 25, 2026 at 8:37AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Advanced Micro Devices ›

Advanced Micro Devices (AMD -8.86%) stock would need to rise about 57% just to get back to its record. As of this writing, shares trade near $413, off about 36% from their Oct. 6 closing high of $649.42.

The fall came fast. Shares first closed more than 35% under the record on Nov. 5, just 30 days after the peak. They sank 17% that day alone.

A bounce to around $585 a week later didn't last. The stock fell again after a short-seller report on Nov. 19, and a broad sell-off in chip stocks on Tuesday pushed it back near its Nov. 5 close.

AMD shareholders have been here before. In daily price data since 1980, the chipmaker's stock has fallen 35% or more from a record six other times, though never this fast.

It eventually recovered every time. But the waits ran from around a year and a half to almost 20 years, and none of those drops ended at the 35% level.

Measured from each record close to the next one, the last comeback was the fastest. After peaking in March 2024, AMD shares hit a new record in October 2025, around 1.6 years later. The drops that started in 1980 and November 2021 both took around 2.1 years to erase, and the one that began in 1997 took about 3 years.

The other two took much longer. After peaking in August 1984, the shares didn't hit a new record until March 1997 -- a wait of around 12.5 years. And after its June 2000 peak, AMD shares needed over 19 years, finally passing that high in January 2020.

AMD's record here is mixed. Four of the six drops were made up within three years, but two took over a decade.

Buying when AMD shares first fell 35% under a record never meant buying near the bottom. From the day each of the six drops crossed that line, the stock fell another 41% to 95% before it bottomed.

Measured from the highs, the lows ranged from 63% under the record (in April 2025) to almost 97% below (in July 2015). The 1984 drop didn't bottom until 1990, around 91% below its peak.

The first year was usually tough, too. A year after each crossing, AMD stock was down four times out of six, with losses of 27% to 45%.

And a rise in that first year didn't mean a fast comeback. The stock was up 30% a year after its August 2024 crossing and hit a new record around two months later. But it was also up 16% a year after its 1984 crossing, early in what became the 12.5-year wait.

This time, AMD's first close under the 35% line was at $404.40. Another 41% drop from there would put the stock around $240. That isn't a forecast, but it's what the smallest of AMD's six past drops would look like from here.

The business, meanwhile, is still growing fast. AMD's revenue for the third quarter of 2026 climbed 45% year over year to a record $13.4 billion, up from $11.5 billion in the second quarter. Management expects roughly $17 billion in fourth-quarter revenue, implying growth of about 66% year over year. This forecast also implies sequential growth of about 27%, accelerating from 16% in Q3.

Data center segment sales, which include the company's AI chips and server processors, almost doubled from the year-ago quarter, hitting $8.5 billion.

The Nov. 19 short-seller report was from Hunterbrook, which disclosed a short position in AMD (a bet that the stock drops). It alleged that a blacklisted Chinese server maker kept getting newer AMD firmware, and Hunterbrook also argued that new U.S. export rules might tighten limits on AMD's foreign business. AMD said the claims misrepresent its business and that it follows U.S. export laws. None of the allegations has been proven.

Based on analysts' average estimate for 2027, the stock trades at a forward price-to-earnings ratio of around 23. For a company expecting growth this fast, that looks like a reasonable price to me.

Overall, I think AMD's business gives investors good reason to own this growth stock. But the stock's own past is a good reason to hold less of it than the business alone could justify.

I'd want a stake small enough that a drop to about $240, or a wait much longer than two years, wouldn't push me to sell.

Stomach problems in autistic children raise new questions about gut bacteria

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

A study found that autistic children with more severe digestive symptoms tended to have more difficulty picking up on social cues.

Children with autism often have stomach problems. What causes them, however, is still up for debate. Some scientists believe the lining of the gut becomes damaged or inflamed.

Little evidence for that explanation was found in a new study.

Autistic children with more severe digestive symptoms tended to have more difficulty picking up on social cues, according to parent questionnaires.

The researchers also found links between certain gut bacteria and digestive symptoms.

The study was led by J. Marc Rhoads. He is professor and chief of the Division of Gastroenterology, Hepatology, and Nutrition at McGovern Medical School at UTHealth Houston.

Many children with autism are unable to explain where they feel pain. In such cases a change in behavior may be the first sign of a problem.

“Families may notice increased irritability, repetitive behaviors, or self-injurious behaviors when a child is experiencing gastrointestinal issues such as gas, constipation, or diarrhea,” Rhoads said.

“For children who are nonverbal, these behaviors may be one of the few ways they can communicate discomfort.”

About one in 31 American eight-year-olds had been identified with autism in 2022. This estimate was published by the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention.

Significant digestive problems have been reported in 31% to 84% of people with autism in earlier studies.

The researchers examined 72 children with autism who were between four and 16 years of age. All of the children had been enrolled in a probiotic trial. Their data were collected between 2016 and 2023.

Boys made up about 78% of the group and roughly a quarter of the children did not speak. Children with known digestive, liver or kidney diseases were excluded from the study. Those who had recently taken antibiotics or probiotics were also left out.

Several symptoms were assessed by the team. These included constipation, gas, stomach pain, unusually smelly stools and waking up at night.

A score of seven or more on a 17-point scale was taken to indicate significant digestive problems. About 63% of the children were placed in this group.

Parents were also asked to complete a questionnaire about the social behavior of their children. Higher scores on this questionnaire reflected more pronounced autism traits.

Higher scores were generally seen in children with worse stomach problems. The connection was not particularly strong but it reached statistical significance.

The clearest difference was observed in social awareness. This area covers abilities such as noticing the facial expressions, feelings or actions of another person.

Children with significant digestive problems scored about 7.5 points higher in this area. Their scores were compared with those of children who had milder symptoms.

The cells that line the intestines are packed closely together. The spaces between them are sealed by specialized proteins.

Substances from inside the gut may pass into the bloodstream if these seals are weakened. Inflammation could then be triggered in the body. This process is commonly described as a leaky gut.

Blood samples were tested for two proteins associated with these seals. Another blood protein linked to intestinal inflammation was measured as well. Stool samples were also analyzed for calprotectin, a protein routinely used by doctors to detect gut inflammation.

Most of the results fell within the normal range. Elevated calprotectin was detected in only two children and neither of them had significant digestive symptoms.

“Biomarkers of intestinal inflammation and gut permeability remained within normal ranges and were not associated with gastrointestinal symptom severity or autism symptom severity,” Rhoads said.

Children with worse stomach problems therefore did not show more signs of inflammation or a leaky gut on these tests.

Mixed results have been produced by earlier research. High calprotectin levels were found in up to a third of autistic children in some studies. No significant difference was found in a more recent review.

The bacterial findings proved to be more revealing than the blood tests.

Additional stool samples were obtained from Texas Children’s Microbiome Center. This allowed the analysis to be expanded to 135 children. The group included children with autism, their siblings and unrelated children without autism.

Fewer types of gut bacteria were present in children with autism than in their siblings. A similar difference was seen when they were compared with unrelated children. However, this second result may have arisen by chance.

The bacterial communities of children with autism also resembled one another more closely. Greater variety was observed among the other children.

Two groups of bacteria were more abundant in autistic children with significant digestive problems. One of these groups is known as Ruminococcus.

Children with higher levels of these bacteria reported more gas and stronger-smelling stools. Night waking was also more frequent in these children. Their overall digestive symptom scores were higher as well.

Ruminococcus feeds on the mucus that protects the intestinal wall. Higher levels of this bacterium in one-year-olds were linked to greater social difficulties at age three in an earlier study.

Another bacterial group called Sarcina was associated with gas, stomach pain, diarrhea and night waking. The researchers noted that Sarcina had not been linked to autism in previous studies.

The study captured a snapshot of digestive symptoms, gut bacteria and behavior.

The children were examined at a single point in time. It was therefore not possible to tell which change came first.

Gut bacteria may cause stomach problems, or digestive problems may alter the bacterial population.

Diet could also play a part in this relationship. Many children with autism prefer a limited range of foods. The bacteria living in the intestines are strongly influenced by what a person eats.

The study also had a few other limitations. Blood was not collected from healthy children for ethical reasons. Results were compared with established normal ranges instead.

Children with known digestive diseases were excluded by the team. Some of the analyses did not account for other factors that might have influenced the findings.

Children could be followed over several years in future studies. Such work may show whether changes in gut bacteria occur before digestive or behavioral changes.

Rhoads believes that everyday habits deserve more attention.

“This study highlights the importance of supporting a healthy gut microbiome in children with autism,” Rhoads said.

“A varied diet rich in fruits, vegetables, and whole grains, along with healthy toileting habits, may help improve both gastrointestinal health and behavior.”

“Our team has also found promising results with probiotic treatment.”

The results of the probiotic trial have not yet been published. Scientists are still working to determine which probiotic supplements are helpful and under what conditions.

The most useful lesson for parents may concern the recognition of discomfort rather than bacteria.

A child may suddenly become irritable, sleep poorly or show more repetitive behaviors. Such changes may be caused by something as ordinary as constipation or stomach pain.

Many children are unable to put this discomfort into words. Behavior may be one of the ways in which they ask for help.

The study is published in the journal Pediatric Research.

Driving for Thanksgiving? These are the worst times to be on the road this week

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Travel  |  Read Source →

Another Thanksgiving week is upon us, which means tens of millions of Americans will once again travel by plane, train, and automobile to see their loved ones. However, this Thanksgiving period will be a little different than in recent years—at least when it comes to transportation. That’s because the American Automobile Association (AAA) says that, for the first time in several years, fewer Americans will be traveling.

“After three years of record highs, the number of travelers is slightly lower partly because of higher travel costs this year,” says Stacey Barber, vice president of AAA travel. “But travelers won’t notice much of a difference on the roads and at the airports, since projected volumes remain near record levels.” 

That means that despite fewer travelers, the roads are still expected to be packed at times. With that in mind, here are the best and worst times to travel by car over the 2026 Thanksgiving period.

Best times to hit the roads for the Thanksgiving travel period

For the 2026 Thanksgiving period, AAA expects 72.9 million people to travel to their holiday destinations by car. That’s down from last year’s 73.1 million automobile travelers, but still a significant amount.

AAA defines the 2026 Thanksgiving period as the seven days lasting from Tuesday, November 24 to Monday, November 30, 2026. But despite the expected 72.9 million road travelers, how congested the streets will be depends heavily on when you travel during the period.

AAA says these are the best times to be on the road (ie: the times with the expected lowest congestion) over the period, according to transportation data and insights provider INRIX data compiled by the auto organization:

  • Tuesday, November 24: Before 1:00 PM
  • Wednesday, November 25: Before 12:00 PM 
  • Thursday, November 26 (Thanksgiving): Minimal Traffic Impact Expected 
  • Friday, November 27: Before 12:00 PM 
  • Saturday, November 28: Before 11:00 AM 
  • Sunday, November 29: Before 11:00 AM 
  • Monday, November 30: Before 1:00 PM 

Worst times to hit the roads for the Thanksgiving travel period

INRIX data shows that the worst days to travel over the Thanksgiving period—that is, the days the congestion is expected to be the highest—are the Tuesday and Wednesday before Thanksgiving, and the Sunday and Monday after. Those are the days when most people choose to travel to and from their holiday destinations.

As for the individual times that are expected to be the worst to travel by car, those are:

  • Tuesday, November 24: 2:00 PM to 8:00 PM 
  • Wednesday, November 25: 12:00 PM to 7:00 PM 
  • Thursday, November 26 (Thanksgiving): Minimal Traffic Impact Expected 
  • Friday, November 27: 1:00 PM to 7:00 PM 
  • Saturday, November 28: 12:00 PM to 9:00 PM 
  • Sunday, November 29: 12:00 PM to 8:00 PM 
  • Monday, November 30: 2:00 PM to 7:00 PM 

Fewer total travelers are expected over the Thanksgiving 2026 period

When combining all modes of transportation—auto, air, and “other,” which includes bus, boat, and train—AAA says 81.48 million people in America are expected to travel over the 2026 Thanksgiving period.

That’s about 121,000 fewer than the 86.61 million who traveled during the 2025 Thanksgiving period. Still, 2026’s expected numbers far surpass the 77.78 million people who traveled in 2019, the last year before the pandemic.

The expected decline in travelers this year is mainly due to higher travel costs, especially gas prices, AAA says. 

Besides a decline of about 200,000 car travelers, AAA says there will also be a drop in air travelers by about 0.2% to 5.96 million (versus 5.97 million in 2025). 

However, more people will travel by “other” modes during this period than last year. “Other” includes travel by bus, train, and cruise.

In the 2026 Thanksgiving period, the other travel segment is expected to reach 2.6 million travelers—up 4.1% from 2.49 million in 2025.
