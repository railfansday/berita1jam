Man City: Why the international break could be vital for Maresca

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

By Axel Clody | 16 Nov 2026 06:59

Manchester City began the season with the impression that adapting to Enzo Maresca’s work would be quick. With seven wins in the opening Premier League rounds, the team took the lead and at one point opened up a six-point advantage over Arsenal. The run, however, was abruptly interrupted.

Defeats to Aston Villa, Brighton and Nottingham Forest dropped the team to third place and allowed the current champions to pull clear at the top.

More than the scorelines, the performances exposed difficulties in competing physically, winning duels and protecting the defence when possession is lost. Maresca himself acknowledged that his team could not keep up with the intensity of the opposition in the first half.

So the November international break comes at a good time. The pause will not erase the problems that have appeared in recent weeks, nor does it mean that all the players will have days off. But it offers the manager a valuable opportunity to assess performance, recover players and work on adjustments that would hardly be possible with a run of matches.

The drop in City’s level cannot be explained only by a lack of efficiency in front of goal.

The team can still control possession and produce offensive volume. Against Forest, for example, it finished the game with 79 per cent possession and 12 shots. The problem is that this control has not been enough to stop opponents finding space and creating dangerous situations.

One of the main issues is defensive protection. City have conceded 17 goals in eight matches and have not kept a clean sheet in any of their last nine games. The high defensive line, a hallmark of Maresca’s teams, demands coordination between the units and a quick ability to react to losing the ball. When the counter-press does not work, the defenders are left exposed to fast attacks and have to defend large spaces.

The absence of Rodri helps to explain part of this difficulty. The Spaniard offered reading of the game, interceptions and the quality to organise the build-up. More than a defensive midfielder who protects the defence, he is a player capable of anticipating danger and stopping counter-attacks before they gather speed. Without him, the rest of the midfield has to share responsibilities that Rodri used to take on almost alone.

Elliot Anderson has been important in this context, but so too is the distribution of roles. The presence of players such as Enzo Fernandez and Rayan Cherki adds technical quality and creativity, but also demands defensive cover. If the two play together without a structure that protects the central area, Anderson can be overburdened in covering and in physical duels.

The break allows Maresca to work on these relationships. One possibility is to adjust the make-up of the midfield, ensuring more balance between creation and protection. Another is to review the team’s positioning without the ball, especially the distance between the defensive line and the most advanced players. It is not necessarily a question of abandoning the style of play, but of finding ways to make it less vulnerable.

The calendar and the physical condition of the squad are another important aspect. City lost rhythm during a demanding period of fixtures, and the recovery of players can help the team regain the intensity needed to execute Maresca’s model.

It will also be important to monitor the situation of Marc Guehi and Josko Gvardiol. Both were substituted at half-time against Nottingham Forest because of physical problems, according to Maresca. The break could be useful to assess the extent of the issues and avoid important players returning before they are fully fit.

There is, however, a caveat: the international break is not a holiday for the whole squad. Several starters have been called up by their national teams, among them Erling Haaland, Gianluigi Donnarumma, Ruben Dias, Gvardiol, Guehi, Enzo Fernandez and Cherki. Travel and international commitments reduce the recovery time for some players, while others will be able to stay in Manchester and work directly with the coaching staff.

Even so, the break offers an opportunity to reorganise the workload and prepare the squad for what follows. In a season in which intensity is essential to the functioning of Maresca’s system, managing physical wear and tear is also part of the recovery process.

The break arrives at a time when City need to recover not only results, but also confidence in their own way of playing. The seven opening wins showed that the team has the resources to compete for the title. The three defeats that followed, on the other hand, revealed that there are still important adjustments to be made to sustain that level over an entire season.

The first challenge after the break will be against Fulham at the Etihad Stadium. Then the team will face an even more significant fixture: the visit to Arsenal on 29 November. The six-point gap makes the direct meeting an important opportunity to close the distance, although the result will not be enough, by itself, to settle the title race.

For Maresca, the international break is a chance to find answers before the run of negative results turns into an even bigger problem. The manager needs to restore defensive solidity, find the best distribution of roles in midfield and make possession dominance translate into effective control of matches again.

City do not need to abandon their identity to react. Above all, they need to find a better balance between what they do with the ball and what they can do when they lose it. If the break allows Maresca to progress with this work and recover important players, the team could return better prepared to face a decisive stretch of the season.

Stefan Borson reveals exactly how Chelsea's finances can explode

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Chelsea's owners can only significantly boost the club's finances by investing in the redevelopment of Stamford Bridge.

That is according to former Manchester City financial adviser Stefan Borson, who exclusively told Football Insider that increasing the stadium's capacity will be very difficult, however.

Ahead of facing Leeds United after the international break, the Blues have introduced a VVIP experience, allowing fans to get up close and personal.

Supporters, who pay a minimum of £750, will be allowed to lay out their favourite player's kit and boots, go into the media room and player tunnel, as well as wave a matchday flag.

The introduction of this experience is a "very profitable" one, and one that has been greeted with a lot of positivity at Stamford Bridge.

Borson has already warned that it won't completely change Chelsea's finances, though, and that the only way the club can do that is with big changes.

Speaking exclusively to Football Insider, Borson has urged Chelsea's owners to speed up plans surrounding the redevelopment of Stamford Bridge.

"Ultimately, it's not going to move the dial. They've got 38,000 seats. And putting another 10,000, 15,000 seats in that stadium is very difficult," he said.

"But that's the thing that moves the dial, not 15 people paying even £1000 on a matchday for a unique experience. It's worth doing, definitely. I don't think it matters what the number is.

"It just doesn't affect the normal fan. So, people criticise it, but they shouldn't. It's the same as a super premium first-class seat on a plane versus economy.

"It shouldn't bother anybody that there are people who are prepared to pay for first class. They help subsidise the entire place."

Stamford Bridge remains a huge talking point in West London, given that it's the ninth-biggest stadium in the Premier League right now.

Keith Wyness told Football Insider that Chelsea have their "best brains" working on redevelopment plans, but it just isn't that simple.

Issues in the surrounding area makes it difficult to expand the stadium outwards, and moving away completely would be rather controversial.

This is a topic that is likely to continue until Chelsea have new owners in situ, and that could be another few years in the future.

Mercari and Suruga-ya to Establish Joint Venture Mercari Surugaya Inc. in the US

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Joint venture to combine the knowledge and AI usage of Mercari and Suruga-ya for the purchase, procurement, and sale of Entertainment & Hobbies items in the US to bring Japanese pop culture to fans overseas

TOKYO, Nov. 16, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Mercari, Inc. (head office: Minato-ku, Tokyo; Representative Executive Officer and CEO: Shintaro Yamada; "Mercari") announces that on November 16, 2026, it decided to establish a joint venture called Mercari Surugaya Inc. (the "Joint Venture") with SURUGA-YA Inc. (head office: Shizuoka-shi, Shizuoka; CEO: Tsunashige Sugiyama; "Suruga-ya") in the US primarily for the purpose of purchasing, procuring, and selling Entertainment & Hobbies items.

The Joint Venture will combine the strengths of Mercari and Suruga-ya to expand Japanese Entertainment & Hobbies items in the US and drive the spread of Japanese pop culture to fans overseas.

In recent years, interest in anime, manga, games, and other Japanese pop culture has surged across the world, with "Made in Japan" and "Used in Japan" items increasingly growing in popularity.

According to a survey by the Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry[1], the total transaction value of crossborder e-commerce purchases from Japanese businesses by consumers in the US and China increased to approximately 4.4 trillion JPY in 2025, showing that the crossborder e-commerce market is continuing to expand. Furthermore, the overseas market for the Japanese anime industry reached approximately 2.2 trillion JPY in 2024 and surpassed the domestic market for two consecutive years[2], indicating the growing popularity of Japanese pop culture in the global market.

Under its mission to circulate all forms of value to unleash the potential in all people, Mercari has positioned global expansion as a pillar of its mid-term management strategy, and launched its marketplace app in the US in 2014. Mercari also brings Japanese items to consumers overseas through its crossborder transaction business launched in 2019[3] and the Mercari Global App launched in September 2025.[4] The Mercari Global App is currently available in Taiwan, Hong Kong, and the US. The GMV of the crossborder business has expanded approximately 19x over the past four years, reaching 112.2 billion JPY in FY2026.6.[5]

Suruga-ya is one of Japan's leading companies specialized in Entertainment & Hobbies items, possessing a network of 160 retail stores across Japan and overseas (as of October 2026), an extensive inventory of tens of millions of items, catalog data covering more than approximately 30 million products, and accumulated expertise in authenticity verification. Demonstrating its impressive specialization in a wide range of categories, such as figurines, trading cards, games, and anime merchandise, Suruga-ya is popular among fans both within Japan and around the world.

Mercari and Suruga-ya concluded a capital and business partnership agreement in December 2025[6] and have since strengthened their collaboration through initiatives such as selling items from Suruga-ya on Mercari. Now, the two companies are taking their partnership one step further to establish a joint venture for both to operate business on the ground in the US.

Suruga-ya possesses extensive inventory, product catalog data, expertise in appraisal and authenticity verification, and a secondhand goods purchasing network in the Japanese Entertainment & Hobbies domain.

The Joint Venture will combine these strengths of Suruga-ya with Mercari's sales channels in the US and expertise in leveraging AI and creating services that can be easily used by anyone, in order to purchase, procure, and sell Japanese Entertainment & Hobbies items in the US.

(1) Expanding the handling of Japanese items in the US

In addition to purchasing and selling Japanese Entertainment & Hobbies items (online purchases and operation of physical stores) in the US, starting with Los Angeles, the Joint Venture will sell such items through Mercari Group services such as Mercari US and the Mercari Global App in order to bring Japanese pop culture closer to fans in the US.

(2) Accumulating and leveraging product data through purchases and sales

Data on market price and demand of Japanese Entertainment & Hobbies items in the US is something that can only be obtained through actual transactions in the US. The Joint Venture will accumulate product data through purchases and sales in the US, combine it with Suruga-ya's extensive product catalogs and appraisal expertise, and leverage it to enhance the user experience of services such as search, recommendations, and authenticity verification, as well as to strengthen both Mercari's and Suruga-ya's businesses.

(3) Enhancing AI-powered operations and the purchase experience

The Joint Venture will leverage AI in operations related to the purchase of items, product catalogs, inventory, and retail (processes such as appraisal, product registration, and price setting) to increase efficiency. AI will also be utilized to enhance the ease of searching for items and supplement item information.

Bitcoin’s price slide to a mid-July low of $62,000 has crushed the overall crypto market, erasing over $380 billion in value last week and pulling the total market cap down to $2.25 trillion. The sudden drop has pushed the Crypto Fear & Greed Index into “Extreme Fear.”

The sell-off has no single clear cause. Binance co-founder Changpeng Zhao (CZ) pointed to five possible reasons for the crypto market crash.

According to CZ, investors may be pulling money out of crypto to chase the massive Anthropic IPO set for November 18, 2026. Anthropic aims to raise between $57.75 billion and $64.35 billion by offering 825 million shares at $70 to $78 apiece.

However, history suggests mega-IPOs often drain crypto liquidity.

Ahead of the historic SpaceX IPO debut on June 12, 2026, which targeted a $75 billion raise, Bitcoin dropped roughly 19% to 29% in the weeks leading up to the listing, dipping to $58K.CZ himself warned that correlation does not prove causation.

One of the clearest signals CZ pointed to is the wave of institutional ETF outflows. By November 13, U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs had seen $1.9 billion in outflows over seven straight days, including $185.4 million on Friday. BlackRock’s IBIT alone accounted for $1.42 billion, or 74% of the total.

Ethereum ETFs also saw $399 million leave during the week of November 9–13, marking their seventh straight week of outflows.

CZ also pointed to several security incidents that have raised concerns about online safety. These included the $6.3 billion Binance hack on October 29. Although Binance said customer funds were safe, the attack raised concerns about security across the crypto industry.

Security researchers also warned about DarkSword, a hacking tool targeting outdated iPhones and crypto wallets.

Later on 9 November, Google also fixed a Chrome security flaw that hackers were actively using.

A few days later, on 12 November, Outlook and other Microsoft 365 services faced an outage.CZ’s point is not that one of these incidents caused Bitcoin’s fall. Instead, he said,

“These security incidents could make some people less willing to keep funds in crypto.”

CZ also linked the crypto decline to higher U.S. inflation and changing Fed rate expectations. October CPI rose 0.4%, above the 0.3% forecast, while core CPI increased 0.3%. This has raised expectations of another Fed rate hike, possibly by 25 or even 50 basis points.

He also pointed to higher Treasury yields and a stronger dollar, which can make investors less willing to buy risky assets like crypto.

U.S. politics has added to the uncertainty around crypto rules. The Democrats’ victory in the November 3 midterm elections gave them control of both the House and Senate.

The CLARITY Act failed a Senate vote in September. It now has one last chance to pass before the current Congress ends on January 3, 2027.

At last, CZ also rejected speculation that Binance itself was selling crypto during the decline.

He said he had actually bought crypto after receiving stablecoins, joking that his own purchase might have been “the cause.”
