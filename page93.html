The Zelda: Ocarina Of Time Easter Eggs That Did And Did Not Make It Into The 2026 Remake

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time for the Nintendo 64 is a game full of Easter Eggs, secrets, and hidden mechanics (did you know if a butterfly lands on your Deku Stick, it summons a fairy?). Now in its second remake, not every secret has made its way forward through the previous iterations, but many have. Below you will learn about a few of the secrets that made the cut, and others that didn’t.

In the courtyard where Link first meets Zelda, there is arguably the most fourth-wall-breaking Easter Egg in the whole game. If you don’t rush to Zelda and instead pause and enter first-person to look around, you will see portraits of other Nintendo characters through the windows, like Mario and Yoshi. In the 3DS remake, the portraits are replaced by an image of a Mario stage.

For the 2026 remake, there are no references to other Nintendo characters. Instead, the tapestry you build over the course of the game is on display. Removing the reference to Nintendo characters outside of the Zelda universe is understandable, but it is a little odd that art representing the full adventure of the game – an adventure that has not yet concluded – is on the wall.

As stated above, the Nintendo character portraits are gone, but there is still a guard you can annoy. If you fire a Deku Seed into the left window, a guard will pop out, yell at you, and then throw a bomb at you. You can’t pick up the bomb. In fact, at this point in the game, you haven’t even seen a bomb.

The Hyrule Castle portrait references to Yoshi and Mario are gone (though Mario and Luigi are still kind of in the game in a way), but Bowser gets a pass for some reason. Both Malon and her dad, Talon, are still wearing Bowser pins on their clothes. And unlike the N64 game where the detail was a little vague, there is no mistaking it here.

Gossip Stones are all over Ocarina of Time and it takes a while to understand what they do. If you hit them with a sword, they tell you the time, and if you play Zelda’s Lullaby near them, they refill your magic. If you do the Happy Mask Salesman’s side quest, interacting with them while wearing the Mask of Truth offers little hints and details about the world. But then there are interactions that are just odd for no reason. Blowing up a bomb near a Gossip Stone still makes it take off like a rocket ship, and using the Megaton Hammer still flattens them for a moment like a Looney Tunes.

And speaking of playing Zelda’s Lullaby for inanimate objects, if you slash a sign to pieces with your sword and regret your words and deeds, you can play the iconic tune to make the signs reform and become readable again.

This detail was exclusive to the 3DS remake of Ocarina of Time, and surprising no one, it has been removed. Ocarina of Time launched in June 2011 and Nintendo released Skyward Sword about five months later in November. For… marketing purposes, I guess, Skyward Sword art could be found in Lonlon Ranch, the Bombchu Shop in Hyrule Castle Town, and the Gerudo Fortress. It was a fun Easter Egg to discover back in 2011, especially since Skyward Sword was still months away, but these days it’s a bit of an odd inclusion.

Though never explicit, the composer ghosts guarding the Sun’s Song (and Link’s first ReDead encounter) have always been assumed to be inspired by Mario and Luigi. They are brothers; Flat is short, squat, and red; Sharp is lanky and green. They still appear in Ocarina of Time this way, which isn't surprising, since they are important characters you must meet to get a very useful song. Fans have also speculated that Talon and Ingo are Mario and Luigi-inspired for similar reasons, but it’s worth noting that they are not brothers.

In the Spirit Temple there is a large statue of a woman and for reasons that have never been explained, Link could attach his hookshot to her breasts. It served no purpose. It didn’t help solve a puzzle. If anything, it was confusing. But we all tried it and there is no shame in admitting it! That was removed for the 3DS remake, and it remains removed in 2026.

This is a very subtle reference to Wind Waker/Breath of the Wild/Tears of the Kingdom and is potentially the only addition to the remake that looks toward the future of the Zelda franchise. Obviously, plenty of characters in Ocarina later appear in Majora’s Mask (another remake of that is hopefully being considered), but it would be inaccurate to call those intentional Easter Eggs.

However, in Kokiri Forest and in the neighboring Lost Woods, the magical debris floating in the air often resembles a Korok as it would later appear in the series. It makes sense, too, because the Koroks are meant to be what the Kokiri transformed into following The Great Flood.

Agentic AI Is Shipping Code. Who Owns The Risk?

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Andrea Scaduto is co-founder and CEO of SecureFlag, a secure coding training platform.

Not long ago, much of the discussion in software engineering circles was about whether developers should use AI to write code. Now, the focus is on agentic AI and agents that run commands with minimal human direction.

Gartner put agentic AI at the top of its 2026 cybersecurity trends, warning that the technology is creating new attack surfaces as employees and developers adopt it, and that no-code platforms and vibe coding are speeding up the spread of unmanaged agents, unsecured code and potential compliance violations.​​

Before AI agents, development gave organizations a fairly clear path to accountability. A developer makes the change, a reviewer checks it and the service team owns what reaches production.

The chain is now more complicated when an AI agent takes a feature request and turns it into code for a developer to review. For example, a product manager provides the instructions, the agent generates the change and the developer approves it. If that change exposes customer data, who owns the decision to ship it?

The most practical answer, I’d argue, is the person who approves the merge. Agent-written code becomes the organization’s code once a developer approves it, so that approval can’t be a formality. If the person signing off can’t explain what a change does and why it’s safe, it shouldn’t be deployed.

Customers won’t accept “the AI wrote it” as an explanation, and neither will auditors or regulators. The EU’s Cyber Resilience Act makes this clear by placing responsibility on the organizations that put products on the market.​

It’s certainly beneficial that teams can now produce more software faster, but they don’t automatically gain the capacity to assess it all. More output also means more changes to approve, and less time to give each one proper attention.

Research from The Consortium for Information & Software Quality estimated that the cost of poor software quality in the U.S. was approximately $2.41 trillion in 2022, with accumulated technical debt tracked separately at around $1.52 trillion. As teams produce more code, even a low rate of flawed code can create more technical debt than they can keep up with.

Some of that debt is also security debt, as security vulnerabilities found in production can become entry points for attackers and cost more to fix in the long run.​

​Any approach to governing agentic AI has to start with knowing where it is being used. Gartner’s research mentioned above recommends identifying both sanctioned and unsanctioned AI agents, establishing controls for each and preparing incident response plans for agent-related risks.

It’s harder than it sounds, as agents work in integrated development environment extensions, command-line tools, CI/CD pipelines and business-user platforms. Calling them all “AI coding assistants” can obscure how differently they operate. An agent that proposes a change for review has a very different level of access and autonomy from one that can modify a repository or deploy code. These differences should determine how each agent is governed.

For each agent, security and engineering leaders should know who uses it and what it is allowed to do. ​

Agents should have only the permissions they need, with human approval reserved for higher-risk actions, such as merging changes to protected branches or modifying authentication and sensitive data.

Routine changes can use the same security checks as human-authored code, with agent-generated changes clearly identified so teams can see where they came from. Some of these checks have limits, though. In my experience, scanners are effective at finding known patterns but much less reliable when a vulnerability depends on business logic or application context.

As co-founder and director of SecureFlag, a company that provides hands-on developer security training, I’m particularly interested in how developers build the skills to work securely with AI agents and review the code they produce. This focus informs my views on agentic AI, though I recognize that training is only one part of governing how these agents are used.​​​

It’s developers who need to find the issues scanners miss, but general awareness training doesn’t necessarily equip them to do so. Instead, Gartner recommends adaptive programs that include AI-specific tasks, and I’ve found hands-on practice in real development environments can help to build the skills to do this.​​

​Organizations can only take responsibility for agents if they know whether the controls around them are working and developers have the skills they need.

The most useful measures come from the code itself, such as comparing vulnerability rates in agent-authored and human-authored changes, and tracking that difference to see if risk goes down. Remediation time and recurring vulnerability classes can also show where problems continue to occur.

It’s also worth tracking how often teams catch problems in agent-authored changes before merge. A rising catch rate could mean controls are working better or agents are producing more problematic code, and it’s important to know which. These measures give a better view of how the organization is managing the risks of agentic development.​

Securing agentic development has two sides. The agent itself needs to be secured, with limits on what it can access and change. The software it produces also needs to be secure, which depends on someone understanding what the agent was asked to do and whether the result matches that intent.

Developers are at the center of both. In many organizations today, agents operate with some of the same access as the developer using them, meaning developers often influence what agents can access without making a deliberate choice. They are also accountable for the changes they approve.

Leadership is responsible for the conditions around that work, including setting boundaries for what agents can access and making sure developers have the skills to configure agents securely and review what they produce. The organization carries the risk either way.

As agents take on more of the development process, organizations need to define where human responsibility lies. Waiting for an incident to establish those boundaries is a costly way to find out.​

New Food Pyramid Puts Healthy Carbs and Plant-Based Foods First

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Follow Healthline on our socials for all things health.

A first-of-its-kind international food pyramid, developed by 22 leading nutritionists from Europe, Canada, the United States, and Australia, was published in Advances in Nutrition in September.

This new food pyramid emphasizes plant-based protein sources, cooking with plant-based oils, and healthier sugars and carbohydrates.

It also echoes the traditional food pyramid used in the United States since 1992.

The new pyramid breaks away from the Trump administration’s updated nutritional guidelines, which are part of Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr.’s Make America Health Again (MAHA) agenda and prioritize red meat over plants.

The new pyramid was designed by a research team led by Brigham Young University professor Karen Della Corte. The researchers noted that this is the first international food pyramid that is designed to reflect scientific agreement across multiple countries and cultures.

They also analyzed evidence from more than 250 scientific publications, while drawing on the EAT-Lancet Planetary Health Diet to identify principles of healthy eating that may be shared.

While the pyramid emphasizes plant-based proteins, it doesn’t exclude animal proteins.

“I find the new international food pyramid a more practical and aligned approach for the prevention of chronic disease,” said Michelle Routhenstein, Preventive Cardiology Dietitian at Entirely Nourished, who wasn’t involved in the creation of the food pyramid.

“The new international food pyramid places greater emphasis on food quality and the importance of building meals around nutrient-dense plant foods, while still allowing room for lean animal proteins such as fish, poultry, eggs, and yogurt,” she added.

While the new pyramid and the current U.S. dietary guidelines differ in many ways, they also agree on certain factors.

For example, both sets of guidelines discourage added sugars, refined carbs, and highly processed snacks.

The differences between the two mainly lie in their emphasis on specific sources of protein and fat. For example, the new pyramid prioritizes plant-based proteins, while the U.S. guidelines place greater emphasis on animal proteins, including red meat.

While both sets of recommendations advise limiting saturated fat, the new pyramid emphasizes unsaturated plant oils, whereas the U.S. guidelines also include butter and beef tallow as cooking fat options.

Routhenstein noted that an important distinction is that this new pyramid goes beyond simply telling people to eat more protein or fewer carbohydrates. Instead, it encourages people to consider the quality and sources of those nutrients.

However, the main emphasis of both guidelines is encouraging people to eat fewer ultra-processed foods and more natural or “real” foods.

Routhenstein told Healthline that research consistently shows that plant-forward eating patterns, including the Mediterranean and DASH diets, are associated with better cardiovascular health and a lower risk of heart disease.

Plant foods provide fiber, potassium, antioxidants, and other beneficial compounds that can help support healthy cholesterol levels, blood pressure, blood sugar, and blood vessel function.

“This is not to say that protein and fat are unimportant, but their sources matter,” Routhenstein said.

Roberta Metsola warns MEPs that AI cannot replace debate in EU lawmaking

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Parliament is rewriting its AI rules after a rise in AI-written amendments, some citing laws that do not exist.

European Parliament President Roberta Metsola

European Parliament President Roberta Metsola has warned MEPs against relying on AI to write laws, telling them that responsibility for every decision stays with elected members.

She spoke on Wednesday at an event in the Parliament on how AI is used in its work, Politico Europe reported.

According to Politico, Metsola said that drafting an amendment with AI cannot replace a real debate with colleagues. In the same way, she added, legal research done with AI cannot replace the judgement that hard decisions need.

She also said that people should be told when AI has been used, because voters must be able to hold lawmakers to account.

Her warning comes as officials worry about a sharp rise in amendments written with AI, and some of them refer to laws that do not exist.

Alessandro Chiocchetti, the Parliament’s secretary-general, gave one example at the event. An MEP had received wrong legal advice from an AI tool, and the Parliament’s lawyers then needed two days to prove it was wrong.

For that reason, Chiocchetti said, drafting and reviewing laws must remain the job of MEPs, even though AI can help staff sort through thousands of amendments. He added that the administration will soon present new guidelines to the Parliament’s leadership.

The new rules will rest on three ideas: be open about using AI, never put sensitive files into a public tool, and check all work before it goes out.

For now, MEPs do not have to say when AI helped them write an amendment, a question or a speech. Staff, by contrast, must already label such work under rules set in 2024.

To make the new rules binding on MEPs, however, the Parliament may need to change its own rules of procedure. That change would go through its constitutional affairs committee.

Meanwhile, the Parliament is building its own AI platform, the EPGenAI Hub. It runs on the Parliament’s own systems and is meant for sensitive work. Commercial tools such as ChatGPT, Anthropic’s Claude and Meta’s Llama will stay available for less sensitive tasks.

The Parliament has already limited AI on its own devices, as earlier this year it switched off built-in AI features on MEPs’ tablets over data protection concerns.

Our lives are defined not by what we have but what we give — on what and whom we spend our energy and time, how we share our material possessions and our mental powers, what we do with our gifts. “Anything you do not give freely and abundantly becomes lost to you,” Annie Dillard wrote in contemplating the nature of creativity. “You open your safe and find ashes.”

Paradoxically, the ability to give hinges on the ability to receive: You must be rested in order to labor, taking the repose it takes to recharge your vital energies; you must be interested in order to be interesting, taking the world in with a greedy curiosity that absorbs its beauty and strangeness in order to metabolize it into meaning.

C.S. Lewis (November 29, 1898–November 22, 1963) offers a wonderfully counterintuitive perspective on our notions of generosity and our ideas about what it means to be interesting in his memoir Surprised by Joy (public library).

With an eye to the ideal structure of the creative life, he writes:

It is a life almost entirely selfish. Selfish, not self-centred: for in such a life my mind would be directed towards a thousand things, not one of which is myself. The distinction is not unimportant. One of the happiest men and most pleasing companions I have ever known was intensely selfish. On the other hand I have known people capable of real sacrifice whose lives were nevertheless a misery to themselves and to others, because self-concern and self-pity filled all their thoughts. Either condition will destroy the soul in the end. But till the end, give me the man who takes the best of everything (even at my expense) and then talks of other things, rather than the man who serves me and talks of himself, and whose very kindnesses are a continual reproach, a continual demand for pity, gratitude, and admiration.

This is also why, Lewis intimates, the “thrill” of our greatest creative breakthroughs — those revelations that give us back to ourselves, out of which we give our best work to the world — is rooted in “self-forgetfulness”:

Only when your whole attention and desire are fixed on something else — whether a distant mountain, or the past, or the gods of Asgard — does the “thrill” arise.

Shut your mouth; open your eyes and ears. Take in what is there.

Couple with John Steinbeck on the art of receiving, then revisit C.S. Lewis on what we long for in our existential longing.

Cristiano Ronaldo has retired from international duty with Portugal.

The country’s greatest-ever player announced his decision via a video to camera on his social media channels.

Ronaldo netted 146 goals in 234 games for Portugal, becoming the all-time leading scorer in the history of men’s international football.

The striker was suspended by the Portuguese FA last month after he stormed out of the Nations League camp in October, having fallen out with manager Jorge Jesus.

Chelsea are interested in signing Everton star Harrison Armstrong, reports claim.

The England Under-21 international has established a reputation as one of the Premier League’s most impressive young midfielders under David Moyes.

TEAMtalk cite reports claiming that Chelsea are compiling weekly scouting reports on the 19-year-old, who continues to go from strength to strength at the Hill Dickinson Stadium.

Chelsea are widely expected to swoop for another top young midfielder in January in Bournemouth’s Alex Scott, 23.

Harry Kane’s bumper new Bayern Munich contract has now been confirmed.

The Ballon d’Or winner has been in talks with the reigning Bundesliga champions for months now regarding a lucrative extension to his previous deal that was due to expire next summer.

Kane is now tied to Bayern until 2029 on a record-breaking contract that is said to make him the highest-paid player in Europe by salary.

The England captain will be 36 by the time it expires, meaning he may well now never return to the Premier League and looks destined to stay second on the list of all-time top scorers behind Alan Shearer.

Manchester United looks set to make a sensational loan swoop for Ivan Toney in January, reports claim.

The struggling Red Devils are desperate for more firepower under Michael Carrick with first-choice striker Benjamin Sesko out until January with his lingering shin injury.

They are more likely to make a loan move for the former Brentford frontman than a permanent offer, however.

Under-21s captain James Rowswell is another player who will be potentially looking to leave Spurs on loan in the New Year, having failed to secure a summer switch.

Celta Vigo defender Javi Rodriguez is attracting interest from a number of clubs across Europe, writes Sam Tabuteau.

Despite reports, though, there has not yet been any contact from Fulham for the 23-year-old.

Rodriguez made his Spain debut in June and is expected to attract interest in January.

Real Madrid are confident of winning the race to sign Manchester United teenager JJ Gabriel, who has not returned to Old Trafford since leaving the club in September.

The 16-year-old is currently training independently in Spain after deciding to pursue a pathway towards senior football elsewhere.

Barcelona and Paris Saint-Germain have also shown interest, but Jose Mourinho’s Real Madrid are considered the leading contenders, according to BBC Sport.

Gabriel has impressed at youth level, scoring a hat-trick on his UEFA Youth League debut, and has obtained an EU passport that could ease a move abroad.

United would receive compensation if he leaves, although the fee would be lower for an overseas transfer.
