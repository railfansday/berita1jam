LinkedIn Leads Brand Engagement at 5.8% as Engagement Falls on YouTube, TikTok and Facebook, Vista Social Study Finds

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

NEW YORK, Nov. 16, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Vista Social today released its Social Media Benchmarks by Industry, 2027 Report, revealing a significant downturn in organic engagement across nearly all major social networks. The study, which tracked more than 54,000 brand profiles, found that median engagement rates fell year-over-year even as posting frequencies remained steady.

The complete 2027 report and industry-specific data are available at https://vistasocial.com/research/social-media-benchmarks/.

The findings indicate that reach is becoming increasingly difficult to secure for corporate accounts. Despite the decline in audience interaction, the study noted that brands on Instagram maintained a median of 4.3 posts per week in both 2025 and 2026, suggesting that many organizations are struggling to adapt their content strategies to shifting algorithm priorities.

The report also identified a retention crisis on X, where 42% of brand profiles lost followers between August and October 2026. This compares to one in five profiles on Facebook and approximately one in 100 on LinkedIn.

"Most benchmarks people quote come from a few hundred big brands, or from surveys of what marketers say they do," said Vitaly Veksler, CEO of Vista Social. "This report shows what tens of thousands of brands actually published and how their audiences responded. Reach is getting harder on most networks, and the brands doing best are not the ones with the biggest audiences or the most posts."

The data was anonymized and aggregated from profiles connected to the Vista Social platform via official network APIs. To ensure statistical integrity, no segment was published unless it included at least 50 profiles from at least 10 distinct accounts.

The Wide-Leg Jeans That Every French Girl (And Kate Moss) Owns

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Last month, while taking in the spring/summer 2027 shows at Paris Fashion Week, I was eager to write about the street style trends bubbling up around the showgoers in town. Which proved harder than anticipated. For one thing, the flashiest dressers had been given full looks by the brands whose shows they were being paid to attend, and reporting on those felt like a somewhat dishonest exercise in journalism. For another, the consensus seems to be that we are living in a post-trend world, and fashion people in particular have little interest in dressing like anyone else. And so I turned my attention to real-life Parisians, among whom one item seemed to proliferate: jeans.

No, this isn’t going to be a trend report on something as perennially popular as denim, but on a particular high-waisted, flared style that I spotted so frequently, I was forced to dredge up what little French remains from my GCSEs to ask a woman where hers were from as I caught her climbing onto a Lime bike outside Le Saint-Gervais bar. “Excuse-moi, madame, votre pantalons… le brand?” She paused for a moment, swung her leg over the saddle, and unleashed a torrent of French from which I managed to decipher just two words: “Anna” and “Gérard Darel”, the latter needing no explanation.

Founded in Saint-Germain-des-Prés in the 1970s, the brand has long been a shorthand for what the rest of the world considers the tenets of French-girl style: discreet, easy-to-wear classics with a cultivated air. (So cultivated, in fact, that the brand acquired the original handwritten Proust Questionnaire in 2003, and recently collaborated with the philosopher Alain de Botton on a card-game version.) Which is all very Left Bank, of course, but perhaps the clearest evidence of its credentials is just how commonplace Gérard Darel is in everyday Parisian life. You can scarcely move for its iconic 24H bag—a soft, logo-free shoulder bag that Vogue once named among the bags of the century, alongside the Hermès Birkin – during the school rush on the Métro, where it is generally to be found stuffed with binders and tangled wired headphones.

But back to the jeans–an American invention, admittedly, but then again, this is the land of French fries. What made my startled Parisienne’s look so good? Much of it comes down to the cut–something Kate Moss, a woman who found fame in nothing more than a pair of jeans, understands, having been photographed in the brand’s Anna jeans as early as 2019. Designed by former creative director Valérie Gerbi and named after her daughter Anna – granddaughter of the brand’s founders, Danièle and Gérard Darel–the design braces at just the right point on the hips, fits snugly through the thighs and flares outwards just below the knee, skimming the top of a pair of shoes to create the illusion of longer legs. The double belt loops, meanwhile, criss-cross along the waistband to give the impression of a cinched waist, while the back pockets perform a similarly flattering service for the bum.

And yet the technicalities of a good fit can only explain so much. Perhaps the true appeal was in the way she wore them–with white ballet flats that I’m convinced were from Repetto’s men’s section (the Luciens) and a navy three-quarter-zip jumper with the sleeves pushed up–or the ease with which disappeared into the Marais, hair whipping in the wind.

We try to stay away from listing core players in this section. Even Purdy's biggest naysayers can't use this performance or "it's the Cowboys' defense" against him.

Surprisingly, McCaffrey had a rushing success rate of only 23.1%. But he made several impact plays on those carries, gained 35 yards after contact, forced two missed tackles, and had two 10+ yard runs, including a 16-yard touchdown.

CMC is here because of his work through the air. He had a third-down target where he beat Caleb Downs for a much-needed first down. McCaffrey came through in the clutch when the offense needed a big play.

McCaffrey became the first running back in NFL history with multiple 65+ yard touchdown receptions in the same game. He had the most receiving yards by a running back since Jamaal Charles's 195 yards in Week 15 of 2013.

Black forced three missed tackles — two of them on one play. He had 53 rush yards, 44 of which came after contact. Again, the success rate was below average (27.3%), but the big plays more than make up for it. Black contributed 13 yards through the air, but he's here for running over a first-down and finishing in the end zone.

Deebo had a 70-yard touchdown on a play where Cowboys defenders didn't figure out he had the ball until he was 20 yards downfield. Samuel had two other carries for four yards. I'd argue that Allen helped get the offense off the mat. The player who wasn't on the roster this time last week had the second-most targets. Allen caught five passes for 61 yards. He had a 25% target share — which is Mike Evans territory.

We expected Allen to be impactful underneath, but he showed that he can create separation at the intermediate level of the field, both on in- and out-breaking routes.

Watkins kicked things off with a 22-yard reception. He had a 27-yarder where Purdy bought time, waiting for someone to get open, while Watkins picked himself up off the ground and made himself available. It was only two catches, but Watkins has proven to be a big-play threat.

Watkins had a couple of blocks that helped spring plays for the offense. He's developing nicely and rounding into a complete player.

Kittle caught three of his four contested targets, something he struggled with over the past couple of games. He had 46 yards after the catch. It wasn't a vintage Kittle day, but if 74 yards feels like an off day, you know you're dealing with a star.

Of the 115 offensive linemen to play at least 50 snaps in Week 10, Colby ranked 112th with a 17.1% pressure rate allowed. He had six pressures. Usually, Colby makes up for it in the run game. Bring on the robots. It's time. Malik Mustapha had a picture-perfect hit. The refs huddled up, talked it over, and still decided Mustapha made illegal contact. Why couldn't replay assist chime in?

U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent Just Issued a Blistering Warning to Nvidia

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

During an interview with Fox News over the weekend, Bessent did not hold back about his concerns on the unfolding scandal at Nvidia.

The market seems to have mixed feelings about the departure of former Nvidia (NVDA -3.40%) CEO Jensen Huang.

An independent online petition requesting that Huang be reinstated now has over 180,000 signatures, including those from prominent business leaders like Elon Musk. President Donald Trump has also reportedly said he wouldn't mind if Huang returned as CEO.

During an interview with Fox News over the weekend, U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent expressed significant concerns about the situation and called for a "dramatic shakeup" at Nvidia. Make no mistake, Bessent just issued a blistering warning to the artificial intelligence (AI) chip giant.

Huang's resignation came after media outlets reported that the Nvidia co-founder had a relationship with a woman named Alice Huang (no relation), who previously was a controlling shareholder in a Singaporean data center company called MegaSpeed.

Other media outlets, such as The New York Times, reported that Huang also had alleged extramarital affairs with Nvidia employees. But the relationship with Alice raises national security concerns.

The Information reported that Alice created a network that illegally diverted $7.5 billion worth of Nvidia chips to China. The U.S. currently has export restrictions on the sale of certain Nvidia chips to China. Nvidia has claimed that it has had virtually zero Chinese chip sales since April 2025.

"I want to keep coming back to what was the culture at Nvidia, and were they pressing these seemingly illicit sales to goose their revenues and their earnings?" Bessent said during the Fox interview. "If this were a bank and they hadn't complied like this, the OCC (Office of the Comptroller of the Currency) would be in there right now, and a lot of the executives would be removed."

Bessent didn't hold back his views on the scandal, either, saying it was clear that some companies buying chips from Nvidia were shell companies working to advance AI interests in China.

Additionally, Bessent clearly does not want to see Huang return as CEO and believes Nvidia needs to get its ducks in a row quickly.

"This makes me think that as the board conducts the search for a new leader, that they need to go outside the company," he said. "I think we need answers in the next 10 days from the board and from the outside counsel."

Bessent has been a close confidant of Trump during his second term. The question is, how much weight does Bessent's warning carry within the administration?

Not only did Trump recently say he wouldn't mind seeing Huang return to Nvidia, but Bessent's questions also suggest divisions between Bessent and Howard Lutnick, another close confidant of Trump and the Secretary of the U.S. Commerce Department.

Bessent told CNBC that some federal agencies wanted to put MegaSpeed on an export blacklist. "But Commerce has the pen," Bessent said.

A White House spokesperson told Reuters last week that Trump has "complete confidence" in Lutnick. U.S. Attorney General Todd Blanche said on Nov. 12 that there is no confirmation yet that Alice was in fact a Chinese spy.

But questions remain about the extent to which Nvidia's chip sales, particularly in the Asia-Pacific region, have been diverted to China, if at all.

As Treasury Secretary, Bessent doesn't have direct authority to punish Nvidia, although historically he has been considered part of Trump's inner circle and has largely been in lockstep with the president. But with Trump seemingly supporting Huang, it's hard to know how much sway Bessent's words carry.

Still, it's clear that Bessent just issued a blistering warning to Nvidia, and I doubt he is alone among officials in the administration with concerns, so Bessent's warning shouldn't fall on deaf ears.
