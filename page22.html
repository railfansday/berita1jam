7-year-old Pokemon fan furious that Magikarp got an SIR card before Gyarados

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

A seven-year-old Pokemon fan has gone viral after demanding that The Pokemon Company give Gyarados the Special Illustration Rare card he believes the classic Water-type deserves.

Gyarados first appeared in the physical Pokemon Trading Card Game’s Base Set and has since received dozens of printings, including ex, GX, V, VMAX and Shining versions. However, collectors still have no physical Gyarados Special Illustration Rare, the full-art rarity marked by two gold stars in the modern era.

The complaint is especially focused on Magikarp’s Illustration Rare from Paldea Evolved. The Shinji Kanda card became one of the set’s most talked-about chase cards because its artwork hides a Gyarados in the background, making the contrast with its evolution’s lack of a SIR even more noticeable.

In the 53-second TikTok, the child asks, “Can somebody explain how Gyarados still doesn’t have a special edition Pokemon card?”

He points out that Gyarados has been part of the Pokemon Trading Card Game since the original Base Set and says the Pokemon has “loads of different cards over the years.” He then calls Gyarados “one of the coolest Pokemon ever” before turning his attention to Magikarp.

“Don’t get me wrong, I love that Magikarp card,” he says, arguing that it makes no sense for Magikarp to have such a striking Illustration Rare while Gyarados still lacks a Special Illustration Rare. He also pitches the kind of artwork he would like to see, imagining Gyarados bursting out of the ocean in a dramatic scene.

“Come on Pokemon, give Gyarados an SIR!” he concludes, before asking viewers which Pokemon should receive one next.

The clip was reposted by Guru Collects on X, where it was described as a child finally having “ENOUGH of the Gyarados disrespect.” The repost helped push the complaint beyond the original TikTok, with collectors joining the conversation over which overlooked Pokemon deserve premium artwork.

Pokemon TCG Pocket does include a Gyarados Illustration Rare, but that digital card is separate from the physical Pokemon TCG releases being discussed in the video. The Pokemon Company has not announced a physical Gyarados SIR, so the seven-year-old’s request remains a fan campaign for now.

US air traffic control is finally phasing out floppy disks after decades of use

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) has announced plans to phase out floppy disks at hundreds of US air traffic control facilities, replacing technology that has been in use for decades.

The outdated storage devices are still used to handle weather data and other critical information at more than 400 facilities across the country, despite floppy disks largely disappearing from everyday use more than 25 years ago.

The announcement comes as the FAA works to modernize the country’s aging air traffic control infrastructure, which continues to rely on technology dating back to the 1980s.

According to Reuters, the FAA plans to replace its floppy disk-based technology by the end of 2028 through the introduction of a new Enterprise Information Display System.

The system will replace four older platforms dating back to the 1980s, bringing important operational information together in one place so air traffic controllers can access updates more quickly.

“We’re moving away from outdated, manual processes and replacing technology that once relied on floppy disks with a modern system that allows controllers to instantly share critical flight alerts, restricted airspace schedules and pilot reports,” FAA Administrator Bryan Bedford said in a statement.

Floppy disks were once a standard method of storing and transferring computer data, but their use declined significantly in the early 2000s. Large-scale production of the storage devices largely ended in 2011.

The FAA’s modernization efforts extend beyond floppy disks. According to Reuters, the agency still relies on decades-old Compaq computers and has even purchased replacement parts through eBay to maintain its existing infrastructure.

Many air traffic control facilities also operate on separate analog computer systems that cannot communicate directly with one another.

“Every facility is a silo,” Bedford told Reuters in a recent interview.

The upgrades come amid ongoing concerns about the reliability of the country’s air traffic control infrastructure.

In September, thousands of flights across the Northeast were delayed or canceled following equipment failures at a Philadelphia air traffic facility and the accidental cutting of a fiber optic cable in New Jersey.

A separate incident on October 13 saw a damaged fiber optic cable disrupt data services at Houston’s George Bush Intercontinental Airport, resulting in a temporary ground stop.

Congress approved $12.5 billion in 2025 to help modernize the aging system, while Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy has requested another $30 billion for additional improvements.

The FAA is also working to eliminate paper flight strips, which controllers have traditionally used to track aircraft movements. That transition began in 2022, with 27 major airports having already moved away from the practice.

However, completing the transition across all 49 major airports included in the program is expected to take until at least 2028, the same year the FAA plans to finish replacing its floppy disk-based systems.

A researcher found over 100,000 words and phrases in “every major language” that are blocked from autocomplete and swipe-type in iOS, Reuters reports. The researcher, Derik Kauffman, says you can still type the words on the blocklist one letter at a time. But they won’t pop up as predictive text suggestions and iOS’s keyboard may automatically alter them when swipe-typing, like changing “left wing conspiracy” to “left wrong conspiracy.”

Kauffman posted the full list online, including the individual lists for different languages.

Most of the words and phrases on the list are offensive, slurs, or related to topics like sex and drugs. Where To Upgrade Weapons In Phantom Blade Zero

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Phantom Blade Zero doesn't follow the RPG trend of having weapons in different tiers that you replace with newer ones. Instead, it lets you upgrade the weapons you're using to match the difficulty of the locations you're in. With this system, you can keep using the same weapon throughout the game if you'd like and just keep upgrading it to stay effective.

However, to upgrade these weapons, you'll need resources specific to the weapon type, similar to how you choose your skills. This means you can upgrade different weapon types, but because each type has finite resources, you need to choose which weapons go best together.

To upgrade your weapons in Phantom Blade Zero, you'll need to use the bells you use to upgrade abilities and fast travel around the map. At the bell, select Upgrade Equipment to open your inventory and choose what you want to improve. Hold the prompted button (X on PS5) to upgrade the weapon you choose.

From this menu, you can choose from your Main Weapons, Phantom Edges and Accessories, and they all get upgraded similarly. Choose the item you want to upgrade, and it will show you what resources you need. Although the resources differ, they all require Remnant Dust, just like upgrading your abilities.

For regular weapons, there are three different weapon types to choose from: One-Handed, Two-Handed and Dual-Wield. To upgrade these, you'll need Forged Metal, Twinned Metal and Broadplate Metal, respectively. You'll also need Solvent, which applies to any weapon you're upgrading.

However, as you level your weapons higher, you'll need to improve the materials, too. For example, for one-handed weapons, you can use Forged Metal to upgrade up to level 10, but you need Polished Forged Metal to upgrade from level 10 to level 20. Furthermore, you'll need Flawless Forged Metal to upgrade beyond level 20 to max level.

The same applies to the solvent you use to upgrade weapons. Solvent is used for upgrading weapons up to level 10, Concentrated Solvent gets you from level 10 to level 20 and Universal Solvent gets you the rest of the way to max level.

The Bashpole gives you one extra way to find hidden treasure.

Upgrading Phantom Edges functions the same as upgrading weapons, but requires different materials. Just like how weapons have three different types - One-Handed, Two-Handed and Dual-Wield - there are five different types of Phantom Edges: Martial, Destruction, Artifice, Ranged, and Summon.

Reverting weapons is one of the game's best features because it lets you freely try different weapons as you play. If you revert an upgraded weapon, it returns to level 1 and refunds all resources used to upgrade it. This means you aren't penalized for experimenting with different weapons.

However, this does get more complicated as you upgrade weapons to higher levels. For levels 1-10, reverting weapons is free, but for higher levels, you have to use a Reforge Talisman. Reforge Talismans are available for both weapons and Phantom Edges, and they are not refunded after you revert levels.

This means you need to be more thoughtful about which weapons you upgrade and revert once you reach higher levels. Luckily, by the end of the game, you'll already know which weapons work best for your play style, so you won't need to revert as much.

Upgrading Accessories is a similar process to upgrading weapons and Phantom Edges. Go to a bell and select Upgrade Equipment, then go to the Accessories tab at the top of the menu.

To upgrade Accessories, you need only one resource along with your Remnant Dust: Tainted Crystal Shards. You can find these around the world like the rest of the in-game resources, but you can not revert accessories like you can with weapons and Phantom Edges.

Once you use them, you can't get Tainted Crystal Shards back.

Soul Tempering is the highest level of upgrading that you can do in Phantom Blade Zero. To do so, you need to find materials throughout the game that are specific to each weapon or Phantom Edge. You unlock Soul Tempering only after you've spoken to Myst at the Order's tavern in Pangzhen.

To Soul Temper a weapon or Phantom Edge, you have to be in the upgrade menu. At the bottom left, a prompt will appear to open the Soul Tempering menu. When you press it, it pulls up the list of weapons or Phantom Edges you have and lets you Soul Temper them if you have the right resources.

You can find Soul Temper materials in various places, such as chests or by defeating bosses. You only need one of each material to Soul Temper a given item, and you can't revert the upgrade or do it more than once.

Earthquake Faults Can Suddenly Become Almost As Fluid as Water, Study Finds

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Scientists have uncovered evidence of an unexpected process that may allow earthquake faults to slip enormous distances.

Rocks along Japan’s Boso Peninsula have preserved evidence of an extraordinary process that may occur during powerful earthquakes. Researchers discovered ancient fault structures suggesting that clay trapped between moving rock surfaces became nearly as fluid as water, potentially allowing the fault to slip enormous distances with little resistance.

The findings, from a research team led by Professor Tetsuro Hirono of Osaka Metropolitan University, could help explain how some earthquakes produce the massive seafloor movements capable of triggering devastating tsunamis.

The researchers examined exposed rocks that formed in an ancient seafloor environment approximately 15 million years ago. Within an old earthquake fault, they identified unusual wavy and curved layers, along with swirling structures resembling vortices formed by moving fluids.

The team described these formations as “turbulent-flow-like.” Using fluid dynamics to analyze their shapes, the scientists found evidence that a process called thermal pressurization had caused clay within the fault to become fluidized.

Thermal pressurization occurs when rapid movement along a fault generates intense frictional heat. As water trapped in microscopic spaces within the rock heats up, pressure increases, reducing the frictional resistance that normally keeps the fault surfaces locked together. This weakening can allow much greater movement during an earthquake.

“Our analysis suggests that the viscosity approached that of water,” Professor Hirono explained. “This would have been a dramatic reduction. The fluidized state would have acted as a form of dynamic lubrication, causing the fault to weaken dramatically. The resulting large-scale slip would have produced a large displacement on the fault near the seafloor, potentially causing a tsunami.”

The mechanism could help explain the extreme seafloor displacement associated with some major earthquakes. In 2011, a magnitude 9.1 earthquake struck eastern Japan, with shaking and fault rupture lasting nearly six minutes. The earthquake shifted the ocean floor enough to generate a devastating tsunami. Although the ancient rocks do not establish whether the same weakening process occurred in 2011, they provide physical evidence of how faults may undergo dramatic changes during seismic events.

The researchers hope to apply their analytical methods to modern faults, using changes in the viscosity of fault materials to better estimate how far those faults might slip during an earthquake. More accurate assessments of fault movement could improve estimates of potential seafloor displacement and associated tsunami hazards.

“Understanding the change in viscosity in fault-slips may allow us to more accurately recognize the magnitude of fault slips, which would support tsunami mitigation efforts,” Professor Hirono concluded.

Reference: “Coseismic turbulence-like flow of fault material along the shallow portion of a plate-subduction-related fault” by Tatsuru Fukuta and Tetsuro Hirono, 27 June 2026, Scientific Reports. DOI: 10.1038/s41598-026-57630-y

An herbal extract may help people cut back on binge drinking

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Researchers tested an herbal approach to cutting back on alcohol, with some benefits persisting three months after treatment ended.

Prescription medicines can help treat alcohol use disorder, but they remain rarely used.

A new clinical trial tested another approach: kudzu root extract taken only on days when people expected to drink heavily.

In the trial, adults who used kudzu that way had fewer binge-drinking days than adults who took a placebo, a look-alike capsule with nothing active in it.

The difference was modest, and three months after treatment ended, it was still there.

Glenn-Milo Santos, a professor of community health systems at the University of California, San Francisco, led the work.

His team enrolled 120 adults with alcohol use disorder, the medical term for drinking that a person struggles to control.

Every volunteer also got a short counseling session each week, so kudzu was tested on top of that support.

“Binge drinking is incredibly common, but we don’t have many tools to offer people who want to cut back,” said Santos.

“Our goal is to expand that toolkit. What’s exciting about this approach is that people can use it when they know they may be at risk of heavy drinking, rather than taking a medication every day.”

For men, a binge means five or more drinks on one occasion. For women, it means four or more.

In a 2022 federal health survey, 17% of U.S. adults said they had binged in the past month, and those who did averaged about 4.6 binges a month.

Binge drinking accounts for more than half of the roughly 80,000 U.S. deaths each year blamed on excessive drinking.

Treatment hasn’t kept up with that toll. Four medicines are approved in the U.S. for alcohol use disorder, yet fewer than 10% of people in the U.S. with the disorder have ever received one.

Worry about side effects keeps some patients and the people treating them away from those drugs, the authors wrote.

Herbal supplements are sold over the counter and are often seen as having fewer side effects, which is part of why the team chose an herbal option.

Kudzu has been used as medicine in China and Japan since the 6th century.

Compounds from its root lowered drinking in animal studies. In one small human study, 14 heavy drinkers who took kudzu extract for a week drank fewer beers in a lab session.

The trial ran from 2019 to 2024 at the San Francisco Department of Public Health. The team recruited volunteers in sexual health clinics, needle exchanges, bars, and on social media.

Everyone had to have had sex while drinking in the previous three months, and 16% were living with HIV.

People with another severe substance use disorder, most often involving cocaine or methamphetamine, were excluded from the trial.

Half the volunteers got kudzu extract capsules, and half got identical placebo capsules.

Neither the volunteers nor the staff knew who received which capsules. Participants’ guesses at the end of the trial suggested that the blinding had worked.

Each person was told to take a dose at least 2.5 hours before a session where they expected to drink heavily.

That lead time came from a 2015 study of 20 adults, in which one dose taken that far ahead cut drinking in a lab session.

The pill bottles had caps that logged every opening. On average, people took about 2.7 doses a week, and they took one on about three in four of the days they expected to binge.

Treatment lasted 12 weeks, and the counseling focused on cutting back gradually rather than quitting outright.

At the start, both groups averaged 2.15 binge days a week. By the final treatment visit, the kudzu group was down to 1.42, while the placebo group was at 2.06.

Across all 12 weeks, the researchers estimated that people taking kudzu had about 27% fewer binge days than people taking the placebo.

The kudzu group also reported more weeks without any binge drinking.

In this group, the share of weeks that included at least one binge fell from 88% to 58%. In the placebo group, it went from 88% to 81%.

Side effects were about as common in one group as in the other. Nausea was reported by seven kudzu users and two placebo users, a difference that could be due to chance.

One kudzu user developed heart inflammation 82 days after their final treatment visit, and the team judged it unrelated to the supplement.

Kudzu didn’t clearly lower the total number of drinks people had in a month. It also didn’t change how often urine tests came back positive for alcohol.

The urine tests pick up drinking from the past one to three days.

About 60% of the kudzu group’s samples were positive at the start of the trial, and about 60% were positive at the end.

People on kudzu didn’t stop drinking, but they reported fewer binges. Their cravings didn’t change either, and neither did their depression scores.

A blood test for steady heavy drinking over the previous few weeks showed no clear difference overall.

Among the people who took kudzu at least 2.7 times a week, though, fewer tested positive than in the placebo group, and their binge days fell further.

The authors cautioned against reading too much into that subgroup. Frequent dosing may have picked out people who drank the most to begin with, and those drinkers had the most room to cut back.

The volunteers came back three months after their 12 weeks ended. Treatment had stopped by then, yet the kudzu group still reported about 32% fewer binge days than the placebo group.

The researchers can’t yet say why. They pointed to research on breaks such as Dry January, where some studies have found that a month without alcohol is followed by lasting cutbacks.

Drinking less during treatment may have given people a chance to rethink their relationship with alcohol and gain confidence in their ability to cut back, the team wrote.

Further studies, including interviews with participants, would be needed to test that idea.

Everyone in the trial was recruited in the San Francisco Bay Area.

Participants reported their drinking through computer-based interviews, and the researchers also collected blood and urine samples.

The weekly counseling may have helped the placebo group cut back too, which could have shrunk the gap. How kudzu performs without that support hasn’t been tested.

How kudzu works is still an open question. One idea is that its main compound, puerarin, widens blood vessels and changes how the body takes in alcohol. That may make the first drinks feel stronger and delay the urge for another.

The trial’s extract came from a commercial supplier, but store products differ in how they’re made and how strong they are.

Santos and his colleagues cautioned that store-bought supplements may not match the standardized capsules used in the trial.

Nor did the trial test whether kudzu helps people who want to quit completely, rather than cut back on heavy drinking.

The kudzu study was a mid-stage trial, and the authors wrote that larger, later-stage trials may be warranted to show whether targeted kudzu holds up as a treatment.

The full study was published in the journal American Journal of Psychiatry.

New 80-Minute Dune Movie Releases On Digital In Just 1 Month

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The Dune franchise is expanding sooner than expected.

Frank Herbert's iconic 1965 sci-fi novel, which follows noble heir Paul Atreides going on a grand adventure on the desert planet Arrakis when his family is betrayed, has launched a sprawling series both on the page and on the screen. Herbert himself wrote five sequels while his son Brian wrote 15 spinoff novels with Kevin J. Anderson. In addition to comic book, game, and television adaptations, the series has been adapted into a 1984 David Lynch movie starring Kyle MacLachlan and a 2021 Denis Villeneuve trilogy starring Timothée Chalamet, which is set to continue with the upcoming Dune: Part Three.

Now, a listing on Apple TV has confirmed that the documentary Dune - The Real Story is set to debut on digital on December 7. That sees the movie dropping for at home viewers exactly two weeks before Dune: Part Three debuts in theaters on December 18. The 80-minute documentary follows the trials and tribulation involved in bringing Dune to the big screen.

The documentary primarily focuses on the franchise's history of film adaptations ahead of the Villeneuve trilogy (which also spawned the HBO spinoff series Dune: Prophecy). This includes the David Lynch movie (which was famously a critical and commercial flop, though it later grew to become a cult film), but it also focuses on attempts to mount Dune movies that ultimately fell through.

The most famous of these is the version that was set to be helmed by The Holy Mountain director Alejandro Jodorowsky. His expansive 10-hour vision included a star-studded cast that featured Orson Welles, Gloria Swanson, Salvador Dalí, David Carradine, Udo Kier, and Mick Jagger, music by Pink Floyd, and designs by H. R. Giger. While the movie never received funding, its pre-production process directly inspired many other iconic movies such as Alien, Raiders of the Lost Ark, Star Wars, and The Terminator.

The story of the Jodorowsky version has been told in detail in the 2013 documentary Jodorowsky's Dune.

Director Ridley Scott also attempted to make a Dune movie in between his sci-fi masterpieces Alien (which debuted in 1979) and Blade Runner (which debuted in 1982), which will be covered in Dune - The Real Story. The all-encompassing documentary was written and directed by Robin Bextor, a documentarian who has helmed a number of similar movies, including Peaky Blinders: The Real Story, Nosferatu: The Real Story, Wicked: The Real Story, Oppenheimer: The Real Story, Twisters: The Real Story, and The Exorcist: Untold. He is also known for music documentaries about subjects such as The Beatles, UB40, and Kiki Dee.
