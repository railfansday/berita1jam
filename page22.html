Life at a new club is always going to bring a transition period, with Mikel Arteta assessing which selection of midfielders works best.

However, the Brazilian may not be set for a longer run of starts in the Gunners' starting XI.

His underlying performances suggest he has been offering plenty in the middle of the park.

The South American is making a difference on both ends of the pitch this season.

Arteta has several midfield options at his disposal, but Guimaraes is making a strong case to become one of the first names on the teamsheet.

However, Arsenal do have plenty of games to play, and the Spaniard cannot risk any of those stars picking up injuries.

High Fat Diet Linked to Higher Colon Cancer Risk, Early Research Shows

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Follow Healthline on our socials for all things health.

In a new study, researchers report that a high fat diet may potentially increase the risk of colon cancer.

The researchers from Arizona State University said a diet high in certain fats can cause lasting changes in intestinal stem cells that lead to the development of tumors and the possibility of colon cancer.

The study was conducted in mice, but the researchers noted changes in the DNA of intestinal stem cells that may explain their growth and survival.

The researchers published their findings on November 12 in the journal Stem Cell Reports.

The researchers said the study is important because 1 in 25 people develop colon cancer in their lifetime. In the United States, that translates into about 108,000 new cases of colon cancer every year. About 55,000 males and 55,000 females are expected to die from the disease in 2026.

Experts not involved in the study say the research provides valuable information, but they have reservations about the results, as the findings do not yet apply to humans.

“The findings provide a potential mechanism linking diet to intestinal cancer development, but we need to be cautious about translating these results to humans,” said Dalia Perelman, MS, CDE, a research dietitian at Stanford University in California.

“We already have evidence from human studies that obesity and certain dietary patterns, particularly those high in processed meats and low in fiber-rich foods, are associated with increased colorectal cancer risk,” Perelman told Healthline. “This study adds a potentially important mechanism to investigate, but it does not establish that dietary fat itself increases colorectal cancer risk in humans.”

Ketan Thanki, MD, a colorectal surgeon specializing in benign and malignant diseases of the colon, rectum, and anus at the MemorialCare Todd Cancer Institute at Long Beach Medical Center in California, shared similar views.

“This study provides a better understanding at the cellular level of how a high fat diet may contribute to colorectal cancer development,” Thanki told Healthline.

“Animal studies are an important starting point for understanding biological processes, but we must be cautious about directly applying their findings to humans,” he added. “Mice and humans share many cellular and biological mechanisms, which makes these studies valuable in identifying potential pathways involved in disease development. However, human diets, genetics, metabolism, and environmental exposures are more complex.”

To reach their findings, the Arizona State researchers fed mice what the scientists described as a Western-style high fat diet for 6 months.

They then compared those results with those from mice given a low fat diet.

The researchers said the mice on the high fat diet gained more weight than the mice on the low-fat diet. They also developed more intestinal stem cells, and those cells grew at a more rapid rate.

They added that mice genetically predisposed to intestinal cancer who were fed a high-fat diet developed more and larger tumors.

The researchers noted changes in the DNA of intestinal stem cells that may help explain their increased growth and survival.

They also pointed out that stem cell behavior returned to normal after high fat diet mice were switched to a low fat diet, although some DNA changes persisted. After four weeks, approximately 18% of these changes remained.

When mice were returned to a high fat diet, the researchers said their intestinal stem cells responded more quickly and strongly than those of mice exposed to the diet for the first time.

They concluded that the study suggests that maintaining a healthy, low fat diet may help regulate intestinal stem cell growth and reduce colon cancer risk.

Kristin Kirkpatrick, RD, president of KAK Consulting and a dietitian at the Cleveland Clinic Department of Wellness & Preventive Medicine in Ohio, wasn’t involved in the study but highlighted a particular aspect of the findings.

“What I found particularly interesting was that even after the mice returned to a low fat diet, some of the molecular changes persisted,” she told Healthline. “This raises questions about how our dietary patterns over time may influence long-term health.”

Experts said these new findings are interesting but noted numerous caveats.

“One important takeaway is that dietary habits over time matter,” said Thanki. “Even if someone has consumed a high fat diet for years, making changes now may still provide meaningful health benefits.”

“I also think these findings are particularly important for children and adolescents, as establishing healthier dietary habits early in life may provide long-term benefits,” he added.

However, Thanki noted that not all fats are the same. “The health risks depend partly on the type of fat consumed, how much is consumed, and what foods make up the overall diet,” he said.

“Diets consistently high in saturated fats, particularly those containing large amounts of processed meats, fried foods, and highly processed products, can contribute to obesity, elevated cholesterol, cardiovascular disease, and metabolic dysfunction. These factors can also contribute to chronic inflammation and may increase the risk of certain cancers, including colorectal cancer,” Thanki continued.

Perelman said the diet used in the study was extremely high in fat, providing 60% of calories from fat, primarily from lard.

“We know that the health effects of dietary fats differ depending on their sources and fatty acid composition, and we cannot assume that a diet rich in unsaturated fats from foods such as olive oil, nuts, and seeds would produce the same effects,” she noted.

Perelman added that the researchers primarily studied stem cells from the small intestine rather than the colon.

“Although these tissues share some biological characteristics, they have important differences, and we cannot assume that the same changes would occur in the colon,” she explained.

Finally, Perelman noted that “the mice consuming the high fat diet also gained weight, making it difficult to separate the effects of dietary fat itself from those associated with increased body fat and other metabolic changes.”

“The goal shouldn’t necessarily be to eliminate fat because our bodies need healthy fats for normal functioning,” said Thanki. “Instead, people should focus on reducing saturated and trans fats while incorporating healthier unsaturated fats in appropriate amounts.”

Thanki had several dietary suggestions. Among them:

“Consistency matters more than perfection. Even modest dietary changes, maintained over time, can have meaningful health benefits,” Thanki said.

Perelman agreed. “The overall quality of the diet matters. Diets that are very high in fat may displace other foods and nutrients that have important health benefits,” she said.

“For colorectal cancer specifically, higher intakes of dietary fiber and whole grains have consistently been associated with lower risk,” Perelman noted.

“Therefore, rather than focusing exclusively on reducing fat, it is important to consider the overall dietary pattern and whether it provides adequate amounts of fiber-rich plant foods and other beneficial nutrients.”

Kirkpatrick agreed that the type of fat is what is important. “A Mediterranean-style diet that includes olive oil, nuts, seeds, and fatty fish can be relatively high in fat but is consistently associated with positive health outcomes,” she said.

“On the other hand, a diet high in saturated fat, processed meats, and ultra-processed foods may increase the risk of certain chronic diseases, particularly when it’s also low in fiber and other protective nutrients.”

Kirkpatrick also noted that many lifestyle habits contribute to a person’s overall health.

“Our health is influenced by so many factors beyond diet, including how we manage stress, our sleep habits, physical activity, genetics, and overall lifestyle,” she said.

Family Dollar agrees to shell out $63 million for deceptive pricing

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The agreement requires Family Dollar to pay $40 million for a redress fund, $20 million for discounts, and $3 million to be split among 17 states.

NORFOLK, Va. (CN) âThe Federal Trade Commission and 17 states announced a $63 million settlement with Family Dollar Thursday over claims of deceptive pricing.

Family Dollar, previously a subsidiary of Virginia-based Dollar Tree before its sale to private equity firms last year, operates over 7,000 stores primarily in low-income rural and urban areas where it acts as some communities’ only accessible source for household needs. The FTC and the states sued under the FTC Act, the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act and dozens of state laws. Part of the settlement requires Family Dollar to offer consumers $20 million in discounts and establish a $40 million redress fund for eligible customers. The 17 states agreed to split $3 million.

Family Dollar has already entered into $800,000 worth of settlements with state attorneys general over claims that it changes the price consumers see on the shelf or website once they reach checkout.

“For consumers living paycheck to paycheck, even a slight price increase at checkout can lead to real, adverse consequences,” the FTC and 17 states, including Alabama and Colorado, said in their complaint. “Despite serving consumers for whom affordability and accuracy are critical, Family Dollar has repeatedly charged higher prices at the register.”

One consumer reported Family Dollar to the Better Business Bureau in 2023, claiming the store charged them full price for cereal that should have been discounted. Consumers from seven states reported Family Dollar in 2025 for refusing to honor a “2 for $20” sale for large diaper packages they saw on the company’s mobile app.

“When you go to the store, you deserve to know exactly how much you are paying for something,” Colorado Attorney General Phil Weiser said in a statement. “When you budget for groceries and anticipate paying a certain shelf price, only for that amount to change at the register, this can greatly affect a consumer. When a business does this, we will hold them accountable.”

State inspectors noticed large discrepancies between the listed price of products and the price charged at the register. Utah found Family Dollar has failed 47% of inspections for inaccurate price labels since 2023. One Utah store overcharged for 12 of 25 items the inspector purchased. In Cuyahoga County, Ohio, the Family Dollar stores failed over 83% of pricing inspections.

The FTC and the states also noted Family Dollar has failed to address years of complaints over its mobile app. They offer examples of customers unable to use coupons from the mobile app because they malfunction at the register.

The settlement requires Family Dollar to adopt a pricing accuracy program. Third-party assessors will conduct annual assessments for four years. Family Dollar must also post settlement notices at its stores and on its website.

“Family Dollar has charged consumers more at the register than the prices it advertises â betraying the trust of millions of American consumers who rely on Family Dollar for essential household goods and necessities and imposing significant financial harm on the communities that can least afford it,” the FTC and the states said in their complaint.

Other states, including New York, Massachusetts and North Carolina, have fined Family Dollar in the past. The settlement comes a few weeks after the FTC and 20 states sued Dollar General for inaccurate pricing.

Family Dollar’s lawyer failed to respond to a request for comment.

“The FTC is committed to ensuring price transparency and fairness so consumers can make informed choices,” Christopher Mufarrige, director of the FTC’s Bureau of Consumer Protection, said in a statement. “Today’s order will ensure Family Dollar stops deceiving consumers about its prices and returns money to consumers harmed by these practices.”

Ho apprezzato le scelte di Aurelien e Marcus di venire nonostante fossero acciaccati, ci hanno provato ed è stato bello vedere la loro voglia di giocare con la Francia".

A livello morale come vede Mbappé?"Lo vedo bene, la buona notizia è che può giocare domenica, e che avrebbe voluto giocare anche domani. Però dobbiamo adottare una politica a zero rischi, la stagione è lunga".

A che soluzioni sta pensando?"Il mio lavoro è selezionare certi giocatori: a volte alcuni sono infortunati e non possono giocare, per cui facciamo altre scelte. Siamo a novembre, il campionato è iniziato da poco e c'è la Champions, è un mese complicato anche per le gare delle nazionali, ma è un mese così. Siamo dispiaciuti per gli assenti, ma è così: i giocatori devono badare alla loro salute".

Oltre a Mbappé, mancano Dembélé, Thuram, Lepaul. Chi gioca in attacco?"L'obiettivo è schierare la miglior squadra possibile, nonostante questo ci sono giocatori in grado di ben figurare. Ci sono tante assenze, nonostante questo metteremo in campo una squadra competitiva".

Diouf può giocare esterno a sinistra?"Sappiamo che è un giocatore che gioca in un'altra posizione qui all'Inter, ma può giocare in vari ruoli. Penso possa farlo anche a sinistra: quando è con noi, è per giocare in quel ruolo, a sinistra".

Sta pensando a Zaire Emery in una posizione diversa rispetto al PSG?"Quello che voglio è vedere i giocatori da vicino: nei rispettivi club ogni allenatore prende la sua decisione e deve gestire il calendario, ma cerco di schierare i calciatori nella loro posizione naturale. Non abbiamo bisogno di spostarlo in una posizione diversa, da terzino destro: giocherà a centrocampo".

Come ha vissuto questo primo periodo da ct? Qualcosa l'ha stupita?"La difficoltà è sempre legata a chi non può giocare, perché è una cosa che non piace a qualsiasi allenatore. Però fa parte del calcio, e lo dobbiamo accettare. Nella partita di andata abbiamo avuto una serata complicata, perché sappiamo che l'Italia, quando vuole fare una bella partita, la fa, soprattutto difensivamente. Io ho giocato 4-5 anni alla Juventus e penso che per un italiano venga prima una buona difesa, poi attaccare. L'Italia lo fa benissimo".

Un giorno le piacerebbe allenare la Juventus?"Sì, però non si sa mai. Lippi mi ha insegnato che non bisogna mai fermarsi, che bisogna insistere sempre. Io all'inizio non giocavo bene: i tifosi e la stampa non mi amavano. Ho sofferto un po', ma Lippi mi ha detto che avrei giocato nella sua Juve: gli devo tutto. Un giorno mi piacerebbe, sì, perché no, se continuerò a fare l'allenatore".

Cosa le ha dato di più il calcio italiano?"Onestamente, la tattica. Con Lippi facevamo tanti minuti di tattica ogni giorno: per uno come me, che voleva sempre il pallone, ha significato imparare tanto. Un maestro come Lippi mi ha insegnato tanto a livello tattico. Inoltre, mi ha dato grandissima fiducia e ho cercato di restituirgliela sul campo: a Milano abbiamo giocato delle belle partite".

A2UI 1.0: Build delightful, fast, reliable, agentic experiences

📅 Nov 11, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

User interactions with agents today are where computers were in the terminal era: capable, and answering almost everything in text. Users can ask an agent for anything by typing or talking, and what comes back is usually a wall of text, a poor way to compare three products or edit and approve a detailed plan.

Today, we're releasing A2UI 1.0, the first stable version of our open protocol for generative UI. It lets AI agents answer with interactive screens, such as a form to confirm or a chart. Developers can use it now with open-source renderer SDKs for the web, Android, and iOS and agent SDKs for Python, TypeScript, and Dart. Companies such as Atlassian, Alation, Bloomberg, and ByteDance are already building with it.

With A2UI, the agent describes what to show, and every app draws the UI with its own native components. The application receives A2UI messages and each app renders the content securely and at native speed. Users get the same low latency, high trust, and complete native fidelity whether they reach a company's agent in its own app or in a third-party AI assistant that hosts MCP apps, such as Gemini or Claude.

You wake up sick. Getting through the morning takes six apps: book a video visit, move your meetings, tell your team, turn on an out-of-office reply, ask your partner to do school pickup and order soup. Ask a text-only agent instead, and you get a numbered wall of text, then round after round of “which ones?”. A2UI won't handle your sick day, but it gives developers the framework to build an agent that can. That agent could answer with one view of all six apps. Changing your plan is a couple of taps instead of another round of messages, and nothing is sent until you confirm.

The same agent output works on any screen. Ask an agent to “move dinner with Priya to Friday”, and it can answer with one view that lines up your calendar, open tables from a restaurant booking app and prices from a rideshare app, so the time that works for everyone is obvious. The developer builds that view once with A2UI, and it renders natively on your phone and on your laptop.

In both examples, A2UI is the framework that simplifies building these experiences across platforms. Developers build the agent and choose the platforms it works with. A2UI gives them the protocol the agent uses to describe each view, a way to combine components from different apps in one view, and renderers that draw it natively on the web, Android, and iOS.

New to A2UI? Start with the A2UI Playground by CopilotKit or check out industry examples.

Atlassian is bringing A2UI to Jira, where teams can assign work items to AI agents. When an agent needs a follow-up from the team to finish a work item, such as a missing detail or a decision, it asks with an interactive card in the work item, and the team answers right there in Jira.

The Alation Intelligence Operating System (AIOS) builds its feeds using A2UI: metric cards, charts, and more. Each chart can show the SQL query and data behind it. Ask Claude for a feed about profitable products, and the feed renders in the conversation using A2UI to render metric cards, charts, and custom components. The same feed with the same information can be viewed directly inside Alation with a nearly identical layout.

ByteDance released an A2UI renderer for Lynx, its cross-platform framework, in June 2026. Lynx keeps streamed screens smooth and ships a default set of components built on its `lynx-ui` library. In the Lynx GenUI Playground, you can watch A2UI messages rendered natively, for example, as a two-day trip planner for Kyoto. Lynx supports A2UI 0.9 today and is adapting to 1.0 with the A2UI team.

Ask Gemini Notebook for a slide deck and it replies with a card that asks two things: presenter slides or a detailed deck, and which topics to focus on. A review card follows with the format, focus, and visual style, which you can edit before it generates the deck. The same cards guide Video Overviews on the web and on mobile.

Organizations can register their own agents that respond with A2UI. Gemini renders those responses using the components in its A2UI component gallery, and supports A2UI 0.9.1 today. The Google Cloud documentation covers registering an agent and hosting one on Cloud Run.

A2UI and MCP Apps work together. Alation already uses both to show its A2UI feeds in Claude. Our June post, A2UI + MCP Apps, covers three ways to combine them, and the A2UI in MCP Apps guide shows how to run A2UI screens inside an MCP App.

Jetpack Compose support for 1.0 is coming soon. Agents in Gemini Enterprise can already use A2UI, and we're working on upgrading Gemini Enterprise to A2UI 1.0. The A2UI roadmap covers the rest, from design-system primitives that render natively on every platform to official SDK support for decision models like Jev.
