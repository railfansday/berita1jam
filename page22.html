11 Things Jensen Huang Actually Said at His First All-Hands Back at Nvidia

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Jensen Huang is going to therapy for 90 minutes a day, rethinking his management style, and says that violating Nvidia’s code of conduct isn’t necessarily a fireable offense. That’s just some of what the newly returned Nvidia CEO told staff during his first all-hands meeting on Tuesday.

Here are some of the most striking things Huang said during the meeting, according to remarks reviewed by WIRED. Nvidia did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

On self-improvement: “I go to therapy every day for an hour and a half. And it's been helping me a lot.”

On accountability: “There are rumors on the internet that apparently I'm a tech bro, and therefore I’m back. Apparently, I'm a tech bro, and because I wear a super nice jacket, that I'm above the law, and I’m above the code of conduct. That is not true. It won't be tolerated. That is not true. It won’t be tolerated, and it wasn't tolerated here.”

“If one of you were to violate a code of conduct, what does that mean? If you were ever to violate a code of conduct, it is not appropriate. I just wanted you to know, it is not right, it's not appropriate, it won't be tolerated. But ‘it won't be tolerated’ does not mean, the moment you violate a code of conduct, we walk you out the door. Facts, circumstances, a lot of assessments. And your management team will still make the decision. That is what is in the best interest of the company and its employees, with all the facts and circumstances.”

On his management style: “The intensity I bring elevates everybody else. But I can go too far. When I go too far, it hurts people, it belittles them, it disempowers them. And that’s not right. And so I need to go figure out how I'm going to bring the intensity, the crazy intensity, to the field.”

On women at Nvidia: “I believe we need to create and continue to sustain a safe environment for all of our women. And if I did anything that put that in jeopardy, I cannot tell you how sad I am, and I'll do everything I can to fix it. We will not tolerate that.”

On succession: “All day long, this company can run autonomously, and you saw it. I promise you, whether I'm here or not, this company is wired up to execute, execute, and execute, and deliver on financial results and financial results.”

“I also didn't believe in succession planning. Let that sink in. I don’t believe in succession planning. I think it’s flawed. The idea of succession planning is not flawed. What people think succession planning means is flawed.”

“There’s a reason why we don’t have succession planning, it’s not because we are unaware of the word. I’m the longest-running tech CEO in history.”

On politics: “There [were] also rumors that apparently I'm back because the highest levels of our government wanted me back. That is also completely untrue. Our board of directors performed their duty as they should have.”

“It is absolutely true that President Trump really, really loves our company,” Huang told employees. “He gloats about Nvidia. You know he does. But there’s a question about politics. Are we now political? Are we partisan? I wanna be very clear with you. President Trump is the United States president. He is my president. And I’ll do everything I can to help this president be informed about how he could make decisions that are in the best interest of our country. … We have to do it, and whoever the next president is will be my president, and I will do exactly the same.”

“Don't think for a second that politics ever play a role in this company, not even a little bit. We are the most apolitical company. I don't love that we talk about politics here. Take it home. I love that we talk about work here.”

‘Hexed’ Creatives on Telling a Modern-Day Witchcraft Story, Casting Hailee Steinfeld, and Their Favorite Easter Eggs

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

SPOILER ALERT: This story discusses mild plot points for “Hexed,” opening in theaters on Wednesday.

Walt Disney Animation Studios’ 65th film “Hexed” tells the story of the hidden world of witches and how, throughout history, they have been misunderstood. At its heart, however, is a story of motherhood and the powerful bond between a mother and daughter.

Disney animated films often kill off a parent to propel a story forward. But the creatives behind “Hexed” embraced that important relationship. Related Stories ‘PAW Patrol’ Spinoff Series ‘Skye’s Pup Squad’ Set for 2027 Release

Directed by Fawn Veerasunthorn and Jason Hand, and co-directed by Josie Trinidad, the film follows Billie (Hailee Steinfeld), a rebellious teen who escapes into the secret world of Hexe, a realm of witches and wizards who live in harmony with nature.

Veerasunthorn explained that the story idea was originally pitched by Trinidad. “She wanted to tell a story about the hidden world of witches. What if there’s a place where they can thrive on their own? The place built by witches for witches.” More importantly, Veerasunthorn notes, Trinidad wanted to tell a story of motherhood and the true heart of the story lies in the complicated, relatable relationship between Billie and her mother, Alice (Rashida Jones).

In developing Billie, she was portrayed as a modern teenager who wears combat boots and has an edgy style. Producer Yvett Merino explains that Billie oozes confidence: “She doesn’t quite fit in. There’s something that feels different about her.”

The key was making the film’s main protagonist relatable. “We all go through that at some point in our lives, especially in those teenage years where you’re just trying to figure out who you are,” Merino says. “She takes her mom’s car. She borrows her mom’s car to go and free the school mascot. She’s imperfect, impulsive and maybe doesn’t always make the best decisions.” Merino goes on to say, “When I was a teenager, I didn’t always make the best decisions. But you know what? I got away with it. We’ll just learn from that lesson.”

Imperfect relationships were key when it came to Alice as well. Alice is protective of Billie and wants to keep her daughter safe. Hand points out, “We’re all imperfect. We’re all trying our hardest, but we’re still figuring it out and that was really important to us.”

Visually, the world of “Hexed” is vibrant and it took over 700 artists to bring it to life, sometimes with up to 40 people working on a sequence. “The animation style pushed boundaries as far as they could by creating a world audiences had not seen before, filled with characters they’d never seen,” Veerasunthorn says.

The idea was that it would feel like a crisp autumn day after it had just rained and the air felt a bit chilly. “It’s a perfect place where magic can thrive,” she adds.

Producer Roy Conli credits the film’s production designer, Lorelay Bové, for her creativity. “When people see this film, they will see a world they’ve never seen; they’ll see characters they’ve never seen before in a Disney film.”

If the textures of Hexe look heightened, that was a deliberate choice. “We turn the dial up so it feels more like a caricature drawing could feel,” Hand explains. “We wanted this world to be so enticing to Billie. All of our decisions within the world of Hexe go back to Billie. When she goes out into that city for the first time, what’s she going to want to see? Why is this world built for her? What is it speaking to her inside?” Hand says. All those factors were important from both a production design standpoint, but also from a character standpoint. “And then for that journey to bring her mom back into it, throws her for a loop. That’s where the story starts to get its juice and interesting.”

Voice casting was important too. Hand recalls seeing Steinfeld in “True Grit” and later in “The Edge of Seventeen.” In that film, Steinfeld plays a troubled teen who had a contentious relationship with their mom. He says, “I always loved her in that role. I still loved her, even though she was doing the worst things and saying terrible stuff and that’s a really great quality.” But it was her voice quality that made her perfect to lead “Hexed.” “Our animators even picked up on particular mannerisms of the way she holds her mouth, little micro expressions that she does.”

Jones was perfect for Alice. Verasunthorn says, “Rashida brings this sense of comedy in a very uptight mom. Those two words don’t go well together, but somehow she makes it work. The depth of vulnerability that we get to see in Alice is quite beautiful.”

“Hexed” wouldn’t be a Disney movie without a villain to stir up a little trouble. Enter Jodie Foster, who voices Queen Celeste, a sinister ruler who stops at nothing to maintain her power.

Was it easy to get her to say yes? Conli says, “We asked her and she did it. She’d never played a villain before. She’s done a phenomenal job at this incredible narcissistic witch that is a little over the top.”

Joining Steinfeld, Jones and Foster are Walton Goggins as Beef Roger Krumchuk, a three-eyed cat.

“He brought so much comedy and so much personality, way beyond what we ever imagined,” Merino says.

As for the film’s Easter eggs, there are plenty to look out for. Among them are Aladdin’s magic carpet in Billie’s bedroom, a floating head fortuneteller that nods to “The Haunted Mansion.” Mother Gothel from “Tangled” appears in a blink-and-you’ll-miss-it moment.

Hand’s favorite one is Cursula, a nod to “The Little Mermaid’s” Ursula. “It’s voiced by Jodi Benson — who provided the voice of Ariel in the original animated film. I love ‘The Little Mermaid’ so much, and so to have Jodi Benson come in and do a complete twist on it and have her play Cursula, she was so amazing. She gave us such energy.”

Merino and Conli concur that the scene with dancing brooms is one that is special for both of them. “It reaches back to our history and goes back to Walt Disney’s “Fantasia,” and the Sorcerer’s Apprentice scene,” Merino says.

“The brooms, that’s 80 years in the past. That’s ‘Fantasia’; it touches on this amazing legacy that we all live with, and we all celebrate.”

ICE later said in a statement to The Guardian that Mendoza and Mejia had overstayed their visas from Mexico and claimed Mejia “hurled hot coffee on the officers” during the incident.

GTA 6 chapter 1 is so good that Im afraid to move on to chapter 2

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

I've run into a very stupid problem with Grand Theft Auto VI, a game I love so far.

Namely, I'm having a surprisingly difficult time moving on from chapter 1 of the game's sprawling crime story. For a couple of real-life days now, I've been sitting on the edge of finishing GTA 6's opening stanza with very little motivation to move on. At the time of writing, I have literally one story mission left to go before the first chapter ends, and almost nothing of substance left to do otherwise.

But as much as I know I need to break through and see the rest of what GTA 6 has to offer, it's hard to resist the call to just live out Jason Duval's mundane daily routine for eternity.

Warning: There are some light spoilers for early parts of GTA 6's story in the following section.

For those who haven't played GTA 6 yet or are still in the early stages like me, chapter 1 follows Jason's meager existence as a sort of paid goon for Brian, a local boatyard owner who dabbles in drug trafficking on the side. The other protagonist, Lucia, gets out of prison partway through the chapter, but for most of it, she's not playable yet. Most of the story and side missions in chapter 1 focus on Jason doing little odd jobs that occasionally spiral into chaotic violence. He spends time with his girlfriend or buddies and otherwise just kind of chills when he's not doing all the crime stuff.

GTA 6 chapter 1 immediately establishes an endearingly ordinary routine for Jason, at least by action video game protagonist standards. Every in-game day I wake up, feed the pet snake, drink coffee, work out, drink a protein shake, and take a shower before heading out to do whatever it takes to get enough money to set up a nest egg for Jason and Lucia.

While there are some minor gameplay incentives to doing all of that, GTA 6 doesn't outright demand any of it from the player. I'm just doing it because it's a legitimately interesting loop, especially by the standards of big-budget mainstream games. I'm not just endlessly traversing between icons on a map and shooting some bad guys when I get to each one. The Best Last-Minute Gifts That Don't Feel Last Minute

This series will help you pick the perfect presents, decor, and activities to make this Christmas the Best Christmas Ever.

We’ve all experienced gift panic—that sinking feeling when you realize you need a present in an hour and you just don’t have one to give. Whether you spent all year chasing the “perfect” gift or simply procrastinated, your time is nearly up. But don’t panic. The clock may have nearly run out, but your options haven’t. Before you resort to re-gifting something from the back of a closet, here’s how to pull off a last-minute gift that is effortless but feels elevated and worthy of the person you’re giving it to.

With the right presentation, even the most last-minute gift can read as thoughtful. Any performer will tell you that the difference between a good show and a bad one comes down to commitment and confidence. The same is true for last-minute gifts: If you act like your gift is thoughtful and intentional, they’ll think it is. The biggest mistake people make is apologizing as they hand over a present—nothing ruins a vibe faster than, “Sorry this is so last-minute.” So give with confidence.

And focus on presentation. A handwritten note that contains a shared memory or personal joke can elevate a present. Use simple but personal wrapping; craft paper and twine, or a reusable gift bag that looks like you planned ahead. Add a small flourish, like a sprig of holly or a fancy bow. Small touches can make it seem like you’ve carefully prepared. What you don’t want are obvious panic gifts that scream, “I grabbed this on the way here.” The goal is effortless, not frantic.

Digital gifts are your friends. You can order something in minutes that arrives instantly online, and with a little thought, will still read as personal and intentional. The trick is choosing a present that speaks to the recipient’s tastes, hobbies, or quirks, and pairing it with a small note to show you remember what they love. From e-books to streaming subscriptions, these gifts hit the sweet spot of thoughtful and last-minute. Here are some suggestion:

When time is running out, gift cards are your secret weapon. They arrive instantly online, don’t require shipping, and with the right presentation, they can feel surprisingly thoughtful. The key is to pick a card that fits the recipient’s interests and add a personal note. The more specific you are, the better. If your giftee is into Italian food, get them an Eataly gift card. If they're a PC gamer, get them a Steam card. If they're into education, a membership to Skillshare is perfect. There's a gift card out there for just about any interest, and all you have to do it google it and email it.

Generic gift cards can be a last-minute lifesaver that can still seem a little personal if you give them the right way.

Pick a theme or design: Many online cards, like Amazon, Etsy, or Starbucks, let you choose designs. The right visual makes the card feel more intentional.

Include a warm, personal message: Even one line can make a digital card feel thoughtful—something like, “I couldn’t pick just one thing for you. Hope this helps you treat yourself.”

Send strategically: Deliver the card by email or text, but consider printing the email or screenshotting it in a small envelope so it feels like a “real” gift.

Sign someone up for a subscription service, write a note explaining you want to keep giving them presents all year, and you're golden. Here are some suggestions:

SnackCrate: This service sends a different snack from around the world every month.

SpiceClub: For the culinary adventurer on your list. A subscription to RawSpiceBar’s Spice Club delivers a curated selection of spices every month.

Mocktail Club: Booze-free fancy drinks delivered once a month? Sign me up.

Thera Box: If you know someone in need of some self-care, this once-a-month box is packed with wellness and relaxation items.

Loot Crate: If your giftee is geeky, consider this monthly surprise box of pop culture treasures.

I have enough crap in my house, so I love getting experiences instead of things. The challenge with experience gifts is usually timing: Many require reservations or coordination, but there are plenty of options that let your giftee schedule the time, making them perfect last-minute gifts.

Museum membership cards: Many institutions sell digital gift memberships that the recipient can activate whenever they like.

Movie tickets: Your local multiplex probably has a gift certificate you can buy online.

Local attraction passes: Some city attractions offer gift cards or “anytime admission” tickets that don’t expire immediately.

Flowers or a small potted plant: Give a seasonal bouquet or a small indoor plant. Add a handwritten note, even a single line, and it might come off as thoughtful.

Gourmet chocolates or candy: Specialty chocolates from a local shop or grocery store display can feel luxe with a little ribbon or bag.

Lottery tickets: Who wouldn't appreciate a bunch of scratch-off lottery tickets?
