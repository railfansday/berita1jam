Before paying extra for premium dog or cat food, find the small-print line on the package that names your pet’s life stage and AAFCO, the group that sets pet food nutrient standards. Any food with that line meets the same standards as pricier brands, so buy the cheapest one your pet does well on.

In each food’s biggest bag at Walmart, the store’s own Pure Balance Chicken & Brown Rice Recipe costs $1.17 a pound, less than half the $2.67 a pound for Blue Buffalo’s Wilderness Grain-Free Chicken Recipe. Both dry dog foods carry that line and have nearly the same calories per pound.

That line is the nutritional adequacy statement, and newer labels add a short version on the front, such as Complete Food for Adult Dogs. The statement says the food was formulated to meet the nutrient standards of the Association of American Feed Control Officials (AAFCO) or passed AAFCO feeding trials. It also names a life stage, such as growth for puppies and kittens, adult maintenance, or all life stages. The FDA says a food has to pass one of those tests to be called complete and balanced, which means it’s meant to be your pet’s only food. One labeled for intermittent or supplemental feeding only isn’t a complete diet.

Premium claims don’t make a food more complete. Natural describes how ingredients are sourced and processed, organic how they’re grown, and human-grade how the food and its ingredients are handled. Grain-free isn’t healthier for most pets either. Veterinary nutritionists at Tufts University call it a marketing concept and say grain allergies are rare.

The FDA has also investigated reports of a heart disease called dilated cardiomyopathy in dogs eating certain foods, many of them grain-free. It hasn’t established that the foods cause the disease, but ask your vet before switching your dog to grain-free.

So compare prices only among foods whose statement names your pet’s life stage, and look at dry food first. In a Tufts price check of cat foods, even the most expensive dry food cost less per day than the cheapest canned one, for the same calories.

If you’d rather cook, have a veterinary nutritionist write the recipe for any homemade dog food or cat food you’d serve daily.

For a grown dog or cat, a store brand whose statement covers adult maintenance or all life stages is a complete diet.

6th Circuit upholds jury win for Ohio State in former graduate student’s sexual harassment lawsuit

📅 Nov 23, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

While the plaintiff argued the district court incorrectly instructed the jury, the 6th Circuit said she’d failed to raise these objections at the proper time.

A former Ohio State University graduate student could not convince a federal appeals court to overturn a jury’s verdict in favor of the school in her sexual harassment lawsuit because she failed to show that her trial was unfairly prejudiced, according to a Tuesday decision.

The plaintiff in Huang v. The Ohio State University alleged that a faculty advisor subjected her to unwanted touching and that, when she resisted his advances, he retaliated by manipulating her candidacy exam for an engineering doctorate. She brought claims under Title VII of the 1964 Civil Rights Act as well as the 14th Amendment’s due process right to bodily integrity.

A district granted summary judgment for Ohio State on the plaintiff’s Title VII claims, holding that she was not an employee of the university within the law’s definition and therefore could not bring those claims. At trial, a jury held for the plaintiff’s advisor. She appealed to the 6th U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals, which reversed and ordered a new trial.

At the second trial, the jury determined that the plaintiff was not an employee because Ohio State and the advisor did not have the “right to control the manner and means by which [the plaintiff] accomplished her work.” The jury also ruled in favor of the advisor on the plaintiff’s 14th Amendment claim.

The plaintiff again appealed to the 6th Circuit, alleging that the district court incorrectly instructed the jury on what facts were relevant to its determination of whether she was an employee. She also claimed that the court abused its discretion in excluding certain evidence and that it improperly prohibited her from requesting economic damages in her closing argument.

Each of these claims failed, the 6th Circuit held.

The plaintiff forfeited her challenge to the court’s jury instructions because she failed to raise it, despite the district court specifically asking whether the parties had objections or edits to its instructions. To grant a third trial on this point “would be unfair to the court and the defendants,” the 6th Circuit said.

In her evidentiary argument, the plaintiff claimed that the district court improperly prevented her from submitting portions of a slideshow containing the results of an Ohio State campus climate survey on sexual assault. She argued that the presentation would have made her allegations more plausible.

However, the plaintiff failed to include the relevant slides in her appeal, leaving the 6th Circuit unable to conclude whether they had sufficient weight to affect the jury’s verdict. The appeals court also could not determine whether the slides offered anything “above and beyond” other testimony in the case.

In all, the plaintiff failed to show that any of her allegations amounted to unfair prejudice warranting a new trial.

The case represents one in a handful of recent jury decisions to favor employers facing Title VII lawsuits. In March, a jury held for Haribo of America in a former employee’s discrimination and retaliation case, determining that the plaintiff’s dismissal was a mutually agreed-upon separation between the parties rather than retaliation.

And in a 2025 case, a jury sided with Levi Strauss in a lawsuit alleging that a former executive did not receive a promotion because of her pregnancy, gender and age.

Ladybird, an independent web browser with a built-from-scratch engine, is finally available for testing. On Monday, Ladybird developer Andreas Kling announced that the browser is launching in alpha on macOS and Linux following more than four years of development.

The release makes Ladybird the first browser in years to arrive with an entirely new engine. We’ve seen many engines emerge over time, but the most popular browsers use just three today: Gecko, Blink, and WebKit. While Gecko, now used in Mozilla Firefox, first appeared in Netscape 6 in 2000, Apple’s WebKit arrived in Safari in 2003 as a fork of KDE’s KHTML. A decade later, the Google-backed Chromium project forked WebKit to create Blink, which now powers Google Chrome, Microsoft Edge, Opera, and more.

Ladybird is backed by a nonprofit organization with the goal of creating “an independent, fast and secure browser that respects user privacy and fosters an open web,” according to its website. Though Ladybird development initially began as part of a C++ project, its developers eventually began porting the code to Rust using AI.

In his post announcing the alpha, Kling notes that the browser built around the Ladybird engine “is modest but functional,” adding that “some websites won’t work” during the alpha. A Windows version of Ladybird will arrive during a “later stage” of the project.

JSX Buys Bankrupt Airline In Major Expansion Beyond 30-Seat Semi-Private Flights

📅 Nov 23, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

JSX has acquired bankrupt New Pacific Airlines, securing a regulatory pathway to operate aircraft with more than 30 passenger seats. The airline, which abandoned its ambitious Boeing 757 operation in November 2025, was acquired in August 2026, although the deal only became public following a November 20 Department of Transportation (DOT) filing, as reported by AeroTime.

Rather than introducing new aircraft, JSX intends to transfer existing Embraer ERJ145-LRs into the acquired airline, increasing seating capacity from 30 to 42 passengers. The acquisition also creates opportunities beyond its immediate plans, potentially including higher-capacity turboprops, while providing greater flexibility amid ongoing regulatory scrutiny of JSX's operating model.

JSX reportedly acquired New Pacific for approximately $3.1 million in consideration. According to the November 20 DOT filing, the airline intends to restart operations under the JSX Air brand using two ERJ145-LRs leased from sister company Delux Public Charter, increasing to six aircraft by the fifth month. Projections include 10,106 flights and approximately 309,000 passenger boardings during the first year.

The key acquisition is New Pacific's FAA Part 121 operating certificate. JSX currently flies under Part 135, selling individual seats through the Part 380 public charter framework. This limits its aircraft to 30 passenger seats, even though its Embraer jets were originally designed to carry more. New Pacific's certificate provides a pathway to operate 42-seat aircraft, subject to regulatory approvals.

The move also offers regulatory flexibility. AP New previously reported how the FAA has proposed tightening oversight of public charter operators, following criticism from major airlines and pilot unions. Although those changes remain unresolved, a Part 121 operation could provide JSX with an alternative growth platform. Responding to questions about the acquisition, the company told View From The Wing:

"JSX is continually evaluating opportunities to support its long-term growth and bring its distinctive flying experience to more customers and more communities. As part of that work, we are pursuing additional pathways that could provide even more ways to deliver our award-winning service in the future."

Originally known as Northern Pacific Airways, the airline planned to connect North America with Japan and South Korea through Anchorage, using Boeing 757-200 aircraft. Inspired by Icelandair's connecting-hub model, the strategy unraveled following Russian airspace restrictions in 2022. The carrier lacked the ETOPS approval required for alternative routings, forcing management to seek other markets.

In July 2023, the airline instead launched domestic flights between Ontario, California, and Las Vegas, subsequently adding Reno and Nashville. Rebranded New Pacific Airlines in September 2023, it abandoned scheduled operations in April 2024 to focus on charter flights. Management then pursued corporate and sports-team charters, converting its Boeing 757s from 181 seats to just 78 premium seats.

However, neither business model proved sustainable. According to January 2026 bankruptcy documents, the wider group burned approximately $5 million monthly during its earlier difficulties, while the NHL contract initially lost money on every flight. Expensive Boeing 757 maintenance and disputed payments worsened liquidity problems. Operations ceased in November last year, and the airline's Part 121 certificate became an important asset in bankruptcy proceedings.

The end of an era for a carrier that experienced more than its fair share of struggles.

JSX's immediate plans involve six 42-seat ERJ145s, where it can increase capacity per aircraft by 40% over the current 30-seat layout. Paxex.aero reports that proposed routes would average 230–345 miles (370–556 km), with approximately six daily sectors per aircraft. Higher capacity could improve economics, although retaining JSX's spacious cabin, currently featuring individual seats and side tables on some aircraft, may become challenging.

The acquisition could also benefit JSX's turboprop strategy. The airline introduced ATR 42-600 flights in December 2025 with 30 premium seats per aircraft, and has outlined plans for up to 25 ATR aircraft, potentially including the larger ATR 72-600. Under Part 121, both types could theoretically accommodate substantially more passengers, provided New Pacific secures the necessary aircraft-specific operating approvals.

Only the 42-seat ERJ145 configuration has been announced. Higher-capacity ATR operations remain hypothetical.
