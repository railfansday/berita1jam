Fantasy Basketball Top 200 Rankings: Franz Wagner has been on a tear lately

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Orlando Magic forward Franz Wagner's start to the season was anything but bad, as he recorded averages of 19.6 points, 6.4 rebounds, 8.5 assists, 1.3 steals and 0.9 three-pointers per game in his first eight appearances. However, the efficiency was lacking, as he shot 43.1 percent from the field and averaged 4.4 turnovers per game during that stretch. There have been no such issues recently, as Wagner has been one of the hottest players in the NBA.

After scoring 30 points or more in four straight games, Wagner is averaging 34.0 points, 7.5 rebounds, 7.8 assists, 1.5 steals, and 2.5 three-pointers per game over his last four. While he's also averaged 4.0 turnovers, his shooting percentages have improved. Wagner's shooting splits over his last four games are 53.3 percent from the field, 35.7 percent from three and 96.8 percent from the foul line.

In his most recent outing, a November 13 win over the Bucks, Wagner recorded a line of 35 points, nine rebounds, 12 assists, one steal, one block and three three-pointers, shooting 13-of-22 from the field. The Magic have been without Paolo Banchero since the second game of the season, and Jalen Suggs just returned from a knee injury. Those injuries have opened things up a bit for Wagner, and he deserves credit for taking advantage of the opportunity.

The question fantasy managers will have to ask themselves is: can Wagner maintain this production level (or something close to it) after Banchero returns, or should they sell high while he's rolling?

Forget Robinson: Pochettino Plots Move For 'Perfect' USA Star In Man Utd Rebuild

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Mauricio Pochettino has identified Weston McKennie as a top target to strengthen his new-look Manchester United squad during the January transfer window. Pochettino is keen to reunite with the Juventus midfielder at Old Trafford after forming a strong relationship in the United States national team set-up.

Pochettino, 54, was appointed as United’s new head coach on Friday following the departure of Michael Carrick. The Argentine has been tasked with turning the club’s fortunes around after a difficult start to the season. Indeed, the Reds sit inside the Premier League relegation zone with just ONE win in 10 matches this season.

Although United still have a large portion of matches to still go until the turn of the year, Pochettino will have one eye on January, as he tries to strengthen his squad. The Reds spent more than £150million in the summer, but went and signed three midfielders, rather than looking to fix other areas, including the back-line.

But it appears that Pochettino may have not even been satisfied with the work that was done on the midfield. Youri Tielemans, Carlos Baleba and Andrey Santos were all brought in by United, as they looked to beef up that position after losing Casemiro.

Now, Pochettino is targeting yet another midfield acquisition with McKennie on his shopping list, according to La Gazzetta dello Sport. The 28-year-old has been a standout performer for Pochettino in the USMNT and is being considered for a shock January move to Old Trafford.

McKennie scored for the USMNT in their 6-0 thrashing of Haiti on Saturday, a game which marked Pochettino’s penultimate in charge before he makes the move to United.

Should McKennie move to the Reds, it would be a second chance for him to impress in the Premier League following a disastrous loan spell at Leeds United in the 2022/23 season.

He struggled as the Whites were relegated from the top flight, and returned to Juve at the end of the campaign with a point-to-prove. And it’s fair to suggest McKennie has made huge improvements to his game since then, and is now integral to Juve and Luciano Spaletti.

After establishing himself as a key member in Spaletti’s side last season, Pochettino was keen to praise McKennie and brand him as “important”. As well as playing in midfield, McKennie can also be used at full-back or even at centre-back, when needed.

“Juventus is Weston McKennie plus 10 players,” Pochettino said last month. “And we're so happy about that, because that means he's an important player for Juventus.”

When asked about Spaletti’s comment that he views McKennie as a ‘perfect’ striker, Pochettino replied: “Like his coach said, he can be the best striker, the best number 10, the number 7, the number 2, the number 4. He can play fullback, he can play midfield. I think he's playing, and he's playing in different positions.

“And of course, it's good for him, it's good for us. It's good to have a player that can play in different positions and to give balance. Sometimes the team, in different positions, as you say, if I see him in different positions, in which position do I think he's important?

“In the moment that we are going to give and to find that balance, I think it's important to see how he can provide balance in case that we want him in the starting 11. And of course, it's good to have this capacity, the capacity from the player to adapt to different positions. That's always good for the coaching staff."

McKennie is one of a number of USMNT players that has been linked with a move to United, including Alex Freeman and Antonee Robinson.

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Wolves will be targeting attacking reinforcements in the January transfer window as they seek an immediate return to the Premier League.

Cesar Peixoto has enjoyed a positive start to life at Molineux, but Wanderers aren’t always impressing with their performances, particularly in attack.

Whilst a return of 26 goals in 15 games is decent, half of those came in the first six games, so they have dried up somewhat recently, which has brought a spotlight on the manager.

Peixoto will no doubt argue that Raul Jimenez’s absence due to a serious knee injury has been a major factor, with Wolves lacking a physical focal point up top.

That’s something that they must address in the New Year, and another area that Peixoto will want to improve is out wide.

Rodrigo Gomes and Mateus Mane have been inconsistent, and whilst Rafiki Said has done well, he is yet to score in Old Gold. Meanwhile, Jean-Ricner Bellegarde has struggled, and Bertrand Traore is yet to play in the league due to injury.

So, it’s something of a problem position for Wolves, and adding a quality option down either flank could make all the difference in the promotion race.

And, one player who should be on Wolves’ radar is youngster Ryan Kavuma-McQueen, who is starring for Portsmouth on loan from Chelsea right now.

Who does ex-Premier League and EFL striker Kevin Phillips think has the loudest stadium atmosphere below the top flight in England?

The 17-year-old is the latest highly-rated talent to come through Cobham, and the Londoners do have high hopes for the teenager.

That has been backed up with how he has adapted to Championship football, with Kavuma-McQueen flourishing in what is a very competitive division.

Despite his age, the teenager has stood up to the physicality of England’s second tier, and his pace, power and skill has made him a joy to watch at times for Pompey.

Whilst Kavuma-McQueen has only registered one goal and two assists in 14 games, it doesn’t tell the whole story, as he has been vital to the way John Mousinho’s side play, and his ability to relieve pressure and carry the ball is important.

So much so, the youngster has already attracted attention, with reports claiming that Sheffield United are monitoring the progress of the Chelsea academy graduate.

It should be said that Portsmouth signed Kavuma-McQueen on a season-long loan, and with the way he has started at Fratton Park, Chelsea won’t be in a rush to cut the move short to send the 17-year-old elsewhere in January.

However, the way he has coped with the Championship means a move to a higher-ranked club can’t be ruled out, which is why Wolves may be the ideal destination.

As outlined above, the Black Country outfit need a new winger, and even though Kavuma-McQueen is only 17, the last few months suggest he could be an upgrade on Peixoto’s current options.

Plus, whilst Portsmouth are a good team, there would be a sense at Chelsea that Kavuma-McQueen could go to another level, and the prospect of the player linking up with the likes of Jimenez, Fer Lopez and Mane is one that could help his development.

Again, there’s no suggestion that Kavuma-McQueen will be available in January, but, even if he’s not, this could still be a deal that Wolves pursue in the summer, whether they win promotion to the Premier League or not.

This is a player that Chelsea will feel has the capability of eventually playing at the highest level, so, if his time at Portsmouth does carry on in this manner, his next step will be to a top-end Championship side or a low-end Premier League one.

By my math, all-in cost (ER + dividend tax) is within ~0.02%/yr between them. Securities lending differences are negligible. DFUS’s lower yield is the main real difference, mostly for future low-income years. I already hold some DFUS.
My preferred core US holding is DFUS, though currently I'm TLH'd out of it. I do DFUS with AVUS and DCOR as partners.

I consider the tax efficiency (primarily due to DFUS not holding REITS), the very small tilts, and Dimensional's non-index implementation (e.g. delayed IPO purchases) to be well worth it.

For an investor in low tax brackets, I think VOO would be more competitive.
In my mind you are approaching this reasonably. I am trying to minimize forced income in my taxable accounts in retirement. For total market holdings in a taxable account my preference is AVUS, DFAU, and DFUS. Due to TLH I'm currently in DFAU. In my 24% bracket with NIIT, their is a tiny edge to VTI and VOO for total cost, but not a lot. At the 32%-35% marginal it is about a wash.

I value diversity and so want in general to hold an ETF that holds more stocks than less. VOO has a bit over 500 holdings, VV around 430, and DFUS around 2200-2300. I have drunk the factor investing Kool-Aid so prefer Avvantis and DFA funds. Any of the the three I mentioned I would be happy to hold for decades. I have looked at DCOR and it is also a solid choice but I'm more comfortable with DFAU over DCOR. For a typical investor, I think using DFUS as a primary position and then VTI, SCHB, ITOT, or SPTM for potential TLH partners is a solid, middle-of-the-road approach.

Just as crucially, it was so different to what Spain do. It was something of England's own. In between, Ezri Konsa scored the plundered set-piece header that his recent performances have deserved. It clearly meant much more to England given the fact Spain are the world champions who had won at Wembley. They're the ones who have done it, the benchmark. But that bounce didn’t come. The equaliser didn't come. England stood up.

And that might be because of a decision Tuchel doesn’t even have to make. Trafford made his claim. England made their mark.

Tuchel has ultimately overseen their most significant win for some time, at least as regards what it means for finally winning that trophy. That’s what all this is about, after all.

Spain can fairly say that what really matters is what happens in tournaments, but it didn’t feel meaningless as they toiled to keep that unbeaten record and the Madrid crowd willed them on. That run is now over. Might that finally signal that this could be England’s time?

It is far too early to say, but this was the end to the year Tuchel wanted. It was the performance England needed.
