You can finally edit an Instagram Story without deleting it

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

There’s a 15-minute window to fix typos and make other changes.

Meta is finally addressing a particularly annoying quirk I'm confident has affected just about all of Instagram's 3 billion users at one point or another. More than a decade after Stories became a central part of the photo-sharing app, the company is giving users the ability to edit their posts after they've been shared.

Want to fix an embarrassing typo? Add or change the music? Write a different caption altogether? All that is now possible without the need to delete a post, Instagram head Adam Mosseri shared. With the change, Instagram users have a 15-minute window to tweak their Stories while preserving views, likes and replies.

Deleting and re-uploading a Story might not seem like a huge inconvenience, but it can be frustrating when it happens within a sequence of posts. Since Stories slides appear reverse chronologically, there's no way to delete and re-share a post and have it appear in the same place if there are other images immediately before and after. It's even worse if you're a professional creator or social media manager and need to worry about a do-over affecting your metrics. So while the 15-minute editing window only gives a little additional flexibility, it does give you the chance to catch errors that have already been posted.

It's not clear why such a seemingly obvious change took so long for Instagram to implement. Meta has long allowed users to edit regular Instagram posts after the fact. "A lot of people have been asking for this, so I'm excited to get this one out there," Mosseri wrote.

The feature is rolling out now. Once editing is available, you'll be able to tweak Stories from the "more" menu and selecting "edit story." Posts that have been edited will include an "edited" label when others view them.

Instagram is also tweaking a few aspects of the Stories composer too. The app is updating its available background color options to include gradients and other effects. It's also making it easier to share and format several images at once.

Mortgage Applications Dip as Rates Hit Three-Week High

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

House keys with a house shaped keychain resting on a mortgage application form

Mortgage applications dipped slightly this week, in keeping with the average 30-year mortgage rate rising to a three-week high.

The latest Market Composite Index from the Mortgage Bankers Association (MBA)—its measure of mortgage loan activity volume and includes purchases and refinances—shows mortgage application activity decreasing 2.3% on a seasonally adjusted basis after last week’s 6.2% increase. On an unadjusted basis, the Index decreased 5% compared with the previous week.

This week’s decline follows weeks of fluctuations as the 30-year fixed mortgage rate still solidly remains over 7%–last week decreasing to 7.11% according to Freddie Mac but currently sitting at 7.46%, according to Mortgage News Daily. Freddie Mac’s next rate report will be released Thursday.

“Mortgage applications dipped as mortgage rates moved higher, with the 30-year fixed mortgage rate up to its highest level in three weeks at 7.35 percent,” said Joel Kan, CMB, MBA’s vice president and deputy chief economist. “Purchase applications were down 18 percent from a year ago, as renewed affordability challenges from elevated mortgage rates have pushed prospective homebuyers to the sidelines.”

MBA’s Refinance Index decreased 2% from the previous week and was 54% lower than the same week one year ago. MBA’s seasonally adjusted Purchase Index–its weekly measurement of nationwide home loan applications based on a sample of about 75% of U.S. mortgage activity–decreased 3% from one week earlier. The unadjusted Purchase Index decreased 7% compared with the previous week and was 18% lower than the same week one year ago, according to the report.

The refinance share of mortgage activity saw an increase this week–to 41.1% from 39.8% the previous week, with Kan pointing to a rise in FHA refinances as most notable.

“Overall refinance activity was down over the week, but FHA refinances increased almost 7 percent,” Kan stated. “The FHA rate was around 30 basis points below the conforming rate, which supported refinance opportunities for some homeowners.”

The FHA share of total applications increased to 16.1% from 15.5% the week prior, with MBA listing the VA share of total applications increasing to 12.8% from 12.4% the week prior. The USDA share of total applications remained unchanged at 0.5 percent from the week prior.

Kan also pointed to additional macroeconomic indicators as potentially weighing on home-purchase application activity into the new year.

“Treasury yields rose last week amid data showing inflation accelerated in October and Fed officials acknowledging that additional rate hikes may be needed in the coming months,” he said.

Are Uranus and Neptune Secretly "Rock Giants"? A Noble Gas Might Hold the Answer

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

For decades, astronomers have divided the planets in the outer solar system into two distinct camps. Jupiter and Saturn were the âgas giantsâ made up primarily of hydrogen and helium. Uranus and Neptune were the âice giantsâ, and were much smaller but were packed with heavier elements. A new paper from Olivier Mousis of the Southwest Research Institute and his co-authors, available in The Planetary Science Journal, explores whether we can use an isotope of a noble gas - argon-40 - to determine whether there are hidden mountains of rock under the ice giantâs thick atmospheres.

Scientists are getting excited because there has been an ongoing debate that these distant worlds are actually covered in rock. But we canât simply fly a probe out a few billion miles and drill down into their surfaces to determine that for ourselves. Instead, they decided to use proxies, such as the concentration of argon-40, as a rough measurement for how much rock there is below the atmosphere.

Understanding why the presence of one noble gas isotope leads to understanding whether rock is present or not requires a bit of a diversion into the world of bananas. Or at least, it gives me an excuse to talk about bananas in a planetary science article. Bananas are radioactive, and the thing causing their radioactivity is potassium-40 - a relatively unstable form of a very common element. When potassium-40 decays, it turns into argon-40, which, as a noble gas, hardly interacts with anything.

Most of this radioactive decay takes place in rocks containing potassium-40, rather than bananas, so one of the most common sources of argon-40 is literally rocks. After it is created, it bubbles up out of those rocks and escapes into its host planetâs atmosphere. In fact, it makes up around 1% of the air youâre breathing right now.

On the ice giants, this same process could hold - argon-40 could form inside a rocky surface, and then bubble up into the planetâs atmosphere over billions of years. So we should be able to send a probe to measure the concentration of argon-40 in their atmospheres, and that would serve as an effective proxy for the existence of rock under the atmosphere.

Thereâs just one catch - rocks arenât really the only source of argon-40. In fact, there was already plenty of it floating around in the protosolar nebula that the Sun and all the planets eventually formed out of. Therefore, Uranus and Neptune could have âtrappedâ this leftover argon-40 before they were even mature, causing argon-40 to eventually appear in their atmosphere, but not from rocky surfaces.

According to the paper, the amount of argon collected by the early versions of Neptune and Uranus depends entirely on where they formed with regards to the âargon ice lineâ. This line, which happens around 23-35 Kelvin, the temperature that argon freezes into solid ice, or is trapped inside water ice, is the determining factor of how much primordial argon Neptune and Uranus had sucked up.

Unfortunately, thereâs still a lot of debate about where exactly in the solar system the ice giants formed. If they formed close to the Sun and eventually migrated back out to the edges of the solar system, they might not have captured much argon ice, and the majority of the argon in their atmospheres would be due to release from rocks. On the other hand, if the theories about them actively moving through the solar system soon after their creation are true, then they would likely have captured a large amount of argon-40, making it useless as an indicator of whether or not the planets have a rocky surface.

Luckily, there is a way to potentially solve this puzzle. Argon-40 has a lighter isotopic cousin, known as argon-36, which is non-radioactive, and doesnât form in decaying rocks. Measuring its level would act like a control group, allowing scientists to estimate how much of the argon present in the atmosphere would have been swallowed at birth, as we have other estimates of the ratio between argon-40 and argon-36 in the early solar system.

Perhaps Míriel believes this is how to prevent her vision of the Great Wave — back in Season 1 — from coming true. Maybe she sees this marriage as a way to keep the King's Men from having complete control of the island kingdom's culture and voice. Either way, her goals are undoubtedly quite different from Pharazôn's.

However you slice it, this marriage appears to be the most viable option for the Númenoreans, though it is clearly a solution that Elendil (Lloyd Owen), who rides after her, believes to be ill-fated. Of course, he's ultimately right. Just as Season 3 kicked off with a gut-punch in the Númenor plot after Eärien's (Ema Horvath) death, so too will this marriage end in disaster. CMA Awards 2026: How to watch the 60th anniversary event and celebrate all things country music

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Grab your best rhinestoned cowboy hats and boots—it is time for the Country Music Association Awards.

Much like the hairstyles in Texas, this year is going to be big, celebrating 60 years of the CMA Awards. From a Dolly Parton tribute to Brooks & Dunn’s special award to the Taylor Swift of it all, there is a little something for everyone live from Nashville’s Bridgestone Arena.

Let’s see what all the yee-hawing is about.

Who is nominated for a CMA Award?

Speaking of the Lone Star State, Ella Langley’s hit “Choosin’ Texas” is one of the singles that helped her score nine CMA Award nominations, the most of anyone this year.

Miranda Lambert is nominated seven times. Ben West and Kacey Musgraves will go into the evening with five nominations each.

Greenhorns Avery Anna, Carter Faith, Emily Ann Roberts, Tucker Wetmore, and Stephen Wilson Jr. are all nominated for New Artist of the Year.

Fan favorites Luke Combs, Cody Johnson, Ella Langley, Chris Stapleton, and Lainey Wilson are all battling it out in the Entertainer of the Year category.

For a full list of nominees, check out the CMA Awards website.

A Swift return

For the first time in 10 years, Taylor Swift will be attending the CMA Awards.

She is nominated for Single of the Year for her Toy Story 5 jam “I Knew It, I Knew You.” Swifties rejoice because the news doesn’t end there. She will also perform for the first time in 13 years.

Who is hosting the 60th CMA Awards?

Guiding the audience through all the awards, performances, and tributes is host Lainey Wilson. This is the third year in a row she has had the honor of MCing the event.

Wilson will be backed up by a slew of presenters. Martina McBride, Glen Powell, Carly Pearce, Michael Bublé, Joe Buck, Jessica Chastain, and Shaboozey are just some of the stars helping hand out the awards.

Additionally, Matt Rife, Luke Bryan, and Lara Spencer are also scheduled to appear.

Who is performing at the 60th CMA Awards?

Swift is far from the only performer to grace the stage. It is a night celebrating country music, after all.

Miranda Lambert, Ella Langley, Tim McGraw, Kacey Musgraves, Luke Combs, Vince Gill, Mumford & Sons, and Chris Stapleton are just some of the big names singing their hearts out.

As part of her hosting duties, Wilson will lead a Dolly Parton tribute in the opening of the show.

Parton passed away in August of this year. The beloved singer has had many celebrations of her incredible legacy including, “CMA Presents: I Will Always Love You: A Celebration of Dolly Parton.” Wilson believes Parton deserved every remembrance possible.

“No amount of honoring will be enough for that lady, and what she has done for this genre, what she has done for the world, what she did for me personally, the friend that she became,” Wilson told Robin Roberts on Good Morning America.

What award is Brooks & Dunn being presented with?

Since hitting the country music scene in 1991 with their hit single “Brand New Man,” Brooks & Dunn have been a fixture in country music.

The duo have won 20 CMA Awards, including Vocal Duo of the Year. At the 60th Annual CMA Awards, they will be presented with the Willie Nelson Lifetime Achievement Award.

Part of this honor is a special performance from the Brothers Osborne, Luke Bryan, Halestorm, HARDY, and Marcus King.

How can I watch or stream the awards live?

To catch all action, tune into ABC at 8 p.m. ET. This covers anyone with an over-the-air antenna and reception, meaning you can watch it live for free the old-fashioned way or via cable TV. And cord-cutters, rejoice. For the first time ever, the CMA Awards will also simulcast live on the Disney+ streaming service.
