Palantir adds three UK police forces to its beat with £10.1M deal

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Five-year contract follows London setback as former Met chief Cressida Dick emerges as a paid adviser

Three UK police forces led by Cambridgeshire Constabulary have awarded Palantir a £10.1 million contract after London Mayor Sadiq Khan blocked a separate deal with the Metropolitan Police.

The five-year agreement covers Bedfordshire Police, Cambridgeshire Constabulary, and Hertfordshire Constabulary, despite warnings from campaigners and politicians about dependence on the supplier.

A contract award notice says the east of England police forces expect the Palantir platform to provide "an enterprise-wide data integration and analytics capability, enabling the secure ingestion, linking, and analysis of data from multiple systems and sources across the policing environment." The contract runs until October 2031, with the estimated value covering all three forces.

News of the award coincided with revelations that former Metropolitan Police commissioner Dame Cressida Dick has worked as a paid adviser to Palantir since September 2025.

A Palantir spokesperson told the Bureau of Investigative Journalism: "Dame Cressida is advising us on how our software could help improve the delivery of services in the public sector, drawing on more than 40 years of public service in a variety of roles."

Dick said her role was limited to advising on how such products could assist public services. She said she had never lobbied or advocated for Palantir, or introduced the company to her contacts.

Dick led the Metropolitan Police, the UK's largest force, between 2017 and 2022. She left after Khan said he had lost confidence in her. She later said she was "intimidated" into leaving.

Separately, Khan stepped in to block the Met from awarding a £50 million contract to Palantir. His office cited serious concerns about the procurement process. Palantir later sued the Met, but the case was settled in October.

The new police contract award follows a report from MPs warning of vendor lock-in to US suppliers, including Palantir. It said: "Our view that Palantir's increasing presence across the public sector represents an unacceptable point of weakness is not ideologically motivated or driven by concerns about the quality of their products.

"The government should retain the ability to pick and choose individual suppliers and safeguard against the risk of vendor lock-in and debilitating dependencies, particularly in areas of critical national importance such as healthcare and national security infrastructure." ®

Syndicated reporting provided by Stats Perform and reviewed by the SoccerNews editorial team.

Manchester United have appointed Mauricio Pochettino as Michael Carrick's successor.

Carrick was sacked on Thursday after leading United to just one win in their first 10 Premier League games in 2026-27 (D5 L4).

Despite coming from 2-0 and 4-2 down to earn a 4-4 draw with Aston Villa in what turned out to be Carrick's final game in charge, United are spending the international break in the relegation zone, sitting 18th.

It is their lowest position 10 games into a top-flight season since 1986-87, when they were 20th.

They have also gone eight Premier League games without a win for the second time, also doing so in April and May 2025, under Ruben Amorim.

And it is Pochettino who is tasked with turning things around at Old Trafford, despite signing a new deal with the United States following the World Cup.

He has signed a reported two-and-a-half-year deal at Old Trafford, becoming the club's eighth permanent manager since the retirement of Alex Ferguson in May 2013.

Pochettino will take charge of the USA for both legs of their CONCACAF Nations League quarter-final against Haiti this international break, before heading to Manchester and preparing for a clash with rivals Liverpool.

Pochettino has been linked with United numerous times in the past and had been in talks with the club in 2022, though they opted to appoint Erik ten Hag instead.

The 54-year-old has previous experience in the Premier League, having taken charge of Southampton, Tottenham and Chelsea across his career.

His most successful spell was with Spurs, whom he led between May 2014 and June 2019, taking charge of 202 Premier League games.

He has overseen the most Premier League games in Spurs' history and recorded the most wins (113) – that is 43 more than any other boss has managed for the club (Harry Redknapp is second with 70).

During Pochettino's tenure at Spurs, they scored 372 goals in the top-flight, which ranked fourth in the division in that time, behind Arsenal (376), Liverpool (394) and Manchester City (470), in addition to reaching the 2019 Champions League final, which they lost to Liverpool.

Of managers to oversee 30+ Premier League games, Pochettino (51%) has the 19th-best win ratio in the competition's history.

Nearly half of U.S. weight-loss drug users are changing how they take them, CDC finds

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Skipping doses was the most common change at 28%, and 38% of users who made changes cited cost, according to the CDC

The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention released a report on Thursday showing that 44% of U.S. adults taking GLP-1 medications such as Wegovy and Zepbound changed their use of the drugs from the way they were prescribed.

Skipping doses topped the list of changes, with 28% of users saying they did it. Among users who made changes, cost was the reason for 38% and availability for 36%.

The data come from the CDC’s Rapid Surveys System, which collected responses from 8,118 U.S. adults in July and August 2025. Overall, an estimated 9.8% of adults said they had used a GLP-1 drug at some point over the past year. The 976 users in the sample received follow-up questions about their use.

Younger adults reported the most changes. Modification rates were 54% among users ages 18 to 39 and 30% among users 65 and older. Delayed prescription fills were reported by 37% of the younger group and 6% of the older group, and 58% of younger users who made changes cited cost, compared with 20% of older users.

Compounded formulations were used by 22.6% of GLP-1 users, and another 8.8% could not say if theirs was one. The Food and Drug Administration does not vet compounded GLP-1 medications for how safe, effective or well made they are before they go to market. Modification rates were 62% among compounded-drug users and 36% among other users.

About 12% of users reported reducing or stopping their medication because of side effects or symptoms. The CDC said more evidence on the safety and effectiveness of these real-world practices could help guide clinical guidelines.

The report noted limitations. The two survey panels had cumulative response rates of 13.3% and 3.8%, and the findings rely on self-reported answers.

The survey was conducted before Eli Lilly $LLY and Novo Nordisk intensified their push to make the drugs easier to get and to take on lower-priced compounded versions, according to Bloomberg. The federal government has also taken steps to broaden access, with Medicare patients among those covered.

Cost has been a barrier for some time. Without insurance or discounts, FDA-approved weight-loss drugs carry a sticker price that can top $1,000 a month, which helped drive demand for compounded versions during shortages of approved products.

The Medicare GLP-1 Bridge program, which began July 1, offers eligible beneficiaries weight-loss drugs for $50 a month.

The Midterms Are Over. History Says This Is What the Stock Market Does Next.

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Washington is divided again, and history has some encouraging data for investors.

Democrats won control of both the House and the Senate in the Nov. 3 midterm elections. That means President Trump will spend his final two years in office working with a Congress controlled by the other party. If you're wondering what this means for your portfolio, history offers some useful perspective.

Here's a statistic that sounds extremely reassuring. The S&P 500 has been higher 12 months after each of the 19 midterm elections since 1950, with an average gain of about 15%.

Even the weakest stretch, which included the 1987 crash, ended with a small gain. But the stock market has never been lower a year after a midterm election.

Five presidents since 1950 have served two full terms, and their final two years tell a mixed story. Here's how the S&P 500 did, including dividends:

One key takeaway is that the year after the midterm was positive all five times. The final year was more mixed, with two big declines. But it's worth noting why. The 2000 drop came as the dot-com bubble burst, and the 2008 drop was during the financial crisis. Neither had much to do with who controlled Congress.

Three of those presidents, Eisenhower, Reagan, and Bush, faced a Congress controlled by the other party in their final two years, just as Trump will. The results ranged from excellent to the 2008 crash. Over a longer period, RBC data shows that years with a Republican president and a Democratic Congress have averaged about a 5% annual gain, the lowest of any combination. Of course, that average is based on just a handful of periods, so I wouldn't read too much into it.

In short, history suggests that the year after a midterm tends to be good for stocks, and that a president's final stretch depends far more on the economy than on politics. Earnings, interest rates, and economic growth will drive the market over the next two years. My plan is the same as always. I'll stay invested and keep buying great businesses, no matter who controls Washington.

Once Upon a Farm is cutting its outlook after a toddler snack bar recall

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The toddler snack maker lowered its full-year net sales forecast to $290 million-$300 million from $327 million-$335 million

Once Upon a Farm lowered its full-year 2026 outlook on Thursday to reflect the voluntary recall of its Tractor Wheels product lines. The company expects net sales of $290 million to $300 million, down from the $327 million to $335 million it had forecast.

The company expects an adjusted EBITDA loss of $4.5 million to $7 million, compared with prior guidance of adjusted EBITDA of $3 million to $4.5 million. Adjusted EBITDA is earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization, with additional adjustments. The company said the recall's effect on the fourth quarter drove the update.

For the third quarter, net sales rose 34.9% to $89.1 million from $66.0 million a year earlier. Net loss narrowed to $3.8 million from $11.3 million. Adjusted EBITDA was a loss of $1.0 million, compared with a profit of $0.9 million a year earlier.

Gross margin fell to 36.1% from 42.1%. The company cited trade investment for a national club program, product mix and cooler placement costs. As of Sept. 30, it held $106.4 million in cash and cash equivalents and no debt.

"We are committed to resolving the Tractor Wheels situation correctly, continuing to earn the trust of families and retailers, and executing against the significant growth opportunity ahead of us," CEO and co-founder John Foraker said in a statement.

Foraker told Bloomberg the company anticipates as much as $30 million in recall expenses, spanning written-off inventory, legal fees and product returns. For 2027, Foraker expects the company to reach breakeven or better on an adjusted EBITDA basis, with net sales no lower than $330 million, according to Bloomberg.

The company plans to change the bars' recipe and packaging before bringing them back to store shelves, Foraker told Bloomberg. It has brought in outside specialists in food safety to review the product, according to Bloomberg.

Once Upon a Farm announced the voluntary recall of its Tractor Wheels soft-baked bars on Oct. 30, citing three consumer complaints about a choking hazard. The bars are labeled for toddlers ages 1.5 to 2.5, and the recall covers all lots across three varieties.

Tractor Wheels accounted for about 18% of the company's revenue this year as of the earlier coverage. Once Upon a Farm stock has lost over 40% of its value since it began trading in February.

What we know about the global Microsoft outage

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Watch: What we know about the Microsoft global outage overnight

A major global Microsoft outage has caused problems, or in some cases disabled entire services, including emails and artificial intelligence products.

Microsoft has said the outage was not caused by a cyber-security incident, but efforts to fix the issues have so far been largely unsuccessful.

Here's what we know about the services affected and what went wrong.

Schools, GPs and businesses hit by global Microsoft outage as company scrambles to solve issue

Microsoft 365 Services, including Teams, Outlook and Microsoft's artificial intelligence software Copilot, have been affected by "connectivity problems".

Customers are reporting varying levels of access - many have not been able to send or receive emails, view certain files or details on their calendar apps.

Some users have been able to use Teams and are using messaging and online meeting functions without issues.

More than 430 million people use Microsoft 365 apps according to Microsoft's website.

The full scale of the outage has not been confirmed, but monitoring website Downdetector had 140,000 people from 74 countries report issues with Outlook on Thursday evening.

The real figure affected is likely much higher because Downdetector relies primarily on users self-reporting.

Microsoft 365 is also used by companies worldwide and is expected to have a knock-on effect to other services.

Tech giants OpenAI and Anthropic have both reported problems as a result of the outage.

ChatGPT-maker OpenAI said it was investigating an issue affecting its connections to Microsoft's services, which has caused some tools to not work as expected.

From city governments in the US to Australian spy agencies, disruption across the globe has been widespread.

Courts from Los Angeles to Texas warned that communications might be delayed.

In Canada, the public broadcaster CBC reported that a healthcare network running several hospitals in Toronto was experiencing email issues, but that patient care was not affected.

The government of Japan also said its shared IT platform had been disrupted.

Across the UK, GP surgeries, pharmacies, police and schools have been affected by the outage.

Some GP surgeries have urged patients not to submit messages via their website while the problem remains, while several large police forces are urging members of the public to call if they want to make a report.

Microsoft has ruled out a cyber-attack and attributed the ongoing outage to "elevated load" on its infrastructure.

The term refers to when servers are handling a much higher volume of stress than they usually do.

In the case of the Microsoft outage, a large number of servers became "unexpectedly unavailable", the company said, which sent added traffic to remaining servers, straining these.

On Friday afternoon, Microsoft said it had seen "encouraging recovery progress" in Europe and improvement in North America.

The company added it is focused on "addressing the remaining areas of disruption" and warned "some users may still experience intermittent issues as recovery actions continue".

In an earlier statement, the tech giant had said that its attempted fixes were "not taking as expected".

Australian cyber-security expert Troy Hunt told the BBC the outage could have far-reaching consequences.

"Microsoft is a massive part of the internet backbone and reminds us how critical email still is.

"Not only does this outage affect many people's ability to work but also things like the ability to communicate about appointments, purchase things that need verification, create or access accounts and services," he said.

Gergely Orosz, a former leading software engineer at several major tech companies including Skype and Uber, said this could be the "most disruptive" business email outage "of all time".

He said it was still difficult to know just how many people have been affected by the disruption, but added he "cannot recall" an outage over business email ever taking this long to return to normal.

Paul Spain from New Zealand-based advisory firm Gorilla Technology said that with e-mails and services offline, companies have practically "lost a business day", which is "frustrating" users".

Gray and Hackney have represented England at youth level but are eligible to play for Scotland, although neither has committed to switching their international allegiance. Pocognoli admits he has already established contact with the pair but insists any decision will ultimately have to come from the players themselves.

His predecessor, Steve Clarke, previously attempted to persuade the likes of Elliot Anderson and Harvey Barnes to represent Scotland, without success. However, the country has enjoyed greater luck in other cases, with Scott McTominay and Che Adams going on to become influential figures after opting to play for the Tartan Army.

Pocognoli is currently satisfied with the depth available to him as Scotland prepare for their final Nations League fixtures against North Macedonia and Switzerland. Nevertheless, he believes expanding the pool of eligible talent must remain an ongoing priority.

Speaking about his efforts to identify potential recruits, Pocognoli said: “I am busy with everything. We know which players are eligible to represent the country one day. It’s won't come from me, it has to come from them to show it. I try to anticipate everything. The best way is to be proactive and creative in certain situations.

“The depth of the squad is already big without these other names. I’m very happy with it because we have a good double-up and triple-up in each position. But it’s part of my role, for sure [to find players]. Not only for me but for other people working at the Scottish FA. We are on it. Gray and Hackney have different international backgrounds, with Pocognoli acknowledging that any potential switch would require patience.

Gray, the son of former Scotland striker Andy Gray, also has family links to Scottish football through Leeds United legends Frank and Eddie Gray, both of whom represented the national side. The Tottenham midfielder has featured for England at every youth level but has yet to make his senior debut.

Hackney, meanwhile, previously represented Scotland at Under-21 level before switching his international allegiance to England.

Pocognoli believes the age of the two players makes them particularly interesting prospects but insists there is no immediate pressure for either to make a decision.

“With Barnes it was different. I knew before my first camp that he was different,” he said. “The two other lads [Gray and Hackney] are young lads. It’s a long way [away]. But if you wait too long, it might not be the right moment. In a year’s time, we will see. Right now, we have players in the squad who are performing well.

“It will always be up to them [current players] to be the priority, because we’ve built things and we’ve had time with the ones who are here right now. But we are on it. We are always proactive in all of these situations. What is very exciting about those two names is that they are young players.

“That’s interesting. We have to look and manage the situation. But it won’t be a definitive choice from them - because we weren’t proactive. That has to be clear. We are proactive.

“The best way to convince players is to show good football, show them they can come to us, enjoy it and be successful. They have to feel when they leave a top club that they arrive here into top conditions and a top environment.

“That’s the best publicity we can offer, to show good football. Yeah [McTominay is an example] but there are many.”

Breakfast News: What NVDA’s CEO Exit Means for You

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Huang built a company with the products, people, and customers to outlast him, and we expect Nvidia to come through this.

Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia's (NVDA -4.34%) chief executive amid controversies over his personal conduct and questions about whether Nvidia's chips reached China through a customer in Singapore.

His exit followed a week of reports about his personal life. The first concerned an affair with an executive at Megaspeed, one of Nvidia's customers in Singapore. U.S. officials suspected the executive of spying for China, which China denied. On Wednesday, a second relationship, with one of Huang's direct reports, came to light. The board met that night, and the company announced Huang's resignation the following morning.

We think investors are right to take Huang's departure seriously. In our view, though, the sell-off prices in more damage than the business has shown so far.

If you've followed Nvidia for any length of time, you know how central Huang has been. He co-founded the company in 1993 and led it for all 33 years. His leadership structure was also known for being unusually flat, as he had 33 direct reports at last count, according to CNBC.

Huang pushed Nvidia as an innovator and technologist. He launched CUDA (software that lets developers program Nvidia's chips) in 2006 and boldly committed the company to artificial intelligence (AI) computing long before it became a mainstream market. He also managed Nvidia's largest customer relationships himself and represented the company in Washington on export rules for China.

That is a lot for one person to hand off, let alone overnight, and Nvidia has no named successor to take it on. For now, CFO Colette Kress, who has worked alongside Huang since 2013 and has joined him on every earnings call, will serve as interim CEO while the board searches for a permanent replacement.

We understand why the market reacted so sharply. A company that leaned this heavily on one person's judgment now has to make its next big decisions without him, and under federal scrutiny. Analyst Dan Ives estimates Nvidia is worth 25% to 30% less without Huang, and some discount is warranted. With shares dropping to $148.80 in Thursday trading and closing at around $166, the market has already applied much of it.

What reassures us is that Huang's exit on its own is unlikely to change Nvidia's revenue much over the next two years. Demand for its chips is still growing quickly. Last quarter, revenue reached $96.2 billion, up 106% from a year earlier, and the company guided to $108 billion for the quarter that ended in October. By any measure, that's a business in good health.

Chips take years to design, so the products Nvidia will sell through 2027 were planned while Huang was still in charge, and customers have already ordered many of them. Even if regulators cut off sales routed through Singapore, we estimate Nvidia would lose no more than about 5% of its revenue.

A broader crackdown would cost more, and the investigations will help decide whether one comes.
