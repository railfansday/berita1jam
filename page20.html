Katherine Center’s novel, The Bodyguard, is not about Christmas. Not even a little bit. So, when I learned its book-to-screen adaptation, Guarding Stars, was part of Netflix’s 2026 Christmas movie lineup, I was confused. So, I had to ask Jared Padalecki why they decided to involve the holiday in this film.

Notably, the novel, The Bodyguard, takes place in the fall, and Jack and Hannah’s whole experience in Texas centers around Thanksgiving. However, for the movie, the story got moved to Montana and pushed back a month, meaning Christmas was the star of the show. So, as a fan of the book, I asked Jared Padalecki, who plays Jack Stapleton and executive produced the project on the 2026 movie schedule with his wife, Genevieve Padalecki, why they did that. Here’s what he said:

When my wife and I, Genevieve, started shopping the book around after we met Katherine and got the rights to the book and to try and make it into a movie, Netflix had a great idea where they kind of said, ‘Hey, you know, there will be [a small number of] people that will watch a generic holiday movie, and [a much larger number of] people who will watch a Christmas movie,’ essentially.

Listen, while there are some great Thanksgiving movies, Christmas movies are talked about way more, and there’s a ton of buzz around them. Folks love the most wonderful time of the year, and Netflix figured more people would tune into Guarding Stars if it was centered around Christmas instead of another holiday.

So, with that in mind, the Padaleckis took that idea to The Bodyguard’s author, Katherine Center. She was all for the change, and simply requested that the spirit of the characters stayed the same. With that in mind, it didn’t really matter to Center where and when Guarding Stars took place, as the Supernatural star explained:

And so we talked with Katherine about it, and she's like, ‘I get it. Like, that makes sense. And I want as many people to become a part of The Bodyguard universe as possible.’ What was really important to her, that she stressed, was that the characters, like Jack and Hannah and the family, they kept their hearts. Ideally, you could put Jack and Hannah in Iowa in March, or you know, in Oregon in August, and still have the same thing happen. And so she was really receptive about, like, ‘Yeah, I understand. Like, people will watch.’

I think they accomplished this goal too. While Guarding Stars is quite different from its source material (the change at the end surprised me), Jack and Hannah, at their cores, are the same. The spirit of the book is 100% in the movie, and it feels very true to the source material in that way.

During our interview, Padalecki went on to praise Center’s open-mindedness toward the changes they made for this movie, saying some creatives can be “really precious” about those things. Overall, she got the vision of the film, and she was thrilled to see this world she created expanding:

But she's like, ‘Yeah, I get that.’ And I think she said it the best. She's like, ‘This isn't replacing The Bodyguard. This is expanding The Bodyguard universe of Jack and Hannah.’

So, that’s why Guarding Stars is a Christmas movie even though The Bodyguard has nothing to do with it. Moving it back to take place in December puts it in a category of films that people flock to at this time of year as they prepare for the holidays. No other holiday can do that at such a high level. So, this change does make a lot of sense.

Plus, who doesn’t want a bit of festive fun in their rom-com?

Now, with the Christmas season upon us, you can stream Guarding Stars with a Netflix subscription. And if you want to see Jack and Hannah over Thanksgiving, you can read The Bodyguard.

Alexander Isak and Florian Wirtz maintained their encouraging trajectory with the goals in a 3-0 victory against Club Brugge, steering Liverpool back to winning ways in the Champions League and re-establishing their push for a top-eight finish.

Isak brought his tally to 10 for the season with a double, crowning his performance with a superb solo chip over the goalkeeper after resisting the challenge of giant defender Brandon Mechele, while his fellow £100million-plus arrival delivered a strike of equal quality.

The dynamic duo stood out as the visitors’ finest performers in a display that started brightly, lost momentum during the middle period, but finished emphatically with two expertly taken finishes.

Following defeat to Fenerbahce in their previous European outing, a swift response against the Belgian champions—who had already overcome Inter Milan and PSV Eindhoven—was essential, and an efficient away performance duly propelled them up to fifth place.

Isak found the net for the third game running inside 13 minutes, benefiting directly from sustained pressure applied to goalkeeper Yann Sommer.

The Sweden striker forced Sommer into a misplaced pass that was quickly seized upon by Bradley Barcola.

Barcola transferred the ball inside to Dominik Szoboszlai, captaining the team for the first time in the absence of Virgil van Dijk, who pulled the ball back for his team-mate to poke home.

Isak excelled in linking the play, combining fluidly with Wirtz, offering the first line of defense, and remaining a constant goal threat, flashing a half-volley wide from Barcola’s knock-down.

He appears increasingly attuned to Andoni Iraola’s gameplan by the day, though his physical resilience will be fully tested as he looks set to make a third starting appearance in a week against Everton.

The form of Wirtz, who registered his sixth goal in five matches across club and international duty, will also be a major concern for the Toffees, with the Germany international brimming with confidence.

Receiving a pass from Rio Ngumoha 30 yards from goal, he had only one thought in mind, taking a touch before deceiving Sommer by rattling a low effort in off the goalkeeper’s right-hand post.

Isak provided the final flourish, gathering Alisson Becker’s pass with his back to goal 40 yards out. Having shrugged off the lumbering Mechele, he matched that intent with a delicate chip to leave Sommer clutching at thin air.

It provided a classy finish to an encounter that had turned into a grind after the visitors failed to capitalise on early dominance, allowing Felix Lemarechal and Joaquin Seys opportunities to threaten.

An injury to Victor Munoz compounded matters for the hosts, though his replacement Ngumoha—Sunday’s man of the match—wriggled into space early in the second half to flash a tight-angle shot across the face of goal.

Liverpool temporarily lost their fluency, though Wirtz and Isak combined to set up Szoboszlai for a chance parried by Sommer, before Alisson responded at his near post to deny Lemarechal and the attacking duo delivered the killer blows.

Hold Your Turkeys! Macy’s 100th Thanksgiving Day Parade Has Been Postponed: Here’s the New Date

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

As it turns out, Mother Nature spares no man nor parade.

After waiting 100 years for the parade’s centennial edition, what’s a few more days?

Macy’s 100th Thanksgiving Day parade will be postponed to Saturday, Nov. 28, because of severe weather.

“The safety of our participants and spectators is our top priority, and after close consultation with our City and agency partners, we have made the decision to move this year’s celebration,” Macy’s representatives said Wednesday.

Even though organizers had already begun inflating the parade’s iconic balloons, the department store, alongside city officials and the New York Police Department, confirmed the Thanksgiving celebration will commence n Saturday at 8:30 a.m. ET instead.

The parade’s delay comes amid a particularly grim forecast for the East Coast. The National Weather Service is predicting a strong storm will impact the area on Thanksgiving Day, inundating it with heavy rain, intense winds and coastal flooding.

RAYE, who recently earned a record-tying 11 nominations at the 2027 Grammys, is set to open the broadcast, which will air on NBC, Peacock and Telemundo. The historic parade will march from the Upper West Side to Macy’s Herald Square flagship store, and include performances by Billboard chart-topping stars Megan Mooney and Teddy Swims. Tony and Grammy-nominated Broadway star Megan Hilty will lead a tribute to the late Dolly Parton, who passed in August at age 80.

Other celebrities to keep an eye out for in the parade’s eye-popping floats include EJAE of KPop Demon Hunters girl group HUNTR/X, Queen Latifah, Jimmy Fallon and The Roots, JoJo, Keke Palmer, Smash Mouth, Mckenna Grace, K-pop boy band RIIZE, Indigo Girls, Natasha Bedingfield, Feid, Icona Pop, Wyclef Jean, Jamie Miller, Leslie Odom Jr., Anthony Ramos, Melissa Joan Hart, Jamie Miller, Holly Robinson Peete and more.

The Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade first marched New York City’s streets in 1924, and it’s only been canceled twice: 1942, 1943 and 1944. During those years, materials for giant balloons were prioritized for World War II efforts over holiday parades. Notably, the balloons were last grounded in 1971 due to severe weather; city regulations dictate that balloons can only fly when wind speeds remain below 23 mph. In 2019, inclement weather forced the balloons to be flown at lower heights than usual.

Officials will address the city tonight at 6 p.m. ET.

This Historic Home Is Built Around a Spectacular Light Well That Looks Like a Connect Four Game

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Long ago, in Barcelona, the laundry of the middle-class residents of the Eixample neighborhood was washed in the wells on Calle Aiguafreda, one of the city’s most picturesque streets. Washerwomen and their families lived in narrow, two-story houses, and the women would go to the wells every day to hand-wash clothes. It is in this unique part of the city that you’ll find one of architect Raúl Sánchez’s latest projects.

The lower entrance to the house and its arbor, on the Calle Aiguafreda side of the building. From here, you can see the living area, a corner of the kitchen island, the bottom of the blue light well, and the white-painted beams. Santina chairs from the 1970s are by Carlo Sant.

“It’s a modest little two-story home on a street that has since lost its original function as a place to wash clothes. We were asked to restore it for a couple and their child,” explains Sánchez. The house faces two streets: Calle Aiguafreda and Calle Granollers. Since there’s a five-foot difference in the elevation of the two streets, Sánchez decided to add a landing at the higher entrance off Granollers, where one flight of stairs descends to a lower level, which opens onto Calle Aiguafreda, and the other ascends to the upper level of the small house, where the bedrooms and a bathroom are located.

In the living room, the floor is made of terra-cotta tiles that echo the traditional clay paving in the home’s garden. A rectangular skylight is offset by 45 degrees from the rest of the room and lacquered in a deep blue, which is complemented by a red reading corner with a Wassily armchair by Marcel Breuer (Knoll), and a yellow folding screen. Between two Amanta armchairs by Mario Bellini (Hay) there’s an Altar side table by Ewe Studio (Tacchini). In front of them is a C cork stool designed by Jasper Morrison (Vitra). Atollo lamp by Vico Magistretti (Oluce).

Sánchez added a new architectural element: a light well that extends from a skylight on the roof through the full height of the building, illuminating even the ground-floor spaces, some of which are partially below street level. Pierced by a series of circular openings, like a Connect 4 game, and coated in a glossy blue lacquer, the light shaft evokes an ancient well while also serving as a contemporary, poetic, and functional architectural element.

Halfway up the ground floor, the house has a second entrance from the street. Sánchez added a landing there with two flights of steps: one leading down to the living area, the other leading up to the bedrooms and the bathroom.

A light well topped with a skylight runs the full height of the building, illuminating the spaces all the way down to the ground floor. The 45-degree rotation relative to the rest of the structure defines the home’s circulation and breaks up any feeling of predictable regularity. Perforated with circular openings and coated in blue lacquer, it gives the home a spectacular architectural feature.

It also stands out as an organizing feature of the home, thanks to its 45-degree rotation relative to the floor plan. “This rotated geometry allows for the dynamic opening and closing of different perspectives and circulation paths, thereby avoiding a predictable corridor-like effect,” says Sánchez. “The circular openings also allow for views between rooms and across different levels.” Cut into all four sides of the light well, the circles allow natural light to enter from the skylight on the roof. As the sunlight moves across the walls of the light well throughout the day, the spectacular blue lacquer comes to life.

A large, custom-designed installation by Raúl Sánchez Architects runs from the kitchen to the living room, organizing and defining the spaces. Most of it matches the color of the walls, but it transitions from pale-yellow cabinets in the kitchen to lacquered wood in the living room. Ceramic pieces by Catalina Montaña (@hellobigmontana).

A stone countertop with subtle yellow veining plays off the chick-yellow fronts of the kitchen cabinets. The 9091 kettle is by Richard Sapper (Alessi). Skan pendant light (Vibia).

At the foot of the staircase leading to Calle Granollers, the mirrored door to the bathroom offers a unique perspective on the living area and the skylight.

The first floor, which is a compact 431 square feet, is designed as an open space with living and dining areas, a small office, and a kitchen. At its center is the deep-blue light well, a graphic element that punctuates the mostly neutral color scheme. A folding screen made of wood and rice paper is a special feature for one of the owners, who once lived in Japan; it can either connect or separate the office from the living area. A small powder room is positioned at a 45-degree angle so the door can’t be seen from the main living area. Near the Aiguafreda entrance, a kitchen island, also placed on a diagonal, is clad in mirrors, helping to create a sense of lightness and maintaining the open feeling and flow of the space.

A view of the living area from the small office. White and terra-cotta dominate, accented by splashes of color. In the foreground is a Kyoto table by Gianfranco Frattini (Poltrona Frau).

The house is rooted in the history of both its neighborhood and the specific alley where it is located, and the chosen materials engage in a dialogue with the past. On the first floor, terra-cotta tiles echo the traditional clay paving found in the home’s outdoor space. Colorful glazed tiles adorn the sinks and bathroom walls, with reddish-tone lacquers and paints recalling the clay soil long found in nearby orchards. The glossy blue color and circular perforations of the skylight elicit water droplets from a washing well.

The light filtering through the circular openings of the blue light well casts a glow on the vanity, which is partially clad in red ceramic tiles that cover the countertop and extend up to the ceiling.

The Dioscuri wall sconces (Artemide) blend right in with the play of colors and both the round and rectangular shapes in the hallway leading to the bathroom.

“The mirror-like surfaces of the doors and the kitchen island, with their slight distortions, evoke the reflections on the surface of the water,” says Sánchez. “Above all, the renovation has made it possible to preserve all the elements that give the home its character and history while also integrating contemporary, energy-efficient systems.”

The light well, painted entirely in blue, is at once architectural, functional, and playful.

The home’s green installations include an aerothermal system for air-conditioning, heating, and hot water; a new floor slab on the first floor to limit indoor humidity; enhanced thermal insulation in the roof; and improvements to the thermal and acoustic performance of the façades and shared walls. In addition, the skylight opens to take advantage of a thermal stack effect, expelling hot air in the summer while capturing heat in the winter–transforming the project’s central design element into a system that is both functional and contributes to the home’s environmental sustainability. The interior renovation is only the first phase of the project: Sánchez is currently redesigning the narrow garden, a plot that’s 13 feet wide by 98 feet long.

A view into the light well, which provides illumination and ventilation.

A view of the hallway and staircase from the primary bedroom, whose entryway is painted red, setting the stage for the entrance into the room. Extra-large, veined-wood cabinet handles adorn the full-height cabinet fronts. In the foreground, a Reno chair by Federico Correa and Alfonso Milá from 1961. The Dioscuri wall sconce is by Artemide.

We walk between six-foot high dry lay stone fences, and later on a sunken path, worn down by this pilgrimage. I admire hórreos of every make: stone, brick, wooden, and a mix. They seem randomly dropped more than thoughtfully placed, at odd angles with what is around them. I know that many have been here since the 13th century, but neither they nor the landscape yield to one another. Hórreos evolved to dry millet until the arrival of the New World crop: corn. Galicia’s climatic peculiarities prevent grain from completely drying in the field. Done mechanically today, in years past the ears were shucked and stored whole to allow air to pass through, to cool and dry the grain to prevent spoilage and fermentation.

In an information sheet I found at tonight’s hostel, it reads:

In 1973, a Decree was approved by the Spanish state to protect all the old granaries and huts existing in Galicia and Asturias, which attempted to address the consequences of the abandonment of the rural way of life and the loss of use of the granaries.

I guess I won’t be transporting any hórreos home to my backyard.

We’re in pulpo territory, and find pulperías and copper cauldrons smoking away outside roadside bars. The Galician preparation for octopus is smoked paprika, sea salt, and a splash of olive oil, all served on a seven-inch wooden plate and eaten with toothpicks. Suzanne largely doesn’t partake because the octopus is known to be an intelligent sea creature; I’d feel bad too if they weren’t so tasty. Kinda like bacon—if they are so smart, then why so tasty? It is a tragic, delicious bargain we’ve made with nature’s smartest species. On a lighter note, we’ve stumbled into a soft homemade cheese, queso con miel, that is drizzled with local Galician honey, and the seasonal padrón peppers.

Padrón peppers – fried with olive oil and sprinkled with course salt.

I walk with David and his mother Maxine, from Australia. She is a 12-month cancer survivor, and as David tells the story, when she was at her lowest… she interjects “so exhausted I couldn’t even hold a cell phone” David pitched walking the Camino. That was 18 months ago, and here they are. I think back to other Aussies I’ve met, and each seemed to be recovering from a major health crisis.

Then, there was Fritz, born in Nebraska, but sent away to boarding school at a young age and never returned, living abroad and traveling the world in the business of finance. He tells me a different side of the story of how the modern Camino came to be. His friend was named Manuel Fraga Iribarne, who in the mid-1990s was the regional governor of Galicia, then one of the poorest, most isolated agricultural regions of Spain. At that time, the Camino was a quiet trail mostly walked by devout Catholics, historians, or avid hikers. (When they invent time travel, this is where I’m returning, to walk the Camino of those days). Working with Father Elías (the priest of O Cebreiro), who was the spiritual father and painted the yellow arrows to guide the way, Fraga had the political and marketing know-how to rebuild the infrastructure we enjoy today. One hears of Father Elías in Catholic stories, but not Manuel Fraga Iribarne. Thank you, Fritz.

This is his eighth Camino, and he is reluctantly walking it because it is a graduation requirement for the two-week course he took in Santiago. Fritz has been so interesting to listen to and walk with, but I recognize in him that, like myself, much of our success has been given to us through the privilege of the stations our parents gave us. As men, much of how we see ourselves is through our work, and Fritz, David, and the other unhappy conservative I walked with are all now retired. Their importance and reason for being is gone, and I think they are looking for something that replaces what they have lost. SH*T, I think to myself, this is the third man I’ve walked with that turns out to be angry and older—what is she, The Camino, trying to teach me?

He has seen the world and chooses to be angry. I see his unwavering support of the current administration being driven more out of retribution for the direction the country was going than any hope or vision for its future. If our country was a garden and overrun by weeds, his solution would be to drown it in Roundup and pave it over. No more weeds. My Dad would say that weeds are just misplaced plants and can have a lot to say diagnostically about the health of the soil. The goal isn’t to keep a weed-free garden, but to produce food, knowing that for the health of the soil, weeds must coexist.

So I walk with Fritz until I can’t. Yes, he is a fast walker, unencumbered, and fresh on the Camino, but I tire of his raw anger, in such contrast to the Anglican faith he professes. My exit comes when he sees his pickup van in case he is ready to stop walking. He goes over to check in and I, who have no pickup van, sit and rest. Fritz continues on, walking over the hill and out of my Camino never to be seen by me again.

GTA 6: How To Permanently Increase All Stats

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

There are three main stats you'll need to pay attention to throughout your Grand Theft Auto 6 playthrough: Strength, Cardio, and Focus. Jason and Lucia each have their own individual stats, which play an important role in everything from surviving intense shootouts to escaping the police and clearing heat on foot.

Strength determines your maximum health, Cardio governs your stamina, and Focus dictates how long you can slow down time during combat. Improving all three will make for a much smoother playthrough, especially as missions become more challenging later on. While certain activities provide temporary benefits, there are also ways to permanently increase each of these stats in GTA 6, making it well worth investing time in both characters outside the main story.

You can check each character's current stats by opening their individual profiles in the Jason & Lucia menu, while the Fitness app on your phone allows you to track your workout progress. Each of the three main stats can be improved through different activities, though neglecting your Strength and Cardio workout windows will eventually cause you to lose some of your progress.

However, certain side missions and Easter eggs in GTA 6 reward you with permanent increases to your maximum health, stamina, or Focus. Unlike the gains you make at the gym, these bonuses don't require regular maintenance to retain. Most of these encounters aren't marked on your map, and there's almost no indication that completing them will improve your stats. The following are all the currently reported encounters that offer permanent stat increases:

One of the more bizarre ways to permanently increase your maximum health in GTA 6 involves participating in a satanic ritual. Head to the northern area of Port Gellhorn between midnight and 4 AM, where you'll find two metalheads hanging out inside an open garage illuminated by red lights.

Approach them while you're alone and agree to accompany them in their van. You'll eventually arrive at a campfire, which you'll need to stare into to trigger a sequence that transports you to an unholy realm. Make your way through the encounter and defeat Satan to receive a permanent increase to your maximum health. You'll also be rewarded with the unique Hellspawn Comshot 12 shotgun, a new tattoo, and a Circles of Hell shirt.

There are several different ways to permanently increase your maximum stamina in GTA 6, making Cardio one of the easiest stats to improve without having to constantly worry about getting to the gym.

To initiate "The Final Flush," head to the men's public restroom on Corsair Key and look for the Stop! Don't Flush poster. Scan the QR code using the WAiNK It app on your phone and respond to the subsequent message with "TRUTH."

You'll eventually arrange a nighttime meeting with Dr. Merle at Frank's Waffles in Tequesta, after which you'll need to accompany him to a sewage treatment facility. Once you've completed Merle's rather unfortunate experiment, make sure you enter the chamber yourself. Doing so will reward you with a permanent increase to your maximum stamina.

Starting in Chapter 2, you'll be able to find a "Pilot Wanted" flyer outside the Starlet Motel office, which introduces you to Kyle McKittrick and his farming operation. Pursue Kyle's questline until you've completed the "Collect Chemicals for Kyle" mission.

Kyle will eventually offer you the opportunity to sample one of his enormous, chemically enhanced strawberries. Make sure you accept his offer, as eating the strawberry will permanently increase your maximum stamina. You'll be able to return to the farm and eat additional strawberries for temporary benefits, but you won't receive another permanent stat increase from doing so.

Head to the construction site along the eastern shore of Lake Tallaloko, just north of Millport, where you'll find an open orange shipping container. Inside the container is a mysterious object labeled "???" that you can interact with to consume a mushroom. Doing so will trigger an elaborate hallucination involving a surreal platforming sequence. You'll need to navigate a series of obstacles and floating platforms to reach the end of the course.

Successfully completing the obstacle course will permanently increase your maximum stamina, and you'll also unlock the Mind Maze Left Leg Award tattoo.

While exploring the wooded areas east of Port Gellhorn, you may come across a group of men participating in a shooting competition. They'll initially invite you to demonstrate your shooting skills by hitting various targets before offering you a $250 wager on an iguana hunting competition.

Accept the wager and shoot more iguanas than your opponent before the timer expires. Winning the competition has been reported to reward players with a permanent increase to their maximum Focus, in addition to the cash prize.

One of the more elaborate mysteries in GTA 6 involves investigating the assassination of President Joe Russo. Following the various clues will eventually lead you to a secret underground government facility in northern Mariana County.

Inside the facility, you'll be able to unlock a room containing a television and a chair. Sitting in the chair triggers a strange sequence set inside a hospital, where you'll need to solve a series of puzzles involving manipulating time.

Complete the sequence and its final confrontation with President Russo to receive a permanent increase to your maximum Focus, as well as a new tattoo. Make sure to collect the unique Brainwasher 1922 pistol near the television when you return to the bunker.

You can also substantially improve your regular Focus stat by participating in shooting range challenges. One of the quickest ways to do this is to visit Rodgers Ranch & Shooting Range near Port Gellhorn, purchase the $5 waiver, and repeatedly complete the Market Range Beginner challenge.

Other recreational activities, such as fishing, playing pool, and golfing, will also contribute toward your Focus progression, though these aren't the same as the one-time maximum Focus upgrades awarded through hidden encounters.

Arguably the most worthwhile side quest you can pursue in GTA 6 involves investigating the UFO encounter near the Starlet Motel. Starting in Chapter 2, head to the wooded area east of Port Gellhorn at around 2 AM and look for an abandoned vehicle with its lights flashing.

Follow the trail leading away from the vehicle, where you'll encounter a woman being abducted by a UFO. Completing this initial encounter will eventually result in your own abduction, after which you'll need to sleep at a safehouse until you experience a strange dream aboard an alien spacecraft. Successfully completing the entire questline will reward you with permanent increases to your maximum health, stamina, and Focus, making it one of the most rewarding optional encounters to complete early in your GTA 6 playthrough.
