The Comfy Pants BA Editors Are Wearing to Thanksgiving

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Travel  |  Read Source →

Thanksgiving is my favorite holiday, not an uncommon feeling around the BA offices. I love the planning, the days (yes: days plural) spent preparing the feast in the kitchen alongside my mom, the gathering of loved ones around a table to reflect, savor, and enjoy. There is one thing, however, that I don’t love: The moment after dinner and before dessert in which I become so full that I must excuse myself and retire to the couch, where I clutch a pillow to my stomach until I’ve digested enough to reemerge.

As somewhat of an expert in feeling Too Full and Bloated™, I can attest that this feeling is made infinitely worse when I’m wearing rigid, tight-waisted pants. Perhaps you can relate? While stretchy sweatpants are an obvious solution here, there are only so many meal scenarios in which it’s socially acceptable to wear them. For those other occasions—the holiday feasts, celebratory meals out, and thousand-course tasting menu dinners—I’ve made it my mission to fill my wardrobe with pants that look polished and feel comfortable, no matter how many helpings of stuffing or “fries for the table” I’ve had. Here are my tried and true favorite pairs for eating big meals in, along with my fellow food-loving colleagues’ go-tos.

I was a die-hard, high-waisted pants gal for the majority of my life. Then I tried Still Here’s Everyday Jeans. A true mid-rise, they hit just above my hip bones and about an inch below my belly button, hugging my body without digging in, even after a holiday dinner or multi-course restaurant meal. The non-stretch denim is soft but sturdy, and the relaxed leg is structured without feeling at all rigid. I own them in Washed Black and Classic Blue and wear them constantly, to everything from casual weeknight dinners (paired with sneakers and a white tee) to weekend date nights (with kitten heels and a silk top). They’ve even become my go-to Friendsgiving uniform in recent years because of how comfortable they are to cook, eat, and host in.

If you haven’t already heard of Rag & Bone’s Miramar line, let me put you on: these pants look like real denim, but are actually made from soft cotton terry. It’s all thanks to the brand’s printing technique, which allows them to digitally print details like weave, stitching, buttons, and pockets onto any fabric. Ever since I got my first pair, I’ve been delighted every time I receive a compliment on my jeans and can reveal that they’re actually—gasp—sweatpants! I’ve owned the Sofie style for a few years now, which have real pockets and a true button fly that give them a more polished look; but my recent obsession are the Sport Stripe Track Pants, which have a flattering wide leg cut and a fully elastic waistband that makes them ideal for multi-course meals.

I invested in a pair of poplin pants from Suzie Kondi this summer and they’ve quickly become my go-to attire whenever I want the comfort of an elastic waistband while still looking put-together. The low-rise waist sits comfortably on my hips and ensures that nothing squeezes my midsection, and the straight leg cut is incredibly flattering. The cotton poplin fabric is breathable and feels quite luxe, and I’ve even noticed that stains come out surprisingly easily (which I learned the hard way by spilling green juice all over myself testing personal blenders). Forever a fan of a monochrome fit, I love pairing mine with the matching Vella shirt.

If you’re looking for a more affordable option, J.Crew’s Luna Pants have gained a well-deserved following on social media this year. They’re just as stylish as the pricier brands, available in several colors, and ring in at under $100.

Many of the above pants feel like sweatpants, but if you’re enjoying a casual dinner, there’s nothing stopping you from wearing actual sweatpants—particularly if you opt for an elevated pair that ditches the typical brushed fleece for cashmere or velour in a straight leg cut. Senior reviews editor Olivia Tarantino and I are both fans of Quince’s Mongolian Cashmere Straight Leg Pants for their ultra-soft feel and flattering cut. If you’re looking to upgrade to something more luxe, Hendricks vouches for the cashmere Everywhere Pant from Guest in Residence: “They’re THE pants for when you can't fathom wearing actual pants. They're comfortable but structured for sweatpants, and they're super warm. I've had these pants for years and they still feel and look good as new.” I’m also partial to Suzie Kondi’s Lito Pants, which are cut from cozy velour and have a slightly flared leg that looks good on everyone. While I don’t own these myself, I steal my mom’s pair every time I’m home to visit (sorry, Mom!).

100% Washable Silk Drawstring Wide Leg Pants

For dressier dinners, I like to opt for a pair of slinky satin pants. The elastic waistband and flowy fabric ensures they stay comfy even after the meal is said and done, while the shine and elegant drape make them easy to dress up. Executive audience development director Casey Hendricks calls La Ligne’s Colby Pants her “everything pants.” She says she wears them for “work, errands, eating, airport, and everything in between. They're so comfortable but also so chic—they make me feel effortlessly put together.” I also love Quince’s 100% Washable Silk Drawstring Wide Leg Pants, which have an impressive sub-$100 price tag and are (as the name suggests) magically machine-washable.

Long gone are the days where I've been an athleisure-on-the-plane kind of girl. In this job (and in life in general), I've started to prefer clothes that are both comfortable and acceptable to wear to a restaurant meal straight from the plane. Enter the Reformation Olina Linen Pants. They're perhaps the only pants that I'd wear on an 11-hour travel day straight into a few-hour-long dinner, and in fact, I have. The linen gives them an elevated look, but the drawstring waist and just-soft-enough fabric make them easy to sit in for a long while. I'm tempted to order them in every color, but my singular pair has lasted many trips, many meals, and many washes thus far. —Kate Kassin, editorial operations manager

As an adult man approaching 40, I want a pair of pants just wide enough to convey I’m in the know without going full MC Hammer. Enter the Uniqlo pleated, relaxed wide pants. With an elastic, drawstring waistband and beltloops, I get to dine out free from navel constrictions and still wear a smart belt. I can go from the office to the bistro to the couch without sacrificing comfort or functionality. To you the casual observer though, I look like I’m wearing slacks. With the loose, high-waisted fit, these pants drape beautifully with a pair of black leather penny loafers. They’re made of 67 percent recycled polyester, which feels virtuous and breezy. —Gabe Hiatt, senior restaurants editor

I am content with my daily uniform of selvedge jeans, leather shoes, and a dark t-shirt or sweater depending on the weather. It's not too formal for working in a kitchen, but acceptable should I find myself in an elevator with someone from Vogue. But running around town all day, with the occasional epic dinner out, means a constant trade-off between style and comfort. Whoever designed 100% cotton jeans for railroad workers and miners just didn't consider the rigors of a 3-hour meal followed by a long train ride home. So lately I have been quietly upgrading my look with Naked and Famous Denim's stretch selvedge jeans. They contain 2% elastane (perhaps you know it as Spandex), just enough to provide a forgiving fit, but with the look and patina of raw denim. They stay conformable enough all day that you can simply ignore them, and think about more important things: like dinner. —Chris Morocco, food director

There is no worse feeling than the cold, hard button of a stiff jean pressing against your full stomach after a particularly big meal. (Who designed jeans anyway?). That's why for Thanksgiving there won't be a firm, oppressive waistband in sight. I already know I'll be donning my Le Bon Shoppe Arc Pants not only for their loose barrel legged silhouette, but for their forgiving, elasticated waist band. Namely, more pie for me, without the feeling of “pants” up against my indulgent belly. Pair with a cashmere festive sweater (preferably oversized) and you can go from the dinner table straight to the couch for your turkey-induced coma in both style and comfort. —June Kim, executive editor

As someone who’s no stranger to a mid-meal top-button release, I’ve developed a deep appreciation for an elastic waistband. Even better when it comes in a pair of pants I’m excited to wear. My current favorite: J.Crew’s Luna pants in lightweight corduroy. Think sweatpants disguised as corduroys, with plenty of room for big Thanksgiving ambitions while still making you feel put together. Honestly, what more could you ask of a pant?

You can dress them up with a sweater and loafers or keep it casual with a T-shirt and sneakers. I’ve worn them so often that I’m already plotting a second pair in a different color. My one caveat: they tend to run a little big, so I’d suggest sizing down. —Gabby Fowler, social media director

Tottenham: Roberto De Zerbi considered surprise Luca Williams-Barnett role for rare Chelsea win

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Travel  |  Read Source →

Your weekly briefing on Tottenham, featuring team news and expert analysis from Sam Tabuteau

I would like to be emailed about offers, event and updates from Evening Standard. Read our privacy notice.

Roberto De Zerbi has revealed he thought about starting teenage sensation Luca Williams-Barnett ahead of Tottenham’s historic victory at Chelsea last month.

Williams-Barnett has been the name on everyone’s lips since his scintillating Premier League debut against Crystal Palace last weekend, nearly grabbing an assist with a superb run and cross for Mohammed Kudus.

The 18-year-old has, however, had to watch as his England contemporaries Rio Mgumoha and Max Dowman have been given ample opportunities to impress, with De Zerbi admitting he had been reluctant to risk him while Spurs struggled for results.

Spurs were winless after the first six league games of this season, but have begun to pick up results in the last few weeks and are looking to make it four wins in a row for the first time in more than 1,000 days when they travel to Leeds in the early kick-off on Saturday afternoon.

De Zerbi feels he has a responsibility to look after Williams-Barnett, who he has previously said has the right blend of arrogance and confidence to be a top player, and admits he thought better of throwing him in against Chelsea, even though he believed he was ready to start the game.

“To find the time is my responsibility because when you manage such young players, with such a big talent like Luca, it is a bigger responsibility for me, and he has to be ready when he goes on the pitch,” De Zerbi said in his pre-Leeds press conference on Friday afternoon.

“I think it (does) not depend on the result, he can play at the beginning. Before the Chelsea game, I thought to play with him in the first XI but at the end you have to find the right moment for them also.”

Williams-Barnett, shortlisted for October’s Creative Moment of the Month by the Premier League for his dazzling bit of skill against Palace, has long been thought of as one of the most exciting talents to come out of Spurs’ academy since Ballon d’Or winner Harry Kane.

His minutes, though, have been limited to two substitute appearances and a total of 14 minutes this season despite his promotion to the first-team.

De Zerbi is aware of Williams-Barnett’s potential, acknowledging that he knew he had a special player on his hands as early as pre-season, and with Spurs finding form, he now feels more open to using him more frequently.

Weezer: Photos From the Billboard Cover Shoot

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Travel  |  Read Source →

Check out new pics of the legendary alt-rockers ahead of their Billboard Live Music Summit appearance.

Rivers Cuomo is reclining in a zero-gravity chair in a musty, windowless room deep within Tampa, Fla.’s Benchmark Arena — baseball cap on his head, a copy of Cormac McCarthy’s Blood Meridian and a pair of headphones beside him. Nearby, there’s a keyboard, a small drum kit and an acoustic guitar that his Weezer bandmate, drummer Patrick Wilson, periodically picks up and noodles on; a jar of peanut butter; a printed, official-looking setlist on a spreadsheet; and a paper bag labeled “clarinet books” in marker.

It’s flummoxing to consider that in just a few hours, this soft-spoken iteration of the Weezer frontman will be channeling the power-pop gods onstage for thousands of screaming fans. But this is Cuomo in power-saving mode, restoring the energy he burned onstage the night prior outside of Fort Lauderdale and didn’t quite recover on the four-hour overnight bus ride across the Sunshine State.

Yet at precisely 9:12 p.m. on this October night — just like every show night this tour — the lights will go down for Weezer’s headlining set, and Cuomo will transform. This is the precise time that, like clockwork, the lights have gone down for every show on the Weezer: The Gathering Tour, and it’s the time they will continue to go down for its remainder; if all goes as planned, Weezer will leave the stage at 10:53 p.m. on the dot.

A prerecorded video plays, setting up the show’s concept: A kid makes a wish for Weezer to play at his house, and when the quartet receives the magical transmission, it links up to answer the call. Finally, after the clip rolls, one of the great American rock bands strides onto the stage — which is decked out with a fake house, fence, yard and power lines to resemble the home from the video.

“Dearly beloved, we’re gathered here tonight,” Cuomo declares into his mic as the band revs up, “to celebrate a thing called Weezer!”

Koopmeiners scores at former club as Juventus beat AZ 2-1 despite trailing on expected goals

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Travel  |  Read Source →

Juventus won 2-1 at AZ Alkmaar on a night when Teun Koopmeiners faced his old club, even though the hosts dominated the expected-goals count. The result strengthens their league-phase position ahead of Sunday's Serie A trip to Fiorentina.

The trip to Alkmaar returned Teun Koopmeiners to his former club. His brother Peer was in the AZ side that Juventus then held off. UEFA picked out the family meeting in its matchday 4 round-up.

Kolo Muani supplied the pass for the opener. Nick Woltemade was credited with the assist for the second goal. Kolo Muani earned a rating of 8.61 from the data provider, with Koopmeiners on 8.2.

AZ pulled one back in the 88th minute. Mexx Meerdink scored from an Elijah Dijkstra assist. The hosts could not find an equaliser in the remaining minutes.

The home side had 56% of possession. They completed 523 passes to Juventus’s 378. Eleven of AZ’s attempts were blocked, against three of Juventus’s.

Both teams put three shots on target. Vicario made two saves for Juventus. AZ’s expected goals total points to the chances the hosts created without converting them.

The figures are consistent with a Juventus side defending a first-half lead. Spalletti’s team committed 20 fouls to AZ’s 13. Andrea Cambiaso, Jeff Ekhator and Juan Cabal were booked for Juventus. Wesley Hoedt was the only AZ player shown a yellow card. Damian Sylwestrzak refereed the match.

The victory was Spalletti’s second in a row in all competitions, following the league win over Napoli. It came after a run that included a 0-2 defeat to Genoa and a 0-3 loss to Lazio. Koopmeiners now has four goals for Juventus in all competitions in 2026, one more than the three he scored for the club in 2025, Sky Sport Italia noted.

For AZ, the result was a third straight defeat in all competitions. Losses to Ajax and Twente came before Thursday’s game.

This season’s league phase has 36 clubs, and each plays eight different opponents. It ends on Thursday 28 January 2027. Juventus host Omonia Nicosia on 26 November at 21:00 CET, while AZ travel to Sparta Praha. Juventus then visit Hapoel Beer-Sheva on 10 December and Ferencváros on 21 January 2027, both at 18:45 CET. They finish at home to Real Sociedad on 28 January 2027 at 21:00 CET.

Serie A comes first. Juventus travel to Fiorentina for matchday 11 on Sunday 8 November, with kick-off at 20:45. AZ host Groningen in the Eredivisie on the same day.

Tesla's Kickflipping Roadster Looks Like A Great Way To Lose Your Lunch

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Travel  |  Read Source →

Browse this and many other websites without banner ads, personalized tracking and video ads.

Car companies have spent the last 140 years figuring out how to perfect ground travel. Tesla decided that winning that war was unimaginative at best. And as we've all seen by now, the carmaker instead took its next-gen Roadster to the sky last month to demonstrate what it might look like to own a flying car one day.

But what would it actually be like to drive (fly?) the thing? This week, Tesla released a video showing a first-person camera view of the Roadster executing its rad kick-flip maneuver and other stunts. It does not look pleasant to experience firsthand.

If you watched the Roadster unveiling last month, this all probably looks familiar. It's the same stunts we saw that day, including the upside-down dive off of the half-pipe and the jump-spin over the Tesla Semi. But seeing it from the car's perspective adds a whole new layer to it, and shows this dizzying experience a person would have from the cockpit.

Watching the cold gas thrusters at work up close during the kickflip gives you a sense of how much goes into ensuring that the car stays upright. The car adjusts the volume of nitrogen that it releases in order to prevent car from over-rotating and transferring from a barrel roll to an out-of-control corkscrew. Just one simple nudge and it lands back on all four wheels.

Then there's the nosedive off of the half-pipe. A bit more correcting to keep the roll and yaw in check—but the Roadster does it without a hiccup.

All of these amazing stunts happened without a driver; Tesla put a dummy in the front seat. Many actual human beings might feel dizzy or even sick from these kinds of tricks.

Tesla hasn't published what kind of g-forces the kickflip generates, but watching the onboard footage also makes me wonder what kind of fighter pilot-style harness and big bolsters are needed to keep the driver in-place versus the road-going version of the car. Heck, you might need to be a fighter pilot to endure these maneuvers.

It's really hard not to appreciate how much engineering went into making the demo happen. Sure, it takes hours to fill up the 600-liter ultra-high-pressure composite-overwrapped pressure vessels with 10,000 PSI of nitrogen for just a few seconds of joy. But man, is it cool to watch from just about any perspective.

And while Tesla can't guarantee that the flying version of its car will reach a single driveway (or be road legal, for that matter), it's still a very cool, very over-engineered party trick unlike anything we'll likely see from a car manufacturer again. If you're going to buy one and put it through its paces, though, I'd recommend having a light lunch.

Aniioki A9 Pro Max review: Basically an electric motorcycle that you can pedal

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Travel  |  Read Source →

There are electric bikes, there are electric motorcycles, and then there are machines like the Aniioki A9 Pro Max that plant a flag somewhere in the increasingly large no-man’s-land between the two.

Technically, it has pedals. You can pedal it fairly effectively too, with the actually adjustable seat. You can even pedal it for fun, which is more than I can say for some of the increasingly motorcycle-shaped “e-bikes” I’ve tested lately.

But let’s get something out of the way right at the beginning: this isn’t a street-legal electric bicycle in the US. Not when we’re talking about dual motors producing up to 6,000W of peak power and speeds of around 45 mph.

And you know what? That’s fine. As long as we’re all honest about what this thing is.

The Aniioki A9 Pro Max is absolutely massive, and the spec sheet makes that abundantly clear.

The version I tested uses a pair of 2,500W direct-drive hub motors, one in each wheel, with Aniioki claiming 3,000W peak power from each motor. That gives the bike a combined peak figure of 6,000W and up to 250 Nm of combined torque. It’s hard to overstate how massive that is.

Then there’s the battery. You can choose between a 60V 70Ah pack with 4.2 kWh of capacity or an even larger 60V 80Ah pack packing 4.8 kWh. For comparison, many typical commuter e-bikes have somewhere around 500-700 Wh of battery. The smaller battery here is roughly six to eight times that size. That’s absolutely in the territory of legit light electric motorcycles.

Aniioki claims up to 230+ miles (370 km) of pedal-assist range, though as usual, you should take maximum e-bike range figures as best-case numbers. Using the throttle and enjoying those big motors is going to dramatically reduce that figure. Even so, with more than 4 kWh of battery underneath you, range anxiety isn’t exactly the first thing that comes to mind.

All that hardware comes with a predictable penalty: weight. The 70Ah model tips the scales at around 188 lb (85 kg), while the 80Ah version approaches 193 lb (87.5 kg). Those aren’t e-bike weights. Those are motorcycle weights. Again, you can see the direction this is going.

Here’s where the A9 Pro Max gets more interesting to me.

A lot of high-powered e-motos include pedals seemingly as a regulatory fig leaf. They’re technically there, but the geometry is so bad that nobody in their right mind would want to pedal the thing farther than across a parking lot. Even some smaller and presumably Class 2 legal e-bikes that have moto-style frames are nearly unpedalable for actual riding over any reasonable distance.

But the A9 here is actually pedalable thanks to having something that more closely resembles a bicycle seat, plus adjustability that lets riders dial in the correct seat height.

The A9 Pro Max feels more like a strange combination of an oversized fat-tire e-bike and a small electric motorcycle. There’s a Shimano 7-speed drivetrain and a huge 72-tooth chainring, meaning the pedals aren’t entirely ornamental.

I actually enjoyed pedaling it with the pedal assist engaged. You’re obviously not going to mistake a nearly 200-pound machine rolling on enormous 26 x 4.8-inch tires for a lightweight fitness bike, but there’s something fun about contributing some human power while two giant hub motors do most of the heavy lifting.

And when you don’t feel like pedaling, there’s a full-twist throttle waiting for you.

That combination is really the appeal here. You can ride it more like an e-bike when the mood strikes, or twist the throttle and treat it more like a lightweight e-moto.

And again, I don’t want to beat a dead horse here, but remember: the pedals don’t make this an electric bicycle. It’s still an e-moto, but it’s one that you can actually have fun pedaling. You can treat it like a mech suit, where pedaling translates into crazy power, as if you have super-human legs. And with the insanely large 72t chainring, pedaling at higher speeds is actually doable. Though I’ll tell you right now, pedaling at 40 mph (64 km/h) is not exactly a normal or natural feeling.

And it’s worth pointing out that at a starting price of US $2,899 (or more for the larger battery capacities), that’s a fairly reasonable price compared to most e-motos that can be more limiting in how and where you can ride them.

Fortunately, Aniioki seems to understand that putting this much power on two wheels requires more serious components than the average bicycle.

The braking setup is particularly wild. There are two massive 300mm brake rotors on the front wheel and another 300mm rotor out back, along with a linked CBS braking system. The front uses custom eight-piston calipers (the pair of quad-piston calipers) while the rear gets a custom six-piston setup.

There’s also an adjustable air-suspension fork with 110mm of travel, rear suspension to smooth out bumps at higher speeds, integrated lighting and turn signals, and a large five-inch display that even incorporates features like Bluetooth audio and a recording camera.

That brings us to the elephant in the room.

In the US, consumer-product rules generally define a low-speed electric bicycle around a motor of less than 750W and a maximum motor-powered speed below 20 mph (32 km/h), while individual states use their own operating laws that typically match the familiar Class 1/2/3 framework. A 6,000W machine capable of around 45 mph (72 km/h) blows straight through normal limits.

So please don’t see pedals and assume this belongs on your local bicycle path.

Where and how you can legally operate something like the A9 Pro Max depends on your local laws and whether it can qualify under another vehicle category. For many owners, the sensible place to enjoy its full performance is going to be private property or other areas where such vehicles are permitted.

That caveat doesn’t make the bike itself bad. It just means buyers need to understand what they’re buying.

And judged on what it actually is rather than what the word “e-bike” on the product page suggests, I had a lot of fun on it.

There are certainly riders who would look at a nearly 200-pound, 45 mph machine and say, “Why not just buy an electric motorcycle?” That’s a perfectly reasonable question.

But I also understand the appeal of this weird middle ground. The A9 Pro Max retains enough bicycle DNA that you can pedal it around and enjoy pedal assist, yet has enough battery and motor underneath you to deliver something much closer to small-motorcycle performance when you twist the throttle.

Imagine going off-road and getting to fly up hills while pedaling hard, but then being able to switch to throttle once your legs get worn out. Sure, it’s not everyone’s cup of tea, but it’s a cup of tea.

This bike is big. It’s heavy. It’s absurdly powerful by e-bike standards. And it absolutely isn’t the machine I’d recommend for someone looking for a normal street-legal electric bicycle.

But if what you actually want is a big, powerful electric two-wheeler that still lets you turn the pedals when the mood strikes, the Aniioki A9 Pro Max makes a surprisingly compelling argument for this increasingly bizarre category of pedal-equipped e-motos.

These Are the 13 Tactics Expert Whitetail Hunters Actually Use to Tag Mature Bucks

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Travel  |  Read Source →

Published Nov 4, 2026 5:13 PM EST

Get everything from whitetail news and opinion pieces, to advanced hunting tactics and cutting-edge gear reviews.

The best deer hunters in the nation (known and unknown) all share a common thread. They are students of deer and the woods, and they never stop learning. As such, they continually experiment with new and advanced deer hunting tactics.

In order to learn some of these secrets (and to share them with you), I called a handful of the best deer hunters I know. Here’s how they find success season after season.

Mark Drury of Drury Outdoors has bagged more big deer than most. His approach is proven, and the process he uses includes saving and analyzing a lot of trail camera data, especially historical information. He has kept every single trail cam pic of a racked buck since 2007.

“Save trail monitoring pictures in an organized fashion for easy review in future years,” Drury says. “Bucks are incredibly habitual from year to year. Study your pics year-round to formulate patterns with individual bucks or areas. [Over time you’ll build] a database that helps lead to patterns in future years, as crop rotations, weather, and moon in future seasons mirror conditions that occurred in the past. It takes a lot of time to assemble and study but saves time in future seasons.”

Grant Woods of Growing Deer TV is one of the most renowned deer scientists in the country. He seems to get inside the mind of whitetails and understands what they do and why they do it. To that point, he stresses making hunting decisions based on what deer do, and not what we think they should do.

“An issue I’ve seen is hunters making setup and tactical decisions based on human thoughts,” Woods says. “The hunters I know that have repeated success filling tags in all types of conditions and habitats always think like a deer. They consider what deer need that day, and what will deer avoid.”

As Paul Beasley, co-host of “Canada in the Rough” TV, says, hunters are creatures of habit. “The longer we hunt a piece of ground, the more established our hunting setups and entry/access/travel points become,” he says. “We tend to rely more on what has worked before, and while deer in general also tend to gravitate to the same trails, food sources, and pinch points, it has been my experience that mature bucks need to be treated as individuals and hunted accordingly.

“The buck I shot this year helps illustrate my point. We have hunted this same 100-acre property for over 10 years, and have always taken deer off of it in the same 50-yard-by-80-yard area. Throughout the spring and into summer, this buck was working that area perfectly, but as hunting season approached, his behavior changed. His appearances on camera began to fall off. So, I scrambled to put up more cameras and found that he hadn’t gone anywhere. He was just using different parts of the property.”

Beasley killed that buck on the second afternoon of the season. He arrowed from a setup that his crew had never harvested a buck from before.

“It was through paying attention to this individual buck’s behavior, and not just doing the same old thing that has always worked, that allowed me to put my tag on him,” Beasley says.

Oftentimes deer hunters do things without thinking. We react, rather than making decisions based on logic. HeadHunters TV’s Nate Hosie agrees, and says we should hunt smarter and not harder.

“Growing up, I used to think the more I was out there the better my chances,” he says. “It’s not completely wrong, but as I’ve grown as a hunter, I’ve learned to pick my moments for when to strike. Wind, temperature, time of year, trail cam intel, [and more] now go into my hunt. The less I’m in and out, and the higher probability my attempts are, the more success I find.”

Rick White is a longtime staple in the outdoor industry and he suggests that hunters go all or nothing with scent management.

“One of the biggest things I try to instill in people is always going to the woods as scent-free as possible,” he says. “Always play the wind. Never intentionally hunt a stand on a bad wind. But also know that deer don’t always come in from where we want them to. So, if you’re not going to follow every step with scent elimination, then save your money.”

It sounds like rudimentary advice, but even experienced hunters make blunders that lead to being seen, heard, and smelled by deer. It’s something we must continually keep at the forefront of our mind. Will this entry route cause bedded deer to see me? Will this exit route blow scent into a bedding area?

Bone Collector Co-Host Nick Mundt knows deer hunting as well as anyone. As a former deer hunting guide and current big deer killer, he stresses the importance of going undetected when getting in and out of your hunting spots.

“Never think the wind will just work,” he says. “Always know that they’ll bust you if the wind is wrong. It dictates every sit.”

Aaron Gaines of Ani-Logics advises hunters to kill a target buck as early as possible. The early season and pre-rut offer opportunities to hunt consistent bucks.

“This is when he’s on a pattern and most predictable,” Gaines says. “To do this, I utilize most recent information from cameras. I’m very disciplined on hunting the right wind. If he’s daylighting, and I’ve got the right wind, things usually work out well.”

Read Next: Why the Last Week of October Is the Best Time to Kill a Big Buck

During the rut, whitetail bucks seem to travel via loops. That’s not hearsay or some hokey hack from your grandaddy’s hunting journal. That’s straight from a deer biologist. More specifically, former CEO of the Quality Deer Management Association and current HuntStand biologist, Brian Murphy.

“The biggest piece of advice I’ve learned over the years is to develop a plan and then stick with it long enough for it to have a reasonable chance of success,” Murphy says. “In other words, informed optimism. The hunters who have done their homework and keep a positive attitude, often after multiple dismal outings, are ultimately the most successful. Knowing from research that bucks often take a few days to complete loops within their home range, it’s not surprising that it takes persistence to increase the odds you will get an opportunity.”

Nathan Mrnak, COO of Base Camp Leasing, is a diehard deer hunter. He spends many days in the field each year. He stresses that it’s OK to move off your original plan and make a move.

“Picking out a stand location can be one of the toughest decisions while setting up on a mature whitetail,” he says. “I have had many first sits that ended with my target buck just out of bow range. Seeing his exact route provided the last bit of intel to moving a set just 50 yards to capitalize the next sit. This has worked time and time again in big timber.”

The most basic element of deer hunting is hunting deer where they are. To do that, you need current information.

Own the Season’s Art Helin knows this well. “Hunt where the bucks want to be during the correct phase of the season,” he says. “Food sources during early season, transition areas during the pre-rut (between field and bedding and staging areas), funnels and ridge edges during the rut (all day or mid-day sits especially), and back to food sources late season.”

During the rut, a lot of deer hunters choose to do all-day sits. Whitetail Properties Land Specialist Ben Harshyne does, too. However, he changes things up.

“I grew up hunting all-day sits in one stand,” he says. “Over time, I learned that deep timber and thick cover are action-packed in the morning and afternoon, yet often go cold by evening. In the afternoon, shift focus to hunting cover and edges near food sources or along travel lines leading to food. Not necessarily the actual food edge, but nearby. Does bed near food and still want to feed — and the bucks know it. Few stands are productive all day, so fine-tune your late-day move to target cover that relates to food.”

Big buck killer Jared Mills has arrowed a lot of big whitetails. He says don’t be afraid to be aggressive, but do it at the right time.

“I think most guys tend to be too conservative when hunting a target buck, staying on the fringes and only getting out when the conditions are perfect,” he says. “That’s fine if you have the entire season to hunt, and there’s little chance of him getting killed by a neighbor, but that’s a very rare scenario for most of us. While the windows of opportunity to encounter a target buck are already small enough, we make them even smaller by not getting in there after him. Be smart with your hunting, of course, but don’t sit around waiting for the absolute perfect time to move in, otherwise, you’ll likely miss a chance.”

Finally, just keep hunting. You won’t always kill a deer on opening day. It might just be closing day. So, keep hunting, and don’t give up.

“Have patience and persistence,” Helin concluded. “The entire season can change, and it can all happen in a short minute. Stay with it, and never give up, but also never get lazy. Always follow the same regimen from day No. 1 until the end.”

Permanent Daylight Saving Time Could Send Millions of Kids to School in the Dark

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Travel  |  Read Source →

Permanent daylight saving time could leave some states with roughly ten times as many dark school commutes, sharpening concerns about children’s safety and sleep.

Keeping daylight saving time through the winter would move sunrise an hour later on the clock, leaving children with fixed school schedules to make more trips in the dark. University of Washington researchers estimate that a nationwide shift would add at least 2 billion dark school commutes compared with the current system of switching between daylight saving time and standard time twice a year.

The U.S. House of Representatives passed a bill this past summer that would make daylight saving time (DST) permanent, ending the fall return to standard time. Nineteen states, including Washington, have already preapproved the change, which would take effect in those states if the bill is signed into law. The legislation is stalled in the U.S. Senate, with school start times a central issue because mornings would be darker from November through March.

The study, published October 29 in Current Biology, examined both school schedules and U.S. traffic collision data. The collision analysis found that a fatal crash involving a school-aged pedestrian was roughly twice as likely to occur during a dark morning as after sunrise.

Morning light also helps set the circadian clock, the body’s internal timing system, which helps regulate mood, sleep, and appetite. Without that light, the body has more difficulty regulating these functions.

“When we send kids to school in the dark, we are depriving them from an opportunity to get that morning daylight,” said lead author Guadalupe Rodríguez Ferrante, a UW postdoctoral scholar in biology. “We were asking what’s the cost for these children. We wanted actual numbers, a real number of kids based on the geographic location of each school.”

Horacio de la Iglesia, UW professor of biology, discusses how permanent Daylight Saving Time will affect commutes for school children. Credit: Sarah McQuate/University of Washington

For 14 of the 19 states that had preapproved permanent DST, the researchers combined school start times, enrollment data, and latitude and longitude measurements. They calculated how many days each child would travel to school in the dark, then added those trips together to compare permanent DST with the current system.

All 14 states would see an increase. Washington would have almost 35 million additional dark trips to school, while Georgia would have the largest increase among the states studied, at 71 million.

Communities on the western edge of their time zones, such as Forks, Washington, would be particularly affected, as would those with early school start times. In Mississippi and Alabama, schools start before 8 a.m. on average. Each state would go from about 2 million dark school commutes under the current system to more than 20 million under permanent DST.

“Some districts probably never experience a dark commute under the current system, and, if this passes, suddenly they will wake up to this new reality that their kids are going to school in the dark,” Rodríguez Ferrante said. “Keep in mind that we used the actual start time listed for each school, and a lot of schools offer breakfast or other things before school, so the kids could be getting there even earlier.”

The nationwide estimate came from extrapolating the results from the 14 states to the rest of the country, with an assumption that makes the total likely conservative.

“For the estimation, we assumed students would be leaving home around 8 a.m.,” Rodríguez Ferrante said. “That’s pretty late, so our calculation is likely an underestimation of the real number.”

“It’s called the ‘Sunshine Protection Act’ because you get an extra hour of light at the end of the day. But you will take that hour from the morning. You could call it the Sunshine Destruction Act,” said senior author Horacio de la Iglesia, a UW professor of biology. “These dark mornings will inequitably affect children who have to go to school at a certain time. It’s a very tangible metric that anybody from any state or socioeconomic background can relate to. Nobody wants their kids to go to school in the dark.”

The researchers also argue that permanent DST would undo the benefits of delayed start times for high school students. Those later starts are intended to accommodate teenagers’ circadian clocks, which are shifted compared with those of younger children and adults.

“I’ve heard the argument, ‘If we’re in permanent DST, we can just delay the school start time to address these issues,'” de la Iglesia said. “Well, if we do that, you will end up going to work later. And everything is going to get delayed. You will get out of work an hour later, and you will miss the opportunity to enjoy your evening light. And essentially, we’re just going to be realigning everybody to solar time. That, by definition, is Standard Time.”
