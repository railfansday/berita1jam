Sci-Fi Cult Classic From 41 Years Ago Launches Kickstarter Campaign For Long-Awaited Sequel

📅 Nov 22, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Ryan Northrup is a Senior Staff Writer on ScreenRant's Movie & TV News team, where he has authored nearly 5,000 articles, including features and reviews. A graduate of McMaster University, Ryan began his career in public relations before attending Vancouver Film School. There, he built lasting industry connections and optioned a feature film screenplay.

Repo Man 2: The Wages of Beer could be on the horizon.

Directed by Alex Cox, Repo Man was released in 1984 and stars Emilio Estevez as a young punk recruited by a car repo agency to track down a Chevrolet Malibu with something mysterious in its trunk. The film became a cult classic due to its wacky story and punk roots.

In a recent interview with The Guardian, Cox confirms that he is now crowdfunding Repo Man 2. The filmmaker teases that the sequel is set in the world of driverless cars, and it features a drastically different role for the titular repo man. Cox shares the news when discussing whether he still believes in the line from the original: "The more you drive, the less intelligent you are." Check out the filmmaker's response below:

That’s something my wife said when we were still students at UCLA and, at the time, I did believe it. Now I think that if you’re not relying on driver-assist technology, driving is an interesting occupation. It enables you to focus on something in the distance rather than staring at a screen in front of you. Right now I’m crowdfunding Repo Man 2, set in a world of driverless cars. Instead of sneaking down people’s drives, hotwiring cars and running away, the repo man just sits at a computer, takes control and drives the car to the sheriff’s substation or repo yard. It’s a different world, but the same kind of malevolence.

Cox then delves into crowdfunding as a means of getting movies made, explaining that major studios and streamers are primarily attracted to popular established properties. The filmmaker says that even the original Repo Man only got made because of personal connections and happenstance, making crowdfunding the best way to get a proper sequel off the ground:

For independent film-makers, yes, because the studios and the big streamers are really only interested in franchises and massive IPs. They want to spend a lot of money, but they don’t really care what the film is. Often they don’t read the script. In the case of Repo Man, it was obvious Universal hadn’t read the script. It got greenlit because of some people Nesmith met in a bar. The people who will back a Kickstarter actually want to see the film.

IndieWire reported in early 2024 that the sequel would be called Repo Man 2: The Wages of Beer. Set after the events of the original movie, the follow-up would feature Otto having aged only 90 minutes, with Kiowa Gordon taking over the role from Estevez.

This original report shared that Cox would be presenting the sequel to buyers at the European Film Market in the hopes of securing pre-sale funding. The filmmaker's latest comments to The Guardian about crowdfunding suggest that his efforts in 2024 may not have been entirely successful.

The Kickstarter for Repo Man 2: The Wages of Beer reveals that, as of writing, $98,125 has been raised out of a $500,000 goal. There are 374 backers for the project so far, with 43 days left in the campaign. As an "all or nothing" campaign, the project will only be funded if it actually reaches its $500,000 goal by January 4. The Kickstarter page reveals more plot information about the follow-up, including a synopses for the three main acts:

REPO MAN 2: THE WAGES OF BEER is the story of Otto, who has aged only 90 minutes since we last saw him, yet has also journeyed far and wide in space and time and seen things we wouldn’t believe.

Act One finds Otto on the job in the near future. No longer does he leave the Repo Office. His long nights are spent at a computer, selecting cars from the endlessly scrolling LA Repo Roll, and seizing control of their onboard computers. In this way, he is able to harass and extort the drivers while directing their vehicles to the police station, or the Repo Yard, which is now run by the Rodriguez Brothers.

In Act Two, fleeing a workplace disaster, Otto decamps to space – as a stowaway on five Billionaire Philanthropists’ mission to Mars. Otto witnesses the tragic accidental deaths of all but one of these beloved Billionaires, while simultaneously trying to repo their vessel and return it to Earth.

Act Three brings Otto back to his home planet, where things have changed a bit.

While it remains to be seen whether Otto will return in a Repo Man sequel, the original movie remains a cult favorite. The film features a cast that includes Harry Dean Stanton, Tracey Walter, Olivia Barash, and Sy Richardson. It was a hit with critics, sporting a 92% on Rotten Tomatoes, with the Popcornmeter not too far behind at 78%.

The film was made on an estimated budget of $1.5 million, meaning the sequel would be making do with quite a bit less if the Kickstarter is successful. Repo Man grossed about $2.3 million, the majority of which came from North America.

The movie landscape of today is vastly different than it was in 1984, and it's not yet clear how much of an appetite there is for a Repo Man 2. With about a month and a half left to go for the campaign on Kickstarter, it seemingly won't be long before the sequel's fate is decided.

English Journalist Ranks 25 Greatest Footballers He’s Seen Live

📅 Nov 22, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

English football journalist John Cross has taken on the impossible task of ranking the greatest footballers he's seen in person, and his list of 25 players is bound to cause a debate.

For any fan, ranking players is a matter of preference, with factors such as playing style, achievements, position and even nationality all coming into play. You'd be hard-pressed to find any Argentine who ranks Cristiano Ronaldo above Lionel Messi.

Cross curated his 25-man list via Mirror Sport by explaining that he's been reporting on football for more than 25 years and, as such, there are many legendary names included along with modern-day stars. Where he ranks the current generation may surprise readers, though it's worth noting he deemed England's 3-2 win over Mexico at the World Cup to be the nation's 'best ever away performance'.

In 25th place is AC Milan hero Marco van Basten, a player who won three Ballons d'Or, though as Cross mentions, he had his career cut short by injuries. The Daily Mirror writer suggests that if not for injuries, he'd have made the top five on this list.

Roberto Carlos is next up, and Cross deemed him a 'brilliant entertainer' and a player who could get you out of your seat, even playing as a left-back. For many fans, this is a fair ranking of the Brazil and Real Madrid icon because there aren't many better players who have played in his position.

The first Premier League name to make Cross's list is Patrick Vieira, with the journalist particularly praising his physicality and leadership. Dubbed 'one of the greatest captains and midfielders to ever grace the English game', the former Arsenal skipper's dominance is hard to deny.

CROSS CONTROVERSY: There was no place for Patrick Vieira's longtime rival Roy Keane in his list.

Paolo Maldini is one of the players you could rank above Carlos on the all-time left-back list, and the Italian icon sits above him on Cross's list. The AC Milan great's peak came during the journalist's childhood, and he recalled watching him during the 1980s and Channel 4's coverage of Serie A.

Another Premier League star, Kevin De Bruyne, earned his place among Cross's favourites. The former Manchester City midfielder was named 'the best player in the best team in English football', though Cross noted that it comes 'with a caveat now' due to the club being found guilty of breaching financial rules.

Kevin De Bruyne, Steven Gerrard, and Patrick Vieira are all among the best midfielders in the league's history.

Sticking with playmaking gems, Cross ranked Luka Modric 20th, branding him 'the epitome of a European midfielder' and 'the greatest Croatian player in history'. He did, however, point out the Real Madrid legend's lack of understanding that 'football's coming home' is a 'self-deprecating joke at ourselves'.

In 19th place is David Beckham, maybe the first name that will stir up controversy, whether that be because he's too high or too low. There aren't many more polarising players in history because some use his celebrity status against him, while others, like Cross, believe he approached the ball like a 'work of art'.

Another Englishman, Frank Lampard, takes 18th, and Cross highlighted his goalscoring consistency along with his mindset to work 'so hard' to turn himself into 'one of the world's best'. He attributed Chelsea's most successful period to the highest-scoring midfielder in top-flight history.

Cross insisted that 'we have to have a wayward hero in there' and claimed that Eric Cantona 'absolutely fits that bill'. Citing his entertainment value, he described the former French striker as creating 'magic stardust' that helped Manchester United end their wait for a title and dominate that era of the Premier League.

It's clear who Cross sides with in the Lampard vs Steven Gerrard vs Paul Scholes debate as he chose Gerrard in 16th, omitting Scholes from his entire list. He mentioned that the former Liverpool captain's long-range goals and passion made him one of his favourite players, recalled his 2005 Champions League win and said that he 'deserved to win more trophies'.

Thierry Henry, Pep Guardiola, Deco and Cesc Fabregas are among the players that have answered the age-old Scholes vs Gerrard vs Lampard debate

Now we get to the more contentious picks, and names like Wayne Rooney falling outside of the top 10 might upset one or two fans. Cross recalled Rooney's Euro 2004 campaign, calling him 'unstoppable' and a 'street footballer' whose intelligence on and off the pitch doesn't get enough credit.

For some, Mohamed Salah isn't an entertaining player to watch, especially in comparison with the likes of Eden Hazard when that conversation comes up. For Cross, his dribbling ability and success at Liverpool make him a 'clear choice' for his list, deeming him a 'fabulous Premier League legend'.

There's a reason Cross asked Arsenal fans for their thoughts, as many regard Thierry Henry as the greatest Premier League player of all time. Given that this is the main competition he covers, it's somewhat surprising the Frenchman doesn't make the top 10, although he does applaud his mentality and natural ability, calling him one of the best he's seen on a weekly basis.

Cross views Dennis Bergkamp as the greatest Arsenal player he's seen, which is why the Dutchman sits above Henry. His admiration of Bergkamp is made clear when he acknowledges him as 'a game-changer for English football' and cites his finish against Newcastle United as being 'from another world'.

Just missing out on the top 10 is Barcelona icon Xavi, who played in a team Cross deemed to be 'the best team in Europe'. There's less analysis of the Spaniard than any other player on his list, as Cross instead chose to focus on Pep Guardiola's team rather than explain Xavi's ranking.

Kicking off Cross's top 10 is Andres Iniesta, whom he referenced in his verdict on Xavi. Between the duo, he argues that Iniesta edges it because of his passing vision and control. He called the 2010 World Cup winner 'just wonderful' and 'a joy to watch'.

Perhaps a more controversial pick is Zlatan Ibrahimovic in ninth, although Cross justified this selection by alluding to the change in opinion of the Swedish striker among English media and fans. He recalled his performance for Sweden in a win over England when he scored 'one of the greatest goals of all time' and believes he is 'one of football's greatest-ever characters'.

Kylian Mbappe is ranked eighth, and for the older generation, that is a perfectly acceptable ranking for a player who has been a revelation but has still yet to win a Ballon d'Or. For the younger generation, it might seem somewhat low because of his dominance during the modern era, winning the World Cup at the age of 19.

The 2026 Ballon d’Or winner will be announced on Monday night in London, England.

Meanwhile, a Ballon d'Or winner who did make Cross's top 10 is Harry Kane, and the journalist thinks we haven't fully appreciated the England captain for many years. Alluding to his goalscoring exploits in recent times at Bayern Munich, he claims that the former Tottenham striker has got even better with age.

Ranking Jude Bellingham sixth is Cross's most daring decision, and he defended this by tipping the Real Madrid star to be the best English player of all time. He references England's World Cup win over Mexico, describing the Birmingham-born midfielder's performance as 'amazing' and calling him 'elegant, driven and just wonderful'.

CROSS CONTROVERSY: Jude Bellingham is the highest ranked English player on the list at the age of 23.

The reaction in the comments section suggests Bellingham's spot is the most controversial call. Placing the 'truly beautiful, iconic and inspirational' Zinedine Zidane above him in fifth isn't as bold, and Cross mentions watching the French midfielder 'own the whole pitch' in a random Serie A game for Juventus, possessing 'the best control of any player' he's seen.

Zidane's former Real Madrid teammate Ronaldo Nazario takes fourth spot, a player whom Cross says 'had everything', including 'skill, power and pace'. He reckons that at his peak, he was 'the best player in the world'.

CROSS CONTROVERSY: There's no place for Ronaldo Nazario's former Brazilian teammate Ronaldinho on this entire list.

Cross admits that Cristiano Ronaldo's recent 'diva carry-ons' (his unceremonious Portugal retirement) have put him off. He still acknowledges his 'eye for the spectacular', arguing that since he left Manchester United for Real Madrid in 2009, he isn't sure English football has possessed a player of his calibre.

Lineker thinks four players in football history are greater than Cristiano Ronaldo.

Taking the runners-up medal in Cross's list is Diego Maradona, though he interestingly claims that 'it's not going to be popular'. The highly reputed journalist calls the late Argentine icon 'unstoppable' and celebrates his 1986 World Cup exploits, claiming that he won Argentina the tournament.

'The greatest dribbler and showman' Cross has ever seen, Lionel Messi, is the best player he has watched in person, and that's often the case for fans who witness the Argentine hero in action. He even reveals that he used to side with Cristiano Ronaldo in the GOAT debate, but deems Messi's 2022 World Cup heroics to have ended that discussion.

Bayern CEO Dreesen responds to fan criticism over Musiala situation

📅 Nov 22, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Dreesen was questioned by Bayern fans over the club's approach to the Germany international, who continues to work towards a return to full fitness following his prolonged absence.

Acknowledging the supporters' concerns, the Bayern chief insisted that the club must give Musiala the necessary time to recover while also recognising its responsibilities towards the player and the wider organisation.

"I acknowledge your comments. I've also spoken publicly about the matter myself. I tried to interpret Uli's words, and he handled the situation sensitively. We are giving Jamal the time he needs, and we have to do so," Dreesen explained.

The 59-year-old added that his previous comments were not intended to create further discussion, but rather to underline his responsibility to protect the player's interests.

"In my role, I have a duty of care towards the player, and I tried to make it clear that Jamal will be given the time he needs to get back to his old self," he continued.

However, Dreesen also acknowledged that Bayern face a difficult situation in balancing Musiala's recovery with the club's sporting responsibilities, describing the circumstances as something football has rarely encountered before.

"Jamal's sporting future is extremely important; he is an extraordinary player. But there is a second responsibility, not just towards the player, but also towards the club," he said.

"It's like in any company when someone is no longer able to perform all of their duties. This is a new situation in football, a situation that we have to learn how to deal with."

Dreesen also referred to the unusual nature of Musiala's ongoing health situation, asking supporters to show understanding as Bayern navigate unfamiliar territory.

"These absence seizures have never occurred in elite sport, particularly in football, before. I ask for your understanding on this," the Bayern CEO concluded.

Addressing the supporter who raised the issue, Dreesen also acknowledged the message behind the criticism, remarking: "Your message is: 'Speak, but speak a bit more quietly.' I think everyone has understood that."

Box Office: A24’s ‘You Can See Everything’ Crosses $100 Million Globally

📅 Nov 22, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

“You Can See Everything,” an unconventional documentary about Theranos founder Elizabeth Holmes in exile, has crossed $100 million at the global box office. Its success extends a hot streak for A24, the indie studio behind last summer’s blockbuster “Backrooms” as well as arthouse hits such as “The Drama” and “Primetime.”

“You Can See Everything” now ranks as the eighth-highest grossing non-music documentary, moving ahead of “Deep Sea 3D,” and the second-highest grossing non-music and non-nature documentary, behind Michael Moore’s “Fahrenheit 9/11.” It’s unlikely to surpass Moore’s anti-Bush jeremiad, which grossed an astonishing $222.4 million when it opened in the height of a fiercely contested presidential election. Those aren’t the only benchmarks with which to measure “You Can See Everything’s” impact. It is now the seventh-highest grossing film in the history of A24, coming in behind “Materialists,” which earned $105.6 million globally. On a domestic basis, “You Can See Everything” is A24’s third-highest grossing film, ranking just behind “Backrooms” ($197.5 million) and “Marty Supreme” ($96 million). It should easily surpass “Marty Supreme” and that film had Timothée Chalamet and a boatload of Oscar noms behind it.

“You Can See Everything,” which is co-directed by Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim, earned $2.5 million this weekend to bring its domestic haul to $93.5 million after six weeks of release. Most of its earnings have been stateside. It’s currently playing in Australia, the U.K. and Scandinavia and the studio said its international rollout will continue over the next several weeks.

Former Denver Broncos linebacker Tom Jackson, member of the famed 'Orange Crush' defense of the 1970's, passed away at the age of 75, the team announced on Sunday morning.

Jackson was selected by the Broncos in the fourth round of the 1973 NFL draft (88th overall) out of Louisville. Jackson only played in eight games his rookie year, but became a long-time contributor for Denver. Jackson ultimately played in 191 career games, third-most all-time in team history (177 starts) over 14 years, recording 20 interceptions (13th-best in team history), returning three picks for touchdowns, 1,531 tackles (935 solo), 44 sacks (10th most in team history) and eight fumble recoveries. Jackson was voted to three Pro Bowls, and a two-time All-Pro. Jackson was with the Denver during their first Super Bowl run in 1977, and was around when the Broncos became one of the most dominant teams of the 1980's, playing in an additional Super Bowl in 1986, his final year. Jackson became a Ring of Fame honoree in 1992.

After Jackson's playing career, he was an analyst for ESPN, teaming up with Chris Berman in breaking down the best action in the NFL.

Jackson's importance during the Orange Crush years were not forgotten. When linebacker Randy Gradishar became the first Pro Football Hall of Fame inductee from the unit, Jackson presented Gradishar for enshrinement. Sadly, Jackson never lived to see his bust in Canton.

"The Denver Broncos are heartbroken by the passing of Ring of Fame linebacker Tom Jackson, a cornerstone of the legendary Orange Crush defense and one of the most beloved players in franchise history," the team said in a statement on Sunday morning. "An inspirational leader and fierce competitor, TJ was a proud member of the Broncos' first Super Bowl team that helped establish a winning tradition in Denver. Off the field, Tom carried himself with class, warmth and humility, forging deep connections and lifelong friendships with teammates and throughout Broncos Country. TJ's dynamic personality, quick wit and football knowledge made him a natural for television and paved the way for a Hall of Fame broadcasting career. For generations of fans, Tom was an iconic fixture on ESPN's Sunday NFL Countdown and NFL Primetime, sharing his thoughtful insight with an unmistakable sense of humor that defined an era of sports broadcasting. Above all, TJ loved the Broncos and was a cherished member of our family for more than 50 years. We extend our deepest sympathies to Tom's wife, Jennifer, his daughters, Taylor and Morgan, and all of his family, friends and teammates."
