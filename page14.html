HBO’s 5-Part Crime Drama Has One of the Best Seasons in TV History

📅 Nov 14, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The Wire’s fourth season provided a far deeper look at Baltimore’s problems

The Wire’s fourth season provided a far deeper look at Baltimore’s problems

At the turn of the 21st century, HBO was the place for prestige crime drama. The launch of The Sopranos in 1999 paved the way for the 2002 premiere of The Wire, which focused on drug dealers in Baltimore and the police officers trying to disrupt their operations. The series is still one of the best shows of all time, and provided breakout roles for huge stars like Idris Elba and Michael B. Jordan. But while it was compelling from the beginning, The Wire truly achieved perfection in season 4 when the scope expanded beyond the drug trade to examine the cycle of institutional failure.

Season 1 of The Wire was tightly focused on the drug empire of Avon Barksdale (Wood Harris) and his business-savvy second-in-command Stringer Bell (Elba). Showrunner David Simon quickly established a brilliant mix of humor and pathos. The show could find comedy in something as simple as cops struggling to get new furniture into their office, and then gut audiences with the death of a relatable drug dealer. Season 2 shifted the focus to the city’s port system, a jarring change as many characters faded into the background to make room for the tragic tale of the Sobotka family. Season 3 was the most tightly linked to the show’s first season, largely completing the story of the Barksdale gang.

That made season 4 perfect for a soft reboot, a shift that was defined by star Dominic West all but disappearing after his relentless detective, Jimmy McNulty, was demoted for pissing off his bosses too many times. The detectives he previously worked with in the major crimes unit continue their work, investigating a vicious new gang that’s filling the void Barksdale left behind. Lester Freamon (Clarke Peters) effectively steps into the McNulty role of pursuing the truth, even when his supervisors would love for him to be a little less dogged, bringing a new methodical nature to the investigations instead of McNulty’s brash confidence. But while the cops vs. criminals beats are familiar, they become just one part of a far more ambitious season that fully capitalized on Simon’s knowledge of the city. (Simon worked as a journalist at The Baltimore Sun for 12 years before creating The Wire.)

The season’s largest secondary plot involves the students at a Baltimore middle school where former cop Roland Pryzbylewski (Jim True-Frost) begins teaching. The young actors do a phenomenal job establishing likable characters in terrible circumstances. You want to root for all of them, from the ambitious Randy Wagstaff (Maestro Harrell) to the fiercely protective Michael Lee (Tristan Wilds). By the end of season 4, the system has profoundly failed almost all of these kids, driving them to lives of addiction and violence. The Wire was always good at making most of its characters sympathetic, but seeing children trapped in such a brutal cycle really drives home the show’s themes of how crime thrives when institutions fail.

That theme also plays out in the mayoral campaign of Tommy Carcetti (Aidan Gillen). Carcetti was introduced in season 3 as an ambitious city council member, but he really shines in season 4 as he runs on reforming the Baltimore Police Department — and then quickly breaks his promises. Gillen’s oily performance as a political survivor who would doom his city to satisfy his own ambitions was what landed him the role of Littlefinger in Game of Thrones. Carcetti is even more compelling a character because he feels like a man who could have done good if he’d been willing to make sacrifices rather than feed his own ego.

The Wire season 4 is able to build so many new arcs because of the strong foundation established in the previous three seasons. The show is dense with rich characters who have grown significantly over the years even as they continue to struggle with many of the same problems. The cops and drug dealers alike feel world-weary as they deal with a more violent new generation represented by Marlo Stanfield (Jamie Hector) and his vicious enforcers Snoop (Felicia Pearson) and Chris Partlow (Gbenga Akinnagbe). Combined with the dark fate of most of the kids and Carcetti’s broken promises, the season creates the bleak feeling that everyone is trapped in a cycle of misery and decay.

Season 5 gave most of The Wire’s characters satisfying ends to their arcs and delivered a scathing critique of the downfall of American newspapers, commentary that has only become more profound 20 years later. But overall, it’s a big step down from season 4, spending too much time on a goofy fake serial killer plot and not enough on the nuanced character-driven stories that made season 4 feel so authentic. There are no bad seasons of The Wire, but season 4 perfectly achieved the show’s goals. It’s an alternately funny, tragic, and profound look at the complex ways that people are betrayed by systems that never really give them a chance to succeed.

Fallout 5 and Fallout Remasters Officially Announced

📅 Nov 14, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Xbox wants more Fallout, and after a yearslong drought of new games, Bethesda Softworks is making it happen. Bethesda plans to release remasters of both Fallout 3 and Fallout: New Vegas for modern platforms, the company said in July in a massive roadmap of the developer's future. Those remasters are part of a larger plan to reinvest in Fallout games — including Fallout 5, which is currently in pre-production — and a new untitled project at Obsidian Entertainment.

"We have multiple Fallout projects in active development right now," Bethesda said in an official announcement this summer. That includes Fallout 5, which is described as the studio's "long-range destination," and "a major Fallout 76 expansion" planned for 2027 called Raven Rock, which will serve as a prequel story to Fallout 3.

Bethesda also confirmed two long-standing rumors, announcing remasters for Fallout 3 and Fallout: New Vegas. The studio did not provide any further details or a release window, but the projects have reportedly been in the works for many years. The existence of a Fallout 3 remaster dates back to at least 2020, when the project's existence was revealed through court documents related to the Federal Trade Commission's (FTC) litigation regarding Microsoft's buyout of Activision Blizzard.

Fallout 3 and Fallout: New Vegas' remasters follow last year's release of The Elder Scrolls 4: Oblivion Remastered, which was co-developed by Virtuos and Bethesda Game Studios. Bethesda also marked the 10-year anniversary of Fallout 4 in 2025, with the release of Fallout 4: Anniversary Edition. That rerelease included the original game, all six of the previously released DLC add-ons, as well as over 150 items pulled from the Fallout 4 Creation Club.

Fallout 3 was originally released in 2008 for PlayStation 3, Windows PC, and Xbox 360. The game was the third major installment in the Fallout series, and the first to be developed by Bethesda after acquiring the rights to the franchise from Interplay Entertainment. Fallout 3 is set in and around Washington D.C. 200 years after a devastating nuclear war, and players control a 19-year-old Vault dweller in search of their missing father.

Fallout: New Vegas followed in 2010, and was developed by Obsidian Entertainment. It was set in the Mojave Desert and casts players as a courier who becomes embroiled in a conflict between various factions who are vying for control of the region.

In addition to new Fallout games, a third season of the Fallout TV series is in the works and recently wrapped filming. Bethesda Game Studios is also hard at work on the next game in its fantasy role-playing game series: The Elder Scrolls 6: Sentinel is now in development.

Here, it’s worth noting that this update came soon after September’s public consultation. During the same, the regulator collected feedback from industry participants.

In this, one of the key proposals is to introduce a daily transfer limit of 5 million Thai baht per customer at each regulated digital asset operator. That said, the limit would apply separately to incoming and outgoing stablecoin transfers. However, the value of these transactions must also be consistent with the customer’s declared income and financial position.

However, it’s important to note that transfers between a customer’s own accounts at two SEC-supervised operators would be exempt from the limit, provided both operators follow the Travel Rule.

Simultaneously, the SEC also wants to strengthen wallet ownership verification. In this, the SEC has enabled customers to deposit stablecoins from their own verified wallets and withdraw them to wallets registered in their names.

Additionally, operators would also need to follow the Travel Rule, analyze customers, identify suspicious accounts, and track transactions connected to high-risk wallets.

These requirements would be done in order to make it harder for criminals to move illicit funds through regulated platforms.

Finally, the proposal also introduces stricter responsibilities for exchanges and brokers. Meanwhile, brokers would be prohibited from using liquidity providers for stablecoin-to-Thai baht trading pairs.

Besides that, brokers and dealers offering off-platform transactions would face a minimum transaction threshold of 3 million baht and would have to publish trading prices for transparency. Brokers would no longer be allowed to arrange off-platform trades directly between customers.

Taken together, these steps would help the SEC combat crimes that are concerning the stablecoin ecosystem in Thailand.

During the "hold harmless" period, states will still assess whether Medicaid applicants and beneficiaries meet the work requirements or qualify for an exclusion, but otherwise eligible people will not lose or be denied coverage solely because they fail to meet the requirement, CMS said.

CMS says the transition is intended to give states time to improve eligibility systems, automate verification, train workers and improve the beneficiary experience.

During the six-month enforcement pause, CMS encourages states to use existing data to automatically verify qualifying activities and exemptions before requesting additional information from beneficiaries.

CMS’ June 2026 interim final rule outlines qualifying activities, including 80 hours per month of work, community service or participation in a work program; enrollment in an educational program at least half-time; a combination of qualifying activities totaling at least 80 hours per month; or meeting the specified monthly income threshold.

The report recommends that plans looking to survive in this complex time should focus on demonstrating their value to plan sponsors and enrollees, particularly around managing the total cost of care. Insurers should also rethink how they engage with members to support a stronger relationship with consumers.

It also means finding ways to think differently about their relationship with providers; a step that will be critical to addressing the cost of care. Payers should look for provider partners that can take on risk, and work proactively with providers to drive better outcomes and improve value.

Abrams said that payer-provider dynamics are often coercive rather than collaborative. In one market, the insurer may be more powerful and in another the provider, but in either case it's based around a show of dominance rather than working together.

"With continued consolidation on both sides, we have seen a kind of polarization where the only mechanism for resolution is who has the biggest market clout," Abrams said.

Ardent Health is acquiring Boone Health, a mid-sized nonprofit system based in Columbia, Missouri, for $47 million in cash, the organizations announced Thursday. It marks Ardent's first entry into the Missouri healthcare market.

The deal, expected to close in the first quarter of 2027, includes a 10-year, $200 million capital commitment on nonprofit Ardent’s part. That money will be invested in Boone’s facilities, workforce and, in the near term, an expansion of its emergency department, according to the announcement.

Ardent has agreed to maintain Boone’s essential hospital services while developing its 392-bed regional referral center, Boone Hospital Center. It also plans to maintain employment for existing Boone employees and providers, continue its charity care policy and establish a local advisory board.

“We are acquiring a strong asset in an attractive and growing midsize market with significant opportunity to drive operating and financial improvement, all while delivering high patient quality care and safety,” Ardent Health President and CEO Dave Caspers said during a Thursday afternoon investor call discussing the deal. “It also represents an effective use of capital at a compelling valuation that we expect will create long-term shareholder value.”

Alongside its main hospital, Ardent would acquire Boone’s medical group, 19 care sites including an outpatient campus and five medical office buildings, related ancillary services and real estate. The system is the second-largest provider in its region with about 30% market share and half a billion dollars of annual revenue, Caspers said, with a local population that is well insured (93% coverage) and growing well ahead of the national average (3.4% in the next five years).

“Boone expands our footprint into a new attractive market,” Caspers explained. “It diversifies our business and enhances scale. Further, adding Boone's regional system to our portfolio is consistent with our strategy of building leading healthcare platforms in growing mid-sized communities. We're highly aligned with Boone Health on culture and mission, including putting the patient at the center of everything we do, with an aim toward best-in-class clinical care, patient quality, safety and access to services.”

The executive noted to investors that Boone’s facilities are relatively modern and well maintained, allowing Ardent to direct more capital toward its growth. The company has also identified “meaningful opportunities to optimize financial performance.” By leveraging its larger scale and expertise to tackle costs (supply chain procurement, vendor consolidation) and revenue cycle processes, Ardent executives said they expect to generate about $5 million to $10 million additional EBITDA within the deal’s first year, and at least $30 million of annual synergies three years after the deal’s close.

Boone Health has been independent since 2021, following over three decades during which it operated as part of the BJC HealthCare system. In 2025, its leadership kicked off a search for potential strategic partners “that could provide additional capital and resources to support the system's continued growth. Ardent was selected after a comprehensive evaluation process led by an independent advisory firm,” according to Thursday’s announcement.

“In Ardent, we identified a partner that supports our mission, respects our culture and can accelerate our vision without compromising what makes Boone Health special,” Jerry Kennett, M.D., chair of Boone’s Board of Trustees, said in the announcement. "Following a thoughtful review, the Board is confident Ardent is the right long-term partner for Boone Health. This agreement protects what our community has built while giving Boone Health the resources it needs to grow."

Ardent Health’s current operations include 30 acute care hospitals and over 280 care sites, several of which are run as part of a joint venture structure with other health system partners. In 2025, the company increased its revenues by 6% to $6.3 billion and grew its adjusted EBITDA to $545 million.

The company recently logged a strong third quarter off the back of solid demand for emergent care and progress on its ongoing performance improvement initiative. In September, it also officially divested from a joint venture with the University of Kansas Health System, a decision that leadership said is part of an ongoing review of its portfolio to ensure long-term strategic fit among its hospitals and partnerships.
