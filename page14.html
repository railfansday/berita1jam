Comparing Lionel Messi and Cristiano Ronaldo’s final international stats

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

A major chapter in the Lionel Messi vs Cristiano Ronaldo rivalry is drawing to a close, with Messi having bade Argentina farewell and Ronaldo having now announced his retirement from international football with Portugal.

With both players’ international careers now over, their total tallies for Portugal and Argentina are finalised. How do they compare on the international stage?

After a legendary career with Argentina, in which he’s comfortably their all-time top goalscorer and appearance-maker, the 39-year-old captain waved goodbye to the fans with a goal and two assists against Benin.

Ronaldo has now confirmed that his time with Portugal is also over, bringing the curtain down on an extraordinary international career. Whether he’ll be given the opportunity to enjoy a farewell appearance in front of his home supporters remains to be seen.

The 41-year-old leaves international football as the most prolific goalscorer in men’s international history, having represented Portugal at five World Cups and six European Championships.

Few players will ever get close to the international numbers Messi and Ronaldo have left behind. Ronaldo finishes with 234 Portugal appearances and 146 goals, both higher than Messi’s final totals of 207 caps and 125 goals for Argentina.

Ronaldo was also the more prolific goalscorer – both in terms of his overall tally and averages. His 146 strikes arrived at one every 127 minutes, compared with Messi’s rate of one every 136. Taking penalties out of the equation leaves Ronaldo ahead again, averaging a goal every 151 minutes to Messi’s 172.

Messi’s advantage comes in the creative department. He provided 67 assists for Argentina, comfortably clear of Ronaldo’s 37, and that swings the overall goal contribution numbers his way: 193 to 183.

There is very little separating them elsewhere. Messi scored 12 direct free-kicks to Ronaldo’s 11 and registered 11 hat-tricks to Ronaldo’s 10. He was also, surprisingly, more reliable from the spot, converting 78% of his penalties compared with Ronaldo’s 69%.

Add everything together, and Messi averaged a goal or assist every 89 minutes of his international career. Ronaldo’s corresponding figure is every 101 minutes, despite his superior individual scoring rate.

Messi also finishes one ahead in the trophy count. Argentina won four major honours during his career, including the World Cup and two Copa Americas, while Ronaldo helped Portugal claim Euro 2016 and two Nations League titles.

The charity, founded by Worthing mum Ann Feloy, has been announced as Worthing FC Supporters Association's chosen charity of the year and a fundraising day was held on Saturday, October 31.

Nearly 4,000 fans attended the game against Sutton United at Woodside Road and representatives of Olly’s Future were there with collection buckets. The charity also received a split of the match-day raffle, bringing the total raised to £870.

Ann set up the charity in memory of her son Oliver Hare, who took his own life on February 14, 2017, just before his 23rd birthday. Its work includes delivering training worldwide to equip people with the skills to spot signs of someone who may be thinking of suicide and have a conversation that could help save their life.

Ann said: “We're delighted Worthing FC has chosen Olly's Future as its charity of the year. My son Oliver loved playing all kinds of sports, from football and rugby to tennis and squash.

“It's a local club being bold enough to get behind the message that suicide prevention is everyone's business, and particularly football where the majority of spectators are men.

“This is what the recent The Royal Foundation of the Prince and Princess of Wales' campaign On Your Side, hosted by Jake Mills Hub of Hope and fronted by Harry Kane, looks like on the ground.

“We all know the facts that three quarters of suicides are by men and that suicide is the biggest killer of men under 50. Supporting our suicide prevention charity goes a long way to reaching that vital target audience and we'll be working with the club to ensure we can help save lives from suicide together.

“However, this comes at a time when the town is in shock about the closure of its Worthing Samaritans branch, which has left volunteers reeling. I've spoken to many in the past week, since they were told of the closure of their premises in an efficiency drive.

“Local volunteers tell me they are being asked to work from home, which they feel is not tenable as it's vital for their own well-being to be with colleagues, or else drive to Brighton to carry out their shifts.

“For every step forward in suicide prevention, there seems to be two steps back. The Samaritans are closing numerous premises around the country - Worthing is not alone.”

The Rebels will be working with Olly’s Future throughout the coming season and the supporters association, run by Natalie Lunn, will be donating some of the proceeds from away match coach travel to the charity.

Natalie said: “I work as a nurse and know how difficult it is to access care for physical health let alone mental health. So when we were looking for a charity to support, I went online and found Olly’s Future, which is based locally.

“I have sons myself and some of their formative years were during Covid and I know how difficult it can be for young people. So,it is great to be able to contribute to a charity doing such valuable work.”

The club produced an enamel badge, linking the Love and Light logo of Olly’s Future with the team, for the fundraising day and this proved popular with supporters.

James Davies, Olly’s Future chief executive, has followed non-league football most of his life and he was part of the fundraising team on the day.

He said: “For us it is excellent to have such a relationship with a club which shows how much it cares about the issue of suicide.

“You see more than 3,000 people gathered together in one space and this offers a chance to open up about issues which are concerning them. They are with friends and are experiencing a sense of belonging as they get together to support the team.

“Non-league teams may not have the resources and the large fan-base of the bigger clubs, but they are very obviously rooted in towns such as Worthing and play a major part in the community. Tickets are also affordable in ways that premiership attendance is not for many people.”

His own recent personal experience has shown how much going to watch a match can mean to people who might otherwise be feeling isolated.

James explained: “I recently lost my grandmother and my grandfather, aged 87, had been her carer in her last years. He has started going each week to watch both Portland United and Weymouth matches along with other family members, which stops him just being at home and alone with his thoughts, and when I went with him, he really did open up about his feelings.”

How the midterms could fuel the K-shaped AI trade

📅 2026-11-05  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The U.S. midterms could widen the AI divide: tighter data center rules may reward tech giants with capacity in hand and squeeze debt-funded companies trying to catch up.

Democratic victories in key gubernatorial races raised the prospect of tougher scrutiny for data center projects, adding to investor concerns about how quickly new capacity can be built and financed.

In Texas, Democratic governor-elect Gina Hinojosa has proposed a statewide moratorium on new data center permits, although projects already under construction would reportedly be exempt.

As the markets digested the results on Wednesday, Microsoft and Amazon each surged more than 6%, while AI infrastructure provider CoreWeave plunged around 10%. Chipmakers Micron and Nvidia fell 7% and 3.17%, respectively.

Differences in borrowing costs are playing a key role in the split, James Sullivan, managing director and co-head of global fundamental research at JPMorgan, told CNBC's "Squawk Box Asia" on Thursday.

Sullivan said borrowing spreads have widened significantly for neocloud companies, leaving their cost of additional capital substantially higher than that of hyperscalers that benefit from more diversified customers and revenue streams.

"The fact that you're seeing this bifurcation in equity performance is largely driven by the view that existing capacity is significantly more valuable now because of incremental supply constraints," he said.

The midterm results could reinforce that view if they make new projects more vulnerable to permitting delays.

BMI warned that a sharp slowdown in data center construction and AI investment could produce a greater-than-expected decline in overall investment, The Business Times reported, citing the firm's post-midterm analysis.

The potential impact is uneven across the construction pipeline. Much of the data center capacity expected to come online in 2027 is already under construction, limiting the near-term impact of new restrictions.

Projects planned for 2028 face greater exposure to permitting delays and regulatory uncertainty, investment bank Nomura said in a note Wednesday about the midterm results.

Tighter regulations on data center construction could become a more significant headwind to AI investment from 2028, the firm said.

Shares of Nvidia-backed neocloud CoreWeave, Nebius and Iren fell at least 30% in October, as tightening credit markets raised questions about how AI infrastructure projects would be financed. CoreWeave's five-year credit default swap spread has surged past 1,100 basis points, reflecting rising costs for investors seeking protection against a potential default.

The distinction may be how well companies can absorb delays and fund the capacity they do build. Hyperscalers have broader cloud businesses and more internally generated cash flow, while lower-rated neoclouds face greater exposure to borrowing costs, energy prices and refinancing conditions, JPMorgan Asset Management said in an Oct. 20 research note.

The asset manager said financially stronger hyperscalers could also secure cheaper capacity or acquire assets if weaker operators scale back.
