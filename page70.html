African TV Giant Primedia Acquires The Africa Channel and Assets, Expanding its Reach Across the Continent and in U.S.

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Primedia, Africa’s leading producer and distributor of television content, has acquired The Africa Channel, one of the few Black-owned U.S. media companies dedicated to creating content for African audiences.

The deal combines The Africa Channel’s Los Angeles-based media businesses with Primedia’s media content production, advertiser relationships and distribution capabilities across the continent of Africa. Primedia plans to invest in TAC’s next phase of growth by expanding worldwide distribution of its linear, FAST and digital services, increasing development and production of original content and building a broader global media brand for audiences of African content around the world. The pact gives Primedia its first owned-and-operated U.S. television network and streaming platform outside of Africa.

TAC’s media assets being acquired include Demand Africa, a streaming platform available as an SVOD/AVOD app, as well as a free, ad-supported streaming television (FAST) channel; TAC Studios, the in-house studio that develops and produces original content; TAC Labs, an AI-driven innovation team that has developed projects for the entertainment and media market with plans to collaborate with Primedia.

“The combination of Primedia’s talent pool and the brilliant team at The Africa Channel brings together an established U.S. platform with Primedia’s tech stack, production, commercial and distribution strength,” Jonathan Procter, group CEO of Primedia, said. “Together, we intend to build a business that can move African news, sports, content, talent and brands more effectively between continents while creating new opportunities across distribution, advertising and content ownership.”

Paula Williams Madison, currently TAC’s principal owner, will become the chair of the advisory board. Through TAC, Madison has ranked among the top leaders in Black-owned media, a segue she made after a long run as a senior executive at NBCUniversal. Narendra Reddy, the current chief operating officer of TAC, will become chief executive officer of TAC and president of Primedia Studios. Nikos Tziotis has been named president of Primedia USA and he will handle global distribution efforts for TAC.

“When we launched The Africa Channel more than two decades ago, the idea was simple but ambitious: Africa deserved to be seen through a broader and more authentic lens and there was a global audience seeking that connection,” Madison said. “Primedia gives TAC the resources to take that mission to a new scale — with more programming, more platforms and more international reach. I look forward to remaining closely involved with Jonathan, Nikosand Narendra as we build the next chapter.”

Reddy’s appointment as CEO is designed to ensure consistency at the firm as the ownership changes hands.

“For 21 years, The Africa Channel has demonstrated the demand for compelling stories from Africa and the global diaspora,” Reddy said. “For distributors, advertisers and creators, our goal is to build an increasingly valuable bridge between Africa and global audiences. Our ambition is not simply to operate another television channel. It is to build a modern global media company around creativity from Africa and the increasingly interconnected diaspora.”

Cambodia’s Repression Hangs Over Francophonie Summit

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Click to expand Image French President Emmanuel Macron (left) with Cambodian Prime Minister Hun Manet during the 20th Francophonie Summit in Phnom Penh, November 15, 2026. © 2026 Chalinee Thirasupa/Reuters

As world leaders recently gathered in Phnom Penh to celebrate the 20th Francophonie Summit with the theme of peace and shared prosperity, French President Emmanuel Macron took to the podium to call publicly for an investigation into the killing of former Cambodian lawmaker and French citizen Lim Kimya. It was a rare public rebuke of the Cambodian government by a foreign leader.

A gunman killed Kimya in Bangkok, Thailand in January 2025. Kimya had served as a parliament member with the opposition Cambodia National Rescue Party before its 2017 dissolution.

A Thai court convicted the gunman, Ekkalak Paenoi, in October 2025 and issued arrest warrants for two Cambodian suspects, Ly Ratanakraksmey and Pich Kimsrin, both of whom have connections to high-ranking Cambodian officials. The Cambodian government—like many of its neighbors in Southeast Asia—is increasingly persecuting activists abroad.

The Cambodian government has yet to acknowledge the arrest warrants or start an impartial investigation. Both men remain at large. France has issued Interpol Red Notices for them on charges of complicity in premeditated murder.

Macron’s raising Kimya’s murder during a joint press conference with Prime Minister Hun Manet sends a strong message that the French government is taking Cambodian authorities’ attacks on activists abroad seriously. The Cambodian government should heed the call and independently and impartially investigate the murder.

But political repression by the Cambodian government runs deeper than one case. Ahead of 2027 local elections, the authorities are escalating their crackdown on the long suppressed political opposition. Young activists in particular, such as the five imprisoned Mother Nature Cambodia environmental activists, have been punished with baseless treason charges and lengthy prison sentences. Concerned governments should publicly and privately urge Cambodian authorities to release the more than 100 Cambodians currently imprisoned for exercising their fundamental freedoms.

Human Rights Watch was among the local and international organizations that pressed Cambodian authorities to release the Mother Nature activists and others ahead of the Francophonie Summit. The ruling Cambodian People’s Party, which exerts complete control over the country’s courts, was apparently unbothered by these efforts.

Other foreign leaders should amplify Macron’s message and publicly call on Cambodia’s government to reopen the space for the political opposition and respect the fundamental rights of all Cambodians, both at home and abroad.

BIG stacks timber volumes with alpine views for new neighborhood in switzerland

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

bjarke ingels group proposes four timber buildings in thun, switzerland with stepped hotel terraces overlooking the alps.

Bjarke Ingels Group / BIG unveils plans for four timber buildings in Thun, Switzerland, where the architecture firm will help shape the entrance to the city’s emerging Siegenthalergut district. The 13,000-square-meter development brings together two hotels and residential buildings, with stacked wooden volumes that project outward above the street. Set against the Bernese Alps, the architecture is defined by deep window grids and large terraces carved into the buildings’ stepped facades.

The proposal, developed with landscape architects August + Margrith Künzel, was selected through a competition organized by Frutiger AG and the city of Thun. Titled Nachwachsende Stadt, or Growing City, the scheme occupies one of the first two plots planned for the new neighborhood. The site sits opposite a sports center and the future headquarters of the Swiss Football Association to give the BIG-designed buildings a prominent position along the city’s western entrance.

At the southwest corner of the site, the team at BIG brings a three-star and a four-star hotel together under one roof. Shared amenity spaces cut diagonally through the structure, creating a series of setbacks that interrupt its otherwise regular timber grid. These shifts make room for outdoor terraces overlooking the neighboring sports facilities, with the Bernese Alps rising in the distance. A restaurant at the top of the building will give guests an even broader view of the surrounding landscape.

At street level, a cantilevered corner marks the main hotel entrance, opening onto a drop-off plaza beneath the projecting upper floors. Commercial spaces line the adjacent boulevard, bringing activity to the building’s ground floor. Across the facade, large square windows sit within deeply framed timber modules, while recessed portions reveal warmer, sheltered spaces behind the exterior grid. The alternating volumes give the hotel a sculptural profile that changes depending on where it is viewed from.

To the east, the remaining timber buildings accommodate residences with flexible layouts and projecting bays. Here, the same geometric language takes on a more domestic scale. Balconies extend outward from the facades, while recessed loggias create sheltered outdoor rooms. The shifting arrangement of these openings gives each elevation a different rhythm, with the projecting volumes bringing daylight deeper into the apartments.

The residential buildings face an internal courtyard, where winding paths move through gardens and planted gathering spaces. Renderings picture grassy areas beneath the timber facades, with balconies overlooking the shared landscape. Small plazas and seating areas are distributed throughout the development, connecting the homes to the surrounding neighborhood.

Of the project’s total 13,000 square meters, approximately 6,500 are dedicated to residential uses, alongside 6,000 square meters of hotel space and another 400 square meters for commercial activities.

The use of timber extends across all four buildings, establishing a consistent material language while helping reduce the development’s embodied carbon footprint. BIG has designed the timber elements for future disassembly and reuse, allowing components to be recovered as the buildings change over time. The deep balconies and roof overhangs also serve a practical purpose, providing natural shade in summer and shelter during the colder months.

Green roofs and rainwater retention systems will help manage water across the site, while approximately 1,800 square meters of photovoltaic panels are planned to generate renewable energy. The development is targeting Switzerland’s SNBS Gold sustainability certification.

The winning proposal will now progress through further design and approval stages, with construction of the district’s first buildings possible from 2028. For BIG, which opened its Zurich office in 2023, the project adds another timber-focused development to its growing Swiss portfolio, alongside the planned Dock A terminal at Zurich Airport.

Florida basketball could be without one of its key backcourt contributors heading into its highly anticipated matchup against No. 8 Houston after guard Boogie Fland aggravated a right shoulder injury during the top-ranked Gators' 86-78 victory over Notre Dame on Tuesday.

Fland initially injured his shoulder against FSU in Tallahassee on Nov. 10. The Florida point guard would miss their next outing against Merrimack on Nov. 13.

According to Zach Abolverdi, Fland is now considered a game-time decision for Wednesday's showdown against the Cougars, leaving his availability uncertain as Florida prepares for a rematch of the 2025 national championship game.

"I think he just kind of reaggravated the same thing," head coach Todd Golden said after the game. "I don't think it's anything too serious, but obviously with it being the shoulder, it was just bothering him and (it's) something that's kind of hard to play through. So we'll check him out tonight, see how he looks and we'll make a decision in the morning to decide if he can play tomorrow or not."

Florida, now 5-0, meets Houston, who is also undefeated at 4-0, in the semifinals Wednesday at 4:30 p.m. ET on ESPN, in a rematch of the 2025 national championship game.

"I don't think (Fland's absence) affected us too negatively to be honest," Golden said. "I thought our guards did a great job. Generally, Boog's an organizer offensively, kind of keeps us in our stuff, and I thought Denzel Aberdeen and Urban Klavzar did a good job that way. ... Fland entered Tuesday's contest averaging 10.0 points and 3.7 assists over three games this season. If the Gators are without him against Houston, head coach Todd Golden said sixth man Urban Klavzar would move into the starting lineup alongside Denzel Aberdeen.

Backpack’s US approval could threaten offshore exchanges’ grip on crypto futures trading

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The exchange plans to bring American and international traders into one regulated marketplace, potentially shifting liquidity and price discovery toward the US.

Crypto exchange Backpack has secured US regulatory approval to launch a futures exchange aimed at uniting American and international trading liquidity.

The Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) designated Backpack's TLD Markets as a regulated futures exchange on Nov. 16, three days after approving its affiliated clearinghouse, TLD Clearing.

The approvals establish the regulatory foundation for Backpack to offer leveraged crypto perpetual futures to American traders while connecting them to the company's international trading operations.

However, Backpack's ambitions extend beyond entering the American derivatives market. The company wants to consolidate its global trading business under a single US-regulated exchange, eliminating the separation between domestic and overseas customers.

“There will be no Backpack international vs Backpack USA. Our entire platform will be operating on our CFTC regulated venue.”

The move represents a significant shift for a company that began in Chicago nearly four years ago before expanding its operations overseas.

Ferrante recalled that Backpack was “forced offshore” early in its development, prompting the company to establish operations across international markets while pursuing regulatory approval.

He described the CFTC approval as the culmination of nearly four years of work to return to the United States.

The proposed structure could reshape how American traders access cryptocurrency derivatives, particularly perpetual futures, which allow investors to maintain leveraged positions without a fixed expiration date.

Historically, regulatory restrictions have separated American crypto derivatives markets from much of the trading activity concentrated on offshore exchanges.

This fragmentation can limit available liquidity, widen the difference between buying and selling prices, and increase the cost of executing large transactions.

Backpack wants to address this by bringing eligible American and international traders into a shared order book, allowing their buy and sell orders to interact within the same regulated marketplace.

Under such an arrangement, an American trader seeking to purchase Bitcoin perpetual futures could potentially execute against sell orders submitted by overseas participants.

A larger pool of competing orders could improve market depth, narrow spreads and reduce the price impact of substantial trades.

Backpack US President Mark Wetjen said the initiative could help shift “price discovery from offshore unregulated venues to the United States.”

The company also plans to offer round-the-clock trading, support fiat and cryptocurrency collateral, and introduce cross-margining, allowing eligible positions to share available collateral.

The exchange will operate through TLD Markets, while its US-registered clearinghouse, TLD Clearing, will handle qualifying transactions.

Backpack's international reach provides an existing business to support this ambition. The company says it serves customers across more than 150 countries and regions and has processed over $450 billion in trading volume.

However, the extent to which that activity will move onto the US-regulated platform remains unclear.

Backpack has not disclosed its initial perpetual futures contracts, which overseas participants will be eligible to access the unified order book, or when trading will begin. Its US website is also currently accepting waitlist registrations.

Guardrail para seguradora

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Posted on Nov 18 • Originally published at g.cloud

Guardrail para seguradora é um mecanismo técnico de governança de IA que impõe restrições operacionais e éticas em sistemas de inteligência artificial usados no setor, garantindo conformidade com as diretrizes da Superintendência de Seguros Privados (SUSEP) — especialmente nas áreas de subscrição, sinistros e atendimento ao cliente. Não há norma específica sobre guardrails na regulamentação atual, mas sua adoção é alinhada às boas práticas de gestão de riscos exigidas pela SUSEP.

Um guardrail é uma camada técnica de contenção que atua antes, durante e após a execução de um modelo de IA. No setor segurador, ele impede que sistemas automatizados tomem decisões contrárias à política de subscrição, apliquem critérios discriminatórios em avaliações de sinistro ou gerem respostas enganosas em canais de atendimento. Diferente de um filtro pós-processamento, um guardrail bem projetado opera em tempo real e é integrado ao fluxo de dados — por exemplo, bloqueando entradas com dados sensíveis não autorizados ou forçando revisão humana quando a confiança do modelo cai abaixo de 85%.

A SUSEP não usa o termo “guardrail”, mas exige mecanismos equivalentes por meio da Circular nº 695/2024, que obriga as seguradoras a instituir políticas de governança de tecnologias emergentes. O art. 5º exige “controles técnicos e procedimentais capazes de assegurar a integridade, confiabilidade e auditabilidade dos sistemas automatizados”. Isso inclui validação contínua de modelos, monitoramento de desvio de desempenho e mitigação de viés — funções centrais de qualquer guardrail robusto.

Comece com mapeamento de riscos por processo: subscrição (viés em scoring), sinistros (decisões inconsistentes com o contrato) e atendimento (geração de informações incorretas). Em seguida, adote camadas complementares: input validation (ex.: rejeição de CPF inválido ou dados biométricos não consentidos), model constraints (ex.: limitação de variáveis sensíveis no treinamento), e output moderation (ex.: verificação de conformidade com cláusulas padrão da SUSEP antes de emitir resposta). Ferramentas como IBM Granite podem ser configuradas com custom guardrails via RAG e prompt engineering controlado — desde que documentados e auditáveis.

Q: Existe penalidade específica para falha em guardrails?

A: Sim. Falhas que resultem em danos ao consumidor ou violação de deveres de boa-fé objetiva podem acarretar multa sob o art. 117 da Lei nº 13.709/2018 e sanções da SUSEP previstas na Resolução CNSP nº 403/2021.

Q: Guardrails são obrigatórios para pequenas seguradoras?

A: Sim. A Circular 695/2024 aplica-se a todas as sociedades supervisionadas pela SUSEP, independentemente do porte ou volume de operações digitais.

Q: Posso usar guardrails baseados em modelos estrangeiros?

A: Sim, desde que adaptados à legislação brasileira — especialmente à LGPD, ao Código de Defesa do Consumidor e às normas técnicas da SUSEP. A validação deve considerar contexto local (ex.: variação regional de risco, cláusulas obrigatórias em apólices).
