The agreement between Canada and China and the cordial relations on display by their leaders contrast with the current Canada-U.S. relationship.

Prime Minister Mark Carney of Canada and China’s leader, Xi Jinping, have struck an agreement to reduce some tariffs between their countries, a largely symbolic move that shows relations between the two countries are warming as Canada’s ties to the United States falter.

Germany 5-0 Netherlands: Jurgen Klopp sends clear message that spells trouble for Nations League rivals

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

By Oliver Thomas | 17 Nov 2026 09:46

Jurgen Klopp is in dreamland after witnessing a “truly extraordinary” performance from his Germany side in a statement 5-0 victory over the Netherlands in the UEFA Nations League on Monday.

The four-time world champions began the night sitting third in League A Group 2 and knew that they had to claim maximum points against the Oranje at the summit to secure top spot and qualify for the quarter-finals.

With so much at stake heading into the sixth and final matchday, Die Mannschaft rose to the occasion in prolific fashion, claiming their biggest win against their Dutch neighbours since 1959 when they won 7-0.

Derry Scherhant opened the scoring just before half time with a sensational maiden international goal, setting the tone for a dominant second-half display.

Overwhelmed by Germany’s relentless high press, the Netherlands had no answer as two goals from Florian Wirtz, and strikes from Nico Schlotterbeck and Brajan Gruda, sealed a statement triumph for Klopp’s side.

“What we’ve just witnessed was truly extraordinary,” the former Liverpool manager told reporters post-match. “The game was extraordinary, and incredibly difficult.”

“Against a really good opponent, you have to find a way to win. We had to adapt and make a few adjustments, some fairly significant ones, but we made them. We took away a lot of their strengths, and then we scored some incredible goals. That was unbelievable.

“It was a fantastic football match and a wonderful experience. I wouldn’t have ever dreamed of having the chance to do this in Berlin in front of a sold-out crowd. In the final international game of the year, it’s amazing.”

In addition to progressing to the quarter-finals, the result served as compelling evidence of Germany's renaissance and progress under Klopp, with his arrival fostering renewed optimism around his youthful squad.

Germany failed to win their opening two Nations League matches, but they ended their group campaign with 11 points (W3 D2 L1) and have conceded a competition-low three goals in six games.

"The moment I arrived here, I fell in love with this team at first sight,” said Klopp. “I'm at the beginning of this journey.

“I simply feel that we have a fantastic football team and a very good footballing idea that can work extremely well. If we can get these highly-talented players firing, then we're going to be a very difficult team to play against.

“We scored some incredible goals and, with increasing confidence, put in a great performance in front of a fantastic crowd. It’s been a lot of fun to work with this team, and we feel like we have a very special group of players here.

“But this is still only just the beginning – after six games, we can’t say that we’ve arrived where we want to be yet, as there are still lots of things we can improve on.”

"Everyone who was here today is going home with a smile, and won't be questioning the German national team for the next three or four months. That's a good thing," Klopp added when referring to the negativity that lingered previously around the national team.

Looking ahead to the quarter-final draw on Friday, Klopp added: "The lads can head home now with a smile on their faces as the group winners. We’ll watch the draw later this week and wait and see what sort of crunch match we’ll get, as there are only strong teams left.”

Germany, who have risen to 11th in the FIFA World Rankings, will play their two-legged quarter-final tie in March 2027, with a first-placed finish in League A rewarded with a second leg on home soil.

Freiburg’s Derry Scherhant celebrates dream Berlin homecoming

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

SC Freiburg attacker Derry Scherhant enjoyed an unforgettable evening in his hometown of Berlin on Monday, scoring Germany’s opening goal in a commanding 5-0 Nations League victory over the Netherlands. The 24-year-old subsequently reflected on his unlikely journey to the national team and took particular satisfaction in proving those who had once questioned his ability to become a professional footballer wrong.

Scherhant had arranged approximately 50 tickets for family members and friends ahead of the encounter at the Olympiastadion. The former Hertha BSC youngster, who grew up just five minutes from the venue, marked the occasion with an outstanding individual performance and a spectacular first-half goal.

Scherhant opened the scoring in the 41st minute after receiving a pass from Joshua Kimmich, cutting inside and dispatching a powerful effort into the upper-right corner. The Freiburg professional had already struck the outside of the post earlier in the half and subsequently played a decisive role in Germany’s fifth goal.

The performance represented another significant milestone in a remarkable international breakthrough that has gathered momentum since Jürgen Klopp took charge of the German national team.

As Get German Football News detailed in its comprehensive October feature on Scherhant’s unusual career trajectory, the winger’s development was anything but straightforward. Scherhant struggled to establish himself at Viktoria Berlin and Tennis Borussia before eventually finding his way into Hertha BSC’s academy in 2020. His early difficulties included repeated questions about his physical development and whether he possessed the necessary qualities to succeed at the professional level.

He ultimately earned a Bundesliga debut with Hertha, although regular opportunities remained elusive during the club’s turbulent relegation campaign in 2022/23. The subsequent spell in the 2. Bundesliga proved important to his development, and SC Freiburg eventually secured his services in the summer of 2025. The move to Breisgau provided Scherhant with a more stable environment, even if initially displacing established attacker Vincenzo Grifo presented another considerable challenge.

GGFN‘s 2026/27 tactical preview of SC Freiburg identified Scherhant as one of the club’s most impressive performers during preseason preparations. The former Hertha attacker subsequently consolidated his place in Julian Schuster’s starting XI.

A particularly impressive Bundesliga performance against Eintracht Frankfurt followed his first senior Germany nomination in September. Scherhant contributed a goal and an assist in the 2-2 draw, providing further evidence of his rapid development.

The remarkable progression continued at international level. Scherhant earned his senior Germany debut during Klopp’s first international window and impressed sufficiently to retain his place when the Bundestrainer reduced his squad ahead of November’s fixtures. Get German Football News subsequently reported on Scherhant and Freiburg teammate Philipp Treu’s appreciation of the supportive environment at their Bundesliga club. Both players credited Freiburg’s collective mentality and stable working conditions with helping them establish themselves at international level.

Scherhant also spoke enthusiastically about the prospect of representing Germany in Berlin. The winger celebrated his 24th birthday during the November international break and regarded the opportunity to return to the Olympiastadion as an extraordinary personal occasion. His performance against the Netherlands ultimately exceeded even those expectations.

The Freiburg attacker became only the third Berlin-born player to score for Germany in an international match staged in the capital, following Thomas Häßler against Bulgaria in November 1995 and Hans Ruch against Scotland in June 1929.

Klopp was particularly impressed by the contributions of Scherhant and fellow attacker Brajan Gruda. The Bundestrainer praised not only their technical abilities but also the considerable defensive work and intensity they contributed to Germany’s convincing victory.

Speaking after Monday’s victory, Scherhant recalled how frequently his chances of reaching professional football had been dismissed during his youth.

“A few years ago, people told me that I wouldn’t make it as a professional,” Scherhant explained. “Then I became a professional. And even then, some people were still saying that it wasn’t enough, that the Regionalliga was the highest level I’d ever reach. It’s simply unbelievable that I’m now standing here, with the satisfaction of having proved those people wrong. 99 percent would have said never. One percent might have said perhaps, if he works really hard.”

Klopp offered his own assessment of Scherhant’s contribution alongside Gruda.

“Derry Scherhant and Brajan Gruda, the way those two play football — my goodness,” Klopp remarked afterwards. “But the amount of work they put in and had to put in today was extraordinary. That’s what ultimately makes a team. Because there are other players who can play football too. But it’s the commitment and passion you bring to it that ultimately matter.”

Scherhant himself struggled to contain his delight after scoring in the stadium he once called home.

“What can I say?” Scherhant said. “It couldn’t have gone any better, scoring the opening goal here in my own city. You couldn’t have dreamed it up any better. It’s truly unbelievable. I had a tremendous amount of fun and I’m simply overjoyed.”

With his postponed birthday celebrations still awaiting him, Scherhant could finally look forward to an evening with family and friends after a homecoming that will remain among the most memorable moments of his young career.

The Homeownership Gap Between Young and Old Americans Has Doubled—These Cities Reveal Why

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The homeownership gap between younger and older Americans has more than doubled over the past four decades, with some of the nation's most expensive cities seeing the sharpest increases, according to a new report.

The analysis, from the Urban Institute, finds that nationally just 43% of households headed by adults aged 25 to 35 owned their homes in 2024, compared with 79% of those headed by adults 65 and older. The 35.8-percentage-point difference is more than twice the 17.5-point gap recorded in 1980.

But that divide pales in comparison to the gaping chasm in some of America's most expensive metropolitan areas. Between 1980 and 2024, the homeownership gap grew by 29 percentage points in Denver, 23 points in New York City, and 22 points in San Francisco, compared with 18.3 points nationally.

But those widening gaps also expose a deeper economic trade-off: The cities offering young Americans the greatest opportunities to earn more may be the very places preventing them from building wealth through homeownership

"The cities with the strongest labor markets tend to be the least affordable, so some will take the better job and rent indefinitely, giving up equity accumulation," explains Hannah Jones, senior economist at Realtor.com®. "Others will pass up higher-paying roles or move to cheaper, lower-opportunity markets in order to buy."

In 1980, younger households in Denver were nearly as likely as their older counterparts to own a home.

At the time, 55% of households headed by adults aged 25 to 35 owned their homes, compared with 67% of households headed by adults 65 and older.

By 2024, the younger group's homeownership rate had fallen to just 37%, while the older group's rate climbed to 78%, expanding the divide from 12 to 41 percentage points.

But the decline in young homeownership may reflect something besides worsening affordability: Denver's success in attracting young workers.

"Two things are likely at work. One is the affordability decline itself. The other is Denver’s growth as an economic hub that appeals to young workers, many of whom arrive as renters," says Jones. "That adds renters to the mix and dilutes the young homeownership rate, even for people who never intended to buy."

There, younger households were already far less likely to own homes in 1980, when their homeownership rate stood at 28%. By 2024, it had slipped only slightly, to 26%.

Older New Yorkers, meanwhile, saw their homeownership rate climb from approximately 43% to 63% over the same period.

"In New York, homeownership has always been expensive and out of reach for most young households, so there was little room to fall," Jones explains. "Young homeownership slipped only slightly, which suggests the current rate is close to a floor."

She suggests low COVID-19 pandemic-era mortgage rates may have helped some older households that had been waiting to purchase finally enter the market.

And regardless of how the divide developed, entering homeownership later can carry lasting financial consequences.

Separate research from Realtor.com, co-authored by Jones, found that purchasing a home by age 30 was associated with 22.5% higher net worth—approximately $119,000 more—by age 50, compared with buying in one's 40s, even after accounting for income, education, and marital status.

Those advantages can extend across generations, too. Children raised in homeowner households were 18.4 percentage points more likely to become homeowners themselves by age 35, the same research found.

One of the Urban Institute's most striking findings concerns a milestone that comes before homeownership: establishing an independent household.

In 1980, young adults in high-cost metropolitan areas were slightly more likely than their counterparts nationally to head their own households. By 2024, that relationship had reversed.

Just 41% of young adults in expensive markets were household heads, the lowest share among the study's housing cost categories.

Instead, more are living with parents, other relatives, or roommates. Research from Realtor.com backs this up, finding that a third of young adults lived with their parents across the country in 2025.

Yet expensive cities haven't lost their appeal. The share of young households living in high-cost markets actually edged up from 28% in 1980 to 31% in 2024, partly reflecting a broader shift toward urban areas.

What's changed is their housing tenure. In those markets, the homeownership rate among younger households fell from 40% to 32% over the same period.

So young adults are continuing to pursue opportunities in expensive cities, but fewer are establishing households of their own once they get there. And another finding suggests that rapidly rising prices may be changing the age composition of metropolitan areas, regardless of how expensive those places were to begin with.

Between 2014 and 2024, metropolitan areas experiencing faster home price appreciation generally saw declines in young adults' share of the population, the Urban Institute found.

The relationship held across high-, moderate-, and low-cost markets. But Jones cautions that the findings don't necessarily mean young people are being priced out.

"That can come from young people leaving, older people moving in, or both," she says. "Florida metros on the included chart may be an example. Their older populations are large and grew significantly during the pandemic, which could erode the young share on its own."

Indeed, the relationship between home prices and demographic changes could run in either direction.

"Young people may be fleeing places where costs are climbing, or older, more financially equipped buyers may be moving in and competing for homes," Jones says. "That pushes prices up, and the newcomers then make up a larger share of the population."

Determining whether younger residents are actually leaving would require examining changes in the number of young adults, not just their share of the population.

Still, other economic research suggests that housing costs can constrain where workers pursue employment.

A May 2025 analysis from the Federal Reserve Bank of Richmond found that geographic mobility has declined across all age groups since the 1990s. Among the contributing factors, researchers identified restrictions on housing supply in economically productive cities, where high costs can discourage workers from relocating to pursue better opportunities.

The researchers also identified another potential obstacle: Older homeowners have become less responsive to rising local housing costs, making them more likely to stay put even as prices climb. In supply-constrained markets, that can make it harder for newcomers to move in.

Meanwhile, a February 2026 analysis from the St. Louis Fed found that home prices had risen approximately 207% from 2000 to 2024, outpacing the roughly 155% increase in per-capita incomes.

Together, the findings suggest that the costs of entering high-opportunity housing markets could affect younger Americans' earnings prospects as well as their ability to build wealth.

There may be a way around that trade-off for some.

"Remote work may be loosening the link between where people live and where the jobs are, which could enable more households to achieve homeownership without giving up job opportunities," Jones says.

But that flexibility is not available to everyone. Workers whose jobs require them to be physically present may face a more difficult choice between buying a home where they can afford one and pursuing careers in the cities where their skills command the highest wages.

And unlike the wealth accumulated by longtime homeowners, the career opportunities they pass up may be difficult to recover later.

Singapore defends status as a ‘trusted business hub’ after the U.S. proposes extending AI chip controls to Southeast Asia

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The U.S. proposed tighter export controls on AI hardware on Monday (Nov. 16), mandating licenses for firms looking to ship advanced chips to four Southeast Asian nations: Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand and Indonesia.

The rule change comes days after reports of Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang’s ties to Megaspeed, a cloud computing firm which allegedly funneled over $7.5 billion of Nvidia chip shipments to Chinese firms via its regional subsidiaries. (Megaspeed says it is cooperating with U.S. inspections and denies wrongdoing)

The new rule also requires large data centers anywhere in the world to get a license if it wants to offer computing power to companies based in China.

Singapore, where Megaspeed is headquartered, is now trying to defend its reputation.

“Singapore is a trusted international business hub, underpinned by a strong rule of law,” a government spokesperson wrote in a press statement dated Nov. 16. “We take our international reputation seriously and do not condone companies using their association with Singapore to circumvent or violate the laws of other countries.”

The country added that it is engaging U.S. authorities to review details of the new measure, while working with affected companies to understand the potential impact on their businesses.

Thailand Vice Minister for Commerce, Kirida Bhaopichitr, told CNBC that they were ‘prepared’ for the new rule. “We don’t want to be a hub for transshipment of these goods from the U.S. to China, so we’ll more closely scrutinize these companies, especially data centers.”

Indonesia, too, said it was reviewing the rule, and “seeking industry input on its potential impact on operations and investment, including in the data centre sector”, according to an AFP report.

Malaysia and Indonesia have worked to step up efforts to curb chip smuggling. As part of an inquiry requested by the U.S. FBI to trace Nvidia chips bought by Megaspeed, Indonesian government officials raided the office of PT Megaspeed International Indonesia, the Indonesian subsidiary of Huang’s firm Megaspeed, earlier this year.

According to a Nov. 16 report by Singapore newspaper The Straits Times, Indonesian officials who inspected the premises found unused server racks at the site with missing graphics processing units (GPUs), which suggests that the chips were never installed there.

On Nov. 13, Malaysia’s Ministry of Investment, Trade and Industry (MITI) said it was carrying out checks with local customs officials to “determine whether any of the chips brought into Malaysia have since been re-exported”, and if so, if they were “in accordance with the law”.

Data center operators in Southeast Asia are also trying to figure out what the new rule means for their business.

“It’s still an interim rule,” Serene Nah, the APAC head of American data center provider Digital Realty, said at a roundtable at the launch of the Digital Realty Innovation Lab in Singapore on Nov. 17, the day after the measure was announced. “There might be ongoing items pertaining to registrations, licenses and things like that, but details are not out yet.”

The data center provider currently operates three facilities in Singapore, and entered Indonesia last March through a joint venture with Bersama Digital Infrastructure Asia, a Singapore-incorporated digital infrastructure firm. In June, it opened a campus in Cyberjaya, Malaysia, which is designed to support approximately 32 MW of data center capacity for hybrid cloud and AI workloads.

The firm’s executives remain positive that the U.S.’s new export rule won’t dampen the demand for AI compute in Southeast Asia, especially due to data sovereignty concerns among businesses and governments in the region.

“AI is pervasive,” said Chris Sharp, Digital Realty’s chief technology officer, at the Singapore facility launch. He pointed to financial services and healthcare players as their key customers in the region. “They want the full benefit of AI capabilities with zero exposure of their infrastructure publicly. They don’t want any data being routed over the internet.”

Global hyperscalers like AWS, Google and Microsoft, too, will likely continue looking to Southeast Asia to build out their data centers, given its abundance of cheap land and electricity.

“We certainly have and will see more enterprise customers wanting to deploy across Southeast Asia,” Nah concluded, adding that firms may keep their Asia headquarters in Singapore and their data center operations in another Southeast Asian country.

Angelica Ang is a Singapore-based journalist who covers the Asia-Pacific region.

Plants mix different breeding strategies to survive in a world with changing climates and fewer bees

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Simulations show plants that reject their own pollen and plants that self-fertilize can coexist for ages, swapping dominance without settling.

Apple trees have a strict biological rule for reproduction. They reject their own pollen. That means they need a completely different type of apple tree nearby to produce fruit.

While many wild plants rely on this exact same partner system, plenty of other plant species have evolved to drop the rule entirely, allowing them to successfully fertilize themselves.

A population holding both kinds was supposed to be passing through on its way to choosing one rule or the other.

In computer simulations of evolving plants, the mixed populations didn’t get there. With nothing in the environment changing, the two kinds kept trading dominance for as long as the simulations ran. Neither kind took over.

The researchers call it a volatile mixed mode. A mixed population in the wild that looks unstable, they wrote, may be in a lasting state of its own.

Tamar Friedlander, a senior lecturer at the Hebrew University of Jerusalem’s Robert H. Smith Institute of Plant Sciences and Genetics in Agriculture, led the work with Amit Jangid.

Ohad Noy Feldheim of the University’s Einstein Institute of Mathematics is the third author. Everything here happened inside a model.

In an interview with Earth.com, Friedlander said the result broke a habit of thought. Biologists expect that when a population has several options, some are better than others, she said.

Over the long run, the population is supposed to reach the better one and stay there. Earlier models had the mix fading away or holding steady at fixed proportions.

“Thus, it was very surprising that we didn’t get either of these under a large parameter regime,” she said.

The populations, in her words, “not only did not settle to either of the ‘pure’ strategies (only self-fertilization or only outcrossing) but also did not settle at fixed proportions of these strategies.”

The rejection system works like a lock and a set of keys. The female side of a flower makes a toxin that stops a pollen tube from growing. Each pollen grain carries proteins that disarm the toxins of other mating types but not its own.

About 40 percent of plant families use some version of this, including wild tomatoes, potatoes, apples, pears, citrus, and coffee. Washington State University‘s tree fruit site has a primer on why orchards need two varieties.

The system can break. A mutation that silences the toxin, or adds a key that fits the plant’s own lock, produces a plant that can fertilize itself.

That plant gains a sure route to seeds when pollen is scarce, but it passes on two sets of genes to each seed instead of one.

It pays for that with inbreeding. Seeds from self-fertilization carry more harmful mutations, and more of them fail.

The plants that reject their own pollen pay a different price. Their pollen is useless on every plant of their own mating type, so a type that grows common finds fewer partners.

Friedlander’s team simulated populations of 500 plants for 100,000 generations, discarding the first 50,000 as a warm-up.

Each plant carried two copies of the gene set, and each copy held one toxin and nine keys. Every protein was written as a string of 18 amino acids that could mutate.

Two dials set the outcome. One was how badly self-fertilized seeds suffer from inbreeding. The other was how loosely the keys fit the locks, which decides how many mating types a population can hold.

With heavy inbreeding costs and loose-fitting keys, every plant ended up rejecting its own pollen. With light costs, every plant ended up self-fertilizing.

In between lay the third state, where the share of self-rejecting plants rose and fell for the rest of the run and the number of mating types swung with it.

Types tended to vanish several at a time, in what the authors describe as avalanches. What triggers those collapses, they wrote, is still obscure.

A shortage of pollen was expected to push populations toward self-fertilization, since a plant that can pollinate itself doesn’t need a visitor. The model found the opposite.

When cross-pollen was scarce, the range of settings in which self-rejection won grew at the expense of the mixed state. Friedlander said the explanation lies in how the two sexes compete.

When pollen is plentiful, every female is sure of a partner and only the males compete, so female proteins evolve mainly to avoid matching their own pollen.

When pollen is scarce, females must compete too, which makes them easier to match and raises the number of mating types a population can support.

Researchers often treat self-fertilization as a plant’s backup plan for when cross-pollination fails, she told Earth.com. Self-fertilizing plants do turn up at the edges of a species’ range, where partners are few.

The move from rejecting self-pollen to accepting it has long been treated as a one-way street. A plant that gives up the rule usually sheds the flower traits that attract insects and purges some of its harmful mutations.

Across much of the model’s settings, though, the end state didn’t depend on the start, and a population that began self-fertilizing could evolve the rejection system from scratch.

The exception was the loosest-fitting keys, where the mutation needed for the reverse trip was so rare that the wait became impossibly long.

The model also held the cost of inbreeding fixed, so it describes the short term, before prolonged self-fertilization purges enough mutations to change the odds.

Mixed populations exist in the wild. Long-term studies that track one population’s proportions over time are lacking, the authors wrote, and those are what would test the prediction.

The model also predicts that mixed populations carry fewer and shorter-lived mating types than purely self-rejecting ones, and more inbreeding written into their genomes. Both could be checked in plant genomes.

Asked which population she would most like to watch, Friedlander pointed to wild tomato species growing in South America.

She also said the model doesn’t apply directly to orchards, where trees are genetically identical and do not evolve. Self-rejecting crops do depend on pollinators, she added, and a decline in them could cut yields.

A warming climate and fewer pollinating insects both change the pressures on how a plant reproduces, the authors wrote.

Whether real populations respond by picking a side, or by swinging between the two for generations, is what a long field study of those wild tomatoes could begin to answer.

The full study was published in the journal PRX Life.
