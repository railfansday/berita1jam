‘Dancing With the Stars’ Planning Huge Rule-Change Next Season to Stop These Celebs From Being Voted Off

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Dancing With the Stars has officially wrapped season 35, and the show is already preparing for season 36 next fall. And apparently producers are making a huge change to the voting process to prevent high-scoring couples (think: Jenna Dewan and Val Chmerkovskiy) from being eliminated too early in the competition.

“Producers have already discussed bringing back the judges’ save for next season. Again, it’s still early but that is more than likely going to be brought back,” a source tells The Sun. “They’ve already made it clear they want to shake it up next season, drastically.”

The judges’ save (which was previously in effect from Season 28 to Season 32) lets the judges step in and decide which of the bottom two pairings goes home after fans have already voted. Ultimately, it’s a way to counter fan favoritism overriding high scores.

DWTS abandoned the judges’ save because it ultimately gave the experts more power than viewers at home. As executive producer Deena Katz told TV Line, “I think it should be about America. And at the end of the day, the judges' save sometimes isn't what America wanted. And [eliminations are based on] half the judges and half America voting, and so that's kind of the reason we did it.”

FYI, Dancing With the Stars’ UK version, Strictly Come Dancing, still uses the judges’ save, and judge Shirley Ballas explained to Extra, “I think it's good that the audience choose. They put two in the bottom. That was their choice. And then, at the end of the day, we get to save the dancer that we think is the best. Because sometimes, on your kind of show... they could send all the good dancers home. And then, you know, you're left with a different final. So it's important that there's something for everybody.”

A 5-Minute Stretch Routine to Relieve Tight Joints and a Stiff Back

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

If you’ve been feeling ultra stiff lately, a five-minute yoga-inspired stretching routine break to release physical tension can feel like a full-body reset, helping you feel better in your body—and even more productive—so you can do all of your favorite activities.

This short practice is ideal when you need a short break during the workday, but it can also serve as a grounding moment before you sit at your desk or when you’re winding down in the evening. You only need a yoga mat and a sturdy surface to practice on. Come exactly as you are.

Lie on your back with your arms and legs straight, as if you were in Savasana. Take some small movements, maybe wiggling your fingers and toes or moving your head side to side. Then let yourself relax for a moment. Inhale as you reach your hands overhead and your feet away from you, stretching your entire body from head to toe. Exhale and release.

Bend your knees, plant your feet on the mat about hip-distance apart, and rest your arms at your sides. Inhale, press your hands and feet into the mat, and lift your hips in Bridge Pose. Exhale and lower your hips to the mat.

Separate your feet slightly wider than your hips. Bend your elbows and draw your arms into a cactus shape. Exhale and allow your knees to lower toward the left. Inhale and return your knees to center. Exhale and lower your knees toward the right. Repeat Windshield Wipers two more times on each side.

Hug both knees toward your chest, grasping your shins or the backs of your thighs with your hands.

Come to a comfortable sitting position. Lift your shoulders toward your ears, then roll them back, down, and forward. Continue drawing circles with your shoulders in one direction for a few rounds, then switch directions.

Interlace your fingers behind your head with your elbows out to the sides. Exhale as you draw your navel toward your spine, round your back, and lower your chin toward your chest in a Cat Pose variation. Inhale as you lift upright and arch your back, drawing your elbows back slightly in a Cow Pose variation. Repeat a few times.

Inhale and reach your arms overhead. Exhale and twist your chest toward the left. Place your right hand on your left knee and your left fingertips on the mat behind you. Take a few breaths here.

Still sitting, come back to center and bring your arms into a cactus shape. Open your hands as wide as you can, spreading your fingers apart. Then shut your hands into tight fists. Repeat opening and closing your hands as many times as feels good.

Sit on the mat with your knees bent, your feet flat on the mat, and your hands on the mat behind your hips, fingertips pointing toward you or toward the back of the mat. Press into your hands and feet as you lift your hips off the mat. Draw your chin toward your chest and gaze forward. Take a few breaths here, then lower your hips to the mat.

Draw the soles of your feet together. Inhale and lift your chest. Your feet can come closer to you or stay farther away. Exhale and fold forward, grasping your ankles or feet in Bound Angle Pose. You can stack your fists on top of each other and rest your forehead on them. Breathe here.

Lie on your back with your arms resting at your sides and your palms pressing into the mat. Bend your knees toward your chest and straighten your legs so your feet are reaching toward the ceiling. Keep your legs slightly bent if it’s more comfortable. Take a few breaths here, then shake out your arms and legs and lower your feet to the mat.

Lie on your back, resting your arms, legs, hands, and feet against the mat in Savasana. Or find a comfortable seated position. Take a few deep breaths before returning to the day.

What Is Smart Aging? Experts Explain the Skin Care Shift Replacing Antiaging

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Hailey Bieber’s preferred term reframes aging as prevention, protection, and smarter skin care.

Every item on this page was chosen by an ELLE editor. We may earn commission on some of the items you choose to buy.

Beauty terminology has evolved quite rapidly in recent years—particularly around aging. What was long labeled antiaging has shifted toward more realistic definitions of what happens to skin and the body as we get older. But the latest buzzword, longevity, as ubiquitous as it’s become, doesn’t exactly fill the gap for those seeking ways to keep skin, hair, and the body looking youthful. Hailey Bieber’s recent use of the term “smart aging” just may be the word substitute the category has been missing.

Natalia Guzman, FNP-C, CANS, a board-certified nurse practitioner at Atelier Aesthetix in Beverly Hills, explains that smart aging is a proactive, science-based approach to maintaining healthy, youthful-looking skin while embracing the natural aging process. “Rather than trying to ‘stop’ aging altogether, which is biologically impossible, smart aging focuses on understanding how our skin changes over time and making informed decisions to preserve a healthy, strong, overall youthful appearance,” Guzman tells ELLE.

From a dermatologic perspective, Dr. Brianna Olamiju, a board-certified dermatologist with Spring Street Dermatology in NYC, offers this explanation: “Smart aging is about being proactive, making informed skin care choices, and supporting the skin’s natural functions over time. It involves protecting the skin from environmental damage, maintaining a healthy skin barrier, stimulating collagen production when appropriate, and addressing concerns such as fine lines, wrinkles, uneven pigmentation, and loss of elasticity,” says Olamiju. Ultimately, she says, smart aging is about helping your skin look and feel its best at every stage of life while embracing the natural aging process.

Guzman emphasizes that aging beautifully isn’t about looking young. “It’s about looking and feeling like the best version of yourself at every stage of life.”

As skin evolves, collagen production gradually declines, cellular turnover slows, and environmental factors such as UV exposure, pollution, and lifestyle habits contribute to visible signs of aging. Guzman adds that smart aging means addressing these changes strategically through preventative skin care, healthy lifestyle choices, and, when appropriate, evidence-based aesthetic treatments. “I like to think of smart aging as investing in your skin’s future and being proactive rather than waiting until significant changes occur and attempting to reverse them,” Guzman says. “A lot of the time, the latter takes much more work, and sometimes it cannot be completely corrected.”

According to Guzman, the biggest difference between smart aging and traditional antiaging is the philosophy behind it. “For decades, the beauty industry has promoted the idea that aging is something we need to fight, prevent, or erase,” says Guzman. The term “antiaging” often focuses on correcting visible signs of aging, such as wrinkles, volume loss, pigmentation, and skin laxity.

Both Guzman and Olamiju agree that smart aging takes a more holistic, comprehensive, and realistic approach. Olamiju says that anti-aging creates the expectation that we should try to look younger indefinitely. “Smart aging shifts the focus toward skin health, prevention, and maintaining long-term skin quality. Rather than viewing wrinkles, fine lines, and changes in skin textureas something that must be eliminated, smart aging encourages us to support the skin as it naturally evolves. It is less about fighting aging and more about aging well,” she explains. This approach, she adds, also emphasizes consistency, prevention, and individualized treatments rather than chasing every new product or cosmetic trend.

Guzman adds that smart aging, instead of fighting age, means working with the skin’s biology to help it function and look its best for as long as possible. “The main difference is that we’re not simply chasing wrinkles. We're creating a long-term strategy that prioritizes skin quality, natural-looking results, and overall skin health,” Guzman emphasizes. “To me, anti-aging is often about correcting what we see today, while smart aging is about preventing and protecting what we’ll see tomorrow.”

Olamiju and Guzman are completely aligned on the first step in a smart aging routine: wearing sunscreen daily. “The foundation of smart aging starts with a consistent skincare routine and healthy lifestyle habits. Daily broad-spectrum sunscreen with an SPF of at least 30 is arguably the most important step because ultraviolet exposure is one of the leading contributors to premature skin aging, including wrinkles, loss of elasticity, and uneven pigmentation,” says Olamiju. Guzman adds that UV exposure contributes significantly to collagen breakdown and changes in skin texture, noting that sunscreen is essential even on cloudy days.

Olamiju also recommends incorporating topical antioxidants, such as vitamin C, to help protect against environmental oxidative stress, along with moisturizers containing ingredients like ceramides and hyaluronic acid to support hydration and the skin barrier. “Retinoids, including retinol and prescription tretinoin, can be beneficial for improving fine lines, skin texture, and collagen production, provided they are appropriate for the individual,” Olamiju describes.

Once your at-home routine is in place, Guzman recommends strategically incorporating preventive and corrective aesthetic treatments. “Depending on the patient’s age, anatomy, and concerns, treatments such as neuromodulators, collagen-stimulating injectables, microneedling, and energy-based devices may help maintain or improve certain aspects of skin appearance,” says Guzman. The key, she adds, is choosing the right treatment for the right patient rather than following every new aesthetic trend. Olamiju includes chemical peels in a very similar list of in-office treatments, depending, of course, on an individual’s concerns, skin type, and goals.

Finally, the experts agree: don’t underestimate lifestyle. Adequate sleep, regular exercise, balanced nutrition rich in antioxidants and healthy fats, avoiding smoking, managing chronic stress, and limiting excessive alcohol consumption all contribute to overall health and can influence how our skin looks and functions.

The best time to start is earlier than most people think, Olamiju shares. Ideally in your 20s, when prevention can have a meaningful long-term impact. “This does not mean that someone in their 20s needs an elaborate skincare routine or aggressive cosmetic treatments. Rather, it is an opportunity to establish healthy habits, including daily sunscreen use, gentle cleansing, and consistent moisturizing,” says Olamiju.

Guzman advises starting a smart aging routine before you feel like you need to correct something, but it’s also never too late to begin. “I encourage patients to think of smart aging as something that evolves with them rather than a routine that begins at a specific birthday,” she adds, offering this decade-by-decade breakdown:

In your 20s. The priority should be prevention. This means establishing healthy skincare habits, wearing sunscreen consistently, maintaining hydration, and introducing targeted active ingredients when appropriate. At this stage, the goal isn’t to aggressively treat aging. It's to protect the skin and build a strong foundation.

In your 30s. You may begin noticing subtle changes in skin texture, fine lines, pigmentation, or elasticity. This is often a good time to reassess your skin care routine and consider professional treatments based on individual needs. It may also be time to consider a little Botox, energy-based treatments, and biostimulators, all depending on the individual’s needs.

A horror short rooted in the toll of sickle cell disease on India’s tribal communities has won the inaugural WIF India x Google Flow Filmathon.

Syam Manohar’s black comedy “Kuli” placed second, earning INR800,000 ($8,300), and Nikhil Shinde’s animated “Pedals of Joy” came third with INR400,000 ($4,200). Women in Film India (WIF India) ran the Filmathon with Google Flow to open up AI filmmaking tools to emerging storytellers around the country. The call drew more than 850 registrations, and a seven-member jury that included directors Shakun Batra and Pan Nalin and Cannes Next head Sten Saluveer narrowed the field to five. The finalists were showcased at the International Film Festival of India (IFFI) in Goa. Aamir Khan Productions CEO Aparna Purohit delivered a keynote at the event.

“Sikkal” had its beginnings in West Africa, where Banerjee saw how hard sickle cell disease hit families and whole communities. She started developing a project called “Sickle” there with a friend who serves as a UNHCR Goodwill Ambassador, but an emergency pulled her back to India before it went further. Back home, she learned that the disease also weighs heavily on India’s tribal populations, many of whom know little about it and have poor access to treatment.

During her research, a tribal affairs official referred to the disease as “Sikkal,” and Banerjee took the word as her title. Loosely based on one real family, the folk-horror film follows a brother haunted by visions of his dead sister and looks at how poverty and a lack of medical care can leave a child to suffer untreated. Banerjee wants the film to add to the debate around World Sickle Cell Day and the 2027 Global Congress, whose theme focuses on transforming sickle cell care.

A Kolkata native who grew up in Pondicherry, Banerjee now makes her home in Port Blair in the Andaman and Nicobar Islands, where her husband is posted with the Indian Air Force, and divides her time between the islands, Delhi and the Nilgiris. Her grandparents produced Bengali-language films. She started her debut novel, “Songs from Then,” at 29 and saw it published at 44. The book follows women caught up in war and driven from their homes during three uprisings and genocides of the past. A would-be producer who showed interest stripped out its core, then told her to write a screenplay before talking about her vision.

That exchange pushed her toward filmmaking. At 45 she won a scholarship to the New York Film Academy and went back to the classroom alongside much younger students, later working in New York, Ghana and Japan. Her short “Again Maybe” screened at Ghana’s National Theatre, and another short, “Another Day in Paradise,” sits in the Women Voices Now library.

“Returning to India, however, was a different story. Somehow, everything began to shrink,” Banerjee said. “Despite the experiences I had accumulated, I found myself outside the industry, without work and, more painfully, without the contacts or networks that are often necessary to find one’s footing.”

Then she came across WIF India. By that point, she said, AI was all she had left.

“My personal experience with AI has been deeply moving. I have used it as a companion through my struggles with mental illness, and later, as a creative tool that helped me translate the images in my head into something tangible,” Banerjee said.

Banerjee now sees “Sikkal” as a calling card for the many stories of hers that remain unmade. “Opaar – The Other Bank,” her first feature, adapted from her novel, is looking for production partners and funding, and she is getting ready to shoot “Faasla,” a segment of an anthology about love and human connection.

“Because, at the end of the day, I want to make films with humans. AI has simply given me a way to begin,” Banerjee said.

Man City were found to have artificially inflated sponsorship agreements and pumped money into the club from its Abu Dhabi owners via disguised payments in a bid to swerve Premier League spending rules.

Man City have continued to insist they had 'irrefutable evidence' proving their innocence, but if the club loses its appeal, the Premier League have a range of sanctions at their disposal – including relegation or expulsion.

Man City face Arsenal in a crucial match on Sunday as they look to make inroads in the Gunners six point lead at the top of the Premier League

Haaland had pushed back against suggestions the case had affected their form during the international break, while stressing he was committed long-term to the club.

The forward's first answer to Norwegian broadcaster TV2 had spanned two minutes and 14 seconds, as he reeled off the teams he had represented from 2009 to 2018, the years covered by the charges after he was asked about the club's situation.

'In 2009, I played for Bryne U9s. In 2010, the U10s. In 2011, the U11s. 2012, the U12s, 2013, the U13s. Shall we continue? We don't need the whole list. 2014, the U14s. 2015, the U15s and the national team,' Haaland said.

'Then the U16s, I think. In 2016, I moved up to Bryne's first team, second team and youth team. In 2017, I went to Molde. In 2018, I was with Molde. The first team, Molde 2 and a bit with the youth team.

'So this is a question I struggle to answer because it's a legal battle…

'It's a case between the Premier League and Manchester City that a panel will deal with. Am I supposed to answer that? I'm not really sure.'

He continued: 'It's a difficult question to answer when I don't really know that much about it.

'I assume the answers will come… I don't know when. I haven't read much about it.

'To be completely honest, I'm focused on trying to perform for Manchester City.

The Man City star, who signed a nine-and-a-half-year contract in late 2024, told Norwegian television earlier this month that his focus was on helping the team to turn their form around.

The draw against Napoli leaves Man City lying sixth in the Champions League table - which would be enough to see Maresca's side to qualify for the last-16.

Man City face a crucial clash on the weekend when they face reigning champions Arsenal in the Premier League, as they look to halve the six-point gap to the leaders at the top of the table.
