Common reasons your Roku TV can turn on by itself and how to fix it

📅 Nov 14, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Some people have been experiencing random power-ons recently.

You're sound asleep in the middle of the night when, all of a sudden, your TV turns on. You know you turned off the TV before you went to bed, no one else is awake, and there's nothing near your remote to accidentally push the power button. What — or who — could have turned on the TV? A bored spirit haunting your home? A burglar looking to take a quick break?

Those scenarios are highly unlikely. But, unfortunately, some Roku TVs and Roku streaming device owners have been jolted out of their slumber by those products inexplicably powering on randomly over the past few months. And no one really knows why it's happening. There may be a few explanations, however, and a couple of potential fixes to try if your Roku device has gone rogue during the wee hours.

When Roku OS version 15.3 went live in August 2026, owners of Roku TVs and Roku streaming devices began reporting these middle-of-the-night disturbances. Not every Roku TV or streaming player has been impacted by this supposed 15.3 bug, but the timing of the reports with the software update seems more than coincidental.

What's odd about that, though, is that there was nothing in the 15.3 update that involved autoplay features or even power management. So, in theory, the changes that were made to the Roku OS shouldn't have spurred these instances of people's Roku TVs "waking up" without being powered on by anyone (or anything) and playing a TV show or movie. But we all know that software updates often introduce bugs with no real rhyme or reason, and that feels like the most logical explanation for these strange occurrences.

Having your Roku TV's HDMI-CEC feature enabled is another very plausible reason the TV powers on randomly. If you're not familiar with HDMI-CEC, it's basically a chain that links all your A/V components with built-in HDMI switching (like soundbars, gaming consoles, receivers and speakers) since they're all connected via HDMI cables. And this allows all of those devices to be controlled with one remote.

So, when you power on your Roku TV, for example, HDMI-CEC essentially notifies all the other connected components to turn on. All it takes is one glitchy signal to confuse the whole chain, which could lead to a random power-up. And there is a history of HDMI-CEC causing strange switching between inputs and components, as well as phantom power-ons. Samsung Anynet+, Sony BRAVIA Sync, LG SimpLink and Roku 1-Touch Play are all just different names for the same HDMI-CEC standard, but each has its own custom software added to it to create the proprietary HDMI-CEC feature for each brand of smart TV.

Unfortunately, compatibility sometimes becomes an issue unless all of your connected devices are made by the same company (though even that doesn't guarantee incident-free performance). This can cause signal miscommunication that results in the random behavior of your Roku TV or streaming device.

There are other possible explanations for your Roku TV turning on by itself. For example, the Fast TV Start feature is a proprietary power-management setting that keeps your Roku TV's OS on in the background even when you shut off the TV.

This allows the TV to turn on instantly without any boot time, and it keeps the TV on alert for voice commands to turn the TV back on. Fast TV Start has also seemingly caused phantom power-on problems in conjunction with the Roku OS 15.3 update, as the always-on network connection kept open the door for overnight glitches.

You may try all of these suggestions and still not have much luck because the root of the issue has not been "officially" identified by Roku, or anyone else, for that matter. And without the root cause, there remains no guaranteed fix. But these possible solutions are certainly worth trying and just might do the trick.

The first thing to try, if you haven't already, is updating your Roku TV's OS to version 16, which was released in October. Fixing the 15.3 bug wasn't part of the update, and no reports have surfaced that OS 16 patched the automatic power-on problem, but it's definitely worth trying. The worst-case scenario is that your Roku TV's OS is fully updated.

Next, disable 1-Touch Play (HDMI-CEC) not just on your Roku TV but on all the other components connected to the TV, regardless of brand. This includes your A/V equipment and gaming consoles. To do so on your Roku TV, go to Settings > System > Control other devices and uncheck 1-Touch Play. Also disable Fast TV Start, which you can find under Settings > System > Power > Fast TV Start, then either clear the Enable Fast TV Start option or check Off to disable it.

If these don't work, try unplugging the TV when you're done watching it each night (no, not ideal), using a smart plug to automatically cut the power for you or consulting Roku's tech support portal at support.roku.com for further assistance.

Liverpool transfer news: 'Significant' interest in £29m centre-back as Andoni Iraola ponders future without Virgil van Dijk

By Lewis Nolan | 14 Nov 2026 23:43

Liverpool are keen on Lens centre-back Pierre Ismaelo Ganiou despite interest from a number of other clubs, the latest report has claimed.

The Premier League is now on pause for the November international break, and that has given Reds supporters the chance to assess the squad.

Andoni Iraola has made a positive start to life at Anfield, with the team's defence having notably improved, led by the likes of Virgil van Dijk and Ronald Araujo.

However, Van Djik has entered the final year of his contract, while Araujo is only on loan from Barcelona and could return to the Spanish side at the end of the season.

Amid uncertainty regarding the future of their defence, Football Insider report that the Reds are one of several clubs keen on signing Lens defender Ganiou, who is valued by Transfermarkt at just over £25m.

Ganiou does not have an extensive history of top-flight football considering he has played just 28 games in Ligue 1 in his entire career, though he has been impressive for Lens.

In five league matches for the French side this season, the 21-year-old has won 69% of his duels, including 73% of his ground duels.

Liverpool have typically looked to sign aerially dominant defenders, so while the Frenchman boasts an impressive 64% duel success rate in the air, he only averages 1.8 aerial duels won per game.

Van Dijk has won an average of 3.8 duels in the air in the league so far, while Jeremy Jacquet has won 2.6 aerial duels in the Premier League, and the Reds would almost certainly aim to get Ganiou closer to those figures.

While Van Dijk is 35 and is into the final year of his contract, he has shown no signs of physical decline, and he has expressed a desire to remain at Anfield.

If he was to extend his stay on Merseyside, the Reds may not need to delve into the market for another centre-back.

Joe Gomez is expected to leave, and he could be replaced in the squad by Araujo, which would then leave the club in need of a right-back.

Jacquet and Giovanni Leoni will be part of the squad for the coming seasons, while many believe that Ifeanyi Ndukwe could play a role for the team once his loan at Levante ends.

England's Euro 2028 mission starts NOW! Thomas Tuchel's men must beat Spain to prove they can compete with the best - or risk their 'DNA' cursing them yet again, writes OLIVER HOLT

📅 2026-11-14  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Published: 16:10 EST, 14 November 2026 | Updated: 19:04 EST, 14 November 2026

It is four months, almost to the day, since England shrank from the call of history and lost their World Cup semi-final to Argentina in Atlanta but Thomas Tuchel may not believe that the Nations League tie against Spain in the Metropolitano on Sunday evening represents his best shot at redemption for the way things played out that afternoon in America’s South.

That is largely because Tuchel does not appear to believe he has anything to redeem himself for. The manner of his side’s capitulation - and the substitutions he made - in the Mercedes-Benz Stadium last July enraged the nation, but Tuchel said the fault was in the damned DNA of the English, that we were underlings, and that our players found it constitutionally impossible to keep hold of the ball.

Whatever Tuchel thinks, the fact remains that the tie against Lamine Yamal, Fabian Ruiz, Pedri and Mikel Oyarzabal and the rest of the world champions at the home of Atletico Madrid offers Tuchel and his players the opportunity to encourage themselves and their fans that they are not destined to be also-rans wired to fall at the final hurdle for evermore.

Spain are formidable opposition. They are on a record-breaking run of 43 matches unbeaten and have won their last 12 in succession. They haven't drawn a game since their June 15 game against Cape Verde in the World Cup. Their last defeat was on March 22, 2024, when they lost 1-0 to Colombia in a friendly. Some ask whether they are the best international side the world has ever seen.

‘Spain seem unbeatable,’ Tuchel said, when he walked into the press room at the Metropolitano on Saturday night and heard hymns of praise being lavished upon the opponents he is about to face.

‘They have a very strong identity and a very strong mentality. We are trying to take it away from them. We are up for it. We are up for the fight. We are up for a spectacular match. We can see how far we can push the limits with the world champions in Madrid. The group is full of self-belief and rightly so.’

England must beat Spain to prove they can compete with the best - even if they have flaws

Thomas Tuchel must show he can find a way around the limitations presented by the 'DNA' he has complained about

This may be the toughest game that Tuchel’s England side play until the European Championships in 2028, where the final will be contested at Wembley, and they will play all their games at home. They need to go into that tournament with the belief that they can beat the best when the pressure is at its highest.

Beating Spain away in the final game of the group phase of the Nations League is not quite the same as a critical match at a major tournament but it is as stiff a challenge as Tuchel is going to face until then. Beating Croatia 7-0 in front of gangs of schoolkids in Rijeka last month was hailed as the best England performance of the Tuchel era. Which says it all, really.

It was a great performance in a relatively meaningless match. Now it is time for him to show that he can marshal an England side to succeed against the very best, not fall apart when the prize is in sight. His protestations of powerlessness in the aftermath of the Argentina defeat were not a good look for a coach who was hired with the idea that his coaching prowess and his game management could get England over the line.

Has he learned anything from the manner of the Argentina defeat? ‘This game against Spain will be proof of concept at the highest level,’ he said, ‘but we are up for it. I think we have taken the next step after the World Cup. The best thing is to trust our process and trust ourselves. We need to be clear about how we want to impose ourselves and I think that is all visible.’

Now it is time for him to show that he can find a way past the limitations of England’s footballing DNA, find a way to compete with Spain and France when it really matters, and find a way to end the culture of near misses that has come to be England’s lot in the last decade of appearances in semi-finals and finals.

England have played six matches since that day of crushing disappointment in Atlanta and scored 22 goals. But in the only one of those matches that really mattered, they lost 3-2 to Spain at Wembley in the opening tie of their Nations League campaign at the end of September, a game in which they were largely outclassed by Luis de la Fuente’s team.

Spain are what England have to aim for. Tuchel mentioned their example and their ability and their mindset straight after the semi-final loss to Argentina. ‘Ball possession plays a crucial role,’ he said at his post-match press conference in Atlanta. ‘It’s maybe not in our DNA like it is in our Spanish DNA or in our Argentinian-Brazilian DNA, to take the ball and control the game with the ball.’

This may well be their toughest game until the final of the 2028 European Championships, if they get that far

The England coach said more recently that England need to go back to trusting a traditional style that emphasises direct, high-intensity physicality rather than trying to copy Spanish patience in ball-retention. England, though, were taught a lesson in sophistication by the Spanish in September.

And for all the attention lavished on Yamal, Spain’s defence is the best in the world. Rodri, the shield for that defence and also the world’s best in the role, is not fit for this game but even without him, Spain’s back-line inspires an awful lot more confidence than England’s.

‘They were the best in the World Cup at the back,’ Declan Rice, England’s central midfielder, said on Saturday. ‘Defence wins you titles. Everyone talks about their attacking talent but you look at their central defenders, their full backs and the way their midfielders contribute to the defence, too.’

That defence will have to stop England’s resurgent front line, led by Ballon d’Or winner Harry Kane, who could be flanked by Anthony Gordon and Morgan Rogers and backed up by the supreme talent of Jude Bellingham just behind them.

Bellingham’s improving relationship with Tuchel, which cannot have been harmed by the inclusion of Bellingham’s brother, Jobe, in this squad, is another reason to hope that England still retain the capacity to improve after the World Cup. The elder Bellingham has become England’s most influential player.

England at their best would be one of the few teams capable of extending them. Rice said on Saturday that the game between the two sides was ‘massive’. England need to prove they can find a way to get closer to the world champions. To beat them, Rice said, would be a ‘massive statement’.

‘Playing against them is a great incentive to win. It will show where we are as a group. We are trying to attack teams full throttle and with players like Harry Kane, Jude Bellingham and Morgan Rogers in our attack, it feels as if it is going to take off one night and maybe tomorrow night will be the night. We can beat Spain. For sure, we can.’

Tuchel needs to prove England can win, not just be noble contenders. They need to prove that their DNA need not be a curse.

The seasonal decline in housing inventory is in full force

Inventory declined to 895,988 as mortgage rates reversed higher to 7.50%. New listings remained fell slightly year over year to 53,162, while weekly pending sales showed 4 weeks of stabilization.  AI Summary

Even with volatility in mortgage rates last week, the seasonal decline in inventory is in full force. Weekly housing demand has continued to stabilize over the last four weeks and the new listing data is not showing any stress in the housing market. We had a lot of crazy things happen last week — in fact I’m writing this article still stuck in Dallas because of the FAA’s nationwide ground stop — but housing data never sleeps, so let’s take a look.

Housing inventory has picked up recently, after mortgage rates went above 6.64% and then shot up toward 7.60%. Inventory growth had been very tame this year, with some weeks negative year over year. As housing inventory returns to normal levels, it takes weaker demand to get stronger growth and rates moving toward 7.60% did that. Still, it’s more about sales taking longer to happen and demand being hit, because new listings data didn’t budge much recently. Now, we are in the full-blown seasonal decline as we get ready to prep for spring 2027.

Inventory growth is running at 7.11% over last year even with easier comps, as last year demand was running toward a 9-month high in December of 2025.

New listings have been in their seasonal decline since June and nothing abnormal with this data line has happened in 2026; in fact, it’s been the best year of new listings since 2022. One concern I had about higher rates is that more people might simply not list their homes — as they did in the second half of 2022 due to higher rates. However, that hasn’t happened; we did see a more noticeable year-over-year decline this last week, but for the most part it’s been a healthy year.

What’s keeping Bitcoin buyers away despite 2 leverage flushes in 5 days?

📅 Nov 14, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Read the best crypto stories of the day in less than 5 minutes. Join to get it daily in your inbox.

Read the best crypto stories of the day in less than 5 minutes. Join to get it daily in your inbox.

On 29th October, the largest hack in crypto’s history took Bitcoin to a low near $76,200. A few days later, on 5th November, an AI-driven tech crash spilled into crypto. The Nasdaq 100 fell by 6.4%, while semiconductor stocks tumbled by 12%.

This chaos set off leveraged liquidations of roughly $1.1 billion and pushed Bitcoin’s price below $74,000.

Two different shocks, a historical Binance breach, and an AI token sell-off. Though they seem unrelated on the surface, this time they were actually the catalyst for the same event. What Bitcoin saw was a surge in speculative positioning, but what the king coin did not see was a buyer.

The market’s first take on the Binance hack was that crypto had passed a test. Bitcoin fell by about 6% from its 28th October close — a fraction of the 15% to 39% drawdowns after the controversies surrounding Celsius, FTX, BlockFi, and Bybit.

Matt Mena, Crypto Research Strategist at 21Shares, told AMBCrypto,

What we find most encouraging is how the market handled the worst of it. The largest hack in crypto’s history caused a far smaller drawdown than past crises; the protocols kept running, and none of the structural drivers broke.

Unfortunately, institutional resilience is not retail demand. The market simply treated a smaller slump as evidence of Bitcoin’s underlying strength. However, whether anyone wanted to buy Bitcoin [BTC] was a separate question, one the hack never answered.

Still, institutional strength hasn’t eased the feeling of dread felt by many retail investors. And, a major factor can explain why.

The most important signal is what happened after the liquidations: Nothing. Bitget Wallet’s analysts put it plainly that the leverage reset “has cleared some speculative excess, but is yet to address the underlying demand problem.”

Looking at the data, one can see why. Most importantly, the ETF flows changed drastically. There were $2.6 billion worth of inflows in September 2026, which turned into $290 million of net outflows in October. Currently, U.S ETF holders sit underwater below their cost basis of $82K.

How ETF holders behave below breakeven remains the key tell for November.

Also, stablecoin supply has not been moving outside the $305 billion zone, a sure sign that liquidity is sitting still.

Finally, one cannot forget the fallout caused by the Binance hack.

When the exchange reopened withdrawals, the queue moved fast. The exchange processed $4.65 billion against $1.01 billion of deposits in under ten hours; net outflows reached about $5.8 billion over 24 hours!

Each of these factors may not matter when seen separately, but when read together, they describe the same thing — capital leaving or sitting still. The leverage is gone, and the bid is not there to replace it.

For 21Shares, $73,000 is the 200-day moving average, and “the line that would reopen the bear-market debate.”

Bitget analysts told AMBCrypto that Bitcoin must hold the $73K–$74K range.

The 73,000-74,000 range is now the zone Bitcoin needs to hold. Stabilization would require more balanced derivatives positioning and sustained positive ETF flows. Reclaiming and holding $80,000 would signal stronger demand, while a decisive break below $73,000 could expose $68,000. If $68,000 also fails to hold, the selloff would suggest more than a temporary leverage flush.

When the same number pops up again and again, it stops being a coincidence and starts pointing to a pattern — that Bitcoin is either in a temporary flush or at the start of something deeper.

If buyers do return, Bitcoin can comfortably start holding $80,000, and stronger demand would emerge as a result. Getting back above $81,000 to $82,000 would finally put the largest holder cohorts back in profit.

Both reports are similarly aligned on what won’t fix Bitcoin’s buyer problem: Macro relief without demand. In fact, Bitget’s analysts asserted,

A durable Bitcoin recovery will depend on improving liquidity and Spot demand, not macro relief alone.

Matt Mena framed the same requirement as a four-part checklist.

We are watching four things into November: Bitcoin holding $76,000 to $78,000 and reclaiming $80,000 to $82,000, Binance completing withdrawals and proving its reserves, credit spreads, oil and long-end yields, and whether ETF flows return. If those turn, the setup for the final weeks of the year remains constructive.

Notice what’s on both lists: ETF flows, liquidity, Spot demand, and positioning. All of them are things buyers have to do.

There is naturally no doubt that macro factors can move Bitcoin’s price. If everything aligns, a rally can happen tomorrow. However, seasoned investors know that this will never be the same as rebuilding buyer interest.

Ultimately, a macro relief rally would leave Bitcoin’s demand problem exactly where it was.

The calendar offers a boost for Bitcoin regardless of which way the ball drops, as December’s Fed expectations hinge on U.S CPI and PPI readings. For the uninitiated, hotter readings will reinforce rate-hike expectations, and a softer core inflation will trigger a relief rally.

However, Bitget’s analysts draw a line between the two outcomes, noting,

This week’s inflation data adds another test. Hotter readings could reinforce December rate-hike expectations, while softer core inflation could trigger a relief rally. But a durable Bitcoin recovery will depend on improving liquidity and spot demand, not macro relief alone.

Basically, a relief rally can be classed as a macro event, not a demand event. The inflation print can only deliver the former, and the latter can only come about by real flows.

Here, it’s also worth being precise about what the 5th November AI crash tells us. 21Shares calls it,

A renewed wobble in artificial intelligence (AI) equities pulled the Nasdaq-100 down almost 4%.

That’s the mechanism in a nutshell. Even this time, crypto’s fall was the result of trading alongside a risk asset.

Still, if Bitcoin’s move below $74,000 was driven by the AI wipeout, then the absence of a bid is more telling. Eventually, we come back to the same problem of missing buyers.

Leverage has been reset twice in a week — Once by Binance’s $6.3 billion hack, once by an AI shocker. And the buyer bid has hardly appeared either time.

That’s what makes $73,000 a critical level to watch. If buyers return and hold this level, they could help Bitcoin reclaim $80,000. This would also imply that BTC’s demand problem is cyclical, not permanent.

However, a break below $73,000 will hint at — and a failure at $68,000 will confirm — that Bitcoin’s breakdown is structural. Bitcoin’s recovery hinges on a single level, and the lack of buyers has never been felt stronger.

Assembly Language for Beginners: Talk to Your Computer in Its Own Language

📅 Nov 14, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Every program you have ever run — your browser, your games, your operating system — eventually becomes Assembly language. It is the lowest-level programming language humans still write by hand: a thin, readable layer over the machine code your CPU actually executes. You probably will not build a web app in Assembly, but learning it teaches you how computers really work: registers, memory, the stack, and why your Python code is slow in some places and fast in others.

Assembly looks intimidating because it has no if statements, no loops, and no variables as you know them. Instead you move values between registers, add them, compare them, and jump to labels. Here is a tiny x86-64 program that exits with code 42:

That is the whole program. mov copies a value into a register, and syscall asks the operating system to do something. There is nothing hiding underneath — what you write is almost exactly what the CPU does.

Assembly is a good first low-level language because it removes the mystery. When you learn it, you stop guessing what "the stack" is: you push values onto it and pop them off yourself. You see why a function call costs something, why a buffer overflow is dangerous, and why a compiler's optimisation matters. It also makes you a better debugger in every other language, because when a program crashes you can read the disassembly instead of staring at it.

How to start: install NASM and a linker on Linux, or use an online assembler like Compiler Explorer's Assembly view. Write the exit program above, assemble it with nasm -f elf64 hello.asm && ld hello.o -o hello, run it, and check the exit code with echo $?. Then write a program that prints "Hello" using the write syscall, then one that adds two numbers. Each step takes an evening, and each one makes the machine less mysterious.

Who is it for? Students studying computer science, embedded developers, security researchers, and anyone who wants to understand performance deeply. If you only want to build websites, you do not need Assembly — but if you want to know what is really happening when your code runs, there is no better teacher.

The limits are honest: Assembly is slow to write, tied to one CPU architecture, and a 100-line program can take a day. You would never write a web app in it. Treat it as a learning tool and a specialist tool — for bootloaders, drivers, game engines' hot loops, and security research — not as your everyday language.

Your first step today: install NASM, assemble the exit program above, and get it to return 42. Then change the number, rebuild, and watch it change. You have just written a program with nothing between you and the CPU — and that feeling is worth the effort.

I think talking about money and normalizing discussing it is the first step. It shouldn't be this hush hush taboo topic. It is a part of everyone's everyday life and the earlier a child learns that the better. Start with simple things and move on to more complicated as they get older. I look forward to teaching my child about compound interest when she is mature enough to comprehend it. I have a few more years to wait.... For now I am happy to ask her how much something costs when she asks for it at the grocery store for example. Associating that there is a cost/desire for an item rather than just a desire is a simple start. Want that box of cheese its? How much does it cost etc. She knows I have "accounts" for her but she has no concept of what kind of accounts. She will know that she should always "save, invest, and spend" in that order. There is still plenty of time and my goal is that she is more financially literate than I ever was at her age. Not a major feat since I never even heard of an Roth Ira till my late 30's. I am very late to the game. She will be better prepared at a younger age.
