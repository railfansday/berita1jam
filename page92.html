What the Packers’ roster could look like vs. the Patriots

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The Green Bay Packers only made one roster move on Saturday, bringing up practice squad tight end Drake Dabney to the game-day roster for action against the New England Patriots. As always, the moves that the Packers make, and don't make, give us hints at what the roster is expected to look like on Sunday. Let's get into the information we have now.

Four players have already been ruled out with injuries, bringing the Packers' 53-man active roster to 49. The players out are tight end Josh Whyle (concussion), offensive lineman Travis Glover (knee), offensive lineman Sean Rhyan (ankle) and linebacker Zaire Franklin (shin).

The Packers lost Whyle in-game against the Carolina Panthers, carrying only three tight ends on game day and scratching Jonnu Smith for the first time this season, while Glover, Rhyan and Franklin did not play.

This season, the Packers have seemingly had no interest in carrying four running backs on the game-day roster. The same can be said about a ninth offensive lineman, despite the team consistently keeping 11 on the active roster.

If you add in those scratches, Green Bay's active roster drops down to 47 players. With the addition of Dabney, who is expected to replace the special teams snaps of Whyle (the only real special teams contributor on offense who isn't a receiver), that gets us to a 48-man gameday roster. It's pretty tidy.

The players listed as questionable going into the weekend were running back MarShawn Lloyd (knee), cornerback Keisean Nixon (groin) and running back Chris Brooks (ankle). Brooks isn't on the active roster yet, as he's going through his practice window after spending time on the injured reserve. If he were going to play on Sunday, you would have seen an activation today.

Nixon is highly likely to play, as the Packers don't really have a replacement for him on the roster, as it stands currently. If he were going to miss this game, we probably would have seen a second call-up from the practice squad on Saturday. Lloyd may or may not play. If he can't go, the team will displace him with Kaleb Johnson, Green Bay's fourth running back, who has been a healthy scratch since Josh Jacobs' return to the team.

Here's what the team will probably look like on Sunday (with the biggest questions being which offensive lineman gets healthy-scratched and if Lloyd can go):

WR: Christian Watson, Matthew Golden, Savion Williams, Bo Melton, J. Michael Sturdivant and Coleman Owen

TE: Tucker Kraft, Mark Redman, Jonnu Smith and Drake Dabney

OL: 8 players up, 3 scratched (two for sure being Sean Rhyan and Travis Glover)

Most likely: Jordan Morgan, Aaron Banks, John Williams, Laken Tomlinson, Anthony Belton, Kevin Zeitler, Mekhi Becton Sr. and Jager Burton active; Donnovan Jennings a healthy scratch, along with Rhyan and Glover being out with injury.

DL: Karl Brooks, Devonte Wyatt, Chris McClellan, Jordon Riley and Anthony Campbell

ED: Lukas Van Ness, Barryn Sorrell, Brenton Cox Jr. and Dani Dennis-Sutton

LB: Isaiah McDuffie, Ty'Ron Hopper, Nick Niemann and Kristian Welch

CB: Keisean Nixon, Brandon Cisse, Carrington Valentine, Benjamin St-Juste and Kamal Hadden

S: Xavier McKinney, Evan Williams, Javon Bullard, Kitan Oladapo

That's your 48-man roster against the Patriots.

Three Billy Edwards TD passes lift UNC to 38-21 win over UConn in Belichick’s return to New England

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Billy Edwards threw for three touchdowns and North Carolina scored 21 points off three turnovers as the Tar Heels defeated UConn 38-21 to snap a two-game losing streak in coach Bill Belichick's return to New England.

It was Belichick's first game back coaching in New England since his tenure with the NFL's Patriots came to an end.

"It's good to be back in New England," Belichick said. "It was an interesting game. I think UConn is a really challenging team, they put a lot of pressure on us.

"The team rebounded pretty well after a couple of tough losses."

Timir Hickman-Collins returned an interception 62 yards for a touchdown and had a fumble recovery to set up a 6-yard touchdown pass from Edwards to Jordan Shipp. Edwards had a fourth-down completion and put the game away with a 3-yard scoring pass to Jelani Thurman with 3:38 remaining after Jalon Thompson's interception gave the ball back to the Tar Heels.

"We've been really harping on (getting) turnovers, every day in practice we have been doing that," Hickman-Collins said. "Today, me and our DB (Jalon Thompson), a lot of us had turnovers."

Kalieb Osborne had completions of 23 and 28 yards and a 25-yard run to set up Cyncir Bowers' 13-yard touchdown run to pull UConn within seven points with 10:01 remaining in the third quarter. Osborne threw for 245 yards, but completed just 17 of 35 passes against a North Carolina defense that was allowing teams to complete 65% of their passes.

"I thought Carolina put us in some tough situations and won the football game," UConn coach Jason Candle said. "I didn't feel like we were at our best. We did some things that set them up in short fields. The turnovers were a big deal today."

Benjamin Hall had a 39-yard touchdown run. He finished with 81 yards on nine carries. Edwards connected with Jelani Thurman on a 3-yard touchdown with 24 seconds remaining in the first half. Thurman now has eight touchdown catches to tie the North Carolina single-season record for TDs by a tight end.

Osborne threw touchdown passes of 18 yards to Ryder Treadway and 24 yards to Cam Abshire as the Huskies trailed 28-14 at halftime.

Peyton Seelmann led North Carolina (5-4) with nine tackles and two sacks. Hayden Pegg had 12 tackles and an interception for UConn (4-5).

Belichick coached the Patriots to six Super Bowl titles. He played at Wesleyan University from 1971-74, which is about 20 miles away from Rentschler Field. Belichick nearly guided North Carolina to a win over No. 1 Miami last week.

Bob Diaco, North Carolina's defensive line coach, coached UConn from 2014-16. UConn won six games in Diaco's second season before losing to Marshall in the St. Petersburg Bowl. He was fired after a 3-9 record in 2016. Diaco went 11-26 in his three seasons with the Huskies.

North Carolina hosts Louisville on Nov. 14.

UConn is home against James Madison on Nov. 14.

The 4 Largest Air Force Bases By Bomber Fleet Presence

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The United States is one of only three nations in the world that possess large, strategic bomber aircraft in its armed forces. The US Air Force operates the second-largest strategic strike fleet of 130 bombers under Air Force Global Strike Command, which, despite being numerically smaller than the Chinese Air Force fleet, is technologically far superior. The impressive fleet is home-based at a few different installations across the US.

This allows it to distribute forces for rapid deployment, centralized maintenance and training, as well as dispersion that reduces the vulnerability of AFGSC to a single, large-scale attack. This review will focus on the four largest airbases, which not only have the highest concentration of planes but also feature unique facilities that specialize in supporting different airframes at each one. The future of strategic strike within the USAF is following a 'high-low' fleet philosophy.

The Air Force is pursuing this with its fighter jets as well. The incoming Northrop Grumman B-21 Raider will replace both the B-2 Spirit and the Rockwell B-1 Lancer as the premier bomber platform. The bases of the bomber fleet are following in step with the transition, which marks a watershed moment in American military history.

Following a host of upgrades, the new Boeing B-52J Stratofortress variant is being positioned as a 'flying arsenal ship.' While the B-21 Raider handles the stealthy penetration, Barksdale's B-52Js will follow behind, launching massive volleys of standoff missiles from outside enemy air defenses. As the tactics and strategy of the Air Force evolve, so too are the biggest airbases from which these incredible warbirds fly.

Whiteman Air Force Base in Missouri is the world’s only base for the B-2 Spirit. Whiteman hosts the entire active fleet of 19 B-2 bombers. These aircraft are operated by the 509th Bomb Wing (active duty) and the 131st Bomb Wing (Missouri Air National Guard). Whiteman has also been officially selected as the second operational base for the next-generation B-21 Raider stealth bomber.

Before becoming a bomber hub, Whiteman was a major Minuteman II Intercontinental Ballistic Missile base. The base maintained over 150 missile silos until all were deactivated in the nuclear disarmament and non-proliferation efforts that followed the collapse of the USSR. The deactivation was driven by the 1991 Strategic Arms Reduction Treaty.

As the missile mission faded, Whiteman was selected as the sole operational base for the B-2. Air & Space Forces Magazine recounted these estimates from an Air Force report on the projected changes at Whiteman to accommodate the new B-21 fleet:

“There would be an increase of 1,021 individuals at Whiteman AFB from 19,408 to 20,429 after all B-21 mission individuals have arrived and all B-2 mission individuals have departed. The number of airfield operations flown per year would increase by 1,980, from 29,771 (…) to 31,751 by the time all the new aircraft arrived. There would be a need for increased base housing as a result."

The first operational B-2, named the 'Spirit of Missouri,' arrived at Whiteman in 1993. The 509th Bomb Wing, which was the same unit that dropped both atomic bombs in the Second World War, moved to the base to begin training on the world's first stealth bomber. The wing achieved Initial Operational Capability in 1997. The B-2 saw its first combat use in 1999 during Operation Allied Force, with crews flying 30-hour nonstop missions from Missouri to Kosovo.

Ellsworth Air Force Base, located near Rapid City, South Dakota, is the current home of the 28th Bomb Wing and is set to become the first operational base of the B-21 Raider. Ellsworth hosts two of the Air Force's three active B-1B Lancer squadrons: the 34th Bomb Squadron (Thunderbirds) and the 37th Bomb Squadron (Tigers). Ellsworth oversees the PRTC, the largest training airspace in the continental United States, spanning four states.

This area allows bombers to practice low-level maneuvers and large-force exercises. The transition at Ellsworth represents the most significant shift in American airpower in decades. The first operational B-21 aircraft are scheduled to arrive in 2027. During the Cold War, the base was the heart of Strategic Air Command, maintaining a dual-leg mission that included both heavy bombers and 150 Minuteman II ICBM silos.

The base is undergoing a massive $1.5 billion+ transformation to accommodate the new stealth armada. Dale White, the Director of Critical Major Weapon Systems at the Pentagon, said in a statement reported by Stripes on February 24 that “we are responsibly accelerating delivery of a critical, combat-effective capability."

The USAF also recently announced a 25% increase in B-21 production capacity. The remaining fleet of B-1B Lancerswill begin retiring in stages to make way for the incoming next-generation bombers. Nine aircraft are slated for divestment in 2028, followed by 10 more in 2029, and the entire B-1 fleet is expected to be retired by 2036.

Dyess Air Force Base in Abilene, Texas, remains one of the most critical strike and airlift hubs in the United States. The 7th Bomb Wing operates approximately 33 B-1B Lancer aircraft. Dyess is the sole formal training unit for all B-1B aircrews in the USAF, managed by the 28th Bomb Squadron. Dyess has also been chosen as the third main operating base for the new B-21 stealth bomber. Per The War Zone, Secretary of the Air Force Troy Meink said about B-21's the production acceleration that:

“The B-21 is foundational to our long-range strike capability and to credible deterrence. Accelerating production capacity now ensures we deliver operational capability to combatant commanders faster, strengthening our ability to outpace, deter, and, if necessary, defeat emerging threats. This is disciplined execution at the speed the security environment demands.”

The arrival of the B-21 will bring nuclear weapons capability back to Dyess for the first time since the 1990s. Work officially began last year on the first B-21 facilities, and 2026 is slated for groundbreaking on a mission planning facility that will be the first major facility built exclusively for the Raider. These revolutionary aircraft will share the tarmac with another major tenant at Dyess, the 317th Airlift Wing. The wing is the largest Lockheed Martin C-130J Super Hercules unit in the world.

Dyess supports approximately 15,000 people, including over 4,100 active-duty aviators and their families. It was the first Department of Defense installation to be powered entirely by renewable wind energy. The base is the largest employer in the Abilene area, contributing over $3.6 billion annually to the Texas economy. Dyess is currently undergoing more than 20 major infrastructure projects designed to support the unique requirements of a 6th-Gen stealth bomber.

This aging bomber continues to adapt, proving its resilience in a changing world.

The 2nd Bomb Wing operates approximately 44 B-52s at Barksdale AFB. The 307th Bomb Wing, an Air Force Reserve unit, also operates an additional 18 to 20 B-52Hs. The base also serves as the permanent headquarters for the AFGSC, which oversees all B-1, B-2, B-52, and B-21 wings, as well as the nation’s three ICBM wings.

The legendary 'Mighty Eighth' Air Force is headquartered at Barksdale and at the forefront of the B-52J upgrade. This includes new Rolls-Royce engines and digital cockpit systems designed to keep the B-52 flying until the 2050s. While Barksdale will remain the primary B-52 hub, it is also deeply involved in the rollout of the B-21 Raider, as the headquarters for AFGSC.

The B-52J will feature the AN/APG-79 AESA radar, which wields the same technology found in the F/A-18 Super Hornet carrier-based, multirole fighter jet. This allows for vastly superior target tracking, mapping, and electronic warfare capabilities. The aircraft's cockpit is being digitized to allow the crew to be reduced from five members to four on some missions.

Michael Maginness, the 2nd Bomb Wing's Commander, told Air & Space Forces Magazine that “the B-52 has been and will continue to be a very strong message of US support for allies and deterrence." He explained that the B-52 does so "just by flying through their airspace or putting a jet down on a runway in a friendly nation.” On this front, Joshua Crowe, a B-52 Engine Maintainer, explained that:

“With the new ones, they’re proposed to have enclosed systems, so you don’t have to spend more time on the back-end servicing. And the longevity of them: they’re supposed to be able to sit on the wing longer in between changes, if not, maybe not even change at all for the lifetime of it. Essentially, we’re going to be able to extend our missions longer and reduce our touch time on the ground so that we can free up those technicians to do other things.”

The most visible change is the replacement of the 1960s-era Pratt & Whitney engines with the Rolls-Royce F130. These engines provide 30% better fuel efficiency, increased range, and significantly reduced maintenance costs. The base is also upgrading to handle engine testing alongside the introduction of the Long-Range Stand-Off cruise missile for the B-52.

10 Classic Books Worth Reading Again and Again

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

There are some books that are so heavy-going or otherwise taxing in a way that means one read is probably enough. And some long books might also be difficult to imagine reading multiple times, even if they might be worth it. Then again, just focusing on reading War and Peace the one time in your lifetime is enough; gotta prioritize that before even entertaining the notion of reading all 1,347,907,856,745 pages of it a second time, you know?

The following books, though, are all great, for starters, and then there are also books that are worth re-reading sooner rather than later. They might have twists that'll change how you experience the story a second time, or they're dense in ways that take multiple reads to unpack, or they're just incredibly entertaining. Also, some are worth re-reading because they're slim and they don’t take long to read, but to keep things interesting, there are a few longer books below that are worth devoting a few dozen hours to one time and then very much worth devoting a further few dozen hours to at a later time.

Undoubtedly a classic and influential work of science fiction, Fahrenheit 451 also stands as something that’s pretty easy to get through, even with the heavy subject matter. So, not easy reading in the traditional sense, but it’s about as digestible as dystopian novels get, largely because Ray Bradbury was very much concerned with telling a straightforward and largely exciting story here alongside exploring all the inevitably deeper stuff thematically.

It’s about someone whose job it is to burn books in a future where reading isn't allowed, though like just about any main character in a dystopian novel, he questions and then resists this seeming status quo. From there, Fahrenheit 451 does a lot and proves quite unpredictable, never really wasting any pages or time, and so tackling it again does feel enticing and really not too intimidating a task, either.

There are some heavy-going sci-fi books out there, including a couple here that are admittedly tense but still worth revisiting (and they're also not too long), but then you’ve got something like The Hitchhiker’s Guide to the Galaxy, which is sci-fi, but it’s a good deal more fun in a conventional way. Not that the book is conventional, but it is going for comedy and science fiction simultaneously, so it’s entertaining with how frequently funny it is.

And it finds humor in the world blowing up and only one person seemingly surviving, then going on a series of misadventures while generally weird things happen, and then things get weirder. The Hitchhiker’s Guide to the Galaxy is kind of episodic, though it does also flow very well, and it’s a breezy read that is easy to go back to, almost like a comfort book, the way some of the best sitcoms out there can work as good comfort shows.

Like in The Hitchhiker’s Guide to the Galaxy, the world ends early on in Swan Song, but it’s not wiped out of the universe in quite the same way. It’s decimated by nuclear war, so it’s pretty much about the destruction of almost everything, with only a few people left, and those people do end up starting a new war of sorts, like a whole battle for what little is left.

It’s intense and lengthy enough that one read might be enough for some, but there is a lot to appreciate and get swept up in here.

And Swan Song is very different in tone from The Hitchhiker’s Guide to the Galaxy, not to mention also standing out because it’s a great deal longer. It’s intense and lengthy enough that one read might be enough for some, but there is a lot to appreciate and get swept up in here; certainly things that can better be recognized or admired on a re-read. And also, enough happens that you're unlikely to remember everything, which adds to the sense of a second read feeling valuable.

The Curious Incident of the Dog in the Night-Time is simple as far as the premise goes, because it’s mostly about a teenage boy who sees the world differently than most people… yeah, let’s go with that. And he gets incredibly invested in trying to find out why a dog in his neighborhood died, going about it like a detective trying to solve a murder, because that’s pretty much what it is.

But it’s a very different sort of murder mystery/detective story, mainly thanks to the unique protagonist and some of the stylistic decisions The Curious Incident of the Dog in the Night-Time chooses to make. It works a whole lot better than you might expect, and it’s a very engaging and easy read. Also, as with the case with most mystery novels, there is an extra sense of appreciation that can be gained from a re-read when you already know the answers/resolution to come.

Agatha Christie has a well-earned reputation for being maybe the ultimate murder-mystery writer. And that’s a big genre, so it’s not really something that can be said casually, but she really does dominate it, with And Then There Were None being her at her best. It’s got the greatest premise imaginable for such a book, because it’s about a bunch of people on an island, and they're killed off one by one.

The opening couple of chapters make up the only stretch that could be deemed slow, but it’s still an economical start in getting all the necessary characters and setting-related stuff established. Once the deaths start, they don’t stop, and it makes And Then There Were None relentless. The first read is always going to be the most thrilling, of course, but as long as you're okay with not being surprised again, there’s an opportunity to appreciate the mystery and some of the foreshadowing a whole lot more (obviously) if you choose to return to this particular one.

Slaughterhouse-Five does a whole lot within a relatively lean number of pages, and it’s the relative shortness of this one that makes it re-visitable. Otherwise, it’s quite heavy-going, being about the horrors of war and a sense of time repeating (war ongoing, never ending, all that), though there is humor here as well, or at least satire. It’s not laugh-out-loud funny, but the dark humor is still a part of it.

So, you won’t feel down the whole time, just a good chunk of the time. It’s hard to call Slaughterhouse-Five fun, but it’s very rewarding and dense enough that any read beyond the first one will inevitably boost one’s understanding of the whole thing. And you can burn through it in a few hours, so if you’ve got the time, it’s like, why not get stuck in your own time loop of continually revisiting Slaughterhouse-Five?

House of Leaves is very unusual and dense, but as far as postmodern books go, it’s not the toughest to follow, at least once you get used to some of the strange stylistic and formatting decisions. There is also a certain density to it because it’s a few different stories in one, with lines blurring between them and it not always being clear what really “happened” or what might be some kind of hallucination by one or more characters.

You could only pick up a little of what House of Leaves is putting down, and you're still likely to be enthralled. Better understanding, as always, can come from a second or third read-through, especially after perusing what other people have said about some of the more perplexing elements (hey, it doesn’t hurt, and it’s allowed for a book like this). House of Leaves is also extremely entertaining and ambitious with all the genres and emotional beats it goes for, so diving into it all again at some point feels pretty much essential.

Libra has an alarming premise, admittedly, but the approach and style make it less alarming. It is about the assassination of John F. Kennedy, and it does have Lee Harvey Oswald as a (or maybe the?) central character, but it’s not about a conspiracy being right or provable, and it more uses that real-life historical event to explore broader things, mainly concerning how hard it is to know anything for sure, and how paranoia-inducing certain historical events (like what happened on November 22, 1963) have been.

It’s among the most unnerving books ever written, and tops the likes of White Noise and Underworld, if you're attempting to single out the best book Don DeLillo’s written, which is no easy feat when those books are also classics. Libra does feel like the easiest of DeLillo’s books to revisit, too, since the questions it raises are so hard to stop thinking about, and there is always the vague hope that thinking/reading a little more may lead to answers. It won’t, but one can dream…

Stephen King gets horror, and he’s also dabbled in plenty of different sorts of horror, leaning more into supernatural/fantastical territory at times, and then, on the other end of things, combining horror and sci-fi in interesting ways, too. With Misery, though, things are pretty grounded, as it’s more of a psychological horror/thriller sort of thing, and its villain is famously someone who manages to be terrifying without having any fantastical or otherworldly powers.

She’s just an overly devoted fan, essentially, and she puts the protagonist (an author like so many King characters before him, and since) through absolute hell. It’s one of King’s best books for sure, and is so engrossing the first time around that the idea of returning to it sooner rather than later feels right. That being said, alongside being engrossing, it is also gross at times, but if you weren't too squeamish for it once, you can probably stomach some of those grisly parts again (go on; I believe in you).

Published in parts, technically, but it is one book, it’s The Lord of the Rings. It’s inevitable, like, seeing this on pretty much any ranking about great books, but it really is that great. And it is an absolute epic of a novel, with J.R.R. Tolkien building immensely on The Hobbit, which is a pretty great prologue worthy of an honorable mention, for present purposes. That’s technically easier to re-read, just because it doesn’t take nearly as much time and dedication to tackle again.

But the idea of re-reading The Hobbit and then not going on to re-read The Lord of the Rings is blasphemy. It’s madness. One does not simply… you know, all that. This is about as good as works of literature from the 20th century ever got, and sure, it’s long, but it’s still such an effortless and also endlessly rewarding read, being the kind of thing everyone should read at least, uh, not once. Read it at least twice. Yeah, everyone should do that. Why not? Life’s short, but it’s not that short.

What does NAT type mean for online gaming?

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

NAT type isn’t always an issue, and depends on how your network is configured.

Inside an online game's settings or on your console you may have seen the NAT Type field and wondered what it meant. In some cases, you might even get a warning that your NAT type is strict and that matchmaking will be affected.

In many cases, this is more informational than anything you need to act on. If your game is working fine in matchmaking and voice chat, then adjustments shouldn't be necessary. But if your network is configured poorly for gaming and you keep getting disconnected or seeing errors, you should make some changes to avoid performance issues. Let's look over what NAT type means, how it affects you and what you can do to adjust it.

NAT stands for network address translation and is a key part of how the internet has worked for some time. IPv4, the original scheme for IP addresses, only has enough room for about four billion unique numbers. That was plenty when the standard was developed in the '80s but it quickly became stretched once everyone got online and started adding dozens of smart devices.

ISPs came up with NAT as a solution. With this, each home has one public IP address and its router translates incoming and outgoing traffic so all devices within it work under a single public IP. It's not a significant roadblock to most network traffic, but for real-time applications like gaming small bits of inconsistency and these layers of translation can have a big effect.

The newer version of the internet protocol, IPv6, has enough possible IP addresses for every grain of sand on Earth to have billions of their own. It thus doesn't require NAT because every device can have its own IP address.

Many modern devices support IPv6, but the transition hasn't fully completed yet. Some devices work in a hybrid mode where they can take advantage of IPv6 but fall back to IPv4 if there are issues or another device along the chain isn't compatible.

NAT type in a game explains how well your device can communicate with the wider internet. On Xbox and PC, you'll see three types: Open, Moderate and Strict. On PlayStation, these are respectively known as Type 1, Type 2 and Type 3. The Nintendo Switch uses A, B and C, with D for connections that are heavily restricted and F if your console can't connect at all. NAT types are not an official network specification, which is why they vary across platforms.

An Open NAT means your gaming console or PC is directly connected to the internet and has no restrictions on what it can communicate with. (As explained above, this isn't actually NAT, but games still designate it as such.) Moderate indicates that your system is behind a router for its internet connection but is still able to communicate as needed. Finally, Strict means your system has necessary ports blocked and can't communicate normally.

Although Open sounds like it should be the goal, that's not really the case. Having your console or PC directly connected to the internet is impractical, as it would require you to connect your machine to your modem, preventing you from using a router to get every other device online unless you used a network switch. Importantly, it would also be insecure since you wouldn't have the firewall or other security features of your router protecting your network traffic.

With an Open NAT type, you can connect to anyone else; and with Moderate you can connect to other Moderate or Open players. Strict is the least compatible, as you can only play with those who have an Open NAT. Usually, someone with an Open NAT is chosen as the game host, while having a Strict NAT means you're more likely to see matchmaking errors, won't be able to join friends and similar.

Your experience is most affected by NAT type in games with peer-to-peer (P2P) connections rather than dedicated servers provided by the developer. However, even in games with their own servers, elements like in-game voice chat, custom lobbies, co-op campaigns and similar might still rely on P2P connections.

NAT type doesn't change your network speed or ping; it only affects who your console can talk to. Improving your router's download speeds is another matter.

To check your NAT type on PS5, go to Settings > Network > Connection Status > Test Internet Connection, and there you'll be able to see your NAT type. On Xbox Series X|S, go to Settings > General > Network Settings and check under Current Network Status. On a Switch or Switch 2, go to Settings > Internet > Test Connection. Some games will also show your NAT in their network settings or on the multiplayer main menu.

If your NAT type is Open/Type 1/A or Moderate/Type 2/B, you're in good shape for multiplayer games. In case you see a worse rating, you can make a few adjustments on your network, but there isn't a simple toggle to flip.

First, make sure you aren't using two routers. This creates a problem called double NAT where there are two layers of address translation happening. If you have a router connected to your ISP's gateway, putting the gateway in bridge mode will prevent double NAT. Anyone who uses a second router as an access point should put it in AP mode so it's not doubling up on router functionality.

Aside from that, the most common fix for a Strict NAT type is enabling UPnP (Universal Plug and Play) on your router. NAT type problems occur because games and online services can't communicate with your console over the relevant ports. Fixing this involves logging into your router and forwarding the relevant ports to your console or gaming PC to unblock relevant communication from the outside, but doing so for every game is inconvenient.

When UPnP is enabled, any device on your network can request ports that it needs forwarded from your router and they will be granted. This is convenient but also creates security risks. If someone malicious gained access to your network, they could abuse UPnP to request ports be opened that give them more control. You can try a UPnP test to see if your network listens for UPnP requests outside your home LAN; if so, it's even more dangerous. Especially if you don't play a lot of different games, spending a bit of time port forwarding is probably worth the security benefit. Port Forward is a good resource for getting started.

Failing all this, the problem can be caused by CGNAT, or carrier-grade NAT. This is when ISPs apply NAT to entire areas that they service, meaning the public IP address of your router goes through its own layer of translation by your ISP. Using IPv6 is a workaround for this, though Xbox is the only console that uses it fully. The PS5 and Switch 2 support IPv6 but require IPv4 alongside it. If enabling IPv6 on your router isn't enough, try contacting your ISP and requesting a dedicated public IP.
