AC Milan devise £53m Endrick transfer plan with Real Madrid buy-back clause

📅 Nov 15, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

By Naim Beneddra | 15 Nov 2026 14:51

AC Milan are reportedly preparing a new approach to sign Real Madrid forward Endrick in January, with a proposed buy-back arrangement designed to convince the Spanish giants to sanction his departure.

The Italian club have previously explored a loan move for the 20-year-old Brazilian, who has struggled for regular playing time under Jose Mourinho despite showing considerable promise during his spell at Lyon last season.

However, Milan are now considering a more ambitious proposal that could see Endrick eventually join them on a permanent basis while allowing Madrid to retain control over his long-term future.

According to La Gazzetta dello Sport, the Rossoneri are weighing up an initial loan with an option or obligation to buy for approximately €60m (£53m), plus bonuses.

Crucially, the agreement would also include a buy-back clause worth around €80m (£70m), giving Real Madrid the opportunity to bring Endrick back to the Santiago Bernabeu if he develops into the player they believe he can become.

The proposed structure resembles Madrid's arrangement with Como for Nico Paz and could provide a potential solution to the disagreements that have previously prevented Endrick from leaving.

Milan's interest in Endrick is not new, but their latest reported strategy represents a change from the short-term loan previously considered.

Rather than simply borrowing the striker for six months, the Serie A club would be prepared to make a substantial financial commitment while offering Madrid assurances over the player's future.

An eventual €60m purchase would represent a major investment for Milan, who are expected to have around €70m available for the January transfer window.

The proposed €80m buy-back clause would give Madrid the possibility of reversing a permanent departure, although there is currently no indication that the Spanish club have accepted the arrangement.

There is also uncertainty over whether Milan would seek an obligation to buy or merely an option, with Italian reports discussing both possibilities.

Either structure would require an agreement over the conditions attached to the purchase and Madrid's subsequent right to re-sign the player.

Real have so far been reluctant to sanction Endrick's exit despite his limited opportunities.

Mourinho has publicly expressed his desire to retain the Brazilian, acknowledging that he has found it difficult to provide him with the playing time his development requires.

Endrick has made only limited appearances this season, contributing one goal and one assist in 141 minutes of football.

His situation follows a productive loan spell at Lyon during the 2025-26 campaign, when he registered eight goals and seven assists in 21 appearances.

Those performances demonstrated his ability to make an impact with regular minutes, strengthening interest from clubs seeking attacking reinforcements.

Real Madrid's previous reluctance to approve a permanent move for Endrick has complicated Milan's efforts, but the inclusion of a buy-back clause could offer a different basis for negotiations.

Milan may also have an advantage through their connections with Endrick's representatives.

Club owner Gerry Cardinale reportedly maintains a positive relationship with Roc Nation, the agency responsible for the Brazilian's career.

Cardinale had already expressed an interest in bringing Endrick to San Siro during the summer and is said to have requested updates whenever Madrid make a definitive decision about the youngster's future.

Former Milan and Real Madrid midfielder Kaka could also become involved in attempts to persuade Endrick to consider the move.

According to La Gazzetta dello Sport, the Brazilian legend is viewed as a potentially influential figure because of his connections with both clubs and his standing in Brazilian football.

There is no confirmation that Kaka has formally entered negotiations or spoken directly to Endrick about joining Milan.

Nevertheless, his possible involvement adds another dimension to the club's pursuit.

Another former Milan favourite, Ronaldinho, has also offered an encouraging assessment of Endrick's ability to succeed in Italian football.

Speaking in an interview with La Gazzetta dello Sport, the 2005 Ballon d'Or winner insisted that his compatriot possesses the qualities required to adapt to Serie A.

"He is a player who can play anywhere in the world," Ronaldinho said.

"He has played in different leagues and done well, in France, Brazil and Spain. He is very young and can play well, yes."

Ronaldinho's comments were an assessment of Endrick's qualities rather than an indication that he is involved in Milan's transfer plans.

The Rossoneri face competition from Roma, where Gian Piero Gasperini is also interested in strengthening his attacking options.

The capital club have been linked with a January loan, while interest from England and France has also been reported.

However, a recent report from Corriere dello Sport suggested that Endrick was not particularly enthusiastic about moving to Milan, potentially presenting another obstacle even if the clubs can agree financial terms.

Milan are therefore maintaining alternative options, with Club Brugge striker Nicolo Tresoldi also under consideration.

The 22-year-old has scored 14 goals in 17 appearances this season and is reportedly valued at approximately €60m (£53m).

Brugge would prefer to retain Tresoldi until the summer, meaning Milan could explore signing one of their attacking targets in January before returning for the other at the end of the campaign.

For Endrick, the decisive factor remains Real Madrid's willingness to reconsider their position. Milan's proposed buy-back arrangement may offer a compromise, but no formal agreement has been reached and Mourinho has not changed his publicly stated desire to keep the striker.

On the big screen, the star has started to play around with genre fare, and Sweeney's list of upcoming movies is filled with exciting sci-fi projects and more.

Among those films is the video game adaptation Split Fiction, which mixes sci-fi and fantasy, and the Netflix Gundam movie with Noah Centineo, which will see Sweeney play a mech pilot in what should be one of her biggest projects to date. However, before both of those movies, the actress came to join a tangential Marvel universe.

Sweeney is one of the major stars who ended up becoming part of Sony's Spider-Man Universe, which has that name because it focused on villains and supporting characters from Marvel Comics tied to Spider-Man, not the hero himself. Sweeney starred in Madame Web, and the film has now swung onto a major streaming service, allowing Marvel fans to experience it again.

Madame Web arrived on HBO Max this Saturday, November 14, and despite it not having been a theatrical hit, the superhero movie is expected to rise quickly on the streamer's nationwide chart, repeating the huge success the film saw on Netflix a couple of years ago. Madame Web is part of the new arrivals on HBO Max in November, with movies like Elf, The First Purge, Happy Feet, Jerry Maguire, Jurassic World: Fallen Kingdom, The Polar Express, Scream, Sherlock Holmes: A Game of Shadows, multiple Planet of the Apes films, and even fellow Marvel movie The Wolverine also joining the streaming service.

The best movies to watch on HBO Max this weekend are Sydney Sweeney's sci-fi thriller, a dystopian sci-fi, and a violent horror franchise entry.

Madame Web follows Dakota Johnson's Cassie Webb, a paramedic who tries to understand her clairvoyant powers, being able to change the future she sees to keep people safe. Sweeney plays Julia Cornwall, one of the three teenagers destined to become Spider-Women and kill the villain Ezekiel Sims, who wears a Spider-Man-like suit. The movie's major Spider-Man connection comes from the fact that Peter Parker is in his mother's belly in Madame Web, with Adam Scott also playing a young version of Uncle Ben, who is Cassie's partner and best friend. Madame Web had a lot of exciting factors, but it just did not work out.

Madame Web sits at a disappointing 10% critics' score on Rotten Tomatoes based on 270 reviews, earning the infamous Rotten designation on the review aggregator. While it is better, Madame Web's 53% audience score is still far from enticing. The universally negative reception to the film led to Sweeney's Marvel movie becoming a box office bomb, as Madame Web only grossed $100.5 million worldwide against a reported budget of $80 million. When accounting for marketing and other expenses, it needed to finish between $160 million and $200 million to break even, which did not happen.

One of the main points of contention in You Can See Everything, the latest A24 movie from Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim, is the fact Elizabeth Holmes and her set-up-to-be-the-villain partner Billy Evans seemed to be going back to the drawing board and creating a system that sounded like a Theranos 2.0. Obviously, several years have passed since Holmes went to prison, and her partner is now working on a new business. He bristled at a comparison to Theranos.

When journalist Matt Gutman asked Evans about “Theranos 3” in an already-viral Sunday Mornings interview, the entrepreneur and husband wanted to set the record straight. Even prior to the interview finally airing in full, we know the man felt his You Can See Everything portrayal was “crazy.” And I’m not even talking about whatever happened with that shower scene.

Per Evans, Gutman is actually a bit behind the times. The journalist asked about the project we see in the documentary, a project that had Evans and Holmes attempting to fix the technology that led to Theranos dealing with legal troubles by approaching the problem in a different way. According to the scientist, that project is dead, and it’s dead for one big reason: The tech was too expensive.

No. I am not working with Elizabeth on [my current project]. Important distinction from the movie: That was a different idea. Completely different technology.

Gutman interrupted and asked, “So, we’re talking about Theranos 3 now?” Evans shook his head, and responded:

It’s not Theranos. At the time we were filming, we were talking about basically using microscopes, taking really good camera images of bacteria, and then using computer vision like driverless cars use to determine that’s a car, that’s a bus, that’s a person… So, that was kind of the idea. Turns out, that the cost was just too high.

Shortly after Holmes was in prison, they scrapped the idea we see them chatting about in You Can See Everything. Despite the way the now-infamous egg scene portrays Evans, he said here he spent about seven months worrying about the kids and life in Texas. Where the kids would go to preschool, what hotels to stay at while visiting Holmes in prison.

Then, he got back to it. Now, he is still working on "the problem," but he feels his research is not the same thing.

Once I finally settled and I regained like my strength and my energy back, I started thinking about, ‘Well, that last one didn’t work. But how could it? And my background’s in photonics, lasers.
