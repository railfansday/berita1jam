Grimes to Star in the First-Ever AI-Generated Film Coming to Theaters: Watch a Clip

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The star voices a character in Gods Don't Give Gifts, but the visuals were fully generated by artificial intelligence.

Grimes has been on the cutting edge of the overlap between artificial intelligence and music, and now, she’s doing the same with the technology and film.

As revealed Thursday (Nov. 5), the alternative singer-songwriter is part of the cast of Gods Don’t Give Gifts, the first AI-generated movie coming to theaters. She lends her voice and physical likeness to her character, Zayinth Alda, described in a release as “a disaffected NiiroCore aristocrat and niece to Caen Halvorith, inventor of the Substrate Migration Protocol.”

The film, which will premiere this winter, explores a dystopian future reality in which “consciousness can be transferred between bodies, memory can be bought and sold, and death is no longer final,” a synopsis reads. In a clip released alongside Grimes’ casting announcement, her AI-animated form chastises another character before storming away with a massive, muscular, faceless creature acting as her guard.

“You dragged me to the slag heaps for this? A slideshow of derivative drivel?” she yells before boarding her futuristic spacecraft.

Though the visuals on screen are produced by AI, Grimes’ voice and dialogue were not. Gossip Goblin, the studio behind the Zack London-directed project, asserts in the release, “Casting the likeness of an actor with their spoken vocal performance will become more common with animated films using AI — audiences will see actors, athletes, musicians giving vocal performances with their likeness in films.”

The announcement is fitting, considering Grimes has long been interested in the ways the controversial technology is developing. She’s now gearing up to release her new album, Psy Opera, which explores the complexities of AI and uses artificially generated music on one of the tracks.

Stormlight Season 1's TV Rating Addressed By Brandon Sanderson

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

The Stormlight Archive might have a very deliberate rating.

Brandon Sanderson has addressed the possible TV rating for The Stormlight Archive season 1, unpacking his expectations for the series. The upcoming Apple TV adaptation of The Stormlight Archive pulls from the pages of Sanderson's epic fantasy series of the same name. The book series takes place on the world of Roshar, which is constantly pummeled by violent storms across various continents. Its story centers around the Knights Radiant, a magical order of beings whose combined powers are destined to help save the world from certain destruction.

Now, speaking on his Halloween livestream Brandon and Emily Sanderson Present: A Night of Fan Mail on Halloween 2026, Sanderson spoke about his expectations for what The Stormlight Archive TV show will be rated. Responding to the question, "What do you intend for the content rating your adaptations to be?" he said he wants his adaptations to be "Marvel movie level," implying a PG-13 or TV-14 aim.

But he says he wouldn't be surprised if The Stormlight Archive winds up with a TV-MA rating. This is because of major battle sequences in season 1 that could end up pushing the boundaries of the show beyond a possible TV-14 ratings. Check out what Sanderson had to say below:

Most of them would be Marvel movie level. Snapshot has constantly been under option, that's a serial killer thriller, that would probably be R-rated if it were made. Stormlight, I mean, there are some battle scenes in there that I don't know would be... it's probably going to be TV-MA. But I think there's going to be some war scenes in Stormlight. We'll have to see.

Based on his statement, it appears Sanderson is aiming for other Cosmere installments, such as his upcoming Mistborn movie, to be more aligned with a PG-13 typical of Marvel movies. However, because The Stormlight Archive can sometimes deal with more intense themes while also featuring battles that could grow violent onscreen, its TV-MA rating seems almost inevitable. Given the complex history of Roshar coupled with the many tribulations of its characters, it wouldn't be surprising if the series dug into its darker themes in more detail.

Although it's unclear how dark the fantasy epic novel series will get when translated from page to screen, Sanderson's biggest concern for content appears to only be violence. This means the adaptation likely isn't going to include TV-MA content that's too graphic outside of battles. But, since these can be fairly intense in the novel, and will likely be even flashier when adapted onscreen, it makes sense for Sanderson to consider a TV-MA rating for the books.

Alongside the development of The Stormlight Archive, Sanderson is also working on the second draft of his Mistborn movie script. His young adult novel series Skyward has also been picked up for adaptation by Tomorrow Studios, best known for their work on the recent live-action One Piece for Netflix. The Stormlight Archive is just one of many onscreen projects Sanderson is juggling, but it's most certainly one of the biggest in his forthcoming catalog.

With the series poised to have a TV-MA rating once the adaptation comes out, it remains to be seen just how hard The Stormlight Archive will push the envelope. But, if it's anything like the books, then violence will be the only major factor that keeps it from achieving a TV-14 rating instead. It may be just one element, but it will keep the Apple TV adaptation faithful to the source material, not compromising Sanderson's vision for the story.

GM Patents Launch Control That Watches the Drag Strip Christmas Tree, Launches for You

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The recent patent describes a system that manages the brakes and throttle to deliver remarkably consistent drag strip blasts away from the line.

Getting a great launch at the drag strip takes more than horsepower and sticky tires. Drivers also need to time their start perfectly; even a tenth of a second can make a difference. Now, General Motors has patented technology that could take that last bit of human involvement out of the equation entirely.

The system uses the vehicle's cameras to watch the drag strip's Christmas Tree, predict when the light will turn green, and launch the car automatically. Better yet, it can actually begin preparing the launch before the green light illuminates. We might be about to see the age of push-button drag racing.

The technology is detailed in U.S. Patent 12,780,203, titled “Methods and Systems for Coordinating Launch Control for Vehicles for Racing.” Originally filed in January 2025, the application was published in July 2026 before finally receiving its patent grant on November 3.

At its core, the system builds on the launch control technology already available in modern performance cars. Right now, most launch control systems work very similarly: the driver applies full brakes and full throttle, and holds both pedals until it's time to go; they let off the brake, and the car rockets away. GM's proposed technology takes over most of that process.

The craziest bit is just how it manages all of this. First, it uses on-board cameras to identify the Christmas tree at the drag strip. Once it recognizes the lights, it notifies the driver and prompts them to activate launch control. Those who want to try this new method could control it much the same as they do with existing systems; the patent describes drivers putting their feet on both pedals and then, once the automatic launch system takes over, the driver can remove both feet and let the car work.

The car can maintain braking pressure and staging torque automatically, even after the driver releases the pedals. The clever part comes next: rather than simply waiting for the green light, the software identifies the sequence of yellow lights and compares it against stored timing patterns to calculate precisely when the race will begin.

That's key, because different events run the lights at different times. For example, a Pro Tree illuminates all three amber lights simultaneously before turning green 0.4 seconds later, whereas Sportsman Tree illuminates the amber lights sequentially with half-second intervals. GM's system could account for those differences automatically. The system is even capable of calculating the delay between commanding movement from the vehicle and the vehicle actually moving forward.

That all said, this is just a patent for now. GM doesn't mention any specific vehicle or plan for production in its filing. If it does bring it to market, we'd be eager to see how close it can come to cutting a perfect light.
