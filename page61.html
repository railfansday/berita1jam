<a href="https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/health-tech/avanlee-care-raises-35m-targets-medicare-advantage-plans" hreflang="en">Avanlee Care, platform for family caregivers, raises $3.5M, targeting Medicare Advantage plans</a>

📅 2026-11-05  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →
Avanlee Care, platform for family caregivers, raises $3.5M, targeting Medicare Advantage plans" style="max-width:100%; height:auto; border-radius:8px;" loading="lazy">

Avanlee Care, a platform to engage family caregivers, has clinched $3.5 million in seed funding, Fierce Healthcare has learned.

The round was led by BEA Venture Fund, with participation from angel investor Esther Dyson, who is Avanlee’s board chair, Chrysalis Ventures and FairBridge VC. Avanlee will use the capital, which brings its total raised to $6 million, to expand across the Medicare Advantage market.

Avanlee Care helps MA plans and other organizations engage caregivers within their member populations, enabling caregivers to take action and organizations to measure the impact. On the Avanlee platform, caregivers can schedule appointments, manage medications, connect with retailers, communicate with their plan and provider, get vaccination reminders and search for providers. Meanwhile, payers get insight into caregiver burnout and member health trend data.

“Avanlee Care is addressing an enormous and underutilized resource within healthcare,” Melissa Bradley, founder and managing partner of BEA Venture Fund, said in a comment. “As healthcare organizations look for more effective ways to improve engagement and outcomes, Avanlee is offering the infrastructure to make caregivers part of that strategy. We believe that creates a significant opportunity to improve both the caregiver experience and healthcare outcomes at scale.”

Founded in 2020, Avanlee began as a direct-to-consumer app, since transitioning into the enterprise space. It is fully integrated with Mark Cuban Cost Plus Drugs as its lead pharmacy partner for discounted medications, and is in discussions with a national, undisclosed MA plan to bring its platform to members with an assigned caregiver. As part of an initial potential pilot with this plan, thousands of members with an assigned caregiver would participate in Avanlee, with a broader expansion planned for mid-2027. Additionally, Avanlee is working toward a separate unnamed integration with a national pharmacy partner for delivering medications.

“We are excited to work with Avanlee Care. We share a common mission: to provide lower costs on prescribed medications, saving money for the unpaid family caregiver and their loved one, the care-receiver,” Mark Cuban, co-founder of Cost Plus Drug Company, told Fierce Healthcare.

Nearly 1 in 4 Americans provide ongoing care for an adult or child with a complex medical condition or a disability, coordinating medications, appointments, transportation, food and other needs. Yet by overlooking the person managing a member’s care and day-to-day living, plans’ efforts to engage MA members are less effective, Avanlee executives argue.

“Caregivers spend an astronomical amount of time having to juggle their career, their families, and suddenly now I’m thrust into this role of I’m a caregiver for my mom…so saving time is huge,” Avanlee Christine, co-founder and CEO of Avanlee Care, told Fierce Healthcare exclusively. Christine, who grew up with a grandfather with Parkinson’s and dementia, was inspired to build the platform that could have supported her mom when she was caregiving.

Avanlee offers caregiver wellness tools on its platform, which 59% of past users have engaged with. Over half of consistent users also reported a decrease in overall stress. In its DTC days, Avanlee supported thousands of families, claiming it saved their caregivers thousands of hours. The wellness tools include a weekly questionnaire on stress and educational resources on topics such as estate planning, life planning and legal services. Caregivers can add other relatives to the platform for extra visibility or support, and Avanlee also facilitates grocery delivery through an integration with Walmart (their pricing applies). “If the caregiver fails, the one ultimately receiving care fails,” Christine said.

This stress survey becomes a key data stream for Avanlee plan partners, allowing them to evaluate caregiver burnout. Avanlee's platform also provides payers with insights about appointment bookings, home health services and medication adherence.

There’s a reason Avanlee is targeting MA to start. Nearly two-thirds of caregivers say MA makes caregiving duties easier to manage than traditional Medicare, according to one survey. Avanlee is also interested in eventually expanding to dual-eligibles. "There's a lot of tailwinds in caregiving for both Medicare and Medicaid and those dually eligible populations,” Eric Scott, Avanlee’s chief growth officer, said.

In order to streamline scheduling, Avanlee is seeking MA partners that allow it to also tap into their provider networks. Avanlee can automatically schedule appointments when prompted by a health plan, such as annual wellness visits or cancer screenings. Caregivers can also manually add their existing appointments to get reminders. In the near future, Avanlee plans to let caregivers book ad hoc appointments for needs beyond those specifically requested by the health plan.

Avanlee charges payers per-member-per-month and is available at no direct cost to the caregiver. It behooves plans to support this population, Christine argues, not only because of their influence over member outcomes, but also because they could become a loyal future customer base. “You want to build a relationship with that adult daughter or son, because you take such good care of their mom,” Christine said.

Avanlee is incorporating AI to reduce friction for caregivers, like summarizing caregiver notes recorded via audio, recommending providers based on caregiver preferences and matching users’ questions with curated resources. It also makes recommendations to caregivers based on a number of insights, including what medications a member is taking, what conditions they have and what their social needs are. These AI features are powered by AWS Bedrock, with requests handled within Avanlee’s cloud environment rather than being sent to a third-party model provider.

“How do we make it the new norm in healthcare that these people are part of healthcare for mom or dad on day one,” Christine said.

How AI-Powered Automation Can Solve the Payment-Posting Bottleneck

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The healthcare industry has spent decades moving its payments off paper. Payers now send their payment and denial detail electronically through a standard file, the Electronic Data Interchange (EDI) 835, which cut out the paper and gave everyone a common format to work from. But while standardizing the data removed some of the manual effort, it didn’t make the work automatic. A file arriving electronically isn’t the same as a payment posting itself. The EDI 835 file lands, and people still open it, read each payer’s codes, post the payments line by line, and work the denials by hand.

That manual work is expensive, and the cost keeps climbing. In Experian Health’s 2025 State of Claims survey, 41 percent of providers reported that at least one in ten of their claims is denied, and Kodiak Solutions estimated that providers lost more than $48 billion to denials and bad debt in 2025, roughly a quarter more than the year before. Staff have to post, reconcile, and work every payment and denial before an account is settled, and most of that still happens by hand.

The reason posting stays manual has less to do with technology than with the rules themselves. Every payer handles their files differently, and the rules shift with each situation and exception so that the edge cases pile up faster than anyone can write them down. Many of those rules aren’t written down at all. They live in the heads of a few experienced billers, which means the organization can’t apply them consistently and can’t easily hand them to a system. When the rules aren’t captured, claims take longer to clear, avoidable denials slip through, and the first submission often collects less than it should have.

Traditional automation handles the clean, predictable claims and stalls on everything else. A rules engine only does what someone programmed into it ahead of time. The first time a payer sends an adjustment code it hasn’t seen, the claim moves to a person for review. Payers vary enough that a large share of claims end up in that queue.

A large language model closes that gap because it reads a claim the way an experienced biller does. It takes in the context; works out what the claim is, which payer rule applies, and how to handle exceptions; and hands it to the system that posts the payment. The model supplies the judgment, and the system posts the payment the same way every time.

Marlabs built a system that does exactly that for a healthcare billing company that handles payments for emergency medical services providers across North America. The company processes about 85,000 payments a month, and both the volume and the payer complexity were outgrowing what its team could keep pace with through hiring.

The most important part was how the system handled the rules the company hadn’t written down. Instead of loading it with a payer rulebook the company didn’t actually have, we had the AI learn from how the billers worked and from what the payers paid. It proposed rules, tested them against real outcomes, and tuned toward the result the business cares about most: collecting more of what insurance will pay on the first submission rather than after rounds of appeals. Knowledge that had lived in a few people’s heads became rules the company could finally see and improve.

Every claim still passes through a person before it’s final. The system posts the claim and shows its work so that a reviewer can see what the AI did on a clean claim and approve it in a few seconds. When the AI isn’t sure, or when a biller had to step in, the claim carries a plain explanation of what looked wrong and what the system recommends. In these cases, AI makes the review faster while accountability for every claim stays where it belongs: with the people and the company.

Those design choices, more than the technology on its own, produced the results. The company now posts about 85 percent of its payments automatically, which frees its team to spend time on the claims that genuinely need judgment. Posting errors fell by 95 percent, and reconciliation across systems is cleaner. The team resolves payer-specific denials faster, and for the first time it can watch its exceptions and their resolutions as they happen. Because we designed the framework to scale within each payer’s compliance requirements, more volume no longer forces more headcount.

Payment posting is one of the clearest places to start, because the sums involved are large and the return shows up directly in cash collected. By mapping the process honestly, teaching a system the rules an organization never wrote down, and keeping people in control of every claim, you can see measurable, tangible results. Handled that way, payment posting turns from a stubborn manual bottleneck into a process the business can scale.

Serata piena di gol e di emozioni in Champions League in questo mercoledì. A partire dal Como, che con una prestazione senza senso strapazza il Lens e vince addirittura 9-1, portandosi al quarto posto nella classifica globale.

Sconfitta invece del Napoli di Max Allegri sul campo del Porto: gli azzurri, in crisi nera, attualmente si trovano al 27esimo posto, sarebbero fuori anche dai playoff.

Tra le grandi vincono l'Arsenal, il Real Madrid e il Manchester City, perde il Liverpool col Fenerbahce. Successo anche per Borussia Dortmund e Club Brugge. Inter dopo questa giornata al 15esimo posto in classifica.
