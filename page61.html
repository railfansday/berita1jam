Manchester United book Michael Carrick sack decision meeting

Manchester United have already booked a meeting to discuss Michael Carrick's future as manager.

Under-pressure Carrick was given two games to effectively save his job, and oversaw a dramatic come-from-behind 3-2 win over Roma followed by an even more chaotic 4-4 draw with Aston Villa.

However, the point against Unai Emery’s men was not enough to pull Man Utd out of the relegation zone, with Carrick becoming the first manager in 40 years to guide the club into the bottom three of the top flight.

According to TEAMtalk, Director of football Jason Wilcox will now discuss Carrick’s future at the club’s Carrington training ground, with a decision needed on if the beleaguered boss will be given time to turn fortunes around or if it a new face in the dugout is needed.

The international break would offer any appointment the chance to settle into the role.

Before the 4-4 draw with Villa, reports suggested director of football Wilcox remained supportive of Carrick, while also being an admirer of Eddie Howe. The report states that his decision will be final.

The club have reportedly assessed several potential alternatives, including Howe, former Bayern Munich and Germany boss Julian Nagelsmann and ex-Chelsea and Tottenham head coach Antonio Conte. Mauricio Pochettino, who recently used a new contract with the USMNT, is being backed in the betting markets.

Speaking to NBC before the game, The Athletic’s David Ornstein said there is a desire for Carrick to turn things around but also claimed that the remains no clear plan should the former midfielder get the boot.

Ornstein said on Sunday: “Think the truth is, there isn’t a firm plan here at Old Trafford for what to do if they were to make a change.

He added: “I don’t know of a clear alternative plan.”

XRP could double while this yield strategy leaves you far behind

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

In a modeled doubling of XRP, a $10,000 XRP–RLUSD pool position reaches about $14,142 versus $20,000 from simply holding XRP, before fees and costs.

The XRP Ledger pool pairing XRP with Ripple’s dollar stablecoin, RLUSD, quotes about 4.7% annualized trading-fee yield. Joining this native pool changes what an XRP holder owns. The position becomes a shifting mix of XRP and the dollar stablecoin, surrendering some exposure to a rebound while cushioning some of a decline.

The trade matters with XRP near $1.16, down 7.1% over seven days in CryptoSlate’s November 7 capture. A hypothetical $10,000 pool position would accrue about $9 in fees inside the liquidity position over one week at the displayed rate if prices stayed unchanged, before costs. That is the scale of income to compare with the portfolio’s changing exposure.

At 13:07 UTC on November 7, XRPL.to’s pool data displayed a 4.7% annualized seven-day fee estimate and 0.09% weekly yield. The identified pool held approximately 1.94 million XRP and 2.25 million RLUSD. Data by xrpl.to.

The specific pool is rhWTXC2m2gGGA9WozUaoMm6kLAVPb1tcS3; its RLUSD issuer is rMxCKbEDwqr76QuheSUMdEGf4B9xJ8m5De, matching Ripple’s official address.

XRPL.to reported about 1.14 million XRP-equivalent of seven-day trading volume and 3,502 XRP-equivalent in estimated fees. Its API guide describes the annualized rate as recent trading fees relative to liquidity, scaled to a year. The quoted income varies with trading and does not lock in a year’s earnings.

Those figures are estimates, not a reconstruction of fees actually earned throughout the week. The displayed fee figure exactly matches seven-day volume multiplied by the current 0.308% trading fee, yet the response records a fee update on November 5, inside that week. Without the earlier rates and transaction-level fees, it does not establish realized income. XRPL also allows eligible auction-slot traders to pay discounted fees.

Under the XRPL’s constant-product mechanics, trading changes the XRP and RLUSD quantities represented by liquidity-provider tokens. Fees accumulate inside the pool and become part of the assets those LP tokens can redeem.

Two comparisons are therefore needed. Holding everything in XRP preserves full XRP price exposure. Leaving an initial 50/50 XRP–RLUSD allocation untouched starts with the same asset mix as the pool but avoids its automatic rebalancing.

Consider equal $10,000 starting investments. In a hypothetical model with RLUSD fixed at $1, no fees or costs, and the pool adjusting to XRP’s new relative price, a 20% XRP rise leaves the pool worth about $10,954. Untouched 50/50 holdings reach $11,000; an all-XRP holding reaches $12,000.

Most of the gap to all-XRP comes from choosing stablecoin exposure in the first place. The smaller gap to untouched 50/50 holdings isolates the rebalancing shortfall commonly called impermanent loss.

The downside comparison changes the conclusion. With XRP down 20%, the modeled pool is worth about $8,944, versus $9,000 for untouched 50/50 holdings and $8,000 for all-XRP. The pool still loses money, but retains more value than the fully exposed XRP position.

Larger moves widen the rebalancing gap. Doubling XRP leaves the modeled pool at $14,142 against $15,000 for untouched 50/50 and $20,000 for all-XRP. Halving XRP leaves $7,071, $7,500 and $5,000, respectively. These are scenarios, not forecasts or measured returns.

Few shows reach a triple-digit episode run; even reaching 100 episodes is a cause for celebration. Chicago Fire has gone two steps further, celebrating its 300th episode, aptly titled "Three Hundred," with a beautiful goodbye to Cruz. After his life-changing injury was revealed in the Chicago Fire season 15 premiere, Cruz found a reason to work through rehab before realizing that his family needs him more than his firehouse crew.

As bittersweet as that arc was, something within Chicago Fire season 15's milestone episode fell flat. The series had to break a trend it had followed in previous milestone episodes, and it shows that the series' heart has been missing for a while.

Several notable characters were absent in this important episode, including Boden, whose absence hurt Chicago Fire's legacy. The other glaringly absent character was Severide's best friend at Firehouse 51, Matt Casey (Jesse Spencer).

In previous milestone episodes of Chicago Fire, Casey was always at the center. The 100th episode included Casey and Dawson applying to adopt Louie, while the 200th episode focused on Casey's exit from the firehouse.

This tradition made sense; from the beginning of the series, Casey has been one of the most central and well-developed characters. Casey took Andrew Darden's loss hard in the series premiere, and the 200th episode featured the perfect connection back to him.

Chicago Fire season 15 had no choice but to move on from the Casey-focused story trend in episode 300. Spencer has moved on from the series. While he has returned for small storylines here and there, they have always been connected to Brett, who he was in a relationship with when he left Chicago for Portland. With Brett now gone and Dawson on missions helping people around the world, he has no major ties left at the firehouse.

Whenever a character returns, there needs to be a meaningful story behind it. Coming back just because it was Episode 300 isn't enough. With Cruz's exit set up, there wasn't room for Casey. Of all Chicago Fire's characters, Otis and Severide were the closest to Cruz, and only one could realistically be included in person.

Casey's absence was certainly felt, along with Boden's. A milestone episode is usually a chance to look back at how far a series has come. The 300th episode should be a monumental moment, as so few shows get to reach this point. Even before streaming, it was rare for a series to reach 300 episodes. It's a testament to how viewers have connected to the characters.

Casey's arcs were always meaningful to the firehouse as a whole. His decision to leave during the 200th episode connected back to the show's opening in a way nobody could have predicted. He stepped up as a father figure, sacrificing everything for someone he still missed and felt guilty about losing. Everyone felt that loss, while Cruz's exit really only affects Severide and Squad 3.

Chicago Fire's key changes for season 15 amount to a soft reboot this fall, but the changes won't fix the issues with the cast size in recent years.

While Chicago Fire season 15, episode 5 delivered some poignant and meaningful moments for Cruz, the rest of the hour fell flat. It largely focused on the new probies, and while it was great to see Kidd step up more as a leader, the arc seemed more about getting Marcus to fit in with Firehouse 51 and want that family. Severide's arc shows him floundering a little as captain, and he could use someone like Chicago Fire's Boden to step in and help guide him.

Even the humorous photo plot didn't quite capture the heart of other fun moments from previous milestone episodes. The only benefit this time was that Herrmann already had a photographer to capture that beautiful ending. Chicago Fire has lost a bit of its spark, and this 300th milestone episode proves it.

Samsung One UI removed the all apps button, but you can easily get it back

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

If you really don't like swiping to bring up the app drawer, don't worry. Samsung's got your back.

One of Android's biggest learning curves is the way Google's operating system organizes apps. Unlike the iPhone, which puts all your apps on the home screen by default, Android tucks them into a dedicated window called an app drawer. This lets you customize your home screen without hundreds of app icons cluttering it up.

On modern versions of Android, including Samsung's feature-packed One UI skin, you slide up on the home screen to bring up the app drawer, but that wasn't always the case. For years, many Android versions had an "all apps" button on the home screen. That button is essentially now redundant, replaced by the aforementioned swiping motion, and Samsung has stopped showing it on the home screen by default in recent One UI updates.

The only problem is that some users rely on the Apps button, either because they aren't aware of the new swipe-up gesture or because they're simply too used to having it around and don't want to adapt. Thankfully, Samsung left the "all apps" button as an option for users who want it back. You'll just need to dive into a settings menu to enable it.

To add the "all apps" button back to your Samsung Galaxy's home screen in One UI 9, you'll need to access the contextual options menu. Find a space on your home screen and long-press it or pinch as if zooming out on a photo. Either input will bring up several options. Tap the Settings button (the gear icon) in the bottom right of the screen, then scroll down to find and toggle on the option labeled Show Apps screen button on Home screen.

Return to your phone's home screen, and you should now see the "all apps" button on the right side of the favorite apps dock at the bottom of the page. Note that the button takes up space you could use for an extra app in your favorites dock, so it's only worth turning back on if you really don't want to start swiping up to access the app drawer instead.

Samsung is traditionally pretty good about retaining features that longtime users may have come to rely on, and offering the option to enable the "all apps" button is a good example. In some cases, though, the Korean giant might be too good. Samsung is the last major manufacturer still using Android 3.0 Honeycomb's three-button on-screen navigation by default, even though stock Android introduced gesture-based navigation with Android 9.0 Pie in 2018. Users who prefer the latter can switch between the two in Settings, though.

Through the air, Surace was sorely missing KJ Duff, as Black III led the team with six catches for 70 yards. Carter had the lone touchdown and finished with 40 yards on two catches, while Raymond added 21 yards with a reception. Elias Coke and Logan Blake rounded out the receiving column with two catches each for 18 and 14 yards respectively, while Thevenin lost two yards as the receivers were held to 161 total yards.

After both teams exchanged punts on their opening drives, Wisconsin drove down the field with the all too familiar chunk plays through the air: 15-yard pass, 9-yard pass, 18-yard run, and a 21-yard pass from Ryan Hopkins to Eugene Hilton Jr. to take an early 7-0 lead before the Scarlet Knights answered with a field goal.

The defense then held steady, allowing one field goal after a Rutgers three-and-out and punt from their own seven yard line set the Badgers up in plus territory. After the lone Knights' touchdown, they allowed another field goal, spurred by a 26-yard pass to Shamar Rigby and a pass interference on Devonte Anderson.

In the second half, it was an entirely different story, as the Rutgers offense sputtering resulted in repeated scoring drives for Wisconsin. Three separate plays went for 16 yards as the Badgers went up 16-10, then UW made Rutgers pay for a targeting call on Tariq Hayer as Hopkins connected with Ryan Schwendeman over the middle.

The game was largely decided on the next sequence with another Rutgers punt giving way to a 66-yard touchdown pass from Hopkins to tight end Jacob Harris, who ran the ball 47 yards up the right sideline while breaking and evading tackles. Rigby and Harris both crossed 70 yards in four and two plays, respectively.

For the Scarlet Knights, Zaire Angoy had a sack and 1.5 tackles for loss, while Hayer led the team in tackles with seven. Angoy and Kromah combined for a three-yard loss play on Darrion Dupree which set up a field goal, while DJ Allen had Rutgers' other sack.

The rush defense held up relatively well, holding the Badgers' run game to 122 total yards. Hilton led Wisconsin with 39 yards on three carries, while lead back Abu Sama had 35 yards on nine carries. The play of the defensive line has certainly improved, as the Badgers were held to just 4.2 yards per carry.

However, they made a true freshman quarterback making his first start look like a seasoned veteran, as Ryan Hopkins went 18-24 for 241 yards, three touchdowns, and no interceptions. Many of the throws were for over ten yards as the defensive gave up big and intermediate plays in space, as par for the course.

In total, Rutgers allowed 31 points, a near-perfect game from Hopkins, and tons of chunk plays all afternoon as Wisconsin turned a 13-10 halftime lead into a 31-10 victory to clinch bowl eligibility.

Next up for the Scarlet Knights is a Nebraska team that has watched a 5-0 start evaporate into four straight losses. After leading Indiana 17-6, the Cornhuskers fell 20-17 to the Hoosiers, then fell 17-14 at Oregon at the hands of Dylan Raiola and then a stunning defeat to Washington after leading most of the way.

Last week, Nebraska scored the opening touchdown, but the Illini scored three straight, and the Huskers were out of answers after Anthony Colandrea found Jacory Barney Jr. for a long score to cut the lead to eight. It was an upset victory for the Illini, and it sends Nebraska to New Jersey completely reeling.

However, the Scarlet Knights have looked all out of sorts, and are without Asamoah the rest of the season while Duff and Lonergan are uncertain for next week and beyond. Rutgers has never defeated Nebraska despite a few close calls, including the last two meetings which were decided by one point and one touchdown, respectively.

It will be hard to envision Rutgers finding a way to win, with the state of their offense being injured and defense struggling deeply, particularly through the air. However, the month of November continues to get easier, as the Scarlet Knights will have a chance to potentially pick off either Nebraska, Penn State, and Michigan State as the team looks to close out 2026 on a brighter note.
