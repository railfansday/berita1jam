Charlie Austin reacts to awkward Derby County, Sammie Szmodics interview

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Former QPR, Burnley and Southampton striker Charlie Austin has backed Derby County forward Sammie Szmodics after his tense interview with a local reporter last weekend.

It has been a turbulent start to the season for Derby, and Szmodics made his frustration clear after the 4-1 defeat at Millwall last Saturday when he was involved in a heated exchange with BBC Radio Derby's Ed Dawes.

It was another day to forget for Derby last Saturday as they were comprehensively beaten by Millwall at The Den, with three of the Lions' goals coming in the opening 17 minutes, and Szmodics fronted up to the media post-match.

However, when Dawes suggested to Szmodics that he would be particularly angry about the situation as he was 'John Eustace's boy', it is fair to say that the 31-year-old was less than impressed, responding: "Yeah, of course, because I'm a professional footballer that wants to win. I'm not John Eustace's boy."

Dawes later attempted to clarify his comments, stating that he was referring to the fact that Szmodics had played for Eustace previously, to which he replied: "Yes, I've played under the manager, I'm not his boy, but we're all in it together."

After spending the second half of last season on loan at Pride Park, Szmodics returned to Derby on a season-long loan in August, and the move will become permanent in the summer for a fee of around £2.7 million — or £5 million if the club win promoted to the Premier League — after an appearance-related clause in the deal was triggered.

Szmodics has scored four goals and provided one assist in 14 appearances this season, but he is set to be out for six weeks after aggravating a knee injury that had been troubling him for much of the campaign in the 4-0 defeat to Norwich City on Tuesday night.

Szmodics' interview has caused plenty of debate over the past week, and Austin has become the latest pundit to give his opinion on the saga, praising the Derby forward for his reaction to what he believes was an inappropriate question.

"They know they're not performing," Austin said on his Chaz Kicks Off! podcast.

"Eustace was the assistant manager at QPR when I went back there, but he was also in the Derby starting XI when Bobby Zamora scored in that play-off final to beat them 1-0 and Richard Keogh cried.

"Hear me out here, Sammie Szmodics saying to that reporter 'I'm not his boy', fair play to you mate, I've got a lot of time for it.

"The reporter was being an absolute one of them mate, let me tell you, suggesting 'you're getting picked when you're not performing because you're his boy'.

"Fair play to Sammie Szmodics for giving him a little bit back.

"Players know they're underperfoming, they don't need someone from the radio coming out with nonsense questions.

"He's had a little nibble back, fair play to him.

"The problem you've got is, you can only do the talking on the pitch, and that's what matters.

"At the moment, Derby are not talking, they're not performing, and they are in trouble."

Szmodics is one of a number of Derby players who have worked under Eustace at one of his previous clubs, and while the pair have a strong relationship, it is understandable that he took exception to Dawes' question after the defeat at Millwall.

After a 10-game winless run, the Rams lifted some of the pressure on Eustace with a 3-1 home victory over Bolton Wanderers on Friday night in Szmodics' absence, but they will still head into the international break sitting in the Championship relegation zone.

Delap’s Nottingham Forest stunners hand Manchester City third league defeat in a row

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Wait long enough, and results will come to reflect performances. Manchester City may have won their first seven games of the season, but they always looked vulnerable, and now have lost three in a row. Nottingham Forest may have been second bottom but, other than the twin curses of missing chances giving away daft goals, they seemed to be playing reasonably well. Put the two together, and the outcome was a dramatic regression to the mean. It turns out the stats were right, after all.

Liam Delap scored twice to remind people just what a good striker he can be. Oliver Glasner, after last week’s anger, can breathe a little easier in his job. And Enzo Maresca remains a paradox, a manager obsessive about control whose teams always seem oddly susceptible to direct running.

After last week’s defeat at Brentford, Glasner said he regretted compromising too much in the early weeks of his reign at Forest. Although he gave no precise details as to what that might mean, he elaborated a little on Thursday, speaking of the need for “trust and respect” in the right balance and suggesting there’d been too much trust and not enough respect this season at Forest: too much Woodstock and not enough “high-performance nature of military.”

Glasner’s brave new uncompromising world was ushered in with just two changes to the starting lineup from the defeat at Brentford last week, Ola Aina and Dan N’Doye dropping out for Daniel Muñoz and Ryan Yates, who both came on at half-time last week. It may have been an underwhelming revolution but it soon worked, Jair Cunha dispossessing Josko Gvardiol and running through to opening the scoring after six minutes, Forest’s first home goal since Morgan Gibbs-White’s strike against Bournemouth 34 minutes into the final game of last season, a drought of 510 minutes.

No side this season had underperformed their xG by such a margin as Forest; they began the weekend third in Opta’s expected table but second bottom of the actual table. To an extent that is a matter of misfortune: defeat at Brentford last week, for instance, was a result of a cross and a shot that both looped in off Nikola Milenkovic; when you concede one shot on target but two goals, you know you’ve been unlucky. And when, on Saturday, three minutes after Forest had taken the lead, a Jérémy Doku shot hit Milenkovic and, rather than looping agonisingly past Matz Sels, cannoned wide for a corner, Forest must really have known their luck was in.

One of the biggest issues for Forest this season has been the form of Delap. A gruelling spell at Chelsea had seemingly sapped his confidence but, 18 minutes in, he surged from inside his own half, ran, as Jair had for the opener, between Rúben Dias and Marc Guéhi, and thumped his shot over the dive of Gianluigi Donnarumma. With confidence, it turns out, Delap is a very different player.

Guéhi’s form, meanwhile, is becoming a major concern for City. With Dias wary of Xaver Schlager running to his right and potentially becoming a passing option, it was surely Guéhi’s responsibility to close Delap down. As it was, the pair left a huge channel, inviting Delap to keep going to score his second goal since joining Forest. Guéhi and Gvardiol were both withdrawn at half-time.

If James McAtee had not missed the target with two very presentable opportunities, Forest could have been out of sight by half-time. It was never, though, going to be that easy. Seven minutes into the second half, a long diagonal set Doku in behind Muñoz, and although his cross was half-cleared, Rayan Cherki slammed in a brilliant half volley. In a moment, the mood changed. All the anxiety stored up by two months of disappointing form bubbled to the surface again.

Forest may feel that, after the season they have had, they are owed some luck. They were nearly paid back a slice of that when a Yates shot deflected off Elliot Anderson and dropped on to the back of the bar. But still they had burned through a fair amount, with blocks, brave headers and clearances off the line when Gibbs-White set Delap away on the counter. A week ago, it would barely have registered as an opportunity but, self-belief restored, Delap swept into the box and drove his finish in off the far post.

Forest are out of the relegation zone and the idea that City might challenge Arsenal for the title, even if there is no points deduction, is beginning to look a little fanciful.

Hansi Flick has stressed the importance of going into the international break at the top of LaLiga as Barcelona prepare to lock horns with Atletico Madrid on Sunday.

Barcelona make the trip to the Metropolitano with a two-point buffer over Diego Simeone's side, while Real Madrid are ready to pounce should either team slip up.

The Blaugrana have won their last four away LaLiga games against Atletico and could become the third team in the competition's history to achieve five consecutive victories at the Rojiblancos' home ground, after Athletic Club in 1955 (W5) and Real Madrid in 2013 (W6).

However, Atletico come into the clash having won their last seven LaLiga matches, only managing a longer winning run in January 2025, when they won eight games in a row.

Simeone's side have also won two of their last five games against Barcelona in all competitions (L3), one more victory than they managed in their previous 10 meetings with the Blaugrana (W1 D1 L8), and Flick is expecting another stern test for his players.

"Every game is important. There are three or four excellent teams in LaLiga that are tight in the standings," Flick told reporters.

"It's important to beat Atletico there. We'll give it our all, but there's a long way to go until the end of the season.

"They are a tough team with a fantastic coach. It will be a demanding match. The previous games are in the past.

"The important thing is tomorrow, and we know that they are a fantastic team, and we want and must win to be at the top of the table before the break."

Zlatan Ibrahimovic Named ‘Cowardly’ Icon as His Toughest Ever Opponent

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Defenders dreaded coming up against Zlatan Ibrahimovic because of his unpredictability, huge frame and ability to find the back of the net from pretty much anywhere on the pitch.

The Swede spent years traveling around Europe to destroy defences, and he spent most of his career in Italy, a league renowned for its defensive style. He relished battling the best warrior-esque centre-backs in Serie A during his era, whether that be for AC Milan, Juventus or Inter Milan.

Ibrahimovic even landed in the Premier League with Manchester United and showed just why he's regarded as one of the best target men in football history. While most of his opponents couldn't handle his power and dominance, he admitted that one did manage to ruffle his feathers and outsmart him physically and mentally.

Ibrahimovic not only went to war with defenders in duels on the ground and in the air but also played mind games with his rivals. It took a big personality to be able to deal with him and Italian football has birthed many gritty and determined centre-backs.

None more so than Giorgio Chiellini, who made life difficult for attackers with his persistence and never-say-die attitude. He made an impression on Ibrahimovic whenever they clashed as he explained back in 2016 (via The Score):

"For me Giorgio Chiellini is the toughest, and the roughest, marker that I have ever faced."

You can easily keep your home network running during a power outage — here's how

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Stay online when the power goes out with the right backup hardware and a little planning.

If your power goes out, your laptop's battery will keep it up and running. It's a convenience that's not afforded to every electronic in your home. For example, networking gear, like a Wi-Fi mesh system or, more importantly, your router, will go dead silent in the event of an outage.

If you're on, say, an important video call for work, losing your connection could make for a rude interruption. It's even worse if you're mid-game, about to score that critical victory and secure bragging rights. Fortunately, there's an easy way to prevent interruptions in the event of a complete power outage.

The first step to keeping your network running in a power outage is a quick checkup on your networking equipment. If any of the devices between the wall jack and your laptop lose power, your connection goes with them. So, you'll want to understand that chain if you don't already. For cable internet, that means a modem and router, or a single gateway device that handles both.

If you have fiber internet, you'll have a router and an optical network terminal (ONT), or the box where the fiber line enters your home. Some providers install a battery backup on the ONT, although these are usually aimed at phone lines, not necessarily your internet connection. So check with your internet provider if you aren't sure.

Keep in mind that if your fiber ONT and router are in different rooms, you'll need two separate backup power units (or one with a long extension cord).

An uninterruptible power supply (UPS) is the best way to keep your internet up and running during a power outage. These devices sit between the power outlet and your networking gear, automatically switching to battery when your home's power goes out.

Like most products, they come in a range of prices. A modest one that can keep a router running for an hour or longer costs well under $100. More expensive models can last longer and even handle more power-hungry devices, like a PC.

It's worth doing a quick test run. Unplug the UPS from the wall when nobody at home is using the internet, and make sure everything stays online. If it does, you're ready for the next outage.

If you already own removable batteries for things like power tools and cordless vacuums, there's a cheaper option. You can buy small inverters compatible with your battery brand, whether it's DeWalt, Milwaukee or Ryobi.

There is a big downside. Unlike a UPS, inverters don't switch your networking gear over to battery power automatically, so you'll need to plug in the networking gear when the power goes out and wait for your equipment to restart. But on the positive side, inverters do let you swap batteries in and out if you get stuck in a longer outage.

As long as you supply the batteries, you can find inverters for as little as $30 for third-party models.

With a backup power source, it's easy to get carried away with keeping unessential devices working. However, whichever backup power option you use, plug in only your networking equipment; this isn't the place for your TV or game console.

And of course, this advice only applies to power outages where your internet provider's connection stays up. Your best option for a complete internet outage is your phone's mobile hotspot or a mobile hotspot device.

5 Most Ambitious LitRPG Books of All Time, Ranked by Goodreads

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Thanks to Matt Dinniman's Dungeon Crawler Carl becoming a legitimate phenomenon around the globe, a lot of casual and genre readers are starting to latch onto the massive worlds of LitRPG. Essentially taking a role-playing, progression type of game and bringing it into a literary form, LitRPG has become one of the hottest genres around. He Who Fights With Monsters has started to pick up a ton of steam in the wake of Dungeon Crawler Carl's success, and may very well be the next to get a screen adaptation. But there are so many LitRPG books and series out there to explore, and a lot of them are nothing like the ones you've already heard about or read.

There are some wildly ambitious LitRPG novels out there, each bringing something bold and new to a space that is doing nothing but expanding and evolving by the minute. Some show off what a story like Dungeon Crawler Carl would look like if the protagonist had their children with them for the dangerous event. Others take readers to remote farms on faraway planets as protagonists try and live peaceful lives. There's a genuinely great LitRPG series where the main character is literally a tree.

LitRPG writers have ambition in spades, delivering some awesome stories to check out that you may not be too familiar with. Below, we've picked out five of the genre's most ambitious, and ranked them according to the rating on Goodreads.

This eight-book Isekai adventure from author SpaizZzer just concluded earlier this fall, and it has stood for nearly a decade as one of the most unique stories in the LitRPG space. If you're not familiar, the main character of the series is a tree, and that premise was turned into eight very popular, highly loved books, which feels like a miracle in and of itself.

The first book of the series begins with a man named Matt being reborn into a fantasy world, which is the usual start to many LitRPG and/or Isekai stories. However, when Matt is reborn, he comes back as a sentient tree, unable to move from his spot. He quickly realizes there is more to his situation meets the eye, as the tree becomes the central battleground in a demon incursion, and Matt (aka TreeTree) needs to use his evolving powers to aid the heroes that find themselves beneath his branches.

So many LitRPG stories focus on adults in situations where they become players in some sort of dangerous adventure or death game, forcing them to level up and adapt to save themselves, and potentially their entire worlds. Erin Ampersand's Apocalypse Parenting series makes that entire concept devastatingly personal as it puts a regular mom in the midst of battle — right alongside her three young children.

The name really says it all. Apocalypse Parenting flips the script on the genre by exploring what it would be like to lead your own kids through the kind of crazy, other-worldly scenarios that are common in Dungeon Crawler Carl and countless other LitRPG novels. It makes for not only an emotional journey, but also one that makes you think a lot about the logistics that might plague most characters you encounter in stories like this.

The Wandering Inn is one of the biggest names in the world of LitRPG, and it has a little bit of everything. The story of Erin Solstice has some slice-of-life vibes to it, as she creates a safe harbor for creatures of all kinds, but it's also filled with a ton of great fantasy elements along the way. It checks so many boxes, which is ambitious in its own right, but it's the sheer size of The Wandering Inn that has it on this list.

The Wandering Inn is one of the longest written works in history, with 20 individual installments released over the last eight years. And those individual novels are often so long that the print versions, which finally started seeing the light of day this year, are actually splitting the books into multiple parts. For example, the original Wandering Inn book was made into two print novels consisting of 640 pages and 784 pages, respectively. It takes a lot to make a story interesting for this long, but Pirateaba has done just that.

There are a lot of slice-of-life LitRPG books out there, focusing less on world-ending battles or death games, and more on level systems can bring to the "slower" aspects of living. Seth Ring's Battle Mage Farmer series is the best of both worlds, delivering on the slice-of-life story without abandoning the bigger tales and stakes of the genre.

John Sutton's tale of trying to build a quiet life as a farmer on the outskirts of an empire comes as he tries his best to avoid the war and devastation all around him. He's at conflict from the jump, wanting nothing more than a simple existence but knowing that he's destined for a lot more. It's a brutally relatable conundrum he finds himself in, and it makes Battle Mage Farmer one of the more intriguing LitRPG series out there.

Speaking of warriors living out their days on hopefully peaceful farmland, Beware of Chicken is a layered story that takes a different approach to nearly every kind of tale you'll find in LitRPGs. It's also not at all what many are expecting from progression fantasy, but that's a big reason why it stands out.

The book begins with the protagonist waking up not only in another world, but in the body of a celestially powerful martial arts master. Instead of taking up the conflict that his former body host was a part of, the new Jin decides he'd rather take his powers home and start a farm. It's relaxing, hilarious, and constantly bubbling over with the threat of life outside the homestead.
