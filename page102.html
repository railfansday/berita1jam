28 Seats: European Carrier Reveals First-Ever Premium Economy Cabin For US Routes

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Irish flag carrier Aer Lingus has become the latest airline to join the premium economy revolution, with the airline having just unveiled its new product offering ahead of its launch next October. It plans to fit 28 spacious recliner seats on ten of its Airbus A330 widebody twinjets. This will represent the bulk of its twin-aisle fleet, with ch-aviation showing that it currently has two Airbus A330-200s and 12 A330-300s.

While the introduction of premium economy for the first time in the Irish flag carrier's history is the main headline, Aer Lingus will also use this refurbishment as an opportunity for a nose-to-tail reimagining of its A330 interiors, with business and economy class also changing. Passengers flying on Aer Lingus' US routes will be the first to benefit from the update, with Dublin to New York JFK being the first route.

Aer Lingus has chosen the RECARO R4 seat for its new premium economy cabin on the Airbus A330, which will be able to accommodate 28 passengers in four seven-abreast (2-3-2) rows. These flyers will be able to look forward to an increased seat pitch of 38 inches, with the seats measuring 18-19 inches in width. They will also offer guests a greater deal of recline, at eight inches. The soft product is strong too.

On this front, Aer Lingus says that its premium economy passengers can expect, among other things, "an upgraded three-course dining experience, complimentary drinks throughout the flight, comfortable bedding, an amenity kit and noise-reducing headphones." All the while, Starlink WiFi will help them to stay more connected inflight than ever before. Lynne Embleton, the CEO of Aer Lingus, said that:

"Our ambition here is simple. We want to give our customers greater choice, comfort, and connectivity, while creating an experience that feels unmistakably Aer Lingus."

It is no secret in the world of modern commercial aviation that the transatlantic market between Europe and North America is one of the most lucrative corridors around. As such, premium demand is often high, and Aer Lingus is set to tap into this trend by launching its newly fitted A330s on the long-haul route from Dublin (DUB) to New York (JFK). These flights will begin in just under a year, on October 1, 2027.

Initially, Aer Lingus plans to deploy its premium economy-equipped A330s on the EI104/105 rotation. These flights are operated today by the A330-300, which is the larger and more numerous of Aer Lingus' two variants of the Airbus A330. At the time of writing, these widebody twinjet aircraft feature a two-class configuration with 30 business class flatbeds and either 283 or 287 economy class seats on board.

As more and more of Aer Lingus' Airbus A330s are retrofitted with the new cabins, the carrier will be able to roll the product out on a growing variety of, as it puts it, "additional North American routes." According to Cirium, an aviation analytics company, its present A330 services across the pond from Dublin serve Boston, Chicago, Las Vegas, Los Angeles, Miami, New York, Orlando, and San Francisco.

The 8-hour flight rule explains when Premium Economy beats Business Class on price, comfort, sleep, and corporate travel value.

With ten of Aer Lingus' A330s being taken out of service for the premium economy refit, this presents an opportunity for the Irish flag carrier to undertake a nose-to-tail reimagining of its widebody twinjet fleet. This is exactly what it plans to do, hence the aforementioned addition of Starlink WiFi. This will also see it refit its business class cabins with 28 Collins Elevation suites in a four-abreast 1-2-1 layout.

Nessuno potrà mai sostituire Lionel Messi. Ma ogni grande ciclo ha bisogno di nuovi interpreti, di giocatori capaci di raccogliere una parte di quella responsabilità e di regalare emozioni. L'Argentina che guarda oltre ha già due possibili protagonisti: Nico Paz e Franco Mastantuono, talenti diversi - ma non troppo - e potenzialmente complementari. Due mancini (e già questo potrebbe bastare), due giocatori cresciuti con il sogno dell'Albiceleste e ora chiamati a raccogliere l'eredità più pesante del calcio moderno: quella lasciata dalla Pulce.

Oggi la bilancia pende inevitabilmente dalla parte di Nico Paz. Non perché il talento di Mastantuono sia inferiore, ma perché il classe 2004 ha già compiuto un passo che il collega deve ancora completare: ha trovato continuità. Il fantasista del Como, chiuso nel Real Madrid delle stelle, è diventato il simbolo della crescita della squadra di Cesc Fabregas, trovando l'ambiente ideale per esprimersi e diventando un riferimento tecnico, un giocatore capace di incidere con numeri impressionanti.

Anche con l'Argentina il suo impatto è stato immediato: 16 presenze e 6 gol, numeri che raccontano una crescita verticale e una fiducia sempre maggiore da parte di Lionel Scaloni. Paz non è più soltanto una promessa: è già un candidato concreto a un ruolo centrale nel nuovo ciclo.

Dall'altra parte c'è Franco Mastantuono, il gioiello che proprio il Real aveva scelto dopo l'esplosione al River Plate e che ora sta affrontando una nuova fase della sua crescita alla Fiorentina. A 19 anni il rendimento è inevitabilmente diverso: il talento è evidente, la personalità anche, ma la continuità rappresenta ancora la prossima frontiera da conquistare. Il passaggio al calcio europeo richiede tempo, ed è un passaggio che Nico Paz non ha dovuto affrontare: Mastantuono ha già mostrato lampi da campione, ma alterna ancora momenti di grande qualità ad altri in cui deve adattarsi ai ritmi, alle responsabilità e alle richieste di un calcio che non aspetta. È un processo normale per un ragazzo della sua età.

La sfida, però, non deve necessariamente trasformarsi in un duello. Anzi, il futuro dell'Argentina potrebbe passare proprio dalla loro convivenza. Paz e Mastantuono hanno caratteristiche diverse e possono completarsi: il talento del Como è un trequartista più creativo, capace di muoversi tra le linee e accendere la manovra, mentre il giocatore della Fiorentina ha maggiore predisposizione ad attaccare gli spazi, partire largo e incidere negli ultimi metri.

Due mancini con qualità tecniche importanti, ma con interpretazioni differenti del ruolo; Scaloni potrebbe quindi non dover essere costretto a scegliere, ma costruire attorno a entrambi la nuova Seleccion. Perché i grandi cicli nascono anche dalla capacità di far convivere talenti diversi, non necessariamente dalla ricerca di un unico erede. Una cosa è certa e dovrà essere chiara: Paz e Mastantuono dovranno costruire la propria storia senza lasciarsi schiacciare dal peso dei paragoni e dalla ricerca - che sarà sicuramente frenetica - di un nuovo Messi.

Axes, cutlass and blunt objects: Nigeria's campaign trail of violence

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

As Nigeria's January 2027 polls inch closer, a spate of violence and political thuggery during campaign rallies and town hall meetings is raising concerns about the safety of citizens participating in the election.

Samuel Adebanjo had gone to the Oregun area of Lagos, the economic heartbeat of Nigeria, to cover a town hall meeting organized by the opposition party, the National Democratic Congress (NDC). But what was supposed to be a routine reporting assignment took a violent turn when a group of armed men stormed the venue and disrupted the gathering. "I was at the entrance, taking photos of people entering the hall when some buses drove in and I heard a loud bang," Samuel recalled. Before he could process what was happening, the men got down and began chasing supporters of the party with axes, cutlasses, and wooden planks, forcing many to flee for safety.

Adebanjo ran towards the parking lot, hoping to find somewhere to hide. But some of the attackers spotted him and gave chase. The journalist with the local newspaper, The Cable, was surrounded by five men who started hitting him to make him let go of his camera. Despite wearing a press jacket, they accused him of being the party's cameraman. "They wanted to collect the camera. I was struggling with them because I didn't want them to take it away. In the process, my middle finger was broken," he said.

During the struggle, one of the thugs raised his axe and struck it towards his wrist. "I used my hand to guard it. I was expecting blood. I never knew it landed on my wristwatch, and that's when it hit me that they're not joking, and that I had to let go of my equipment."

He was left groaning in agonizing pain. By the time the violence subsided and he was taken to the hospital, he had sustained a broken middle finger and internal injuries around his ribs. The attack also left several other people injured and properties, including vehicles belonging to supporters of the party, destroyed.

As the January 16, 2027 general election inches closer, incidents like this are becoming increasingly common, as incumbent Bola Tinubu battles for re-election on a ticket that revolves around combating insecurity and resolving the country's biting economic crisis.

Adebanjo's experience is not an isolated incident. According to the National Peace Committee, 109 breaches of the National Peace Accord have been recorded across the country's 26 states and the federal capital, Abuja. Between August 19, when the campaign kicked off in the West African country, and October 27, political violence and thuggery, as well as misinformation and disinformation, top the violations, accounting for 33 and 29 incidents respectively.

Campaign rallies, town hall meetings and other political events organized by parties have been marred by violence, threats and intimidation, prompting analysts to caution that rising violence could taint the credibility of the elections and discourage voter participation. On November 15, a group of men attacked the campaign convoy of former Kano state governor and National Democratic Congress vice-presidential candidate Rabiu Kwankwanso during a visit to the Bichi area of Kano. Similar incidents were recorded in both Sokoto and Kebbi when former Vice President Atiku Abubakar took his campaign to the northwestern states.

Civil society groups, including Amnesty International, have condemned the spate of election-related violence and intimidation across the country, urging security agencies to bring perpetrators to book. President Tinubu has also condemned the rising incidents of violence at political events, warning that intimidation and political thuggery capable of disrupting public order would not be tolerated.

Political violence is not new to Nigeria; nearly all of the country's elections have been tainted by violence and intimidation, including the deadly post-election violence that followed the 2011 elections, where at least 800 people were killed over days of rioting in states across northern Nigeria. Raheem Mutiu, a Nigerian researcher at Armed Conflict Location & Event Data (ACLED), a global organization that collects data on conflicts and political violence worldwide, told DW that the pattern ahead of the coming polls is shaping up to show "political groups more agitated and determined to win elections by all means."

Mutiu described the spate of violence since the campaign was flagged off as mind-boggling. "Within the period of two months, we have recorded over 30 politically linked attacks with 10 fatalities. But what this one points to is that politically linked attacks have become more frequent and geographically widespread ahead of the January 2027 general election," he said.

The country's multiparty politics has entrenched a winner-takes-all system that fuels fierce competition among parties and, at times, a willingness to resort to violence. There's also a growing intolerance for opposition among party supporters, although perpetrators are difficult to identify because they often act anonymously and as proxies for the interests they support.

"What we have seen again is that supporters across the political spectrum are involved in the attacks, but intensity of their violence and engagement largely depends on their local stronghold and territorial dominance," Mutiu added.

A voter choosing under threat is not choosing freely, Dengiyefa Angalapu, a researcher at the Abuja-based Center for Democracy and Development, told DW. He noted that apart from the physical attacks, the spread of fake news and hate speech narratives, which form part of the non-physical breaches of the peace accord, "can deceive voters and undermine trust in genuine information."

Angalapu stressed that the growing threat of electoral violence could further undermine voter participation in a country already struggling with low turnout. More than 103 million Nigerians are registered to vote in the 2027 general elections, according to the country's electoral authority. However, the country has consistently recorded low voter participation, with the 2023 general elections witnessing the lowest turnout since Nigeria's return to democratic rule in 1999. Only 27% of the nearly 94 million registered voters cast their ballots.

"Intimidation adds another pressure: someone who fears punishment for their political preference may avoid meetings or withdraw from politics altogether," Angalapu added.

Adebanjo is still recovering from his injuries. The attack has left him with a bigger question about safety ahead of the polls: "If this could happen to me, a journalist, how much more the citizens? I am not sure, at this stage, people will even come out to vote."

Premier League KMI Panel unanimously admit to another refereeing error which wronged Liverpool

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The Premier League’s Key Match Incidents (KMI) Panel has adjudged that Liverpool had a penalty wrongly given against them in a recent fixture.

The Reds won their last game before the international break, beating Crystal Palace 3-1 at Selhurst Park, although Alisson Becker was denied a clean sheet by a stoppage-time spot kick awarded to the home side and converted by Eddie Nketiah.

The penalty was awarded for what referee Tony Harrington deemed to be a foul by Milos Kerkez on Dario Osorio when the two players unfortunately clashed heads, and Andoni Iraola said afterwards that he was ‘very upset‘ over that decision.

The Premier League’s KMI Panel has now published its findings from the most recent top-flight matchday (via premierleague.com), and all five independent assessors adjudged that the penalty award against Liverpool was incorrect.

However, there was also unanimous agreement that VAR was right not to intervene, as it was decreed that no ‘clear and obvious’ error had taken place.

The panel concluded: ‘The panel unanimously felt that the referee’s call of a penalty for a challenge by Kerkez on Osorio was incorrect, with the contact being inevitable given the jumping actions of both players.

‘However, the non-intervention of VAR was supported on the basis it was not a clear and obvious error.’

Record crowds, legends - Bordeaux bid to escape amateur game

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Bordeaux fans protested when the club were demoted to the sixth tier

The latest stop on Zinedine Zidane's homecoming tour was an important one for the new France head coach. When Les Bleus took on Turkey in Bordeaux on Sunday night, the ex-midfielder was returning to the city in which he spent four formative years of his playing career, between 1992 and 1996.

"I became a man in Bordeaux," Zidane fondly recalled in the build-up. France edged triumphed 2-1 with an uninspiring display, but it did not dampen the mood of the 40,000 fans inside the Stade Atlantique.

While the 1998 World Cup winner was not necessarily in familiar surroundings (the out-of-town stadium was built for Euro 2016, 20 years after his departure for Juventus), locals made every effort to make him feel at home, holding up a commemorative tifo before kick-off and chanting Zizou's name throughout.

Just a few miles up the road earlier that afternoon, Zidane's former club were playing their fifth match of the season in the sixth-tier Regional 1, in a north-western suburb of the city.

Thousands of Bordeaux fans had made the short away outing to Saint-Medard-en-Jalles' 3000-seater ground to watch their team seal a fifth win in five this season.

Girondins de Bordeaux, the six-time French champions, who were once regulars in European cups, now find themselves facing amateur opposition on a weekly basis.

Since 2022, the club has plummeted from Ligue 1 to the sixth tier through a combination of on-pitch struggles, mismanagement at all levels, and sanctions due to their financial troubles.

The club was taken over in 2021 by Gerard Lopez, who was previously in charge of Formula 1's Lotus as well as another French side, Lille.

While Bordeaux were already hamstrung by debts at that point, the situation on and off the pitch worsened under the Spanish-Luxembourgish businessman as the men's first team finished rock bottom of Ligue 1 in his first season.

A couple of seasons in Ligue 2 followed, before the club was docked down to the fourth tier in 2024. In their first National 2 season, they notably signed Andy Carroll but ultimately missed out on promotion.

The following campaign saw a repeat of that near miss, and the club was expelled from national-level football altogether this summer over their finances.

With Bordeaux left on the brink and whittled down to just eight full-time employees (down from around 250 a few years beforehand), Lopez sold up to Park Bench, a group of mainly British USA-based investors who also own Scottish side Dunfermline as well as a minority stake in Spanish second tier side Cordoba.

The supporters turned out in their numbers for the first home match of the campaign, which had been delayed amid the uncertainty over whether the club could continue running, against Stade Montois.

More than 11,000 tickets sold, a record crowd for that level of French football.

"140 years of history, we won't be stopped," read one of the banners held up by the Ultramarines fan group, who also made it a point to thank the seven first-team players who had stayed despite the club's double relegation.

The team is centred around a core of players with Ligue 1 and Ligue 2 experience. Steve Shamal, who scored a hat-trick in that 4-1 victory over Stade Montois, previously turned out for the likes of Caen and Annecy in the second tier.

"We know we should be higher up, but that certainty can also be a trap," said Shamal after the game. "We shouldn't think that it's going to be easy.

"We need to respect our opponents if we're going to put in good performances."

As the sixth tier of French football is regionalised, only the league winner in the Nouvelle Aquitaine region's top flight will earn promotion.

Their on-pitch success so far this season comes as little surprise given the gulf in quality compared to the rest of the league. Over the summer, Bordeaux notably managed to hold on to Europa League-winning full-back Almamy Toure, while former Belgian youth international striker Baptiste Guillaume also joined.

Several former players have also looked to get involved in Bordeaux's revival.

Benoit Tremoulinas, the former French international who was a part of the title-winning squad in 2008-09, notably announced that at the age of 40 he would be lacing up his boots again seven years after injury forced him to retire.

Paul Baysse, the former centre-back, is an assistant to head coach Arnaud Cormier.

Benoit Tremoulinas spent six years at Bordeaux, winning a league title and two cups, before also twice winning the Europa League with Sevilla

Perhaps the most significant endorsement the new owners could have hoped for was that of Alain Giresse, widely regarded as the club's greatest player. The Euro 1984 winner, who won three league titles while playing more than 500 times for the Marine et Blanc, was in attendance for the match against Stade Montois. He took the ceremonial kick-off before giving a full-time dressing room speech.

The new management unveiled their long-term plans for the club on Monday at Le Haillan, the city-owned training ground which includes a chateau that hosts the club's headquarters.

"We are humbled to have this responsibility," Park Bench investor Jeremy Green said.

"The club doesn't belong to Park Bench, it belongs to Bordeaux, it belongs to the 11,000 people who came to watch a Regional 1 game, to the players and to the community. We have a responsibility to rebuild this club.

"Our commitment to the community and to the players is that we will be fiscally responsible and patient.

"We're not a financial institution, we're a collection of individuals who bring with them business, sporting, analytical, and entrepreneurship skills."

Dominique Delport, the club's new president, revealed he knew Green and Twining through his wife, who studied at Oxford with them.

"We can learn from the club's golden years," the former journalist and advertising executive told BBC Sport, before adding that he would look to bring club legends to every league game.

Bordeaux won the Coupe de France as recently as 2013

While the club was sold for a symbolic euro, it did involve taking on long-standing debts which spanned transfer fees, stadium rent, and a compensation claim by former head coach Vladimir Petkovic.

Last Friday, the city's commercial court approved a request to restructure the debt as part of the club's continuation plan.

When Bordeaux were expelled from national-level football by the French FA at the start of the summer, the club used several avenues to appeal while the takeover was being finalised.

"We thought we had acquired the club in National 1 [the fourth tier]," Green and co-investor James Twining, told BBC Sport.

"Initially we walked away, but we were encouraged to come back," Green added. "We established that we were going to be able to restructure things and make it work.

"People around the club and in the local community encouraged us to try to find a way."

The six-time French champions have a long way to go before returning to the top flight.

"We're committing to a sensible financial structure, to a well-run club, and we're talking about 10 years to get this club back to Ligue 1," the new investors added.

A crucial step towards that long-term goal will be returning to the professional leagues, which would allow them to re-open the youth academy they were made to close when they dropped down.

Rebuilding the women's team, who were involved in the Champions League only a few seasons ago, is another one of the new owners' objectives.

Keeping the fans onside throughout the process will also be key, not least after the rift that opened up between groups during the previous ownership.

In any case, if the record-breaking turnout for Bordeaux's return to league action is anything to go by, their support seems unlikely to waver anytime soon.
