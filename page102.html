Worcestershire trio aim to make more Cup magic

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

It's now 55 seasons since non-league Hereford United's 2-1 fourth-round replay win over top-flight Newcastle United entered English football folklore as arguably the greatest FA Cup shock of all time.

Elsewhere in the Midlands, though, there have been many other upsets and moments of cup magic too.

Telford United (in 1984) and Kidderminster Harriers (in 1994) both made it to the fifth round, and Worcestershire village side Alvechurch hold a record they will keep forever - unless the rules are changed again - after taking four replays to see off Oxford City in a qualifying round tie in 1971.

Worcester City were involved in a record-breaking penalty shootout against Scunthorpe in 2014 - the same year they famously beat Coventry City.

But, while the memory of that day at Hereford - Ronnie Radford et al - surpasses all other memories, across the Worcestershire border, it perhaps still irks a bit.

Accompanied by fallen 1970s non-league stars Stafford Rangers, Worcestershire clubs make up three of the four non-EFL Midlands sides to have made it through to the first round proper - the Harriers plus seventh-tier duo Alvechurch and Worcester, who all face EFL clubs. How will they fare this time?

5th in League One v 6th in Southern League Premier Central (Tier 7)

On paper, it looks a very tall order for Alvechurch. But not every non-league club has got an FA Cup pedigree like the Church's manager Kyle Storer.

The former Bedworth United, Tamworth, Hinckley United, Atherstone Town, Nuneaton Town, Kiddy Harriers, Wrexham, Cheltenham Town, Solihull Moors, Nuneaton Borough, AFC Telford United, Kettering Town and Alvechurch player is not just a walking advert for Midlands non-league football, he has got plenty of FA Cup "previous" to be taken into consideration.

"I've been very fortunate," Storer told BBC Midlands Today.

"I got to the fourth round with Kiddy Harriers, when we played at Sunderland in front of 40,000. And that was having beaten Peterborough over two games with a team that had Joe Lolley, Marvin Johnson and Chey Dunkley."

Kyle Storer (background) led Kiddy Harriers out for their 1-0 fourth-round defeat by Sunderland at the Stadium of Light [Shutterstock]

"And, when I was at Tamworth, we won at Bournemouth [Storer actually scored the winner], then took Stoke to a replay. And we got Norwich at home in third round the following year."

Dion Dublin and Darren Huckerby both netted twice in a 4-1 win but Storer had the last word by getting one back. "It was voted goal of the round too," he grins.

"I've had some amazing FA Cup runs but let's hope we can go and create another day to remember. Let's hope we can cause some damage.

"in terms of football prospects, it's a tough one. They're flying, aren't they? But, in financial terms, then it's amazing."

23rd in League Two v 17th in Southern League Premier Central (Tier7)

For the younger generations, ask any Worcester City fan what the FA Cup means to them and they will bring up the 2013-14 season.

City caused "Pandemonium at the Ricoh", as BBC Hereford & Worcester freelance reporter Dan Johnson excitedly put it, when they beat then League One side Coventry.

Then came that 14-13 spotkicks defeat by Scunthorpe United in that astonishing, record-breaking 30-penalty shootout.

City also made the first round the following season when, despite having Troy Deeney's brother Ellis in the side, they were well beaten 3-0 at Bramall Lane by Sheffield United.

This is their first appearance in the first round proper since then.

But, for the result that still counts as City's biggest FA Cup scalp, you have to go way back to January 1959 - and the arrival of Liverpool at their old St George's Lane home.

FA Cup highlights: Liverpool lose to Worcester City

It was 11 months before the great Bill Shankly arrived at Liverpool to create the legendary 'Mighty Red' machine which became kings of English football and conquered all of Europe in the 1960s, 70s and 80s.

At the time, they were still in Division Two - and not promoted until 1962 after eight years in the second tier - but it's a memory that then schoolboy, Roger King, will ever forget.

"The game should have been on the Saturday but it was postponed," Roger told BBC Midlands Today. "It was quite icy.

"I was at school then, Christopher Whitehead Secondary Modern. The game was rearranged for the following Wednesday afternoon and the only way you could attend was to get the ticket signed by my father, to take the headmaster and he gave you permission to have the afternoon off.

"I can't remember much of the game but I do remember the crowd. We got to the ground at one o'clock and didn't get in until gone two o'clock. St George's Lane was just a residential street and it was thronged with thousands.

"A lot of people talk about Hereford and their notable win, but we'll never forget our win over Liverpool."

Can City cause another upset on Saturday? Well, Danny Cowley's Colchester have not won since a 1-0 victory over Rochdale on 29 August - 12 games ago - and have slipped to 23rd in League Two.

It might not be Liverpool or Coventry, but it would be another significant scalp for Worcester. And it was in Essex 68 years ago that their Cup heroics all began, when they drew 0-0 with Chelmsford and beat them in a first-round replay.

13th in National League v 20th in League One

After early exits in the qualifying rounds over the last two seasons, when they were a sixth-tier side, regular cup fighters Kidderminster Harriers make ther first appearance in round one since losing 2-1 at home to Fleetwood in 2023.

After promotion in May, Harriers made a great start back in the fifth tier under new manager Paul Wotton. But they have now not won in eight league games - their only victory in that time coming at home to fellow Midlanders Solihull Moors in the fourth qualifying round.

Now they face Grant McCann's Doncaster, who have themselves won just three league games so far this season.

"You always want to win your next game," Wotton told BBC Hereford & Worcester. "And we're at home. And there's no pressure on us.

"I know the manager well. I've played against him many times and I've been assistant manager when he's been the manager on the opposite bench.

"We're full time, so we'll have no excuses fitness-wise but they're two leagues higher than us.

"It's still a big jump for us. We're a million miles from League One.

"But don't let anyone ever tell you can't do anything. We'll be as committed as we possibly can."

Airbnb can invest in Brian Chesky’s AI lab, its quarterly filing shows

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Airbnb’s technology chief holds a stake in Chesky’s AI lab, and independent directors approved the deal. It landed with Airbnb’s fastest revenue growth in three years and a 2027 date for its own AI agent.

Brian Chesky at an Airbnb event in San Francisco in 2018

Brian Chesky’s AI lab paid Airbnb $7.1M for some of its intellectual property and to cover certain expenses, Airbnb disclosed in its quarterly filing on Thursday. Chesky, Airbnb’s chief executive, founded the lab and controls it.

Under the deal, signed this month, Airbnb can choose to join the lab’s future funding rounds. It also gets a licence to use some of the lab’s technology inside its own business. Chief technology officer Ahmad Al-Dahle holds a stake in the lab and works for it too. The Information, which reported the disclosure, said Chesky has funded the lab himself so far, citing a source. TNW has not independently verified that.

“Their roles with the AI Lab do not change their duties and responsibilities to Airbnb,” the filing said of the two executives.

A special committee of independent board members approved the deal under Airbnb’s policy on related-party transactions. The filing does not name the lab or say what it is building.

Al-Dahle joined Airbnb in January from Meta, where he led generative AI, Bloomberg reported at the time. We reported in June that Chesky was building an AI lab of his own.

The filing came with Airbnb’s third-quarter results. Revenue rose 19% to $4.9bn, its fastest growth in more than three years, and net income reached $1.9bn. Nights and seats booked rose 16%. Airbnb now expects revenue to grow in the high teens this year, up from at least the mid-teens. It also approved up to $12bn more in share buybacks.

“It’s rare for a marketplace of our size to reaccelerate growth, but that’s exactly what we’re doing,” Chesky told analysts on Thursday’s earnings call.

Airbnb last raised its forecast in August. Hotels, which it began adding this year, grew “markedly faster” than homes in the quarter, Chesky said.

Shares jumped more than 10% on Friday, Reuters reported. That set Airbnb apart from Booking, Expedia and Tripadvisor. All three have pointed to higher airfares linked to the Iran war and to unrest in parts of Europe, Reuters said.

Airbnb plans to start rolling out its own AI agent for planning and booking trips in the first half of 2027, it said alongside the results. Instead of a chat window, the agent will build each answer from maps, photos, comparisons, calendars and itineraries. It will also handle changes, refunds and payment problems after a booking.

Chesky argued that general assistants such as Meta’s Muse and Instinct are not built for travel. They still send Airbnb little business, he said on the call, as Business Insider reported.

“We’re not seeing a lot of traffic, and even fewer bookings, from Muse and Instinct yet,” Chesky said.

Agent traffic peaked in early October and has not grown since, he added. Airbnb’s own support assistant now resolves about 40% of customer issues without a human. Support costs per booking fell more than 17% from a year earlier, the company said.

Godzilla Minus Zero Has A Post-Credits Surprise (But It's Not A Scene)

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Godzilla Minus Zero's credits don't finish with a bonus post-credits scene teasing Gojira's next adventure, but that doesn't mean you should leave as soon as they start rolling. Picking up two years after Godzilla Minus One's ending, Takashi Yamazaki's sequel turns the Oscar-winning Japanese monster movies into a proper franchise.

As a result, there is an entirely new set of expectations for Godzilla Minus Zero. The 2023 original was not guaranteed to get a sequel, leaving all of Yamazaki's ideas for where the story could go to happen before the credits began and audiences risked missing any additional setup. But it is much more common for sequels to include credits scenes to promise audiences more movies.

Like its predecessor, Godzilla Minus Zero does not include a post-credits scene of any kind. That is not a sign to leave the theater as soon as the movie wraps up. There is a special audio cue for those who stick around for the whole time.

Warning: The following contains spoilers for Godzilla Minus Zero!At the very end of Godzilla Minus Zero's credits, there is audio of Godzilla stomping through rubble several times, followed by one final roar. This is the same way that Godzilla Minus One's credits ended, using the audio as a final tease for audiences rather than using a proper after-credits scene.

The meaning of Godzilla Minus Zero's post-credits audio has multiple interpretations. It is not used on every Toho Godzilla movie, so this could have become Yamazaki's signature way to finish his entries. However, since Godzilla is still alive and free at the end of Godzilla Minus Zero, it could also be argued that this is a tease that more destructive encounters between humans and Gojira will come.

Even before the final Godzilla Minus Zero audio surprise, the credits are worth sticking around for. Naoki Satō's incredibly epic score plays throughout the entire thing, so the credits become a final chance to experience his great music. Furthermore, it is also an opportunity to appreciate everyone who worked so hard to make Godzilla Minus Zero a reality.

The audio-only tease may be a bit disappointing for anyone hoping for a proper post-credits scene setting up a third film, but it is par for the course with this franchise. Godzilla Minus One didn't have one either. More broadly speaking, Godzilla movies rarely implement using Hollywood's franchise-expanding tool.

Across the 40 Godzilla movies released between Toho and American studios, only a handful have post-credits scenes. The last was 2019's Godzilla: King of the Monsters. You have to go back to 2003's Godzilla: Tokyo S.O.S. to find a live-action Toho production that included a proper post-credits scene. Otherwise, Toho's animated trilogy of Godzilla: Planet of the Monsters, Godzilla: City on the Edge of Battle, and Godzilla: The Planet Eater all had one.

Godzilla fans have not been conditioned to expect credits scenes in the monster's movies. It is traditional for these films to let their endings be where the story ends. This forces the films to include any sequel setup before the credits roll and lessens the possibility of teasing fans with stories that may not happen.

Even without a traditional post-credits scene, Godzilla Minus Zero still leaves the door open for this franchise to continue if Yamazaki wants to make a third. The movie ends with Kōichi, Noriko, and Akiko going on the run, Kanji becoming a captive of the United States, and Godzilla heading back into the ocean after killing the powerful King Ghidorah.

All of those threads could be expanded upon in a Godzilla Minus Zero sequel. But none have made it so a third film is mandatory to ensure Yamazaki's take on the kaiju goes unfinished. After all, Godzilla Minus Zero was promoted as the franchise's end by Toho.

Producer Kazuaki Kishida previously discussed Godzilla Minus Zero's sequel chances with ScreenRant at New York Comic Con, saying, "So this was true from Minus One to Minus Zero as well, but while we're working on any one Godzilla film, we don't think about any other film but the one we're working on. Because we have no idea what's going to happen with it."

Only time will tell if the story continues beyond Godzilla Minus Zero, but the post-credits audio is a clever way of hinting at Godzilla being involved in more destruction, even if we never see it.

Feedback Needed: Project by former DOJ lawyers shows people how to participate in federal rulemaking process

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

When Rebecca Bond left her position as chief of the Justice Department’s Disability Rights Section in May 2025, she knew what she wanted to do next.

“I wanted to help people understand the power that they have to impact policy by telling their stories when a rule is open for comment,” says Bond, who spent decades working with federal regulations. That taught her how important public input is in the rulemaking process and taught her that there’s a general lack of understanding as to how rulemaking affects people in simple ways.

Additionally, Bond says many people don’t know that they can participate in the process. In October, she and Kristin Stitcher, former deputy chief of the Disability Rights Section, launched Public Powered Policy, a project dedicated to informing the public about the rulemaking process and empowering them to participate in it.

They were joined initially by a former paralegal who has since started attending law school and now serves as an adviser.

“Our goal is to reach a large swath of people, not just lawyers,” says Bond, who serves as the project’s executive director. “We are trying to reach the teachers in Missouri and the potato farmers in Idaho.”

A podcast, Regularly Speaking, marked the group’s launch. It focused on the Federal Trade Commission and what responsibility online platforms should have when scammers use their advertising tools to impersonate banks or government agencies. The show is available on Apple, Spotify, and YouTube.

Stitcher says that while rulemaking can sound dull, it’s astonishing how much it impacts people’s day-to-day living.

Rules “can affect the quality of your water and air, what’s in your kids’ school lunches, and what transportation is available to you,” says Stitcher, the group’s director of policy and programs.

The group also communicates timely information about upcoming rules via Instagram reels, using the handle @publicpoweredpolicy. And Bond and Stitcher plan to educate the public through writing opinion pieces and training grassroots organizations.

Public Powered Policy is sponsored by the Social Good Fund, a California nonprofit. Bond also received a Leadership in Government Fellowship administered by Assets Under Movement, and the project welcomes individual donations.

The Public Powered Policy website explains the basics of how the administrative rules are written, the process for draft versions, and how to participate in public comment and make an impact.

Someone giving specific feedback about “lived experience” can influence what a rule ultimately says, according to Stitcher and Bond.

“Our goal is not to tell people what to think or comment, but to give them the information on how to comment and let them decide how they want to do that or if they want to do that,” Stitcher says.

Stitcher advises people commenting on rules to simply tell their stories with as much personal detail as possible.

“You are the expert in your own life,” Stitcher says. “They won’t know unless you tell them.”

Inside the Governor’s Mansions That Will Soon Be Home to 19 Newly Elected Leaders

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

In January, the United States’ 19 newly elected governors won’t just have inauguration day on their calendars, but moving day, too. All but five states have governor’s mansions for their leaders to live and work from, with the exceptions being Arizona, Idaho, Massachusetts, Rhode Island, and Vermont. These executive residences tend to be grand in style and scale, often possessing a long, rich history—even some haunted ones.

Sometimes, governors forego their official residences in favor of privately owned homes. For example, California governor Gavin Newsom recently listed the $7.5 million Fair Oaks home he lived in instead of the official executive residence, a 19th-century Victorian in Sacramento. Montana’s governor’s mansion is currently empty and in disrepair, while a governor hasn’t inhabited the official residence in Nebraska in over a decade.

Most states, however, have longstanding traditions of their leaders living in their official governor’s mansions. Virginia has the oldest continuously inhabited executive residence—every governor has lived there since 1813. If our new league of governors opts for tradition, here’s where they will be resting their heads for the next few years.

Republican Tommy Tuberville won Alabama’s gubernatorial race and will be able to move into a 119-year-old mansion in Montgomery following his inauguration. Built in 1907 by architect T. Weatherly Carter, the 17-room neoclassical residence features a row of four Corinthian columns at its front entrance, which opens to a foyer with a grand bifurcated staircase.

California’s new governor-elect is democrat Xavier Becerra, who will soon reside at a three-story Victorian in Sacramento. Built in 1877, the 20,000-square-foot house was thoroughly renovated in 2015, decades after Ronald and Nancy Reagan’s brief stint living there in 1967 before deeming it uninhabitable. The mansion was then used as a museum until its 2015 update, when it became home to the state’s governors once again. It has 30 rooms, Italian marble fireplaces, original wood floors, and a kidney-shaped swimming pool.

In Denver, democrat Phil Weiser will receive the keys to the red brick governor’s mansion known as Colorado’s Home. Built in 1908, it has been the state’s official governor’s residence since 1960. The Colonial Revival–style home has 27 rooms and shares its one-acre grounds with gardens, a historic gazebo, a greenhouse, and a carriage house. Thanks to John Hickenlooper, governor from 2011 to 2019, the home also has a rotating selection of local beers on tap.

Republican Byron Donalds just made history as Florida’s first Black governor. The state’s Greek Revival–style governor’s mansion in Tallahassee was completed in 1956 on the site of the original governor’s mansion built in 1907, which had fallen into disrepair. A screened swimming pool, a greenhouse, and a rose garden are among its amenities.

Democrat Keisha Lance Bottoms has the distinction of being the first Black woman governor-elect in the United States. Her soon-to-be residence in Atlanta is a 30-room Greek Revival mansion that was built in 1967 by architect A. Thomas Bradbury. Across three levels, it measures in at 30,000 square feet.

Iowa’s governor’s mansion, known as Terrace Hill, will soon be home to democrat Rob Sand. Bitcoin ETFs bought nearly twice what miners produced in Q3—but $430 million left on Thursday

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

ETF buyers absorbed more than new issuance in Q3, but concentrated purchases and older-holder distribution complicate Bitcoin’s scarcity case.

US spot Bitcoin ETF outflows totaled $430 million on Nov. 5, according to Farside Investors.

Bitcoin's ETF buyers added 79,800 BTC in the third quarter, against 41,300 BTC newly mined, according to 21Shares' Nov. 2 report. The comparison supports the scarcity argument, but offers limited reassurance about demand today: most of that buying arrived in two short windows, while older-holder balances fell by much more.

For Bitcoin holders assessing a supply squeeze, the unresolved question is whether fund demand persists between those buying bursts. New issuance is only one part of the picture. Coins already held by existing investors can also move, so purchases exceeding mining output do not establish how much supply buyers must absorb at a given price.

The 21Shares figures cover Q3 through Sept. 30. Aug. 17-27 and Sept. 21-25 accounted for 91% of ETF inflows measured in Bitcoin.

The same report recorded a 248,000 BTC reduction in long-term-holder balances during Q3. That larger change puts existing holdings alongside issuance in the scarcity discussion. However, it is a net balance decline, not a measured total of executed market sales.

Glassnode's long-term-holder position metric offers a more recent directional check. Its public page showed approximately negative 151,885 BTC in a 10:39 UTC capture on Nov. 6, labeled “24 hours ago.” That relative label does not establish a particular trading session.

The metric measures the rolling 30-day change in the cohort's supply. Coins mature into the long-term group as they age, while spending moves supply out. A negative reading means spending outweighs maturation, rather than that the displayed amount was sold during one day.

Glassnode classifies holders using an entity's averaged purchasing date, with gradual weighting centered on 155 days, and excludes exchange-wallet supply. The reading therefore describes a particular cohort, rather than all potential sellers. Its rolling window also differs from Q3, preventing a direct claim that distribution has accelerated or slowed.

The more persuasive recovery signal would be repeated ETF inflows alongside slower net distribution from older holders. Persistent redemptions with continued distribution would weaken the case.

Kia’s PV7 electric van stretches over 17 feet and seats 9

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Kia is expanding its electric van lineup with the PV7, a larger PV5 sibling that can carry nine people or a whole lot of cargo.

The PV5, Kia’s first fully electric van from its Platform Beyond Vehicle (PBV) business, is proving to be a bigger hit than expected.

A year after it went on sale, the PV5 quickly became Europe’s most popular small electric van. In the first half of 2026, it was the top seller in its segment, accounting for 37% of electric C-segment van sales across Europe.

Next year, Kia will launch the PV7, the second electric van from its PBV business. Kia revealed the PV7 at IAA Transportation in Hanover, Germany, in September.

It’s based on the same E-GMP.S platform as the PV5, but the PV7 features a considerably longer wheelbase and stretched body, opening up more space inside.

The PV7 is over 17.5 ft long, measuring 5,350 mm long, 1,995 mm wide, and 1,990 mm high, with a 3,500 mm wheelbase. In comparison, the PV5 is just 4,695 mm long, 1,895 mm wide, and 1,915 mm tall, with a 2,995 mm wheelbase.

That’s even bigger than the Volkswagen ID. Buzz Long-Wheelbase (LWB), which measures 4,962 mm in length with a 3,239 mm wheelbase.

Like the PV5, the PV7 will initially be available in Passenger and Cargo body styles, but Kia plans to launch 23 different variants, or “top hats,” over the next few years.

It will be offered with two nickel-cobalt-manganese (NCM) battery packs: 71.5 kWh and 96.2 kWh. The Long Range Cargo model offers an all-electric WLTP range of over 460 km (285 miles). Even at full capacity, Kia said the new PV7 achieves nearly 400 km (248 miles) of range and still has 350 liters of trunk space.

Front-wheel drive (FWD) will be standard at launch, but Kia plans to add an all-wheel drive (AWD) option later.

Depending on the configuration, the FWD electric powertrain delivers up to 268 horsepower (200 kW) and 309 lb-ft (420 Nm) of torque.

Thanks to its 800V architecture, the Kia PV7’s battery can charge from 10% to 80% in about 25 minutes with a 350 kW DC fast charger. It will be Kia’s first vehicle with two charging ports, including a front AC/DC port and an optional rear side AC port.

With vehicle-to-load (V2L) capabilities, it can serve as a mobile power source for tools, equipment, a campsite, or other external electronic devices.

The PV7 Cargo, designed for businesses, fleets, and logistics, is available in 2- and 3-seat configurations and offers up to 6.1 cubic meters of cargo space. The High Roof variant offers even more, with up to 8 cubic meters.

Maximum payload is up to 1,165 kg (2,568 lbs) for the Cargo Long and up to 1,200 kg (2,645 lbs) for the compact model.

The PV7 Cargo Long features a 550 mm loading height and, according to Kia, enough capacity for up to three Euro pallets (800 x 1,200 mm). An added heavy-duty mode adjusts the vehicle’s response to varying payloads to help maintain stability and control while driving.

Depending on the use, buyers can choose between full-open twin-swing tailgate doors or a lift-up tailgate.

The PV7 Passenger, on the other hand, is designed for personal use and families, or can also be used as a shuttle with room for up to nine passengers in the global version. In Korea, it will seat up to 11 passengers.

The 7-seater features Long-Rail Easy Remove Seats, enabling various interior seating configurations. The 9-seater variant, on the other hand, is available with or without a fourth row.

Inside, both versions feature Hyundai’s new Pleos Connect infotainment system, with 12.9″ and 14.6″ display screens and physical controls for commonly used functions.

The system runs Android Automotive OS, making it more like a smartphone with apps and customizable screens. It also includes Hyundai’s Gleo AI, an AI-powered in-car companion that can answer questions, control navigation or vehicle functions, and more.

Kia said the PV7 is based on a “Mobile Workspace” concept, with a layered dashboard and center console to optimize space.

The Kia PV7 Passenger and Cargo will go on sale in Korea and Europe in the second half of 2027. It will then roll out to new markets with new derivatives and conversions.

Kia will announce PV7 prices closer to its launch. In Korea, the PV5 Passenger and Cargo Compact starts at 45.9 million won ($34,000) and 41 million won ($30,000), respectively. In Germany, the PV5 Passenger 5-seater starts at €38,690 ($43,000).
