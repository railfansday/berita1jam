Project Hail Mary, this year's stunning Andy Weir adaptation starring Ryan Gosling, is one such movie. It may have been difficult to catch in IMAX, however, as IMAX screens are a hot commodity and the film has been out of theaters for a while. The film adaptation of Andy Weir's 2021 novel​​​​​​​ of the same name follows Ryland Grace (Gosling) as he wakes up on a spaceship without his memory. After years of openly saying he wanted the role, Gosling's casting was finally revealed at San Diego Comic-Con​​​​​​​. Nvidia said its AI data center loan guarantees won't kick in until facilities are built

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The company's maximum gross exposure on guarantees totaled $118 billion as of Oct. 25, and it has made no payments under them

Nvidia $NVDA said on Tuesday that its guarantees for data center buildouts take effect phase by phase, once each related building is completed and ready for service.

The largest guarantee, entered into in August, provides credit support on the land, power and shell buildout for about 4.25 gigawatts across nine buildings at SB Energy's PORTS-Pike Technology Campus in Ohio, the company said. The campus will host Nvidia infrastructure under 20-year leases to OpenAI, subject to limited exceptions.

The first guarantee is expected to take effect in fiscal 2029, Nvidia said. Its exposure builds as each building comes online, up to a maximum of $105 billion, then declines over each 20-year lease term as OpenAI makes lease payments. Nvidia has the option to provide credit support in phases for about 3.8 additional gigawatts as the site scales.

Maximum gross exposure on all of Nvidia's guarantees totaled $118 billion as of Oct. 25. That figure includes $105 billion in SB Energy credit support guarantees, $9.5 billion in power and systems credit support guarantees and $3.5 billion in land, power and shell guarantees for neoclouds. Nvidia said it has made no payments under its guarantees.

Nvidia said separate commitments to neoclouds take effect once the neoclouds secure financing to buy the data center infrastructure and the related compute is deployed, made operational and accepted by Nvidia. If those conditions are not resolved on time, certain commitments could be reduced or eliminated, the company said.

Interim CEO and CFO Colette Kress told analysts on Tuesday's earnings call that a guarantee ends if a customer is delayed or runs into trouble building, according to TechCrunch. "Our guarantee doesn't exist anymore," she said. Asked whether the terms were new, Kress said they simply spelled out conditions that already applied and did not signal a shift in direction.

Kress said Nvidia will treat help with land, power and shell for data centers as a top priority while reviewing which types of companies it will help finance, according to TechCrunch.

Nvidia reported fiscal third-quarter revenue of $115.8 billion, up 103% from a year earlier, on Tuesday. The results were its first since co-founder Jensen Huang resigned as CEO last week after admitting to an affair with a former executive at Singapore-based Megaspeed, a major Nvidia customer. Kress took over as interim CEO while keeping her CFO role, and the board began a search for a permanent chief executive.

Newsmax sued to block Fox from integrating its $22 billion Roku deal

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Newsmax says the combined Fox-Roku would hold more than 70% of free ad-supported streaming TV, according to its complaint

Newsmax sued Fox in California federal court on Wednesday to stop the company from integrating Roku $ROKU into its operations, arguing the $22 billion merger harms competition, according to Bloomberg.

According to Bloomberg, the complaint says combining Roku with Tubi, Fox's free streaming service, would hand Fox over 70% of the free ad-supported streaming TV market. Newsmax argues that Fox, owner of the Fox News channel, has a motive to put Newsmax at a disadvantage in streaming television.

Fox completed its acquisition of Roku on Nov. 6. The Justice Department closed its antitrust investigation the same day and said Fox had committed to keeping Roku an open and neutral content partner.

The commitments include keeping non-Fox content available on Roku for a minimum of three years and refraining from discriminating against others' content in search results, according to Bloomberg. The Justice Department did not file the commitments in federal court, which skipped a public review by a judge.

Newsmax calls the commitments inadequate and unenforceable by a court, according to Bloomberg. Newsmax says it requested that Fox sign a binding contract, but Fox refused. Fox did not respond to a request for comment.

Fox announced the deal in June. In September, the company said it expected the deal to close in the first half of 2027, after the Justice Department sent second requests for information to Fox and Roku.

Under the terms, Roku stockholders receive $95.30 in cash and 0.9799 shares of Fox Class A common stock for each Roku share. Fox said it expects about $400 million of run-rate cost synergies within two years of closing.

"Beginning today, our top priority for the combined company is to continue to relentlessly serve audiences and partners, while reinforcing the openness and choice that is the essence of the Roku platform," Fox Executive Chair and CEO Lachlan Murdoch said in a statement at closing.

Should You Buy Walmart Stock Before Nov. 19? History Has a Clear Answer.

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Walmart's earnings days are a coin flip, but its long-term record tells a much clearer story.

By Micah Zimmerman – Nov 18, 2026 at 1:51PM EST

Pop quiz: What did Walmart (WMT -0.16%) stock do the last four times the company reported earnings?

If you guessed "went up, because it's Walmart," you're one for four. Shares jumped about 6.5% after the November 2025 report, slipped about 1.4% in February, dropped about 7% in May, and tumbled about 9% in August.

Walmart reports again before the opening bell on Thursday, Nov. 19, with results due around 7 a.m. ET and a call with management an hour later. So should you buy before then? History gives a pretty clear answer -- just not the one you might expect.

Those four reactions had more to do with the forecast than the quarter. In May, sales beat expectations, but cautious guidance amid soaring gas prices sent shares lower. In August, Walmart raised its full-year outlook and still fell as Wall Street fixated on weaker-than-expected U.S. same-store sales. Walmart tends to lowball that forecast, too. After February's soft outlook, Baird analyst Peter Benedict pointed to the company's "history of conservatism."

Thursday's bar is high. Shares trade at about 46 times earnings and sit near their 52-week high after climbing about 7% in the past month. That's a rich P/E ratio, and CFO John David Rainey noted in August that Walmart was absorbing more than $2 billion in extra fuel costs "in arguably a softer consumer environment."

Zoom out, though, and those scary drops shrink fast. Someone who bought two days before August's plunge is still ahead today, and shares are up about 26% from a week before last November's report.

The longer view says the same thing. In its latest annual report, Walmart showed that $100 invested in February 2021, with dividends reinvested, grew to about $272 by early 2026, versus about $201 for the S&P 500. The interesting part is that the first two years were dull, with that $100 worth only about $106 by early 2023. That's the trouble with market timing. The payoff often shows up while you're not looking.

Walmart has also raised its dividend for 53 straight years. This move of over 50 straight years of raises makes it a Dividend King.

What keeps me interested is what Walmart actually does in the 90-odd days between earnings reports. Furner, a three-decade Walmart veteran, took over as CEO on Feb. 1, and he's betting on speed. "Speed isn't simply a fulfillment metric; it's an acquisition strategy," he said in August, adding that fast-delivery customers shop more often and are more likely to join Walmart+.

The newest piece arrives by air. On Nov. 5, Walmart agreed to add 300 Zipline drone delivery sites nationwide. "We see customers try drone delivery for the novelty and then continue to come back to it for the convenience," said Greg Cathey, who leads Walmart's e-commerce fulfillment transformation.

Walmart is also quietly making money on a lot more than groceries. Its advertising business, which includes Walmart Connect and Vizio, and its memberships both continued to grow last quarter. "The math isn't simply one plus one equals two," Furner said of how those businesses feed each other.

Then there's artificial intelligence. Walmart already sells through ChatGPT, Google's Gemini, and others, and it's rolling out Agent Profiles that let shoppers set a spending limit for an AI agent. "We price products, not people," the company wrote, promising agents see the same prices humans do. This holiday season, its Sparky assistant can even make an item's shelf tag light up in the store so you can find it.

So, should you buy Walmart stock before Nov. 19? History says the date barely matters. If you're buying just to catch a pop, the last four reports say you're flipping a coin. If you want to own Walmart for the next decade, buy when you're ready, maybe in pieces, so a rough Thursday becomes a chance to add more instead of a reason to panic.

Exchanging vows on the summit of Mount Bierstadt (14,065 feet)

GTA 6 Has Flock-Like Cameras To Track Your Movements

If you thought you were going to escape the nightmare of our growing surveillance state by blowing off some steam in the sprawling world of "Grand Theft Auto 6" this week, I've got some bad news for you. Just like if you actually boosted a car on the streets of Miami, there are cameras and police databases to keep an eye on your every simulated illegal movement. Just having the fastest car on the map isn't going to cut it anymore. To successfully make Vice City their domain, players are going to have to think strategically.

Video leaked in August showed the main character speeding by traffic cameras. The heads-up display showed three connected dots to indicate the car was being targeted by a license plate scanning camera, according to PCGamer. Passing those cameras will alert the cops to your location, and the you'll immediately enter a red zone where the authorities are hunting for you.

If you keep driving along the route where you alerted the cameras, the authorities will keep following you, just like in real life. The best way to escape those searching eyes of the flock-like cameras (and therefore the cops) is to low-key drive out of the red zone, just like in previous iterations of the game. Cops will also respond in more force (aka you'll gain more stars) if they know for sure your character is the perp they're seeking. Changing clothes, cars and weapons after a heist can help you give the cops the slip, says Techradar.

It's fair to say that the Jalopnik crew is wildly excited to tear through Vice City on some vaguely familiar but, for licensing purposes, completely unique high-end sports cars and grow our own individual empires. Whatever keeps us inside and away from the watchful eye of our tech overlords. When you emerge from your self-imposed video game exile, rubbing your eyes in the full light of day, just know you didn't waste a week of your life. You've actually just helped make real world driving safer.

For the second time this season, the San Francisco 49ers are set to play an international game, and once again, their travel schedule could end up being a key factor in the game's outcome.

Back in Week 1, the 49ers were sent to Australia to play the Rams, and the two teams took opposite approaches to travel. The 49ers headed to Melbourne a full week before the game while the Rams showed up just 24 hours before kickoff. By the time the game kicked off, the 49ers were fully acclimated to the 17-hour time difference, and it showed: San Francisco won 27-7.

The 49ers outsmarted the Rams with their travel plan in Week 1, and they might try to do the same this week against the Vikings in Mexico City.  Mexico's capital creates a unique challenge for NFL teams because it sits at an elevation of about 7,350 feet. To put that in perspective, Denver has the highest altitude of any NFL city at 5,280 feet, over a quarter-mile of elevation below what the 49ers and Vikings will be dealing with in Mexico.

When you're playing at an altitude that high, it's not easy to get acclimated, so the 49ers have decided to spend their entire practice week in Colorado Springs at Air Force Academy's Falcon Stadium, which sits at 6,621 feet.

Niners coach Kyle Shanahan was raised in Denver, so he knows how difficult it can be to adjust to high altitude, which is why he decided to move the team's base to Colorado this week.

"I think it helps," Shanahan said this week, when discussing practice at a higher altitude. "I grew up mainly in Denver, and I always know how bad it would be for me. It didn't matter how good a shape you're in. Like, when I came home from college, you'd run up the stairs and be out of breath. It takes some time to get used to it. It just is what it is with altitude. I know when you go to play in Denver, it's always a challenge, and I know Mexico City's higher than Denver. It's not make-or-break by any means, but I do think it makes you a couple percent better."

The 49ers have shown that they're willing to adjust their travel plan for the chance to be a "couple percent" better. They did it for Australia, and now, they're doing it for this trip. This will mark the NFL's first game in Mexico City since 2022, and the 49ers also played in that game. Four years ago, they also spent a full week in Colorado before heading to Mexico, and it apparently helped, because they ended up beating the Cardinals 38-10.

On the Vikings' end, they won't be making any changes to their practice location to prepare for the high altitude in Mexico. Instead, the Vikings will spend the week at their practice facility in Eagan, Minnesota, which sits at an altitude of 945 feet.

Minnesota might not be adjusting its travel schedule, but the team did install an altitude chamber to help players acclimate.

The chamber allows the players to get a workout in at a "high" altitude, but riding a stationary bike or running on a treadmill isn't exactly the same thing as practicing actual football.

Defensive coordinator Brian Flores previously coached a game in Mexico City as a member of the Patriots' coaching staff in 2017 — a game New England won 33-8 over the Raiders — so he has some first-hand experience with what the trip will be like. During an interview on Tuesday, Flores admitted he'll probably have to change his substitution patterns since the high altitude will likely cause his players to get winded faster.

"There's less oxygen, it's a tough place to play," Flores said of Mexico City. "It's certainly taxing. As a staff, we're already talking about, 'Hey, we're going have to rotate these guys and everybody's going to have to be ready to play.' Flores has faced Shanahan three times as a head coach or defensive coordinator, and he's won all three games, but this presents a different challenge. The Vikings have had the best defense in the NFL this year, but it will be interesting to see if the altitude changes that. If the defense is winded, it will be tough for them to keep up with San Francisco's offense.

Although the 49ers should be acclimated to the altitude by the time they arrive in Mexico City, the same can't be said for Minnesota. The Vikings will be stealing a page from the Rams' playbook when it comes to traveling this week. The Rams showed up in Australia one day before the game, and that's exactly what the Vikings plan on doing, with the team arriving on Saturday.

Oklahoma’s governor sees universities as ‘stuck in their ways.’ His fix, a 3-year bachelor’s degree, is already spreading fast

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Oklahoma Gov. Kevin Stitt has a problem with the four-year degree, and he brought it to a room full of the people who hire its graduates.

“The academic community are kind of stuck in their ways. They don’t like to change,” Stitt said Monday at the Fortune 500 Innovation Forum in Detroit, on a panel with U.S. Steel CEO David Burritt. He said when he digs into the degree programs at the University of Oklahoma and Oklahoma State, “there’s a book this thick and then you’ve got like three people graduating from these different things.”

He added: “I’m not saying that degree is not important, but you can’t be all things [to] all people.”

His fix is a 90-credit bachelor’s degree, 30 credits short of the standard 120, or roughly a year less of tuition. In-state tuition and fees at public four-year colleges averaged $11,950 in the 2024-25 school year, according to the College Board. Stitt said the idea is to keep the rigor of the requirements, trim some electives, and “get kids ready for the workforce quicker.”

RAND counted 119 reduced-credit bachelor’s programs publicly announced across the U.S. as of May 2026, up from five in 2024, most of them in “applied and workforce-facing fields.” Jason Lee, a senior policy researcher at RAND, has since told the Wall Street Journal the number of shortened bachelor’s programs announced this year are up to roughly 150.

Stitt signed an executive order in February ordering a 90-day study of 90-credit “accelerated” degrees, and the Legislature followed with HB 3315, which required the State Regents to study feasibility. In June, the regents took action to create a framework for the degrees.

“A three-year degree speeds up workforce entry and makes so much sense for students who are ready to work and support a family, right here in Oklahoma,” Stitt said in a statement at the time.

On Sept. 3, the regents approved four reduced-credit degrees ranging from 90 to 103 credit hours, according to the Oklahoma Council of Public Affairs: social work at the University of Oklahoma, two at Oklahoma State, in artificial intelligence and in business, and workforce development at Rogers State University.

“A college degree doesn’t have to take four years just because it always has,” Stitt posted on X.

Other states are moving, too. Tennessee’s higher education commission approved a policy in July allowing bachelor’s degrees of as few as 90 credit hours. Texas Gov. Greg Abbott directed the state’s coordinating board to develop pathways in August. The University of North Carolina system approved a pilot in April, and 11 campuses have submitted proposals, according to the James G. Martin Center.

The three-year degree tests an old question in economics: how much of a degree’s value comes from what students learn, and how much from what it tells employers. Michael Spence shared the 2001 Nobel Prize in economics, in part, for showing how education can work as a signal, costly proof of ability that employers can’t see directly. A shorter degree is cheaper to get. Nobody knows yet whether it signals as much. Much of Europe has run three-year bachelor’s degrees since the Bologna reforms began in 1999.

RAND found that business, information technology, and cybersecurity look most workable for shorter degrees, while fields with clinical or licensing requirements are less adaptable. How graduate schools, employers, and licensing boards will treat the degrees remains uncertain, RAND found.

“We don’t have employers who said, ‘Gee, we’ve had three-year graduates,’” Madeleine Green, executive director of the College-in-3 Exchange, told Inside Higher Ed this month.

The programs are still small. Johnson & Wales University has 135 students across four three-year programs, and Weber State has 30 across three, according to Inside Higher Ed.

Faculty groups have pushed back. The American Association of University Professors and the American Federation of Teachers objected to Massachusetts’ approvals on grounds of “academic integrity.”

“This is not innovation. This is just cutting corners,” AAUP president Todd Wolfson told the Associated Press in August. A year less of tuition is also a year less of revenue for the schools.

Stitt’s other pitch to the CEOs was apprenticeships. He said he had visited Switzerland and Germany, where compulsory education stops after the first year of high school and students start working inside companies, and that Oklahoma made liability insurance fixes so companies could take on high school students. He told the room about a 19-year-old in Switzerland who wanted to be an architect. After three years at an architecture firm, she could work as a draftsperson, and university was still open to her.

“We’ve got to rethink that in our workforce needs here in the U.S.,” Stitt said.

Colorado Trail and Continental Divide Trail ‘Extensively Damaged’ After Severe Flooding: Here’s What We Know

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Travel  |  Read Source →

This article was updated on November 18, 2026, at 11:48 a.m. with additional information regarding the condition of the Colorado Trail section 28 and the Continental Divide Trail.

In just over a week, two massive flooding events hit southwestern Colorado, destroying homes, businesses, and infrastructure across numerous mountain towns. But property is not all that the floods affected. Parts of both the Continental Divide Trail (CDT) and the Colorado Trail (CT) suffered damage that locals and trail organizations are calling “extensive” and “severe”, and which could take a very long time to repair.

“Rebuilding these damaged sections will not be quick, easy, or cheap,” Paul Talley, executive director of the Colorado Trail Foundation (CTF), said in a November 5 update on the floods. “But we are committed to rebuilding.”

As many of these areas are still inaccessible, there is very little confirmed information on their current state. Videos circulating online show missing bridges at key river crossings, and reports from the USFS and other agencies indicate that trailheads are damaged, and “heavy debris” is blocking some sections. But there has yet to be a full, official report on damage to these trails.

Here is what we know so far based on official updates, local reports, and videos.

Junction Creek is the CT’s southernmost section. Its trailhead, just outside of Durango, is also the CT’s southern terminus. Junction Creek Trail crosses multiple footbridges over the creek along its 19-mile stretch. A video posted to Instagram shows the Junction Creek trailhead sign that many hikers use for their CT terminus photo now standing directly at the edge of a tall wash filled with flood debris.

“Junction Creek Trail #553 suffered a near-total loss in places where it parallels and crosses Junction Creek,” a USFS conditions report from November 13 reads. “Miles of trail as well as multiple bridges crossing Junction Creek were wiped out, replaced with massive piles of boulders, trees, and other flood debris.”

According to the report, both the lowest trail bridge at 2.6 miles from the trailhead and the Walls Gulch Trail Bridge at 14.6 miles were destroyed. One social media video shows the first footbridge washed away completely. The USFS posted its own video of the road to Junction Creek to Facebook, showing a “20-foot waterfall” over the edge of the destroyed road.

“There is no doubt that trails across the forest have suffered severe damage,” the San Juan National Forest wrote.

In an interview with The Gazette, Talley said, “The Colorado Trail Foundation has never experienced an event that damaged this much trail. This is the largest section of trail ever damaged in our 52 years.”

Authorities claim that recovery work will likely begin in spring and carry into summer 2027 due to weather. They discouraged hikers from even attempting to set out on the area’s trails.

“Record flooding across Southwest Colorado has left San Juan National Forest roads, trails, and other infrastructure inaccessible, severely damaged, and, in many cases, impassable,” a USDA Forest Service Facebook post reads. “Don’t become a statistic—rescue will not be readily available due to the current demand for search and rescue services.”

The CTF said that it will be working over the winter with USFS, local partners, and volunteers to determine timelines and project budgets for the restoration efforts.

“It will be months before we can better understand what the next steps need to be on the sections damaged by climate events this season,” wrote Talley in his November 5 update.

There is even less information regarding damage to the CDT, which also passes through the flood-affected areas of Southwestern Colorado.

The Continental Divide Trail Coalition (CDTC) posted on Facebook on October 23, warning hikers that flooding had made “travel to and from the CDT trailhead at Wolf Creek Pass impossible,” and that “hikers may need to make alternate plans to avoid the area.”

On November 2, the CDTC posted another update for CDT travelers. “The extent of damage to the CDT, access roads, and CDT communities is still unknown,” it said. While the interactive map on its website shows no current closures of the trail due to flooding, the CDTC advised hikers to use “extreme caution and check with local authorities before visiting.”

Outside of Colorado, New Mexico also experienced severe flooding. However, Audra Labert with the CDTC told Backpacker, “The area of New Mexico near the southern terminus did not experience significant damage from flooding.”

While the full extent of the damage to these trails remains to be seen, what’s certain is that the CTF, CDTC, USFS, and other state agencies have a great deal of restoration work to do next summer. Volunteers will be in high demand, and Talley, of the CTF, promised to be open about how hikers can help.

“While we do not yet know what it will take to rebuild, we do know what we will need,” Talley wrote. “We will need our supporters, we will need our dedicated volunteers, and we will need that community spirit that makes the CT so special.”

Inside Well Beyond Breast Cancer’s Fundraiser to Give Survivors Access to Rehabilitation

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Many don’t realize that breast cancer care doesn’t end with treatment.

Under violet lights, hanging florals, and rattan lanterns, guests at Well Beyond Breast Cancer’s third annual fundraising event mingled, cried happy tears, and came together in New York City to support survivors. Well Beyond Breast Cancer is a nonprofit organization that helps survivors access rehabilitation services after breast cancer treatment. Founder Amanda Grilli, an oncology physical therapist, decided to start the organization just three years ago after moving to the United States from Canada and recognizing a major gap in care.

“I was shocked by the differences in the health care system between the two countries,” Grilli told ELLE. “I quickly realized that the majority of individuals who are affected by breast cancer don’t have access to a therapist like myself because they’re experiencing so many barriers, whether that’s financial toxicity, insurance gaps, or a fragmented health care system. [Many] people aren’t able to access this care on their own.”

For breast cancer survivors, care continues even after surgery, chemotherapy, or radiation end. But rehabilitation is a major piece of breast cancer recovery that often isn’t considered. Grilli says, “So many people experience lingering pain, or they have difficulty lifting their arms. What these therapists are doing is helping people overcome these side effects so that they can get back to living well.”

Guests had the opportunity to hear from one of the organization’s beneficiaries, Allison Berg, an independent documentary filmmaker who was diagnosed with breast cancer in 2025. Even after treatment, her recovery continued. Berg described shooting pain through her arms and fingertips, tightness every time she reached for anything, and tender scars. “My body is part of my equipment,” she shared. “I was having trouble just sitting at a laptop and typing. How was I ever going to carry gear and hold a camera again?” Well Beyond Breast Cancer covered 12 one-hour rehabilitation sessions for Berg, and in September, she was able to shoot for a new film.

Prior to the event yesterday, Well Beyond Breast Cancer had funded 250 breast cancer rehab appointments and allocated more than $50,000 to direct patient care. Last night, at ILA Penthouse in Manhattan, guests were able to participate in a live auction, which is still ongoing and has already raised thousands of dollars. Before they left, guests were also invited to share a wish with the “Keeper of Wishes,” a figurehead with “hopes” pinned to her dress, creating a growing display and fountain of hope that continued throughout the night.
