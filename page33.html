Helsing backer Plural raises $1.1B to fund Europe’s next moonshots

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Operator-led VC firm Plural has raised €1 billion ($1.1 billion) in new capital, joining a growing cohort of larger European funds that also includes Mundi Ventures’ Kembara and EQT-managed Scaleup Europe Fund.

Despite its size, Plural’s fundraise isn’t (only) for a growth fund: two thirds will go to its third early-stage fund, and one third to a new “opportunities” fund making select follow-on investments, TechCrunch learned from Plural partners Khaled Helioui and Carina Namih.

The two buckets are fixed, but the check size is not. Plural is “still very committed to being an early stage fund,” Namih said. But Helioui added that some of the early-stage “moonshots” it plans to back may require “raising a couple hundred million” to get off the ground.

“Moonshot” is an appropriate term to describe some of Plural’s portfolio highlights, such as Proxima Fusion and The Exploration Company — and its new fund will be looking for equally promising opportunities in energy, AI infrastructure, defense, health, and more.

Many of these are capital-intensive, but this is no longer stopping European entrepreneurs, especially second-time founders, and companies like Daniel Ek’s Helsing are “not isolated cases,” said Helioui, who backed the startup’s seed round alongside two Plural partners.

“If you’ve built and scaled a company, chances are, you’re more likely to be driven and passionate about having an impact on Europe’s ability to defend itself, procure its own energy, or own its operations and manufacturing, rather than just another app,” he said.

With bigger pockets, Plural will be able to keep up longer with the larger and larger rounds its portfolio companies have gone on to raise. It also made an effort to raise more of this capital from Europe, Namih said.

While Europe’s institutional LPs were, for a long time, more likely to back banker types than founders hoping to displace those bankers, the current shift towards Europe’s sovereignty and resilience creates tailwinds for Plural, whose office in Tallinn played a key role in its decision to invest in defense tech — including in a missile company, Hypersonica.

“The reality is today still, you have a different level of conversation and a different sense of appreciation of the urgency and the criticality of these topics depending on how far from the border to Russia you are,” said Helioui.

Two of Plural’s six partners are Estonians, and Skype alumni: Wise cofounder Taavet Hinrikus and Teleport’s Sten Tamkivi. However, there is more to Plural than Tallinn; the fund also has offices in London, where Helioui, Namih and Ian Hogarth are based; and in Paris, where it hired former Uber executive Pierre-Dimitri Gore-Coty as a full-time partner in October 2025.

According to a press release, each partner will lead a small number of new investments a year. These companies will then get access to Plural’s growing portfolio support, which already ranges from hiring and communications to finance, Helioui said. That’s another indicator that VC firms in Europe are increasingly starting to look like their U.S. peers.

Hertha BSC’s attacking numbers underline impressive start to 2. Bundesliga season

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

The Berliners have scored 25 goals so far, the joint-third highest total in the division, while their underlying numbers suggest their attacking success is built on more than simply clinical finishing.

According to Bundesliga Match Facts powered by AWS, Hertha have accumulated an expected goals (xGoals) rating of 22.9, the second-highest figure in the division. Only Heidenheim have generated a higher total, highlighting the quality of opportunities the capital club have consistently created.

Their shot efficiency rating of 2.1 also places them sixth in the division. Although several teams have been more clinical in front of goal, Hertha’s combination of regular chance creation and effective finishing has established them as one of the 2. Bundesliga’s most dangerous attacking sides.

One of the encouraging aspects of Hertha’s attacking development has been the contribution of different players throughout the squad, having relied heavily on individuals in years gone by.

Sebastian Grønning has provided an important focal point in the final third, while other members of the squad such as Oluwaseun Adewumi, Josip Brekalo and Mads Pedersen have also demonstrated their ability to contribute in attacking areas.

Rather than depending entirely on one goalscorer, Hertha have demonstrated an ability to find contributions from different areas of the pitch.

Their 5-3 victory over Eintracht Braunschweig provided an excellent example of that attacking depth. Despite falling two goals behind, Hertha responded with an impressive comeback, demonstrating their ability to create opportunities and punish opposition mistakes.

Their 2-1 victory over Wolfsburg before the break was another significant result, with Adewumi and Deyovaisio Zeefuik finding the net in front of a packed Olympiastadion. Against one of their promotion rivals, Hertha showed they could translate their attacking quality into important points.

The relationship between Hertha’s expected goals and actual goals scored is particularly interesting.

Their 22.9 xGoals figure indicates that they have consistently created opportunities worth more than 22 expected goals, while their 25 goals demonstrate that they have also managed to convert those chances effectively.

Although the difference suggests a degree of overperformance in front of goal, the gap is relatively modest compared to the overall volume of opportunities created.

This is important when assessing whether their current position is sustainable. Hertha are not simply benefiting from an unusually clinical spell; their underlying chance creation suggests they have the attacking foundations to remain competitive throughout the campaign.

Ranking second for xGoals, joint-third for goals scored and sixth for shot efficiency, they are performing strongly across all three attacking indicators.

For a club that has endured several frustrating years since dropping out of the Bundesliga in 2023, the current campaign represents a welcome change in fortunes.

Hertha have not only produced the results required to reach the summit but have also developed an attacking profile that compares favourably with the division’s strongest sides.

There is still a considerable amount of football to be played, and maintaining their current standards over an entire campaign will be the real challenge.

However, the numbers provide plenty of encouragement for supporters in the capital. With consistent chance creation, contributions from across the squad and an increasingly convincing attacking record, Hertha have established themselves as genuine contenders for promotion.

Their position at the top of 2. Bundesliga is no longer simply a reflection of a good run of results. The underlying statistics suggest there are solid reasons to believe their resurgence can continue.

Man City: Why the international break could be vital for Maresca

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

By Axel Clody | 16 Nov 2026 06:59

Manchester City began the season with the impression that adapting to Enzo Maresca’s work would be quick. With seven wins in the opening Premier League rounds, the team took the lead and at one point opened up a six-point advantage over Arsenal. The run, however, was abruptly interrupted.

Defeats to Aston Villa, Brighton and Nottingham Forest dropped the team to third place and allowed the current champions to pull clear at the top.

More than the scorelines, the performances exposed difficulties in competing physically, winning duels and protecting the defence when possession is lost. Maresca himself acknowledged that his team could not keep up with the intensity of the opposition in the first half.

So the November international break comes at a good time. The pause will not erase the problems that have appeared in recent weeks, nor does it mean that all the players will have days off. But it offers the manager a valuable opportunity to assess performance, recover players and work on adjustments that would hardly be possible with a run of matches.

The drop in City’s level cannot be explained only by a lack of efficiency in front of goal.

The team can still control possession and produce offensive volume. Against Forest, for example, it finished the game with 79 per cent possession and 12 shots. The problem is that this control has not been enough to stop opponents finding space and creating dangerous situations.

One of the main issues is defensive protection. City have conceded 17 goals in eight matches and have not kept a clean sheet in any of their last nine games. The high defensive line, a hallmark of Maresca’s teams, demands coordination between the units and a quick ability to react to losing the ball. When the counter-press does not work, the defenders are left exposed to fast attacks and have to defend large spaces.

The absence of Rodri helps to explain part of this difficulty. The Spaniard offered reading of the game, interceptions and the quality to organise the build-up. More than a defensive midfielder who protects the defence, he is a player capable of anticipating danger and stopping counter-attacks before they gather speed. Without him, the rest of the midfield has to share responsibilities that Rodri used to take on almost alone.

Elliot Anderson has been important in this context, but so too is the distribution of roles. The presence of players such as Enzo Fernandez and Rayan Cherki adds technical quality and creativity, but also demands defensive cover. If the two play together without a structure that protects the central area, Anderson can be overburdened in covering and in physical duels.

The break allows Maresca to work on these relationships. One possibility is to adjust the make-up of the midfield, ensuring more balance between creation and protection. Another is to review the team’s positioning without the ball, especially the distance between the defensive line and the most advanced players. It is not necessarily a question of abandoning the style of play, but of finding ways to make it less vulnerable.

The calendar and the physical condition of the squad are another important aspect. City lost rhythm during a demanding period of fixtures, and the recovery of players can help the team regain the intensity needed to execute Maresca’s model.

It will also be important to monitor the situation of Marc Guehi and Josko Gvardiol. Both were substituted at half-time against Nottingham Forest because of physical problems, according to Maresca. The break could be useful to assess the extent of the issues and avoid important players returning before they are fully fit.

There is, however, a caveat: the international break is not a holiday for the whole squad. Several starters have been called up by their national teams, among them Erling Haaland, Gianluigi Donnarumma, Ruben Dias, Gvardiol, Guehi, Enzo Fernandez and Cherki. Travel and international commitments reduce the recovery time for some players, while others will be able to stay in Manchester and work directly with the coaching staff.

Even so, the break offers an opportunity to reorganise the workload and prepare the squad for what follows. In a season in which intensity is essential to the functioning of Maresca’s system, managing physical wear and tear is also part of the recovery process.

The break arrives at a time when City need to recover not only results, but also confidence in their own way of playing. The seven opening wins showed that the team has the resources to compete for the title. The three defeats that followed, on the other hand, revealed that there are still important adjustments to be made to sustain that level over an entire season.

The first challenge after the break will be against Fulham at the Etihad Stadium. Then the team will face an even more significant fixture: the visit to Arsenal on 29 November. The six-point gap makes the direct meeting an important opportunity to close the distance, although the result will not be enough, by itself, to settle the title race.

For Maresca, the international break is a chance to find answers before the run of negative results turns into an even bigger problem. The manager needs to restore defensive solidity, find the best distribution of roles in midfield and make possession dominance translate into effective control of matches again.

City do not need to abandon their identity to react. Above all, they need to find a better balance between what they do with the ball and what they can do when they lose it. If the break allows Maresca to progress with this work and recover important players, the team could return better prepared to face a decisive stretch of the season.

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Chelsea's owners can only significantly boost the club's finances by investing in the redevelopment of Stamford Bridge.

That is according to former Manchester City financial adviser Stefan Borson, who exclusively told Football Insider that increasing the stadium's capacity will be very difficult, however.

Ahead of facing Leeds United after the international break, the Blues have introduced a VVIP experience, allowing fans to get up close and personal.

Supporters, who pay a minimum of £750, will be allowed to lay out their favourite player's kit and boots, go into the media room and player tunnel, as well as wave a matchday flag.

The introduction of this experience is a "very profitable" one, and one that has been greeted with a lot of positivity at Stamford Bridge.

Borson has already warned that it won't completely change Chelsea's finances, though, and that the only way the club can do that is with big changes.

Speaking exclusively to Football Insider, Borson has urged Chelsea's owners to speed up plans surrounding the redevelopment of Stamford Bridge.

"Ultimately, it's not going to move the dial. They've got 38,000 seats. And putting another 10,000, 15,000 seats in that stadium is very difficult," he said.

"But that's the thing that moves the dial, not 15 people paying even £1000 on a matchday for a unique experience. It's worth doing, definitely. I don't think it matters what the number is.

"It just doesn't affect the normal fan. So, people criticise it, but they shouldn't. It's the same as a super premium first-class seat on a plane versus economy.

"It shouldn't bother anybody that there are people who are prepared to pay for first class. They help subsidise the entire place."

Stamford Bridge remains a huge talking point in West London, given that it's the ninth-biggest stadium in the Premier League right now.
