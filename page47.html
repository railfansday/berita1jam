The new Marvel Comics app is a major improvement over Marvel Unlimited. Powered by Webtoon, this new mobile and desktop app gives you instant access to over 35,000 Marvel comic books, or over 85 years' worth of stories from The House of Ideas. While the new app isn’t perfect, it delivers easier navigation and a slicker interface than its predecessor.

I’m not familiar with Webtoon, but I used Marvel Unlimited for many years, as I detailed in my Marvel Unlimited — here's the 5 best classic comic books to read first article. I’ve used the Marvel Comics app for the past week and have largely enjoyed the experience. This is especially true because I haven’t read new Marvel comic books in a couple of years. The app provides a nice way to catch up where I left off — not to mention reading or re-reading the classics.

Here’s everything you need to know about the new Marvel Comics app, along with my hands-on experience using the app on some of the best tablets.

The new Marvel Comics app offers over 35,000 comics from the company's extensive catalog of Marvel, Star Wars, and 20th Century Studios. The Base subscription costs $9.99 a month or $69.99 a year, while the Ultimate tier costs $12.49 a month or $99.99 a year.

As Marvel detailed when it announced its partnership with Webtoon Entertainment for the new Marvel Comics app, Webtoon built the app from the ground up for mobile and web. Like the company’s own comic book reading platform, the Marvel Comics app lets you discover new and old books faster and makes reading more flexible across devices. The app supports traditional page-based comics (single-issue “floppies”) and vertical-scroll formats, such as Marvel Infinite.

The dynamic cover-scroll interface lets you browse comics right on the home screen and dive into the dedicated Discover section to find specific titles, characters, and creators. You can follow your favorites by clicking the follow button so you don’t miss new books or revisit older titles. The Pull List is especially useful because it surfaces the latest issues from followed series, characters, or creators. There is even an Upcoming Release section that shows which books will be available so you can add them to your Pull List ahead of release.

The Marvel Comics app offers two subscription tiers. The Base tier costs $9.99 a month or $69.99 a year (both plus tax). This gives you access to the full Marvel Comics digital library, with new issues arriving as soon as three months after their print release. The Ultimate tier gives you access to new comics as soon as 30 days after their print release, on top of the full library. This tier costs $12.49 per month or $99.99 a year (plus tax for both).

At first glance, the new Marvel Comics app doesn’t appear all that different from the old Marvel Unlimited app. As before, the interface has colorful comic book covers on a black background. However, the differences become apparent when you take a closer look at the dedicated sections on the Home page.

The Continue Reading section is there as before and is self-explanatory. This lists up to 20 books, so you’ll have plenty of space if you’re reading a lot of them. The Pull List directly under that section shows ma the latest issues from the series, characters, and creators you follow. You’ll find many of these books in the Latest Comics section further below, though this latter one has all the latest issues even if you don’t follow them.

Beyond that, there are several sections featuring recommendations and guides based on what you follow, along with showcases for whatever’s the hottest thing at Marvel—which, at time of writing, is the new Midnight imprint.

I tested the app on the iPad mini 8, iPad Pro M5, Amazon Alexa Tablet 12 Pro, and on my iPhone 16 Pro Max. I found that the app was just as snappy and responsive on all of these platforms, which include iOS 27, iPadOS 27, and Android 17. The interface is slightly different on Android, with the Home, Explore, Library, and Account tabs residing at the bottom of the Home page instead of at the top like on Apple devices.

Though I’ve gotten used to it, I find the Home page somewhat cluttered. I think all of the recommendations and guides are fantastic for brand-new readers, but if you’re like me and mainly want to see the new comics, it can be a lot to sift through. This is something that the rival DC Universe Infinite app has mastered, as you get the latest releases and trending series up top and dedicated reading guides at the bottom.

I have mixed feelings about the Explore tab. While it does let you browse through specific books/characters/creators, I wish it were something more user-friendly than an alphabetical list of whatever you’re exploring. Sifting from Amazing Spider-Man to X-Men isn’t exactly streamlined, let alone engaging. That said, typing what you’re searching for (such as Daredevil) brings you to a page with guides and series that are easier to navigate.

Lastly, I wish there were a dedicated settings option so that I could change the background from black to white. Yes, this would make the app appear more like DC Universe Infinite and less "Marvel-y," but having the option would be good, as the all-black background can be eye-straining for me.

The actual reading experience is largely the same as before. No matter which device I read on, drawings and text appear sharp and vivid. Page swipes are fast if you’re reading a full page like I typically do. I don’t often use the guided panel view on tablets, but it’s essential when reading on the iPad mini or my iPhone, since my eyes aren’t what they used to be. When you finish a book, you can then read the next issue or something the app recommends.

The biggest upgrade for me is getting new comics one month after they release physically in comic book shops. Again, I’m trying to get back into Marvel comics, so I want to be as up to date as possible. I also don’t think it’s a coincidence that this app launched a little over a month after the first Midnight books shipped in October. It was certainly nice seeing the new horror-centric takes on X-Men, Fantastic Four, and Spider-Man.

While this is a nice upgrade, I can’t say I’m surprised that the highest tier of the Marvel Comics app offers new books a month after retail. Why? Because this is something the DC Universe Infinite app has offered for over a year. Now, I can be up to date on both of The Big Two’s comics, which is awesome.

It’s still early days for the Marvel Comics app, so I’m sure Webtoon will tidy it up over time. If we can get fewer recommendations on the home page, a more streamlined search function, and the ability to change the background color, I'd be a lot happier.

Now that Marvel Comics has a new CEO, I think it’s the right time to return to the house Stan Lee and Jack Kirby (and many other legends) built. If you prefer reading comics digitally, or don’t want to pay $4.99 for single issues, then the Marvel Comics app is an excellent way to read the latest from Marvel, not to mention revisit your favorite stories from the past.

The $69 Base tier costs the same as Marvel Unlimited’s used to, and is a great way to dive in. If you’re more serious about your comics, the $99 Ultimate tier is well worth it—and also $20 less than DC’s Ultra yearly subscription plan. But whichever tier you get, you’re getting your money’s worth.

LAS VEGAS—Direct-to-consumer health companies Noom and Hims & Hers are making a play for the booming peptide market as the federal government loosens restrictions on compounded treatments.

The companies are betting that peptides could be the next big thing in consumer health. For Noom, the new peptide offering extends the company's business beyond weight loss into the broader longevity and wellness market. Hims & Hers signaled months ago that it was ready to tap into the multi-billion-dollar peptide market.

Federal policy shifts and U.S. Food and Drug Administration enforcement discretion around compounded peptides have opened new pathways for telehealth and compounding pharmacies.

On Tuesday, Noom launched OpenRx as a separate peptide and preventive health brand. At launch, OpenRx offers two injectable options: sermorelin, a peptide frequently prescribed for muscle support, and NAD+, a coenzyme frequently prescribed for energy. OpenRx also offers two oral dissolvable tablet options: the first formulation combines the peptides PT-141 and oxytocin with tadalafil to support sexual health. The second ODT formulation is the peptide oxytocin to support intimacy.

Noom says it plans to expand into other areas including recovery, muscle health, skin, gut health and sleep. Peptides are small pieces of protein that occur naturally in the body and help control functions such as growth, healing and appetite.

“Peptides are an important portion of the formulary of healthy aging,” Geoff Cook, CEO of Noom, said in a statement. “OpenRx enables our flagship Noom brand to deepen into cardiometabolic health while the new brand OpenRx supports patients with conditions too often neglected by traditional care models, like energy, muscle composition, skin health, sexual health, sleep, and recovery. Together, both brands support the same mission: to empower everyone, everywhere to live better longer – every day.”

Noom says every OpenRx prescription is written by a licensed clinician following an evaluation of the patient’s health history, goals and clinical appropriateness. Treatments are fulfilled through Noom’s U.S.-based pharmacy network, which operates under state boards of pharmacy oversight and adheres to USP compounding standards, the company said.

Noom is currently reviewing BPC-157 and TB-500 as part of its ongoing process of evaluating emerging treatments for inclusion in our formulary, Jeffrey Egler, M.D., Noom's chief medical officer, said in a statement. "Each therapy offered by OpenRx is selected because our clinical team and I believe it is appropriate for improving patient health. The process involves continuous monitoring of safety-related signals, gathering the judgment of our medical directors and the medical directors of affiliated clinical networks based on years of prescribing experience with the compounds in question, ensuring a compliant and stable supply chain, sourcing of active pharmaceutical ingredients, and setting up processes for compliance with state and federal adverse event reporting guidelines," Egler said.

Online health and wellness brand Hims & Hers announced earlier this month it planned to roll out a peptides business. As part of the company's new Peptides+ specialty, launched today, it plans to initially sell five U.S.-compounded peptide and longevity treatments—Sermorelin, NAD+, Glutathione, Vitamin B12 and L-Carnitine, with plans to add more peptides and therapies over time.

The company also plans to offer U.S.-made BPC-157 and TB-500 "consistent with regulatory requirements," the company said.

A survey the company conducted in October of 2,000 adults found that more than half (55%) said they were interested in or curious about peptide therapies, and 40% of U.S. adults say they are interested or very interested in a telehealth offering of injectable prescription treatments, including peptides and other compounded treatments. Nearly 1 in 10 U.S. adults (9%) say they currently use peptides, not including FDA-approved peptides such as Ozempic, Wegovy and insulin, the survey found.

Peptides are in the spotlight due to the massive success of FDA-approved GLP-1 weight loss medications like Ozempic and Wegovy, which are peptide drugs. Wellness influencers and biohackers on social media are promoting other lab-made peptides for fast-tracked healing, anti-aging and muscle growth.

Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. has championed looser federal rules on trendy, unapproved peptide injections. In July, an FDA advisory committee recommended broader access to six unapproved peptides. The panel recommended adding these six substances to the Section 503A Bulks List, which would remove the restrictions on how they are produced by compounders, Fierce Pharma reported.

Stopping short of a formal declaration allowing their use, Kennedy has cleared the way for the compounding of six popular wellness peptides, Fierce Pharma reported. Under Kennedy’s "enforcement discretion policy" issued in late October, HHS will not take enforcement action against pharmacies, physicians and federal facilities compounding six specific peptides, assuming those parties meet certain specific qualifications.

But medical experts have raised safety and clinical evidence questions around unapproved or experimental compounds. The American Medical Association (AMA) publicly opposes the Trump administration’s plan to make certain unapproved peptides more widely available for human use.

In a letter (PDF) to the Department of Health and Human Services (HHS), the AMA pushed back what it called a "rushed" decision to permit the compounding of certain peptides because they have not been approved for safe use by the FDA.

Unproven peptides may pose serious health risks, including allergic reactions, infections, hormonal imbalances and other unexpected side effects, according to the FDA website.

"We should not bypass the science and put patients at risk by making them guinea pigs for unapproved drugs," AMA President Willie Underwood III, M.D., said in a statement. "If these substances are safe and effective, the evidence should demonstrate that through the FDA’s rigorous review process."

Hims & Hers' peptide therapy program provides a "clinically guided, personalized plan" for consumers "backed by a licensed provider who understands these therapies," according to Anant Vinjamoori, M.D., chief medical officer of the Hims brand.

Hims & Hers wants to "set the standard" for peptide therapy and offer consistency in the quality of the products, Vinjamoori said in an interview on the sidelines of the HLTH conference this week.

"If you think about what we have to bring to the table with our scale and resources, I would argue there's no one better equipped to do it than us," Vinjamoori said.

Hims & Hers has invested in its supply chain and expanded its domestic manufacturing facilities. In 2025, the company acquired a Menlo Park peptide facility to run API validation and stability testing to support its specialized peptide offerings.

"That's why we're investing in our supply chain and doing a lot of the hard work that, frankly, is not the fastest path to making these available. There are pharmacies that are already out there that are making these available, and there are companies out there that will simply just plug into those pharmacies and service patients. But there are inconsistencies," he noted.

Until now, getting these types of medications has often meant relying on unreliable and unregulated online sellers, or a high-cost longevity clinic, Hims & Hers executives said. Hims & Hers' peptides offering gives consumers more accessible care, "backed by real medical leadership and with consistent clinical oversight," the company said in a press release.

Hims & Hers believes its scale gives it an opportunity and responsibility to help build the evidence base around peptides, using growing patient interest not just to expand access but to better understand how the treatments can be used safely and effectively, Vinjamoori said.

Expanding into peptides fits with Hims & Hers' ongoing focus on personalized health, he noted. "We started with sexual health, moved on to hair loss, and then we've continued to broaden into other categories such as hormone replacement, GLP-1s, and now peptides, and many others to come. And the common theme in all of this is that we're talking about helping people accomplish a specific goal that they have. We're not talking about curing a disease or treating a disease per se," he said. "We're talking about, 'Hey, there's something you want to do in your life that you're not able to do right now. Let's help you figure out how we can put together a plan to help you accomplish that."

"This is a big part of the future of medicine itself," Vinjamoori added. "It's been the missing part of the equation, where we've been very focused on treating and responding to disease, but not as much, not just on prevention, but really on helping people use their health as an asset and as a tool to help them accomplish what they want in life."

With Hims & Hers' peptide therapy offering, consumers start with a clinical visit with a licensed provider that includes a comprehensive intake of health history and goals. The company said a licensed provider trained in peptide therapies assesses each individual and, if appropriate, tailors a personalized protocol to their needs.

Hims & Hers treatments are compounded in the U.S. and dispensed directly by licensed, U.S. pharmacies regulated by state boards of pharmacy and "held to rigorous quality and safety standards," the company said.

The buzz around peptides ties into consumers' growing interest in taking control of their health "driven by the cultural zeitgeist moment," Vinjamoori told Fierce Healthcare.

"There's a variety of different factors that catalyze that. I think the COVID-19 pandemic catalyzed a lot of it for a variety of reasons. I think you see accelerations in biotechnology where people now see something like GLP-1s that can help with a problem that seemed intractable before. I think people are now interested in figuring out what is the range of solutions that could help me. I also think peptides represent something that has a lot of promise, a lot of excitement," he said.

"But there are also some cautions that we need to take as well. Something we think a lot about is how do we meet this moment of excitement and enthusiasm with some degree of rigor and guardrails," Vinjamoori said.

"It starts with the product itself. How do we ensure the quality? The compounds are of the highest quality and are compounded in the most rigorous way, made available in the most rigorous way. That's why we are trying to have as much oversight as we can into the full process of making these available," he said.

He asserted that informed consent and transparency with consumers are critical. "I've spent a lot of time with the team to make sure that we understand that people have this enthusiasm, but we need to make sure that they understand what they're getting into. There is a difference between this and FDA-approved therapies," he said.

"Sometimes people should try the standard of care before they engage with some of these things, and so we want to make sure we have these conversations with people openly and honestly. But at the same time, we also want to respect their choice," he added.

Hims & Hers Peptides+ is available with its Care+ program that gives customers access to twice-a-year lab testing to track their progress, unlimited support and ongoing check-ins from a dedicated care team. Customers can also message providers trained in peptide therapy 24/7 with any questions. The Hims Men’s panel includes at-home blood collection and access to lab tests that provide insights across 22 biomarkers, including testosterone, vitamin D, and cholesterol. The Hers Women’s panel tests 22 biomarkers across hormones, vitamins, and heart health.

Aegis Defenders is a fantastic game, brilliantly blending the tower defence and platforming genres in a unique way to produce a memorable and enjoyable experience.

Alwa’s Legacy is an excellent example of how to do a proper sequel; it takes everything that Alwa's Awakening did well and builds on it, adds in a few new ideas of its own, and corrects the issues that were previously present.

It may be a little on the short side, but Alwa’s Legacy is the special sort of game that only comes around once in a great while, reminding you of the kind of quality that’s possible when a dedicated crew puts in the time to make a polished and tightly designed final product.

While this turn-based affair might look, move, and feel just like a real board game, Armello is actually a completely original creation — and one that does more than simply doff its cap at the world of tabletop gaming. From the deep tactics of its living board game gameplay to the sheer charm of its worldbuilding and animal character designs, it's a quest filled with back-stabbing, political power plays, and rampaging monsters that’s different each and every time you play. It’s at its absolute best when played in multiplayer, that human factor making for an even more unpredictable battle for the corrupted throne. The Switch version has had its ups and downs since launch, with some updates making things worse, but it's apparently back to being in a playable state on Nintendo's console. As such, you’d be doing yourself a disservice not to add this anthropomorphic tale to your wishlist.

Astral Ascent is a prime example of a game that amounts to more than the sum of its parts. Its strong character design, hi-bit visuals, tight combat, memorable bosses, and broad build variety have all featured in games you’ve probably played before—there’s nothing ‘new’ here.

But to write it off as just 'another one of those' in a crowded genre would be to miss out on one of the most delightful and surprising releases of 2023. Astral Ascent is comfortably one of the best roguelites available on the Switch. Don't miss it.

Catherine: Full Body is a fantastic revamp of a bona fide cult classic. With an excellent new character and several new endings slickly inserted into an already highly entertaining narrative – not to mention a slew of fun new modes – this is the definitive version of an outstanding game.

If you've never played Catherine before, then you're in for an absolute treat, and if you have, we'd say there's enough new content here to make it worth diving in all over again.

An extremely impressive first game from The Wild Gentlemen, Chicken Police - Paint it RED! is one of the best visual novel/adventure games we've played in a long, long time and one hell of an absorbing tale. It looks and runs great on the Switch, with only a couple of really minuscule interface issues which are easily ignored.

At roughly eight or nine hours, the story is surprisingly lengthy, but we were never bored and wanted to investigate every little thing just to wring out more precious droplets of banter and fascination. Chicken Police is a polished, captivating experience and we're very excited for whatever's coming next from this team.

It's difficult to get across exactly why Danganronpa V3: Killing Harmony is so good without spoiling vast swathes of it, so we'll keep it simple. You'll come into the game baffled as to what the writers were thinking with some of these characters, and walk away loving each and every one of them.

There isn't a single moment of slack throughout the whole 40-hour playtime, it has the most extensive post-game of any title in the series, and one of the best endings to any game ever made. Go in blind and we promise you'll be in for the ride of your life.

Even so many years after its 2005 debut, Devil May Cry 3 remains one of the very best action games ever made. It may have aged in places, but that turbo-charged, combo-heavy action feels as fresh today as it did back in the mid-noughties.

The new Freestyle mode – which lets players swap combat styles on the fly at any point during the action – is a genuinely excellent addition to the package, and the ability to play Bloody Palace in local co-op is the cherry on top of a Switch port that should appeal to anyone interested in sampling the devilish delights of this all-time classic.

Dragon Ball FighterZ on the Switch is, simply put, one of the best fighting games we've seen on the system. Arc System Works went the extra mile in capturing the essence of the source material and distilled it into an incredible brawler that has lost nothing in the transition to Nintendo's hybrid console.

The stunning visuals, intense action, and easy-to-master controls make FighterZ a game all fans of the genre should have in their library. If you only play this kind of game casually, it remains a must-own.

Every now and then, a game comes along that captures our hearts and reminds us why we love the medium in the first place. Forager features wonderfully designed crafting mechanics, addictive progression systems and more charm than you can possibly handle. Although it has very minor drawbacks, there's enough good stuff present here for us to confidently recommend adding Forager to your collection.

Heretic + Hexen is yet another slam dunk for Nightdive Studios. This new release revamps both titles with modern controls, bonus content, and thoughtful changes to the core design that make each game a delight to play.

Granted, these are still FPS titles from the mid-'90s, so your mileage will definitely vary, but for returning fans and those with a particular fondness for old-school 'boomer shooters', you really can't go wrong. Now let's get a native Switch 2 port with 120fps please, Nightdive.

An absolute pleasure from start to finish, what Kaze and the Wild Masks lacks in originality it makes up for in the strength of its level design, responsive controls, kinetic move set and attractive visuals. Excellent, action-packed platforming through and through, with great gameplay variety and gimmicks that don’t compromise on what the game is best at — challenging, fast-paced obstacle courses and deviously hidden secret areas.

Easiest sell? Kaze and the Wild Masks is to Donkey Kong Country what Freedom Planet was to Sonic the Hedgehog. Don’t miss this one.

It may be over in the blink of an eye, but Kraino Origins proves itself to be a well-crafted and deeply enjoyable old-school action platformer while it lasts. There’s a little bit of Mega Man in here, a little more Castlevania, some Super Mario Bros. 3, but Kraino doesn’t feel overly derivative of such titles, largely because of its excellent level design. With some excellent 8-bit pixel art, this is an affordable and brief entry in the neo-retro genre, and one that we would definitely recommend.

The original La-Mulana was a masterpiece in its own right, but we’d contend that it’s been edged out by the sequel. What we have here is an expertly-paced, crushingly difficult, and utterly mystifying adventure that constantly beckons you to explore deeper and acquaint yourself with its unique charms. La-Mulana 2 definitely isn’t for everybody – if you consider yourself an impatient or clumsy player, steer clear – but those who put in the time and effort to crack this nut will be deeply satisfied that they did so. We’d give La-Mulana 2 a high recommendation for anybody looking for a deep and challenging new Metroidvania for their Switch; it’s an acquired taste, but one you will come to savour.

Mega Man X Legacy Collection is a fantastic re-release of several must-play gaming classics, presenting them as authentically as possible while still including a few modern features on top that help to improve the overall experience and keep it from feeling antiquated.

Throw in the new X Challenge mode and an enormous archive of museum content and this stands as the definitive way to play the Mega Man X games in the modern era. Four fantastic games, a bevvy of extra content, and the ability to play at home or on the go — nothing not to like here.

Mega Man Zero/ZX Legacy Collection is everything that you could’ve hoped it to be. This is ultimately six great Mega Man games presented to you with a slew of customisable features, extra content, and quality-of-life updates.

If you’re a fan of either Mega Man or side-scrolling action games in general, you owe it to yourself to give this release a go and see what all the fuss is about. These are hard and occasionally frustrating games, but they offer up some rewarding, action-heavy gameplay that even today stands among the best of its class. Don’t pass this collection up.

Monster Hunter Generations Ultimate may lack the accessibility that made Monster Hunter: World such a smash hit, but it more than makes up for it by being a sort of ‘greatest hits’ collection of the high points of the series, giving you hundreds of hours of content to play through. Couple this with the HD visuals, easy-to-use multiplayer, and the ability to play the full experience on the go, and you’ve got a game that will appeal to both veterans and newcomers alike.

It may not necessarily represent the future of the series, but this is a fantastic experience in its own right and a worthy follow-up to the 3DS original, and one that no Switch owner will want to be without, even if series newcomers might want to start with Rise first.

A developer using an AI assistant can open a pull request in minutes, yet the change still sits in a queue until a human reviewer picks it up. That is why agent-written pull requests wait 17.6 hours for a first review against 3.4 hours for human ones, even as enterprises spent $4.0 billion on AI coding tools in 2025, seven times the year before.

Today we sit down with Ori Keren, co-founder and CEO of LinearB, to discuss how the company grew from engineering dashboards into a context layer for agents, how it sells to boards that want proof of AI's return and where developer productivity goes once agents write much of the code.

Ishan Pandey: Welcome to Behind the Startup, Ori. Tell us about your journey and what made you build LinearB in the first place?

Ori Keren: Thanks for having me, Ishan. I started writing code as a kid, programming in BASIC on a Sinclair Spectrum in 1987, and I got my first developer job in 2000. From there I moved into management. I ran an engineering group at Interwise, which AT&T acquired, and then I was VP of R&D at CloudLock, which Cisco acquired in 2016. That's where I met Dan Lines, my co-founder.

What stayed with me from those years is how blind software development is. You run a team of a hundred engineers, and every week the business asks when something will ship or why it's late. You answer from gut feel and status meetings. Meanwhile, every answer is sitting in Git, in Jira and in your CI system, just never connected.

So in 2019, Dan and I started LinearB to connect it. We wanted to give engineering leaders real data on how their teams deliver, instead of gut feel, and then help them act on it. Helping teams act on the data is still what drives everything we build, and today those teams include agents.

Ishan Pandey: LinearB started in 2019 by giving engineering leaders delivery metrics, yet you have now rebuilt the platform around a knowledge graph of the whole software delivery lifecycle. What did you see in customer data over the past year that told you this called for a rebuild of the company's core?

Ori Keren: Two things, and they showed up in the data at the same time.

The first was that AI made writing code cheap but didn't make delivery faster. Our benchmarks cover 8.1 million pull requests from 4,800 teams. AI-assisted pull requests are about 2.6 times larger than unassisted ones. Pull requests opened by agents wait 17.6 hours for a first review, against 3.4 hours for human ones. The bottleneck simply moved downstream, into review, merge and ownership.

The second was why agents struggle. An agent starts every task cold. It has a good idea of the code, but it doesn't have the business context. It doesn't know that this service had two incidents last month, that this file has been rewritten five times this quarter, or who owns it. We had all of that, linked from ticket to release, for every customer.

That's when it clicked. Everyone else is focused on writing code. We hold a knowledge graph of everything happening across delivery, and that's exactly what agents and teams need. A dashboard can't hand that context to an agent, so we rebuilt LinearB around the graph itself. Context is the new king.

Ishan Pandey: When you raised your Series B in 2022, LinearB served more than 5,000 development teams. How has the customer base grown since then and which kind of company is now expanding fastest on the platform?

Ori Keren: We've moved up market. In 2022, a lot of our growth came bottom up, from engineering managers at fast-moving tech companies. Today our core is the enterprise, meaning organizations with hundreds or thousands of engineers, several AI coding tools running at once, and a board asking what it's getting for the spend. We're also seeing strong growth among healthcare organizations.

The pattern is consistent. A company adopts Copilot here, Cursor there and Claude Code somewhere else. Within a quarter or two, leadership realizes it can't answer basic questions, like whether the code holds up, where it's working and what it's costing. That's when they come to us, and that's when they expand.

Kraken is a good example of what that looks like. They used our MCP with an AI assistant to find out what was slowing their code reviews. It turned out to be time zones and reviewer availability, not change size. Within four months, they cut review time at the 90th percentile by more than half.

Ishan Pandey: Boards and CFOs now ask directly whether AI coding spend is paying off. How has that changed who signs a LinearB contract, from the VP of engineering to the CFO or CIO, along with how a deal is won?

Ori Keren: The SVP of engineering or CTO is still our executive buyer.

Who they answer to. Ken Ringdahl, the CTO at Emburse, puts our charts straight into his board decks because his board asks specifically about AI use and ROI. That's now normal.

There are a lot of company-wide AI initiatives now, driven by the CFO or CEO, and in the companies we work with, engineering is usually where AI spend is highest.

So finance is in the room far more often. Engineering leaders bring us in because they need numbers that hold up in front of a CFO, not a vanity metric from a vendor's own dashboard.

That changes how a deal is won. Five years ago, we won by showing a better cycle-time chart. Today we win by answering the finance question in the evaluation itself. We connect the data, and within days we show which teams are getting value from AI, which aren't, and what each delivered change costs. Token spend makes this sharper. In our 2026 AI benchmarks, which cover 253 engineering organizations, developers at the 90th percentile now spend $35.20 per coding day on tokens, up nearly 75% in three months. When a CFO sees that line, the conversation moves fast.

Ishan Pandey: LinearB built an audience through the Dev Interrupted podcast and free developer tools long before Gartner gave the category a Magic Quadrant. How does that community-led motion now work alongside enterprise sales and which channel brings in your largest customers today?

Ori Keren: Dev Interrupted was never meant as a lead generator. We started it because engineering leaders had nowhere to talk about the craft of running engineering. That trust turned out to be our moat. When a category gets a Magic Quadrant, every buyer suddenly has a shortlist. Our edge is that many of the people on the buying side have listened to the show for years, used our free tools, or borrowed our benchmarks for their own planning.

Today the two motions feed each other. Community and free tools create familiarity, often with a director or a staff engineer. Benchmarks and research give the champion something credible to take upstairs. Enterprise sales then turns that into an organization-wide rollout with security review, procurement and an ROI case.

Our largest deals close through our enterprise sales team, and when we trace them back, someone inside almost always knew LinearB before the first sales call. That's the compounding effect we're after.

Ishan Pandey: Your effort skill sends simple changes through cheaper agent runs while giving risky changes more care and more tokens. How do you price a product whose value is partly measured in tokens saved and what will the next skills for review and merge add for customers?

Ori Keren: First, a clarification. The effort skill isn't a cost-cutting tool that happens to touch quality. It's a judgment tool. It looks at a change's complexity and risk, using the full knowledge graph, and decides how hard the agent should work. Sometimes that means fewer tokens. On a fragile payments service, it means more, with careful, tested edits. The goal is the right effort on each change.

That's why we don't price on tokens saved. If we took a cut of savings, we'd be rewarded for making agents cheap rather than for making delivery better. Customers judge us on the cost of each delivered change, not the raw token bill.

The next skills go where the bottleneck now sits, in review and merge. They'll use the same context to help teams decide where human attention is needed and what can move faster. After that comes governance of AI-written code and spend optimization.

Ishan Pandey: Gartner sizes the developer productivity insight market at about $400 million while enterprises already spend billions on AI coding tools. How large can this category become once it measures and guides agents as well as people over the next three years?

Ori Keren: The $400 million figure describes the developer productivity insight category, platforms that give engineering leaders visibility into how their teams deliver software. We've always operated in that category, and we've also always gone beyond it. Our philosophy has been to explore, measure, act, and the act part now shows up in our agent skills.

Once a company spends that much on agents, a layer that decides how those agents should work, where they should spend effort and whether their output is safe to ship isn't a reporting tool anymore. It's part of the delivery system, like CI/CD or observability. So the right comparison isn't to other dashboards. It's a share of total spend on software delivery, human and agent combined.

What I'm confident about is the direction. We think it's time for the category to stop being something leaders look at once a month and become something agents consult on every change.

Ishan Pandey: For CTOs and VPs of engineering whose boards will ask them this year to prove AI's return, what is the first thing you would tell them to measure and which older metric should they stop reporting?

Ori Keren: Measure the cost per effective delivered change, not code generated, pull requests opened or lines accepted from an assistant. An effective change is one that merged, shipped and didn't come back as rework. Take what you spend on people and tokens, and divide it by those changes. You want effective changes trending up and the cost of each one trending down. That's the number that tells a board whether AI is paying off, and it holds up when finance pushes on it.

Emburse tracks exactly this. Over seven months, most of its code moved to AI, and its cost per effective pull request fell about 35%.

Right next to it, track what each model costs against the quality of the code it produces. The differences are bigger than most teams expect. Kraken found that two engineers can ship the same work while one burns thousands of dollars in tokens and the other spends hundreds, depending on which models they use and how.

Then stop reporting raw throughput, like pull requests or commits per developer, and above all, stop reporting AI adoption rates. Telling a board that 90% of your engineers use AI says nothing about return.

Inside engineering, though, keep measuring how work flows through your software factory. The bottlenecks haven't gone away. They've moved to review and release.

For CTOs and VPs of engineering heading into a board meeting, the practical takeaway is his metric. Cost per effective delivered change puts people and token spend over work that merged, shipped and stayed fixed, which gives a CFO a number to test. With AI coding spend compounding and the measurement category growing more than 40% a year, I expect the teams that adopt this kind of accounting early to have the easiest budget conversations of 2027. Leaders who want to compare their own teams can start with LinearB's free benchmarks report or the LinearB platform.

WoW: Forever Exploit Lands Streamers In Hot Water After Farming Thousands Of Gold

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

A lucrative World of Warcraft: Forever dungeon-farming exploit that netted some players hundreds of gold per hour has been hotfixed, and Blizzard is taking action against accounts that (temporarily) walked away with thousands in loot.

For the dedicated few who have already reached max level in the new version of Blizzard's MMORPG, a new endgame dungeon, Blackmaw Hold, proved to be quite lucrative. In the dungeon, a room with 30 non-elite enemies proved to be the perfect spot for area-of-effect gold farming, since it's easy to access.

As detailed by Wowhead, the farm was as simple as running to the room in question, killing all the mobs, looting them, resetting the dungeon, and repeating. Not only did the enemies drop anywhere from 25 to 50 silver each, but they also dropped items that could be sold either to a vendor or on the auction house for even more profit.

And some players, including prominent streamers, did just that during Forever's second weekend. One streamer, Ziqo, who was previously in hot water for accepting illegal gold during the game's beta, revealed he made 4,000 gold from the farm. On stream, Ziqo named other streamers who did the same and said his only regret was choosing to sleep for four hours instead of farming longer.

On places like the WoW: Forever subreddit, players worried about the effect such a huge amount of questionably obtained gold would have on the MMO's economy, especially since the game has been out for less than two weeks. Many called on Blizzard to take action against accounts, including streamers, who benefited from the farm.

Blizzard issued a hotfix on November 16, making the enemies from Blackmaw Hold no longer drop loot. In a forum post the following day, Blizzard explained what went wrong and why it was taking action. While Blizzard said finding good farming spots is part of old-school WoW, the enemies players were farming in Blackmaw Hold were incorrectly dropping loot that would normally be reserved for elites, an obvious mistake.

Players who ran the dungeon a few times and "benefited organically from the extra loot did nothing wrong" and don't need to worry, Blizzard said. However, a few "hundred players worldwide" spent "significant time exclusively farming that group of enemies."

Those players are now in more serious trouble. Blizzard locked their ability to engage in Forever's wider economy following the exploit, and said it is removing gold they earned through "exploitative gains." Additionally, "significant abusers" of the exploit will be handed account suspensions.

"As always, we advise all players to refrain from abusing game mechanics that are clearly unintended," Blizzard said. "We will continue to take action against intentional abuse."

The Blackmaw Hold farm wasn't the first gold controversy in Forever, and it certainly won't be the last. During the game's beta, streamers were caught accepting suspicious amounts of gold and encouraging their communities to illegally buy gold on their behalf. As a result, Blizzard issued suspensions, made in-game changes, and made it clear it would crack down not only on accounts selling illegal gold, but also on those buying or accepting it.

Farming exploits aside, Forever's launch has been relatively smooth, though Blizzard can't seem to decide how much experience players should get in dungeons. Despite Forever's launch breaking WoW concurrent-player records, it still wasn't enough to get the MMO nominated for Best Ongoing Game at The Game Awards 2026.
