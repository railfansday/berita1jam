Netflix's New Firefly Replacement Series Can Be Binged In Less Than 4 Hours

📅 Nov 15, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

While it has been a long time since Firefly graced the airwaves, Netflix’s new space western is a four-hour binge that offers an ideal alternative to the iconic series. There are few one-season TV shows that have quite as long a half life as the short-lived space western Firefly. Firefly’s 14-episode run was met with mixed reviews and underwhelming ratings, but that wasn’t the end of the space opera’s story.

Firefly’s superb DVD sales, as well as the outspoken fan community online, prompted a movie spinoff in the form of 2005’s Serenity. Even though Serenity wasn’t a box office hit, the movie was beloved by critics, and a massive array of subsequent sci-fi shows acknowledged Firefly as a formative influence. There is SyFy’s space opera Killjoys, which often feels like the show Firefly could have been, as well as 2015’s Dark Matter, which was a love letter to Firefly in tone and premise alike.

In 2016, Jonathan Nolan’s Westworld reboot took Firefly’s mix of a futuristic sci-fi setting and traditional western elements and turned these same ingredients into a darker, more austere dystopian mystery box series. More recently, Netflix’s latest high-profile show has blended western and sci-fi tropes in a fresh, original story that has more than a touch of Firefly to it. An animated show created by LeSean Thomas, Bass X Machina takes place in a steampunk wild west where supernatural threats, giant robots, and gunslinging cowboys vie for dominance.

Within this pitiless milieu, Brian Tyree Henry voices the titular Bass Reeves, a bounty hunter and Civil War veteran. Joining Bass is Tati Gabrille’s Dana, a teenage engineer searching for her uncle who has disappeared, and Cree Summer’s Ahni, a magical mystic. With a superb ensemble voice cast including Wayne Knight and Janelle Monáe, Bass X Machina has no shortage of star power.

However, much like Apple TV’s Murderbot, this sci-fi series isn’t reliant on its impressive cast alone to keep viewers engaged. With epic, immersive battles, constant twists, and a wild mix of genres at its core, Bass X Machina is a truly fresh take on the real-life story of Bass Reeves that is unpredictable, inventive, and brimming with the unlikely fusion of sci-fi and western plot elements that made Firefly so memorable all those years ago.

On the big screen, attempts to mix sci-fi and westerns have often fallen flat. One need only look at expensive misfires like 2011’s Cowboys & Aliens and 1999’s Wild Wild West to know that, for whatever reason, the pairing doesn’t seem to attract interest from audiences at the box office. Moreover, even the kindest critics would struggle to call these movies overlooked classics.

Westerns and sci-fi don’t seem to mix at a practical level when it comes to feature films, at least not on a blockbuster scale. In contrast, Bass X Machina proves that Netflix’s animated series could take the same character who was the subject of a po-faced Taylor Sheridan Western series, Lawman: Bass Reeves, and have him fighting killer machines alongside paranormal mystics in a twisted reinvention of the Wild West.

With stunning imagery that calls to mind Netflix’s earlier genre blender Blue Eye Samurai, Bass X Machina admittedly makes a pretty dramatic visual contrast with Firefly’s deliberately low-tech vision of the far future. However, fans of Firefly’s fast-paced action, sharp dialogue, themes of found family, and truly original mix of the two genres will find plenty to love here.

Only time will tell if Bass X Machina will receive a second season, or if the show will suffer the same ignominious fate as Firefly as well as sharing its atypical mix of genres. However, for now, Netflix’s Bass X Machina is an ideal substitute for any Firefly fans who still miss the influential show.

Paul Bettany, who reprised his role as Vision and executive produced the MCU series on Disney+, noted that he and Kevin Feige ultimately agreed the show was worth letting it “stand on its own” as opposed to distracting audiences with another big character.

“There were questions about it, whether that should happen, whether it opened up too much for the MCU, whether they wanted to do it later. I really don’t think you should distract from that by giving the audience that at the end of the [show]. It will just fundamentally all become about WandaVision again, and this should and can stand on its own.’ And I think he was right.” Watch on Deadline

Although Wanda doesn’t reunite with Vision or their sons in the series, the post-credits scene showed what appeared to be Doctor Doom reviving her from the rubble of Wundagore following the events of Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness (2022).

With the scene appearing to hint at her presence in Avengers: Doomsday, Bettany admitted that he “would love” to reunite with Olsen for another series in their continuity, following WandaVision (2021), Agatha All Along (2024) and VisionQuest.

“Gosh, I would love for that to be. I don’t know,” he said. “I mean, at the moment, it doesn’t feel that way, but I’ve died so many times in this show, so you never actually know.”

After Vision was reunited with both their sons Tommy (Ruaridh Mollica) and Billy (Joe Locke) in the VisionQuest finale, it’s possible we’ll see a family reunion with Wanda in Avengers: Doomsday, premiering Dec. 18 in theaters.

XRP to $1.3? Kalshi Traders Make Weak November Bets

📅 2026-11-15  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

XRP has continued to fall to levels not seen in the last three months as prolonged market volatility continues to intensify, causing the asset to post bigger daily price losses.

While momentum appears to be fading rapidly, the slow price movements have caused expectations for a November recovery to weaken, especially among prediction market traders.

Latest data from leading prediction market Kalshi shows that XRP traders have little confidence that the asset can still reclaim $1.30 before the end of the month.

While the asset saw higher expectations from Kalshi traders last month, it appears that its recent price dip below $1 has lowered their expectations as traders are assigning low probabilities to XRP reaching higher price targets this month.

Following their predictions, XRP currently has 7% odds of trading above $1.40, while the probability of reaching $1.50 stands at 8%.

More notably, bets on XRP surging to around $1.60 are even weaker, with only a 4% implied probability, suggesting that traders have increasingly lost confidence in XRP’s potential to achieve a major recovery before this month runs out.

The weak XRP predictions from Kalshi traders have come as XRP struggles to maintain momentum around its recent price levels, falling substantially below its local highs.

As of the time of writing, XRP is trading around $0.9892, representing a substantial decline of about 3.94% over the past 24 hours and an 11.3% drop over the last week.

It is important to note that the Kalshi data only reflects traders' positioning amid current market conditions and therefore does not guarantee XRP’s precise price movement in the future.

While XRP has continued to struggle below the major $1 level, it would need to stage a major recovery to reclaim the $1.30 level.

Calvin and Hobbes’ 5 Greatest Spaceman Spiff Comic Strips

📅 Nov 15, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Calvin and Hobbes wasn't Bill Watterson's first pitch for a comic strip. C&H's protagonists began as side characters in Watterson's comic The Doghouse before his original syndicate suggested that they ought to be the focus. "Marvin" became "Calvin," the rest of The Doghouse's cast was dropped, and while United Feature Syndicate still didn't take the resulting comic, Universal Press Syndicate did.

Before then, Watterson had drawn cartoons about a spacefaring character as early as college. Watterson went on to make that character the focus of one of his rejected comics: a mustachioed, cigar-smoking parody of adventurers like Flash Gordon. Shortly after Calvin and Hobbes began, Watterson reworked "Mort" into Calvin's fantasy alter ego Spaceman Spiff.

Over the course of Calvin and Hobbes' run, Spiff was one of Calvin's most frequent escapist characters. Whenever Calvin was caught in a boring or unpleasant situation, he frequently retreated into his Spiff persona to pass time or even make a hasty retreat. In practice, that gave Watterson a handy excuse to take Calvin and Hobbes' art well outside its usual mundane setting, with tons of gross aliens, impossible landscapes, and great phrases like "eat hot electric death" that don't usually come up in everyday conversation.

Much like the rest of Calvin and Hobbes, Spaceman Spiff really hit his stride in the comic's last three years, when Watterson began doing his trademark "unbreakable" Sunday strips. Some of the most colorful and evocative C&H comics are Spaceman Spiff adventures from the mid-'90s, but Spiff made a few solid appearances before that point. Here are some of our picks for the best Spaceman Spiff comics in Calvin and Hobbes.

When Calvin gets sick during his summer vacation, his first reaction is to get annoyed. Sick days are for the school year. I think we all can relate.

Unfortunately for all involved, Calvin's mom takes him to the doctor. That includes a checkup, an injection, and a retreat into Calvin's Spiff mode, as he imagines himself as a prisoner of aliens rather than a little kid trapped in an examination room.

Calvin's teacher Mrs. Wormwood catches a lot of strays in Spaceman Spiff cartoons. Sometimes I want to see a version of C&H from her perspective. Most of her class are apparently boring normal kids who do the reading and don't get in unusual amounts of trouble. Then there's Calvin, who's as much of a chaos agent as a six-year-old can be. Mrs. Wormwood and Mr. Wilson from Dennis the Menace would have a lot to talk about.

In this early installation, Calvin gets called on in class and seamlessly incorporates Mrs. Wormwood's history question into his daydream. It's not being called on in class; it's being interrogated by the Hideous Zorgs of Planet X-13. (Look at those bug-men. Those are aliens that deserve the word "Hideous" being part of their proper noun.)

The most extreme (in the '90s sense) Spaceman Spiff adventure was a week-long story where Calvin got deeply enough into character that he staged a breakout from elementary school. Called to the board to answer a math problem, Calvin instead decides to shoot his way out (with a rubber band), then run for the door.

Through what one assumes is the relative indifference of school staff, Calvin manages to make it all the way home before his mom catches him. Unfortunately, Spiff can't get Calvin out of the ensuing consequences.

Right from the start, Bill Watterson has said that one of his primary influences on Calvin & Hobbes is George Herriman's Golden Age newspaper comic Krazy Kat, which ran from 1913 to 1944. That was already some inside baseball in the 1980s when Calvin and Hobbes was new, as Krazy Kat’s modern following is almost exclusively made up of historians, fanatics, and other cartoonists. It’s not dead media yet, though, as historians and preservationists have made a point of keeping collections of Krazy Kat in print. Curious readers can check out Fantagraphics' George Herriman Library hardcover collections, which are still in print at time of writing.

If you check out Krazy Kat, its influence on Calvin and Hobbes is obvious immediately, particularly in its colored Sunday comics. Herriman's characters inhabited a cartoonish version of the Arizona desert, and many of Watterson's Spaceman Spiff strips feature him visiting alien planets with similar arid backgrounds. This largely-wordless strip from 1993 is a perfect example, with so much of it devoted to Calvin's imagined spaceship journey from an alien valley to home.

One of the best Spaceman Spiff strips comes in the last year of Calvin and Hobbes. Without dialogue, we see the entire comic from Calvin's perspective, as he's entertaining himself during yet another boring class by imagining himself behind the stick of Spiff's flying saucer.

Suddenly, Mrs. Wormwood interrupts his daydream and breaks his train of thought. As soon as she isn't paying attention anymore, Calvin switches to a new scenario: this time, he's a dinosaur stalking prey.

There's a hidden way you can check which services are running on your Android phone

📅 Nov 15, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Just like a desktop PC, your Android phone has a built-in task manager. Here is how to find it, monitor your memory and safely limit background apps.

If you use a desktop PC, you may be familiar with the task manager, which lets you view all the processes running in the background of your computer and taking up resources. What you may not know is that Android phones (even budget models) have the same feature, but it's kept hidden away from users, requiring a little digging to find it. If you want to keep an eye on what's using your phone's memory, it can be worth uncovering this hidden display, but you should proceed with caution before you start closing processes willy-nilly.

To check what processes are running, you first need to activate developer mode to make your phone faster. Under the Settings menu, scroll down to the bottom, tap About Phone and look for the Build number. If it's not there, you may have to enter an additional Software information menu, depending on your phone's model and Android version. Once you find it, keep tapping the Build number option until you get a message that developer mode has turned on or a dialog box asking you to confirm. Then, back out to the main Settings menu. You'll find Developer options on this main screen, or under a System submenu. Tap the option, then open Running Services. You can also select the three dots in the top right of the screen to select Show cached processes and see what's been recently suspended but still held in memory.

This is Android's version of the task manager, showing you all the running services currently providing frameworks for the apps on your phone, along with how much memory they're using. But don't start closing processes to free up that precious memory. While the task manager on PC often shows plenty of bloat that you can safely close, that's not usually the case for Android phones. The processes you see here are more likely managing features like Bluetooth, your keyboard and location settings. Shutting these down can seriously hamper your phone.

Both the Running Services and Memory menus are more like diagnostic tools. If you're having problems with your phone, it can be helpful to check here and see if anything seems wildly out of order, like a single process hogging the majority of your phone's memory. You may want to stop the related process until you can fix the underlying issue, but be prepared to restart your phone if anything goes wrong.

A better way of controlling background processes is by limiting their use. In the Developer options menu, under the Apps heading, you'll find Background process limit. This lets you limit the number of apps allowed to run in the background at one time. This default setting triggers an automatic limit based on how much RAM your phone has, which is a crucial factor in making older Android phones last longer. However, you can also change the limit to between one and four to free up memory.

Keep in mind that this will make multitasking harder and will cause a bigger performance hit at the moment you re-open apps, since your phone will have to fully restart them now.

You can also limit how individual apps behave in the background. In the Settings menu, head to Apps, select a specific app and tap Battery. The default Optimized option automatically adjusts the app's background permissions based on how you use it, but switching to Restricted limits its activity when suspended.

Jurgen Klopp's Germany are pushing to qualify for the Nations League quarter-finals for just the second time, but they face a tough test to reach the last eight.

Die Mannschaft are currently third in Group A2, level on eight points with Greece and three behind the Netherlands, and their chance to go through leans on the fact UEFA prioritises head-to-head points between tied teams.

Klopp's side were beaten 1-0 by the Azure and White on September 27 before drawing 0-0 with them on matchday four.

However, Ivan Jovanovic's men were hammered 7-0 by the Dutch on Friday, meaning that a German victory over the Netherlands on Monday would see them progress ahead of Greece regardless of their result against Serbia.

Die Mannschaft were held to a 1-1 stalemate by Oranje on matchday one, with Cody Gakpo equalising in second-half stoppage time to deny his former Liverpool manager maximum points.

However, Klopp's team have remained undefeated in the group since their loss to Greece, most recently beating Serbia 3-1 on Friday, when they raced into a 2-0 lead within the first 14 minutes thanks to an own-goal from Srdjan Babic and a follow-up by Florian Wirtz.

Meanwhile, Xavi's Netherlands have yet to taste defeat in the Nations League this term, and are in a commanding position at the top of their group.

Top of the pile with 11 points, Oranje's 7-0 demolition of Greece last time out - following a 2-2 draw on October 1 - means the only possibility of them finishing below the top two is if there is a dramatic 14-goal swing in the Azure and White's favour.

On the other hand, Oranje are unbeaten in their last seven matches on the road, last suffered a penalty shootout defeat against Spain in March 2025.

Germany have no new injury issues to contend with, though they are missing Bayern Munich stars Jamal Musiala and Serge Gnabry due to respective muscle problems.

In their absence, Klopp could continue with Brajan Gruda, Florian Wirtz and Derry Scherhant behind Arsenal man Kai Havertz up top.

As for the Netherlands, they were forced to take off Frenkie De Jong at half time against Greece after he suffered back pain, and he has now left the national squad to return to Barcelona.

Teun Koopmeiners looks set to step in and fill the gap alongside Joey Veerman at the base of Xavi's midfield, while Tijani Reijnders operates in the number 10 role.

Further forward, Donyell Malen has been a standout for Roma this season with 12 goals across all competitions, and he should lead the line on Monday while Cody Gakpo and Crysencio Summerville provide width.

Captain Virgil van Dijk has sat out this international break with complaints of physical discomfort, so expect to see Tottenham Hotspur's Micky van de Ven and Jan Paul van Hecker start at centre-back.
