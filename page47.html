Every Track From ‘Grand Theft Auto VI: The Album’ Ranked — Now Including the Deluxe Bonus Tracks

📅 Nov 23, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

GTA's accompanying soundtrack arrived 24 hours after the long-anticipated Rockstar video game — and they didn't waste time releasing the deluxe edition days later.

Monocultural moments in society have become extremely rare in the mid-2020s, but the arrival of Grand Theft Auto VI has brought about as strong a unified pop-culture tidal wave as we’ve seen this decade.

Rockstar Games released the Grand Theft Auto VI: The Album on Friday (Nov. 20) through Atlantic Records as an accompanying soundtrack to the long-awaited video game, which arrived 24 hours earlier.

Rockstar didn’t spare any expense when piecing together the 34-track album, which features Playboi Carti, Dua Lipa, Cardi B, Lana Del Rey, Gunna, Don Toliver, Kodak Black, Yung Lean, Future, Metro Boomin, Travis Scott, Fred again.., PinkPantheress, Morgan Wallen, Tame Impala, Fuerza Regida and more.

GTA VI will look to make even more history with a No. 1 debut on the charts — as no video game soundtrack has topped the Billboard 200 before.

Artist-hosted radio stations dominate the Vice City airwaves, with stations helmed or curated by the likes of Bad Bunny, Lana Del Rey, Morgan Wallen, Trina and Trick Daddy, PinkPantheress, Burna Boy and Robyn.

The Grand Theft Auto VI festivities will continue into the weekend, with a music festival at Miami’s Mana Wynwood on Nov. 21. The festival is set to have Cardi B, Travis Scott, Rauw Alejandro and more hit the stage, including a Cactus Jack ensemble featuring Scott, Don Toliver, SoFaygo and a cameo from Florida’s own Kodak Black.

After GTA hit pop culture with a tsunami takeover, Rockstar Games and Atlantic Records continued to flood the marketplace with a 23-track deluxe edition of the album, which arrived on Monday (Nov. 23).

Take a break from perusing Leonida with Jason and Lucia and find all of the tracks from the Grand Theft Auto: The Album along with the new deluxe edition (sans the five Overmono songs that feel more like a score) ranked in order below.

Who knew Omar Apollo had a funky little party-starter like this up his sleeve? Drawing on the iconic Stalag riddim immortalized by Sister Nancy’s “Bam Bam,” “No Me Busques” bursts with a wailing chorus with an infectious call-and-response hook. Oh, and that festive saxophone riff. Lyrically, there’s not much to it beyond Apollo’s repeated insistence that someone stop looking for him. The groove does most of the talking. — ISABELA RAYGOZA

This track is a little different from what I’m used to, but I like it. It plays during the opening credits of GTA VI and serves as the opening track on the game’s album. Sonically, it has a heavy ’80s-inspired synth pulse, electro-disco drums and electric piano that make me feel like it could fit perfectly into any season of Stranger Things.

It’s the perfect way to open the game’s soundtrack. The song is about starting from zero and hustling your way toward success, which feels right at home in the world of GTA. It sets the tone for the kind of journey players are about to embark on. — CHRISTOPHER CLAXTON

Sak pase? Kodak Black teams up with DJ Michael Brun and the duo connects with their Haitian roots, as Yak mixes in Haitian Creole while playfully roasting someone’s baldy haircut and rapping about flipping cocaine. — M.S.

GTA is supposed to have something for everyone, and the metalheads make their presence felt with the thrash aggression of Power Trip’s “Mutual Death Warrant.” I’m ready to either run through a wall or fire up Guitar Hero after pressing play thanks to the Dallas band. — M.S.

The Dare’s synth-heavy brand of dance-punk has invaded the mainstream thanks to pop stars like Charli xcx and ADÉLA leaning into and elevating the subgenre. He serves up the robotic “Interesting” here, which will spin on the EDM radio stations around Leonida and get The Dare a set at Space replica The Vault in Vice City. — M.S.

This is an interesting track and an unapologetically raunchy celebration of sexual confidence, which is exactly the kind of energy we can expect from Sexyy Red. Real Dimez and Sexyy lean into their provocative personalities for a playful record that doesn’t take itself too seriously. Even the title “Cooties” plays on the childhood idea that girls have cooties and that boys stay away from them because they’re afraid of infection. Now, the tables have turned and these ladies are having fun with the concept. — C.C.

A dance-house track that makes it clear this duo doesn’t play around — and that anyone can catch a beatdown. — C.C.

Primal percussion, glitchy electronics and unbridled sexual energy collide on Arca’s self-produced “Sandunguea.” The Venezuelan experimentalist delivers a delirious invitation to dance and indulge, peppering her verses with colorful slang from her homeland. Against abrasive beats, her vocals switch between flirtation and feverish demands, breathy moans and fragmented chants. “Nuestra pasarela es la calle” (the street is our runway), she declares. In true Arca fashion, the dance floor becomes a space for uninhibited self-expression, where pleasure and chaos go hand in hand. — I.R.

“All Again” is about missing someone after a relationship has ended, and wondering whether the love, memories and heartbreak were worth it in the end. It captures that feeling of looking back on a relationship and questioning whether you’d put yourself through it all over again. I’m not sure I would. Would you? — C.C.

Rina Sawayama made waves with her assist on Charli xcx’s “Beg for You” in 2022, and the Japanese pop artist/model is back in the mainstream with the brooding “TRDWFE.” I’m going to be hearing Sawayama asking us to “submit” repeatedly when my head hits the pillow tonight — although “TRDWFE” sounds like it was tailor-made more for the likes of HBO’s The Idol than the GTA vibe. — MICHAEL SAPONARA

With Travis Scott all over Grand Theft Auto‘s rollout, it’s only right he reserved a slot for one of Cactus Jack’s offspring. Sofaygo makes the most of his appearance on “Cashout,” a syrupy melodic blend that SahBabii would’ve been a perfect running mate for. — M.S.

It’s clear that 21 Savage will stand ten toes down on his reputation, street credibility and distrust of his opps. Throughout the track, he draws a line between people who talk about violence and those who are actually willing to stand on business. Savage doesn’t want listeners to forget that actions speak louder than words, and if you’re not really about it, he has no time to waste on you. Don’t forget, Twitter fingers will get you nowhere. As he puts it, “We get up and go at all the smoke, you n—as go live.” — C.C.

Metro is back on production for this one. It’s a quick track about flexing, so you already know the deal: money, cars, clothes and relationships. Thug keeps it short and straight to the point, delivering the kind of flex-heavy record that fits right into the GTA lifestyle. Just remember, anything born out of a criminal lifestyle comes with real consequences. — C.C.

“You Move Me” was made for a top-down cruise through Leonida. NEIL FRANCES’ blend of disco and EDM gives a callback to 1980s Vice City, but something that still feels like it could exist in the modern dance world. — M.S.

Young Nudy lives at the intersection of gaming and rap, so Nudy making an appearance on the soundtrack shouldn’t come as much of a surprise. He serves up the sinister “Don Da Da,” which finds him as a kingpin calling shots, while the track could’ve been an offshoot that didn’t make the cut from his Just Doin Me project. — M.S.

Fcukers is back. The NYC duo’s first release since Ö comes with the house-leaning “Only One” and they’re here to start the party and blow out your speakers. If there’s a rave somewhere in Leonida, Fcukers is headlining. Can we get Tinashe to sprinkle her Popstar magic on a remix? Vocalist Shanny Wise even moonlights as a co-host of GTA Radio’s Stockyard FM alongside PinkPantheress and The Dare in the video game. — M.S.

All the baddies to the front, please! “GMA” stands for “Get Money Anthem,” because if you can’t make money in GTA, what the hell can you do? Hope y’all caught that reference. This is an upbeat party anthem celebrating women who make their own money, no matter how they earn it. JT isn’t knocking anybody’s hustle, and at this point, neither am I. Whether you’re stacking cash in the game or in real life, the message is to stay focused on your goals and tune out the criticism. Shoutout to all the ladies working traditional 9-to-5s, earning college degrees, dancing or hustling on the side. We see you! — C.C.

Keith Richards puts his own spin on Jimmy Reed’s 1961 blues classic “Bright Lights, Big City” for Grand Theft Auto VI: The Album. His version takes a different approach from Reed’s original, but Keith sounds right at home here. It’s a fresh take on a blues classic, and one that fits perfectly into the world of GTA. — C.C.

Fame is a hell of a drug, but Denzel Curry and 454 haven’t forgotten where they came from. “FLA Flow” is a tribute to Florida’s music and hip-hop culture, with both artists reflecting on the street history and neighborhoods that shaped them. — C.C.

With GTA returning to Vice City, having Kodak Black involved was a must. He’s even a character playing himself in the video game’s campaign. A Yak and Don Toliver collaboration seems like an odd pairing on its face — then you mix in the chaotic skittering production, and both the artists and listeners are playing catch-up for the duration of “Let’s Rock.” — M.S.

Opium’s HXG and Tana invite Quavo to their rage rap world with “RCKSTRL!VING” and Huncho makes it work with his trap-laden rhymes mixing in with the grungy soundscape. The rapper-rockstar lifestyle plays an integral role in GTA VI‘s chapter three when the scene switches to downtown Vice City. — M.S.

Offset will always be unapologetic, and “I Just Came To Pop It” is no different. Wealth, status, street credibility and a refusal to back down from anyone are all on display. Offset brags about the lifestyle he’s built, from luxury cars to expensive jewelry, while reflecting on his past in the trap and the life he gets to live today. The contrast between his street beginnings and his multimillion-dollar lifestyle is a reminder of just how far he’s come. You earned it, so pop your s—t, Offset! — C.C.

Tee Grizzley is not one to mess with. “IKYFL” is an unapologetic statement about loyalty, street credibility, success and refusing to let anyone question how far he’s come. The track captures Tee’s mentality of protecting his circle, enjoying the rewards of his success and staying ready for anyone who tries to challenge him. This is one of those tracks that makes you feel untouchable when you’re cruising through Vice City. — C.C.

“Got Dat” debuted on Vice City Rap Radio in GTA VI, and lands on streaming services with the album’s deluxe. Pooh Shiesty’s menacing trap-laden rhymes are a perfect fit for GTA, but, unfortunately, Pooh living out that outlaw lifestyle has landed him behind bars for the foreseeable future. — M.S.

Morgan Wallen colors outside the lines of country while leaning on his pop and R&B influences for “Last Thing You Need.” It’s a smooth, laid-back track for fans to throw on for a late-night cruise through the swamp lands of GTA‘s Leonida. — M.S.

Just when you think you’ve settled into the sleek reggaetón groove of “X,” JHAYCO throws a dance-floor curveball. Co-produced by the Puerto Rican star alongside Tainy and COZY, the track finds Jhay assuring a love interest that he’s nothing like her ex, dishing out a stream of suggestive compliments. But the real thrill comes during the bridge, when bright synths and a pulsating electronic beat give way to a euphoric break before the dembow kicks back in. It’s a catchy little club banger that doubles as a reminder of Jhay’s knack for melding reggaetón with EDM. — I.R.

Of course, the GTA team got the legend Robert Glasper to help close out the album! Bringing together bassist Stanley Clarke, drummer Yussef Dayes and pianist Robert Glasper, this track is straight vibes. No lyrics needed, just let the musicians do their thing. Named after Miami’s historic Overtown neighborhood, the track connects the game’s Vice City map to the real-world roots of Miami. A smooth way to bring the album home. — C.C.

Grand Theft Auto VI: The Album wouldn’t be complete without an Afro-fusion track like this. I love the simple, chant-like hook. This might become my new go-to morning affirmation before heading to work or starting a session in GTA. It’s an uplifting song about resilience, bouncing back from setbacks and continuing to reach for greater heights no matter who or what tries to hold you back.

This is the perfect song to play after a mission doesn’t go as planned. Burna is reminding us that no matter the setback or how people treat you, you’re still unstoppable. Nobody is better than you, so keep pushing and get “bigger, bigger, bigger, bigger, hey!” — C.C.

This is baby-making music. Dâm-Funk and The Time’s Jesse Johnson supply the groovy, funky vibes that have been a staple of the GTA series for over two decades. I’m glad the soundtrack composers made room for them here, because Gen Z isn’t going to know what hit them when a new generation of gamers presses play. — M.S.

Grief, trauma and the emotional exhaustion of being unable to escape memories of someone you’ve lost are at the center of HEALTH’s “NIGHTMARE.” The song uses the idea of a nightmare as a metaphor for being trapped in an endless cycle of emotional pain, where the past continues to haunt you. However, by the second half of the track, HEALTH begins shifting toward the idea of waking up, moving forward and learning to separate the past from the present. — C.C.

Tove Lo wasn’t a name I expected to see on the tracklist for GTA VI, but she makes it work and shuts down any detractors who didn’t think she’d belong. “Blood in Your Eyes” is the blaring experimental pop that should be pumping the dance floor at Leonida’s version of the Space and E11EVEN nightclubs in the game. — M.S.

JENNIE is taking another “Power Trip” with this dance-pop track. In JENNIE’s city, there are no limits. This is a celebration of confidence, status and the freedom to do whatever you want without worrying about who approves. She embraces a larger-than-life persona, putting herself at the center of attention while making it clear that she knows her worth. But there’s nothing wrong with a little self-love, so is this really a power trip? — C.C.

If you see Future and Metro Boomin on a track, nine times out of 10, it’s going to be a banger, and this song proves just that. Shoutout to Yung Lean, because I like the track, but I’m not entirely sure if his presence fits. We have Metro handling the production, of course, and Future’s trap-influenced sound just works perfectly over Metro’s production. The two have such a natural chemistry, and this track is another example of why their collaborations rarely miss.– C.C.

Zara Larsson is chasing carefree desire with “Fast Emotion,” and she has no shame about it. She just wants you to “make it reckless, leave [her] breathless, make [her] heart race.” This track is bright, upbeat and full of energy, making it perfect for cruising down Vice City. “Fast Emotion” is all about craving an intense, spontaneous connection and allowing yourself to get swept up in the excitement of the moment. Through imagery of summer nights, physical attraction and speed, Zara captures a romance that feels exhilarating. — C.C.

Outside of Drake, no rapper made more of 2026 than Don Toliver. “TF?” provides the gloomy soundscape where Toliver is at home, compared to “Let’s Rock.” The raunchy tune could’ve been left on the cutting room floor during his OCTANE sessions, but is a worthy addition to the GTA album. — M.S.

A Cherokee loaded with contraband, police sirens blaring and a desperate escape through the city — “Macacoa 2000” opens like a scene straight out of Grand Theft Auto. Over hard-hitting reggaetón produced by Dímelo Ninow, reminiscent of the genre’s gritty 2000s heyday, Rauw Alejandro narrates a chaotic cop chase in a rapid-fire flow. Near the end, the track switches gears into sultrier territory, with the silky R&B vocals that marked his breakthrough. But beneath the bravado is a deeper reverence for reggaetón’s roots. At the beginning, he salutes the genre’s pioneers and reflects on his own longevity: “Se trata de trascender en la música” (It’s about transcending through music). — I.R.

Lana Del Rey shines as Delta Dawne in her role as a co-host of the rebellious Back Country Radio station in GTA. It’s only right she also delivered on the soundtrack, as LDR revels in a tragic romance for her “Crazy for You” ballad. Enter a Jason and Lucia romance scene from the campaign, with Lana providing the accompaniment. — M.S.

“50 Bands” is a braggadocious celebration of money, luxury, sexual confidence and unapologetic competition. The track is packed with personality as Latto and Flo Milli trade witty punchlines back and forth. Both ladies are flexing their looks, money and influence while taking shots at anyone they feel can’t keep up. When Latto and Flo Milli get together, “throw somе racks on it, make it plural.” — C.C.

Fuerza Regida trades corridos for a breezy reggaetón groove on “Suzuki,” a flirtatious, raunchy cut that finds the San Bernardino hitmakers venturing into new territory. Produced by Synthetic, the track glides on glossy electronic textures and a laid-back dembow bounce, while frontman Jesús Ortiz Paz delivers a string of shameless come-ons. From pole dancing and risqué fantasies to a cheeky reference to tusi (a.k.a. pink cocaine), the lyrics leave little to the imagination. The beat may be easygoing, but JOP clearly has no intention of behaving himself. — I.R.

“Aprovecha que tienes mi atención!” Anitta brings “Touch & Go” to life with this English- and Spanish-language version of “Vai Dar Caô,” originally featuring Ebony and Papatinho. Unfortunately, this version is missing those features — but Anitta still delivers the energy when it comes to casual attraction, sexual freedom and knowing how to leave someone wanting more.

On “Touch & Go,” Anitta enjoys the physical chemistry of a relationship, but has no interest in turning it into a deeper emotional commitment. And that sounds like a lot of y’all reading this! You get close, enjoy the moment and move on before the other person starts expecting anything more. A hot, flirtatious atmosphere can be fun for a while, but there’s no shame in wanting something more serious either. — C.C.

If half of Camila Cabello’s heart is in Havana, the other half lies in Miami. The dulcet “Sweet Like Candy” is a solid effort from CC — and like a good piece of candy, it’s not too heavy or sour, as the Fifth Harmony solo star sticks the landing with a fun, surface-level pop song. It also makes me think about where she has been in the last two years, with Cabello largely out of the pop scene since 2024’s C,XOXO. Time for a comeback, Camila. — M.S.

“Everybody getting’ loose!” Produced by Guy-Manuel de Homem-Christo, Travis definitely found his pocket on this one. I can see this track growing in popularity over time, and it’s already one of my favorites on the album. You have to crank the volume all the way up for this one. There’s so much electronic-infused chaos packed into the production, making it feel like hip-hop built for the club. It fits flawlessly into the GTA universe. — C.C.

Gunna turned back the clock to the days long before he was running 5Ks and flexed a six-pack as a gym rat. The Atlanta native reunites with frequent collaborator Turbo on the production side for a hard-hitting trap banger that boasts a flow that will sound familiar to early Gunna fans from the days of his Drip or Drown and Drip Season series, when he was still rising through the rap ranks. — M.S.

For all the tenderness with which Kali Uchis and Helado Negro sing to each other, their message couldn’t be more blunt: lárgate (or “f–k off”). The Colombian American singer and Ecuadorian American musician explore the emotional contradictions of a relationship that’s run its course, alternating between wistful reflections and outright dismissal. The sweetness of their vocal interplay almost belies the bitterness of their words. — I.R.

Cardi B will always pop her s—t, and “Don’t Chart” is no different. From the Bronx all the way to Vice City, Cardi is talking her talk! The track marks her second release of 2026, following the arrival of “AH HA” in July. This is Cardi through and through: she’s “still poppin’, still winnin’, [and] you still hatin’.” The bouncy production is packed with flex after flex, touching on confidence, wealth, chart success and designer labels. Thank you, Cardi, for another boastful tongue-in-cheek anthem. — C.C.

“INTERNET $TAR” is a celebration of internet fame, individuality and the freedom to embrace who you are, even when the world doesn’t fully understand or appreciate you. Slayyyter presents herself as influential, memorable and impossible to replicate — which, if you know her, is exactly the point. — C.C.

I imagine Playboi Carti has thousands of songs in a folder on hard drives that he could lend out when needed. Whether it was Carti himself or right-hand producer F1LTHY, they made the right choice going with “Ahh Yea.” For Carti’s standards, “Ahh Yea” leans more into the poppier side of his artistry, and could easily be seen playing on the GTA radio while gamers cruise around Leonida. “Ahh Yea” seems like a holdover from the MUSIC era, and I wish we got more loosies like this from King Vamp. — M.S.

Argentine duo CA7RIEL & Paco Amoroso bring their irreverence to the dance floor on “Sexy Magic,” a bouncy, seductive electronic collaboration with British pop artist PinkPantheress, producer Fred again.. and French house veteran Étienne de Crécy. Riding a groovy bassline and shimmering synths, the track pairs Paco’s playful Spanish delivery with PinkPantheress’ breathy English vocals. Psychedelic around the edges and funky at its core, “Sexy Magic” sounds tailor-made for a neon-lit night cruising through Vice City. — I.R.

Travis Scott has been part of the Grand Theft Auto VI rollout every step of the way. Between attending events and making Cactus Jack x GTA merchandise collections, nobody’s been more hyped in hip-hop for the return of the iconic video game series than La Flame.

Scott makes a second appearance on the soundtrack with “Burn This S–t Down,” which sounds as if it has been the most creatively inspired he’s been in the last couple of years. It’s giving me that feeling of a hungry Cactus Jack in the mid-2010s, when he was pushing the boundaries of the genre and making his melodic rage part of hip-hop’s mainstream. That’s a good sign of things to come for La Flame, who should be in album mode for 2027.

“Burn” is a three-part rollercoaster, which he’s experimented with in the past (“SICKO MODE”). Scott plays with melody and then pushes the pace to bar up before turning the title into an eerie chant with an expansive sound that could score a film scene. The final portion will sound familiar to GTA VI gamers, as it was debuted during the explosive final mission of chapter one. — M.S.

New York is in the building! That opening line in the first verse, “You look prettier in person,” hits in this social media era. We all know what it’s like to meet someone in person after finding them online and being happy that they actually look like their pictures — or, in this case, look even better. Boogie, you did your thing with this one, my boy. This is about to go platinum in the crib: “I don’t wanna fight, I wanna dance tonight” — a perfect track for a late-night drive through Vice City.— C.C.

Dua Lipa has proven to be the queen of a soundtrack when she dished out the top 10 Hot 100 bop “Dance the Night” for Barbie in 2023. With an intro that seems to draw from Phil Collins’ “Don’t Lose My Number” and later interpolates KISS’ “I Was Made for Lovin’ You,” the pop star has a penchant for taking songs of yesteryear and making them digestible for today’s audiences. “Go Easy” is no different for the hitmaker, as Dua clears the decks and gears up to launch a new era in the first quarter of ’27. — M.S.

“Flower in a Hurricane” is the preeminent song from GTA VI. In a short time, it’s already essentially become the video game’s theme song, providing the soundtrack to key scenes, clips and compilations going viral on social media. Justice meshes its EDM roots with King Gizzard & the Lizard Wizard’s rock while SZA and Kevin Parker join forces to sing about surviving the storm to make it out alive on the other side. It perfectly balances the angst of everything falling apart at any moment on the journey to Vice City glory. — M.S.

Researchers Just Sent an Unblockable Message Through a Solid Wall Using Gravity

📅 Nov 23, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

Researchers have demonstrated an unusual way to transmit information through solid barriers using gravity rather than electromagnetic signals.

Physicists at the University of Queensland have transmitted a message through a brick wall using gravity instead of electromagnetic signals. Their unconventional communication system relied on a moving elevator counterweight and an extremely sensitive instrument capable of detecting tiny changes in the gravitational field.

The experiment took place in UQ’s Parnell Building, where researchers moved a 1.8-metric-ton (about 4,000 pounds) elevator counterweight between two positions representing binary 0 and 1. A gravimeter positioned in a nearby bathroom detected the gravitational changes and decoded a single word: “Gravity.”

Unlike radio waves and other electromagnetic signals, gravitational fields cannot be blocked by ordinary building materials. This means information carried by changes in gravity could potentially reach places where conventional wireless communication struggles.

Dr. Charles Woffinden from UQ’s School of Mathematics and Physics explained the advantage. “Unlike the electromagnetic signals used in modern wireless communication, a gravitational signal cannot simply be screened out by placing ordinary material in its path,” he said. “Anyone who has lost phone reception in a tunnel or struggled with Wi-Fi through a solid wall will understand the appeal.”

“While the present system is far from practical, it demonstrates the possibility of communicating using a method that ordinary physical barriers cannot block.”

The communication experiment grew out of an unrelated effort to measure vehicle weights without physical contact. Dr. Andrew Groszek and his colleagues were developing a system that could estimate the mass of trucks and other heavy vehicles by detecting their gravitational attraction.

“We were aiming to determine the mass of trucks and other large vehicles by measuring their tiny gravitational pull,” Dr. Groszek said. “Once we had shown that the idea worked, we realized the same physics could be used to encode and transmit information.”

The researchers applied that principle to communication by moving a large mass between fixed positions. Each movement altered the gravitational field measured by the detector, allowing the team to encode binary information without transmitting electromagnetic signals.

Although the experiment successfully transmitted information, its data rate was exceptionally low. Professor Andrew White identified the aging elevator as a major constraint.

“Our lift was snail-paced – it’s from the 1950s – so our experiment transmitted just 1 bit per minute, meaning it took an hour to send a single word,” Professor White said.

“The speed of gravity communication would come down to how big the mass is, how fast you can move it, and the sensitivity of the detector.”

“It took more than 100 years to go from the first telegraph system to the mobile phone, so while gravity communication is not practical today, it could be in the future.”

Professor Matthew Davis said the experiment established that information can be deliberately encoded in a gravitational field and recovered by a detector. The achievement demonstrates the underlying physics, although substantial improvements would be needed before the technique could serve as a useful communication system.

“It’s not often that you get to demonstrate an entirely new way of communicating,” Professor Davis said. “We have shown experimentally that information can be encoded in the gravitational field and recovered at the other end.”

The researchers also modeled how more sensitive quantum sensors might improve the technique. Unlike the classical gravimeter used in the experiment, such sensors could potentially detect weaker gravitational changes at greater distances.

“Our experiment used a classical gravity sensor, but our modeling indicates that quantum sensors could, with rather ambitious assumptions, support ranges of up to about 500 meters (about 1,640 feet) and data rates of around 15 bits per minute.”

Those figures are theoretical projections, not experimentally demonstrated capabilities. Gravity-based communication remains far too slow for practical applications, but more advanced detectors could eventually make it possible to exchange information in environments that radio waves, light, and other conventional signals cannot penetrate.

China's Z.ai Says Its New AI Model Can Think Like One Four Times Its Size

Chinese AI developer Z.ai released GLM-5.4 on Monday, introducing a new architecture that it claims delivers the reasoning capabilities of models nearly four times its size without requiring nearly as many parameters.

It also supports a one-million-token context window, accepts images and video, and can generate up to 128,000 tokens in a single response, according to its technical documentation. Parameters are the internal values a model learns during training, while tokens are the chunks of text it processes and generates—roughly equivalent to words or parts of words.

The release comes as American companies including OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google dominate the highest positions in AI performance rankings. Chinese developers, meanwhile, have increasingly focused on making powerful models cheaper to run, particularly for coding and autonomous agents.

Z.ai has been among China's strongest competitors. As Decrypt reported in August, its predecessor GLM-5.3 was already competitive with leading American coding models, although it still trailed the best proprietary systems on several software engineering tests.

GLM-5.4 introduces what Z.ai calls a Loop Transformer, which processes information through the same 28 neural network layers twice. Traditional Transformers generally pass information through each layer once, so reusing layers effectively increases computational depth without increasing the number of stored parameters.

The company combines this approach with gated updates, which regulate how much information changes between passes, and a hybrid memory cache that avoids storing an entirely separate copy of previous calculations. Sparse attention also helps the model focus its computing power on relevant information rather than processing everything equally.

In controlled experiments on a smaller 30-billion-parameter model, Z.ai reported that its looping technique increased accuracy on AIME 2025, a mathematics examination benchmark scored by the percentage of correctly solved problems, from 19.17% to 30.42%.

GPQA Diamond, a graduate-level science question-answering test also scored by correct-answer percentage, improved from 47.58% to 56.26%. The experiments required approximately 10% additional training compute, while reducing generated reasoning tokens by 10% to 14% on several tests.

Those results concern the experimental architecture, not independently established performance for GLM-5.4 itself. Z.ai has not published a comprehensive flagship benchmark comparison demonstrating that the new model matches Claude or GPT.

The company is also promoting what it calls Generalized Coding: using programming to accomplish tasks beyond software development. Its demonstrations include building a video game, creating 3D environments, and designing a six-axis robotic arm using engineering software.

The company's previous flagship costs $1.40 per million input tokens and $4.40 per million output tokens through its API. By comparison, OpenAI's GPT-6.2 Sol costs $2 and $10, while Anthropic's Claude Opus 5.5 costs $4 and $20, respectively.

At those published rates, GLM-5.3 is considerably cheaper than its American counterparts, although GLM-5.4's own API pricing has not been announced. Its actual performance-per-dollar advantage therefore cannot yet be calculated.

USB-C chargers are pretty standardized at this point, but there’s a fun new trend of adding displays to your power brick that I’ve been very much enjoying, and the new PicoGo Pro from Baseus is a great example with three ports, optimized charging, and a cute little character to top it off – and for Black Friday, it’s pretty deeply discounted, too.

The Baseus PicoGo Pro (AH22) is a 100W, 3-port USB-C charger designed to top off your laptop, phone, and/or tablet, even all at the same time. For anyone who travels often or just has a lot of devices on hand, chargers like this are a must. Having something you don’t have to think twice about when it comes to charging rates is just so nice, and these have come a long way, too.

Using GaN tech, PicoGo Pro is very compact at about 2 inches tall and a little over an inch wide. I remember having single-port chargers that could barely fast-charge my phone that were twice this size just a few years ago. GaN just absolutely rules, and Baseus is using a special version that reduces heat and improves efficiency.

But the two reasons I really like this charger aren’t strictly to do with GaN.

The first is the built-in display. Again, that’s a trend throughout the industry. Anker has several chargers out with built-in displays as the company helped to really make this popular – the Anker Prime 160W brick is really similar to PicoGo Pro (at a higher price), and it’s the only I’ve been using for months before trying out this Baseus model.

Baseus has a cute little animated character that accompanies your charging, with little eyes when the charger isn’t active and some fun animations to show your charging rate in real-time. Neat! You can “press” the button to move through the UI and even see the specific charging rate of each connected device. You’ll also see that the brick can recognize the device you have plugged in, though Android devices just show a generic “SAMSUNG” or whatever brand you plug in – Anker, again, had a similar set of features in a brick released earlier in 2026, but some of those features were iPhone-only, and that brick was only a 1-port, 45W charger anyway.

But, like that Anker brick, Baseus supports “smart” charging that adjusts to your device. Plugging in a Galaxy Z Fold 8 that was around 65% full, I saw the brick adjust its charging speed to tailor to the phone, initially going up to full speed before settling down around 28W~. You can manually adjust this with “Turbo” or “Gentle” modes, the former maxing out the charging rate and the latter settling in around 15W for a slower charge. That flexibility is great because it means you can adjust the charging rate to what you need in that moment. Full-speed for a quick top-off on a busy day, slower charging overnight to preserve battery health.

Baseus isn’t doing much new here, though the GaN tech mentioned earlier is enhanced by some architecture and algorithm adjustments the company has made behind the scenes, but it’s a really good implementation.

Beyond that, it’s also priced pretty well.

Baseus PicoGo Pro’s full price is $59.99, but you’ll probably rarely see it there. Technically, today is its launch date, and it’s already down by over 30% at $39.70 for Black Friday. Value-wise, this one is hard to beat.
