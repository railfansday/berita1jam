Chainalysis says hackers stole credentials and tokens for all of its customers

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Company said a “multifront cyberattack” included AI agents and an on-path data snatch.

Blockchain analysis firm Chainalysis said that hackers have obtained sensitive personal information—including names, usernames, password hashes, and live tokens—for all of its users following a breach of its network security.

The accomplishment could be a major coup for the attackers. Both Chainalysis and public sources have revealed a long list of high-profile customers, including government agencies such as the FBI, US Immigrations and Customs Enforcement agency, and the US Air Force, as well as private companies such as Microsoft, Coinbase, and Barclays. It’s unclear if the data theft exposed customers to additional threats.

Last Friday, Chainalysis said it had been the target of a cybersecurity incident in October that was similar to the hacks of other platforms during the same month. The external actor used AI agent-assisted offensive techniques to gain unauthorized access to Chainalysis systems and internal tools. An email the company sent to affected customers said some “non-sensitive customer data, such as user IDs, full names, email addresses, and organization role labels for users provisioned on our platform were accessed.” It’s not clear why the company considered such information “non-sensitive.”

In a Wednesday update, the company said that part of the “ongoing multifront cyberattack” involved an “on-path attack” that exposed a subset of customer passwords, and that the attack vector had been identified and fixed. Also known as an adversary-in-the-middle attack, on-path attacks happen when a hacker gains access to a piece of infrastructure sending traffic back and forth between two parties. Chainalysis has yet to say where the infrastructure compromise occurred and if the data obtained from it was TLS encrypted.

The company said later that day that an investigation two days earlier (on Monday) had revealed that “certain customer information including names, usernames, password hashes and live tokens for all Chainalysis users were exposed to a malicious actor.” It also said it had detected “abnormal activity” and in response took down its login page and know-your-transaction service, which monitors threats to cryptocurrency holdings in real time. The company has also removed access to its Reactor and Data Solutions services.

In an update on Friday, Chainalysis said it had begun phasing in customer access to KYT and Address Screening and expected more customers to follow. It asked customers to submit their trusted IP addresses so they can be added to an allow list and to prepare to reset credentials once they regain access. The credential rotation is mandatory.

The company says its investigation has yet to reveal any evidence that any investigations data has been accessed or Chainalysis attribution data has been manipulated.

Several important questions remain unanswered. As noted, the source that made the on-path attack possible is still unknown. It’s not clear how useful the information obtained in such an attack would be unless the attackers also had the ability to decrypt the traffic they were monitoring. If that was the case, the attackers might have gained broad control of the company’s infrastructure.

Chainalysis also didn’t explain why it waited until Wednesday to disclose the data theft of all customers’ personal information when its investigation discovered the exposure two days earlier. Further, the company has provided no details about the AI agents used in the attack and how they worked.

A Chainalysis representative said the company wasn’t commenting beyond the statements in its blog post.

Tankers carrying Russian diesel to the US change course

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Despite an ostensible deal between Donald Trump and Vladimir Putin to bring diesel to the US, tankers are reportedly unable to dock due to existing sanctions.

Several sanctioned tankers headed for the US carrying Russian diesel are said to have changed course, one toward Brazil and the other into the Mediterranean, according to data from marine data company Kpler and vessel traffic site MarineTraffic.com.

The tankers reportedly departed from the Baltic Sea port of Primorsk last month and had declared their destinations as the US east coast.

This all comes after US President Donald Trump eased sanctions on Russian oil last month, in a deal with with Russian leader Vladimir Putin to secure a large amount of diesel directly to the US at a time when diesel prices have skyrocketed across the country.

The agreement caused shock and drew criticism in Ukraine and Europe. But it also was a logistical challenge, given that sanctions against Russia still remain in place.

It was also widely reported at the time that the amount of diesel in question was a fraction of the US daily demand.

UK newspaper The Financial Times (FT) reported Wednesday that two of the tankers appeared to have changed their destination and were no longer headed to the United States.

Russian envoy and negotiator Kirill Dmitriev said on Thursday that the US-bound diesel tankers were still on course.

"Fake. Tankers destined for the US continue on course," Dmitriev said on X, linking to the FT story.

Putin's spokesman Dmitry Peskov said detail of shipments were a "commercial" matter that could not be made public.

"All diesel will find its buyers," he added, without elaborating.

A third vessel was still in the North Atlantic headed toward the US but changed its destination from New York to a vague "for orders," without listing an unloading point.

Sherrill accused the Trump administration of lacking authority in overriding the import ban enacted by Congress more than four years ago.

"No president can unilaterally override a statute. Trump's license will not absolve you of legal and moral accountability," the letter read.

Justice Department Explains Why It Gave Fox-Roku Merger Its Greenlight, Outlines Company Commitments

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The Justice Department issued a statement on its clearance of Fox Corp.‘s $22 billion aquisition of Roku, concluding that the transaction was “not likely to result in harm to competition.”

In a statement, the Antitrust Division said that they concluded that the concluded that the “entry of free, ad-supported streaming providers would be timely, likely, and sufficient to make a substantial risk of harm to competition unlikely. Additionally, the investigation found that, because Roku relies on a broad offering of content from many providers to attract consumers, the merged firm would suffer monetary and reputational harm if it attempted to use the Roku platform to harm Fox’s content rivals. Per the DOJ, Fox committed that Roku would “remain an open and neutral content partner post-merger.”

Along with Amazon Fire, Roku is a leading gateway to streaming, declaring last April that it reaches 100 million households globally. The company works with a wide range of programmers, which casts Fox in an influential role with its rivals on the content side. Patience, professionalism paying off for NY Giants’ RB Devin Singletary

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

New York Giants running back Devin Singletary was inactive from Week 3-5. In Weeks 1,2, and 6 — while the Giants were trying to figure out a workable backfield rotation — Singletary carried the ball only 13 times.

Yet, there he was on the final play of overtime in Week 7 against the Houston Texans, the last man standing from what had been a four-man competition. An unhappy Najee Harris had been cut days before. Tyrone Tracy suffered a hamstring injury in the first half that would land him on IR. One play earlier, Cam Skattebo had injured his own hamstring on a career-best 42-yard run.

So, it was Singletary left to take the handoff from J.J. McCarthy at the Texans' 30-yard line in a tie game. Suddenly, he was in the secondary. Then, after a couple of nifty cuts, he was in the end zone and the Giants had won a game.

In the post-game madness, a jubilant Singletary and head coach John Harbaugh landed in a heap on the ground.

Singletary said this week that "I still don't know" if Harbaugh tackled him, or vice versa.

"I think we just tripped," Singletary said. "As Coach would say, it's football joy. You go through a lot to be able to get to football joy. We were just so happy, so excited. He was telling me how much he loved me. I was telling him how much I loved him."

Singletary said there "was never no bad feelings" between himself and Harbaugh because of the eight-veteran's lack of opportunities. Singletary, 29, understand that it was "business."

Of course Singletary, who has more than 1,100 carries and 5,000 NFL rushing yards, didn't like not playing. He did appreciate Harbaugh's honesty.

"One thing I do respect about him, he kept it real with me. You don't always see that in the league. That's why I always got so much respect for him for that reason," Singletary said. "It was just, this is what it is right now. I need you to stay patient and we'll see how it plays out. Simple as that.

"I was like, I appreciate that. Some people won't even tell you that. They'll just leave you there with no answers, with nothing."

It played out such that Singletary got to play the hero against the team he gained a career-high in rushing yards for in 2023.

"I wouldn't say frustrating," Singletary said in reference to his lack of playing time. "It's like, yes, part of it is how the game goes. It's a lot of competition. It's a deep backfield. But as far as being a pro, that's me. That's how I was brought up.

"I'm going to always go about my business the right way. Stay ready, because when that opportunity comes, you want to be ready to seize the moment instead of being so mad or whatever it is, sad or whatever, and you can't capture the moment when it's here."

After the Texans game, Harbaugh said Singletary's lack of playing time was "on me."

"We were looking for the formula, and Devin wasn't — he was the odd man out for a number of weeks. Not because of anything he did or didn't do, he just was the odd man out, and that's exactly what I told him," Harbaugh said. "He asked me, 'what can I do?' I said, 'there's nothing you can do, you're doing everything you can do, and your time will come'. It was going to come. And look where it came, right there, and he came through."

Offensive coordinator Matt spoke this week about how appreciative he is of Singletary's professionalism.

"We went through the whole thing with the different running backs and four or five guys and he's been here from day one, from OTA's, and through training camp, through the season, wasn't playing, wasn't active. And he'd sit back in practice when he wasn't playing and not say a word, but just be completely professional," Nagy said. "And we talked, and we talked about great things happen to great people and I just think back to that Texans game, and when he scored that touchdown at the end, the discussions that we had in practice when he wasn't playing, he never became negative, he never made it about him. He was as great of a professional player as there was, and look what happened.

"And I think it's a story for young kids, it's a story for vets to be patient, to understand things happen for a reason and when he scored that touchdown, I couldn't think but anything of how he handled himself the entire last five weeks. And we talked about it in the locker room, and I think for him, what a credit and we're lucky to have him."

Baseball fans might recall the name Tommy Henrich, an outfielder/first baseman for the New York Yankees from 1937-1950. His nickname was 'Old Reliable'. In a way, it is what Singletary has become for Harbaugh's Giants.

Now, with Tracy on IR and Skattebo perhaps unable to play Sunday against the Eagles, Singletary is likely to be the lead back.

"I know what I can do. I want to be able to display that," Singletary said. "We'll see what this week brings, but yeah, I'm excited."

Sen. Chris Murphy released from hospital after stroke, will miss votes for 'several weeks' as he rehabs

📅 2026-11-06  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Connecticut Democratic Sen. Chris Murphy said Friday he had been released from a hospital after suffering a stroke last weekend.

Murphy, 53, said he will be in a rehabilitation center for the next "several weeks," meaning he will miss votes in the Senate when Congress returns on Monday.

"The doctors are very encouraged by the progress I've made so far," he said in a statement.

"In order to take my recovery seriously and get back to work on the quickest timeline possible, I will be spending several weeks at a rehabilitation center after being discharged from the hospital today," said Murphy, who has been discussed as a possible presidential candidate in 2028.

Dr. Jose Suarez, chief of Neurosciences Critical Care at The Johns Hopkins Hospital, in a statement released by Murphy's office, said, "Senator Murphy experienced a small hemorrhagic stroke."

"He has shown tremendous progress, and at the current rate of improvement, we expect that he will make a full recovery following rehabilitation," Suarez said.

Murphy had been in the hospital since having a stroke at his son's hockey game.

"Last Saturday afternoon, I felt something was not right with my body and called 9-1-1 out of an abundance of caution, and I'm glad I did," Murphy said in his statement. "I was diagnosed with a small bleed in the thalamus region of my brain."

The senator said he was grateful "for the outpouring of support my family and I have received over the last few days.""This has been a scary experience for me and my family, and I appreciate the continued respect for our privacy," Murphy said. "As I'm working towards a full recovery, I am staying engaged with my team and colleagues remotely, and my staff continues to carry out our legislative priorities, serve constituents, and ensure the work of my office continues uninterrupted."

Murphy's stroke came three days before Democratic candidates, in a stunning election result, won more than enough races to flip the Senate and House of Representatives from Republican majority control in January.

Murphy, who was not up for reelection this year, before the election had campaigned for Democratic candidates around the country, most recently in Michigan, Minnesota and Florida. He had been scheduled to campaign in New Hampshire on Sunday with Rep. Chris Pappas, who on Tuesday won election to the Senate.

As climbing becomes more popular, and thus more profitable, one may question whether these gyms will inevitably become the norm.

But in Great Barrington, a small town of a little more than 7,000 residents in Western Massachusetts, entrepreneur Nick Friedman is moving in the opposite direction: starting a new, independent gym that gives away all its profits.

You can trace the 38-year-old’s vision for an altruistic gym back to his childhood, when his dad put his career on hold to demand that General Electric clean up the river that ran through town—and Friedman’s property. In the 1970s, General Electric dumped polychlorinated biphenyls (PCBs) into the Housatonic River, so Friedman’s dad fought to hold the company liable. GE settled, paying $250 million.

In the process, Friedman learned the importance of activism. The cause close to Friedman’s heart is climbing access, which is not exactly easy to come by in Great Barrington or the Berkshires—but not for a lack of rock. While he calls the climbing in Great Barrington world-class, he says that privately owned land and conservation organizations that don’t understand the value of climbing make access a problem.

“Great Barrington is this unique case study in incredible climbing that is barred from true public domain due to land access challenges,” said Friedman, who has been deeply involved in local access work. In 2023, Friedman, who also serves on the board of the Western Massachusetts Climbers’ Coalition (WMCC), purchased a 70-acre parcel in the Farley Ledges climbing area. This bought WMCC time to organize the permits and funding to purchase it from Friedman earlier this year. While Farley Ledges currently remains closed due to an access issue involving a private landowner, WMCC is raising funds to construct a new parking lot and trail to reopen climbing.

Making these land purchases and forging the agreements necessary to maintain and expand access in Western Massachusetts takes a lot of resources, Friedman explains. That’s where his new gym, Berkshire Boulders, which opens on November 6, comes in. He plans to donate 100% of his profits to community organizations, including WMCC and Access Fund.

“The WMCC is very excited about the opening of Berkshire Boulders and the potential it has to positively impact climbing access in Western Massachusetts,” said Regina Madden, WMCC communications coordinator. Protecting and maintaining access requires ongoing resources, so a local business committed to supporting that mission creates an excellent opportunity. In addition, Berkshire Boulders can serve to bridge the gap between indoor and outdoor climbing, helping to foster a stronger sense of stewardship and community.

While Friedman may pursue nonprofit status down the line, he says it was faster to get the gym off the ground as a business that gave away its profits, rather than a 501(c)3 nonprofit. And as someone who has specialized professionally in startups, he adds that he’s always been “intrigued” by hybrid models that blend for-profit business models with nonprofit values.

Currently, the organizations Berkshire Boulders plans to support include WMCC and Access Fund, as well as non-climbing local organizations including the Railroad Street Youth Project and Greenagers, which fosters sustainability work among youth.

“I felt that a climbing gym could play a central role in that effort and become this anchor and the starting point for a broader vision of bringing outdoor recreation more front and center in this small town,” Friedman said.

Friedman doesn’t see accessibility as a binary, “indoors or outdoors” issue; he views it holistically. This is why he’s made it the gym’s mission to improve climbing access indoors and out. He envisions a gym as a place where you can learn the sport, meet partners, and find people to help you make the gym-to-crag transition. In Friedman’s view, that gym-to-crag transition is the end game. That’s why he decorated Berkshire Boulders’ walls with photographs of Great Barrington rock, and rents out crashpads to climbers who want to try bouldering outside, but lack the gear.

But the other end game is community, which he says is hard to foster when you’re answering to private equity investors. Berkshire Boulders’ KPIs will instead revolve “around community impact.”

One way he’s doing that is by offering hardship memberships. Each month, Berkshire Boulders will award free memberships to low-income individuals through a lottery system. This addresses the known problem of affordability in the gym industry—one so notable that the American Alpine Club declared it an access issue in 2024. Friedman says he’s already approved one hardship membership application, but doesn’t have a monthly quota in mind yet. “We do expect to expand that program significantly over the next several months,” Friedman said.

A focus on community KPIs, however, will not come at the expense of a high-quality bouldering facility. Friedman insists that he is committed to a top-tier facility. He is partnering with local hold-maker Decoy and plans to change the setting across the 15-foot-high walls every two weeks. The gym also features a custom-built, “artistically created” adjustable spray wall. Using the Clift app, climbers will be able to set sequences, replicate local outdoor problems, and track leaderboards.

Currently, for Friedman, Berkshire Boulders is a passion project. His personal endgame is not to make a living off the gym—he still works full-time as the chief strategy officer at a small-batch cannabis startup. He also hired an experienced general manager to run the gym. Rather, that Friedman hopes to ultimately do is drive positive change in his local climbing community.

“Personally, I would love to see the Great Barrington climbing become a published resource for the public,” Friedman said. It’s a long game, but a worthy one, in his view, to turn the local climbing from an underground, tricky access dance to a veritable destination with routes documented on Mountain Project, a guidebook, and formal parking and approach trails.

Beyond local impact, could Berkshire Boulders, if successful, inspire a rising tide of small, mission-driven gyms in this era of indoor climbing corporatization? Friedman believes smaller gyms with a focus on community have a better shot at bringing together passionate climbers than the larger conglomerate gyms. “I would love to see an expansion of smaller independent gyms regionally,” he says.
