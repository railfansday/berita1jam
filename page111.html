Inside Celebrity Chef Dook Chase’s Renovated New Orleans Home—and the Dream Kitchen He Calls His Sanctuary

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Celebrity chef Dook Chase is intentionally never far from a kitchen, be it inside one of his renowned restaurants or his dream family home in New Orleans.

Chase, 44, is the fourth-generation owner of Dooky Chase’s Restaurant, the legendary culinary destination and cultural institution that’s been operating in the Big Easy since 1941. The NOLA native also runs his modern Creole eatery, Chapter IV, commuting between both businesses from a Southern-style, two-story residence that he shares with his wife, Gretchen, their daughter, Sidney, and son, Vito.

“It's a seven-minute commute to both restaurants,” Chase tells Realtor.com® in the latest Celebrity Sanctuary. “I can visit each one in its peak time. I can get a text from any server saying, ‘Hey, a table came in that really wanted to see you,’ and I [say], ‘Tell them I'll be there in seven minutes.’ I can hop in the car and get to each.”

Personally connecting with the local community over a meal is a practice that is baked into Chase’s family backstory and a tradition he proudly upholds today.

“When you think about Dooky Chase Restaurant and my grandmother, chef Leah Chase, in the time that she helmed that kitchen ... the history of our restaurant is really through the Civil Rights Movement, opening doors to everyone in the community: Blacks and whites, together, at a time when laws didn't allow it. But we always allowed it at our restaurant,” the Le Cordon Bleu-educated executive chef explains. “My grandparents, great-grandparents, [and] certainly my parents understood the importance of learning from each and every person ... and we were able to do that through food.

“One thing that I love about my family's history and what we've been able to do every Thanksgiving is, we open our restaurant on Thanksgiving Day,” he continues. “So we gather with our community first, and then we close, clean up, set the tables all over again, and then [our] families come in. It’s a way to serve others and then enjoy your day right after.”

Chase’s Thanksgiving menu is typically an “endless” buffet of classic Southern dishes, including his favorite, gumbo, followed by multiple dessert options. However, this year, the “Family Recipe Showdown” star is bringing some fresh inspiration to the table thanks to his time working with fellow chef Christina Tosi and actress Drew Barrymore on the latest “Pillsbury Bake-off Classic” competition show, due to air on Nov. 23 on CBS and Paramount+.

“We have different experiences and different backgrounds, but ... we learn from one another,” Chase says. “That's what I enjoyed about this bake-off. This really brings people together and unites them [to] think about the memories of their childhoods, their families, their celebrations, and you felt that in that room.”

In this edition of Celebrity Sanctuary, Chase welcomes a look at his hospitable kitchen where he often hosts gatherings with family and friends on ordinary and special occasions alike. Plus, he explains how the heart of the 1952-built, 4,400-square-foot, five-bedroom, five-bathroom home helps him focus on the moment he’s in as much as the meal he’s making.

Our previous home is a block away from here. I would exercise in the morning, run up and down [this] oak tree–lined street, and I would tell my wife, “If something ever comes up for sale, I'm buying.”

As I was running one morning, [the previous owner’s] children were putting up a sign, and I asked, “Can you wait a day? Don't put up the sign. Let's talk. And then if it doesn't work, put up a sign.”

They waited that day. It happened to work out. I think why it worked out was because their father understood the family atmosphere of what he wanted that next owner to be. When we shared the story of who I was and all about my family, it was like, “This is what I want to happen in this house.”

It's a beautiful house. I really enjoy it ... and am excited to raise my family and the next generation of us here.

The architectural style of this home is Southern New Orleans. We have the big oak tree. We have the brick porch. We have the wood doors. We have shutters.

I bought this home in 2009, and we moved in 2010. When we got it, it was completely gutted from the devastation of Hurricane Katrina. We renovated it and brought it to where it is today. We added on to the porch to add a new front door section where you can be greeted by artwork or by us, and then eventually get to the stairs.

One of the great things we were able to do in this house is save some of the original hardwood. The rooms upstairs that didn't have hardwood, that had carpet, we brought [the hardwood] to those rooms, refurbished them, and then downstairs, laid all new hardwood down. Most of the windows we were able to keep.

Downstairs, we wanted to open it up. Back in the day, they had walls where you would walk through a door into the kitchen, so it was closed off to the other rooms. For me as a chef, when I'm cooking, I want to see everybody. It's where people gather, so that kitchen really dictates every movement that's going on downstairs. Whether you're in the living room, the dining room, the den—you can see and smell what's going on in that kitchen.

Sanctuary, for me, it means home. It means peace. It means comfort. It's almost like you're getting a big hug that wraps you and says, “I got you.”

My comfort zone is the kitchen. It's where I find peace. That's where I can block out all the noise and really home in on what's happening right now.

The home kitchen allows you to slow down. It's different timing. There's not 200 people in the dining room waiting for their meals; there might be just three hungry people. It allows you to slow down and to really think and to really enjoy the meal that you're cooking or the meal that you're eating.

Also, we use my home as a test kitchen. We’ve done many cookbooks. Most of those recipes I've tested in my home versus tested in a restaurant, because it allows you to slow down. It allows you to measure. It allows you to take your time and say, “If I was a home cook reading this cookbook, what do I need to have?” It gives me that hybrid space of really focusing and slowing down and getting it right versus having extra things come through in and out of that kitchen. In your home, you really have that peace to enjoy the process.

We have a big six-burner stove, a griddle, two ovens, all that good stuff. We have a gray backsplash and a stainless-steel hood. A double-door refrigerator.

The countertops are like a granite marble. I have equipment on top of the countertop: We have a mixer. I love nugget ice, so there's a nugget ice machine.

The cabinets are traditional, white cabinets. They have either copper or brass [hardware]. The wall is painted a gray tone. Going into the den has a navy blue. The colors I like ... enhanced by all of my wife's tastes.

There's a center where I can move a butcher block if we want to roll out any pastry. It's open. It's not an island, but like an island. On the other side, it's a higher top where there’s almost bar seating.

My kitchen overlooks the dining room. There is a little chandelier there. Our table has windows bringing in a natural light that overlooks an oak tree.

To the left of that is a seating area that has beautiful artwork. Art is huge in my family, so we have artwork everywhere. It has four chairs and a sofa, where we have a coffee table, seating, some statues, some other creative art. Then to the right of that, it's the den and another little nook where we keep a bar.

We have enough room for people to move around. Everything has natural light, and it just flows into different rooms, but the kitchen is the center. It's the focal point of the downstairs of my house. When you have a party, the kitchen is always going to be the focal point of it. No matter what's going on in any other room, people gather in the kitchen.

We have a lot of fun for holidays, a lot of different traditions that we do here. On Christmas Eve, the family gets together and we hang till about 2 in the morning. Crazy, I know, but it's pretty cool.

My family is huge and faith [is] huge, so we certainly have a cross in there that symbolizes it’s a blessing.

I always call the people in my family that are no longer with us our guardian angels, so [I have] symbols all throughout the kitchen that's highlighting both of my grandmothers in different elements. There's a red pot that we may keep on the stove like we keep in the restaurant that my grandmother loved. We never cook with them at the restaurant. We never really cook with them at home. It was always just that showpiece, and she loved it.

Different small things that may have been from different people, whether it was my side or my wife's side, those things give you back memories of those people, so we have that all throughout the house for cherished memories and reminders that they're always with us. They never really leave us. They keep guiding us, and that's what I love about it.

I'm happy with [the kitchen] as is. I love this house. I think the only changes would be if my two kids, as they get older, they learn how to clean a little better!

But that's the beautiful thing about our home and many homes. That dining room table services as so many purposes. You're eating there. You're doing homework there. You’re having discussion there. You’re having people over there. So it does tend to get overwhelmed in terms of how it looks, but that's how important it is. That's how important food is. It's everything. It's learning. It's education. It's who we are. It's culture. It's family time. So I don't mind it.

Prediction: Nebius Stock Will Trade At This Price at the End of 2027

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Nebius has taken a big beating in recent months, but it could make a parabolic move in 2027.

By Harsh Chauhan – Nov 19, 2026 at 4:14AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Nebius Group ›

Neocloud infrastructure providers are playing a central role in the booming adoption of artificial intelligence (AI), which explains why Nebius Group (NBIS +0.87%) has been growing at an incredible pace.

Nebius provides a full-stack cloud AI infrastructure platform that allows customers to rent computing capacity to train, fine-tune, and deploy AI models, while its software suite enables them to run inference. The market rewarded Nebius stock handsomely for its incredible growth in 2025 and in the first half of 2026.

However, the AI trade has soured in recent months. Investors are concerned about whether the heavy investments to build more AI data centers are sustainable, especially given that the Federal Reserve is anticipated to increase interest rates once again. This explains why several AI infrastructure stocks, including Nebius, have taken a big beating in recent months.

In fact, Nebius has lost 61% of its value over the past three months. The company's latest results haven't boosted investor confidence in the stock either. Let's see why that has been the case.

Nebius released third-quarter results on Nov. 12. The company's revenue increased by an incredible 571% year over year to $981 million. Even better, it posted $91 million in non-GAAP net income, a significant improvement over the year-ago period's loss of $100.4 million. Nebius' cost of revenue as a percentage of revenue dropped by seven percentage points year over year to 22%.

Its operating costs and expenses fell to 95% of revenue in Q3, a major improvement over the year-ago period's 189%. Notably, Nebius crushed Wall Street's revenue expectation of $907.5 million, and its adjusted loss per share of $0.31 was well below the consensus estimate of $0.69.

However, a couple of issues with Nebius' quarterly report led investors to press the panic button. The stock fell nearly 17% after releasing its report, as the company lowered its 2026 revenue guidance range to $3 billion-$3.2 billion from the prior range of $3 billion-$3.4 billion.

Nebius pointed out that some of the data center capacity provided to the company by a third party is shifting into the second quarter next year. So, the shift in timing of the additional data center capacity that was supposed to come online will dent its top-line growth prospects for 2026.

The second factor that spooked Nebius investors was its 2027 capital expenditure forecast of $40 billion to $45 billion. What's more, the company has increased its 2026 capex guidance to $27 billion, up from the $20 billion-$25 billion range it forecast earlier. Investors are skeptical of Nebius' aggressive capex growth because it has been taking on debt to fund its data center build-out.

The company had just over $14 billion in long-term debt at the end of the third quarter, well above the $4.1 billion debt it reported at the end of 2025. As a result, its interest expenses have ballooned to $295 million in the first nine months of 2026, up from just $19.5 million in the same period last year. However, investors may be overreacting to the company's capex guidance for 2027, as it has a smart strategy in place to reduce balance sheet strain.

Nebius is forecasting a 4x increase in revenue in 2027 to a range of $12 billion to $13 billion. The company will add more than 1 gigawatt (GW) of new capacity in 2027 to get to its estimated revenue range. That explains the major increase in its capex next year, though investors should note that customer prepayments will fund the incremental outlay.

Nebius is expecting $17 billion-$20 billion in customer prepayments next year, which means it won't have to rely entirely on debt and equity financing to fund the build-out. That probably explains why Nebius expects to hit breakeven on a pre-tax basis in the fourth quarter of 2027.

Additionally, the shortage of AI data center infrastructure is allowing Nebius to raise prices. The company charged an average of $25 million a year for each megawatt (MW) of data center capacity in Q3, up from a $20 million-$25 million range in the second quarter. Another point worth noting is that Nebius has booked over $12 billion in new customer contracts in Q3, which is in multiples of its top line.

All this indicates that Nebius could regain investor confidence, driven by its ability to deliver strong revenue growth and its target of becoming profitable next year. Not surprisingly, analysts are bullish about this AI stock's trajectory over the coming year.

Nebius has a 12-month median price target of $245, implying 129% upside. Nebius could easily hit that mark, and even head higher. Assuming its 2027 revenue lands at the midpoint of its forecast -- at $12.5 billion -- and it trades at 6.5 times sales (in line with the U.S. tech sector's average price-to-sales ratio), its market cap could reach $81 billion.

For comparison, Nebius has a market cap of $31.4 billion. So, savvy investors can consider using Nebius' pullback to buy a stock that can outperform Wall Street's expectations in 2027 and become a potential multibagger.

President Donald Trump meets with Russian President Vladimir Putin ahead of trilateral talks with Chinese President Xi Jinping.

President Donald Trump's visit to China for the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit featured an elaborate welcome, warm exchanges with Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin, and a declaration of American technological dominance on Chinese soil.

The gathering in Shenzhen culminated in the first-ever trilateral meeting between the leaders of the United States, China and Russia, with nuclear arms reductions and the war in Ukraine among the issues on the table.

Here are some of the standout moments from Trump's visit.

XI BREAKS PROTOCOL TO PERSONALLY WELCOME TRUMP AHEAD OF HISTORIC TRILATERAL SUMMIT AND MORE TOP HEADLINES

Trump declared the United States the world leader in "superintelligence" during remarks at the summit Thursday, making the assertion in one of China's major technology hubs.

"The United States is also proud to be the world leader in superintelligence, the greatest technological transformation since the Industrial Revolution," Trump told APEC leaders.

The remarks came as Washington and Beijing compete for leadership in artificial intelligence and advanced semiconductors.

Trump joined Xi and Putin for the first-ever trilateral meeting between the leaders of the United States, China and Russia just after 3 a.m. ET, calling the gathering an opportunity to reduce nuclear weapons and address global conflicts.

"As the world's three largest nuclear powers by far, the United States, Russia and China are uniquely positioned to discuss how to reduce the number of nuclear weapons on Earth," Trump said.

The president also said he had held extensive discussions with Putin about ending the war in Ukraine.

Trump also raised preventing Iran from obtaining nuclear weapons and said he would propose ways for the three countries to protect their sovereignty from the International Criminal Court, which he accused of exceeding its jurisdiction.

A Bloomberg reporter was barred from the trilateral meeting after asking Trump during his bilateral with Xi whether Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang had been "disloyal to the United States," according to White House pool reports. Chinese officials warned that shouting questions was considered extremely rude and that reporters could be removed for doing so, despite White House efforts to secure the Bloomberg reporter's access.

Xi personally greeted Trump upon his arrival in Shenzhen Wednesday, meeting the president at the foot of the aircraft stairs in an elaborate welcome.

The two exchanged greetings before standing together for the American and Chinese national anthems. A military flyover followed, with five jets leaving trails of colored smoke overhead.

42 Years Later, The Terminator's Most Famous Line Is Still The Greatest Sci-Fi Movie Quote

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

"The Terminator" bowed in 1984 but was so ahead of its time it could easily debut today and be just as, if not more relevant. The film's encapsulation of society's collective fear of a technological future gave what would have otherwise been a sci-fi slasher B-movie real depth. Paired with James Cameron's still-developing yet impressive filmmaking skills, honed over years spent toiling for Roger Corman's New World Pictures and a particularly unfortunate experience on 1982's "Piranha II: The Spawning," that remarkable prescience made the film an instant classic that also happened to give its star, Arnold Schwarzenegger, his most memorable catchphrase.

Long before 2019's "Terminator: Dark Fate" made Schwarzenegger give up on the franchise altogether, Cameron's original masterpiece combined elements of the burgeoning slasher genre with sci-fi and action spectacle to create a truly original moviegoing experience (even if the insatiably litigious Harlan Ellison claimed "The Terminator" was all his idea). The fact that any movie gets produced is a marvel in its own way, but "The Terminator" was a real miracle, wrought by the sheer will of its director and his producing partner Gale Anne Hurd to actually get the film made. Their persistence paid off. What could have easily been an overlooked low-budget sci-fi actioner became a modest sensation, making $78.4 million on a $6.4 million budget and launching Cameron's career.

Clearly, the writer/director had tapped into something larger with his story of a murderous cyborg sent back through time to kill the woman who would birth the leader of the human resistance. Audiences were enraptured and 42 years later the movie remains just as chillingly pertinent and wildly entertaining as it was in the mid-'80s. As such, Arnie's most famous line arguably remains the greatest sci-fi quote of all time.

Arnold Schwarzenegger initially had no interest in "The Terminator, which considering this film (alongside his lead performance in 1982's "Conan the Barbarian") turned him into a movie star is hard to believe. Similarly, the Austrian Oak was reluctant to deliver what has since become the movie's most memorable line. Thankfully, James Cameron won him over.

The scene in question sees Arnie's Terminator arrive at a police station in search of Linda Hamilton's Sarah Connor. There, he's told by the officer on duty to take a seat and wait. Big mistake. Presumably, this unstoppable killer robot could have just punched his way through the glass facade between him and the officer before rampaging through the station to find his target. But that's no fun. Instead, Cameron — who would later jump a semi-truck into a Los Angeles viaduct in "Terminator 2: Judgment Day" — decided to have Arnie's robot ram a sedan through the doors of the station and into the reception desk, pinning the officer to the wall. Before he does so, however, the Terminator says the line.

After the officer tells him to take a seat, Schwarzenegger calmly takes a look around, leans in and says three words: "I'll be back." Of course, he's referring to the fact he'll soon ram the policeman between a wall and a car. But like the movie in which they're spoken, the words proved to be oddly prophetic. The Terminator did return, multiple times. Only the aforementioned "T2" lived up to the original, but the simple phrase, "I'll be back," almost came to represent the way in which "The Terminator" and its sequel remain perennially relevant as we hurtle towards the AI-driven garbage future that awaits.

"That Terminator is out there. It can't be bargained with. It can't be reasoned with. It doesn't feel pity, or remorse, or fear. And it absolutely will not stop, ever, until you are dead!"

"You still don't get it, do you? He'll find her! That's what he does! That's all he does! You can't stop him! He'll wade through you, reach down her throat, and pull her f****** heart out!"

"Your son gave me a message to give to you. He made me memorize it. 'Thank you, Sarah, for your courage through the dark years. I can't help you with what you must soon face except to say that the future is not set. You must be stronger than you imagine you can be. You must survive or I will never exist.' That's all."

Intel CEO Lip-Bu Tan says Intel has signed its first outside 18A customer

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Intel did not name the client, which will use the 18A-P manufacturing process that entered risk production in June.

Intel has signed its first external customer for its 18A chipmaking technology, chief executive Lip-Bu Tan said on Wednesday. He spoke at the TomorrowX Summit in Austin, Texas, CNBC reported.

“And then 18A, I’m involved in production and we just signed the first customer,” Tan said.

Tan did not name the customer. Intel’s chief technology officer, Pushkar Ranade, later said on X that the client had signed up for 18A-P, an improved version of the 18A process.

Intel moved 18A-P into risk production in June. That is the stage where a chipmaker makes small batches to prove a process works before mass production. Intel says 18A-P is fully compatible with 18A designs.

Intel shares rose 2.6% in extended trading after the comments, according to Seeking Alpha.

A big outside customer is key to Intel’s plan to compete with TSMC as a contract chipmaker. Until now, Intel has mostly made chips for its own products.

However, Intel has announced other foundry deals before. In August, Japanese chip designer Socionext said it would use 18A-P to develop new chips. Intel did not explain how the new customer differs from that deal.

Reports have also linked Apple to 18A-P for future Mac chips. Neither company has confirmed a deal.

Xinhua Silk Road: Yunnan Tin Group boosts innovation to drive industrial upgrading

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

BEIJING, Nov. 19, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- At the production workshop of Yunnan Tin New Materials Co., Ltd., solder paste, solder balls and solder wire are rolling off production lines and heading to semiconductor packaging companies worldwide. These seemingly ordinary metal products are becoming highly sought-after "computing-power metals" in the era of artificial intelligence (AI).

With a history of over 140 years, Yunnan Tin Group (Holding) Co., Ltd., the parent company of Yunnan Tin New Materials, is strengthening its full-industry-chain coordination and technological innovation capabilities amid the transformation driven by computing infrastructure.

Through technological breakthroughs and process optimization, Yunnan Tin Group has unlocked previously untapped low-grade resources and improved resource recovery rates. In 2025, its Datun tin mine achieved a tin recovery rate of 79.21 percent, above the national average of 65-75 percent.

The group's tin industry branch has adopted an integrated smart management system, helping it cut operating costs by 18 percent and improve management efficiency by nearly 20 percent.

Since 2021, the group has established more than 10 national- and provincial-level innovation platforms and entities, including a tin-indium laboratory, building a robust innovation ecosystem.

As tin demand expands beyond traditional electronics into emerging sectors such as new energy, semiconductors and AI computing, Yunnan Tin Group is pushing technological boundaries to meet higher material standards.

Yunnan Tin New Materials has developed ultra-fine solder wire with a diameter of 0.1 millimeters, while its BGA solder balls can be as small as 50 micrometers. Its solder paste portfolio covers three major series and eight product grades, catering to the trend toward increasingly compact and powerful electronic devices.

The Yunnan tin-indium laboratory focuses on green separation and high-value utilization of tin resources, comprehensive utilization of tin tailings, and the development of high-end tin-indium interconnection materials.

In 2026, the laboratory secured approval for two national-level research projects, and submitted proposals for three national standards and one industry standard.

Looking ahead, Yunnan Tin Group will focus on agile manufacturing, precision marketing and new product development to provide "China wisdom" to support the upgrading of global tin and indium industrial chains. It aims to become a world-class enterprise in the industry by 2035.
