Apple has iPads running macOS already, but don’t get too excited

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

With iPads running the same chips as Macs, many have wished Apple would give us the option of running macOS on them instead of the somewhat clunky iPadOS windowing system. Apple’s previous arguments against converging devices have been somewhat weakened by the launch of the touchscreen MacBook Pro.

A new report says that Apple already has iPads running macOS internally, and that work on a giant iPad continues, but don’t hold your breath for a public launch of either …

As iPad hardware has grown more powerful over the years, the software has struggled to keep up. We saw split-screen capabilities first, with full windowing finally coming to the devices in iPadOS 26 last year.

The UI is still rather clunky compared to macOS, however, leading a significant number of people to wish that Apple simply offered the option of using the more powerful operating system on its tablet devices.

In a podcast Q&A, Bloomberg’s Mark Gurman says that Apple is already doing this internally, but there’s no sign of any plans to make it available outside the company.

Apple has iPads internally running macOS, and they’ve had that for some time. The fact that they have the underlying architecture of Apple Silicon that’s the same on the iPad and the Mac gives them the flexibility in order to test these types of things.

In terms if they’re actually going to do it – would they bring macOS to the iPad itself – at this point I don’t think that’s going to happen anytime soon. I’ve checked into this and there’s no ongoing operating system project right now in terms of a brand new operating system for the iPad – there is no work being done to consumerize macOs on the iPad.

Gurman says Apple seems set to continue to make piecemeal changes over time to bring iPad capabilities closer to those of the Mac but is showing no signs of full convergence.

He reiterated that work on a long-rumored giant-sized folding iPad continues.

The other thing I want to say is that I’m really excited for this potential of Project J312. That is, this giant 19-inch, 20-inch foldable iPad. And it’s basically like a gigantic iPhone Duo with no display on the outside.

However, he says there’s no guarantee it will ever launch, saying the timings have already slipped from 2027 through 2028 to 2029, but both cost and yield issues may still kill the project. If it does launch, it’s expected to cost anywhere from $3,000 to $5,000.

Luca Guadagnino Apologizes for Controversial Costume Design Comments But Defends Working With Fashion Designers: They’re More ‘in Touch With the Pulse of How We Live’

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Luca Guadagnino apologized to costume designers at the Marrakech Film Festival on Wednesday, a year after drawing backlash for comments he made about his preference for working with fashion designers on his films. However, the Italian filmmaker doubled down on his admiration for his frequent collaborator Jonathan Anderson, who most recently designed the costumes for his upcoming Silicon Valley drama “Artificial.”

“I apologize to costume designers,” Guadagnino said during a conversation at the festival. “There are so many amazing costume designers that I admire so greatly through the history of cinema and contemporary history.” Related Stories Emma Stone Ambushes Druski’s ‘SNL’ Monologue in Adorable Fashion ‘SNL’: Druski Dons Whiteface as Elizabeth Holmes in ‘You Can See Everything’ Parody

The controversy dates back to last November, when Guadagnino, speaking with fashion journalist Tim Blanks at the Business of Fashion’s annual VOICES conference, suggested that traditional costume designers were more concerned with the garments themselves than the bodies inhabiting them.

His comments drew the ire of several prominent costume designers, including Oscar winners Jenny Beavan (“Mad Max: Fury Road,” “Cruella”) and Kate Hawley (“Frankenstein”), who took to the comments section of Business of Fashion’s Instagram post to dispute his characterization of their craft. Beavan wrote at the time, “I can only add I am thankful he will never want to work with me!”

In Marrakech, Guadagnino further explained his stance. “It’s just that I like to be in conversation with people who have an immediate touch with the pulse of how we live,” he said.

Guadagnino singled out Anderson, who, in addition to serving as creative director of Dior, has designed the costumes for three of the filmmaker’s movies: “Challengers,” “Queer” and “Artificial.” “I know he’s someone who sees life in front of him and cares about it,” Guadagnino said of Anderson. “It doesn’t deal with fashion, per se.”

The pair’s latest collaboration, “Artificial,” chronicles the rise of OpenAI and the dramatic ousting and subsequent reinstatement of its CEO Sam Altman in November 2023. The film stars Andrew Garfield as Altman, Yura Borisov as OpenAI co-founder Ilya Sutskever and Ike Barinholtz as Elon Musk.

“I cannot think of anything more far from an idea of fashion or sophistication or form than Silicon Valley and tech bros,” Guadagnino said. “They actually are gross the way they present themselves.”

The filmmaker went on to examine how the sartorial codes of the tech industry reflect a broader shift in how the world’s wealthiest and most powerful people communicate their status. “[Anderson] was so smart in understanding every one of these characters and truly never had the temptation of painting something in a better color,” Guadagnino said.

“I believe that’s also a language of power,” he continued. “The flip-flops of Zuckerberg are a comfortable item he wears, but also a way for him to indicate his position toward your position.”

“Artificial,” which had its world premiere at the New York Film Festival in October, will screen at Marrakech Film Festival on Nov. 26. The film will be released by Neon in select theaters on Christmas Day before expanding nationwide on Jan. 8.

Amber fossils reveal how grasshoppers may have walked on water 100 million years ago

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Walking on water represents one of the most striking modes of locomotion in the natural world and plays a crucial role in animals' colonization of ecological niches at the water surface. However, insects specialized for this locomotion (obligate surface dwellers) belong to only two groups: the living Gerromorpha (Hemiptera) and extinct Chresmodidae (stem group of Polyneoptera). Elucidating how extinct insects moved on the water surface has proven highly challenging, and the adaptive evolution of different insect lineages to ecological niches at the water surface remains poorly understood.

Robert Pattinson was just announced as the next James Bond, and the internet has spent the days since joking that no Hollywood role is safe from him. I thought those jokes had about run their course until Ralph Fiennes weighed in. The actor who terrified an entire generation of Harry Potter fans as Lord Voldemort was asked who should take over the role in HBO's upcoming television series, and his answer was so dry that I almost missed how funny it was.

Fiennes was speaking with the Associated Press at the New York premiere of The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping on Nov. 16. The conversation turned to the new book-to-screen adaptation of the Harry Potter novels and whether he'd consider returning as He Who Must Not Be Named. Fiennes indicated he'd had his fill of the character. As for who should put on the robes next, he offered one name:

Maybe they'll offer it to Robert Pattinson.

I love how little effort went into that joke. The Red Dragon veteran actor didn't need to explain it, either. Following Pattinson's Bond casting, social media has been having a field day with the idea that he's taking every acting job. One popular variation suggests that artificial intelligence isn't coming for anyone's job because Pattinson will get there first. The OG Potter villain apparently decided to throw one more highly coveted part onto the pile.

Of course, there's another reason his suggestion is particularly good, and it involves a Harry Potter movie I still have a hard time watching without getting a little upset. Pattinson played Cedric Diggory in 2005's Harry Potter and the Goblet of Fire, the same film that introduced Fiennes as the fully resurrected Voldemort. Lufthansa Turns Airplane Etiquette Into Clothing

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Lufthansa is turning airplane etiquette into a clothing collection, including a new shirt reminding passengers that there is no need to stand up immediately after landing. I like the idea and agree with much of the advice, though standing up is not necessarily the problem…pushing past everyone else is.

Lufthansa has announced that four items from its Travel Etiquette Collection will go on sale through the WorldShop beginning November 27, 2026.

The collection debuted in August and was not originally intended for sale. Lufthansa says the response prompted a retail edition, including a new shirt based on the most popular suggestion from its social media community.

Each design is limited to 250 individually numbered pieces. Lufthansa will donate its licensing proceeds to help alliance, the Lufthansa Group’s aid organization (that is the licensing revenue, rather than necessarily the entire purchase price).

Prices were not included in the announcement.

First off, I love these…simple yet stylish.

As to the messaging itself, I understand why the shirt appeals to people. We have all watched passengers spring up as though standing in the aisle will somehow make the jet bridge attach faster.

And if the aircraft is still moving or the seatbelt sign remains illuminated, it should go without saying that you stay seated.

But once the aircraft is parked and passengers are permitted to get up, I have no objection to someone standing. After several hours in an economy class seat, stretching your legs is perfectly understandable. An aisle passenger can also retrieve a bag and be ready to leave when the rows ahead have cleared.

The problem is the person who charges forward from row 28 and wedges himself beside row 19, apparently convinced that everyone else is sitting there for his convenience.

Stand if you want. Just wait your turn to leave.

(The only exception I would make are the circumstances in which the crew asks passengers to remain seated so travelers with tight connections can get off first. That requires everyone to cooperate, including those of us who might otherwise prefer to stand…but I also have a problem with the “free riders” in those situations…another issue for another day.)

My favorite in this collection is the “Recline Responsibly” message, which strikes me as common sense, but something so many passengers are so rude about.

If an airline installs a reclining seat, passengers should generally be able to use that feature. But look behind you first and recline slowly. Giving the person behind you a moment to adjust a laptop or move a drink costs you very little.

During meal service, the seat should be upright, but other than that, recline away…just gently.

We spend so much time arguing about whether reclining is acceptable that we sometimes overlook how much the manner of doing it matters. A slow adjustment is very different from throwing your seat back without warning.

Lufthansa will begin selling four limited-edition etiquette items on November 27. I like the collection, particularly the reminder to recline responsibly. But I can’t say I’ll be up late waiting to buy it…how about you?

The Tech Sell-Off Is Creating the Best Buying Window in 2 Years. Here’s What I’m Buying.

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The recent tech rout didn't care which businesses were great and which weren't, and that's exactly why I'm buying two of the best.

By Micah Zimmerman – Nov 25, 2026 at 8:38AM EST

If you've checked your portfolio lately, I'm sorry. I looked at mine, too.

The Nasdaq had its worst day since April 2025 on Nov. 5, and things got worse after Nvidia (NVDA -5.86%) said on Nov. 12 that Jensen Huang had resigned as CEO. Not even Nvidia's better-than-expected earnings could stop the slide. By Nov. 20, all three major averages were in correction, the Nasdaq was negative for 2026, and the Nasdaq and Dow had just logged their worst week in more than six years.

Then the scenery changed. Nvidia's board unanimously reinstated Huang, who said, "I can't wait to get back to work." Nvidia shares jumped 31% for their best day since 2003, and the Nasdaq posted its best day since April 2025. The relief faded fast, as Nvidia gave back about 7% in the next session and pulled other chip stocks down with it.

Huang's return answered one question, not the bigger one: Is AI spending slowing? Democratic midterm wins and signs that big tech may rein in spending have raised doubts about the AI build-out, and traders have been bracing for the Federal Reserve to raise interest rates in December. Nvidia's board also says its investigation will proceed to conclusion.

I can't call the bottom, because market timing rarely works. But in the latest sell-off, the market sold the great businesses right along with the shaky ones. Here are the two I'm buying.

ASML (ASML -3.45%) is the only company in the world that makes extreme ultraviolet lithography machines, the tools Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing uses to print the most advanced chips. That's about as wide an economic moat as you'll find in tech.

ASML got whipsawed with the rest of the chip group, once dropping 5.8% in a single session as chip stocks sold off across Asia and Europe. Even after the bounce, shares closed Nov. 23 about 31% below their 52-week high.

The selling assumes chipmakers will pull back. ASML's customers say otherwise. "Our customers continue to increase their short- and medium-term equipment demand forecasts with us," CEO Christophe Fouquet said last month, adding that "some customers are expressing an interest in placing orders with us for 2029 and even 2030." He added, "our supply as currently planned for both 2027 and 2028 will not fully meet the demand of our customers."

China is the risk I'm watching. In mid-November, the Commerce Department moved to close a loophole that let Chinese firms rent Nvidia computing power abroad, and ASML still expects China to be about 20% of sales.

Shopify (SHOP -1.48%) had nothing to do with Nvidia's drama, yet it fell 4% in a single session during the sell-off. Even after the rebound, it closed Nov. 23 about 26% below its 52-week high. With a P/E ratio near 80, it's exactly the kind of stock that gets hit when rate-hike fears grow.

The business looks nothing like the stock. Revenue grew 32% last quarter, and CFO Jeff Hoffmeister said, "We extended our track record of consistent execution, with 32% revenue growth and a 22% free cash flow margin." Its guidance calls for revenue growth in the "low thirties" this quarter, and President Harley Finkelstein said, "I do think this is the first holiday season where AI-assisted shopping capabilities will become mainstream."

Recent shopper news has been encouraging. Walmart (WMT +2.11%) raised its full-year outlook. Target (TGT +4.68%) posted its best sales growth in more than five years. The test comes soon: Shopify usually reports its merchants' Black Friday-Cyber Monday sales the Tuesday after Cyber Monday. A weak number could push the stock lower. Still, as Finkelstein put it after Shopify's earnings-day drop, "we're going to keep building for the long term and ignore the noise day to day."

That's my plan, too. In recent weeks, the market panicked over one set of headlines and cheered another. ASML and Shopify kept growing through both, and I'll take their shares at a discount.

The interesting Mason Rudolph comments that came right before Aaron Rodgers’ shock benching

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Mike McCarthy may have tipped his hand more than a week before he moved on from Aaron Rodgers in an absolute stunner.

Longtime NFL reporter Charles Robinson re-posted a clip after the news broke of Rodgers' benching of Mike Tirico and Cris Collinsworth discussing backup Mason Rudolph after he replaced Rodgers at the end of the team's blowout "Sunday Night Football" loss to the Bengals on Nov. 15.

"Kind of an interesting deal in Pittsburgh as we talked to Mike McCarthy last night," Tirico said late in the fourth quarter. "They're a roster with four quarterbacks, people were looking for people at the trade deadline. No real movement of the quarterbacks here. Aaron says it's his last year, Mason Rudolph has been around the Steelers facility for quite some time, Will Howard, Drew Allar, Penn Stater, drafted. Mike McCarthy said, 'I've got some pretty good quarterbacks here.'"

Collinsworth followed: "He even brought up the idea that hey, everybody assumes we're going to take a quarterback with Aaron retiring. We've got pretty good quarterbacks in here, as if, 'I don't know about that. I want one of these young quarterbacks or do I want to take one of these guys that we've had a full year to develop here and let them play?' If it falls to them in the right way, they probably will. If not, I think they just roll out Mason Rudolph. They've been impressed."

Robinson initially posted the clip stating that it shows why teams should not let coaches dictate teams' quarterback plans and why he believes McCarthy said something of note in the production meeting, citing that the 31-year-old Rudolph is not a viable long-term option.

Tuesday's news made that segment raise even more eyebrows.

"Absolutely time to revisit this #Steelers clip. Given what just happened with Rodgers, I refuse to believe Mike McCarthy didn't say something in the production meeting," he tweeted.

"There's no way. The Rudolph stuff is wildly on the nose of where we are right now with Rodgers getting benched."

Production meetings happen between coaches and announcers before games, providing a mix of on- and off-the-record details to help the broadcasters prepare for the game.

That Tirico and Collinsworth shared the Rudolph discussion indicated that McCarthy did not mind it getting out there, and it's certainly notable that Rudolph is now starting Friday.

This could potentially be a seven-game audition for Rudolph — and possibly Howard and/or Allar — to show they should receive the chance to start next season versus drafting a quarterback early.

The Steelers always knew this would be Rodgers' last season and this upcoming quarterback draft class has depth, although there are questions about how good some of the options are.

Presumed first-rounders like Arch Manning and Dante Moore have only seen their stock fall, and the Steelers currently own the 21st pick in the 2027 NFL Draft.

It's hard to envision a team with contending ambitions like the Steelers would truly believe he gives them their best chance to win in 2027.

There are also questions about the viability of Howard, a 2025 sixth-rounder, but Allar provides a potentially intriguing option.
