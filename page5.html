The LIT price took a hit on yesterday’s exploit news, dropping more than 20% to $2.50. However, the team’s response and USDC fund recovery have seen the price recover to just under $3, surging +10% over the past 24 hours.

On November 16, 2026, Lighter, the high-performance decentralized perpetuals exchange that settles on Ethereum with zero-knowledge proofs, activated Guardian mode and paused all withdrawals and new pool creation.

On-chain data showed roughly $10.39M in USDC leaving the platform’s Ethereum settlement contract in a single withdrawal. Some researchers put the total impact closer to $15.5M across two large market-maker accounts. The attacker used a leaked API key tied to a trader’s account. The root cause was insecure custom signing code that reused cryptographic nonces, allowing the attacker to reconstruct the private key from public signatures.

With the key, the attacker created a public pool from the compromised account, acquired most of its shares, moved the victim’s USDC into the pool, and redeemed it. Importantly, Lighter’s core protocol and smart contracts were never compromised; this was an account-level key compromise, not a smart-contract exploit. Funds were rapidly swapped into ETH, with portions routed to Hyperliquid. LIT initially dropped by 20% on the news before buyers stepped in.

BREAKING: Hackers steal nearly $15.5 MILLION from Lighter using leaked API keys.$LIT crashed 18% on the news. pic.twitter.com/Rf2PsTSSRd

Lighter moved fast. Within hours, the team publicly confirmed the cause, named the attacker wallet, and made clear that the protocol itself remained intact. The Security Council recovered approximately $5M in USDC and returned it to the affected trader. Guardian mode was deactivated the same day and withdrawals resumed. New safeguards followed immediately: wallet signatures are now required for transfers into public pools, API key signatures are non-public, IP allowlists are planned, per-key permission limits are in place, and automated monitoring for nonce reuse is active.

Users running custom or third-party signing software were strongly advised to rotate keys and switch to Lighter’s official SDKs. The message was clear: the platform treated the incident as a user-key hygiene issue and hardened the surrounding controls without ever having a core vulnerability.

That combination of transparent communication, partial recovery, rapid resumption of operations, and concrete security upgrades is exactly the kind of response that historically flips sentiment from fear to “they handled it cleanly.”

Price is now reclaiming short-term moving averages while volume has expanded on the green candles, classic signs of aggressive dip-buying rather than distribution. On the higher timeframe, the broader structure remains constructive: a multi-month base that previously supported a run toward the $5+ region is still intact.

Cillian Murphy's 28 Years Later: Part III Will Only Happen Under One Condition, Says Director Danny Boyle

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Are we going to see "28 Years Later: Part III?" Director Danny Boyle is certainly interested in doing it and Alex Garland has written a script. The problem? Money. This year's "28 Years Later: The Bone Temple" finally delivered the moment fans have been waiting for, bringing back Cillian Murphy as Jim. It was a small appearance teeing up something much larger in a presumed third installment. As Boyle recently explained, that movie is only going to happen if they can find the money.

Speaking recently with Deadline during an event for his new movie "Ink," Boyle's tabloid media origin story that will make you feel sick, the Oscar-winning director addressed the status of the third and presumably final entry in the "28" franchise. "There is a plan for a third part of '28 Years Later.' Alex has written a fantastic script," Boyle said. "We gotta raise the money to make it."

Sony Pictures produced both "28 Years Later" and "The Bone Temple." And while "28 Years Later" killed it at the box office, the sequel didn't. That's why Sony isn't eager to give it the green light. Speaking further, Boyle said that the third installment would bring the whole franchise to a close:

"[Cillian Murphy]'s up for it, and it's a fantastic part. Alex has written this fantastic part for him. Those of you who saw 'The Bone Temple,' the second part to '28 Years Later,' he's teased at the end of that. The third film does complete the story in a beautiful, mathematically perfect way, in which the circle is closed."

Creatively speaking, critics and audiences generally agreed that both of the recent "28" movies were damn good. But Hollywood is a business and money became an issue pretty quickly. "28 Years Later" made $151 million against a budget in the $60 million range. That was good enough. "28 Years Later: The Bone Temple" flopped at the box office though, bringing in just $58.5 million worldwide on a similar budget. Unfortunately, the math doesn't math for anyone who wants to make money in this business.

That's why "28 Years Later: Part III" probably won't happen. Admittedly, "probably" is doing some heavy lifting there. Cillian Murphy is an Oscar-winning actor for his work in "Oppenheimer," Danny Boyle is an Oscar-winning director, and Alex Garland is an Oscar-nominated screenwriter. It's a powerhouse team. That makes it unlikely but not impossible.

The most realistic path to getting it made would be partnering with a streaming service. Netflix is releasing Boyle's new movie "Ink" and the streamer also has a partnership with Sony. That would be the logical choice. But if Boyle and Garland want it as a more traditional theatrical release? That's where it gets more complicated and far less likely.

As for where it all went wrong? The bonkers ending in "28 Years Later" left audiences divided, for one. There was also a very short, roughly six-month gap between installments, which seemingly hurt more than it helped. But playing Monday morning quarterback doesn't help now. It's really about Boyle and co. getting creative to see if they can cobble together the money to bring this story to a close.

Economic Headwinds Drag Homebuilder Confidence to New Low

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Homebuilder confidence in November’s National Association of Home Builders (NAHB)/Wells Fargo Housing Market Index (HMI) report slumped four points to 25, a new 14- year low amid low consumer sentiment and significant pressure on the housing sector.

Last month’s homebuilder confidence score, a measly 29 points, was already a 14-year low. This month’s new slump sends homebuilder confidence even further back in time to April 2012.

NAHB blamed a variety of causes, the most important of which were “high mortgage rates, ongoing inflation pressure, elevated energy and material costs, (and) rising labor shortages and hesitant buyers.”

“Builders are confronting multiple challenges in today’s economy,” said NAHB Chairman Bill Owens in a statement. “Stepped-up immigration enforcement is keeping legal workers off job sites, deepening labor shortages and driving up costs. Higher fuel prices are prompting subcontractors to raise their rates sharply, and with fewer buyers in the market, more builders are scaling back construction.”

Robert Dietz, NAHB chief economist, focused on the housing market’s supply-side challenges, homing in on “building material costs pushed higher by tariffs, an ongoing shortage of construction workers and excessive regulations that undermine housing affordability.”

Dietz also added that “(m)ortgage rates above 7%, softer consumer confidence and an uncertain economic outlook are holding buyers back.”

The NAHB/Wells Fargo HMI, an ongoing monthly survey of more than 40 years, asks builders to rate their perceptions of current single-family home sales, potential single-family home sales in the next six months and prospective buyer traffic. Builders’ perceptions on each of these three component indices is scaled onto a range of zero to 100, which is then used to calculate a general homebuilder confidence level.

The three component indices this month were, unsurprisingly, also down. Current sales conditions dropped six points to 28, while future sales conditions dropped two points to 30.

Prospective buyer traffic ticked down two points to 16, another new low for this report. The last time prospective buyer traffic reached these troughs was 15 years ago (excluding a brief period at the start of the pandemic in 2020).

The NAHB/Wells Fargo HMI this month also reported that 46% of builders reduced prices, up 3% from last month. This is the highest share of builders cutting prices in the post-Covid period.

Almost seven-in-10 (68%) builders offered incentives, also up 3%, representing a post-Covid high. November 2026 is now the 20th consecutive month in which at least 60% of builders utilized sales incentives.

Regional HMI scores were similarly gloomy. All four regions experienced declines, but the Northeast and the Midwest experienced the largest declines point-wise at four and five points, respectively. The Northeast now sits at 34 points, the Midwest at 35, and the South and West both tumbled three points to 27 and 23 points, respectively.

The Best Coffee-Table Books to Gift Your Beautiful, Discerning Friends

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Every product on this page was chosen by a Harper's BAZAAR editor. We may earn commission on some of the items you choose to buy.

These books will please everyone on your gift list this year

Every year holidays come around, and a slight panic sets in at the thought of finding gifts that the people you love will get excited about—and that will make them feel seen. The real trick is to keep a list throughout the year of everything you see that reminds you of this person or that person. But because sometimes you don’t write things down because you’re “sure you’ll remember,” and because even if you do, we need all the help we can get in discovering cool things, we’ve put together a list of our favorite coffee-table books to give (and to receive, if we’re being honest) this year.

From fashion monographs to punk rock album covers to a guide to medieval wellness, we promise that we’ve found something that will thrill even your most cynical friend.

Richard Avedon was undoubtedly one of the greatest photographers of the 20th century and this collection gathers his work for Harper’s Bazaar from 1944 to 1965. As the world experienced generation-defining shifts, Avedon was zeroed in on his style, creating indelible images that would go on to change fashion. Bonus? It pairs perfectly with Ron Howard’s documentary on the photographer, also titled simply Avedon.

This massive pink tome, assembled by Coppola herself from her own collection of photographs, scripts, collages, and all manner of feminine detritus, offers a revealing and inspirational look at the director’s process and world-building.

With wit and humor, medievalist Larisa Grollemond sheds light on the way humans lived—and thrived(?)—during the middle ages. Is it true they never bathed? Did they have practice birth control? Were witches in fact, doctors? This books holds the answers.

Raymond Pettibon may now be a certified blue-chip artist, but he began his career in the 1970s drawing flyers and record covers for punk bands in Southen California like Minutemen and Black Flag. (He also designed their iconic four-bar logo, which has now been meme-fied.) This book collects his musical body of work, which also includes the Foo Fighters and Lana del Rey—that’s his work on the cover of Norman Fucking Rockwell.

The elusive Martin Margiela may very well be the designer that has most influenced contemporary fashion. Nowhere is that more evident than in this book that looks at the time when the Belgian designer was at the helm of Hermés. The enduring appeal of the collections he created from 1997 to 2002 would go on to set the tone for the whole quiet luxury moment we’ve been living under—except better.

Jeff Goodwin was the makeup artist for David Lynch’s iconic film Blue Velvet. His personal trove of Polaroid photos capture impromptu behind-the-scenes moments and reveal how he refined the makeup looks; they’re accompanied by his own memories of the filming experience.

How many people’s fashion obsessions began as they endlessly dressed and undressed their dolls—be they Barbie, Bratz, or even American Girl dolls? This tome looks at the symbiotic relationship between dolls and real-life grown-up fashion, zeroing in on the works of designers like Comme des Garçons’ Rei Kawakubo and Viktor & Rolf’s Viktor Horsting and Rolf Snoeren, among others.

This is a book designed to inspire. For “Kitchen Piece,” Ono suggests one “hangs a canvas on a wall” and “throws all the leftovers you have” on it, with the caveat that “you may prepare special food for the piece.” After spending time with Ono’s instructions, one realizes there is art to be found literally everywhere.

This is the only book about Carolyn Bessette Kennedy that one may ever need. Sunita Kumar Nair talks to CBK’s friends and, through a carefully curated edit of images, breaks down her style. She also reveals the true essence of the person behind all those infinitely rebloggable images.

Past Continuous is the name of an exhibit at the Metropolitan Museum of Art in New York that brings together the world of Lee Krasner and her husband Jackson Pollock and looks at their career arcs in tandem. It begins with a selection of their paintings before the pair met, and traces their influences, taking a closer look at how their presences wove in and out of each other’s work.

This tome celebrates America’s 250th anniversary through over 200 pages filled with iconic images that have defined the country throughout its history, from images of cowboys wearing their worn-in Levi’s and immigrants landing at Ellis Island to Martin Luther King and Elvis Presley.

Diane Arbus’s unflinching photographs captured humanity in all its beautiful and exacting rawness. Documents tells the history of her short-lived career through essays, criticism, and other ephemera from 1967 through the present day; it’s a comprehensive guide to understanding her work.

Glen Luchford photographed a few of Prada’s most iconic ad campaigns of the 1990s. This book brings them all together along with alternate shots and test shots, and is an essential for both Prada aficionados and lovers of the decade.

Created in 1909 by Arthur E. Waite and Pamela Colman Smith in London, the appropriately titled Waite Smith tarot deck is the most popular deck in the world. This book looks at its history, uncovering the details and meaning of each individual card—and it comes with its own set of cards.

This year, the Fashion Museum Antwerp is celebrating its 40th anniversary with an exhibition dedicated to the “Antwerp Six,” the name the fashion media gave to a group of designers—Dries van Noten, Ann Demeulemeeter, Marina Yee, Dirk Bikkembergs, Walter Van Beirendonck, and Dirk Van Seene—that arrived in London in the mid ’80s fresh after their graduation from the Royal Academy of Fine Art. For those who can’t make it to Belgium to see the show, the book may prove to be the next best thing, chock full of photographs, illustrations, newspaper clippings, and essays. They may not have all become household names, but their influence can still be felt on the runways today.

Brianna Capozzi’s unique view of womanhood has made her one of the most sought-after photographers in fashion. Her latest monograph features a mix of magazine shots of famous singers and actors with images of impromptu shoots staged with her best friends. The result is a visual shot of adrenaline and a reminder that girls—well, women—just wanna have fun!
