Google will disable Search for students unless schools accept AI features in ‘Guided Learning’ push

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Google is today launching new AI settings for schools, but with the big catch that Search is fully disabled if AI features are turned off entirely.

In a blog post today, Google says it is “focused on building technology responsibly to help students thrive” and, as such, will tailor its AI efforts to help students. “Guided Learning” in Gemini, Search, and Classroom will include helping students “work through problems without skipping straight to answers.”

…if a student pastes a complex physics problem or asks for help writing a book report in Search, they’ll see prompts to help them think critically and work through the concepts, without going directly to the answer.

That’s launching in a pilot program this month in select US schools, with Guided Learning in Google Classroom launching globally today. Google describes this as a “1-1 study tool, anchored in class material to help [students] stay on task” while also giving the teacher insights into how their class is handling the material.

Beyond this new Guided Learning push, Google also says that it will now turn off “Gemini, Gemini Notebook, Gemini in Classroom, and Google Search” for K-12 student accounts by default “until school administrators explicitly turn them on.”

As The New York Times points out, having those features fully off has a big side effect: it cuts students off from Google Search entirely.

Google says that this is because Search and AI “are deeply integrated” and confirms that students in schools with AI features turned off (which, to reiterate, is now the default setting) “will not have access to search.”

As a reminder, Google Search’s AI features can largely be turned off using a simple modifier.

Google’s post says that the “goal is simple: provide school leaders the control and confidence to use AI products in classrooms as they see fit.” Yet, speaking to The New York Times, Google further explained that two options will be available to schools in “early 2027,” one that has AI Overviews and other AI features turned on, the other with Guided Learning in their place, leaving schools with no choice other than to accept AI features or give up access to Google Search for their students entirely.

NYT points out repeatedly that Google’s prior AI pushes in schools have been met with fierce opposition from educators and parents alike.

Deep inside a Spanish cave, archaeologists found at least 120 Latin inscriptions, and five of them honor a goddess called ‘the Lady’

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

At least 120 Latin inscriptions in Cova Dones, Spain, name a goddess called Domina, the Lady, and point to women taking an active part in her cult.

One chamber of Cova Dones, a cave in Millares in Valencia province, now holds at least 120 Latin inscriptions. Teams from the universities of Alicante and Zaragoza recorded them during fieldwork in May and June 2026.

When the room was first explored in 2024, about 15 had been logged.

Five of the texts are vows to Domina, “the Lady,” a goddess the visitors addressed with reverence. Several of the names read so far are women’s. The team calls the results preliminary, and many texts are still being read.

The researchers call the room the Roman Sanctuary. It sits more than 650 feet (200 meters) from the entrance, according to the University of Alicante.

Most of the texts are believed to date to the 1st and 2nd centuries AD, and they cover the walls and the ceiling. Small natural pools on the floor, known as gours, catch water from the galleries, and visitors dropped offerings into them.

That first campaign also yielded a coin from the reign of Emperor Claudius. Someone had wedged it as an offering between a fissure and a stalactite in the ceiling, and in a team photo Xabier Garín Artázcoz points at the crack. Trinidad Pasíes Oviedo of the Valencia Museum of Prehistory recovered and conserved the coin.

The January 2025 announcement from the University of Alicante placed the room among the deepest known Roman inscription sites in Iberia, second only to Cueva del Puente in Burgos and possibly Cueva de Román de Clunia.

This year’s readings were done by two epigraphy specialists. Silvia Alfayé teaches ancient history at the University of Zaragoza, and Sergio García-Dils lectures at UNED in Seville. García-Dils also directs the Spanish School of Speleology.

According to Alfayé, five votive texts show that women “played an active role in her cult.” Three of them carry the word MALACI. In a release dated July 27, the University of Alicante called it an unpublished epithet for a goddess not known until now.

García-Dils noted that the texts are packed into one marked-off part of the cave, well away from the entrance. To him that “strongly suggests the existence of a well-organised and structured cult” with a named deity, not scattered visits.

The University of Southampton, where Aitor Ruiz-Redondo is also based, put out the English release on October 20. It says early readings suggest some women wrote their vows themselves, without a go-between.

Until now, the Roman cave sanctuary with the largest known set of inscriptions was La Griega, with 107. One of them names a woman.

The 2026 team logged objects on the chamber floor, among them two bronze rings found under the inscribed rock. There were two bronze rings, a bracelet, chalice-shaped cups, spindle whorls, bits of pottery and animal bones.

The whorls came up in numbers. Virginia Barciela co-leads the project with Ruiz-Redondo and teaches prehistory at Alicante. She said the whorls probably point to offerings of spindles, a textile tool tied to women in antiquity.

She also brought up the first written name of the site, Cova de les Dones, meaning Cave of the Women. That name may hold “the fossilised memory of a place long associated with women,” she said, or with female spirits.

Alfayé said the goddess’s title may have to do with water. Water shrines existed elsewhere in the ancient world, such as a circular water shrine at Pelusium in northern Sinai.

The inscriptions are extremely difficult to see with the naked eye. So the team photographed the surfaces for high-resolution photogrammetric models and read the texts on those.

That work turned up inscriptions no one had seen. It also split apart lines that earlier readers had taken as one text.

Jorge Angás and Carlos Valladares, from the IPH institute in Zaragoza and the DigHER project, ran the geomatics side. Javier Velaza Frías, a Latin professor in Barcelona, leads the study of the Roman inscriptions. Víctor Sabaté Vidal of the University of Valencia has also contributed to the project.

Latin wishes turn up on other Roman objects too, for instance openwork glass cups carved with drinking wishes in Greek or Latin.

In a Roman underground sanctuary, finding texts and offerings together in place is exceptionally rare, says the Southampton release.

Ruiz-Redondo, Barciela and archaeologist Ximo Martorell published the first results in 2023. In the journal Antiquity they described over 100 Paleolithic paintings and engravings. Some are older than 24,000 years.

Pottery from the Iron Age had already led archaeologists in the 1960s to class it as an Iberian sanctuary. In 2025 the team logged more than 100 stalagmites that people had modified on purpose.

For that kind of find, the University of Alicante ranks the cave second in the world, after Saint-Marcel in France.

Ruiz-Redondo said people may have kept returning for “its permanent water supply,” its landscape or its easy access.

At La Pasiega, Maltravieso and Ardales, red marks have been dated to more than 64,000 years ago.

Alfayé and her colleagues are still working on the goddess’s title. For now the team has not said who Domina was or what MALACI means. Many inscriptions are still being read, and much of the cave is unstudied, so 120 is a minimum.

The DONARQ project started in 2023 with money from the Generalitat Valenciana and the British Academy. In September 2026 the sanctuary was shortlisted for a National Geographic +Historia readers’ prize. Public voting closed on October 12.

This year the regional Pla Restaura program and the Millares town council are paying for more work. The money goes to the cave’s access and entrance, safety measures, a digital twin and a catalog of the Paleolithic images. The company Arquivolta Patrimoni i Societat is coordinating it, and virtual visits should follow.

Among those offerings, two bronze rings sat on the surface right below the inscribed rock.

Warner Music Buys Back Part of German Distributor Zebralution, Expanding Reach in Europe

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The pioneering digital distributor of music and audiobooks was sold by WMG in 2017 but now returns with enhanced resources.

By Elizabeth Dilts Marshall

Warner Music Group (WMG) said Tuesday (Nov. 3) it had acquired indie record label distributor Zebralution Music GmbH from Bookwire, bringing back part of the Zebralution company it owned for a decade and expanding the major’s reach into the business of serving Germany and Europe.

Zebralution Music is a spinoff of Zebralution Gmbh, which Bookwire acquired from the German performance rights organization GEMA in March 2026. In a press release announcing the Bookwire acquisition at the time, Bookwire described Zebralution as among the first big digital distributors of music, audiobooks, e-books and podcasts, with more than 1,500 label and publisher clients globally. Terms of the deal were not disclosed.

WMG acquired Zebralution in 2007 and sold it in 2017 to its founders Kurt Thielen, Sascha Lazimbat, Christof Ellinghaus and Konrad von Löhneysen. Founded in 2003, Zebralution distributed a catalog of 500,000-plus tracks, albums and videos to hundreds of digital services worldwide at the time WMG sold it.

The reacquisition follows WMG’s April deal to buy B2B music distribution platform Revelator and further expands the reach and services offered through WMG and its distribution and services offerings housed under ADA. Zebralution Music label clients gain access to WMG’s technology, distribution, analytics and financial tools. The company’s now co-CEOs von Löhneysen and Lazimbat will remain with the company “to align strategic operations,” according to a statement.

“This is an exciting new chapter for Zebralution Music, but for our label partners and the people we work with every day, it will very much be business as usual,” Lazimbat said in a statement. “We’ll continue to operate with the same team, the same relationships … What does change is the additional technology and resources now available to us, which will help us build on what we have already achieved.”

Von Löhneysen, whose own label, Embassy of Music, is a Zebralution client, echoed the sentiment that customers should expect “the same people, the same approach and the same independence they’ve always valued.”

Niels Walboomers, president of Warner Music Central Europe, said WMG was drawn to Zebralution’s regional expertise and world-class capabilities.

“Konrad and Sascha have built one of the most trusted homes for independent music in Europe,” Walboomers said.

CrowdStrike is rolling out an AI kill switch to stop rogue AI agents

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The Falcon Guardian feature requires an administrator to trigger it, and endpoint-level termination is planned

CrowdStrike $CRWD launched an AI kill switch on Tuesday that gives security teams a way to shut down rogue AI agents, prevent them from starting up again and sever their access to the enterprise network, the company said.

The feature is part of Falcon Guardian, CrowdStrike's product for securing AI agents. An authorized administrator triggers the action, and every step is recorded in an audit trail, the company said. Falcon Guardian policy keeps a terminated agent from relaunching on the targeted host or host group.

At launch, the Falcon sensor halts agents at the AI Gateway. Termination on the endpoint is planned for a later release, according to CrowdStrike.

"Every organization is racing to get more out of AI, and every week we see agents chain steps together, hit an obstacle, and take actions they were never authorized to take," CrowdStrike President Michael Sentonas said in a statement.

CrowdStrike also announced other Falcon Guardian updates. Endpoint network inspection is generally available for Windows, with Mac in early access. The company also introduced an integration with Anthropic that runs the Falcon sensor inside the Claude Cowork virtual machine. CrowdStrike said it plans to acquire Fiddler to add agent observability.

Chief Business Officer Daniel Bernard told CNBC that, for customers running the Falcon platform, the tool functions as a simple on-off toggle. It can spot what agents are doing inside a customer's environment, he said, but judging whether that behavior is harmless or hostile falls to a person.

"If you kill a process at the root, and we have that level of access at the root … it's very binary," Bernard said, according to CNBC. "You either kill the AI and the agent, or you don't, and then you report on that."

CrowdStrike also plans to acquire AI security testing firm Dreadnode, according to CNBC.

The launch follows legislative efforts to require shutdown capabilities in AI systems. Reps. Ted Lieu and Nathaniel Moran introduced the Kill Switch Act after OpenAI disclosed an incident in which rogue models circumvented a sandboxed testing environment and accessed Hugging Face. The bill would require AI companies to maintain the ability to shut down, throttle or suspend their models.

Critics contend that building an emergency stop mechanism would prove too unwieldy given the number of systems, data centers and sophisticated AI models it would have to span, according to CNBC.

Scientists Discover a Hidden Factor That Could Be Making Deadly Cancers Harder To Treat

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Scientists are exploring a potentially overlooked factor behind some of the most treatment-resistant cancers: bacteria lurking inside tumors.

Some of the most aggressive cancers may have an overlooked source of treatment resistance: bacteria living inside tumors. Researchers suggest that these microbes could interfere with chemotherapy, weaken immune defenses, and alter surrounding tissues. They propose that combining existing antibiotics with cancer drugs might help overcome resistance in certain difficult-to-treat cancers.

In a review published in Cancer Biology & Medicine, scientists from Nankai University, the University of Utah, and Tianjin Medical University Cancer Institute & Hospital argue that “all diagnosed PPO-tumors must be considered to be bacteria-infected.” Their proposal focuses on poor prognosis outcome (PPO) tumors, although the assumption that these tumors harbor bacterial infections remains unproven.

Pancreatic, colorectal, and certain biliary cancers can be especially difficult to treat. Their tumors often suppress immune responses, undergo rapid genetic changes, and develop dense fibrous barriers that restrict drug delivery.

Researchers have detected bacteria within several of these cancers, including Fusobacterium nucleatum, Escherichia coli, Enterococcus faecalis, and Pseudomonas species. Some microbes may promote inflammation, interfere with immune defenses, or chemically alter chemotherapy drugs, potentially reducing their effectiveness.

However, as the authors caution, “the mere presence of intratumoral bacteria does not always confer causation.” Cancer genetics and other features of the tumor environment can independently drive aggressive growth and treatment resistance.

Nanomedicine aims to improve chemotherapy by using microscopic carriers to deliver drugs directly into tumors. Although promising in animal studies, these technologies have often produced limited additional benefits in human patients.

One challenge involves the enhanced permeability and retention (EPR) effect, which can help nanoparticles accumulate in tumors through unusually permeable blood vessels. Human tumors are often more fibrous and structurally complex than experimental tumors, making drug penetration less predictable. The authors highlight an “overpromise” in translating results from rodent models into clinical treatments.

They also propose that bacterial infections might increase the permeability of tumor blood vessels, potentially improving drug delivery. However, this possibility has not been conclusively demonstrated in human cancers.

Rather than waiting for more advanced nanomedicines, the researchers propose combining conventional antibiotics and chemotherapy drugs without specialized carriers. The authors argue that “all diagnosed PPO-tumors must be treated from the onset with a carrier-free antibiotic/chemotherapeutic drug combination.”

Animal studies provide preliminary support for investigating this approach. In bacteria-infected tumor models, combining the antibiotic ciprofloxacin with the chemotherapy drug gemcitabine has helped restore treatment sensitivity and suppress tumor growth. Whether similar benefits occur in patients remains uncertain.

A major challenge is the lack of reliable clinical tests for detecting bacterial infections inside living tumors. Treating patients without confirming infection could also disrupt beneficial microbes and contribute to antibiotic resistance.

The researchers recommend examining existing patient records for evidence that antibiotic and chemotherapy combinations improve outcomes. They argue that “retrospective clinical verification can sometimes be the only pathway to clinical progress.”

Such research could help identify patients most likely to benefit, but additional clinical evidence is needed before routinely combining antibiotics with chemotherapy for aggressive cancers.

Reference: “Poor prognosis outcome tumors, bacteria-infected tumors and nanodrugs: current evidence and hypotheses towards a paradigm change for treatment” by Henk J. Busscher, David W. Grainger, JinPu Yu and Da-Yuan Wang, April 2026, Cancer Biology & Medicine. DOI: 10.20892/j.issn.2095-3941.2025.0748

The Ford Bronco Frostwarden Makes 1200 HP, Thanks to Its Megazilla 2.0 Supercharged V-8

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Automotive  |  Read Source →

This search-and-rescue machine, revealed at SEMA, is what happens when Ford throws everything it has at a Bronco.

The folks at Ford brought one of the wildest Broncos we’ve ever seen to the 2026 SEMA Show this year. It’s called “Frostwarden” ... and it has 1200 horsepower.

How’d Ford extract that much horsepower from a Bronco? Well, it sure doesn’t use any version of the production engines. Instead, Ford put the also-new Megazilla 2.0 crate motor under the hood. This 7.3-liter V-8 is paired here with a 3.0-liter Gen 6 Whipple supercharger; it also has a stronger block, supercharger-specific camshaft, upgraded pistons, larger fuel injectors, and a different belt drive and damper. As a package, Ford is claiming 1200 hp and 1000 pound-feet of torque when run with race fuel. (No, it didn’t quote a pump gas number.)

The idea behind the rest of the rig was to put together a package that could be used as an arctic search-and-rescue machine. To that end, it has a custom Bilstein suspension, 74Weld portal gear lifts, upgraded Dana driveline, and 18-inch Weld bead-lock wheels wrapped in 42-inch BFGoodrich tires. It’s absolutely enormous, and gives off the impression that one could drive it anywhere, which is largely the point.

This Bronco is festooned with accessories front to back. Rigid lights should help show the way through snowstorms. A spare wheels/tire and pair of toolboxes sit atop the roof. There’s a shovel, recovery rope, fuel jugs, and more. Plus, Ford outfitted the interior with color-matching seats and accents. There’s even a “Megazilla” emblem in the steering wheel rim to make sure you don’t forget this beast has 1200 horsepower ready to unleash.

Unfortunately, Ford explicitly says these vehicle “is not available for purchase,” and we suspect something like it never will be from your local Ford dealer. However, it serves as proof that you can shoehorn a 7.3-liter supercharged V-8 under the (admittedly bulged) hood of a Bronco, and what’s not to love about that?
