Dave Challinor confirms Championship job desire and rival interest before Sheffield Wednesday swoop

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Sheffield Wednesday officially announced Dave Challinor as their new boss earlier this week. The move marked the 51-year-old’s first job since leaving Stockport County in the summer, but his success with the Hatters meant SWFC weren’t alone in eyeing him.

Speaking to The Star, Challinor revealed other clubs wanted ‘conversations’, and his eyes were initially on a step up to the Championship before joining the Owls – a club with ambitions of returning to the second tier. He said: “Without going into too much detail, obviously people wanted to have conversations. I was in the fortunate position where I didn’t need to jump back in. I came out of a really good football club, and we had success, so I had the luxury of being able to be a little bit selective and wait.

“My natural progression would have been to try and get a Championship opportunity. That made the number of clubs I am appealing to was probably pretty small. The next opportunity either needed to give me that chance, or be one I could grow with. I certainly couldn’t have picked a bigger one in terms of this club and the amount of growth it’s got in it.”

Challinor was more than justified in initially eyeing a step up to the Championship after his Stockport County departure. He took the Hatters from the National League to the brink of promotion from League One, but there was a mutual agreement that it was a good time to part ways.

His back-to-back top six finishes in League One and promotion pedigree made him the ideal candidate for Sheffield Wednesday. And, if the Chester-born boss wasn’t going to land a Championship job, moving to a third-tier side with promotion ambitions made for the next best option.

Arguably though, the SWFC job is more exciting than many in the Championship. The ongoing rebuild under new ownership and desire to wake this sleeping giant makes the managerial post a highly attractive one, so Challinor is progressing in job stature despite staying in the same league.

Challinor is a coup for the Owls, and it’s an exciting step for him too. It’s a win-win appointment that benefits all parties, as long as results follow.

Challinor watched on from the stands as Sheffield Wednesday played out a 1-1 draw with Blackpool under Matt Hamshaw last week. The new boss has been able to get to work with his squad this week though, and the first game is just around the corner.

SWFC face League Two side Oldham Athletic in the FA Cup on Sunday. It marks a favourable start for Challinor, both in terms of the level of the opposition and the fact that there aren’t points at stake.

David Storch has been eager to stress that this appointment is part of a long-term rebuild, and success will not be defined by how this season goes. Nevertheless, Challinor will be determined to get Wednesday firing as soon as he can to strengthen their case for a spot in League One’s top six at the very least.

Discord is finally giving up on Electron, the cross-platform framework that lets developers build desktop apps using web technologies. Electron web apps are notoriously heavy on system resources, and Discord is now moving its desktop app fully to Rust — a modern programming language designed for speed and memory safety.

“We’ve been early adopters of Rust since 2018 and a lot of our infra, voice tech, and even some of the current client is already Rust, the holy grail was getting the whole client there,” explains Discord chief technology officer Stanislav Vishnevskiy in a post on X. “That means Electron goes away, and so does React Native, mobile keeps native UI but shares app code with desktop via Rust.”

Vishnevskiy also shared an image of the Discord Windows client using just 290MB of RAM, which is a clear improvement as you only have to participate in a few servers and RAM usage can easily hit 1GB or more using the current Windows app.

It’s not clear when Discord will make the full switch over to Rust, but Vishnevskiy says the company “hope to get it into testing with users soon.”

Critical security vulnerability affects OpenSSH on Alpine Linux systems

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Version 10.6p2 of OpenSSH has been released with a workaround for a critical vulnerability that affects Linux systems that use musl libc with PAM disabled, such as Alpine Linux. OpenSSH developer Damien Miller said:

If you operate a Linux system that uses musl libc without PAM (Alpine Linux is one example) then stop reading and set PasswordAuthentication=no or disable SSH for these systems IMMEDIATELY.

This vulnerability does not exist on non-Linux systems, Linux/glibc systems and Linux systems that use PAM for password authentication.

We consider the root cause of this bug to be outside OpenSSH itself, but we're in a good position to deliver a mitigation quickly.

The Alpine Linux project has announced that it will be backporting the OpenSSH fix "all the way back to Alpine 3.16", which was released in May 2022.

Air Serbia Flight To Nowhere: A320 Forced To Return To Belgrade Over Mysterious Security Concern

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Politics  |  Read Source →

An Air Serbia flight to Moscow made it as far as Lithuania before turning around and returning to Belgrade over a security concern involving one passenger. Serbian media identified that passenger as the country’s former intelligence chief, who allegedly boarded without completing security screening. He denies wrongdoing.

As flagged by One Mile At A Time, Air Serbia flight JU134 departed Belgrade for Moscow on November 3, 2026, but turned back over southern Lithuania. According to Serbian media, the Airbus A320 left at 6:48 PM and returned shortly before 10:00 PM. Passengers spent more than three hours flying only to end up where they started.

Air Serbia confirmed that airport authorities requested additional security checks concerning one passenger. The airline said it returned in accordance with the instructions it received, but did not publicly identify that passenger.

The passenger in question has since been identified as Aleksandar Vulin, Serbia’s former intelligence chief. It appears that someone helped him board without completing the required screening and that the airport’s corporate security director ordered the return after reviewing surveillance footage.

Vulin insists he completed the required checks, was carrying no prohibited items, and was being targeted for political reasons (his pro-Russian party lost in a snap election last month). He said his trip concerned negotiations over Serbia’s gas arrangement with Russia.

Passengers and their luggage underwent additional screening in Belgrade. The flight eventually continued to Moscow with a replacement crew, arriving about seven hours late.

If the reports are accurate, the obvious question is how someone was allowed to board without completing the required checks. Political prominence should not provide an exemption from an aviation security procedure, though I understand how easy it is to open an employee access door for a VIP).

But Vulin disputes that account, and the airline’s statement establishes only that authorities requested additional screening. That leaves important questions about what happened before departure and what prompted the return. Vulin was allowed to continue on the delayed flight to Moscow.

Belgrade’s Higher Public Prosecutor’s Office has requested an investigation into the circumstances, including whether procedures were followed, who authorized the aircraft’s return, and what security assessment supported that decision.

That inquiry is appropriate. If someone bypassed screening, there should be accountability for how it happened. If the checks were properly completed, authorities need to explain why an aircraft full of passengers was brought back.

Sony published its latest financial report today, and within it are updates on how well some of its first-party games have sold. Even though the report only covers the most recent quarter, which ended on September 30, 2026, it has sales figures for Wolverine’s first 50 days, right up until November 4.

According to those figures, Wolverine has sold through 2.4 million copies. That’s a big number. A number that most studios would bite your hand off for. However, those aren’t Marvel numbers, as is demonstrated by how well Spider-Man 2 sold at launch, which you can see from the webslinger’s figures a few rows up on the same Sony document.

According to that document, Spider-Man 2 sold 2.5 million copies during its first 24 hours. That means, despite having 50 times longer to hit that target, Wolverine has still fallen short. There’s an argument to be made that it’s unfair to compare the two, but it’s also unfair to suggest there is such a disparity between how well-known the heroes are that it should take Wolverine 50 times longer to hit the same targets.

There is no world in which a Wolverine game is, or was ever expected to, sell anywhere close to as well as a Spider-Man game. Spider-Man is among the most popular and recognizable characters on the planet. Thankfully, none of them are. All three of Insomniac’s Spidey games are critically acclaimed and well-loved by fans.

Wolverine, while also immensely popular, doesn’t have that same cache, and therefore doesn’t have the luxury of his games selling millions just because his face is on the cover.
