Oscar Health is seeing the “strongest year” in its history in 2026, CEO Mark Bertolini told investors during the company’s third quarter earnings call Thursday morning.

Total revenue is expected to be in the range of $19 billion to $19.3 billion, which Blackley notes is “an increase of $300 million” from its prior outlook.

The CFPB said it is narrowing supervisory and enforcement activity, closing 86% of open supervisory actions and cutting scheduled exams by about 50%. It is also reviewing ability-to-repay and mortgage servicing rules, along with revised fair lending and Section 1071 requirements.  AI Summary

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) said that it is narrowing its supervisory and enforcement activities and closing most of its outstanding supervisory actions, as well as pursuing changes to mortgage lending and servicing rules, according to its latest semi-annual report, released Oct. 27.

The report, which covers the first half of 2026, outlines the bureau’s efforts to align its work with the Trump administration’s priorities.

During the broader reporting period of July 2025 through June 2026, the CFPB closed 1,327 of the 1,552 supervisory actions that were open as of July 1, 2025, or approximately 86%. The bureau said the closures reflected either institutions sufficiently addressing the issues or findings that no longer aligned with its priorities.

The CFPB also reassessed all 80 open examinations and closed 66 that did not align with its revised priorities. The remaining 14 continued with a focus on correcting violations, addressing compliance weaknesses and providing relief to affected consumers.

The bureau also reduced the number of scheduled examinations by approximately 50%, shifting its attention toward depository institutions, activities it deemed actual consumer fraud and areas within its statutory authority. Mortgage origination and servicing remain among its supervisory priorities.

Under its new approach, the CFPB said examinations will focus on clearly identified products and potential violations tied to specific laws and regulations. The bureau also plans to avoid duplicating oversight already conducted by state or other federal regulators.

The CFPB is pursuing several mortgage-related regulatory changes under President Donald Trump’s executive order on promoting access to mortgage credit. Among its priorities is a review of the ability-to-repay requirements and qualified mortgage definitions under Regulation Z. The bureau is evaluating whether to modify the requirements, which apply to most residential mortgages.

The CFPB is also considering a final rule to streamline mortgage servicing requirements under Regulation X. The proposal, initially issued in July 2024, is intended to simplify the loss-mitigation review process and make it easier for servicers to assist borrowers who experience payment difficulties.

In July 2026, after the report’s primary reporting period, the bureau also issued a request for information seeking feedback on ways to promote access to mortgage credit.

The CFPB additionally finalized changes to its fair lending and small-business lending rules. In April, the bureau amended Regulation B, which implements the Equal Credit Opportunity Act. The rule removed longstanding language supporting disparate-impact liability and revised restrictions on discouraging prospective credit applicants.

It also prohibited for-profit creditors from using race, color, national origin or sex as eligibility criteria for Special Purpose Credit Programs.

A separate May rule revised requirements for small-business lending data collection under Section 1071 of the Dodd-Frank Act. Among other changes, it lowered the annual revenue threshold for businesses covered by the rule from $5 million to $1 million, removed certain data collection requirements and extended the compliance date to Jan. 1, 2028.

The bureau said it did not cite any fair lending violations during the reporting period, and that while it continued investigations, it instead focused its efforts on cases involving intentional discrimination with identifiable victims. It also made no referrals to the Department of Justice for potential patterns or practices of lending discrimination.

Overall, the CFPB said it closed approximately 40% of its pending investigations as part of its effort to align enforcement with its revised priorities.

The bureau reported receiving approximately 8.27 million consumer complaints between July 1, 2025, and June 30, 2026. It sent approximately 7.5 million complaints to companies for review, and companies responded to 99.6% of those complaints.

The report’s complaint figures come as the CFPB has moved to limit the public availability of consumer complaints. In August, the bureau said it would stop publishing unverified complaint narratives and related data visualizations in its public database, arguing that the accounts present only one side of a dispute and may not reflect violations of law.

The bureau also said it would continue collecting complaints, reviewing company responses and sharing information with regulators. Previously published narratives will remain available through its Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) reading room.

Nemi Plans Nasdaq Listing After SPAC Merger with BHAV Acquisition Corp

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Nemi Corp. and BHAV Acquisition Corp announced on November 5, 2026 that they had entered a definitive agreement to combine, valuing Nemi at $400 million pre-money and setting up a planned Nasdaq listing for the manufacturing and physical-AI company.

The consideration is entirely stock: 40 million shares at $10 each, covering Nemi’s existing equity, outstanding equity awards and securities issued in its pre-closing financing. There is no earnout. Nemi is targeting a $50 million private investment in public equity, or PIPE, at $10 per share, a financing goal rather than money already raised. Assuming a full PIPE and no redemptions by BHAV public shareholders, the companies estimate approximately $142.5 million in cash at closing after estimated expenses, drawn from BHAV’s $100 million trust account and the PIPE.

Proceeds are intended for additional factory capacity, purchases of manufacturers already serving Nemi’s markets, continued development of its Large Manufacturing Model and Phase 2 robotics, and working capital. The acquisition plan is central to the growth model: buy conventional manufacturers, then deploy Nemi’s software in their operations. No definitive acquisition agreements had been signed when the transaction was announced.

Nemi operates more than 300,000 square feet of manufacturing space in India, producing precision metal parts, tooling, electronics, batteries, motors, plastics and complex assemblies. It sells that capacity as Manufacturing-as-a-Service and also operates an electric-vehicle business. Those plants are integral to its AI strategy: chief executive Anirudh Ravi Narayanan said hardware data is largely proprietary and closed, so parts Nemi designs and builds can become data for subsequent work.

The Large Manufacturing Model, or LMM, has three connected suites. Anvil is intended to convert an engineering brief into computer-aided design, electronics design, simulation and a released bill of materials. Orion covers factory operations and robotics, while Atlas handles commercial and after-sales work and is designed to return field data to subsequent designs. Nemi’s product site describes Orion as a digital-twin factory system. The tools use third-party AI models selected for each task; Nemi does not build its own foundation models, and the LMM name is an analogy to large language models.

Deployment remains uneven across that stack. The digital-twin enterprise-resource-planning system is deployed, factory orchestration is in pilot and low-cost robotics are still in development. Nemi plans to test each stage in its own plants before deploying it in acquired manufacturers, making the existing factories both operating businesses and test sites for the software.

Nemi reported more than 50 enterprise customers across manufacturing and electric vehicles, including Indian defense, aerospace and space organizations reached directly or through partners, plus automotive manufacturers. As of October 14, 2026, it had more than $14 million of open purchase orders from 16 customers, subject to contractual milestones. Those orders had not yet been delivered and invoiced and include blanket orders from a fleet customer. Customers have also signed non-binding letters of intent indicating more than $100 million of potential revenue through 2028, which may not become orders or revenue.

The financial disclosures separate subsidiary performance from the consolidated operating group. Nemi reported that Manufacturing-as-a-Service revenue grew 38% year over year in fiscal 2025 with a 32.5% gross margin, although those subsidiary figures include sales to other Nemi companies. Audited consolidated statements for BNC Motors and its subsidiaries show gross margin rising to 17.7% in 2025 from 3.1% in 2024. The subsidiary result therefore cannot be read as a companywide margin.

Across more than 35 customer and internal programs over 12 months, Nemi measured design work finishing about three times faster with roughly one-third as many engineers as its pre-LMM baselines, which it characterized as approximately nine times the design output per engineer. The measurements come from unaudited management records. One drone-powertrain program was delivered in four months against management’s estimated typical timeline of 16 months, at about 20% lower parts cost.

Nemi defines Operating ARR as the latest quarter’s external revenue multiplied by four, excluding intercompany revenue. It is a run-rate, not contracted revenue or a measure prepared under U.S. generally accepted accounting principles. The company reported approximately $3.7 million for the quarter ended September 30, 2026, based on provisional, unaudited accounts.

Management estimates approximately $20 million of Operating ARR at closing, assuming $50 million of pre-closing financing supplies the working capital and capacity needed to execute the demand pipeline. Its approximately $120 million target for the quarter ending 12 months after closing assumes about $100 million of funding before and at closing, planned capacity additions and roughly $30 million of Operating ARR from acquisitions that have not been committed. Nemi calls both figures management estimates rather than forecasts.

Closing is expected in the first quarter of 2027 and requires approval by both companies’ shareholders, regulatory approvals, an effective registration statement and other customary conditions. Nemi would become the listed parent company. A $30 million minimum available-cash condition can be waived by the parties. Shares held by Nemi’s founder and affiliates, plus sponsor shares allocated to BHAV chief executive Giri Devanur, would become Class B stock carrying ten votes per share, with the same economic rights as Class A shares and one-for-one conversion into Class A.

The proposal therefore pairs an operating business with improving margins against a growth target that relies on new capital, added capacity and uncommitted acquisitions. The central question for the listing is whether Nemi’s factory-generated data and reported engineering gains can support that expansion while the financing and acquisitions behind the target remain incomplete.

Record-low temperature created on China’s space station

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Every print subscription comes with full digital access

A Bose-Einstein condensate reached about 10 trillionths of a kelvin

An experiment on China's Tiangong space station (rendering shown) reached a record-low temperature in a state of matter called a Bose-Einstein condensate.

Shujianyang/Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)

Science has reached a new low. Temperature, that is.

Scientists cooled a collection of atoms to about 10 picokelvins, or 10 trillionths of a kelvin. That’s barely a smidge above absolute zero (−273.15° Celsius). The atoms formed a Bose-Einstein condensate, a quantum state of matter, colder than any before, researchers report November 5 in Science.

To achieve that record-low chill, scientists took their work to space. The atoms orbited Earth aboard China’s Tiangong space station. There, researchers used laser-based traps to contain 20,000 rubidium atoms and reduce their motion, thereby cooling them. At an atomic level, temperature is tied to motion: Atoms and molecules in the air around us move more slowly when it’s colder. Similarly, in systems of cold atoms, scientists can define an effective temperature based on how slowly the atoms spread apart when they are allowed to expand.

We summarize the week's scientific breakthroughs every Thursday.

On Earth, gravitational forces cause clouds of atoms to sag, reducing the effectiveness of cooling. But on the space station, the atoms experienced microgravity, the apparent weightlessness of an object in free fall.

To make a Bose-Einstein condensate, a disorderly gaggle of atoms is cooled until the atoms all take on the same quantum state, acting as one entity. By experimenting with ultracold atoms, researchers can study exotic states of matter and other mysterious physics. And they can test fundamental concepts, such as the equivalence principle — the idea that explains why objects of different masses fall at the same rate in a vacuum.

Spacefaring Bose-Einstein condensates were previously made on the International Space Station, at effective temperatures as low as 52 picokelvins. And an experiment on Earth, in which atoms were dropped down a tower to induce weightlessness, reached 38 picokelvins. But drop tower experiments are limited by the time their free fall lasts. That’s not an issue for experiments in Earth’s orbit. So cold atoms in space are really taking off.

Greece Approves 10% Crypto Capital Gains Tax as First Framework Lands Below Draft Rate

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Greece's parliament approved a 10% capital gains tax on cryptocurrencies, the country's first dedicated crypto tax framework, with annual gains up to €500 exempt.

Greece’s parliament approved on Thursday a bill that imposes a 10% capital gains tax on cryptocurrencies, the first comprehensive legal framework for taxing crypto assets in the country.

Cryptocurrency capital gains of up to €500 ($563) a year will be exempt from the new tax, the bill says. For bitcoin holders in Greece, that threshold is a small buffer, not a carve-out for larger positions.

“Further implementation directives will be published in the coming weeks, which will also define transparency and audit rules,” a senior finance ministry official told Reuters.

An earlier draft circulated in October had floated a 15% rate. The version lawmakers passed lands five points lower, while keeping the €500 annual exemption that was already in that proposal.

The tax is set to apply from Jan. 1, 2027, Greek coverage of the finance ministry bill says. It is levied on the net gain — the difference between the acquisition price and the transfer price — not on the full sale proceeds. Trading fees and other costs tied to the deal can factor into that calculation.

A taxable transfer is not limited to selling for euros. Using cryptocurrency to buy goods or services, or contributing it to cover or increase company capital, can also crystallize a gain under the new rules. That matters for anyone in Greece who pays a merchant in bitcoin rather than converting first.

Simply holding bitcoin while its euro value rises does not, by itself, create a tax bill. Crypto-to-crypto swaps, such as bitcoin for another token, are treated as non-taxable events in the explanatory material that accompanied the bill, matching the approach France has used to avoid taxing every hop between coins.

Net annual gains at or below €500 are exempt. Greek analyses of the text say that once net gains clear that ceiling, the 10% rate applies to the full net gain for the year, not only the slice above €500. Losses above €500 in a tax year may be carried forward for up to five years and offset only against future crypto capital gains.

Purchases of crypto assets are also being pulled into Greece’s existing “source of funds” checks from 2027, so buyers may need to show that the euros used to acquire bitcoin or other tokens came from declared income or other lawful capital. The rule does not add a separate purchase tax; it widens the audit trail around how coins were funded.

European Union countries do not share a unified tax system for the sector. Rates on cryptocurrency gains typically run from about 8% to 30% across member states and are usually levied on capital gains, the finance ministry official said. Greece’s 10% sits toward the softer end of that band, below France’s often-cited 30% treatment and above Cyprus’s 8%.

The same patchwork is shifting elsewhere in the bloc. The Netherlands has moved toward a realized capital gains tax from 2028, while Germany and Portugal still exempt crypto held longer than a year. Since January, the EU’s DAC8 reporting rules have required crypto platforms to send user and transaction data to national tax authorities — a tool Greece already wrote into domestic law.

State revenue from the new levy could not be projected, the official said, because the vast majority of investors use platforms abroad, leaving the size of Greece’s cryptocurrency market hard to measure.

For bitcoin holders who trade on foreign exchanges, that gap is the practical test. The rate is now set in law; whether Athens can match 10% on paper with enforceable cost-basis data from offshore platforms is the question the coming directives will have to answer.

Oldest evidence of cannabis use discovered in 2,700-year-old royal tomb

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

Published Nov 5, 2026 2:00 PM EST

The use of cannabis is documented across cultures stretching back thousands of years, but it frequently relates to harvesting the plant for hemp and other non-psychoactive products. That said, one Bronze Age society of horseriders living in present-day Russia called the Scythians may offer the oldest example of intentionally ingesting cannabis for its intoxicating properties. In the Eurasian steppes of Siberia, archaeologists have discovered a princely Scythian tomb complex featuring an estimated 1,000 cannabis seeds intentionally placed among the dead.

“The seeds show that cannabis played an important role in the rituals surrounding the burial,” Gino Caspari, an archaeologist at the University of Bern in Switzerland, explained in a statement.

These weren’t simply symbolic objects, either. As Caspari and his colleagues detail in a study published today in the journal Science, human hair samples from the central burial room indicate Scythian royals also partook in cannabis use.

The Scythians were a group of ancient eastern Iranian nomadic equestrians renowned for their horseback skills. The community migrated from Central Asia into present-day Ukraine and southern Russia beginning in the eight and ninth centuries BCE, and stayed there for roughly the next 500 years. Their regional influence eventually waned before the Scythians ultimately assimilating into neighboring populations after being displaced by the Goths during the third century CE.

The burial complex Caspari’s team examined is located in Tuva, a south Siberian republic of Russia located along Mongolia’s northern border. Although ancient plant material like leaves, flowers, and resin generally decompose long before archaeologists can ever locate them, seeds sometimes endure when shielded from the elements. In this case, the royal tomb ensured near optimal conditions to protect the cannabis remnants. Laboratory radiocarbon dating of six recovered seeds indicate Bronze Age attendants likely placed them inside the burial chamber during the early eighth century BCE.

The exact details of Scythian cannabis cultivation and use remains unclear. Researchers theorize the plants were wild variants that naturally grew in the region. At the same time, hair analysis through methods like high-resolution mass spectrometry revealed the clear presence of cannabinol (CBN), which is produced by the oxidation of cannabis’ primary intoxicant, tetrahydrocannabinol (THC). The team also noted weak signs consistent with THC itself.

The detection of these molecular signals doesn’t reveal how much cannabis the Scythian royals ingested, or the methods they used. At the same time, other Central Asian archaeological sites contained cannabinoid chemical remnants on heated stones and inside vessels, which strongly suggest inhalation.

“The molecular traces in the hair and the cannabis seeds come from the same grave, and suggest that cannabis was used for its intoxicating properties,” concluded Caspari.

Archaeologists are now sequencing the DNA from some of the tomb’s seed samples, which will help them trace the plant’s spread and later human domestication. Cannabis wasn’t the only addition to the royal Scythian tomb, however. Prior work at the site has yielded the skeletal remnants of 18 horses arranged on the burial mound, along with an assortment of human bones.

“In this princely tomb, the burials were accompanied by extensive horse sacrifices and, most likely, human sacrifices as well,” added Caspari.

The acquisition targets a specific architectural problem: enterprise systems that run transactions are commonly separated from those that analyze the resulting activity. EDB argues that this fragmentation creates latency, cost and vendor lock-in, while a single agentic workflow may need to transact, analyze and act on the same business context.

Within EDB Postgres AI, Postgres supplies the operational record and ClickHouse analyzes large volumes of events and business activity. Closer integration is intended to reduce data movement and integration effort, helping applications and agents understand activity and act on it. The wider architecture combines a multi-model data layer, data-layer governance, an agentic runtime and a sovereign control plane, with permissions enforced where the data resides.

The integration roadmap includes a shared Apache Iceberg catalog and consistent access controls across Postgres and ClickHouse, connecting transactional workloads, real-time analytics and open lakehouse architectures. Governance is designed to determine what agents can access and do at the data layer, provide an audit trail and leave an off switch under customer control.

I had an equal partnership – and I still became the default parent

Here's how I reclaimed my time without compromising care for my little ones.

By Lais de Oliveira Updated Nov. 05, 2026

Every now and then, my husband picks up his phone and car keys, and announces he’s going out for something spontaneous. Lunch with a friend. A bike ride. He’s gone in about ninety seconds if I don’t stop him at the door.

And I generally don’t, because I want my husband to have time for himself.

Standing behind the closed door, I ask: When was the last time I left this house by myself, without planning it, negotiating it, or trading for it? Not once since my first child was born… three and a half years ago. Then it hits me: I am the proverbial default parent.

In my life, I have moved countries on 48 hours’ notice. I have started businesses and communities on four continents, landed in cities where I knew no one, and figured it out from the ground. Yet here I am, unable to walk out my own front door without a negotiation.

Now I am the kind of person who books a haircut six weeks out.

My husband carries himself with the lightness of someone who isn’t carrying the majority of the parenting load.

The mental one: how many diapers, ounces of milk, and clothing changes need to be packed for a single outing. The emotional one: forecasting ways to address my little one’s growing feelings or managing the conditions to prevent a meltdown. The logistical one: managing energy levels and knowing a missed nap isn’t worth the spontaneity of welcoming last-minute visitors (the hosting of which, by the way, would fall on me).

He doesn’t carry any of that because I do. And I know I’m not alone in this. (There are even several Bluey episodes about it).

But I thought my husband and I would be different. And to his credit, he does a lot for the family, and has always been willing to work towards a situation where I felt supported. Still, for over three years, our day-to-day behaviors set our sails in a different direction; the mere idea that we were working toward a mostly equitable deal blinded me to the fact that, too stereotypically, I was the one carrying the invisible load of parenting.

For a while, I stewed silently, filing away every every “just tell me what to do” and every unplanned walk out the door.

But my resentment wasn’t getting me closer to the future I actually wanted, which is to raise my kids alongside a partner I trust. Anger is honest information, but a terrible operating system. It costs you the very thing you’re angry about losing: your own energy.

So I stopped waiting for space to appear and started claiming it. Here are the steps I took:

The first step was recognizing there was a real problem. Only then could I have a productive, fact-based conversation with my husband.

What helped: talking it out, sharing the situation with friends and watching them nod before I finished the sentence. Those conversations helped clear the fog and made it clear: I had become the default parent without ever applying for the role, and I needed it recognized as a real job, with real requirements, not something invisible and easily taken for granted.

Clarity is a prerequisite for being heard, and it is what moved the conversations with my husband past defensiveness. Until then, the idea that we had a “mostly equitable deal” was blurring his perspective too.

We didn’t get it right at once. Several conversations left one of us hurt, misunderstood, or underappreciated. But we persisted in having the difficult ones. Those conversations eventually gave us enough clarity to lay out, with clinical precision, the ways our partnership had been uneven. We could dissect a messy situation and turn resentment into discernment, naming the areas where I was lacking support and the areas where both of us were lacking appreciation.

Clarity let us stand across from each other, our vision for an equitable partnership leading the way instead of resentment for how it had been.

And we learned something more important: an equitable relationship isn’t found. It’s built. That shared understanding made the next steps possible.

Once we both understood what my resentment had been trying to say, in more positive, proactive terms, my needs became clearer and easier to ask for. And my husband finally had something concrete to act on.

We put our partnership to work: I defined clear steps to reclaim my own freedom, and he redefined what it means to be a supportive partner.

From there, we made a real plan: a family calendar that protects my time the same way it protects his and provides a predictable rhythm. The gym, daily. Tango on Monday nights. A weekday or two blocked out because I’m in the city for meetings. An evening for dinner with a friend.

And, every so often, a half-day on the weekend for my own creative retreat, which is where I am as I type this: in a literal room of my own, my office and studio, with a door that closes.

I still won’t leave the house on a whim. But neither will he. We plan ahead, tell the other person, and arrange childcare if we both want some time out.

Relationships tarnish under unmanaged resentment. Bringing back the shine takes work.

From my end, I had to listen more, or proactively ask for his perspective on an issue I’d raised, and work to transmute resentment into something more productive: naming my unmet need. I had to really care about his perspective, and trust that he had been going for “supportive partner” all along, even if our early attempts had been clumsy and left scars.

From his end, he needed to become more observant and make efforts to show up more often in ways that were meaningful to me: offering to hire help for things previously expected to come from me, checking in before making plans on his own, and looping me in before inviting friends and family.

We also started scheduling more romance, whether a babysitter-powered date or quality time at home. But I’d say this came as a consequence of everything above: there was no mood for a date while our relationship was permeated by resentment. The difficult conversations had to come first.

The work most mothers do is largely invisible. But invisible isn’t the same as involuntary.

I can’t undo how the default got set, or how my own behavior kept reinforcing it. What I can do is refuse to let the default compromise my own health, my relationship, and the example I’m setting for my children. I want them to have the courage to create a life they’re proud to call theirs.

Some of that courage looks like walking out your own front door on a Saturday, keys in hand, for no reason at all: knowing the little ones are safe, and well cared for, because you made it so.

Xinhua Unveils Bionic Humanoid Robot News Anchors with UBTECH

Xinhua News Agency unveiled two bionic humanoid robot news anchors developed with UBTECH on November 5, 2026, at the Artificial Intelligence and Chinese Modernization forum in Beijing, describing them as the world’s first of their kind.

The male anchor is Xinyu and the female anchor is Xinyan. Both use life-size human proportions and were customized for news scenarios. Xinhua says it holds complete independent intellectual-property rights for the jointly developed robots. During the forum, one robot read the opening news on stage, providing an immediate demonstration of the presentation role.

Chinese film director Zhang Yimou served as chief image designer and led the embodied visual design. His stated aim was to balance the solemn, rigorous requirements of news presentation with natural bionic realism. The design work drew on camera aesthetics and the shaping of personal demeanor, tying the robots’ appearance to the visual conventions of broadcast journalism rather than treating human resemblance as a standalone objective.

The anchors connect through a technical interface to Xinhua Yudian, the agency’s AI current-affairs information agent and authoritative corpus. Xinhua says this arrangement makes the content traceable, secure and controllable. UBTECH’s robotics contribution spans bionic skin, embodied-intelligence hardware, an operating system, an emotional large model and manufacturing of the complete robots.

Zhou Jian, UBTECH’s founder, chairman and CEO, said the resulting brain–expression–control technical chain retrieves reviewed, authoritative current-affairs information in real time and avoids dissemination risks from generative-AI hallucinations. The design makes content governance part of the robot’s operating pipeline: Xinhua’s corpus supplies the reviewed material that the machine retrieves and presents.

Qian Tong, director of Xinhua’s New Media Center, described the robots as a new type of editorial job within the newsroom, with journalists and editors remaining the central editorial actors. Their assigned tasks include information presentation, multilingual output and live interaction in support of editorial staff. That remit positions the humanoids as presentation and interaction tools within an editorial workflow, not as autonomous authors of the news they voice.

Xinhua reports more than 220 digital and intelligent tools introduced in recent years under an approach of empowering journalists rather than replacing them. Forum participants identified human-led, machine-assisted work as a basic principle for intelligent journalism, with human judgment, field reporting and fact-checking remaining responsibilities that AI should not displace.

Zhou listed research and study bases, city reception venues, overseas cultural exchanges and deep-space exploration reporting as future applications. Those are planned scenarios, distinct from the opening-news reading demonstrated at the launch. The proposed settings extend the concept from the demonstrated presentation task into public reception, cultural exchange and specialized reporting.

The New Media Center also plans the Xinhua News Agency AI Industry Alliance (Beijing Base) to link government, media, technology companies and think tanks in an innovation vehicle spanning industry, academia, research and application. The debut was accompanied by a signing ceremony for the base and the launch of a research project on mobile news-image technology and authenticity standards. Those initiatives place the physical anchor demonstration alongside work on industry coordination and the verification of visual news content.

The forum formed part of the seventh World Media Summit, which is co-hosted by Xinhua and the Beijing municipal government and runs through November 9, 2026. It brings together nearly 500 delegates from nearly 330 mainstream media outlets, think tanks, government departments and international organizations across 112 countries and regions.

The debut ties a highly visible humanoid interface to a less visible editorial system built around reviewed source material, defined newsroom tasks and human control. The central open question is how that system performs in live interaction and multilingual output as Xinhua pursues applications beyond the demonstrated on-stage reading.

The study, published in Careerminds’ white paper, The AI Redeployment Gap: What Companies Lose by Hiring over Reskilling, reveals that 92% of HR professionals and 88% of people managers believe employees within their organizations could be reskilled for AI-related roles.
