Sony Pushes PS5 Owners To Trade In Consoles Amid Unprecedented Price Hikes

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Sony pursues new trade-in options as prices for the PS5 surge

The future of affordable video game consoles is not looking good, and hardware makers like Sony know it. The PlayStation 5 has undergone two major price hikes while the Xbox Series X has jumped in price three times in a single year. The AI-driven VRAM crisis at the heart of the issue is expected to worsen. Given the supply constraints, Sony has limited options for selling PS5s at prices customers might find acceptable.

It doesn't seem like a coincidence, then, that some PS5 owners have started receiving notifications directly from Sony about a new partnership with the tech sustainability company Back Market. The company advocates ending ‘fast tech’ and operates, among other services, a marketplace for refurbished electronics.

According to Sony, current PS5 owners can receive up to $400 for their old consoles on Back Market — provided the hardware is in good condition and the user includes two controllers. That's more money than competitors like GameStop offer. At the same time, when used or refurbished PS5s sell somewhere between $600 and $1,300, Sony's offer may not be the most appealing deal for most consumers. This may appeal mainly to users who planned to upgrade anyway — a group that is growing as the release of Grand Theft Auto 6 nears.

Like Sony, Xbox has spent part of 2026 trying to encourage its fans to trade in their consoles for cash. Microsoft frames it as a pragmatic option for users who aren’t using their consoles — an unintended acknowledgment of Xbox’s weaker sales pitch this generation. More recently, and to the skepticism of many, Xbox CEO Asha Sharma claimed that demand for Xbox consoles has exceeded supply; she proposed mitigating the issue with an expansion of Xbox's refurbishment program.

The timing of both programs coincides with the imminent release of major games like GTA 6 and the holiday season, when consoles are in demand. How this plays out remains to be seen. Given the PlayStation 6's rumored exorbitant price tag and the sudden explosion around the PS5's jailbreak, some users might have extra incentive to hold onto their old consoles.

Researchers warned of North Korean hacking tactics years before record $6.3B Binance heist

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Crypto giant Binance says North Korean hackers used fake social media profiles and the lure of a potential job offer as part of a months-long scheme aimed at tricking an employee into downloading malicious software onto their laptop.

Researchers have repeatedly warned about long-running social engineering attacks that can go on for months.

This latest attack allowed the hackers to patiently worm their way into Binance’s systems to steal a record $6.3 billion in crypto last week, the company has now confirmed. The hack, disclosed in an updated statement posted on X on Tuesday, prompted the world’s largest crypto exchange to suspend customer withdrawals across its network.

The FBI and blockchain investigators have blamed North Korea for the heist. The country has been long accused of carrying out massive cryptocurrency thefts in recent years to fund its internationally sanctioned nuclear weapons program.

In its latest statement, Binance said that North Korean hackers, posing as recruiters hiring for a job, contacted a software engineer who works on wallets at the company. Following two interviews with the purported company, the engineer was instructed to download an apparent coding assignment as part of the interview process. When opened, the assignment contained malicious code that compromised the engineer’s work-issued laptop. Binance said its security tools did not detect the malware.

That foothold in Binance’s network allowed the hackers to access an internal tool capable of taking over the work computers of two other engineers on the wallet team. One of those engineers had unwittingly downloaded a tampered app containing password-stealing malware months earlier after searching for the software in search engine results. While Binance replaced that compromised engineer’s laptop, the hackers used the engineer’s stolen credentials to break into the computer of a third engineer, whose job allowed them access to systems capable of controlling billions of dollars in crypto.

Binance said the attack “should not have happened,” and that it had improved its network security following the theft.

The hack underscores an ongoing tactic in the North Korean regime’s repertoire of attacks targeting the crypto industry and high-net worth individuals. Researchers have warned for years that North Korean hackers pose as venture capitalists, investors, and recruiters using dossiers of stolen American identities to trick employees into granting access to their systems.

These attacks are widely known across the crypto industry and have been the subject of repeated warnings from the U.S. government and cybersecurity giants in recent years. Some half of all intrusions at U.S. tech companies last year were attributed to North Koreans posing as remote IT workers and fake recruiters.

By Binance’s own account, the company faced at least 57 suspected attacks from North Korea, which it said reflect the “persistent threat posed by state-sponsored actors targeting the cryptocurrency industry.”

But how the hackers were able to convince the engineer into downloading malware given the highly publicized threat, and bypass security weaknesses in Binance’s own network, will likely be the focus of investigators’ attention.

While the case exposes how North Korean hackers use social engineering, such as offers of employment, to trick employees into granting access to their systems, it’s ultimately the responsibility of the company to ensure its systems cannot run unauthorized or untrusted software that could cause a compromise.

Binance spokesperson Ross O’Leary referred to the company’s public statement but declined to answer TechCrunch’s questions on the record about the incident. Those questions included how Binance prevents unauthorized or malicious code from running on its employees’ laptops, if at all, and whether any third parties had performed cybersecurity audits of Binance’s network prior to the hack.

The company said it would bring in cybersecurity experts to review the incident and has put new security measures in place to prevent a repeat incident.

The heist remains the largest theft of crypto in history.

Google is cutting schools off from Search if they restrict AI

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Google is turning off Gemini and Search by default for K-12 student accounts until school administrators enable them

David Paul Morris/Bloomberg via Getty Images

Google $GOOGL is cutting off K-12 student access to Google Search at schools that do not enable its Gemini AI products, according to NBC News. The company announced the changes to its education products on Tuesday, including updated controls for Gemini, Gemini Notebook, Gemini in Classroom and Google Search.

Chris Phillips, Google's vice president and general manager of education, wrote in a blog post that access to those products "will be entirely off for K-12 student accounts until school administrators explicitly turn them on," according to NBC News. Before Tuesday's change, Gemini was switched on by default in any school that used Google Classroom.

"We believe schools should have control over AI in their classrooms and in providing administrators with controls to help them manage their approach," Phillips wrote, according to NBC News.

Google said it is bringing Guided Learning to Google Classroom globally and adding Guided Learning capabilities to Google Search. Administrators who activate Search can also enable a "guided learning mode" that recognizes when a student drops homework questions into the search box, according to The Wall Street Journal. Under that setting, the chatbot is built to walk students through a problem one stage at a time without handing over the final answer.

Districts that offer Search cannot fully turn off its AI Overview and AI Mode features, according to The Wall Street Journal.

Randi Weingarten, president of the American Federation of Teachers, criticized the decision. "It is unfortunate that Google has prioritized its business interests over the education of our children," she told NBC News. Josh Golin, executive director of the advocacy group Fairplay, described Google's approach in a statement as an effort to "strong-arm schools into using its AI products," according to NBC News.

The change follows pressure over Google's AI tools in classrooms. Sen. Richard Blumenthal, a Connecticut Democrat, and Sen. Josh Hawley, a Missouri Republican, sent a letter to Google CEO Sundar Pichai asking what the company knows about how its AI products affect children's mental health and learning. They set a Nov. 2 deadline for a reply.

The senators cited a September investigation by The Wall Street Journal, which relied on more than 60 interviews with clinicians, parents, students, school officials and educators. It found that many students had come to lean on Google's AI tools for both their schoolwork and their emotional needs.

In early October, 13 parent advocacy organizations, including the Digital Childhood Institute and Schools Beyond Screens, sent an open letter urging districts to disable student use of both Gemini and YouTube, according to NBC News.

Apple Opens Siri AI Developer Preview for Custom App Integrations

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Apple announced on November 3, 2026 that its Siri AI Developer Preview is open to all developers through the iOS 27.3, iPadOS 27.3, macOS 27.3, and visionOS 27.3 betas. Apps can expose custom content and actions through App Intents, allowing Siri AI to handle requests such as checking deliveries, booking reservations, and placing orders.

The opening extends Siri AI beyond actions and data that fit Apple’s system-defined app schemas. Integrations can draw on personal-context understanding and onscreen awareness, letting people phrase requests in their own words, and Siri AI can surface any entity in Spotlight’s semantic index rather than only entities matching an existing schema. Apple has begun rolling out related capabilities across its own apps, including Calendar, Files, Mail, Maps, Messages, Photos, and Shortcuts; examples include finding a previously visited coffee shop and archiving files unopened for more than a year.

Under the Developer Preview documentation, invocation depends on context. A person does not need to name an app while it is in the foreground, while a Live Activity is active, or when continuing a Siri conversation that already included the app. Background requests require the app’s name. For example, a ride-hailing customer could follow a Live Activity and then ask Siri to change the pickup address without restating the app.

Siri AI can also find content in one app, transfer it to a specified second app, and perform an action there. Apple’s example sends a user’s favorite sneakers through a messaging app after resolving the recipient through Contacts. Requested custom content can appear in developer-supplied card snippets that use the app’s own views and branding. No new entitlement or separate developer program is required.

Apple’s implementation guidance makes exposure selective: developers decide which content Siri may retrieve and which actions it may perform. A Swift macro, @AppIntent(.tool), turns a new or existing custom intent into a tool Siri can call. Intents already conforming to a Siri-supported schema in a primary domain do not need that annotation.

Titles and descriptions for intents, parameters, and entity properties help Siri interpret natural-language wording, distinguish similar actions, and formulate responses. Developers can index entities in Spotlight, associate visible app content with entities for onscreen context, and donate intents when users perform matching actions to supply behavioral cues. Transferable representations allow custom entities to move between apps during cross-app requests.

Access can be narrowed for sensitive data. Authentication policies can protect an entire entity or selected properties, while an internal visibility setting prevents Siri, Shortcuts, and other system features from reading a property. Apple’s finance example requires authentication for an account nickname and balance but keeps the routing number internal to the app and its extensions.

For multi-step work, app skills supplement individual tool intents. A skill is a Markdown file whose metadata helps Siri decide whether it matches a request; Siri consults the instruction body only after making that match. The instructions can describe action order, which intent to prefer, practices to avoid, app-specific domain knowledge, and when to ask clarifying questions. A shopping skill could coordinate searching for items, adding them to a cart, choosing payment and delivery methods, reviewing the cart, and confirming the purchase.

Xcode’s Siri Integration Advisor offers implementation feedback, and the Siri Evaluations framework lets developers script requests, measure responses, and detect changes as intents and entities evolve. Developers can run development-signed builds on supported devices and share them with internal TestFlight testers. External TestFlight testers cannot use the preview, and its functionality does not work in App Store versions even when those apps contain the new integration.

Apple says the first customer release is expected in a few months, with App Store submissions scheduled to open in the spring. Further terms and usage policies for Siri AI-enabled experiences will follow. Siri AI requires an Apple Intelligence-enabled device set to a supported language. It is available in English, French, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese, and Spanish, with Traditional Chinese, Norwegian, Turkish, and Vietnamese listed as forthcoming.

Siri AI is unavailable in the EU on iOS, iPadOS, and watchOS, and Apple says it is working with the EU on a possible route to availability. It is not available to users under 13, and some features may not be available in every region or language.

Certain Apple Intelligence features that rely on server-side models, including Siri AI, are subject to daily usage limits. Apple says expanded access to those features will be available for a fee in the future.

The immediate change is a build-and-test opening rather than a full customer release. Developers can define the data and actions Siri AI may use, orchestrate them across workflows, and evaluate the results internally, while customer availability, App Store submissions, and detailed usage policies remain tied to later stages of Apple’s rollout.
