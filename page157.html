How Berlin's Arab queers are building their community

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

As the co-founder of the ADIRA Drag Festival, Lebanese performance artist, drag performer, DJ and cultural organizer Hassandra has become an essential part of Berlin's queer artistic landscape.

"Adira" is an Arabic slang term used in Levantine queer culture to describe someone with a strong presence — someone powerful and accomplished.

It is also the name of a Berlin-based collective that curates events and club nights celebrating the diversity of queer Arab* culture. The asterisk signals the inclusion of non-Arab ethnic minorities and people with diverse identities and roots in Southwest Asia and North Africa.

Among the collective's events is the ADIRA Drag Festival, which celebrated its third edition in Berlin in late October.

This year's theme, "Who's Your Diva?!," brought together drag performers paying tribute to legendary figures from across the Eastern Mediterranean who have become queer icons — from Egyptian music legend Umm Kulthum to Turkish star Bülent Ersoy and Lebanese pop icon Haifa Wehbe.

Hassandra, co-founder of ADIRA and the ADIRA Drag Festival, was among the performers, taking on the persona of Lebanese pop star Elissa.

"It is very important for me to bring my Lebanese Arab identity and culture into what I do, because I think it's a very beautiful culture. And there's a lot of campy, glamorous, beautiful divas," Hassandra said.

Elissa's "whole discography is very romantic, raw, soft, kind of like very Adele vibes," Hassandra told DW ahead of the October 30 show. "However, her personality is known to be the extreme opposite. She's very feisty. Very blunt, very unfiltered. Not soft at all as a person." The star is also known for speaking out on political issues in Lebanon. She has publicly voiced support for LGBTQ+ communities in the Middle East, while also frequently sparking controversy across the Arab world with her outspoken positions on other political issues.

Hassandra's ADIRA Drag Festival performance addresses this complexity, ultimately "tapping into her sort of controversial political opinions," the drag artist explained.

This reflects one of the ideas Hassandra hopes to promote with the theme of this year's festival. "As queer people, we tend to idealize a lot of pop divas. But these pop divas are very imperfect in their nature," Hassandra said. The show provides an opportunity to critically revisit these divas' legacy through a more critical lens. "And this sort of critical lens can be taken into the outside world, as in: never follow anyone in life blindly. No one should follow any politician, any political party, any pop diva, any activist blindly."

Born in Beirut in 1994, Hassandra studied theater and stage acting at the Lebanese University before moving to Berlin in 2017. "I would say it was it was just a very sort of impulsive decision at the age of 22. I mean, there's no doubt that the economic situation was really bad in Lebanon and continued to become even worse," the artist said.

In Berlin, Hassandra became increasingly involved in the city's independent art scene and queer nightlife. Drag emerged as another form of self-expression for the actor.

Through performances that combine theatrical intensity, political humor, grotesque aesthetics and moments of tenderness, Hassandra challenges conventional understandings of gender and identity.

Their work explores themes of home, nostalgia, displacement and queer Arab culture. These ideas are also central to ADIRA, which Hassandra founded in 2023 with Syrian-German DJ and cultural producer Zuher Jazmati, also known as xanax_attax.

Through panel discussions, performances and music events, ADIRA fosters collective participation and community-building.

As Islamophobia and homophobia rise alongside the far right, spaces like ADIRA offer a sense of belonging and support for Arab queer communities.

That sense of community has become even more important following the deadly Islamist terrorist attack during Berlin's Christopher Street Day pride parade in July.

In the aftermath, Hassandra felt caught between their different identities. As an Arab queer person, "you fall in this position where then you start receiving a lot of racism," they said.

"And I think this is why it's important, for us queer Arabs, to say we are sad over what happened, but that we also reject any of the racism that comes from that," Hassandra said. "I think we [the Arab community] are being used in a lot of ways as a punching bag for a lot of things that are happening."

Rather than allowing the attack to fuel hatred and racism, Hassandra said, "we should make Germany a safer place for everyone to live in."  "There's place for everyone. It's a huge country," they added.

Ben Johnson reacts to Bears' statement win over Seahawks

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

The Chicago Bears' 34-17 win over the Seattle Seahawks on Monday Night Football was a statement win for this team. However, Bears head coach Ben Johnson was not looking to make a statement since this team is still growing.

"When the pressure's high, we want to be at our best. And all three phases did that," Johnson said. "We didn't come here to make a statement, we're still building."

This may have been Chicago's best overall win of the year with contributions from all three phases. Austin Booker made two big plays for the Bears, blocking a field goal in the fourth quarter and tipping the ball, which led to an interception by James Lynch.

Defensive tackle Jeffery Simmons also had an amazing debut for the Bears with two tackles for loss, including a sack as well as recording a quarterback hit and six total tackles. His presence also elevated Montez Sweat, who he was teammates with at Mississippi State. Sweat had his best game of the season by far with three quarterback hits and a sack.

Offensively, Chicago could not be denied in the red zone, scoring on all four of their red zone trips, including three rushing touchdowns from running back D'Andre Swift. Quarterback Caleb Williams had his third game of the season with completion percentage greater than 70 yards. He also become the first quarterback to throw for over 200 yards against Seattle.

Still, there are things to improve on. Their secondary has been dealing with injuries, including being without their top cornerback in coverage, Malik Muhammad. However, the biggest point of improvement is reducing their penalties. Chicago had 10 penalties in this game, making this their fifth game of the season with at least eight penalties. The Bears were able to overcome these penalties, but they could be even better if they were not penalized so much.

The British luxury house has enlisted Pierce Brosnan, Fiona Shaw, Cara Delevingne, Gabrielle Union and Dwyane Wade for its 2026 holiday campaign, a star-studded celebration of the brand’s 170th anniversary directed by filmmaker Harmony Korine.

Titled “Wrapped in Burberry: Celebrating 170 Years,” the short film takes place at Longleat House, a historic English estate in Wiltshire, where Brosnan and Shaw play hosts to an extravagant holiday gathering. The guest list also includes Brosnan’s son Dylan, “Bridgerton” star Nico Parker, “Pachinko” actress Kim Min-ha and Chinese actor Wu Lei, with a cameo from Emma Thynn, Marchioness of Bath. Why West Ham fans should welcome - and be wary of - Scott Parker appointment

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Scott Parker is the frontrunner to succeed Nuno Espirito Santo, who was sacked as West Ham head coach on Tuesday morning with the club’s promotion hopes hanging in the balance.

West Ham were widely considered favourites to bounce straight back to the Premier League this season, but after six wins from their first 13 Championship games, they are down in eighth place.

Having recorded a £104.2million loss in the last financial year, the club are acutely aware of the financial imperative of returning to the top flight at the first opportunity and have moved swiftly to make a change in the dugout following successive home defeats to Southampton and West Brom.

Parker, a club legend who won three successive Player of the Year awards during a four-year stint in east London, is seen as someone who can not only get supporters back onside but has experience getting teams promoted from the Championship.

The 46-year-old has earned promotion to the Premier League on three separate occasions across spells with Fulham, Bournemouth and Burnley, but his success has not come without criticism.

His time at Fulham, who offered him his first senior managerial role in May 2019, was dominated by tensions with the board, and Parker openly criticised the club’s long-term structure and recruitment.

Despite taking Fulham up from the Championship, Parker then struggled to build a competitive Premier League side and presided over a dispiriting relegation campaign, with his pragmatic style of play proving increasingly unpopular with supporters.

Parker left Fulham by mutual consent after their relegation in 2021 before swiftly taking charge at Bournemouth. While not direct rivals, the move left a bitter taste for some, with Bournemouth in direct competition with Fulham for promotion from the Championship.

Both teams went up automatically during the 2021/22 season. But while Fulham were able to stabilise under the guidance of Marco Silva, similar issues reared their head for Parker, and he was sacked four games into the season after questioning the direction and commitment of the club following a 9-0 defeat to Liverpool.

Parker’s comments, in which he said his team were "ill-equipped at this level", led Bournemouth’s then-owner Maxim Demin to hit back, stating that for the club to progress, everyone must “show belief in and respect for one another.”

Scott Parker is a manager with pedigree at this level but also plenty of baggage

West Ham will be aware of Parker’s history, including an ill-fated 67-day spell at Belgian side Club Brugge, and they will have made it clear during negotiations that he must secure promotion to secure his future.

After a toxic end to Nuno’s tenure, there will be a fair amount of goodwill for the former England international given his cult status at the club and his knowledge of the league.

The Championship is relentless, though, and results need to change quickly if West Ham are to keep pace with the top of the table and stop the chasing pack from gaining encouragement.

In Parker, West Ham believe they have a safe pair of hands who knows the club and knows how to get out of the Championship. Supporters are, however, bound to be more sceptical.

Parker’s vision for West Ham will almost certainly be built on defensive solidity, and with good reason. West Ham have conceded 18 goals in 13 league games this season, a rate of 1.4 goals per game.

Given the quality of the squad at his disposal, though, there will also be an expectation to entertain. West Ham, for all their struggles, have at times blown teams away this season, scoring in every game this term.

Parker is a manager with pedigree at this level but also plenty of baggage. West Ham can’t say they have not been warned.
