We Tried The World-Famous Apple Pie In A Bag—Is It Worth The Hype?

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Delish editors handpick every product we feature. We may earn commission from the links on this page.

Thanksgiving is only a few weeks away, so it’s already time to start planning your holiday menu. And with so many labor-intensive savory dishes on my list, I prefer to outsource dessert to the pros. Grocery store pies are often average at best, but there’s one out there that might actually be better than homemade.

The Elegant Farmer is a family-owned business based in Mukwonago, Wisconsin. In addition to operating an orchard with a dozen apple varieties, they’re best known as a specialty food store—largely thanks to its award-winning Apple Pie in a Paper Bag.

Over the past several decades, the signature dessert has achieved national acclaim and developed a cult following. Gourmet and The Wall Street Journal have dubbed it the best apple pie in America, and The Elegant Farmer even bested Bobby Flay in a 2008 episode of Throwdown.

Now, dessert lovers nationwide can get one of these famous pies delivered through Goldbelly. However, a single pie costs a whopping $70, including shipping. But is the Apple Pie in a Paper Bag worth the hype—and the sticker shock? I got my hands on one to find out.

The bag isn’t just a marketing schtick. 26 years ago, The Elegant Farmer’s former owners, Dan and Karen Scheel, wanted to sell a new dessert using the apples grown in their orchard. Karen found a recipe in a cookbook for a pie baked in a paper bag, so she enlisted their team of bakers to develop their own version.

The paper bag offers two benefits. The sealed environment traps moisture as it bakes to keep the Ida Red apples juicy, but it also prevents the crust from burning. Partway through baking, The Elegant Farmer cuts a hole in the center of the bag so that the top crust is perfectly golden by the end.

When you order this pie, it arrives pre-baked and frozen in a shrink-wrapped cardboard box. All you need to do is warm it straight from frozen in a 325°F oven for about an hour. If you thaw it in the refrigerator first, you can reheat it at 425°F for just 8 to 10 minutes. Yes, it’s that easy.

The instructions direct you to place the pie on a baking tray, but I’d recommend going one step further and laying down a protective layer of parchment first. A considerable amount of caramelized sugar collects at the bottom of the bag and can leak into the oven. Adding the extra barrier is a simple step that spares you from scrubbing it off later.

An hour later, the pie was warm and ready to serve. The torn paper and cracked crust admittedly don’t make this the most visually stunning pie in the world. But once our editors took a bite, any aesthetic concerns went out the window.

Contributing Writer Steven Morea said the apples taste like “they were just picked.” Digital Director Katelyn Lunders appreciated the abundance of apples and how the filling wasn’t too sweet. “I can’t stop eating it,” she added. (Lunders is already planning on ordering another for her family Thanksgiving, by the way.)

The crust got equally rave reviews. “I would buy this just for the crust,” said Senior Editor Samantha MacAvoy. The pronounced butter flavor, golden caramelization, and crunchy texture perfectly offset the softness of the fruit. Even Digital Community Editor Taylor Worden—a self-proclaimed pie hater—fell in love with it. And Test Kitchen Manager Alejandro Valdes Lora kept his opinion short and sweet: “I’m obsessed with this.”

If our office’s unanimous praise is any indication, The Elegant Farmer’s Apple Pie in a Paper Bag is absolutely worth the hype. We may hesitate to spend $70 on this pie on any ordinary day. But for special occasions like Thanksgiving, it’s a convenient showstopper that’ll easily upstage everything else on your holiday menu.

EXCLUSIVE: When The Institute returns for its second season this month, it will feature a scene that was personally requested by author Stephen King, according to the show's lead star.

The Institute is based on King's 2019 novel, which tells the story of kids with telekinetic and telepathic powers (including Luke Ellis) who get abducted by a mysterious organization known as the Institute. Six years later, MGM+ and creator/showrunner Benjamin Cavell debuted the TV adaptation, which stars Ben Barnes, Joe Freeman, Simone Miller, Fionn Laird and Mary-Louise Parker.

King serves as one of the executive producers on The Institute alongside Cavell, Jack Bender and others. In an interview with ScreenRant's Grant Hermanns, Barnes, who plays Tim Jamieson, revealed that the bestselling horror author had one request for The Institute season 2. He "very deliberately" wanted to see the children use their powers to "get cash out of an ATM."

King had that visual in his head, which Barnes can relate to because "when you're dreaming and conjuring stories, or writing songs, or anything, you have something very specific in your head."

Cavell already had a connection to King before working on The Institute, having co-created and executive-produced Paramount+'s 2020 adaptation of The Stand, so the author was familiar with Cavell and knew how he worked, especially when it came to bringing his stories to life on the screen.

King was "quite thrilled" with how The Institute turned out, according to Barnes, who explained that Cavell made sure to incorporate certain themes that King typically includes in his books, like "fighting back against the man" and the "powers that be," as well as how innocence can be corrupted and "really ask[ing] difficult questions of people that are in charge."

Ben Barnes: "We didn't particularly have individual connections with Stephen King, but obviously, Ben Cavell, our showrunner, did, and I think he was quite thrilled with how the first season turned out thematically. Certainly, in terms of the thing that you associate with Stephen King so deliberately, his concerns about fighting back against the man, against the powers that be, the corruption of innocence, the power of youth — in this case, the supernatural power of youth — to stand up for itself, and kind of really ask difficult questions of people that are in charge. I think so long as we carried through those themes into the second season, then we were going to be on the right path. I did hear that he very deliberately wanted the kids to use their superpowers to get cash out of an ATM. [Laughs] That was his one visual, in his head, which I connect to because when you're dreaming and conjuring stories, or writing songs, or anything, you have something very specific in your head that you're like, 'Oh, it'd be cool to see that.' I love that he deliberately said, 'I want to see that.'"

Barnes and everyone who works on The Institute knew that if they continued to carry those messages forward into season 2, "then we were going to be on the right path."

The first season earned a 64% critic score and a 69% audience score on Rotten Tomatoes. Now, a little over a year later, the cast and crew are back for the second season, with Game of Thrones Alfie Allen joining the cast as billionaire Nolan Reeves.

After The Institute season 1 finale saw the characters destroy the facility, Luke, Tim and the other survivors are now working towards their next goal: to uncover the crimes committed by the Institute.

King's stories have been adapted numerous times throughout his career, a trend that's not slowing down anytime soon. Recently, there have been movies based on The Monkey, The Long Walk and The Running Man as well as Mike Flanagan's hit new TV show Carrie, which has already been renewed for a second season.

The Institute is also coming back for season 2, as the drama, secrets and conspiracies continue to grow with each episode.

The three Americans currently living off Earth just made sure their electoral voices were heard.

Anil Menon told us today (Nov. 3) that he and fellow NASA astronauts Luke Delaney and Jessica Watkins voted in the U.S. midterm elections from the International Space Station (ISS).

"Yes, you can even vote from space! All three U.S. crew members had the privilege of voting from space station. We filled out an electronic ballot and sent it securely to the election officials," Menon wrote in an X post this morning.

That post included a 29-second video, in which Menon — wearing, like many of us today, an "I voted" sticker — shows us his "voting booth."

It's actually his room on the ISS, Menon explained. "Inside of it is my computer, where I was able to fill out an electronic form and send it to Harris County so I could vote today," he said.

Most NASA astronauts register to vote in Texas' Harris County; it's home to Johnson Space Center in Houston, the agency's human spaceflight hub.

Americans have been able to vote from space since 1997, when Texas passed a law allowing this to happen. The first person to take advantage of the legislation was NASA astronaut David Wolf, who cast his ballot from Russia's Mir space station that same year. (The ISS didn't welcome its first crew until November 2000.)

"Just like any other American away from home, astronauts may fill out a Federal Post Card Application to request an absentee ballot," NASA officials wrote in an astronaut-voting explainer.

The process is somewhat involved, however.

"After an astronaut fills out an electronic ballot aboard the orbiting laboratory, the document flows through NASA's Tracking and Data Relay Satellite System to a ground antenna at the agency's White Sands Test Facility in Las Cruces, New Mexico," they added. "From New Mexico, NASA transfers the ballot to the Mission Control Center at NASA Johnson and then on to the county clerk responsible for casting the ballot. To preserve the vote's integrity, the ballot is encrypted and accessible only by the astronaut and the clerk."

Delaney and Watkins launched to the ISS on Oct. 1 on SpaceX's Crew-13 mission. Menon has been there since mid-July, arriving aboard a Russian Soyuz spacecraft.

The trio currently share the orbiting lab with four other people — Canadian Space Agency astronaut Joshua Kutryk and cosmonauts Anna Kikina, Pyotr Dubrov and Sergey Teteryatnikov. Kutryk and Teteryatnikov are members of Crew-13, whereas Dubrov and Kikina shared the Soyuz with Menon.

This year things changed, and I would likely save a lot on a catastrophic plan. Irony is I no longer qualify (See above posts). But for reference, my options this year include:

Florida Blue MyBlue Catastrophic, $942/mo premium, $12K deductible, $12K max OOP (which I don't qualify for)
Florida Blue MyBlue Bronze, $1178/mo premium, $11975 deductible, $12K max OOP (yes this is virtually the same as the Catastrophic plan)
Amerihealth Bronze Enhanced, $1383/mo premium, $4500 deductible, $12K max OOP

So right now I'm deciding between what's effectively a catastrophic plan, or a plan with a more reasonable deductible that's going to cost $2460 more per year.
What really needs to happen IMO is very high deductible plans need to be added to the exchange, say maybe two options of $25,000 or $50,000 deductible with no annual coverage limits, and very low premiums of $100-$300 depending on age ranging from youngest to age 64. This way at people have SOME kind of option to protect their assets from being wiped out due to a major unforeseen health event while not robbing them blind every month for a plan most otherwise could be simply paying out of pocket to cover monthly medical expenses (if any).
Agree 100%. I don't understand why I can't buy this product; it's exactly what I want/need. And before anyone answers "The reason is that it would not be profitable", my response is "How do we know?" Insurers aren't allowed to offer a plan like this due to ACA requirements.
I've been watching for this over the last few years and have yet to see anything impeding the ability to offer such plans other than the carriers, hospitals and lobbyists. At this point I am fully convinced the only reason carriers aren't offering it is because some people would shift from other plans and reduce their existing profits, and hospitals may be stuck trying to collect on bills larger than they "prefer" to chase if people end up not paying. 7 Upcoming Fantasy Shows, Ranked by Hype

2026 has been a pretty solid year for fantasy television. So far, the year has seen new seasons of shows like House of the Dragon and Interview with the Vampire, as well as brand-new series like Avatar: Seven Havens and Witch Hat Atelier. However, as fun as those shows are, there are several exciting fantasy shows in the pipeline right now that could potentially blow those hits out of the water.

With release dates across the rest of 2026, 2027, and beyond, several upcoming shows have garnered sky-high hype among fantasy fans, drumming up lots of conversations ahead of their premieres. Not all of the conversations are positive, of course, and there’s at least one upcoming project that’s been talked about for all the wrong reasons, but on the whole, the future of fantasy television does look pretty bright. Without further ado, here are some of the most fascinating upcoming fantasy shows ranked by hype.

Based on J. K. Rowling’s bestselling fantasy books, Harry Potter is an upcoming fantasy series that’s the second adaptation of the novels after the film series. Starring Dominic McLaughlin as Harry, Alastair Stout as Ron Weasley, and Arabella Stanton as Hermione Granger, the show’s first season adapts the first book, Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone, and details Harry’s discovery of the wizarding world and his first year at Hogwarts School of Witchcraft and Wizardry. The ensemble cast also includes John Lithgow as Albus Dumbledore, Janet McTeer as Minerva McGonagall, Paapa Essiedu as Severus Snape, and Nick Frost as Rubeus Hagrid.

HBO has big plans for Harry Potter, with early reports estimating that the new show could span a decade of production. The series has already been renewed for a second season as well. However, the whole project has been largely met with the opposite of hype. Comparisons to the movies (the last of which came out in 2011), negative public opinion of J.K. Rowling, and the idea that the whole project is a naked cash grab have all led to a generally dismal reception from the core fanbase ahead of the show’s release.

Created by Richard Garfield, Magic: The Gathering is one of the most successful game franchises of all time. A trading card game that’s expanded to various other media, the franchise is set across multiple realities and centered on Planeswalkers, dueling wizards with the ability to cross between worlds. While the game has been adapted as comics and novels before, it’s yet to get a movie or TV adaptation.

The Magic: The Gathering series adaptation has been in development since 2019 and has undergone many behind-the-scenes changes, but it finally looks like the show might actually make it to screens. The new show will be a Netflix animated series with Terry Matalas (12 Monkeys) serving as showrunner. The series was previewed at the 2025 Annecy International Animation Film Festival, but it doesn’t yet have a release date. Nonetheless, fans of the franchise are certainly excited for the show’s release and for the upcoming movie adaptation directed by Matt Johnson (Tony).

One of the bestselling romantasy books of the 2020s, Rebecca Yarros’s Fourth Wing is the first book of the Empyrean new adult fantasy romance series. A series adaptation of the books has been in the works at Prime Video since 2023, with Yarros serving as an executive producer on the project alongside Michael B. Jordan, Lisa Joy, Jonathan Nolan, Liz Pelletier, Athena Wickham, and showrunner Meredith Averill. The show, like the book, follows the story of Violet Sorrengail, who joins the Basgiath War College and trains to become a dragon rider, encountering deadly quests, gruelling competition, and unexpected romance. Esther McGregor will star as Violet, with Emilio Sakraya as her rival/love interest, Xaden Riorson.

Fourth Wing is one of the biggest BookTok hits of the romantasy genre, so its series adaptation has a lot of people very excited, and with good reason. Featuring fast-paced action, high-tension romance, and, of course, dragons, Fourth Wing has all the ingredients to become a fantasy TV landmark to rival Game of Thrones. Whether it will actually achieve that potential remains to be seen.

Mike Flanagan’s The Dark Tower is an upcoming multi-season series adaptation of the fantasy book series by Stephen King. Published between 1982 and 2012, the books revolve around Roland Deschain, a gunslinger on an unending quest across a surreal landscape in search of the titular tower, which sits at the center of a vast multiverse. The books were previously adapted as a 2017 film, which was widely panned by critics, but the series is a complete reboot of the story, and it’s been reported that it will star Sam Strike and Jasper Pääkkönen.

Spanning eight connected novels, one novella, a children’s book, and connections to almost all of King’s celebrated novels, The Dark Tower is the holy grail of Stephen King adaptations. It’s easy to see why the upcoming series has so many people excited, even though it doesn’t have a release date or any concrete details as yet. What little we know about The Dark Tower so far suggests an in-depth adaptation that could prove to be Flanagan’s most ambitious project yet and one of the most fascinating fantasy shows ever made. Or, like the movie, it could just be an expensive failure; only time will tell.

It’s no exaggeration to say that Brandon Sanderson is one of the biggest names in fantasy literature today, and The Stormlight Archive novel series, part of Sanderson’s Cosmere universe, is one of the author’s most popular hits. Currently comprising five novels and two novellas (with plans for a total of 10 novels), it’s an expansive saga set on the battered world of Roshar, which is threatened by magical storms, political tensions, and a looming cataclysm. The books have already inspired a VR experience and a tabletop game, and will soon become an Apple TV series.

As part of Sanderson’s deal with the streaming platform, the author will be personally leading adaptations of his Mistborn novels and The Stormlight Archive, the first as a movie and the second as a TV show, the first season of which will consist of 10 episodes.
