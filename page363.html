Marvel Officially Confirms 7 More Characters For Avengers: Doomsday

📅 Sep 25, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Warning: Contains spoilers for Avengers Endgame: Encore AND VisionQuest!Even more characters are in Avengers: Doomsday than previously known, as Marvel has confirmed some additional members. Ahead of the blockbuster's December 18 release date, one of the lingering questions is who else from the Marvel Cinematic Universe, and the broader Marvel multiverse, will make surprise appearances.

Marvel Studios originally confirmed 27 Avengers: Doomsday cast members with its chair reveal video as production began. This confirmed Doctor Doom, Thor, Ant-Man, Captain America, Loki, the Thunderbolts, the Fantastic Four, Wakandans, a multiversal X-Men team, and more would appear. During filming and the marketing campaign, more Doomsday characters were confirmed, including Steve Rogers and Peggy Carter.

However, some major MCU characters were not announced to be returning. Speculation has continued about whether Scarlet Witch, Deadpool, Wolverine, a Spider-Man, Captain Marvel, Doctor Strange, and more will appear. Less than three months before Avengers: Doomsday hits theaters, seven more characters have officially been confirmed.

This is primarily thanks to Avengers Endgame: Encore. The special re-release of the Infinity Saga's culmination includes new footage setting up Doomsday. The new scenes in Avengers Endgame: Encore become Marvel's way of telling everyone that the following characters will appear in Avengers: Doomsday after all. But the MCU confirmed another key addition through VisionQuest's finale.

Scarlet Witch is confirmed for Avengers: Doomsday at long last. Even though audiences last saw Wanda Maximoff die in Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness's ending, Elizabeth Olsen's fan-favorite character is alive once again. This was confirmed through VisionQuest's post-credits scene, which was added to Disney+ as a surprise (it wasn't even included in press screeners).

The scene shows Doctor Doom arrive at the ruins of Mount Wundagore shortly after Scarlet Witch made the structure collapse on top of her. He lifts the destruction using magic until he finds Wanda's lifeless body. He commands, "Scarlet Witch, awaken. Your power will be mine," and Wanda's eyes immediately open as she gasps for breath.

This is Marvel's way of announcing that Scarlet Witch does have a role in Doomsday after all. She is expected to be one of Doom's three Latverian witches and fight on his side as the multiverse collapses. It's a reveal that pairs perfectly with the first of the new additions Avengers Endgame: Encore confirms.

Despite Mark Ruffalo previously denying that he was in Avengers: Doomsday, this is no longer true. Avengers Endgame: Encore includes a post-credits scene showing Bruce Banner in a bunker after the events of Spider-Man: Brand New Day to isolate himself from the world, for his safety and the safety of others.

Bruce's return in Doomsday is set up when he is surprisingly visited by Doctor Doom. The MCU's new villain says he's there to "collect" him, with Doom using magic to transport Hulk away. Bruce does not go willingly or without getting too angry, as a Hulk roar erupts while he is whisked away.

It is not directly confirmed in the scene where Doom takes Hulk, but his castle in Latveria on Earth-828 makes sense. The implication is that Hulk will be used as a weapon by Doctor Doom in his multiversal conquest plans, especially when Scarlet Witch's resurrection is taken into consideration. At the very least, Doom has removed the incredibly strong Avenger from the equation of defending the Sacred Timeline.

Ruffalo's Hulk has been in every Avengers movie, so his return in Doomsday would keep the streak alive. The difference this time is that Hulk could only function as a villain for the Avengers if he's aligned with Doom. While some may argue the scene is more so set up for why Hulk isn't in Doomsday and only returns in Avengers: Secret Wars, that is not the expectation Marvel has set by releasing it now.

The second of Avengers Endgame: Encore's post-credits scenes confirm multiple members of the Time Variance Authority (TVA) will appear, with Mobius M. Mobius at the forefront. Owen Wilson's TVA analyst was introduced in the Loki Disney+ show, taking on more responsibility to protect the multiverse as the series unfolded.

While he was last seen walking away from that life in Loki season 2's ending, he's back in business for Avengers: Doomsday. The Encore scene shows him walking into the TVA headquarters for a day's work, only to learn that an incursion is taking place. Mobius quickly realizes this is not an isolated event, as 3000 universes are experiencing them. He is seemingly in charge of the organization's day-to-day operations now.

Once the TVA realizes that more worlds are colliding, Ouroboros is the one to understand what is happening. The other familiar face among the TVA station is Casey. Played by Eugene Cordero, the TVA clerk was a recurring character in Loki. He became part of the core group fighting to protect the multiverse and change the TVA's purpose. This led him to become part of a new council to help steer the organization down the right path.

Casey is the first to notice that two Earths are firing missiles at each other, hoping to destroy the other Earth to stop the incursion. She might not wind up having too large a functional role in the movie beyond helping audiences who didn't watch Loki quickly get up to speed on the TVA's purpose in the multiverse landscape.

One of fashion’s earliest street-style photographers, Tommy Ton has always had a unique point of view on what makes an outfit sing. There is something about red that fashion simply cannot get enough of. Crimson, poppy, scarlet, cherry, oxblood—the shades are seemingly endless, and so is our fascination with them. What began as an easy way to inject color into an otherwise neutral wardrobe has evolved into something much bigger. At this point, I would say fashion has developed a full-blown case of scarlet fever.

Traditionally, I associated red with evening dressing. There was something inherently glamorous, perhaps even a little theatrical, about wearing it after dark. But that distinction has all but disappeared. Red has found its way into every corner of the wardrobe, from casual daytime looks to evening ensembles, and some of the most compelling ways I've seen it worn lately are decidedly understated.

Not so long ago, a red accessory was the easiest entry point: a handbag, a pair of shoes, or perhaps a flash of red socks. It was the little punctuation mark that made an outfit feel more considered. Today, however, we're embracing red as the starting point of an entire look. Take Polo Ralph Lauren's red Harrington jacket, a beautifully executed Chanel cap-toe pump, or even a luxurious red sweater casually tied around the waist. I particularly love the latter, bringing an unexpected flash of color to jeans or tailored trousers. It's a simple styling gesture that can transform an entire outfit.

And then there is the appeal of wearing red from head to toe. Who could forget the monochromatic look that came down Michael Rider's Celine runway this past season? There was an almost collective gasp. Seeing such a saturated look presented with that kind of conviction was a reminder of just how arresting the color can be. Red wasn't merely an accent; it was the entire statement.

There is a certain pleasure in committing to one color, allowing different textures, proportions, and shades to create interest. A scarlet knit paired with matching trousers, or a cherry-red anorak worn over an equally saturated ensemble, makes quite a statement. After seasons of pared-back palettes and quiet luxury, there is something refreshing about dressing with a little more conviction.

Depending on the shade, red can be romantic, provocative, sporty, polished, or even a little rebellious. Cherry has a playful quality, scarlet carries a certain theatricality, while oxblood offers a moodier, more understated interpretation. Perhaps that's why our infatuation with the color has lasted so long. It has been reinterpreted countless times, yet somehow manages to feel exciting all over again.

Whatever the reason, I'm not quite ready to give up on red. Whether worn as a subtle accent, an everyday staple, or embraced from head to toe, it has a way of making even the most familiar wardrobe feel exciting again. And judging by the way we're wearing it now, I don't see our scarlet fever subsiding anytime soon.

Is more more, or is less more? For years, we’ve been told that good taste is knowing when to stop. Coco Chanel’s famous advice about looking in the mirror before leaving the house and removing one thing has become something of a fashion commandment for many. But I’ve always wondered what happens when we do the opposite. What if, instead of taking something away, you add another necklace, another ring, another earring, another bracelet? What if the most interesting thing about getting dressed is not knowing when to stop, but having the confidence not to?

After seasons of quiet luxury and wardrobes edited down to the essentials, I find myself drawn to a more expressive approach to accessorizing. Somewhere along the way, restraint became synonymous with sophistication, and the absence of adornment was elevated into a marker of taste. But minimalism, for all its elegance, can become its own uniform. When everyone is wearing the same monogram pendant or oversized belt buckle, how much individuality is left?

Jewelry is most interesting when it disrupts that uniformity. I love the woman with rings on nearly every finger, a wrist stacked with cuffs and bracelets, necklaces layered at different lengths and crowded with pendants, and earrings that seem to be having a conversation of their own. There is something wonderfully assured about a look that refuses to be fully resolved. It doesn’t ask whether every piece matches, whether the proportions are balanced, or whether one more accessory might be too much. It simply embraces the pleasure of wearing what you love.

What strikes me most about this more-is-more approach is that it rarely feels calculated. The best examples are always a little imperfect: a delicate chain mixed with a heavy pendant; a diamond ring beside an inexpensive silver band; a charm-laden brooch that clinks with every movement. Nothing needs to match, nor should it. The most compelling combinations look as if they came together over time, collected from different places, people, and phases of one’s life. The result feels lived-in rather than meticulously styled.

Perhaps this is why the current appetite for accessorizing feels so refreshing. Not everything needs to be versatile, timeless, or sensible. Some things are worth wearing simply because they make you happy.

So the next time you reach for just one necklace, consider adding another. Then perhaps a third. Put on the rings you usually save for evenings out, stack your bracelets, and let the earrings be a little outrageous. You can always take something off. Should I Use a Sleep Tracker With a Smart Bed?

Some smart beds offer contactless sleep tracking, which eliminates the need for an additional wearable. That said, they do carry some limitations. They’re generally not as accurate as contact-based trackers and can overestimate sleep stages and miscalculate duration. They often can’t track the same breadth of physiological measurements as smartwatches and smart rings do. And they can misattribute data from a partner or pet sharing the bed. But they’re convenient, especially if you dislike wearing trackers to bed.

Personally, I still sleep with my Apple Watch Series 12 and/or Oura Ring 5 to bed to compare. But, if I forget to wear either, my smart bed is auto-tracking my slumber in the background.

You are very likely going to want an adjustable base to go with your smart bed in order to enjoy everything your smart bed has to offer. This is especially true if you are purchasing one with the expectation of being able to move the top and bottom of the bed into different angles, like “TV watching” and “zero gravity” settings.

What’s the Difference Between an Adjustable Bed and a Smart Bed?

Generally speaking, adjustable beds and smart beds are similar in that they both can raise or lower different sections of your mattress, with a remote control or companion app. Smart beds take it up a notch by incorporating additional features, such as sleep tracking, temperature control, and automated adjustments.

Can a Smart Bed Work for All Sleeping Positions?

Smart beds often come in adjustable firmness levels, and therefore can work for all sleeping positions. However, just like with any other mattress, the fabric technology is a huge deciding factor when it comes to comfort.

For example, the Bryte Balance Pro is a much better choice for stomach sleepers or if you have a heavier body type. Coil support simply does not compare to even the firmest of inflated air chambers, which are usually what make smart beds' firmness levels “adjustable.” On the other hand, the Rejuvenate has a softer feel right off the bat because of its memory foam layers, making it ideal for side sleepers who need pressure relief.

Do I Need Wi-Fi Connectivity for My Smart Bed to Work?

Yes—if the power goes out, your smart bed will likely go offline. Smart beds are factored into smart home technology, meaning they rely on Wi-Fi for full functionality, especially to track sleep data and maintain the firmness level you've chosen.
