Cortaca Jug rivalry returns to Yankee Stadium. What to know

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

For the second time in four years, one of college football's biggest rivalries will be played at one of pro sports' most recognizable venues.

Unbeaten SUNY Cortland will face Route 13 rival Ithaca College in the annual Cortaca Jug game at 1 p.m. Saturday, Nov. 14 at Yankee Stadium in the Bronx.

Cortland, three years removed from a national championship, brings a 9-0 record into the contest and is ranked ninth in Division III by D3football.com and 10th by the American Football Coaches Association. The Red Dragons finished with a 7-0 record in Empire 8 play with last week's 42-24 victory over SUNY Brockport, securing an automatic berth into the NCAA Division III Tournament while extending their conference winning streak to 40 games.

Ithaca is 7-2 and comes off a 14-10 loss to Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute in a game that decided the Liberty League title. The Bombers' seven-game winning streak came to a close and they ended up 6-1 in league play.

This regular-season finale will be the third meeting between the teams in the New York City area. Ithaca beat Cortland, 32-20, in front of an announced Division III record crowd of 45,161 fans at MetLife Stadium in East Rutherford, New Jersey in 2019. In 2022, Ithaca beat Cortland, 34-17, in front of 40,232 fans at Yankee Stadium.

"Cortland and Ithaca both help set the standard in Division III athletics, and our Cortaca Jug rivalry demonstrates that shared excellence," Mike Urtz, Cortland's athletic director, said in a press release when it was announced the rivalry would return to Yankee Stadium.

"It's been called the 'biggest little game in the nation' for a reason, and it's fitting to be played in one of the most iconic sports venues in the entire world."

The Ithaca-Cortland rivalry dates back to 1930, when Cortland topped Ithaca by a 12-0 score. Ithaca won last year's meeting, 26-21, at home.

This will be the 67th Cortaca Jug game and the 84th all-time meeting between the schools. Ithaca leads the series 44-36-3.

The Cortaca Jug made its debut in 1959, the first Cortaca Jug game. There are now three Cortaca Jugs after the first two ran out of space to chronicle scores.

Nicholas Lang, an all-state pick in football and basketball at Union-Endicott, is the top receiving weapon for the Bombers with 45 catches for 712 yards and seven touchdowns this season. Lang, a senior, caught touchdown passes of 44 and 7 yards in last year's triumph over Cortland. He has 108 yards and a touchdown on the ground this season.

IC quarterback Michael Reed has completed 134 of 222 passes for 15 touchdowns, with four interceptions. He is second on the team with 516 rushing yards on 102 carries, including seven touchdowns.

Chris Scully is the Bombers' top rusher with 516 yards and 12 touchdowns on 102 rushes.

Red Dragons quarterback Mike Rescigno, who starred at Arlington High in New York's Section 1, has rushed 120 times for 902 yards and 10 touchdowns. The junior's passing numbers include 115 completions in 182 attempts, with 16 touchdowns and four interceptions.

Ethan Gallo is second on the team with 587 rushing yards on 118 attempts, including 14 touchdowns.

Four players have at least 300 receiving yards, led by Jacque LaPrarie with 19 catches for 415 yards and six touchdowns.

Aidan Lochner leads the defense with 62 tackles, and Joe Galea has a team-high 5 1/2 sacks.

Along with Lang, other Section 4 high school alumni on Ithaca's roster are defensive back Cooper Hughes from U-E, receiver Jack Gannon from Vestal, and defensive lineman Lucas Boccasini from Owego Free Academy.

Section 4 players on Cortland's roster include receiver Will Brainard from Trumansburg, offensive lineman Talan O'Connor from Groton, tight end Evan English from Owego, and running back Jay Miles from Dryden.

LinkedIn brings its B2B ad targeting to U.K. connected TVs

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Join us Dec. 1-3 in Nashville for the Digiday Programmatic Marketing Summit

In its latest effort to capitalize on growing demand for video, LinkedIn is expanding its CTV ads to the U.K.

The launch follows its staged U.S. rollout, which began in April 2024, before becoming widely available across the U.S. and Canada in 2025. It brings LinkedIn’s professional audience targeting to CTV, allowing advertisers to reach U.K. decision-makers by job role, industry and seniority while they watch ad-supported streaming content. For example, a software company looking to reach senior IT decision-makers could use LinkedIn’s targeting to serve CTV ads to viewers in relevant job roles and industries, rather than relying solely on broader demographic or household-level targeting.

So far, the platform has partnered with LG and Titan OS for its U.K. CTV ads offering, and it’s available to all advertisers in the market.

“When you think about professional identities and how you want to reach and engage with them, we’re a platform where people tell us who they are,” said Jae Oh, senior director of product management at LinkedIn. “They get verified for the jobs that they have, and we’re able to support a higher fidelity of reach when it comes to how you reach those audiences at scale.”

LinkedIn is positioning CTV as more than just an awareness play, arguing that its professional audience targeting can help advertisers reach decision-makers earlier in the buying process and influence them further down the funnel.

“For so long CTV has been seen as like you naturally think it’s an awareness play, but actually where we’ve seen a real success is when clients use it as part of a full-funnel strategy,” said Milka Privodanova, vice president of LinkedIn Marketing Solutions, large customers — international at LinkedIn.

The company claims that people exposed to a brand’s CTV ad cost 30% less to convert into sales leads. Cisco, which has already tested the targeting update, saw 52% incremental reach beyond its LinkedIn in-feed activity, 141% higher lead form completion rates and 35% higher click-through rates among audiences exposed to its CTV ads, according to its LinkedIn case study.

But the proposition comes with questions over cost and targeting accuracy. Accuracast, for example, evaluated LinkedIn’s CTV offering when it was only available in the U.S. and found its pricing significantly higher than other direct CTV providers, alongside high minimum spend requirements.

“There’s definitely a business case for it — our B2B clients would love to get more reach and eyeballs on connected TVs — but if we’re going to pay LinkedIn’s usual premium pricing, we’d want more clarity on how LinkedIn plans to accurately target their users on these devices,” said the agency’s group CEO Farhad Divecha.

The idea is that advertisers can use CTV to introduce their brand to decision-makers, retarget and nurture those audiences through video and other LinkedIn ad formats to encourage them to take action. The platform is also looking to improve how advertisers measure CTV’s contribution to business outcomes beyond clicks and lead form completions. Oh said LinkedIn is also investing in revenue and pipeline attribution, alongside incrementality testing, to help advertisers understand CTV’s contribution to business outcomes.

“You will see deeper investment when it comes to incrementality testing or experimentation to prove that these different tactics you’re employing work for you,” Oh said.

The U.K. CTV expansion gives LinkedIn another way to tap into the demand for video, by  extending its video offering beyond the platform and onto the TV screen.

Video advertising on LinkedIn is growing 25% year-over-year, according to Privodanova, though she declined to share how much CTV contributes to that growth.

LinkedIn has been stitching together the pieces of its CTV business since 2024 when it lined up Roku and Samsung Ads as supply partners and launch LinkedIn Premier, a managed product built on NBCUniversal’s streaming inventory, A deal for premium Paramount inventory followed in mid-2025, along with integrations with Innovid and Sprinkly meant to make CTV campaigns easier to run.

“As a premium advertising platform, LinkedIn will need to demonstrate that its audience data can translate into meaningful reach and measurable impact,” said Andy Hunt, head of paid media at agency Brave Bison. “Targeting professionals through household devices presents its own challenges around signal quality, accuracy and attribution. Advertisers will want to understand how effectively CTV exposure can be connected to wider campaign performance, rather than simply adding another awareness metric.”

The Qantas Airbus A321XLR That Has 3 Fewer Economy Seats

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

In a move that puts traveler comfort ahead of scraping every shred of profit for a jetliner, Qantas pulled out three seats from its Airbus A321XLR that are requisite revenue-generating hardware for most shareholders. The aircraft, tail number VH-OGD, is the first delivery of a 48-seat subfleet that will include a fourth bathroom, using space that was reserved for the usual row of seats in the main cabin. The modification highlights a design tension inherent to the long-range narrowbody platform.

The cabin reconfiguration was designed in response to customer feedback that followed the first three deliveries of the type. The overarching tone from passengers, aviation commentators, and the media was scathing and derisive, often framing the issue as a ‘legacy stink’ left behind by the airline’s highly controversial former CEO, Alan Joyce.

Thus, instead of 180 economy and 20 business, the updated cabin of Qantas’ long-haul narrowbody now seats 197 travelers in ‘coach.’ The customer feedback Qantas received was immediate and highly critical of the airline's management, forcing a mid-stream redesign before the bulk of the fleet was even built. Consequently, the airline also committed to retrofitting the first three A321XLR aircraft already in its fleet with the extra toilet during future heavy maintenance checks.

Qantas ordered the new extra-long-range Airbus single-aisles in 2022 to replace aging Boeing 737-800s. In 2025, Qantas launched commercial A321XLR flights, beginning September service with Sydney and Melbourne. Sky TG24 noted that while marketing focused on cabin width and fuel efficiency, the media attention was honed in on the jet’s layout flaws. It was specifically highlighted that the first three aircraft of Australia’s flag carrier had fewer toilets in economy than its low-cost competitors, Ryanair, Scoot, and Jetstar.

For comparison, a standard Ryanair Boeing 737 carries 197 passengers with three toilets, like the new Qantas A321XLR. That ratio averages 66 people per facility. A Scoot Airbus A320 accommodates 180 passengers with three toilets, or 60 per facility. Meanwhile, a Jetstar A320 seats 186 with three units, averaging 62. In contrast, the original Qantas cabin provided just two toilets for 180 seats, at a 50% elevated ratio of 90 people per facility.

As Runway Girl Network reported, the airline stated that the first aircraft will be finalized in 2022 with three toilets to match outgoing 737 specifications. Management has since promised all 48 jets on order will feature four toilets following the updated design. The layout assigns three facilities to economy and one to business class. However, The Guardian has emphasized that the first three airframes will likely operate unchanged for approximately three years before undergoing a formal retrofit.

The first modified four-lavatory aircraft was currently scheduled for a December 2025 delivery. It didn’t ultimately reach Australia until late February of this year, according to Australian Aviation. The arrival resolved the initial cabin design crisis, shifting the industry conversation away from consumer complaints. Yet, analysts turned to the manufacturing choices and cabin design priorities that had led to the ill-equipped jets’ arrival in the first place.

Packing 200 passengers into a narrow fuselage turns cabin calculus into a test of passenger patience. While three total lavatories average one per 67-person full cabin, the forward facility is reserved for business class. That left only two rear toilets for 180 economy travelers, inflating the ratio to 90 people per facility. An arrangement that fell below the amenities relative to the legacy aircraft it replaced despite Qantas’ claims.

One Mile at a Time calculated that the outgoing Boeing 737-800 provided two toilets for a cabin of 162 economy seats, which equals 81 passengers each. The A321XLR added 18 coach seats compared to the 737 without expanding toilet capacity. Airline Geeks also reported that the legacy jet carried 174 total customers, increasing total flyer volume by 15%.

That design deviated drastically from the standard of the rest of the jets in the full-service airline’s fleet. While budget carriers maximize efficiency by allowing open passenger access to all facilities, AeroLOPA noted that Qantas' own standards typically maintain a ratio between 42 and 49 passengers per lavatory on long-haul airframes. This underscored the unusual deficit in the new narrowbody layout, which fell well behind the carrier's traditional passenger-comfort standard in its international widebody fleet.

General industry benchmarks added to the negative optics that resulted after the first three planes began service. Other prominent ‘legacy style’ operators of the Airbus A321 family, including Delta Air Lines, routinely equip their economy cabins with at least three dedicated lavatories on short and medium range routes.

What will the Australian flag carrier choose?

The A321XLR delivers a single-aisle operational reach of 4,700 nautical miles (8,704 km), supporting flight durations approaching 11 hours. This capability enables operators like Iberia to service long-haul routes like Madrid to Boston.

Many A321neos are equipped with Airbus' SpaceFlex module, which combines the rear galley and lavatory footprint which increases passenger seating capacity. Yet, according to AeroLOPA, mid-cabin restrooms were first rejected by all six of the original launch customers: Iberia, American Airlines, Aer Lingus, Air Canada, Qantas, and United Airlines. Due to that design choice, facilities were concentrated at the extremities of the aircraft, which caused serious bottlenecks during lengthy flights lasting eight to eleven hours.

The practical consequences immediately manifested during real-world operations for more carriers than just Qantas. Iberia’s configuration placed all three economy lavatories at the extreme rear of the fuselage, with one unit dual-functioning as a flight attendant jumpseat. This layout restricted active availability to just two units for 168 economy passengers. It resulted in persistent queues that routinely clogged the rear galley workspace, according to at least one Runway Girl Network review.

Even so, Qantas still implemented the most aggressive configuration among these initial operators at the outset. Deploying the dense 200-seat layout well exceeds the 182 seats found aboard Iberia and Air Canada variants or the 184 on Aer Lingus, 155 on American, and 150 on United jets.

Qantas will have one of the highest passenger capacities onboard its Airbus A321XLRs.

Tail number VH-OGD was the first A321XLR incorporating a mid-cabin product redesign driven by preliminary operational feedback. Eliminating a three-seat economy row contracted total cabin capacity from 200 to 197 seats, leaving 20 in business and 177 in economy. This adjustment reduced the economy passenger-to-toilet ratio from 90 to approximately 59, or a 34% reduction in demand.

The rear aisle congestion also caused significant disruptions to the crew's workflow. Flight attendants frequently had to pause meal services, back up their food carts, and manually clear crowds out of the aisle just to pass. Because the new front-economy toilet can accommodate a substantial portion of the cabin's passengers, the back aisle remains open and makes everything easier for the cabin crew, which also improves the customer experience in-flight.

While sacrificing three seats removes only 1.5% of the total aircraft inventory, the configuration change greatly impacted the amenity available on each flight. The cut marginally limits the maximum revenue potential on Qantas’ routes with high demand and slightly raises unit operating expenses per seat. The thumb rest gets the biggest rethink. It’s usually the most wasted area of a mouse, a bit of plastic to rest your thumb on. Here, it’s the way into the new virtual dial, called the Actions Ring, which makes it feel like part of the design rather than an afterthought.

The haptics are a lovely touch. They kick in when you make a selection and when you open and cycle through the Actions Ring, so I can keep my eyes on the screen, click and scroll, and still feel a little resistance and feedback. This also helps with gesture control, too, if you go as far as needing that kind of input. It’s subtle, but it makes the mouse feel more connected to what you’re doing.

As you may have guessed, the Actions Ring is the real star of the new features. It’s an on-screen dial that expands what the mouse can do, making it easy to open folders or switch between apps without dropping into the Windows menus. You can tailor the ring to the job, from something as basic as Google to Photoshop or DaVinci Resolve. Support isn’t universal, though. The best digital art and design apps like Krita, Affinity Photo and CorelDRAW are covered, but smaller apps like Escape Motions Rebelle aren’t a default option, so you’ll need a workaround: assign function keys in Options+, then match them to the app’s own shortcuts for undo, clear layer or brush toggles. It works, but you need to know your keys or set them up ahead of time.

This also means getting to grips with Options+ and its app features, which is a lot of learning for a mouse. I’d like it to work more without that software running in the background. But if you persevere, it adds real depth and makes the mouse faster to use and more flexible. Once it’s set up, you’ll love it. You can tweak everything from haptic strength to scroll speed, and I’d probably be moaning if I couldn’t fiddle.

The haptics, gestures, and Actions Ring are a step beyond the MX Master 3S, and creatives who map shortcuts to the ring will get the most from this mouse.

So, should you buy the MX Master 4 Special Edition? If you’re coming from a basic mouse, absolutely. The ergonomics, scrolling and customisation make it a brilliant tool for anyone who works at a computer all day, whether in creative apps or simply navigating menus and Google.

If you already own an MX Master 3S, it comes down to how you work. The haptics and Actions Ring are genuinely useful and, for creative apps, a new, easier way to work, so it’s worth it if you’ll map shortcuts to the ring or, like me, hate sorting through Windows menus and would rather one-click into the regular apps and files. For everyone else, they’re not enough to make a perfectly good mouse obsolete.
