The “Heart Like a Truck” singer ditched her signature flare pants and Western hat for a classic Oscar de la Renta gown at the 2026 CMA Awards.

Wilson—who is serving as host for the evening’s festivities—looked stunning in a light yellow feathered gown, which featured a short train and a halter neckline. The 34-year-old—who is also nominated for Entertainer of the Year, Female Vocalist of the Year and Single of the Year for her hit “Somewhere Over Laredo”—has made no secret of her penchant for bell bottoms and a cowboy hat over the years, explaining that she was inspired to create her signature look after arriving in Nashville.

“I get to Nashville and I realized pretty quickly that as a female in this business, you have to do something that's a little outside of the box,” she shared during an interview with the Country Music Hall of Fame and Museum in April. “I wasn't gonna get away with wearing a ball cap and skinny jeans.”

“It's cool to look out into the crowd now and see everybody in their bell bottoms and their hats,” she shared. “It feels like a little bit of a movement and it just makes me happy.”

A Study of 50,000 Children Reveals the Real Impact of Daily Iron Pills

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

A major scientific review involving more than 50,000 children and adolescents reveals that daily iron supplementation can significantly reduce the risk of iron deficiency anemia.

A daily iron supplement can substantially reduce the risk of iron deficiency anemia in children and teenagers, but it does not appear to improve growth or protect against common illnesses. These findings come from a major Cochrane review of 114 studies involving more than 50,000 participants.

Led by researchers at Cornell University’s Joan Klein Jacobs Center for Precision Nutrition and Health, the review examined daily iron supplements taken alone or combined with folic acid and other vitamins and minerals. Many of the studies were conducted in Asia and involved children younger than five.

The strongest evidence showed that iron alone probably reduces the risk of iron deficiency anemia compared with receiving no treatment or a placebo. Combining iron with folic acid probably offers similar protection, although that conclusion was based on just one study. Supplements containing iron alongside other vitamins and minerals may also help. Evidence suggests that iron supplementation can reduce anemia and iron deficiency more broadly, but confidence in several findings remains limited.

Iron is essential for producing hemoglobin, the protein in red blood cells that transports oxygen from the lungs throughout the body. When iron levels become too low, the body cannot produce enough healthy hemoglobin, potentially leading to iron deficiency anemia.

“Iron deficiency remains one of the most common nutritional problems affecting children worldwide,” says first author Dr Kripa Rajagopalan. “Our review shows that daily iron supplements do what they are meant to do, which is reduce iron deficiency anemia, without any measurable impact on the common illnesses parents and health workers worry about.”

Although iron supplements helped prevent iron deficiency anemia, the researchers found no improvement in children’s height or weight relative to others of the same age and sex. Iron alone also had no measurable effect on the occurrence of cough, fever, or respiratory illness.

Adding folic acid to iron made little to no difference in rates of diarrhea or respiratory illness. Results for other supplement combinations were less conclusive, with evidence ranging from moderate to very low certainty.

Daily iron supplementation may cause stool discoloration, but iron alone appears to have little or no effect on vomiting or diarrhea. Its relationship with constipation remains uncertain. When iron was included in supplements containing other vitamins and minerals, vomiting was probably more common than with otherwise identical supplements that lacked iron.

“While the evidence base supports the use of daily iron supplements where needed, some adverse effects do occur. Stool discoloration and, in some formulations, vomiting were more common with iron, and the certainty of evidence for several outcomes, including anemia and iron deficiency themselves, was low or very low,” explains senior author Dr Saurabh Mehta.

Uncertainty about longer-term outcomes has persisted for years. The World Health Organization’s guidelines, last updated in 2016, identified gaps in evidence concerning the long-term effects of iron supplementation in children. The new review brings together a much larger body of research, although the strength of the evidence still varies across outcomes.

Anemia affects an estimated 874 million children and women worldwide. Young children face a particularly heavy burden, with approximately 40% of children younger than five affected in 2019. Iron deficiency accounts for roughly half of these cases.

Preventing anemia is a central objective of the global health strategy for women, children, and adolescents through 2030. Iron supplementation is already widely used to prevent and treat anemia, particularly among adolescents and women in populations where iron deficiency is common.

“These findings give decision-makers a stronger evidence base for guidance on iron supplementation in children and adolescents,” says co-author Dr Julia L. Finkelstein. “The data supports continued use of iron supplementation where iron deficiency is a concern.”

The researchers caution that their findings do not justify giving every child or adolescent a daily iron tablet. Decisions about supplementation should account for individual nutritional needs, local rates of iron deficiency and anemia, and the specific supplement formulation being used.

Reference: “Daily iron supplementation for prevention or treatment of iron‐deficiency anaemia in infants, children, and adolescents” by Kripa Rajagopalan, Laura Hackl, Juan Pablo Peña-Rosas, Maria Nieves Garcia-Casal, Mochammad Rizal, Neel H Mehta, Michael Roudik, Saiful Islam, Meghan M Trumbull-Kennedy, Nabila Reem Khondakar, Juan VA Franco, Julia L Finkelstein and Saurabh Mehta, 17 November 2026, Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. DOI: 10.1002/14651858.CD013227.pub2

The 8 Best-Performed Audiobooks of All Time, Ranked

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The concept of audiobooks was born alongside Thomas Edison's phonograph in the late 19th century, but actual "Talking Books" as a commercial category appeared in the 1930s as a tool for visually impaired readers without knowledge of or access to Braille to experience literature. In the years since, the medium has evolved greatly alongside technology like cassette tapes and CDs. Now, in the digital era, audiobooks have become such an abundant and mainstream product that even people with no kind of visual impairment have come to choose them as their preferred method of reading.

But while several incredible audiobooks have been produced over the years, a special few can easily be counted among the best, most vividly performed in the medium's history. A truly great narrator feels like they have a genuinely profound connection to the material, delivering each line with appropriate emotional weight and making it easier to understand the subtext of the original book. If they portray each character's voice with a distinct characterization, pace their cadence well, and fit the book's genre well, any narrator can make their audiobook performance go down in history as legendary.

Some of its narrative elements haven't aged perfectly, but in no way does that detract from the fact that Harper Lee's To Kill a Mockingbird is one of the greatest and most foundational American books ever written about racial prejudice, systemic injustice, and human nature. It was the novel that earned Lee a Pulitzer Prize in 1961, and it has aged wonderfully as one of the most influential Southern Gothic novels of the entire 20th century. Who better to narrate such a quintessential piece of Southern American literature than legendary Texas native Sissy Spacek?

Through sheer vocal authenticity and dramatic restraint, Spacek makes this one of the best audiobooks of all time. For one, the actress effectively uses her Southern drawl and cadence to make the small-town Alabama setting feel all the more immersive, elevating Lee's beautiful prose to make it feel all the more melodic. She also perfectly captures the spirit and personality of every character, with the way she marks the contrast between child Scout and adult Scout standing out as a particularly memorable narrative choice.

Where many of the best-narrated audiobooks in history were one-person shows, the Lincoln in the Bardo audiobook stands out as perhaps the greatest audiobook ever narrated by an ensemble cast. Written by George Saunders, this 2017 experimental novel is narrated by a shifting crowd of 166 spirits who are often unaware that they are dead. It's full of wonderfully surreal magical realism, making it such a special book that Saunders, who usually recorded his audiobooks himself, decided that he needed a full cast in order to do the text justice.

Saunders' friend Nick Offerman and his wife, Megam Mullally, agreed to perform in the audiobook cast and recruit a bunch of other narrators, including celebrities like Don Cheadle and non-celebrities like Saunders' family and friends. With a gargantuan 166-person cast, Lincoln in the Bardo easily takes the cake as the audiobook with the biggest narrator ensemble in history, and the resulting product feels surprisingly even and creatively coherent. With such different voices appearing over the course of seven and a half hours, the book's rhythmic prose, hypnotic pacing, and sense of humor are all elevated.

The Lord of the Rings began with J. R. R. Tolkien's publisher's request that he write a sequel to the mega-successful children's fantasy book The Hobbit. What started as more of a simple follow-up gradually started transforming into something far bigger and more ambitious, until we finally got what many would confidently call the most important and influential fantasy novel of the 20th century. Indeed, though it was published in three volumes between 1954 and 1955, The Lord of the Rings is technically all one huge fantasy epic, easily one of the most ambitious high fantasy books of all time.

After his participation in Peter Jackson's Middle-earth film trilogy, it was Andy Serkis who narrated the second-ever unabridged audiobook edition of the novel, also published in three separate volumes. Clocking in at roughly 65 hours, his take on this towering achievement of fantasy storytelling brings a unique and unforgettable voice not only to Gollum, but to every other character that takes part in Frodo's journey. Serkis also fully commits to the many songs and poems that Tolkien sprinkled across the novel, adding further vocal variety to an audiobook that was already exquisitely varied.

British author Joe Abercrombie's The First Law series is one of the greatest pieces of grimdark fantasy of the 21st century, composed of two trilogies, three standalone novels, and two short story collections. The book that started it all was The Blade Itself, the first chapter in the trilogy that's also titled The First Law. While it's undoubtedly one of the most essential dark fantasy masterpieces of the last 20 years, it's usually agreed upon among fans that several of its sequels are even better. When it comes to audiobook versions, though, there's no beating the original.

English actor and stage performer Steven Pacey, known for his work in shows like Blake's 7 and MobLand, narrates the Blade Itself audiobook with precisely the kind of gritty realism and razor-sharp comedic timing that a masterpiece like this would need. It's a masterclass in voice control where Pacey switches between the dramatically distinct voice of each character with the utmost ease, as well as a masterclass in dry British wit that complements Abercrombie's dark sense of humor flawlessly. As the book moves quickly from brutal action to slow-burning political scheming to moments of pure dark comedy, Pacey matches the rhythm of such transitions perfectly.

There can be no conversation about the best-narrated audiobooks in history without talking about the best-narrated biographies and autobiographies. Readers may point to many different books when asked about what the best-narrated autobiography of all time is, but there's one title that's bound to come up more often than most: Born a Crime, the comedic autobiography of South African comedian, writer, and political commentator Trevor Noah.

The book is primarily focused on Noah's childhood in South Africa after he was born to an illegal interracial relationship during the apartheid era, and this audiobook version has long been regarded as the gold standard of non-fiction narrated by the author. As a natural polyglot, a seasoned stand-up comedian, and an expert speaker thanks to his experience as a television host, Noah narrates Born a Crime not only with the deepest emotional intimacy possible, but also with a level of cultural authenticity that feels essential to the book.

J. K. Rowling's Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone (published in the United States as Harry Potter and the Sorcerer's Stone) took a couple of years to become a true commercial juggernaut, kicking off the most financially successful fantasy book series of all time. As such, it was entirely unsurprising when Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows broke the record as the book with the most volumes sold within 24 hours of release, instantly cementing itself as one of the best books of the 2000s.

Audiobook editions were released simultaneously on the day of the book's publication, with the UK edition being narrated by Stephen Fry and the US edition being narrated by Jim Dale. While both Fry and Dale broke the record for the most distinct character voices recorded by an individual on an audiobook at the time (with a whopping 146), there's something special about Dale's performance that earned him the 2008 Grammy for the Best Spoken Word Album for Children. Theatrical, energetic, and full of emotionally cathartic moments, Dale's narration is an all-timer.

The LitRPG genre is a category of speculative fiction where the story explicitly incorporates mechanics and tropes from video games and tabletop RPGs into the narrative, and as far as LitRPG novels go, it doesn't get any better than Matt Dinniman's Dungeon Crawler Carl series. The series was initially self-published until it was acquired by Ace Books in 2024. The first of the bunch is 2020's Dungeon Crawler Carl, one of those sci-fi and fantasy books that are perfect from start to finish.

Dinniman's studio, Soundbooth Theater, produced an audiobook of Dungeon Crawler Carl in 2021, narrated by voice artist Jeff Hays. Widely cited as a rare case where the audiobook edition of a book practically eclipses the print version, the Dungeon Crawler Carl audiobook is anchored by Hays' jaw-dropping vocal range, at times making it feel like you're listening to a full cast rather than a single performer. Seamlessly paced and full of humor, Hays' narration is absolute perfection.

Andy Weir has only published four novels so far in his career, but they have already been enough for him to establish and cement himself as one of the leading hard sci-fi authors currently working. His latest work is Project Hail Mary, widely praised as his magnum opus and one of those books that are a masterclass in hard sci-fi writing. The winner of the 2022 Audie Award for Audiobook of the Year, Ray Porter's unabridged audiobook has come to be praised by many as the single best-performed audiobook of all time.

After listening to Porter's performance once, it's hard to imagine ever revisiting Project Hail Mary in any other way again. It's the definitive way to experience the story, not only because the medium couples perfectly with the novel's heavy focus on themes of communication. Porter captures both Grace and Rocky's voices faultlessly, and he manages to turn the many complex scientific elements into exposition that feels surprisingly conversational and humorous. Energetic, well-paced, emotional, and tonally varied, it's some of the greatest narration that any audiobook has ever delivered.

Anthropic Priced Its IPO at $83. Here's What I'd Pay for the Stock.

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Anthropic's explosive growth has made its IPO one of the biggest events in AI. But its enormous valuation could limit investors' future returns.

By Matt DiLallo – Nov 18, 2026 at 8:30PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Anthropic, PBC ›

Anthropic's (ANT +0.00%) initial public offering (IPO) is one of the biggest events in the AI industry. The developer of Claude has grown its revenue at an extraordinary pace as businesses adopt its AI tools. The AI giant just priced its IPO at $83 per share, above its expected range of $70 to $78 per share.

However, that price already assumes considerable success. Even if Anthropic becomes one of the world's most successful AI companies, its stock could still deliver disappointing returns from here.

I ran the numbers on what Anthropic's stock could be worth over the next five and 10 years. Here's what they suggest, and the price I'd be willing to pay for the AI stock.

Anthropic recorded $33.2 billion in revenue in just the first nine months of 2026, up a blistering 1,200% compared to the same period last year. Its third-quarter revenue alone was around $17 billion, and its annualized revenue run rate reached $81 billion by Oct. 31, up $10 billion from the prior month.

Enterprise adoption of Claude, its coding tools, API usage, and integrations give Anthropic several avenues for continued growth. However, AI infrastructure and model training are extraordinarily expensive. Anthropic committed to spending a staggering $895.4 billion on compute and infrastructure via purchase commitments and lease agreements. Meanwhile, Anthropic's revenue growth will also almost certainly slow as its business scales.

At $83 per share, Anthropic has a valuation of about $1.5 trillion. That's around 18 times its $81 billion run rate.

That run rate isn't the same as reported annual sales. It extrapolates recent results, which inflates a company growing this quickly. If you drive to work, price each commuting mile at the IRS business mileage rate, which counts what a car costs to own, not just gas. Pay for transit, vanpools, and parking with pretax money through a commuter benefit at work. Then drive fewer days with a carpool or a remote day or two a week.

Gas is the commuting cost you notice. Insurance, repairs, and the value your car loses with each mile never show up at the pump.

The IRS business mileage rate, 76 cents a mile, counts them along with the gas. The IRS sets it from a yearly study of what cars cost to own and drive. It’s an average, and the full cost of owning a car varies by model.

Say you drive 20 miles each way, 230 days a year, and count each mile at 76 cents. That’s 9,200 miles and almost $7,000 a year. If you ride transit, add up a year of fares and parking instead.

Next, pay what you can with pretax money. Under the IRS’s fringe benefit rules, a commuter benefit at work lets you buy transit passes and vanpool rides with up to $340 a month of pretax pay. A separate $340 a month can go to parking at or near work, or where you catch transit, a vanpool, or a carpool. That pay skips federal income tax and the 7.65% Social Security and Medicare tax, so in the 22% federal bracket each dollar of fares or parking costs about 70 cents.

Then cut the trips. Working from home two of your five days takes 40% off those 9,200 miles and brings the cost at 76 cents a mile down from almost $7,000 to about $4,200. Gas and upkeep on the skipped miles stop at once, and a car driven less lasts more years.

On the days you still go in, each of these cuts your driving.

Look in your benefits portal for a commuter benefit, along with other workplace perks you may be missing, then ask your manager about working from home a day or two a week.

Those deliveries included three standard A321neos and one A321XLR, registered N311NY. With no further Boeing deliveries expected this year, Airbus remains central to American's near-term fleet renewal strategy.

American's standard A321neo fleet has reached 90 aircraft, with two more expected before the end of the year. The airline is also anticipating one additional A321XLR, extending its investment in long-range, single-aisle aircraft. If all three outstanding deliveries arrive as planned, American will finish 2026 with 92 standard A321neos, alongside its growing fleet of 12 A321XLRs.

American's third-quarter financial disclosures showed that it had 165 Airbus A320-family aircraft on order as of September 30, with seven scheduled for delivery during the final quarter. The subsequent acceleration in October, when four A321-family aircraft arrived within a week, highlighted the program's pace. American also expects another 27 A321-family deliveries in 2027, although the precise split between the standard A321neo and A321XLR has not been disclosed.

The A321XLR is particularly important to American's international expansion, allowing it to serve longer routes with fewer passengers than a widebody aircraft would typically accommodate.

Brian Znotins, American's former Senior Vice President of Network Planning, highlighted the aircraft's importance when announcing the carrier's 2027 international expansion.

"The A321XLR is transforming our international network, expanding it to offer destinations to satisfy every travel palate. Combined with our investments enhancing our onboard product and in-flight experience, travelers have more reasons to choose American when they fly around the world."

American received its first A321XLR in October 2025, becoming the first US operator of the type. Its original 50-aircraft commitment was subsequently reduced to 40, including five delivered by the end of 2025. Each aircraft features 20 Flagship Suite business class seats, 12 premium economy seats, and 123 economy seats, emphasizing premium revenue over maximum capacity.

The XLR restores some of the long-range narrowbody capability American lost when it retired its 757s in 2020. Its economics make thinner transatlantic routes viable without deploying a much larger 787 Dreamliner. The aircraft initially entered service on premium transcontinental flights, before expanding into international markets as more aircraft became available.

American's 2027 schedule demonstrates its ambitions, with four additional European routes assigned to the XLR. Philadelphia-Vienna, measuring approximately 4,321 miles (6,955 km), is particularly notable as it will become the world's longest narrowbody flight. The route will operate daily from May 6 through January 2028, while New York gains new services to Amsterdam and Nice.

13 new routes were planned but now only seven are scheduled.

The standard A321neo remains central to American's high-capacity narrowbody strategy. With its fleet now at 90 aircraft, it still has firm orders for 126 more airframes as the airline continues investing in the type to support busy domestic routes and replace older aircraft.

Configured with 196 seats, including 20 First Class and 47 Main Cabin Extra seats, American's legacy A321neos accommodate 41 more passengers than the XLR. It is particularly valuable on busy domestic routes connecting American's major hubs and supports the gradual replacement of older narrowbodies. Its versatility extends internationally, with Philadelphia-Reykjavík scheduled to launch in May 2027.

American's Boeing 737 MAX 8s complement the A321neos, offering 172 seats on routes requiring less capacity. The airline also has substantial outstanding Boeing narrowbody orders, including the larger 737 MAX 10, although deliveries of that variant are not expected until 2029 or later.
