Arsenal transfer news: ‘You couldn’t blame’ Saliba for Real Madrid move, warns former Gunner

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

By Axel Clody | 06 Nov 2026 06:59

William Gallas has spoken about Real Madrid’s interest in William Saliba in recent months. The former defender does not see the French international turning down the Spanish giants.

During the last summer transfer window, Arsenal rejected a £144.5m (€170m) swap deal for William Saliba with Real Madrid. Real Madrid were prepared to send Rodrygo to London to land the French international. Arsenal immediately rejected the proposal and made the former Saint-Etienne player untouchable.

In September 2025, the centre-back extended his contract with the English club until June 2030. A sign of long-term commitment? According to William Gallas, the 25-year-old will find it hard to turn down a fresh approach from the Spanish giants.

‘Are Arsenal at a high enough level now that William Saliba would turn down Real Madrid if they came in for him?’ the former Arsenal defender (2006-2010) wondered in an interview with SeatPick, before answering his own question: ‘I know a lot of people will be mad about what I’m going to say right now, but if we are talking about the history of both clubs, Real Madrid and Arsenal, then Real Madrid is Real Madrid. They are on a different level.’

So, although Arsenal are once again establishing themselves as a powerhouse in England and Europe, Gallas believes Real Madrid, who have not won a trophy for a year and a half, remain a more attractive destination, even a dream for players.

‘Madrid is Madrid, and I’m telling you, any player who has the opportunity to go to Madrid, they will try to go there, and you can’t blame them. You couldn’t blame a player like Saliba if that is his decision,’ added the 49-year-old. Could a transfer take place?

Real Madrid may soon need to strengthen the heart of their defence. Antonio Rudiger is under contract until June 2027, while Eder Militao’s deal expires in June 2028 and Raul Asencio does not appear to feature in Jose Mourinho’s plans.

Real Madrid could therefore try their luck again with William Saliba in 2027.

If the 25-year-old Frenchman, who is valued at £85m (€100m) on the market, opens the door to an exit, Arsenal will have no interest in doing the Spanish giants any favours and will surely want to set a new record for the transfer of a defender.

That record is held by Josko Gvardiol, who joined Manchester City for £76.5m (€90m). It is a figure that would make everyone think twice, even Real Madrid.

Chainalysis says breach exposed password hashes and live tokens

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Chainalysis disclosed password hashes and live tokens were exposed after initially reporting only non-sensitive customer data was accessed.

Chainalysis on Wednesday disclosed a wider impact from a cyberattack it initially described as involving only non-sensitive customer information.

“On Monday, we discovered certain customer information including names, usernames, password hashes and live tokens for all Chainalysis users were exposed to a malicious actor,” the blockchain analytics firm said in an update to its incident notice.

The company did not disclose whether each type of information was exposed for every user and declined to provide Cointelegraph with additional information beyond its published disclosure.

Chainalysis initially said on Oct. 30 that the intrusion exposed user IDs, names and email addresses but found no evidence that attackers accessed authentication credentials or session tokens.

Another Wednesday update stated an “on-path attack” exposed some customers’ passwords. The company said it had contained and remediated that attack vector and was locking affected accounts and requiring password resets.

Despite the broader exposure, Chainalysis said it still had no evidence that attackers accessed investigation data or manipulated its attribution data.

Why managers can learn a lot from leaders like Napoleon - Pulis

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The November international break is almost upon us, which always means one thing - the managerial merry-go-round is about to properly get into gear.

It's known as 'sacking season' for a reason - the new campaign has started to take shape and I know from experience that any manager who is not winning games will always be sleeping with just one eye closed, and the other one open.

At any club on a poor run of results, the owners and supporters will be questioning whether the manager is the right fit to continue.

This two-week break from domestic games is the ideal opportunity to blood the new incumbent and give them a period of getting to know everyone before the games start up again.

That means it is 'hiring season' too, but there are always far more out-of-work managers than vacant positions.

Since the start of last season, 60 different managers have resigned or been sacked by teams in the Premier League and Football League. Three of them left to take up offers from other clubs but, in total, 37 of them - or 62% - are not currently in management.

For most managers, it's rarely the case where you walk into a new club within a few weeks of leaving your last one, so what do out-of-work managers do between jobs to make sure they are ready to take the next opportunity that fits?

Known as 'The Bald Eagle', Jim Smith was renowned as a master of winning promotion during a management career that spanned almost 40 years. He is pictured here celebrating after leading Derby into the Premier League in 1996

It is more than 32 years since I received my marching orders from my first job at Bournemouth, who were in what is now known as League One. I was devastated.

I had - as I always did - given everything I could do to benefit the club, and with it being my first sacking I honestly did not know where my next step would take me.

But, on the second day of me being out of work, my home phone rang and at the end of the line was Jim Smith, who was in charge of Portsmouth, a few miles down the south coast.

I knew Jim then, but not really well. I'd been a manager for two years, but by then he had already spent 25 years in the dugout, at eight different clubs.

He asked how I was and, before I could even reply, recited these immortal words, which always stuck with me - "son, you're not a real manager until you get sacked at least three times - so get your backside off the sofa and get on with getting another job" - then he put the phone down!

I've used that sentence myself many times since to managers who have lost their job and are struggling to come to terms with it.

That can happen a lot. People outside the game maybe don't realise how difficult it can be when you are sacked, and how it affects certain individuals differently - you can easily fall off the merry-go-round and, as a manager, you never know for sure whether you will get another chance.

Bruce Rioch only spent one season at Arsenal but was responsible for bringing a certain Dennis Bergkamp to Highbury from Inter Milan, making the legendary Dutch striker his first signing in June 1995

Someone else who phoned me up in the summer of 1994 was another great name in the game as a player and manager, Bruce Rioch.

At the time, Bruce was having a lot of success as manager of Bolton - he would get the Arsenal job a few months later - and he asked me if I would scout for him while I was trying to find my next manager's post.

He was very keen to get me involved and very helpful in keeping me busy watching games.

I was equally keen on helping Bruce and ended up scouting for him for that entire season. To this day, I owe him so much in respect of what he asked me to do, because of what I learned during that time.

Football is the best team sport in the world and the fundamental thing I picked up that season is that its 'product', the players, were the secret to success.

By that, I mean that I realised that at all levels there are good players, and at all levels there are managers working on budgets. I learned that knowing the level you were at, and placing the right players at that level who you could afford, would be an enormous factor in your chances of success, or failure.

Yes, there is more to it than that - you then also had to pick good characters, coach them well and motivate them with a driving force they could handle and enjoy - but you have to start by getting the right players for the level you are at.

The following summer, I was appointed manager of Gillingham, who had just finished 89th out of the 92 clubs across the top four divisions.

I was lucky enough to inherit a club who had quite a few players out of contract, so it enabled me to reset the first team with several players I had watched during my time scouting for Bruce and Bolton.

All the hours and miles I travelled that year paid off, with Gillingham finishing second in what is now League Two and gaining promotion in my first season. Those players who we recruited kick-started four years of my managerial career in Kent, and left me with some fantastic on-field memories.

Napoleon (left) rose through the ranks to become the Emperor of the French as well as building a reputation as one of the great military commanders, and inspired Pulis (right) with his leadership skills

My time and success at Priestfield opened up an ever-revolving door of work and rest.

Whenever I was out of work in the years that followed, watching games still occupied my midweeks and weekends for the same reasons as before - I wanted to watch players who I might end up signing.

Lots of games I went to were watched from the terraces with either home or away supporters, which was always interesting and illuminating - I've always valued the views of match-going fans on different players.

Down the years, I was also invited to spend a few weeks coaching abroad, in Kuwait and the United States, by friends I had in the game.

Whatever I was doing, I always felt like I was learning, and not just by watching football around the world. I became an avid reader of sports books and also finding out about great leaders of men through history.

So, as well as visiting Bayer Leverkusen, Athletic Bilbao and RB Leipzig - who back then had only just started their rise from the bottom tier of German football - I would analyse and investigate historic battles or events.

Napoleon Bonaparte really kept me busy. His attention to detail and his command of his generals was only superseded by his troop's loyalty to him - they would literally die for him!

His 250th birthday was celebrated in Corsica in the summer of 2019, and a French newspaper invited me to go along and give a British perspective on how we see Napoleon as a leader and a person.

I was delighted to accept and was amazed by how many people from all over Europe turned up too.

Hated by many countries, but still adored by his followers, Napoleon also produced the Napoleonic Code, which is still the foundation of French civil law today.

The Charge of the Light Brigade was a disastrous assault by British cavalry led by Lord Cardigan against Russian forces during the Battle of Balaclava in 1854

As well as great military triumphs, I studied defeats too - they are as much a part of football management as victories, after all - and you can compare the organisational brilliance of Napoleon to the disaster of the Battle of Balaclava and the Charge of the Light Brigade.

One summer, I travelled out to Crimea to witness the scene of that tragic charge by the bravest of brave men.

Lord Raglan, the British commander-in-chief, was based on the high ground and could see everything in the Russian positions and the brigades.

His order to attack was ambiguous and although Lord Cardigan, who led the charge, questioned it, he still followed it through - leading to one of the most infamous military disasters in history.

It should never have happened but, just as in football, leadership and communication from the top was key. The instructions were passed down from Raglan to Lord Lucan to Cardigan and became garbled - too many cooks, in my opinion!

The last book which really stuck with me, in terms of inspiring me as a leader, was the story of HMS Endurance - disaster strikes during a journey through the ice fields of the southern hemisphere when the ship becomes stuck.

I cannot do justice to the deeds of Sir Ernest Shackleton in this column but his leadership of his men, along with his number two Frank Worsley, was extraordinary.

Both dovetailed in a way that gave their men the energy, desire and determination to believe and follow instructions in the most appalling conditions, never once questioning their leaders' abilities to help them survive.

Over the years, I've never stopped reading about, or watching, great leaders like that - there is so much that young coaches can learn from other sports or history that will benefit then, the same way it did me.

Going to games was still the most important thing when I was between jobs, because I would always believe my own eyes when watching players.

There were times when that became more of a challenge, especially at the back end of my career, but times change and if you are not in tune with what is happening in the game then you can get dropped very quickly.

But I am sure that most of my managerial compatriots would also agree with me that experience in all walks of life can be invaluable.

I learned so much when I was out of work, about all aspects of leadership, individually and collectively.

My advice to managers out of work now is the same that I received from Jim, all those years ago - pick yourselves up and get off the sofa to get back in work as quickly as possible.

You should never be afraid of dropping down a level the way I did, too. It is better to get a job, any job, than be out of football full-stop. That hasn't changed since my day, either.

Tony Pulis was speaking to BBC Sport's Chris Bevan.

International sales on cult martial arts splatter comedy “African Kung-Fu Nazis,” its sequel and a behind-the-scenes documentary have gone to Hong Kong-based Cappu Films.

The deal, struck with filmmaker and producer Sebastian Stein of Humanmade Films, was unveiled on the sidelines of Taiwan Creative Content Fest (TCCF) in Taipei. It covers “African Kung-Fu Nazis I,” “African Kung-Fu Nazis II” and the feature documentary “African Kung-Fu Nazis – The Doc.” It applies only to rights and territories that are still open, and leaves deals already in place untouched. Japanese distributor Transformer gave both features a theatrical run in Japan, opening the original in June 2021 and the sequel four years later in July 2025. Busch Media Group handled the first film’s German release on DVD, Blu-ray and VOD. The sequel played Sitges’ Brigadoon sidebar in 2025. In 2026 it went on to the Brussels International Fantastic Film Festival (BIFFF), Grossmann and Macabro, where it picked up an honorable mention.

Mixing kung fu action, gore, horror and dark comedy, the series takes its cues from vintage Hong Kong martial arts films and from Ghana’s independent filmmaking scene. In the first film, Ghanaian kung fu fighters square off against send-ups of wartime figures from Germany and Japan, among them Adolf Hitler, Hideki Tojo and “Horseman” Goering.

The sequel teams one-armed fighter Addo with Sek Tin to take on cyborg “Adolf Robotler,” who wants to become president of Ghana. Stein said the film, a Ghana-Japan-Germany production shot mostly in Ghana, was paid for largely through crowdfunding, with Japanese fans of the first film providing much of the backing.

“African Kung-Fu Nazis – The Doc” goes behind the camera on the original, following the independent shoot that started the franchise.

“With their love for Chinese kung fu, our Ghanaian heroes smash the threat of fascism,” said Stein, CEO of Humanmade Films. “Total freedom, beautiful madness. Because you won’t drink champagne if you don’t take a risk!”

Cappu and the filmmakers also plan a line of art-led merchandise, to be produced via sister company Winners Gallery and built on the franchise’s characters and look, as part of the international marketing push. Concepts and release plans have yet to be finalized.

“We love fiercely independent horror and cult films, and we want to support filmmakers who are doing something that is entirely their own,” said Janice Woo of Cappu Films. “There’s a real love of kung fu cinema in these films. We come from production ourselves, so we understand how much it takes to get a film like this made. We’re excited to work with Sebastian and help bring all three films to more audiences.”

Cappu also operates out of Beijing and handles select releases in its home market and mainland China. Its slate ranges from Tony Leung Chiu-wai crime thriller “Fox Hunt” to Shan Jiang’s “Ephemera,” a 2026 Tribeca Festival U.S. Narrative Competition premiere. The seventh edition of Taiwan Creative Content Fest (TCCF) kicked off today with talk of bolstering Taiwan’s intellectual property (IP); reaching out to international markets through co-production and exports; and a reassuring message on government support.

Sue Wang, chair of TCCF organizer Taiwan Creative Content Agency (TAICCA), opened proceedings by first acknowledging the disruption that has rocked the content industries globally over the past year, before talking about how the government agency she heads aims to provide solutions.

“The past few years have been a difficult journey for everyone, littered with many challenges,” Wang said. “At earlier editions of TCCF, we were talking about streaming and the change in consumption habits. Now we face even more changes and revolutions, which have become a given in our industry. When we create, we have to think about IP, licensing and maximizing the IP universe. Macau jails pro-democracy campaigner in city's first national security case

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Au was sentenced to 12 years and six months' jail for "subversion against the political power of the state"

Macau has sentenced an ex-lawmaker and pro-democracy campaigner to 12 years and six months' jail in the city's first national security case.

Following a closed-door trial, Au Kam San, 69, was convicted on Friday of "subversion against the political power of the state", among other offences.

His wife Virginia Cheang condemned the ruling, calling the case a "groundless persecution designed to humiliate Au".

Macau was handed back to China in 1999, ending over 400 years of Portuguese administration. Its national security law came into effect in 2009, criminalising acts of treason, secession, and subversion against the Chinese Communist Party.

The law was revised in 2023, broadening its scope to include non-violent acts of secession, and again this year, to allow for trials to be held behind closed doors.

Prosecutors had accused Au of assisting and collaborating with "external hostile organisations" by collecting, preparing and disseminating information that was "manifestly filled with political claims of a subversive nature against the state".

These took place "over a long period of time", the prosecutors claim.

Au was arrested in July 2025 and later charged with three counts of subverting the state's power, three counts of endangering national security by establishing contact with foreign organisations, and one count of breach of confidentiality.

He was found guilty of four of the seven charges following a three-day trial which ended in September. A judge had ordered that the trial be held behind closed doors "to ensure that no prejudice is caused to the interests of national security".

Macau's authorities never publicly disclosed the details of the case. Au's family had earlier told the media Au was not allowed to seek his own legal representation and had instead been assigned a lawyer by the court.

Au's wife Cheang said she hoped authorities would grant her and her family permission to regularly write to and visit Au in prison.

"I dare not hope for more as I believe that will only lead to greater disappointment," she said in an open letter on Friday.

XRP Ledger's Biggest Lending Upgrade Just Got Closer

📅 2026-11-06  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The XRP Ledger (XRPL) is on the verge of a significant decentralized finance (DeFi) upgrade that could potentially open up new lending possibilities and liquidity for the XRP Ledger network.

According to Vet (@Vet_X0), a notable XRPL validator and community member, the much-anticipated Lending Protocol amendment has now gained two more Yes votes from validators, moving one step closer to activation.

The Lending Protocol amendment now boasts 21 Yes votes from a total of 35 trusted validators (60%). The amendment needs eight more Yes votes to reach the minimum threshold of 29 validators.

Reaching this threshold doesn’t mean that the upgrade will instantly go live. Instead, reaching the supermajority threshold will initiate the mandatory two-week activation period, which requires a minimum of 80% of validators to support the amendment.

The upgrade, known as XLS-66, is geared towards adding a native lending feature to the XRP Ledger.

In contrast to most crypto lending services, where borrowers are typically required to provide more collateral than the loan amount, XLS-66 is specially crafted to enable fixed-term and non-collateralized lending.

This will enable qualified borrowers to secure loans without the need to hold cryptocurrency collateral that exceeds the loan amount.

According to the official XLS-66 document, the Lending Protocol will enable the pooling of funds via so-called Single Asset Vaults, which can then be loaned to borrowers under set terms.

The proposed lending system involves three key actors: depositors, loan brokers and borrowers.

While depositors are in charge of depositing crypto or supported assets into a vault, loan brokers are tasked with brokering lending deals as well as evaluating borrowers.

For their part, borrowers would receive the loans and pay them back based on the set schedule.

The upgrade could potentially empower financial institutions and other entities to put capital to use on the XRP Ledger, as opposed to just holding or moving around assets.

It may also open up new prospects for earning interest on deposited assets in lending vaults. The amount of interest, however, would largely depend on the actual lending activity and risks.

According to the latest validator data, this amendment has reached 23 out of 35 votes. It still requires six more votes to hit the threshold.

Furthermore, the LendingProtocolV1_1 amendment, which offers upgrades including closed-ended vaults, updated interest accounting, etc., also currently has 21 Yes votes.

All three amendments are currently being considered, and none has yet reached supermajority.

Melissa Joan Hart Explains It All: Hot Flashes, Menopause, and Turning 50

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Follow Healthline on our socials for all things health.

Actor Melissa Joan Hart has a vivid memory of her mother standing in front of their open freezer to cool off. As a child, she didn’t understand why. Now, as a 50-year-old woman, Hart knows that her mom was experiencing hot flashes.

“[She’s] only 20 years older than me, so maybe I missed the part where she was going through [menopause], but there was no conversation about it,” Hart told Healthline. “I feel like it’s just becoming a hot topic with women, where…the taboo is going away from it now.”

Her own perimenopausal symptoms started with night sweats.

“[It] was really jarring, and the first few times, you’re not really sure what that was…do I need to turn the air down or what’s going on here?” Hart said.

Over time, she learned that she was experiencing moderate to severe hot flashes, which affect up to 80% of women during the menopause transition and can persist for 7 to 10 years.

Doctors categorize hot flashes as mild, moderate, or severe, said Diana Bitner, MD, OB-GYN, at Corewell Health.

“[So] if they’re mild, it’s a sense of warmth (maybe in the chest, or the neck, or the face). A moderate hot flash is where there’s associated sweating, and a severe hot flash is something that stops you in your tracks, wakes you up from the middle of the night,” Bitner told Healthline.

Hart has mostly experienced hot flashes while home or at her three sons’ activities, and they have yet to interfere with her work. However, according to research, one in three women report moderate to severe challenges in managing their work due to symptoms related to menopause.

“It’s just so sad that about 10% of women leave the workforce due to menopause,” said Bitner. “And so it can really affect our ability to perform at these key years of not only institutional memory, but also of earning power, and it can really affect the whole community.”

Bitner said women should feel empowered to bring up their menopausal symptoms or questions with their doctor.

“[This] is something that was taboo for so long…women didn’t really talk about this, whether it was because of embarrassment, or [they] weren’t sure if anyone was experiencing it,” she said. “[There’s] no reason, in this day and age, we need to suffer through it…it’s something that there are treatment options for.”

In fact, Bitner said providing specific details about their symptoms, such as how many mild, moderate, or severe hot flashes they’ve experienced over the past week, can help doctors better assess patients’ needs.

Hart plans to discuss treatment options for her hot flashes with her doctor soon. In the last few years, she said she has put emphasis on her midlife health.

“I just was sick of waking up with aches and pains in general [thinking], ‘is this the rest of my life?’” she said.

She started listening to self-help books in the car while driving her kids around and found ways to put herself first.

“My kids are a little bit older, so I kind of have that freedom to be able to do that,” she said. “[But] along with getting older, comes the menopause, comes that different time in life. And I think it’s a good time to reevaluate [how] our bodies can’t handle what [they] used to handle, and let’s figure it out.”

Hart has partnered with pharmaceutical company Bayer on its “Life Doesn’t Stop for a Hot Flash” campaign, which encourages women to discuss menopause symptoms with their doctors and promotes Lynkuet, a hormone-free prescription medication for moderate to severe hot flashes associated with menopause.

“It’s called ‘Life Doesn’t Stop for a Hot Flash’ campaign because it shouldn’t, because we should be able to push through, and this should be a time in our lives when our kids are getting older, and we’re able to kind of reinvent ourselves,” said Hart. “And I think it’s an exciting time, and it shouldn’t be something that we’re afraid of.”

She is embracing speaking openly about midlife health.

“I hit 50 this year, and it was, like, a huge milestone, and I think that’s something to be proud of, and not something to shy away from,” she said.

Hart also hopes to challenge the longstanding barriers women face in Hollywood as they age, including fewer opportunities for leading roles.

“[I] think we’re seeing that the generation before, and my generation, it feels like we’re pushing those boundaries…and we’re saying, you know, that women at this age have a lot to give, have a lot of information to share, have a lot of stories to tell and experiences and adventures still ahead of us, and, you know, we’re not ready to stop,” Hart said.

In everyday life, Bitner said that while some patients avoid talking about midlife issues like menopause with their doctors, she is seeing more patients who are confident being forthcoming.

As more women talk openly about menopause, she hopes to include men in the conversation, too. In fact, she invites her patients to bring their partners to appointments, so they can gain an understanding of menopause and the experiences their partners face, such as hot flashes, sleep issues, vaginal dryness, and discomfort or pain during intercourse.

“[Often], there’s, you know, comments that come up [from men who] say, ‘How do I talk about this without getting in trouble?’” said Bitner. “[We] want everyone to join this conversation…it doesn’t have to be just her issue because we know it’s not.”
