Coffee consumption among U.S. adults remained relatively stable between 2003 and 2018, but the average number of calories consumed from coffee beverages increased by nearly 40%, according to a study published in the Journal of the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics.

By 2017–2018, U.S. adults consumed an average of about 13 ounces of coffee beverages per day. Meanwhile, the average daily calorie intake from these beverages had increased from 43 to 60 calories since 2003–2004.

By 2018, non-dairy creamer had become the largest source of calories added to coffee, surpassing sugar or syrup and dairy milk.

The researchers analyzed data from eight consecutive cycles of the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) from 2003 to 2018. The study included 30,459 U.S. adults ages 20 and older who provided two 24-hour dietary recalls.

Kristin Kirkpatrick, president of KAK, Consulting, LLC, and a dietitian at the Cleveland Clinic Department of Wellness & Preventive Medicine in Cleveland, Ohio, who wasn’t involved in the study, told Healthline that these findings didn’t surprise her.

“The variety of coffee drinks, both at coffee houses and grocery stores, as well as options for creamers in the grocery store, make it easy to trade in black coffee for something closer to dessert,” she said.

She also added, however, that not all non-dairy creamers are created equal.

“Some are quite natural, with minimal ingredients, including no added sugars and no additives. Others are ingredient-heavy with either a large amount of sugar (real or artificial) and additives, so it’s really based on the type that is purchased,” she noted.

“Starting the day with [something] sweet may make it difficult to limit sugary foods later in the day. Starting the day with a more bitter approach may [be] an important factor in keeping sugar down, as bitter compounds may positively impact appetite-regulating pathways,” said Kirkpatrick.

Letter From the Editor-in-Chief: What a Decade of Bedtime Stories Taught Me About Health

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

I’ve read to my children each night at bedtime for as long as I (and they) can remember. We started with the likes of “Goodnight Batcave” and the lesser-known-in-the-US but oh-so-wonderful collection of verse-driven classics from former UK Children’s Laureate Julia Donaldson.

We moved on to B.J. Novak’s anarchic love letter to the power of words (and to great typography), “The Book With No Pictures.”

We eventually worked our way up to Roald Dahl’s wickedly dark back catalog, discovering more complex characters, language and plotting as we went.

When my eldest, Amelia, hit 6, it was time to roll out the big guns.

Over the last 5 years (she’s now 11), I’ve read the seven Harry Potter books aloud twice, complete with all the voices: once to Amelia on her own, once to both her and her brother, Henry, two years her junior.

I had feared she wouldn’t be keen on another helping, but Amelia immersed herself once more in the world of Hogwarts with no hesitation and took immense joy in watching Henry discover for himself every twist and turn on Harry’s journey.

We have done the same thing with Tolkien’s “The Hobbit” and his fantasy masterpiece “The Lord of the Rings,” and are currently midway through “The Two Towers” on our second go around.

There’s a huge amount of reciprocal joy we take from this nightly ritual, beyond the practical benefits of providing a calming demarcation between the end of the day and the wind-down to sleep.

For me, it comes as either a break in my working day or a signal that it’s over, depending on how hectic things are.

For the kids, it’s an all-too-rare chance to get me all to themselves, no deadlines, screens, or other distractions to pull me away from being anything but 100% present.

For all three of us, it’s a literal (and literary!) warm blanket — a chance to escape under the covers to familiar worlds and familiar faces. It is, in short, incredibly good for our well-being.

And yet, in the world of health information — including on this site — thousands, if not millions, of reading hours are dedicated to the benefits of nutrition, sleep, and exercise as the building blocks of good health. Reading itself rarely makes that shortlist.

For me, it unequivocally deserves a place on it. Research seems to support that case. Even if we need more of it to really understand how and why.

A recent editorial in Psychological Medicine co-authored by Barbara Sahakian, Professor of Clinical Neuropsychology at Cambridge University, draws on a broad range of research linking reading for pleasure to better mental health and greater empathy.

The positive impact of reading on young people — just like Amelia and Henry — lso carries evidence.

Among more than 10,000 US adolescents, those who started reading for pleasure early had fewer mental health problems, spent less time on screens and slept more. Although, per an earlier column of mine, I can’t vouch for the sleep side of things when it comes to early bird Henry.

For many, reading can be a form of therapy that improves well-being. Bibliotherapy is a complementary therapeutic tool for emotional release and reduced stress and anxiety, with its roots traced back to ancient civilizations.

There’s even research suggesting a link between a love of reading and a lower risk of cognitive decline.

In a 2003 study of people aged 75 and over, reading was associated with a 35% lower risk of dementia over roughly five years.

A separate 14-year study from 2022 found that people who read at least weekly saw less cognitive decline, whatever their level of education.

A happy (and healthy) by-product of this love of being read to is that both my kids have now started reading to me at bedtime. Henry recently read “Jeffy the Burglar’s Cat” to us, a lesser-known but no less compelling chapter book by Ursula Moray Williams that was a staple of my own childhood. And we’ve enjoyed several books from Amelia, including the wistful tale of friendship and the circle of life, “Charlotte’s Web” by E.B. White – not a dry eye in the house for the final few chapters of that one.

They’ve also gone off and re-read the Harry Potter books and “The Hobbit” to themselves.

The research above links reading to many health benefits: better attention and memory, fewer mental health problems, more sleep, less time on screens, and greater empathy. I haven’t measured any of those impacts empirically in our house. Nor am I naive to the fact that as they get older, they’ll ultimately grow out of me reading to them, with school, extracurricular activities, their friends, and inevitably screens competing for more and more of their time.

Come what may, though, I know that a child who considers missing a reading session as the most cardinal of cardinal sins, and who scrambles for the chance to read aloud to their dad at bedtime is, at that moment, concentrating, empathizing, discovering and growing.

Researchers are clear that studies espousing the benefits of reading show association rather than proof, and it’s evident that more experimental work and deeper research are needed to ensure the complex cause and effect stands on firm scientific ground.

Regardless, it’s a healthy habit Amelia, Henry and I are very happy to keep, and with a bit of luck, I’ll get to pass it on to their kids one day.

When will Cristiano Ronaldo score goal No. 1000 after international retirement announcement?

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Cristiano Ronaldo is now just 15 goals away from reaching 1,000 goals, the landmark he has openly targeted as the defining achievement of the final years of his career. But when will he get there?

At 41, the question is no longer whether Ronaldo can reach the four-figure mark. It is how long it will take him to get there.

The Portugal captain remains prolific for Al-Nassr, having taken his tally to 985 with a hat-trick in a 5-3 victory over Al Kholood on October 29. But his international future is suddenly in doubt following a falling-out with manager Jorge Jesus, and he’s finally opened up about the possibility of retiring.

We’ve crunched the numbers and mapped out the various scenarios.

“I want to reach 1,000 goals,” Ronaldo said, unveiling his last major career ambition on Rio Ferdinand’s YouTube channel just over two years ago.

“If I don’t have any injuries, this for me is the most important [thing], I want that. For me, the best mark that I can have in football is to reach, first, 900 goals. After, my challenge is to be at 1,000 goals.

“All the goals I have scored, they have video. They all have video. Listen, I respect all of them [Pele and Di Stefano]. And if you want more goals, I can bring them from training, too. And I will prove to the people after. They prefer this player, or this is the best one. I don’t care about that.”

Ronaldo has maintained the importance of that ambition, and reiterated that desire as a major goal, alongside winning a third Nations League title with Portugal.

“One thousand goals, yes, and the national team. Winning the Nations League, the competition starts tomorrow [Thursday], it’s a new age.”

With all that in mind, here’s our calculations for when Ronaldo could potentially reach the milestone of 1000 goals in his professional career.

– Barring any major career-ending injuries, Ronaldo still looks likely to reach the milestone of a thousand goals at some point in 2027. Possibly as early as January or February, particularly if he maintains his recent scoring form.

– It’s possible that if Ronaldo enjoys a resurgence and goes on a hot streak akin to his most prolific peak at Real Madrid, he could do it before the end of the year, and his latest hat-trick suggests that isn’t entirely out of the question. He would still need 15 goals in little over two months, though.

– If you take his recent rate of scoring (27 goals in 42 appearances during the calendar year of 2026) as what he’ll likely maintain, another 24 appearances would be required to score the remaining 15 goals. That would take us through to around February or March 2027. That calculation, however, assumes he’ll continue featuring for Portugal.

– There is an argument that Ronaldo could be set to improve upon that scoring rate. He’s been written off on the international stage after poor showings in the last two major summer tournaments, but he scored eight goals in nine games in the 2024-25 Nations League and a further five goals in qualifying for the 2026 World Cup. Rediscover that form, up his rate to around 1.00 goals per game, and fill his boots in the Saudi Pro League, and Ronaldo could conceivably reach four figures early in 2027. At that rate, he’d need just 15 more appearances.

– There is a counter-argument that Ronaldo will instead slow down in these final furlongs of his career. There had been signs of that earlier in the season, but his hat-trick against Al Kholood was a timely reminder of his enduring goalscoring prowess. His 27 goals in 42 appearances in 2026 work out at 0.64 goals per game. Maintain that rate, and he’ll need another 24 games to score the 15 goals required to reach number one thousand. That should be comfortably achievable before the end of Al-Nassr’s 2026-27 campaign, which is due to end in late May 2027.

– The biggest unknown now is Portugal. Ronaldo missed their last two matches following his falling-out with Jorge Jesus, and while he’s since issued a lengthy statement, he still has a suspension to serve. Whether he returns to the international fold remains to be seen. If he doesn’t, that’s potentially a fair few goals and appearances knocked off our projections.

– What kind of form he’ll show to get there is ultimately anyone’s guess. His latest hat-trick against Al Kholood, following a quieter couple of outings against Neom SC and Al Faysaly, suggests he’s still capable of producing spectacular goalscoring performances for Al-Nassr. But the uncertainty surrounding his international future makes predicting an exact date that bit trickier.

– We’ll hedge our bets and estimate that Ronaldo’s form over the coming months will be somewhere between his spectacular peak and a severe decline. He is playing in the Saudi Pro League, after all, and his team is set up to feed him chances. We’ve pencilled in a few more goals for Portugal, assuming he resolves his differences with Jesus, and expect him to keep ticking along for Al-Nassr. With just 15 goals left to score, and his recent hat-trick suggesting he’s finding his rhythm again, we’ve brought forward our prediction. We’re now backing Ronaldo to score his thousandth career goal in February 2027, although his international situation could yet push that date back.

Tommy’s Take on Red–the Color Fashion Can’t (and Shouldn’t) Quit

📅 Jun 21, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

One of fashion’s earliest street-style photographers, Tommy Ton has always had a unique point of view on what makes an outfit sing. His subjects—individuals with style and substance in equal measure—aren’t following trends. They’re expressing something deeper, and that difference is what makes his shots so compelling. Sure, the clothes are always great, but his takes capture the true essence of the wearer. In monthly installments surrounding a fashion theme, he'll explore the outfits that caught his eye and why. Here’s Tommy’s Take.

There is something about red that fashion simply cannot get enough of. Crimson, poppy, scarlet, cherry, oxblood—the shades are seemingly endless, and so is our fascination with them. What began as an easy way to inject color into an otherwise neutral wardrobe has evolved into something much bigger. At this point, I would say fashion has developed a full-blown case of scarlet fever.

Traditionally, I associated red with evening dressing. There was something inherently glamorous, perhaps even a little theatrical, about wearing it after dark. But that distinction has all but disappeared. Red has found its way into every corner of the wardrobe, from casual daytime looks to evening ensembles, and some of the most compelling ways I've seen it worn lately are decidedly understated.

Not so long ago, a red accessory was the easiest entry point: a handbag, a pair of shoes, or perhaps a flash of red socks. It was the little punctuation mark that made an outfit feel more considered. Today, however, we're embracing red as the starting point of an entire look. Take Polo Ralph Lauren's red Harrington jacket, a beautifully executed Chanel cap-toe pump, or even a luxurious red sweater casually tied around the waist. I particularly love the latter, bringing an unexpected flash of color to jeans or tailored trousers. It's a simple styling gesture that can transform an entire outfit.

And then there is the appeal of wearing red from head to toe. Who could forget the monochromatic look that came down Michael Rider's Celine runway this past season? There was an almost collective gasp. Seeing such a saturated look presented with that kind of conviction was a reminder of just how arresting the color can be. Red wasn't merely an accent; it was the entire statement.

There is a certain pleasure in committing to one color, allowing different textures, proportions, and shades to create interest. A scarlet knit paired with matching trousers, or a cherry-red anorak worn over an equally saturated ensemble, makes quite a statement. After seasons of pared-back palettes and quiet luxury, there is something refreshing about dressing with a little more conviction.

Depending on the shade, red can be romantic, provocative, sporty, polished, or even a little rebellious. Cherry has a playful quality, scarlet carries a certain theatricality, while oxblood offers a moodier, more understated interpretation. Perhaps that's why our infatuation with the color has lasted so long. It has been reinterpreted countless times, yet somehow manages to feel exciting all over again.

Whatever the reason, I'm not quite ready to give up on red. Whether worn as a subtle accent, an everyday staple, or embraced from head to toe, it has a way of making even the most familiar wardrobe feel exciting again. And judging by the way we're wearing it now, I don't see our scarlet fever subsiding anytime soon.

Is more more, or is less more? For years, we’ve been told that good taste is knowing when to stop. Coco Chanel’s famous advice about looking in the mirror before leaving the house and removing one thing has become something of a fashion commandment for many. But I’ve always wondered what happens when we do the opposite. What if, instead of taking something away, you add another necklace, another ring, another earring, another bracelet? What if the most interesting thing about getting dressed is not knowing when to stop, but having the confidence not to?

After seasons of quiet luxury and wardrobes edited down to the essentials, I find myself drawn to a more expressive approach to accessorizing. Somewhere along the way, restraint became synonymous with sophistication, and the absence of adornment was elevated into a marker of taste. But minimalism, for all its elegance, can become its own uniform. When everyone is wearing the same monogram pendant or oversized belt buckle, how much individuality is left?

Jewelry is most interesting when it disrupts that uniformity. I love the woman with rings on nearly every finger, a wrist stacked with cuffs and bracelets, necklaces layered at different lengths and crowded with pendants, and earrings that seem to be having a conversation of their own. There is something wonderfully assured about a look that refuses to be fully resolved. It doesn’t ask whether every piece matches, whether the proportions are balanced, or whether one more accessory might be too much. It simply embraces the pleasure of wearing what you love.

What strikes me most about this more-is-more approach is that it rarely feels calculated. The best examples are always a little imperfect: a delicate chain mixed with a heavy pendant; a diamond ring beside an inexpensive silver band; a charm-laden brooch that clinks with every movement. Nothing needs to match, nor should it. The most compelling combinations look as if they came together over time, collected from different places, people, and phases of one’s life. The result feels lived-in rather than meticulously styled.

Perhaps this is why the current appetite for accessorizing feels so refreshing. Not everything needs to be versatile, timeless, or sensible. Some things are worth wearing simply because they make you happy.

So the next time you reach for just one necklace, consider adding another. Then perhaps a third. Put on the rings you usually save for evenings out, stack your bracelets, and let the earrings be a little outrageous. You can always take something off. But there is a certain joy in discovering that you don’t want to.

In an industry as vast and layered as fashion, it’s worth asking: Who inspires the tastemakers? Who shapes the eye of the people who decide what is photographed and published, what earns a place on the runway, and what ultimately finds its way onto the racks of the world’s most coveted department stores?

Designers long occupied the highest tier of fashion’s hierarchy of influence. Together with editors, journalists, stylists, and buyers, they defined what we desired before we had the language to desire it ourselves. Now that we live under the influence of algorithms and celebrity and influencer culture, genuine style has become increasingly difficult to distinguish from mere visibility. True originality is quieter—and infinitely more compelling.

Several times a year, the industry converges on the world’s fashion capitals. Equal parts work and reunion, Fashion Week carries the unmistakable energy of summer camp, bringing together friends and colleagues who have spent months apart. Between the action of the shows themselves, another spectacle quietly unfolds on the sidewalks and in the queues. Fashion week remains one of the world’s greatest people-watching opportunities.

It is often here, unexpectedly, that the most memorable style reveals itself. Not on the runway, but on an unfamiliar face glimpsed again and again throughout the week. You begin to notice the subtle details: the effortless knot of a belt cinching a jacket, the unexpected layering of heirloom pendants, the confidence of someone dressing entirely for themselves rather than for the cameras. Before long, you’ve developed a style crush on someone whose name you may never learn.

This is an ode to those women: the ones who influence the influential. The insiders whose instinct, rather than their following, has earned the admiration of fashion’s most discerning insiders. They are, quite simply, your favorite fashion insider’s favorite fashion insider.

Fashion experts have always emphasized investing in a wardrobe of foundational essentials: the perfect white shirt, a tailored suit, a little black dress, and so on. The answer is quite simple: focus on accessories. Small accents, whether it’s a bag in an interesting color, a brooch that has a deeper meaning, or a belt that invites a closer look, can instantly give your wardrobe more clarity and a fresh perspective. Clothes come and go, but a beautifully curated collection of accessories stays in rotation for longer than a single season when you invest wisely in the right pieces.
