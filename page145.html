20 Fantastic Animated Series That Outshine Most Live-Action Shows

📅 Feb 19, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

It's widely agreed by most that animated film and television are incredibly and unfairly disregarded by many as true art. So much more can be accomplished visually and stylistically in the realm of animation. It's especially impressive when there's a great animated television show, requiring not just a longer runtime to animate, but typically released every week.

Animated shows are so wonderful. Being serialized projects doesn't make them only for kids, though, like typical Saturday morning cartoons are (however, some of these are). If animated television has shown the world anything, it's that animation can be for everyone, kids and adults — sometimes even adults only. While some may see them as just "cartoons" and nothing to take too seriously, in fact, many animated products often find themselves being better than what one would find in live-action projects.

With the X-Men coming to the Marvel Cinematic Universe, people are all theorizing and wondering what the plot could be like for this new iteration. With the cast that's been announced being so young, many people have been hoping — praying — that they take from the likes of the animated series, X-Men: Evolution.

This series brings the iconic characters into a much younger age, making them high schoolers, rather than teachers at Xavier's School for Gifted Youngsters. Making the main team so much younger than they are typically seen brings the opportunity for all-new kinds of interpersonal drama that can't be found outside of it. It makes one wonder why they aren't depicted as teenagers more often (it also kills the controversial love triangle that is commonly between Wolverine, Jean Grey, and Cyclops—thank goodness).

While 2011 may seem like a disastrous year for the Green Lantern character, many don't realize that was only in cinema. The hero had an animated series that began the same year that is genuinely one of the best adaptations of the Hal Jordan (Josh Keaton) character. Overshadowed by the terrible Green Lantern movie, though, Green Lantern: The Animated Series was never given the shot it deserved.

The way this series adapts the entire Green Lantern Corps is almost perfect. It manages to have some mature themes and stories while still being wholly accessible for younger audiences. It feels like a space opera in its scale, but still juggles personal relationships and character arcs as if it were a grounded street-level show somehow.

If someone hasn't already heard the comic book fans of the world raving about 1992's X-Men: The Animated Series, they've probably been living under a rock for a few decades. It's so popular that it even got revived with Disney+'s X-Men '97—people love it that much. For good reason, too, as X-Men: The Animated Series has some of the best superhero storytelling on television.

This five-year-long show has some of the deepest, most meaningful storytelling in animated superhero television. The X-Men have always had this over most Marvel Comics properties. The show accurately and truthfully depicts themes and messages about discrimination and bigotry while still being an action-packed, kid-friendly romp.

One of the most popular modern anime is none other than the 2016 series My Hero Academia. Kohei Horikoshi—the mangaka of the franchise—does an exceptional job blending typical Western superhero tropes and character archetypes with the shonen anime style. My Hero Academia is different than what most people expect from a superhero show, with a unique premise, world, and characters that can only be found in this franchise.

The premise of a young boy in a mostly superpowered society who has no abilities getting the greatest power on the planet from the literal Symbol of Peace is already engrossing. But when mixed with the one-of-a-kind characters surrounding him — with equally special power sets — and emotionally compelling stories and character arcs, My Hero Academia becomes a truly distinctive and special series.

From the exceptional minds of Greg Weisman and Brandon Vietti, Young Justice is one of those shows that truly captures the teenage experience (much like another popular show of Weisman's). Taking a page out of Teen Titans, Young Justice depicts a team of sidekicks and young heroes coming together to form a team of their own.

Unlike Teen Titans, this show goes even deeper with its mature storytelling — less anime-inspired. When it comes to good animated DC Comics content, Young Justice is up there with the best of the best. The storytelling at play, as well as the character arcs, make Young Justice one of the best DC Comics animated properties out there.

It was always going to be next to impossible to perfectly follow up Avatar: The Last Airbender, but with this in mind, the studios behind both that series and The Legend of Korra did pretty dang well when it came to developing the latter. The sequel series, following an all-new Avatar, Korra (Janet Varney), is not perfect, but still an exceptional show, at the end of the day.

People give The Legend of Korra a lot of flak for simply not being the same series Avatar: The Last Airbender was. Expecting the follow-up of one of the best television series of all time to be even better is the definition of overly wishful thinking. At the end of the day, though, The Legend of Korra still finds itself being better than a plethora of series out there, thanks to how well-written it is, especially when it comes to the development of the show's world and characters.

Kids who grew up on Regular Show consistently call back to it even today, thanks to how hilarious it is. Many attribute Regular Show to their appreciation for absurdist humor, and absolutely adore the wonderful Mordecai (J. G. Quintel) and Rigby (William Salyers), along with the numerous characters that surround them.

Regular Show gets used as a meme—people use the absurd situations that Mordecai and Rigby consistently find themselves in on a typical Tuesday as a joke, all the time, which has kept the memory of the show alive. People simply love this show, and some even beg for it to make a comeback somehow, despite the ending of the original series being incredibly good and satisfying as it is.

One of the most beloved superhero animated series of the last few years is, without a doubt, Invincible. Based on the Skybound comic book of the same name, written by Robert Kirkman, this show came to Prime Video and captured the hearts of the world almost immediately. Despite some failings in the animation department, at times, Invincible still remains one of the most popular shows on the air at the moment.

Thanks to the work of some amazing vocal performances from the likes of Steven Yeun, J.K. Simmons, Gillian Jacobs, Seth Rogen, Walton Goggins, and many more, the characters pop off the screen and come to life. This, combined with some great storytelling thanks to Kirkman and the new writers on the writing team, makes Invincible such a great watch.

One of the greatest accomplishments to come from Disney Channel/Disney XD is, without a doubt, the ever-iconic Gravity Falls. Brought to the screen by Alex Hirsch, this animated series does an abundance of things right. It crafts a compelling mystery across the entire plot, while crafting just as interesting ones each week in a serialized format.

There's genuinely so much to love about this show. Despite being an animated show on Disney Channel/Disney XD, anyone can watch and enjoy Gravity Falls. The humor is stellar, the voice acting is lively, the story is engaging, and all of the character arcs are compelling. Not to mention, it went the extra mile to get audiences invested by hiding little hints, secrets, clues, and more into the show that could be used to discover more — the intro theme has a backwards message at the end, which changed each season to act as another cipher.

One of the greatest anime ever made comes from the studio that made the extremely popular My Hero Academia, Studio Bones. After a first series that was pretty mixed, they brought the Fullmetal Alchemist franchise back in 2009 with a show that was more accurate and faithful to the original manga, which was called Fullmetal Alchemist: Brotherhood.

Thanks to how amazing the manga by Hiromu Arakawa is, Fullmetal Alchemist: Brotherhood has found itself being one of the most beloved anime of the modern age, with a whopping and clean 100% on Rotten Tomatoes. It's such an excellently told story that audiences can't help but get engaged and care about it. Not to mention, Studio Bones animated it phenomenally well.

You get the power that is really about taking the hit so that nobody else has to. Strength in the MCU always lands on people who were already carrying too much, which is exactly why it suits you.

Europa Conference League Prize Money Breakdown (2026/2027)

📅 Mar 13, 2025  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The 2026/27 season marks the sixth season of existence for the UEFA Conference League, which was introduced in 2021 as a third-tier continental competition for European teams. Since then, some have questioned the Conference League and its existence, but it is hard to argue that the competition is anything other than beneficial.

The Conference League, be it through their county’s coefficient or their own league rankings, gives teams a chance in Europe that they otherwise may not have access to. While a side could qualify for the Europa or Champions League, they may not be well-equipped for the calibre of the tournament but have a squad that perfectly suits the Conference League.

In its early life thus far, the Conference League has made a claim for being the most unpredictable competition in Europe, with Roma, West Ham United, Olympiacos, Chelsea and Crystal Palace having won the competition.

Along with its other European competitions, the Conference League was revamped ahead of the 2024/25 campaign, with a league stage replacing the traditional group stage formatting. As clubs battle it out to clinch the crown once more, it is worth asking, just how is the prize money in the Conference League is divided.

For the clubs that reached the league stage of the Conference League in the 2026/27 season, they were all awarded £2.8 million for their participation. Not only that, but any victory saw teams awarded a further £349,000, with a draw seeing the sides earn just under £116,000.

Much akin to the new-look format of the Champions League and Europa League, the top eight sides in the Conference League table progress automatically to the round of 16, with teams playing just six games in that stage. Any side placed ninth, 24th or anywhere in-between, progresses to the play-offs, where eight games are contested to determine the final half of the last 16.

Teams that advance from the league stage do so with a reward of £175,000, a number that jumps quite substantially for the sides that were successful in winning their play-off clashes. Qualification for the Conference League round of 16 also means an extra £698,000 comes into the team’s accounts.

Beyond the play-off matches, the remainder of the Conference League’s format is entirely the same as European competitions have been in the past. After the round of 16 sits the quarter-finals, with the teams that qualify for it each year all having a tentative eye on the trophy as one of the competition’s final participants.

In 2026/27, clubs that qualify for the quarter-finals do so with a bonus of just under £1.1 million, with the quarter-finals being the first stage of the Conference League–other than the base league stage–to offer participants a six-figure sum for reaching it.

The semi-finals of the Conference League, at least for the 2026/27 season, take place in the final week of April and the first week of May, with the four teams remaining by that point set to battle it out for a spot in the final. In reaching the final quartet of sides in the competition, the clubs will be awarded about £2.1 million.

Then, to the final itself. Set to be played at Besiktas Park in Turkey on June 2, 2027, it will determine the new Conference League champions. The teams that reach the final will receive almost £3.5 million while the winner will be given an extra £2.6 million on top of that.

2025/26 Conference League Prize Money Breakdown

Last season’s edition of European competitions were the second to be conducted with a new format. As such, the money that the teams in 2025/26 received differed slightly from what could have been expected when the traditional group stage format was still in place.

The changes made to the competition meant that sides received around £200,000 more solely for making it to the league stage than they would have with traditional groups. Though bonuses for a win or draw became slightly lower than they once were, as did the amount received for reaching the knock-out stages, from there, things have changed.

Where once teams were given just over £500,000 for reaching the round of 16, they now get almost £698,000. Similarly, teams now receive around £300,000 more for getting to the quarter-finals than they once did. Those in the semi-finals get an extra £500,000 and the two sides that make the Conference League final stand to earn a lot more than previous years, especially the winners.

The perfect Thanksgiving turkey is easier to make than you might think. Whether you're a first-timer planning a Friendsgiving dinner for your closest pals or the one whose house the whole family gathers at year after year, this recipe will help make your turkey the star of the dinner table. Let this be the year you ace Thanksgiving hosting and find your annual go-to guide for everything—roasting time, thawing, the secret to perfectly browned skin—on how to present the perfect roast bird.

Remove the giblets, trussing, and pop-up thermometers from the turkey. Pat the turkey completely dry inside and out with paper towels. If you plan to cut the wishbone out now, face the turkey neck opening toward you and locate the V-shaped wishbone in the neck cavity. With a sharp and flexible boning knife, using a short but firm swiping motion, gently cut the bone from the breast meat by making long incisions on both sides of the bone. Pry the bone loose with a paper towel or clean kitchen towel and discard. Using your fingers, separate the turkey breast from the skin.

In a spice grinder or mortar and pestle, grind all of the spices until they're finely ground and well combined. This will allow the spices to adhere to the surface of the turkey, especially the dried thyme.

Cover the entire turkey with the seasoning mix, turning the turkey over to season the back as well. Be sure to season under the breast skin and in the cavity. Transfer the turkey to a roasting rack. Refrigerate, uncovered, until cold, at least 12 hours and up to 2 days.

Arrange a rack in the lower third of the oven with no rack above it; preheat to 450°. Gently pat the turkey with paper towels to remove any excess moisture. Place the shallot, lemon, garlic, fresh thyme, and sage in the turkey cavity and neck opening. Be sure not to overfill the turkey. Err on the side of not stuffing everything in if it doesn’t fit. The turkey cavity needs airflow to cook evenly, and blocking that airflow will result in a longer cook time and a drier turkey. Drizzle the turkey with oil. Insert a leave-in probe thermometer (if using) into the turkey thigh or breast and transfer the roasting pan to the oven.

Roast the turkey for 30 minutes. Reduce the oven temperature to 350°. Continue to roast until the temperature in the thickest part of the breast registers 155° and the thigh registers 165°, about 1 hour and 30 minutes more, up to 2 hours more, rotating once during cooking to help the skin brown evenly. Let the turkey rest, uncovered, for 45 minutes before carving.

40 Greatest Football Players Of All Time (2026)

📅 Dec 21, 2022  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

One of the great football debates will continue to rage on so long as the sport exists. Indeed, deciding who is the greatest footballer of all time is never easy, particularly as fans, wearing their bias boldly, claim their club legend is the best.

It tends to be a common theme that a lot of people consider Cristiano Ronaldo, Lionel Messi, Diego Maradona and Pele to be the most prominent names in the hat for being the best player to have ever played the game, but always in different orders.

Unsurprisingly, all four of those names make it onto our list, but who else joins them in a ranking of the 40 greatest footballers of all-time?

Factors taken into consideration when ranking these players are as follows:

For Liverpool fans, Kenny Dalglish is the club's greatest ever footballer. He scored the winning goal in the 1978 European Cup final, going on to win that competition three more times, while also delivering six league titles to Anfield, not to mention a whole host of other honours. Celtic fans also hold him in similar esteem after he won four league titles, four Scottish Cups, and a Scottish League Cup with the Bhoys, too.

'King Kenny' also made 102 appearances for Scotland, their most ever, and is the joint-leading goalscorer (on 30, with Denis Law). His influence on British football cannot be overstated, as he went on to win the Premier League as Blackburn Rovers manager, having already coached Liverpool to three First Division titles. He truly dominated football throughout the 1970s and 80s.

Michael Laudrup was well and truly one of the most graceful midfielders to ever play the sport. 'I've been trying to be like him since I was a kid," Andres Iniesta once said of his childhood idol. That should tell you all you need to know about the Danish maestro.

Laudrup won La Liga titles with both Barcelona and Real Madrid, and also won Serie A in Italy with Juventus and Eredivisie with Ajax. Despite playing with some of the greatest footballers of all time, both Romario and Raul have described the Dane as the best player they ever played with. Michel Platini once said, “Michael had everything except for one thing: he wasn’t selfish enough.” Perhaps he would rank a little higher on this list if he was, but his unselfishness certainly didn't hold him back much.

Luis Suarez (not to be mistaken with the Uruguayan striker of the same name) was a wonderfully talented Spanish midfielder. Had he been born four or five decades later, he would have slotted seamlessly alongside the likes of Xavi, Iniesta and Sergio Busquets. Instead, he simply led the way for them as a pioneer of the game.

Indeed, Suarez won La Liga twice with Barcelona, which helped him seal the 1960 Ballon d'Or. He would then deliver three Serie A titles for Inter Milan, while also winning two European Cups in the same decade. He played more like an attacking midfielder (bit like Iniesta) in his youth, but then became a deep-lying playmaker (more akin to Xavi) as he aged.

Bobby Moore is one of the true iconic footballing captains of the sport, famously leading England to their only ever World Cup triumph in 1966. The legendary defender was best known at club level for his time with West Ham, leading them to FA Cup glory in 1964, while also winning the Charity Shield and European Cup Winners' Cup with the Hammers.

Pele spent his career making a mockery of centre-backs across the globe, more on that later, but Moore was one of just two men who truly gave the Brazilian a tough time. The late striker recalled: "You see, whoever I played against, I had the best defender marking me, but the best were Bobby Moore and Franz Beckenbauer."

Modern football fans will best know Giuseppe Meazza as the alternate title to the San Siro, after it was renamed in his honour back in March 1980. Spending the best years of his career with Inter, and returning there at the end of his career, winning three Serie A titles and later managing them, it's no wonder he's so loved in those parts. He also briefly played for AC Milan.

His greatest success came at the international level, though, as he led Italy to win two consecutive World Cups – in 1934, and then as captain in 1938. Despite playing so many years ago, only one man has more than his 33 goals for Italy (Luigi Riva, 42). Nicknamed Il genio (The Genius) by the Italian press, Meazza was a true joy to watch and will forever be one of the greatest strikers in football history.

Sir Stanley Matthews is one of the best English players ever. He was knighted in 1965 for his achievements in football while he was still at Stoke City. He remains the only footballer to be knighted during his playing career. Although he came from Stoke, his best achievement came at Blackpool, where he won the FA Cup in 1953.

Three years later, he won the inaugural Ballon d'Or, beating the likes of Alfredo Di Stefano and Ferenc Puskas to first place. He also remains in the English record books for making an appearance in a top-flight match at the age of 50.

Gareth Bale being on this list might surprise some, particularly when someone like Eden Hazard isn't. However, he was consistently world-class and epitomised the idea of 'hard work'. He made a name for himself at Tottenham Hotspur. The Welshman won just one trophy there - the 2008 Carling Cup - but he carried the team on his back.Bale famously broke onto the scene with a hat-trick away to European champions Inter Milan in 2010.

He eventually moved to Real Madrid for a world-record fee where he won everything he could. Bale's overhead kick to win the 2018 Champions League against Liverpool remains one of the greatest goals in the competition's history.

Manuel Neuer is renowned for being one of the first 'modern' goalkeepers, who was extremely skillful with the ball at his feet and has adopted the role of sweeper-keeper for many years. In fact, in 2020, he was awarded the International Federation of Football History & Statistics (IFFHS) goalkeeper of the decade award for his performances between 2011 and 2020.

He has won an abundance of team trophies as well. Most notably, he won the World Cup with Germany in 2014. Domestically, he has won the Bundesliga 13 times and the Champions League twice, with the most recent of these triumphs coming in the 2019/20 campaign.

Carlos Alberto is most well-known for captaining the Brazil team in the 1970 World Cup final. The Selecao beat Italy 4-1 in the final, with Alberto scoring the last goal, which is considered one of the best goals in football history. After a period of swift passes between the men in yellow, Pele laid the ball off to the right-back who smashed the ball into the bottom corner in front of over 107,000 people at Estadio Azteca.

Alberto was recognised for his achievements for club and country in 1998 when he was selected for the World Team of the 20th century. Domestically, he won 16 trophies for Fluminense, Santos and New York Cosmos before eventually retiring in 1982.

Kaka won the Ballon d'Or in 2007 after winning the Champions League with AC Milan. The team and individual achievements solidified his status as one of the greatest players of all time. He also won the World Cup in 2002 in his first year representing his country, highlighting his legendary status at an early stage. For AC Milan, he excelled over six years from 2003-2009, scoring 75 goals in 270 matches.

His spell at Real Madrid in the years was not as impressive, but he still managed to score 29 goals in 120 matches while winning La Liga and the Copa del Rey. The Brazilian will always be remembered as one of the sport's best entertainers — and one of the best Brazilians.

A shredder is still largely a mechanical device, a motor driving a set of cutters, augmented in many models by sensors and electronic controls. Unlike the shredders you may remember from old movies, most modern machines no longer turn documents into long linguini-like strips. Cross-cut shredders slice paper both lengthwise and crosswise into small rectangular scraps. Micro-cut models slice and dice those scraps into even tinier bits, leaving hundreds or even thousands of petite particles.

For most home and home-office use, either cutting method provides plenty of protection. Most shredders these days offer micro-cut machines with either P-4 security, which makes reconstructing a document the most puzzling jigsaw puzzle ever, or P-5 security, whose even more diminutive document destruction methods are favored by MDs, CPAs, JDs, or by other professionals with initials in their titles.

The larger and more powerful your shredder, the more sheets it can generally chew through at one time. Just don’t treat the number printed on the box as a dare.

Manufacturers’ rated sheet capacities are best-case numbers, usually based on neatly aligned standard-weight bond paper. Thicker stock, folds, staples, uneven piles, and stuffed envelopes can quickly turn a “12-sheet” machine into something more comfortable with eight or nine. Feeding a few sheets below the rated maximum is one of the easiest ways to avoid jams and unnecessary strain.

Manual shredders require you to stand there feeding paper in batches. Auto-feed machines let you load a stack, walk away, and get something else done while the shredder eats.

Here again, don’t take the rated auto-feed sheet capacity too literally. An “80-sheet” or “200-sheet” auto feeder may accommodate that many pristine sheets under ideal conditions, but real paperwork is rarely pristine. Wrinkles, folds, different paper weights, staples, and uneven stacks can reduce how much you can reliably leave it to swallow unattended. Plus, the bin on a high-capacity auto-feed model may fill before the maximum number of sheets are fed into it.

Auto-feed mechanisms also add more rollers, sensors, and jam-clearing compartments. And more moving mechanical parts create more places for something to eventually go wrong.

However, if you routinely accumulate large piles, the time a highly rated auto-feed shredder saves can easily outweigh the added complexity and potential mechanical issues.

A bigger bin means fewer trips to empty it, but gallon capacity tells only part of the story. Pull-out drawers are lighter and easier to clean and empty. Bins that require lifting the often heavy shredder mechanism off the top of a wastepaper basket can potentially scatter loose particles still in the cutter mechanism.

Also pay attention to what happens when you pull that drawer out. An easy-to-remove bin that keeps most of its shredded paper actually in the bin will save you emptying and cleanup aggravation, especially if you don’t wait until the bin is filled to its brim.

Shredders may look sturdy and impervious, but most are actually quite finicky eaters. Nearly every shredder will eventually jam if you feed it too much at one time or feed it the wrong type, shape, or mix of paper. More advanced models with more advanced anti-jam systems sense an overload before one occurs, or either automatically reverse or allow you to manually reverse to relieve the jam before you need to perform paper-removal surgery from the cutters with tweezers or needle-nose pliers.

The cheapest jam protection, however, is restraint. If a machine claims 12 sheets, regularly feeding it eight or nine instead will lower your odds of spending quality time violently tugging mangled paper back out of its teeth.

Most current shredders can swallow staples and paper clips, and many can also chew up plastic credit cards. CDs and other thicker media such as metal cards are less universally welcome.

Check the manufacturer’s instructions before you buy for what non-paper materials a shredder can successfully chew and before feeding anything other than paper into its cutters. As with nearly all things, don’t be penny wise and pound foolish. You will find you will shred more than you anticipated—it’s just human nature to use a new device more than you though you would. Overall, shredders costing several hundreds of dollars make the most sense for people who regularly devour large stacks or have a specialized need. Don’t decide what to spend simply by asking how private your paperwork is; ask how much of it you need to turn into confetti and how often both now and in the future.

The most important shredder “spec” may be how little time it forces you to spend with it. Sheet capacity, runtime, security level, and bin capacity are the headline numbers I’d look at first. Runtime is particularly useful because it tells you something about how much work the machine can comfortably handle before demanding a rest.

But some of the things that most determine whether you’ll love or hate a shredder aren’t specs at all. Nearly every decent modern shredder can reduce ordinary documents beyond casual recognition. The bigger differences are how often it jams, how easily those jams clear, how cleanly the bin empties, and how much confetti winds up everywhere except inside it.

Check owner reviews for recurring complaints about jams, overheating, finicky sensors, messy bins, and other annoyances. AI agents on online sites can summarize customer reviews to answer many of these before-you-buy questions.

With shredders, old doesn’t necessarily mean obsolete. Sometimes it means proven.Unlike most consumer electronics, shredders don’t become outdated because they lack this year’s faster processor, new operating system, wireless standard, or other bleeding-edge technology. An older model that’s still attracting buyers and good reviews may actually offer something a brand-new machine can’t: years of accumulated evidence about how well it holds up.

Before buying an older model, check whether the manufacturer has introduced a newer or revised version. When shopping online, search the manufacturer’s current lineup or, if the retailer provides an AI or shopping assistant, ask whether there’s a newer version of the model you’re considering. Then compare old to new rather than automatically assuming new means better.

The best shredder maintenance is self-restraint. Don’t continually stuff the cutters to their rated maximum, allow the bin overflow, or feed the machine materials it wasn’t designed to digest.

Lubrication requirements vary. Some manufacturers recommend shredder oil applied at regular intervals, others suggest pre-lubricated oil sheets, and some models claim to require little or no lubrication at all. Follow the instructions for your particular machine rather than assuming every shredder requires the same type of mechanical TLC.

Beyond that, maintenance should be pretty painless: empty the bin before the paper snow reaches the cutters, occasionally clean dusty sensors if the manufacturer recommends it, and clear jams rather than repeatedly hammering the reverse button and hoping for divine intervention.

Despite the name, most modern “shredders” no longer shred paper into long strips. While cross cut and micro cut are clues for how small the ultimate shred pieces will be, P-ratings, part of the DIN 66399 security standard, put an actual number on “small.”

P-ratings run from P-1 through P-7; the higher the number, the smaller the pieces produced. For ordinary household paperwork, P-3 is already plenty secure. A cross-cut P-3 machine reduces a document to hundreds of pieces, which is more than enough discouragement for anyone contemplating reconstructing your bills, junk mail, receipts, or other everyday paperwork. After all, a document pieced back together like a taped mosaic isn’t likely to fool many people.

P-4 is the sweet spot for buyers who want their paperwork diced considerably finer without spending much more, and P-4 is common among consumer micro-cut shredders. P-5 turns documents into even tinier bits and makes sense for especially sensitive financial, legal, medical, or professional paperwork. P-6 and P-7 go further still, into security levels intended for documents whose contents matter considerably more than last month’s Visa bill.

For most of us, then, P-3 is plenty, P-4 buys extra peace of mind, and P-5 is for paperwork you’d really rather nobody put back together.

You want a strong motor with enough juice to shave or cut to the right length cleanly, smoothly, and with a minimal number of passes (more passes make for more skin irritation).
