Dems descend on Denver to size up 2028. We asked who wants in the race

📅 Nov 14, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

At the "What’s Next" gathering in Denver, Democratic leaders weighed the party’s future and whether they could see themselves in the 2028 presidential race.

DENVER — Democrats are already making their case for 2028.

Fresh off sweeping midterm victories, party leaders and rising stars descended on Denver for the "What’s Next" conference. Several potential presidential contenders tested their 2028 messaging before a crowd of activists, strategists and operatives.

The conversations offered an early look at who may be considering a run and who is already stepping aside.

Less than 24 hours after announcing a challenge to longtime Senate Democratic leader Sen. Chuck Schumer, D-N.Y., Sen. Chris Van Hollen, D-Md., told reporters he was "no longer pursuing" a presidential bid.

"I believe that the size and scale of these majorities, provides an opportunity for the United States Senate to answer the call of the American people," Van Hollen told reporters in Denver, as he pointed what will be at least a 55-seat Democratic majority in the chamber.

Van Hollen’s comments came as Sen. Cory Booker, D-N.J., weighed whether to make another run for the White House in 2028.

Sen. Cory Booker, D-N.J., is among the Democratic figures drawing attention as the party begins looking toward 2028. (Paul Steinhauser/Fox News Digital)

Booker said he is giving "a lot of hard thought" to a potential 2028 presidential campaign, though he said his focus now is pushing for what he called an "American renewal."

"My purpose right now, I want to see America in the next five years have the biggest renewal, the biggest resetting of the economic table so it works for all Americans," Booker told Fox News Digital.

He pointed to the cost of housing, healthcare and childcare as evidence that many Americans are struggling to get ahead. "The gap I’m concerned about is not the gap between left and right anymore, it’s a gap between the American people and the American dream, and I want to see that gap close," he said.

Asked what he might do differently if he ran again after his unsuccessful 2020 campaign, Booker said candidates should be "their authentic self." He also warned that political divisions are pulling the country apart, saying, "We are not each other’s enemies. We are our fellow patriots. And we all need to start pulling in the same direction."

Former Transportation Secretary Pete Buttigieg, who ran for the Democratic presidential nomination in 2020, said he would have no news about a potential 2028 bid "anytime soon." (Paul Steinhauser/Fox News Digital)

While Van Hollen ruled out a presidential bid, former Transportation Secretary Pete Buttigieg, a 2020 Democratic candidate, told reporters in Denver he would not have "any news to make about that anytime soon."

Even so, Buttigieg has not ruled out another run.

Pennsylvania Gov. Josh Shapiro, a potential 2028 contender, said he wants to be "a loud voice" in shaping the Democratic Party’s future during the "What's Next" conference in Denver, Colorado on Nov. 13, 2026. (Paul Steinhauser/Fox News Digital)

Pennsylvania Gov. Josh Shapiro, fresh off of major victories in his home state, said, "I've decided to be a loud voice in this process that we're going through right now as a party to put ourselves in the best position to compete."

When asked about his 2028 timeline, Shapiro said "that would be a personal decision my wife and my kids and I make."

"When we're ready to say something, we'll let you know," he added.

Sen. Elissa Slotkin, D-Mich., told reporters in Denver she is seriously considering a 2028 presidential run during the 'What's Next' conference on Nov. 13, 2026. (Jessica Sonkin/Fox News)

Sen. Elissa Slotkin of Michigan told reporters that she was "seriously" considering a 2028 presidential run.

"I haven't made any decisions. Whether it's me or somebody else, I just want to be a part of that next generation of leaders going forward that does things differently and understands who they serve," she added.

Pointing to a new crop of Democrats elected in the midterms, Slotkin said "we had an amazing election night that elected a whole bunch of new folks who represent that."

Sen. Ruben Gallego, D-Ariz., said his young family will weigh heavily in his decision on whether to run for president in 2028. Gallego spoke to reporters on the sidelines of the "What's Next" conference in Denver. (Paul Steinhauser/Fox News Digital)

Meanwhile, Sen. Ruben Gallego of Arizona said his young family will be a major consideration in his decision.

"I have three kids, nine, three and a one-year-old. You know, it's hard to manage that schedule and I have to figure out how to still be a good dad and run for office," he said.

"If I run I'll probably be the only one that has kids that age that doesn't necessarily come from a lot of money. So I'm going to have to figure all that out," he added.

Sen. Raphael Warnock, D-Ga., who has not said whether he will seek the 2028 nomination, used his speech at "What's Next" to argue Democrats can build a winning coalition across the country.

"In America, all things are possible," he said, pointing to Democratic victories from New Hampshire to Mississippi.

Rep. Ro Khanna, D-Calif., said he is "certainly considering" a 2028 presidential run while speaking at the "What’s Next" conference in Denver on Nov. 13, 2026. (Paul Steinhauser/Fox News Digital)

Khanna, one of the few leaders on the progressive left attending a conference organized by a centrist Democratic group, added "I think after the reception here, I'm, more likely to consider it. I mean, but I'm going around the country seeing whether the message will resonate."

"Mezzo secolo di storia al servizio del calcio italiano". La nota per i 50 anni dell'ADISE

📅 Nov 14, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Domani, 15 novembre 2026, l’ADISE (Associazione Italiana Direttori Sportivi) celebra i suoi primi cinquant’anni di storia. Di seguito il comunicato integrale:

"Mezzo secolo durante il quale ha accompagnato l’evoluzione del calcio italiano, contribuendo in maniera determinante al riconoscimento, alla qualificazione e alla tutela di una delle figure centrali nell’organizzazione delle società calcistiche: il direttore sportivo. Bisogna tornare agli anni Sessanta e Settanta per comprendere le ragioni di quella iniziativa. Il calcio italiano stava attraversando una profonda trasformazione e, accanto alla tradizionale figura del segretario, storico collaboratore del presidente nella gestione delle attività societarie, cominciava ad affermarsi una nuova professionalità, chiamata a occuparsi della programmazione tecnica, della scelta dei calciatori e delle trattative di mercato.

Fu proprio un gruppo di questi dirigenti, consapevole della necessità di dare identità, dignità e riconoscimento a una professione emergente, a costituire il 15 novembre 1976 l’ADISE, originariamente Associazione dei Direttori e dei Segretari delle società sportive. Il primo presidente fu Carlo Montanari, dirigente del Bologna, affiancato dai vicepresidenti Franco Manni della Fiorentina e Giulio Cappelli del Lecco. Il primo Consiglio Direttivo comprendeva anche Sergio Sacchero, segretario del Monza, Giacomo Randazzo dell’Atalanta e numerosi altri esponenti della dirigenza calcistica nazionale, tra i quali Giancarlo Beltrami, Giorgio Vitali, Piero Aggradi, Giancarlo Fiumi e Arturo Silvestri. La prima sede dell’Associazione fu stabilita a Monza, in via Manzoni 9.

Da quella iniziativa prese avvio un percorso destinato a lasciare un segno profondo nell’organizzazione del calcio italiano. L’ADISE divenne progressivamente l’interlocutore delle istituzioni calcistiche per il riconoscimento della figura del direttore sportivo, sostenendone la regolamentazione professionale, la definizione delle competenze e la tutela dei rapporti di lavoro con le società. Un impegno che ha trovato importanti risultati nella disciplina federale della professione e soprattutto nella stipulazione degli accordi collettivi con le Leghe, fondamentali per assicurare garanzie contrattuali e giuridiche a una categoria sempre più qualificata. Una storia fatta anche di uomini che hanno segnato il calcio italiano. Dopo Carlo Montanari, alla guida dell’Associazione si sono succeduti Franco Dalcin, Pierpaolo Marino e Carlo Regalia, fino all’attuale presidente Giuseppe Marotta, oggi presidente dell’Inter e tra le personalità più autorevoli della dirigenza calcistica internazionale.

Presidenze che hanno attraversato stagioni diAerenti, contribuendo a consolidare il prestigio e la rappresentatività dell’ADISE. Ma parlare dell’ADISE significa inevitabilmente parlare anche del Calciomercato, con il quale l’Associazione ha costruito un rapporto storico e privilegiato. Fin dagli anni Ottanta, e in particolare con l’aAidamento uAiciale della sua organizzazione nel 1989, l’ADISE è stata protagonista degli appuntamenti milanesi dedicati alle trattative. Erano gli anni dei grandi alberghi, dei corridoi aAollati, dei salotti e dei celebri box delle società, nei quali si incontravano presidenti, direttori sportivi, allenatori e operatori di mercato. Un mondo che ha alimentato pagine indimenticabili della storia del calcio italiano e che l’Associazione ha accompagnato nella sua progressiva trasformazione verso un’organizzazione moderna e strutturata.

Ancora oggi, attraverso la collaborazione con Master Group Sport, l’ADISE continua a essere protagonista dell’organizzazione del Calciomercato, appuntamento che rappresenta non soltanto il luogo delle trattative, ma anche un’importante occasione di confronto, aggiornamento e incontro tra le diverse componenti del sistema calcistico. Altrettanto significativo è stato, nel corso dei decenni, l’impegno per la formazione dei dirigenti sportivi. Dalla storica esperienza dei corsi federali di Coverciano alle più recenti iniziative promosse dalle università statali e telematiche, l’Associazione ha sostenuto la necessità di una preparazione sempre più elevata, adeguata alle responsabilità tecniche, giuridiche, economiche e manageriali richieste dal calcio contemporaneo. Un’attenzione estesa anche al mondo dilettantistico, attraverso la valorizzazione dei collaboratori della gestione sportiva, figure che operano nelle società e nelle strutture della Lega Nazionale Dilettanti.

Una realtà alla quale l’ADISE dedica particolare attenzione anche attraverso la propria organizzazione territoriale e i rapporti con i Comitati Regionali. Oggi, con sede operativa a Bologna e un’intensa attività istituzionale che si sviluppa anche a Milano, l’ADISE continua a rappresentare un riferimento per i propri associati, impegnandosi nella tutela professionale, nell’aggiornamento degli accordi collettivi e nel confronto con la FIGC e le Leghe. Le trasformazioni introdotte dalla riforma del lavoro sportivo, l’internazionalizzazione del mercato e la presenza di nuove figure professionali rendono ancora più attuale la necessità di difendere l’identità e le competenze del direttore sportivo, evitando sovrapposizioni di ruoli e garantendo una preparazione all’altezza delle nuove esigenze. Cinquant’anni dopo, l’ADISE conserva dunque intatta la propria missione originaria: rappresentare, qualificare e tutelare i direttori sportivi e le figure della gestione sportiva, accompagnandone l’evoluzione professionale. Dal calcio dei segretari e delle prime trattative negli alberghi a quello delle grandi operazioni internazionali, l’Associazione ha attraversato mezzo secolo di trasformazioni senza perdere il proprio ruolo di riferimento. Un anniversario che celebra non soltanto una storia associativa, ma anche il contributo di intere generazioni di dirigenti alla crescita e alla modernizzazione del calcio italiano".

Meetmebythesea wins first Paddy Power Gold Cup for McManus

📅 Nov 14, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Jockey Harry Cobden rode Meetmebythesea to a first Paddy Power Gold Cup success for owner JP McManus at Cheltenham on Saturday.

The 2-1 favourite was level with champion trainer Dan Skelton's Blueking D'Oroux (10-3) going over the last fence before pulling away to win by two-and-a-half lengths.

Torneo - a 12-1 chance - was almost three lengths further back in third place.

Cobden, the 2023-24 champion jump jockey, pumped his fists in the air as he went across the finish line.

"These big days don't come along very often and I'm going to enjoy every single one of them from now on," the 28-year-old, who in May started as McManus' retained jockey in Britain and Ireland, told ITV Sport.

"To ride a horse like this in a race like that, you just have to pinch yourself to be in this position.

"It's wonderful for the boss [McManus], he puts so much into racing, I'm just the lucky man that's allowed to be part of it."

Meetmebythesea took top billing in a reduced field of 10 horses, with five non-runners including the highly fancied July Flower.

But the six-year-old lived up to that expectation, although trainer Ben Pauling stopped short of touting Meetmebythesea's chances of competing in the Gold Cup back at Cheltenham's March Festival.

"He jumped brilliantly and travelled really well... and showed a bit of guts there to win well," said Pauling, before adding "I don't know what I see him as", when asked if Meetbythesea could be a future Gold Cup horse.

"He needed to do that today. I think he showed a lot more than we have seen from him so far, but he's just really good.

"You don't know what you have got under the bonnet until you compete in races like that, but that was really cool."

It was a second big victory for Meetmebythesea at Cheltenham after winning there as a novice at the Festival in March.

What kept Nashville Predators' win streak alive? Andrew Brunette's answer is 'belief

📅 Nov 14, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

It looked like the Nashville Predators' season-high win streak was about to end.

Through two periods against Vegas on Nov. 14, the Predators trailed by a goal at T-Mobile Arena, being out-paced in all three zones by the Golden Knights.

Then, in the third period, something changed. It started with Ryan O'Reilly scoring his second goal of the game.

Finishing a play from behind the goal line, O'Reilly tied the game with 10:16 left in the third, infusing Nashville's bench with confidence. From there, the Predators flooded the Golden Knights' zone with shots over the final 10 minutes, nearly scoring the go-ahead goal several times.

Nashville (11-4-5, 27 points) held Vegas without a shot over the final 9:32 of regulation, out-shooting the Golden Knights 9-1 through the end of overtime, then winning the game 3-2 in a shootout.

Predators coach Andrew Brunette described the turnaround as a group effort, a sudden belief they could keep the streak going against the Golden Knights (13-5-3, 29 points).

"That's why we're on a winning streak. Everybody's kind of chipped in. Everybody's got belief in each other, belief in the group," Brunette said.

In overtime, the Predators outshot the Golden Knights 4-1, including one by captain Roman Josi that trickled just wide of goaltender Adin Hill. Josi would get another chance in the shootout, scoring on Hill to clinch the game for Nashville. It was Josi's first successful shootout attempt since Oct. 30, 2021.

The win kept Nashville in second place in the Central Division and held together the league's longest win streak to date, matching the Carolina Hurricanes' seven-game streak between Oct. 3-17.

Brunette pointed out the win wouldn't happen without goaltender Juuse Saros, who made a season-high 40 saves, including 32 in the first and second periods.

"(Saros) kept us hanging around," Brunette said. "I didn't love our first 25 or 35 minutes of the game. The last 25 (minutes) and through overtime looked like us."

The Predators go for their eighth straight win on Nov. 15 (8 p.m. CT, The Spot Nashville 28) against the San Jose Sharks. The longest streak in franchise history is 10 games, set between Feb. 19 and March 8 during the 2017-18 season.

Skip The Oven: This Easy Turkey Dinner Is Ready In Half The Time

📅 Nov 14, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

With Thanksgiving just around the corner, many of us are gearing up to spend time with friends and family, and enjoy a delicious meal. For those who are hosting and doing the cooking, however, there is considerably more to the holiday. Aside from cleaning the house top to bottom, preparing a huge meal, including a whole turkey, can be stressful to say the least. Eschewing the turkey might take a major burden off your plate, but if you don't want to go fully historical with your Thanksgiving feast (the Plymouth settlers likely ate different game birds and venison) you might instead opt for oven-free, skillet-cooked turkey meatballs in a savory gravy.

Meatballs are an excellent option because you can stuff the flavors of Thanksgiving turkey and stuffing into them, minus the hours of cook time. In fact, if you consider a 20 pound turkey takes four to five hours to roast, and making turkey meatballs takes an hour, tops (depending on how long it takes you to roll them all out), you're saving yourself a lot of time. It also means your oven is completely free for all the delicious sides, like hopefully mistake-free green bean casserole and hot, pillowy rolls. Further, this is a great meal to pull out any time of the year when you want a Thanksgiving turkey-like dinner, without the fuss.

If you're looking for inspiration on what ingredients to use in your turkey meatballs, we have some suggestions (and it's worth noting that none of these ideas add any significant time to the meatball preparation). It's not the best idea to use straight ground turkey because it tends to be quite lean; as you can see in the video above, the creator also includes Italian sausage, which has much-needed fat to help keep the balls moist and tender. If you don't want to use sausage, though, you could take Ina Garten's advice and use prosciutto, or see if you can find ground turkey thighs, as opposed to breast meat, because it has a higher fat content.

Of course to get that real Thanksgiving dinner taste, you'll also want to use plenty of herbs. rune bosse puts living trees on life support in a vision of ecological collapse

📅 Nov 14, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

equipped with respirators and artificial lighting, rune bosse's 'tree lungs' imagines a future where nature itself may need life support.

Danish artist Rune Bosse places living trees on artificial life support in Lung Trees, an installation presented at the Danish Architecture Center in Copenhagen as part of its Age of Nature exhibition, which ran through September 2026. Each tree stands inside a transparent, inflated plastic enclosure, and is surrounded by mechanical equipment and artificial lighting. With their branches reaching toward overhead lamps, each tree seems to have been removed from the forest and placed under intensive care.

The installation imagines a future in which trees might depend on technology to survive, even as humans continue to depend on the ecological systems that trees help sustain. It reverses a relationship we tend to take for granted. Forests have long been described as the lungs of the planet, absorbing carbon dioxide through photosynthesis and releasing oxygen. Here, Bosse gives those lungs a mechanical counterpart, and suggests a world in which the natural systems supporting human life might themselves require artificial support.

The installation brings together living vegetation and an exposed network of machines. Transparent plastic sheeting stretches over metal frames, enclosing the trees while allowing visitors to see the soil and branches inside. Artificial lights illuminate the foliage, while equipment supplies air to the enclosed environment. The temporary structures have the practical appearance of makeshift greenhouses, although their association with medical equipment gives them a more unsettling character.

In a related 2025 presentation at Berlin’s Neue Nationalgalerie, titled Lungetræ / Lungenbaum, Bosse made the breathing movement particularly visible. A living tree and a section of forest floor were enclosed within a flexible PVC membrane attached to a metal framework. At regular intervals, a respirator pushed air into the structure, causing the plastic to expand and slowly collapse around the tree. The movement resembles the inflation of a giant lung, giving physical form to the invisible exchange of gases between a tree and its surroundings.

Bosse’s interest in trees extends well beyond the gallery. Alongside his training at the Royal Danish Academy of Fine Arts, the artist studied forestry and nature management, developing a practice that follows the growth of living plants over time. At Oremandsgaard, an estate in southern Denmark, he has established an outdoor forest laboratory where he experiments with shaping trees as they grow. Some are bent into unusual forms, while others are guided to grow together. Their final shapes emerge over years, influenced by the conditions of the surrounding landscape.

This work grew partly from his frustration with bringing natural materials into museums, where plants often have to be cut, dried or preserved before they can be exhibited. His outdoor experiments allow trees to remain alive and continue changing long after the artist’s initial intervention. Lung Trees approaches the same relationship from a different direction. Inside the gallery, keeping the trees alive becomes part of the artwork itself, exposing the equipment and artificial conditions that make their temporary existence possible.

The installation touches on an uncomfortable contradiction in the way humans approach ecological preservation. By enclosing the trees and supplying them with artificial conditions, Bosse creates an image of protection that also suggests dependency. The machinery appears to care for the living organisms, yet the entire arrangement places their survival within a system designed and operated by humans. The trees remain living participants in the work, although their relationship with the surrounding environment has been fundamentally altered.

Bosse first developed Lungetræer in 2019, and its more recent presentations give the work a continuing relevance amid growing concerns about biodiversity loss and the deterioration of ecosystems. The installation offers a speculative glimpse of what technological preservation might look like when ecological conditions can no longer support life on their own. Its most unsettling quality comes from the simplicity of the scene.

A living tree stands inside a plastic chamber while a machine pushes air around it. The visitor watches the artificial lung expand and contract, confronted with the possibility that the relationship between humans and forests could one day become this fragile.

Smart glasses could replace your monitor but they shouldn't — here's why

📅 Nov 14, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Some people are already swapping their monitors for smart glasses, and the results are mixed.

Imagine taking your computer monitor and setting it out by the curb. Instead of sitting at your desk surrounded by screens, maybe all you'll need one day is a pair of smart glasses. As smart glasses ride a fresh wave of interest, some users are already experimenting to see whether the tech could be up for the challenge of replacing their monitors.

There could be some perks to using smart glasses as a replacement. For one, they're more private — no one will be peering over your shoulder. Smart glasses could also declutter your workspace and offer you more flexibility. Instead of hunching over your laptop, trying to get work done on a flight, you can use your smart glasses to look at multiple virtual monitors adjusted to your preferences.

Even so, you might not want to clear off your desk right away. From comfort issues like eye strain to display limitations, here's why smart glasses might not be ready to take the place of your computer monitor just yet.

In the life of a desk worker, there are plenty of considerations around comfort. How you sit, how you hold your head and how often you take breaks from the screen can all contribute to issues like back pain or headaches. Those who have replaced their monitors with smart glasses have encountered some sticking points as well.

An article from Lifehacker's Stephen Johnson noted that his Xreal One Pro glasses heated up when he used them for long periods, and he could feel the heat on his forehead. Different models of glasses may deal with the dissipation of heat in different ways.

On the flip side, YouTuber Jasmine Uniza said in a video that her smart glasses were comfortable, though she mostly wears them for about an hour and a half to two hours at a time. She also felt that her posture improved because she was adjusting the virtual screen to her body rather than adjusting her body to the screen sitting on her desk.

Some have also noted that eye strain could be an issue. Johnson joked that he was ignoring the old warning not to sit too close to the TV, with a screen about half an inch from his eyes. Eye strain occurs when your eyes get tired from exposure to bright lights or screens, or from focusing intently on something for a while. While it usually isn't serious and usually goes away with rest, it can be annoying.

Various testers also noted that your mileage will vary with display and resolution. Cheaper glasses might not offer a sharp enough resolution, making it harder to read text or do more precise work.

Aside from physical discomfort, some users have commented on the awkwardness of sitting on video calls wearing dark lenses or the potentially isolating effect of having glasses on at work. Others have said it was hard to switch activities because they had to take off the glasses or look past them. Writing in a physical notebook, for example, became more complicated.

Several accounts from people testing whether they can replace their monitors suggested that the glasses might be better suited to entertainment, like gaming and watching movies, than office work. Watching a movie by yourself on your couch, for example, will likely bypass the challenges of switching tasks, wearing the glasses for eight hours a day or hopping on a Zoom call with a bunch of coworkers who are wondering why you're wearing sunglasses indoors.

Smart glasses have come a long way, becoming lighter and more streamlined over time. For now they suit movie night better than the workday, but they could eventually make monitors obsolete.
