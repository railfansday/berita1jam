ChatGPT ads match to queries OpenAI's own system writes. Advertisers can't see them, and OpenAI says prompt-level reports aren't planned. Here's what it means.

OpenAI doesn’t plan to provide ChatGPT advertisers with reports showing the prompts or inferred intent behind their ads. When Adweek asked whether advertisers could expect such reports, Vijaye Raji, OpenAI’s CTO of applications, said they should not expect them.

Three executives who run OpenAI’s ad system told Adweek that ChatGPT ads are matched to search queries written by the company’s own AI agent. Advertisers don’t see those queries, only aggregate results.

OpenAI’s basics page for advertisers explains that Ads Manager Beta reporting currently includes impressions, clicks, spend, CTR, average CPC, average CPM and conversions.

OpenAI’s “About this ad” panel tells users why they saw an ad and what performance data advertisers receive.

OpenAI’s ad system begins by creating one or more search queries based upon buying intent determined from the conversation, per the company’s ads engineering post published Oct. 21 by Ritendra Datta and Karthik Gopalratnam. OpenAI calls the model that creates these search queries the orchestrator. Using these search queries, the ad system retrieves candidate ads, filters them, scores them and identifies the top candidates to be entered into an auction to set the price and select the winner. Returning no ad is called “an ordinary, successful outcome” in the post.

As described in the post, if a user asks about a two-day Yosemite trip and then about a shoe brand’s new product lines, the ad system may create a query representing that conversation such as “shoes appropriate for hiking trip,” even though the user did not connect the two questions.

According to the post, when ads personalization is turned on, the orchestrator will also consider “nonsensitive information about the user’s past conversations.”

ChatGPT users in the European Economic Area and Switzerland will not have access to personalized ads at first, according to OpenAI’s ads FAQ for users. Brooke Osmundson covered the engineering post in October.

Advertisers share context hints about where an ad might fit, and OpenAI says these hints help guide matching without determining it.

“Hints are not targeting rules or delivery instructions. They do not guarantee that your ad will appear for particular conversations, audiences, or situations, and they cannot enforce geographic limits, schedules, or exclusions.”

The basics page also says that hints “are not exact-match keywords.” Negative Phrases help qualifying advertisers limit where their ads appear, for “narrow placement requirements specific to their brand’s own policies.” The option is not necessarily available to every advertiser.

Google explains that for some AI searches, the search terms provided in Google Ads reporting reflect the user’s intent rather than the exact query. According to Google’s ad group prioritization page, for searches done through Lens, AI Mode, AI Overviews and autocomplete, “the search term shown in your reporting represents the best approximation of the user’s intent.” Brooke posted about Google’s update to that page in May.

OpenAI does not list a search terms report on its basics page.

ChatGPT advertisers can determine what a campaign delivered, but they cannot see the conversations or queries that led to the results. Click-based and conversion-based campaigns can use the conversion tracking OpenAI supports through Ads Manager Beta. The basics page also describes how UTM parameters added to landing page URLs persist on ad clicks.

OpenAI recommends writing your context hints in terms of customer needs and scenarios instead of listing keywords. Also, use OpenAI’s product controls where available to restrict delivery by geography.

OpenAI says in its ads FAQ for users that it “may explore additional measurement insights over time while continuing to protect user privacy.” OpenAI sends new feature announcements by email to account holders. Check the reporting list on the basics page for updates.

The story revolves around a group of wealthy Texas housewives who sport designer fashions and are all heavily armed. They’re at the center of everything in their small conservative town of Maple Brook, Texas, whether it's passing judgment from the pews at church, throwing back margaritas at a local honky tonk, or having ill-advised affairs.

These women are messy with a capital M, and that’s before they find themselves at the center of a murder investigation. Here's a quick recap of everything you need to remember about The Hunting Wives’ twisty first season before Season 2 premieres.

The series follows Tesla-driving, East Coast liberal transplant Sophie O’Neill (Snow), who moves to Maple Brook with her family for a fresh start after a devastating personal trauma. Her husband Graham (Evan Jonigkeit) lands a job with Jed Banks (Dermot Mulroney), a wealthy oil businessman who is being courted to run for governor. Sophie hits it off immediately with his wife, Margo (Akerman), bonding over prescription drugs and anxiety in a fundraiser bathroom.

Abby’s murder is the primary narrative drive of The Hunting Wives' first season, pushing Margo and Sophie closer together as they scramble to cover up their night of blackout-drunk escapades with a pair of high school boys. Along the way, the two ultimately give in to the attraction that’s been simmering between them since the series’ first episode and become intimate, not only sharing steamy hook-ups, but building a real emotional bond.

Unfortunately, all of that is shattered when Sophie realizes that Margo actually killed Abby. The teen found out that Margo not only had an affair with Brad, but had gotten pregnant and had an abortion. Abby's threats to expose Margo's secrets led to Margo shooting her with Sophie’s gun. Margo ultimately frames Brad’s mother, Jill, for the crime, leading to a standoff in the Thompson home that results in several other deaths, including Abby’s mother, Starr (Chrissy Metz), and Jill herself. How Trump’s latest tariff moves hand India an edge over rival Asian exporters

📅 2026-11-26  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The latest U.S. tariff rates announced Wednesday have signaled that Washington is willing to offer India an edge over its competitors if New Delhi agrees to finalize a long-pending trade deal.

The Office of the United States Trade Representative has proposed a tariff of 17% on Indian exports if New Delhi "were to reach an Agreement on Reciprocal Trade" with Washington. It added that without a trade deal, India could face tariffs of up to 25% under Section 301 investigations encompassing excess production capacity that harms U.S. interests.

Meanwhile, the USTR has proposed tariffs of 18% on Bangladesh, Cambodia, Thailand, and 20% on China and Vietnam, subject to their respective trade deals, handing India a competitive advantage over rival exporters.

The U.S. expects the announcement of a lower tariff rate will encourage New Delhi to sign off on a trade deal before the G20, Atman Trivedi, partner at Washington-based DGA Albright Stonebridge Group, told CNBC.

India has got a "better bargain with Washington than its main competitors," and it may be enough for the Indian government "to declare victory and secure domestic backing for the deal," Trivedi said.

In February, U.S. President Donald Trump had announced a deal with India, claiming that New Delhi had accepted to "Buy American" at a much higher level after the U.S. agreed to slash tariffs to 18% from 50% earlier.

The India-U.S. trade deal has been pending with both sides indicating there was little room to grant concessions, while the Supreme Court annulling Trump tariffs allowed New Delhi to demand preferential tariffs that give it an edge over competitors.

"Today's announcement increases the urgency of reaching an agreement," Ronak D. Desai, visiting fellow at Hoover Institution, Stanford University, told CNBC. He added that while the prospect of a 17% tariff may strengthen the economic case for the deal, the threat of a 25% tariff could stir the sensitivity of the Indian government.

"The Modi government will be particularly sensitive to any perception that India is conceding under American Pressure," Desai said.

India's commerce ministry on Wednesday said it "does not have structural excess capacity in any sector," denying the findings of the USTR investigation, but added that it remains committed to "working closely with the United States towards the early conclusion of the India-U.S. Bilateral Trade Agreement."

Despite India facing steep tariffs, which were raised to 50%, New Delhi's trade surplus with the U.S. rose to $57.3 billion in 2025 from $45.2 billion in 2024.

Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi and Trump are likely to meet in December for the G20 leaders' meeting in Florida, and several experts believe the two leaders could seal the trade deal on the sidelines of the summit.

Kim Jong Un tours nuclear material sites, urges wider use of tactical nuclear weapons

📅 Nov 26, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

North Korea's Kim Jong Un visited sites that produce nuclear material and told his military to focus on tactical weapons, saying his country's nuclear status is irreversible.

North Korean leader Kim Jong Un toured factories that make nuclear material, along with a research institute, and pressed for the wider use of tactical nuclear weapons, state media reported on Thursday.

The visit took place on Wednesday, but the state news agency KCNA did not provide the location of the sites. Photos showed Kim walking between rows of what appeared to be centrifuges for enriching uranium. Analysts also spotted small, standardized tactical warheads such as the Hwasan-31.

Kim said North Korea's production capacity for nuclear material is being steadily expanded. He said tactical weapons should be used in different areas and for different missions, citing the danger from a hostile state on his border, referring to neighbor South Korea.

He also accused the United States of encouraging its allies to seek nuclear-powered submarines and to share nuclear weapons.

"Our possession of nuclear weapons is an absolutely irreversible choice as long as malicious forces exist that use nuclear weapons as a means of achieving hegemony," Kim said, adding that he would treat any attempt to deny North Korea's nuclear status, which was written into its constitution in 2023, as a declaration of war.

Kim said Pyongyang would not seek approval from other countries.

Lim Eul-chul, a professor at South Korea's Kyungnam University, said the visit signals Kim's desire to change the circumstances of any future talks on North Korea's weapons programs.

"This is not so much North Korea permanently closing the door to dialogue with the United States as a strategy of raising the bar: 'denuclearisation talks' are over, and if talks open in future, it will deal only on a 'nuclear state versus nuclear state' basis," he told Reuters news agency.

South Korean news agency Yonhap said this was Kim's fourth visit to a nuclear facility since September 2024. It came about a week after US President Donald Trumpvisited China for the APEC summit, where Kim did not publicly respond to an offer to meet.

US announces new tariffs on host of countries including China

📅 Nov 26, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Watch: What we know about Trump's Thanksgiving tariffs

The US has announced a fresh raft of tariffs as President Donald Trump pushes ahead with his signature trade policy.

The new taxes charged on imports to America are set to hit many of the country's biggest trading partners - China, the European Union and 12 other jurisdictions, including Japan, South Korea and Taiwan.

The White House began investigating the trade practices of other countries in March after the US Supreme Court ruled Trump's previously announced Liberation Day tariffs were illegal.

China is facing the highest tariff rate of up to 40%, followed by Vietnam at up to 35%, with others facing either 20% or 25%.

The US Trade Representative (USTR) announced the proposed tariffs together with extensive reports of how each jurisdiction's trade practices purportedly impact American commerce.

The new duties are not expected to take effect until next year and have to go through rounds of public consultation and, potentially, hearings.

Tariffs are taxes levied on imported goods. The amount is typically calculated as a percentage of a good's value and paid to the government by businesses importing products.

Some of these rates will be effectively reduced because of caps on tariffs Trump has agreed to. Individual countries also face different levies.

The proposed tariffs will affect goods from China, the European Union, Singapore, Switzerland, Indonesia, Malaysia, Cambodia, Thailand, Korea, Vietnam, Taiwan, Bangladesh, Japan, and India.

The USTR's Jamieson Greer said the latest tariffs issued would "level the playing field for American workers and chart a new, more sustainable course for the world economy".

He said that many of America's trading partners had "for decades" adopted industrial strategies which included "targeting manufacturing, wage and labor suppression".

They also employ "other market distorting practices that tilt the trade balance in their favour while eroding America's manufacturing base", Greer said.

But economists have warned tariffs can fuel inflation and push prices up for consumers if businesses pass on higher costs, as well as dampen global trade.

Trump has repeatedly threatened or imposed tariffs during his second term, arguing they will boost US manufacturing and create jobs.

Despite a poor result for the Republican party in the midterm elections, the president has continued to press on with his flagship trade policy.

Trump is also seeking to cut the US trade deficit - the gap between the value of goods it buys from other countries and those it sells to them.

Trump's so-called Liberation Day tariffs were struck down by the Supreme Court earlier this year

These new levies are build on a "stronger legal foundation" under US law compared with Trump's April 2025 Liberation Day tariffs, Singapore Management University economics lecturer Chang Pao-Li said.

Trump previously invoked emergency presidential powers to impose tariffs that have since been struck down, while his latest approach relies on the USTR's authority to impose tariffs to address certain foreign trade practices, she said.

The levies are still likely to face legal challenges, but there has been a "historical precedent" for such tariffs remaining in force, Chang said.

Many tariffs imposed under Section 301 - a law that allows the US to probe and retaliate against foreign trade practices - during Trump's first term remained in effect during his successor Joe Biden's administration.

However, Stephen Brown, chief North America economist for Capital Economics, said the latest tariff announcement was "not as alarming as it looks", due to the taxes "essentially replacing" previously announced tariffs made in the summer.

"The increase in the effective tariff rate looks set to be a touch lower than we've been assuming for our forecasts, although the new tariffs will still provide a marginal boost to prices," he said in a note.

The additional tariff burden is likely to be small because a limited number of goods are subject to the new duties, Brown added.

Jurisdictions affected by the new wave of tariffs have voiced their disapproval over the Trump administration's move.

Washington has also proposed a 10% tariff on goods from Brazil, as part of a separate investigation over what it says are "harmful trade practices" undercutting American workers.

Brazilian authorities expressed their "outrage" at the announcement, saying that the move is aimed at pressuring the government to change regulations around issues like digital trade.

The South American nation faces duties on finished aircraft and sugar sold to the US.

"Brazil will not yield to such threats," President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva's office said.

The European Commission rejected the new US tariffs, saying it would investigate the reasons for bringing them in. It pointed out that tariffs are limited to the 15% previously agreed.

The latest tariff rate is "not consistent" with the reality of bilateral economic-trade cooperation between the US and Vietnam, Vietnamese Deputy Prime Minister Bui Thanh Son said.

The Japanese government said the US tariffs were "regrettable", while the Singapore government said it disagrees with the White House's findings and that it is engaging the USTR to explore ways to reduce the tariff.

South Korea's trade minister said the proposed 15% tariff will be "more than a decent outcome" as it would fall in line with a previously agreed limit with Washington.

Last year, Seoul pledged $350bn (Â£267bn) in US investments in exchange for lower tariffs as part of a trade deal.

US trading partners have been "waiting for this shoe to drop" and have argued that they are being wrongly accused of producing too much, said the Asia Society Policy Institute's Wendy Cutler, a former senior US trade negotiator.

"Many believe that China, not them, should be the target for US action," she added.

Chinese Foreign Ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun described the new tariffs as a form of "political manipulation", describing as "false" the US claim that China produces more than it can consume.

The tariff announcement is likely to increase tensions between the US and China, the world's two biggest economies, who remain locked in a trade dispute and the AI race.

The two countries have also been embroiled in a tit-for-tat tariffs war, which is currently on hold.

Despite last week's meeting between Trump and China's President Xi Jinping, the two sides made no announcement on the trade war truce, which is set to expire on 10 January.

Beijing has remained vague on how it will respond but for now seems "prepared to live with the new rates provided they don't exceed 20%", Cutler said.
