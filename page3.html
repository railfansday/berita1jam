Aaron Donald on having no sacks: "They come in bunches"

📅 2026-11-13  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Rams defensive tackle Aaron Donald has played seven games this season. He has no sacks.

“Sacks come in bunches,” Donald told reporters on Thursday. “It’s going to come. Just keep playing ball and keep finding ways to win. That’s the most important thing is winning. No pressure, I’m not worried about it. I’ve been playing football since I was five, six years old. They come in bunches. They’ll come eventually so just keep doing what I need to do to help this team. When they present themselves, hopefully hold the ball and take advantage of it.”

On Sunday, Donald finally played with Myles Garrett, the player whose arrival in June sparked Donald’s decision to return after two years of retirement. Garrett, in his first game since Week 1, had 3.0 sacks and a forced fumble.

Regardless, the Rams won, pushing themselves above .500 for the first time this year. On Sunday at Arizona, they have a chance to win their fourth in a row.

And Donald has another chance to get his first sack since he returned from retirement.

Latin Grammys 2026: Rosalía Wins Album of the Year

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Janet Jackson also presented Daddy Yankee with the person of the year honor.

Spanish singer Rosalía took home the top prize of album of the year at the 2026 Latin Grammys on Thursday night for her 2025 album Lux.

She opened the 27th annual Latin Grammy Awards, live from Sin City, with an emotive medley of her Lux tracks “La Rumba Del Perdón,” “La Perla” and “Dios Es Un Stalker,” joined by angelic dancers ornamented with halos atop a stage meant to look like heavenly clouds.

It was a reminder of the expansiveness of her album: an offbeat, full-hearted embrace of her classical training through huge orchestral movements, inspired by the lives of female saints. Related Stories TV How to Stream the 2026 Latin Grammys Online Movies 'Wet,' Starring Connor Storrie, Rebecca Ferguson and Odessa A'zion, Acquired by Lionsgate

Her “La Perla” also won song of the year, and during her speech, she thanked Mexico and invited her collaborators on the song, the American urban sierreño trio Yahritza Y Su Esencia, to join her on stage.

Daddy Yankee, the 2026 Latin Grammy Awards’ person of the year and first Latin urbano artist to receive the honor, ripped through his biggest hits an hour into Thursday’s award show, treating the stage like a stadium floor.

It was one for the history books: There were outfit changes, set changes and his native Puerto Rico always at the forefront as he ran through “Ella Me Levantó,” “Con Calma,” “BOTZ,” “Despacito,” “Lo Que Pasó, Pasó,” “Sonríele,” “Rompe,” “Pokito” and, of course, “Gasolina” — joined by Bizarrap, Bad Gyal, Feid, Jay Wheeler, French Montana, LL Cool J and Diljit Dosanjh.

When it came time for Daddy Yankee to receive his honor, it was delivered to him by Janet Jackson “with admiration and respect,” as she said in her speech. When he accepted the award, he concluded by saying “Puerto Rico, recuerda. … Puerto Rico no es pequeño. Pequeño el mapa.” In English: “Puerto Rico, remember. … Puerto Rico is not small. The map is small.”

The award show took place live from Las Vegas’ MGM Grand Garden Arena. It aired live on Univision, UniMás, Galavisión and ViX and hosted by actor, producer and musician Roselyn Sánchez.

For the first award of the night, Pablo Alboran and Backstreet Boys’ Howie D presented Juanes with best pop/rock album for Juanesteban.

Performances were at a high level: Karol G was joined by a full orchestra and 12 Mariachis for her música mexicana ballad “Ese Hombre es Malo.” Mon Laferte broke hearts with her soulful “Femme Fatale.” Silvana Estrada’s “Dime” bridged culture and heritage with her signature Venezuelan cuatro, a four-stringed instrument similar in structure to a ukulele.

Kany García stunned with her acoustic, aflamencado single “La Niña Que Fui.” The Uruguayan-born Jorge Drexler’s rendition of “Las Palabras” felt like theater. And Argentine rapper Milo J performed “Solifican12” and “Gil,” joined by the Latin American folk icon Gustavo Santaolalla.

Carín León brought the house down with “Desde Que Te Tengo,” as did Juanes and Vivir Quintana with “Humano.” Elena Rose was joined by sword-wielding dancers for “Alma.”

Brazilian superstar Anitta did a medley of her “Equilibrium” hits — “Desgraça,” “Choka Choka” and “Meia Noite” among them — an eclectic mix of Brazilian funk, samba, bossa nova, semba, reggae, electronic pop performed in Portuguese, Spanish and English.

Soon after, she was back on stage accepting the trophy for Portuguese language contemporary pop album. She used her speech to shine a light on the Black culture of her native Brazil.

There was a touching tribute to the late ranchera icon, Mexican singer-songwriter José Alfredo Jiménez, produced by Edgar Barrera: The Warning covered “Te Solté la Rienda,” Reik did “La Media Vuelta,” Sofía Reyes and Yami Safdie dueted “Si Nos Dejan” and Camila Fernández brought it home with a big belting “El Rey.” Not soon afterward, she headed back to the stage to perform a full-throated “Serpiente De Cascabel.”

It was the first of two tributes: The second was for Willie Colón, the social activist and Grammy-nominated architect of urban salsa music, who died in February of this year. His son, Miguel Colón, as well as Nathy Peluso and Luis Figueroa and others performed.

Awards were handed out quickly, too. Karol G took home the trophy for urban music album for Tropicoqueta. Xavi won contemporary Mexican album for Dosis, his first Latin Grammy. Fonseca was presented the trophy for contemporary tropical album shortly thereafter for his album, Antes Que El Tiempo Se Vaya.

Best new artist went to Colombian singer Aria Vega, who gave her acceptance speech through tears. This year, the Latin Recording Academy made changes to the category so that only artists with fewer than three albums or 25 singles released were eligible to win.

Most of the evening’s awards were handed out during a pre-televised Premiere Ceremony. That included Bobby Pulido winning the Latin Grammy for best Tejano album for his release Bobby Pulido & Friends: Una Tuya y Una Mía (Vol. 2/En Vivo). Just days earlier, Pulido made history in the U.S. midterm elections by securing a seat in the House of Representatives for the Democrats by flipping a Texas district seat.

“I want to tell you that I’m retiring from music, and I’m leaving with so much gratitude,” he said in his acceptance speech.

Commanders help Cowboys’ NFC East hopes with TNF win over Giants

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The NFC East just became a tiny bit easier for the Dallas Cowboys. They owe the Washington Commanders big time.

On Thursday night there was an NFC East tilt that went down to the wire. When it looked like the New York Giants (5-3-1) were going to take a serious step forward, a leap to be honest, in the NFC East race, Jayden Daniels hit Stefon Diggs to give Washington the late lead that held until the end.

In case you are unaware, a Giants win on Thursday night would have just about sealed the NFC East race. Nothing is ever official obviously, but it would have given them six wins to date and with the tie from their previous matchup against Washington that would have put them at 6.5 in an effective sense.

New York's remaining schedule suggests that they should come close to finishing around 9-10 wins.

Given that the Cowboys currently own a 4-5 record, and that they play the San Francisco 49ers on Sunday, envisioning them finishing with 10 or 11 wins to clear New York's potential total with the tie is difficult, and obviously the lower the bar the more ideal the situation.

Dallas still gets to play New York one more time so they can at least impact things in a direct sense that one time. They are far from out of the woods, but this outcome made a division title, and therefore playoff appearance, a little bit less silly to talk or dream about.

For what it's worth, the Commanders (3-5-1) are also now a factor. Their remaining schedule could see them find a way, too.

The dream lives another day. As you were.

Sienna Spiro won’t be making the trip to Australia next week, as planned, where she was expected to perform at the 2026 ARIA Awards.

US disrupts North Korea's $400 million crypto scam, FBI says

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The US has seized servers linked to a North Korean scam that used fake video meetings and deepfakes to steal more than $400 million in crypto, and has moved to seize $1.2 billion that was taken from Binance.

The United States Justice Department said Thursday it had seized cloud infrastructure used by North Korean hackers in a video-call scam that has stolen more than $400 million (€361 million) in cryptocurrency over 18 months.

It also said it had court approval to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion taken in the recent Binance hack.

The Federal Bureau of Investigation calls the scam "FaceStealer."

According to the Justice Department, hackers sent meeting invitations from accounts taken over from earlier victims. Once a target joins, the hackers record their webcam video and play a prerecorded clip of a previous victim the target knows, so trusted contacts appear to be present. The FBI says hackers are also starting to use AI-generated deepfakes of well-known crypto figures.

The hackers then stage a fake technical error and offer a link to "fix" it, which installs malware, the department said. In some cases, they waited months, or over a year, before stealing funds.

After that, they use the victim's contacts to find new targets.

Most targets work in crypto and other internet-related fields, and the hackers are increasingly going after open-source software developers, apparently to enable supply-chain attacks, the department said.

Under a federal warrant, the FBI seized more than 900 webcam videos of victims.

"If you can't imagine a scenario where you might fall prey to it, you are not thinking creatively enough," said Patrick Grandy, who heads the FBI's Los Angeles field office.

The FBI ties FaceStealer to a hacking cluster known as Sapphire Sleet or UNC1069. It is separate from the North Korean military hackers blamed for the Binance theft.

In the Binance case, the FBI said the group known as TraderTraitor stole more than $6 billion (5.4 billion euros) on or around October 29. AP put the figure at about $6.3 billion (5.7 billion euros) and called it the biggest crypto heist on record. US agencies and private firms have since frozen more than $2.7 billion (2.44 billion euros), the department said.

Authorities usually file a forfeiture claim in court, after which a judge decides who has a rightful claim to the recovered funds, AP reported.

"North Korea's government bankrolls its rogue regime and illicit weapons program through cryptocurrency theft,"  said M. Scott Proctor, the department's principal deputy assistant attorney general for national security.

North Korea has denied involvement in the Binance hack.

Thought-Watching for Athletes and Others

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

After years or decades, problems and patterns can become deeply entrenched. Be a fly on the wall during a sample couples therapy session and witness the slow, deliberate work of changing those patterns for a more enduring love.

Posted November 12, 2026 | Reviewed by Margaret Foley

Let’s begin with a challenging drill: What thoughts and feelings appear as you read the words “Let's begin with a challenging drill?” Do you have a sense of “Let’s do this!” or “Ugh, I’m not in the mood”? Are you curious? Excited? Nervous? Simply notice whatever thoughts and feelings show up without judging them as good or bad, right or wrong.

OK, now here’s the challenge. Get four large paper clips and your cell phone or a stopwatch. Separate the paper clips and set them on the table. Before you begin, take 10 slow, deep breaths. Then time yourself and, using only your dominant hand (the hand you write with), link all four paper clips together end to end in a chain. Most elite athletes can do this task in 30 seconds.

Before you begin, though, there is one additional part to the exercise: While you connect your paper clips, do your best to notice your thinking. When you are ready, start your timer and begin. Stop the timer when you have linked all four paper clips.

What did you say to yourself, either internally or out loud? Were your thoughts kind or unkind? Helpful or unhelpful?

Do you have thoughts like these when you are training and competing? Or when you are doing homework, taking a test, working on a project, or talking to someone you are interested in?

If some, or a lot, of your internal dialogue is unkind, unhelpful, and untrue, please know you are not alone. Most people have this negative internal chatter and self-­critical play-­by-play. I fondly call it Unkind Mind.

As author Grace Lichtenstein writes, Unkind Mind is our real opponent: “Your opponent, in the end, is never really the player on the other side of the net, or the swimmer in the next lane, or the team on the other side of the field, or even the bar you must high-­jump. Your opponent is yourself, your negative internal voices.”

Sports psychologist Keith Kaufman has conducted multiple research studies regarding the benefits of mindfulness in sports. His research supports a primary premise of my book A Still Quiet Place for Athletes: that with practice you can learn to watch Unkind Mind without believing it, taking it personally, or letting it prevent you from fully expressing your talent. "Athletes can learn to recognize their mind wandering toward thoughts about limitations during competition (e.g., 'I can’t sustain this pace') but then mindfully label these experiences as just thoughts, let them go and bring attention back to a present moment anchor like their breathing. Through this process, athletes can free themselves from judgments that might unnecessarily determine their limits."

And everyone—athletes, business people, performing artists, health care providers, young people, parents—can benefit from learning to observe their thoughts without believing them or taking them personally.

During the next week, practice simply noticing your thoughts without believing them or taking them personally. See if it helps you in your daily life—in training and competition, at work or school, on stage, or in your relationships.

Five thoughts from Cisco’s first quarter of fiscal 2027

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

GUEST COLUMN by Zeus Kerravala

Cisco Systems Inc. opened fiscal 2027 with a quarter that should put any question about whether artificial intelligence will act as a massive tailwind for it to rest. Revenue and earnings hit records, growth rates were the best Cisco has posted in more than 15 years, and management raised its full-year outlook and AI infrastructure revenue target.

While Cisco has delivered a string of solid quarters, some investors are concerned about the durability of the growth. After listening to the analyst call, I maintain that accelerated growth is what Cisco watchers should expect, as AI will act as a catalyst for several years. Here are my five thoughts, plus the one metric I think investors should care about most:

Asked why this isn’t simply the top of a hyperscaler spending cycle, Chief Executive Chuck Robbins (pictured) cited five growth drivers: hyperscale AI, the campus refresh, the “post-Mythos” security upgrade cycle, sovereign infrastructure and enterprise hybrid data centers. “All five of these are multiyear, multibillion,” he said. “And they’re all in relatively early stages.” In particular, the enterprise opportunity is in its infancy. Using the baseball analogy for “what inning” we’re in, the pitchers are still warming up, and enterprise is what Cisco does best.

Though hyperscalers have been driving AI growth across the industry, only one of the five trends Robbins highlighted was related to that group of companies. Chief Financial Officer Mark Patterson reinforced the point by breaking down the guidance raise, noting that a large share came from Cisco’s core, non-hyperscale business. “It’s not just campus,” he said. “It’s broad-based, and the core continues to do quite well.”

The market still views the enterprise as a slow-refresh business. The call made a strong case that it’s about to become the bigger part of Cisco’s AI story.

During the investor Q&A, a data point was worth highlighting. Robbins said that of the campus switching Cisco sold this quarter, “only 9% was really coming from the new portfolio.” Despite multiple quarters of strong campus growth, the installed base has barely moved to the next generation.

Two forces will push this base to accelerate an upgrade. One is agentic AI, which Robbins said is driving “modernization for bandwidth reasons” because agents generate traffic that campus networks were never designed to handle. The other is the security fallout from frontier models such as Anthropic’s Claude Mythos — the Mythos moment. “We’re beginning to hear from customers who are attributing orders to early Mythos implications,” Robbins said, noting that European service providers are upgrading managed customer premises equipment.

Robbins added that customers “got to get the architecture exactly right.” That suggests a long, deliberate redesign rather than a buying spree, which is characteristic of a multiyear cycle.

Cisco has long been criticized for building its own silicon as the industry shifted to merchant silicon. Robbins said that outside of optics, the hyperscale AI business is “100% Silicon One-based platforms.” The P200 continues to win scale-across deals, and the G300 is now in customer labs.

Asked why Cisco keeps winning those deals, Robbins kept it simple: “It’s really about performance and power. And there’s just this whole focus on diversity of silicon in the space.” The second point is the more strategic one. No hyperscaler wants a single supplier for any critical layer of its AI fabric, and Cisco is the credible second source.

The bigger long-term play is the enterprise. Silicon One is already shipping across parts of the enterprise portfolio, and Robbins said the target to convert all of it by fiscal 2029 “is still on track.” That gives Cisco a single silicon architecture from the hyperscale data center to the branch, with cost and roadmap control competitors can’t match.

The reality is that at Cisco’s volumes, it makes no sense to buy merchant silicon. Not only can it build it cheaper, but it can also optimize it specifically for its own features and capabilities.

A theme running through the call is that AI is decentralizing. “Our enterprise customers will be running AI applications in the cloud, but they’re also investing heavily in private data centers,” Robbins said. “They want to run open-weight and open-source models in their own data centers. They want to train on their own data in their own data centers.”

Sovereignty is driving in the same direction, with Cisco extending its Sovereign Critical Infrastructure portfolio from Europe to Canada and preannouncing India. Robbins noted that Cisco is “one of the few companies that has maintained a robust on-premises portfolio.” For years, analysts treated that on-premises focus as a liability. In a world where countries and regulated industries want operational control, it has become an advantage.

Equally important is what Cisco isn’t chasing. Robbins called neocloud exposure “relatively immaterial,” adding that “we’ve been very conservative relative to the credit situations.” That’s a disciplined choice. Cisco is growing into the safest parts of the AI build-out rather than financing its riskiest buyers.

Given the number of neoclouds, one could look at them as a massive pot of gold, but they often required vendor financing. If the neocloud isn’t successful, it’s the financing organization left holding the bag. This was the case with CLECs in the Internet days. Cisco was conservative then and will continue to be.

Security has been the weak spot in Cisco’s story, so this quarter’s progress matters. Robbins said core security orders, excluding Splunk, were “the best it’s been in three years,” with firewalls, Zero Trust secure access, and AI Defense all gaining customers. Patterson said Cisco is “a little bit above the expectations that we set out” and feels “good about turning the corner.”

The refresh cycle drives near-term numbers, but the strategic prize is trust for AI agents. Robbins said enterprise conversations now center on “agentic security, agentic identity, agentic observability, as well as the new security architecture needed for the AI era.” The network is where agent traffic flows, making it the natural enforcement point. Cisco owns more of that network than anyone.

Investor concerns received clear answers. On margins, Patterson said, “gross margins are pretty much right on target,” and on supply, “we’re seeing not a lot of issues at all.” Robbins added that Cisco is growing revenue “with less headcount than we had at the beginning” two years ago.

The number I’d watch most closely isn’t AI revenue, campus orders, or gross margin. It’s the growth of Cisco Cloud Control. Robbins mentioned it almost in passing, noting thousands of sign-ups and integrations with AI Canvas, Cisco IQ and Cisco Model Foundry. Most people will think of Cloud Control as a network management tool, but that badly understates its potential.

Cloud Control is now Cisco’s core product. The best analogy is VMware vCenter, the data center’s control point that turned a collection of products into a sticky platform. Cheaper, sometimes free hypervisors never made a dent in VMware’s share because the operational control gained from vCenter far outweighed the few bucks saved on hypervisor licenses. Cloud Control is broader and potentially more impactful because Cisco is positioning it as the control point for enterprise AI, not just the network through the following capabilities.

Cisco’s numerous network and security competitors have their own agentic operations platforms, and many beat Cisco to market. However, Cisco played the long game. In my conversations with Chief Product Officer Jeetu Patel, he has been emphatic about how important Cloud Control is to Cisco’s vision of shifting to a platform company. It enables customers to manage the underlying infrastructure, including third parties, through a single dashboard.

If Cloud Control becomes the enterprise IT console for managing AI-era infrastructure, Cisco stops competing on boxes and starts competing on control. Hardware cycles always ebb and flow, so always being best of breed is impossible. Whether this one becomes a transformation for Cisco depends on whether customers run their environments on Cloud Control. My advice to Cisco is to start disclosing active usage, partner integrations, and attach rates for the platform.

My advice to investors is to start asking for them. That number will tell you what Cisco is worth five years from now.
