Buck & Eddie’s 9-1-1 Fakeout Reconciliation Spells A Bigger Problem In Season 10

When Buck took over caring for his biological son, Theo, in 9-1-1 season 10, it seemed like a chance for significant growth — and it was, but in an unexpected way. Unfortunately, caring for children is complicated, especially when they’ve been orphaned and traumatized. Buck realized he couldn’t care for Theo and gave him up, leading Eddie to foster the kid without telling Buck. To say this caused friction this season would be an understatement.

Buck and Eddie have been at odds for most of the season's first six episodes because they have different parenting styles (and, of course, Buck felt betrayed that Eddie didn’t tell him he was taking Theo in). Watching them dance around each other has been uncomfortable and disappointing for Buddie fans, so it was a relief when they finally reconciled in the 9-1-1 mid-season finale. However, they only kind of reconciled. They might have more issues to work through when 9-1-1 season 10 returns in January.

9-1-1 season 10, episode 6, “Et Tu?” crammed a lot in, including a rather poignant plot for Buck involving a woman he meets at his addiction support meeting. The two click, and Buck temporarily falls back into old bad habits. He can't handle his feelings, and they sleep together. However, between the introduction of the woman’s child and her nearly dying from an overdose, Buck ends up facing his feelings about the Theo situation. He admits he doesn’t believe he was the best thing for Theo at the moment, but he loves and misses him.

That finally forces Buck and Eddie to talk, and they apologize to each other. However, it wasn’t quite a proper reconciliation. They didn’t immediately jump back into their old dynamic (as is their norm), and a lingering awkwardness remained, as Buck turned down an invitation to say hello to Theo because he wasn’t ready. While it’s great that 9-1-1 has finally stopped the two from actively fighting, nothing is truly resolved.

Eddie wasn’t wrong to foster Theo, because it was far less traumatizing than putting the kid into the system, but he was wrong not to tell Buck about it. Buck wasn’t wrong in how he felt, but the way he dealt with those feelings was far from healthy. It’s still unclear, though, how much Buck and Eddie really understand where the other is coming from (which is honestly a bit odd considering their years of friendship). Essentially, the two men have a lot of emotional ground to cover for true reconciliation, but that might not happen any time soon.

Buck and Eddie might not be actively fighting or fully mad at each other now, but they also aren’t back to their old selves. Combined with Buck's reluctance to see Theo, it looks like the duo will spend far less time together outside of work once the show returns. Only seeing each other at work, without truly figuring out their issues, will leave them in a limbo where they're professional but still distant.

This sets them up to push each other away. If they get caught up in life outside of work and that life doesn’t involve each other much, it will be easy for them to ignore the elephant in the room. That dynamic could cause them to drift apart. It’s reasonable to believe that 9-1-1 will swoop in and do what it always does by having them slide back into the way they’ve always been, but that would be a disservice to Buck and Eddie’s relationship.

ABC has confirmed its 2027 midseason premiere dates, confirming when viewers can expect new episodes of Will Trent, Shifting Gears, and many more.

Once Buck figures out his feelings and what role he could play in Theo’s life, he and Eddie need to have an actual conversation about their issues. These guys are in their thirties now; 9-1-1 should allow them to act like the actual adults they are. Melodrama is fine, especially when it’s long been the core of the show, but this season’s Eddie and Buck drama could have been solved eons ago with a few beers and honest communication.

How much storage does Apple Intelligence use on your iPhone?

📅 Nov 22, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Apple Intelligence's conveniences come at a large storage cost.

Apple Intelligence received some sizable updates and improvements with iOS 27, and all those new features take up an awfully large amount of space on your phone. For those not interested in using Apple Intelligence, or unwilling to give up copious amounts of storage, there's a way to reclaim what the update ate up. This goes beyond disabling your iPhone's Apple Intelligence features, but only takes a few minutes to pull off.

The exact amount of space Apple Intelligence takes up on iOS 27 is not universal. According to Apple, it requires up to 14GB on the iPhone Duo, iPhone Air and iPhone 17 Pro or later, and 8GB on all other compatible iPhone models. However, some folks have reported the features taking up between 9GB and 16GB. For those with only 128GB of storage, that's over 12 percent of your space taken up by something you might not even want to use.

In iOS 27, disabling Apple Intelligence isn't as simple as flipping a single toggle like you could before. Even if you shut off the various features, iOS keeps them around in case you change your mind. However, Apple Intelligence only works when your device language and Siri language are set to the same option.

To get your space back, you'll thus have to tweak Siri's language while leaving your iPhone's language the same:

Go to Settings, then Siri, and select Language.

Pick another variation of English, like English (Canada).

Restart your phone, and you should be able to see the iPhone storage you've saved.

Be aware that since this is a workaround, it might break from future updates. If you're interested in some AI features but not others, you can selectively toggle Apple Intelligence tools in iOS 27. However, this isn't likely to save any noticeable space unless your phone runs critically low on storage. In that case, people have reported iOS deleting unused Apple Intelligence files to make room.

One of the biggest losses from turning off Apple Intelligence is the new-and-improved Siri, which now boasts contextual understanding, the ability to take actions in other apps and more conversational back-and-forth. It can find information like hotel reservations and flight numbers, check updated info online, tell you about what's on your screen and even draft writing for you. Your camera app also gains a Siri mode, which provides information about anything you point the lens at or take a picture of.

Several other Apple apps have received their own AI-driven upgrades, too. The Photos app benefits from improved image cleanup, Safari will organize your tabs by topic, Shortcuts can be created with natural language and much more. You'll also get automatic proofreading across apps, and while on calls, your phone will offer information relevant to the conversation (like flight details or reservation confirmation numbers).

All these new features can be quite useful, but the amount of space Apple Intelligence takes up is a major consideration. It really comes down to whether you value space or convenience more.

Prediction: These Will Be AMD’s and Nvidia’s Stock Prices by 2028

📅 Nov 22, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Shares of both semiconductor companies can soar impressively, but one of them seems primed for bigger gains.

By Harsh Chauhan – Nov 22, 2026 at 10:44AM EST

Advanced Micro Devices (AMD -13.25%) and Nvidia (NVDA -14.01%) have experienced contrasting fortunes on the stock market in 2026, with one clearly outperforming the other.

Nvidia, the artificial intelligence (AI) pioneer, has had a forgettable year. Shares of the semiconductor bellwether have dropped 20% in 2026, as of this writing. The company's outstanding growth has been overshadowed by recent developments that led to the resignation of co-founder and ex-CEO Jensen Huang.

On the other hand, AMD stock has surged 140% this year. The company's growing prominence in the AI chip market has boosted investor confidence in the stock, and it seems well-positioned to sustain healthy growth over the next couple of years.

We will examine AMD and Nvidia's prospects and valuations in this article to determine where their stock prices could be in two years.

AMD has been playing second fiddle to Nvidia in the AI data center graphics processing unit (GPU) market, but the shift in AI workloads from training to inference and agentic AI has created a huge tailwind for the company.

AMD sells both data center GPUs and server central processing units (CPUs), and the increase in demand for the latter has supercharged the company's growth. The chip designer's revenue increased by 45% year over year in the third quarter to $13.4 billion, and it is now forecasting a much stronger increase of 66% in its top line in the current quarter.

Server CPUs are playing a key role in this acceleration. AMD noted that its server CPU revenue more than doubled in Q3 compared to the year-ago period. The company was originally expecting an 80% increase in this segment. Looking ahead, AMD expects server revenue to increase by more than 100% in 2027, driven by the launch of its next-generation Venice processors.

AMD points out that its partners have committed to launch over 240 platforms based on the Venice CPU, which is 20% higher than the previous-generation Turin server CPU. Even better, AMD forecasts the server CPU total addressable market (TAM) to jump to $220 billion in 2030, a massive improvement over just $26 billion last year.

Additionally, AMD has been winning share in server CPUs. Mercury Research notes that AMD's server CPU share increased by 7.7 percentage points year over year in the third quarter to 35.5%. So, the company still has a lot of room for growth in server CPUs, which is one of the biggest reasons why its earnings per share (EPS) are predicted to more than triple between 2026 and 2028.

AMD EPS Estimates for Current Fiscal Year data by YCharts

AMD trades at 31 times forward earnings, which is a premium to the tech-focused Nasdaq-100 index's forward earnings multiple of 21. However, the company's incredible EPS growth potential justifies that premium. Assuming AMD maintains its forward earnings multiple of 31 after two years and its EPS reaches $25.60 in 2028, the company's stock price could increase to $794.

That's a potential jump of 45%. However, don't be surprised to see this semiconductor stock commanding a bigger premium on account of its phenomenal earnings growth, which could pave the way for bigger gains by 2028.

This has been a forgettable year for Nvidia, and recent developments at the company have made 2026 even worse for investors. A cloud of uncertainty hangs over Nvidia as the company searches for its next CEO.

However, the company's growth engine continues to chug along nicely. This is evidenced by Nvidia's fiscal 2027 third-quarter results (for the quarter ended Oct. 25), which were released on Nov. 17. Nvidia's revenue jumped by 103% year over year to $115.8 billion, while non-GAAP EPS increased by 111% to $2.68.

Wall Street was looking for EPS of $2.48 on revenue of $109.1 billion. However, the strong demand for Nvidia's AI chips, especially its latest Vera Rubin systems, helped it coast past Wall Street's expectations. Nvidia's guidance was the icing on the cake. It expects $130 billion in revenue in the current quarter, while analysts were looking for $125.1 billion.

The biggest surprise from Nvidia's quarterly report was its fiscal 2028 guidance. The company expects a 75% increase in revenue for the next fiscal year, up from its prior estimate of 70%. Not surprisingly, Nvidia's revenue estimates for the next couple of fiscal years have moved higher.

NVDA Revenue Estimates for Current Fiscal Year data by YCharts

Importantly, the robust increase in its top line will boost its bottom line as well.

NVDA EPS Estimates for Current Fiscal Year data by YCharts

We saw earlier in the article that the Nasdaq-100 index has a forward earnings multiple of 21. Assuming Nvidia trades at that multiple at the end of fiscal 2029 (which will end in January 2029) and its EPS reaches $21.75, its stock price will hit $457. That's triple Nvidia's current stock price, indicating that it could become a multibagger in just over two years.

So, Nvidia can outperform AMD despite trading at a cheaper earnings multiple, which is why buying this beaten-down AI stock could make investors significantly richer by 2028.

By Jennifer K. Robbennolt, Alice Curtis Campbell Professor of Law and Professor of Psychology, University of Illinois College of Law

Thousands of lawsuits against social media companies brought by individuals, school districts, and other government entities are making their way through the courts. The cases generally allege that the companies caused harm to youth by designing their social media platforms to foster compulsive use and failing to adequately warn users about the potential harms of their platforms.

The first of these cases brought by an individual plaintiff was decided by a jury in March 2026—a bellwether case that will inform decision-making in future cases (K. G. M. v. Meta Platforms, Inc.). The plaintiff, identified by her initials, K.G.M., claimed that Meta and Google had used the “engineering of addiction” to design their apps in ways that encouraged users, particularly young users, to remain on the platforms for extended periods. K.G.M. claimed that she began using social media compulsively as a child and that this problematic social media use contributed to depression, anxiety, self-harm, and damage to her relationships. A jury agreed and awarded her $6 million in compensatory and punitive damages. Social media companies ByteDance (TikTok) and Snap (Snapchat) are also defendants in the broader set of lawsuits, but each settled with K.G.M. before her case went to trial.

Historically, efforts to hold social media companies liable for harms associated with their platforms have faced a significant legal obstacle: Section 230 of the 1996 Communications Decency Act shields tech companies from liability for harms caused by user-generated content. In this case, however, the legal claims were based not on the content posted on the platforms but rather on the platform design. The lawsuit focused on design features such as infinite scrolling, video autoplay, variable reinforcement, constant push notifications, and beauty filters. K.G.M.’s lawyer likened these features to a “digital casino” designed to keep users on the social media platforms, arguing that adolescents are particularly vulnerable to their effects.

One challenging aspect of this litigation is establishing a causal relationship between the alleged negligence—the platforms’ design choices—and the mental health harms claimed by plaintiffs. To be successful, a plaintiff must show both that a platform’s design feature can contribute to adverse mental health outcomes and that it did so in the plaintiff’s individual case. The social media companies argue that adolescent mental health is complex and question whether any particular mental health outcome can be causally linked to the design of a social media platform.

Reports from APA (Health Advisory on Social Media Use in Adolescence, 2023) and the National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine (Galea, S., et al. [Eds.], Social Media and Adolescent Health, 2024) note that this causation question is complex. Psychological studies have found that social media is associated with a variety of negative effects, including anxiety, depression, and attention problems. Yet social media is ubiquitous, and links between mental health and social media use are likely bidirectional, complicating causal conclusions. In addition, individual differences make some users more susceptible than others to the relevant design features and to developing patterns of problematic use. Research also shows that social media can have positive effects on mental health, including healthy socialization and self-expression.

Research that more precisely identifies the effects of specific design features on mental health outcomes and examines how individual differences might moderate those effects will be critical in informing the ongoing litigation and any other regulatory or voluntary reforms. Longitudinal, experimental, and quasi-experimental studies will be particularly important in determining which features give rise to problematic use, under what conditions, and for which young people.

Why Chris Pratt Was Recast In Taylor Sheridan's Sicario Meets The Terminal List Action Thriller

📅 Nov 22, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Despite being the original lead of Taylor Sheridan's gritty Sicario meets The Terminal List action thriller, here's why Chris Pratt dropped out. While he initially broke through with comic roles on the likes of Parks and Recreation, Chris Pratt's movie and TV show career has seen him taking a hard right into action in recent years.

From The Tomorrow War to Mercy, it's increasingly common to see Pratt playing soldiers or cops. Pratt's Amazon franchise The Terminal List just scored a season three renewal too, alongside a prequel show. In short, the star would be perfect for a Taylor Sheridan military actioner, and that almost came to pass in 2018 when he signed on for Sheridan's thriller F.A.S.T.

This follows a former soldier recruited by the DEA to take down drug dealers in his local town, and the film was set to be Sheridan's next directorial outing following Wind River. That didn't come to pass, though, and while F.A.S.T. will finally arrive nearly a decade later in 2027, it won't be Pratt scowling on the poster whilst holding an assault rifle.

Sheridan was an actor before switching to screenwriting, after realizing his career had peaked with a supporting role on Sons of Anarchy. In a burst of creativity, Sheridan penned his Neo-Western American Frontier trilogy, consisting of Sicario, Hell or High Water and Wind River. F.A.S.T. was another script Sheridan wrote during this era, and one that Warner Bros. greenlit with a budget in the $75 million range (via Dark Horizons).

Chris Pratt soon signed on, but it soon hit a roadblock due to the surprise success of Sheridan's Yellowstone. Sheridan left the director's chair on F.A.S.T. to focus on the latter's second season, while Gavin O'Connor (The Accountant) stepped in as helmer. Ultimately, that version of the film was shelved, as Warners were concerned about making an R-rated film at that budget level.

With the movie dead (at least for the time being), Pratt moved on to other projects and Sheridan became one of the biggest names on television. After a long period away from the big screen, Sheridan resurrected F.A.S.T. as a producer, with his regular collaborator Ben Richardson (who helmed many episodes of 1883 and 1923) as the new director. The ever-busy Pratt did not return, however, and is likely a little too old to portray the role as written.

For a time, it looked like Sheridan would become the next big director of dark, R-rated thrillers and Neo-Westerns, like Walter Hill. Directing F.A.S.T. in 2019 would have been a level up for Sheridan, being a big-budget actioner with a major star. Had he actually made the film, and it proved a success, it's possible his career would have gone in a different direction.

Instead, Sheridan's Yellowstone universe proved a success, and the ever-prolific writer/producer was soon knocking out more hit shows like Lioness and Tulsa King. While he eventually directed a mid-budget movie called Those Who Wish Me Dead in 2021, his TV schedule has kept him away from the types of cinematic thrillers he made his name with.

Of course, Sheridan had no way of knowing that his decision to step away from F.A.S.T. would have such a profound impact on his career. At the time, it was a way to keep control of a TV series that meant a lot to him, but as a consequence, he became an in-demand showrunner. Who knows, had he chosen F.A.S.T. over Yellowstone, maybe Sheridan would be the one directing the upcoming Call of Duty adaptation instead of simply co-writing it; instead, Peter Berg is taking on that responsibility.

For somebody who had never written a script until his 40s, Sheridan learned fast. Sheridan quickly became known for his quotable dialogue and memorable characters, and F.A.S.T. itself won acclaim when the script did the rounds during the late 2010s. There is a glowing review of the thriller on the screenwriting blog ScriptShadow, which praises how Sheridan takes a familiar premise but approaches it from fresh angles.

The review specifically cites a scene where the main character is talking with a therapist about his wartime trauma. While F.A.S.T.'s protagonist keeps claiming he's fine, throughout his session he is flashing back to horrific combat experiences. It also sounds like the story has a lot in common with Lioness, and deals with the murkier side of warfare as the main character leads a misfit band of soldiers into a private war.

One of the downsides of Sheridan's move to television is that the lean writing style that made his films so great has fallen away. Outside of 1883, some of his shows - especially later seasons of Yellowstone - felt padded simply to fill an episode count. Part of what makes F.A.S.T. so exciting is that it will be a one-and-done action thriller, penned during the same creative run that led to Sicario and Wind River.

It's unknown if Pratt was approached about returning to F.A.S.T., but it's for the best that he moved on. The movie isn't a million miles from The Terminal List, which is also about a former soldier going beyond the law. As written, F.A.S.T.'s lead is a younger character, and the role has now gone to Brandon Sklenar.

Sklenar earned his big breakthrough playing manly hunter Spencer Dutton in 1923, and he has since appeared in hits like The Housemaid with Sydney Sweeney. F.A.S.T. will be his first major test as a leading man, and Sheridan clearly has faith in his 1923 star to pull it off. It helps that the 2027 thriller surrounds Sklenar with a supporting cast that includes screen vets like Jason Clarke, LaKeith Stanfield and Trevante Rhodes (Moonlight).

With F.A.S.T. and Call of Duty, it seems Sheridan is working his way back towards theaters. He hasn't directed a film since 2021, but if F.A.S.T. proves a hit, he might finally step back behind the viewfinder. The movie doesn't have much in the way of competition in May 2027 - though time will tell how audiences greet Mel Gibson's The Resurrection of the Christ: Part One when it arrives earlier that month.
