Ronaldo's most iconic moments as he retires from international football

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Cristiano Ronaldo became the first player to win five Champions League trophies and remains the top scorer in the tournament's history

Cristiano Ronaldo says his international career with Portugal is over, meaning the 41-year-old will just play on with Al-Nassr.

The main thing driving him on now is hoping to reach an unprecedented 1,000 career goals - he is currently on 985.

BBC Sport looks at some of the most iconic moments of a career that has also taken in spells at Sporting, Manchester United, Real Madrid and Juventus.

Ronaldo spurred on by 1,000 goals and Portugal glory

Cristiano Ronaldo had one season in the Sporting first team before moving to Old Trafford

After 31 first-team games and five goals for Sporting, Ronaldo caught the eye of Manchester United in a pre-season friendly in 2003, setting up two goals in a 3-1 win in Lisbon.

Ronaldo revealed that United boss Alex Ferguson took him into the dressing room afterwards to meet the players.

Five days later he joined the Red Devils for Â£12m.

"After we played Sporting the lads in the dressing room talked about him constantly, and on the plane back from the game they urged me to sign him - that's how highly they rated him," said Ferguson.

"He is one of the most exciting young players I've ever seen."

Cristiano Ronaldo has won league titles in England, Spain, Italy and Saudi Arabia

Ronaldo won the FA Cup in his first season at United, scoring in the final against Millwall.

But it was in 2006-07 that his career really exploded. He hit 17 Premier League goals, his first season of double figures, was named player of the year and young player of the year - and won his first league title.

The following season was even better, winning his first Champions League - scoring in the final - his first European Golden Shoe for top scorer, and another league title.

Cristiano Ronaldo won the first Puskas award - and it remains the only Champions League goal to win it

Ronaldo scored 118 goals in 292 games in his first spell at United, and one was ranked above all the others.

He hit an absolute rocket of a shot into the top corner from 40 yards in his homeland against Porto, a goal which took them into the Champions League semi-finals.

It won the inaugural Fifa Puskas award for goal of the year.

Months later, Ronaldo joined Real Madrid for Â£80m.

Cristiano Ronaldo helped Real Madrid to win their first Copa del Rey in seven years

Ronaldo's first season at Real Madrid produced no trophies and his second saw just the Copa del Rey won.

That final was especially notable because it is the first time Ronaldo had scored the winning goal in a final. And it was in El Clasico, one of four meetings in 18 days in all competitions.

He rose at the far post to power in a trademark header from Angel di Maria's cross in extra time as Real beat Barcelona 1-0.

Cristiano Ronaldo scored 14 goals in 14 games against Levante

Ronaldo, then 30, scored in a 3-0 La Liga win over Levante in October 2015 to become the club's all-time top scorer with his 324th goal in 310 games.

The 2015-16 campaign would go on to be the sixth season in a row that Ronaldo scored 50 or more goals in all competitions.

Ronaldo ended up extending that record by 126 goals - leaving Real with 450 goals in just 438 games.

Cristiano Ronaldo is the top scorer in European Championship history - with 14 goals - as well as lifting the trophy once

The one thing missing from Ronaldo's CV was international success.

And that came in 2016 when Portugal won the European Championship by beating hosts France 1-0 in the final in Paris.

Ronaldo ended up taking on an unofficial coaching role in that final after going off injured in tears midway through the first half.

He was named in the team of the tournament after three goals and three assists. Ronaldo retires as the top scorer in Euros history, with 14 goals.

Cristiano Ronaldo has won five Ballons d'Or, only behind Lionel Messi

Cristiano Ronaldo won his fifth Ballon d'Or, equalling Lionel Messi's record at the time, after winning the Champions League and La Liga in 2016-17. He netted twice in that European final over Juventus.

It followed on from the best player in the world awards in 2008, 2013, 2014 and 2016.

Sadly for Ronaldo, Messi would go on to win three more Ballons d'Or, meaning he won their personal rivalry 8-5.

Cristiano Ronaldo scored one of his eight Champions League hat-tricks in the 2017 win over Bayern Munich

Ronaldo became the first player to score 100 Champions League goals with a hat-trick in the 2016-17 quarter-final second leg win over Bayern Munich.

Only Messi and Robert Lewandowski have joined him to date in the century club.

Ronaldo would go on to score 141 goals in 187 games (also a record) in Europe's top competition. For six seasons in a row he was the tournament's top scorer or joint top scorer.

In 2018 came one of the most iconic moments of Ronaldo's career, maybe the greatest Champions League goal ever scored.

In the quarter-final, Ronaldo leapt into the air before acrobatically firing into the corner of the net with an incredible bicycle kick against Juventus.

Even the Juve fans stood up to applaud the player who would join their club months later.

It was nominated for the Puskas Award but he finished second to Mohamed Salah's goal for Liverpool against Everton.

The night Juventus rose to applaud Ronaldo

Cristiano Ronaldo was the first man to win five Champions Leagues, although he was not the last as Real Madrid kept winning it without him

Ronaldo won two La Liga titles and two Copa del Rey trophies at Real Madrid, but it was in Europe's elite club competition where he became a true legend.

He had already won the Champions League in 2008 with United, and went on to win it four more times with Real.

The first was in 2014 before a remarkable three times in a row in 2016, 2017 and 2018 (beating Liverpool in the Gareth Bale/Loris Karius final) to become the first player to win the Champions League five times.

Since his departure, former Real team-mates Toni Kroos, Dani Carvajal, Luka Modric and Nacho have gone on to win six.

Cristiano Ronaldo had a penalty saved by Gavin Bazunu before netting twice to deny Ireland a famous win in Faro

Ali Daei scored 109 goals for Iran between 1993 and 2006 - and it was felt like maybe his world record for international goals in men's football would never be broken.

But Ronaldo's two late headers in a World Cup qualifying win over the Republic of Ireland in 2021 took him onto 111 goals.

He kept adding to that total and finished on 146 goals in 234 caps - also a record for international appearances.

Cristiano Ronaldo was the first player in professional football to ever hit 900 official goals

Ronaldo continued to smash the goals in for Juventus, in his second spell at United and at Al-Nassr but failed to really replicate many truly magic moments of the past.

However he did create some history in 2024 as he became the first player to score 900 top-level career goals as Portugal beat Croatia.

That number is now 985 - so he is only 15 off an unprecedented 1,000th goal.

He has now hit five for Sporting, 450 for Real Madrid, 145 for Manchester United, 101 for Juventus, 138 for Al-Nassr and 146 for Portugal.

He recently confirmed that he is aiming to hit the 1,000 mark.

But old rival Messi is on 931 goals, the second highest scorer ever, so Ronaldo cannot afford to dawdle too much...

Legend Of Zelda: Majora's Mask Remake Teased As Ocarina Of Time Launches

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Unless you have simply no interest in it at all, you were likely stirred by the return of The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time as a long-awaited remake at the start of November. It was already one of the most beloved games of all-time, so all Nintendo really had to do was update the visuals, add some quality-of-life features and keep everything else the same.

Happily, that's largely what they did, and it may well end up being one of the best games of the last few years. Critic consensus certainly seems to think so, with the game currently the highest rated of the last year on Metacritic, sitting on a fairly staggering rating of 96 at the time of writing.

It's certainly testament to the fact that older games in the franchise returning like this will always be appealing to fans, and it's a lot less demanding on a development team than creating an entirely new game. That leads us to the exciting question of where we go next as players, and if a recent teaser is to be believed, we could be moving on to the sequel.

Though it didn't quite manage to capture the same ridiculous level of devotion as Ocarina, Majora's Mask remains one of the most beloved games in the series to date. It adopted a darker and more abstract tone than the previous effort, and it had some fascinating mechanics that other games simply weren't doing at the time.

As first reported by GameRant, the Nintendo Today App has seen a newly animated version of Majora’s Mask’s key visual art published within it. Running at just seven seconds, it features all the game's characters in full motion, with Young Link taking center stage once again.

This new Majora's Mask animation is only visible to Nintendo Today users who have selected The Legend of Zelda as the app’s overall theme, which you can do yourself for free in the settings.

As much as it might be a simple coincidence, the signs are starting to stack up that Majora's Mask could be Nintendo's next target for the remake treatment. Ocarina of Time is already proving to be a huge success, and it would make sense if they had a logical next step to embark on.

As well as being the sequel, Majora's Mask has also been cropping us elsewhere, providing further clues that it could be the next game up to bat. The biggest of those is that the IMDB page of Adult Link VA Noboyuki Hiyama was updated with an unannounced game currently in post-production called The Legend of Zelda: Majora’s Mask HD. Neither Hiyama nor Nintendo have commented on the listing, though it remains up on the page to this day.

In any case, as someone who grew up playing the game, it really would be nice to see Majora's Mask return for the modern generation of hardware. Add to that the next game in the mainline Legend of Zelda series, and fans could be in for a bumper few years.

The traditional back squat has long been the staple of those who want to crush their quads, but standing under a heavy barbell is not for everyone. Mature gym goers, or anyone with mobility issues, may find that the downward force of carrying a heavy bar across the shoulders leads to stress on the spine. Fortunately, IFBB Pro and physique transformation coach, Tanya Johansen swears by the Spanish squat.

“Try a Spanish squat,” recommends the Bikini Pro competitor. “It fries your quads… when done correctly.”

The Spanish squat is a popular movement utilized for rehabbing the knees, but it also pounds the quadriceps, providing the perfect environment for muscle growth. To try it for yourself, stand in front of a cable station or power rack and anchor a resistance band around the support, looping around the back of your knees. You can stand inside a single loop, or loop the band around each knee, as demonstrated by Johansen.

Next, move back a little to make sure there is tension on the band. Now, you should be able to squat by sitting back with the band keeping you upright. Keep your shins vertical, and place emphasis on the quads as you lift and lower your body.  “Band behind your knees, sit back into it, stay tall, let your knees come forward,” summarized the coach, who specializes in physiques that are over 40.

Because there is no barbell compressing your body, the Spanish squat can be executed without overloading the back and knee joints. The quads can still be worked hard with no additional weight, and for those looking to improve their range of mobility, Spanish squats are awesome for building knee strength because they rehab the patellar and its related tendons.

“Over 40? Even better reason,” advises the coach of the Spanish squat. “You don’t need a heavy bar on your back to grow your legs. Forget heavy. Make the muscle do the work.”  And, for those who use the barbell as a crux for their ego, this IFBB Pro has some real talk. “You’re not in there to impress anyone,” she explained. “You’re in there to make sure your quads earn it.”

To make this move more challenging, you can bring in an isometric hold at the top of the squat, pausing before descending back down. You can also slow down the lowering phase for additional time under tension. Aim for 3 sets of 8 to 12 reps.

“It’s not the weight. It’s the work,” concluded Johansen, who’s own physique is proof positive that training smart can bring serious progress.

That isn't to say that The Last Witch Hunter is a phenomenal piece of cinema, mind you—but it is an action-based urban fantasy popcorn flick that absolutely delivers on the spectacle, and for that reason, it's worth a watch.

The Last Witch Hunter exists in the same vein as films like Van Helsing or Underworld. It's a fantasy that anchors itself in an "old world" but updates the material to the present day. It could also be compared to I, Frankenstein (which is maybe not the best selling point), but Diesel's action-packed low-budget fantasy is better on just about every front. The 2015 feature follows a lone protagonist, a widower named Kaulder, who has been cursed with immortality by the Witch Queen (Julie Engelbrecht), who he kills after she has killed his family. With time on his side, Kaulder chooses to use his new curse to spend his days hunting down the sorcerers who have made the world a living hell. To help in his quest, Kaulder has allied himself with an organization called the "Axe and Cross," which assigns him a new "Dolan" every lifetime, from Michael Caine at the beginning to newcomer Elijah Wood. Of course, that begins to change when Chloe (Rose Leslie) enters the picture.

For what it is, The Last Witch Hunter is actually a lot of fun. Not only is it a unique take on the witch-hunter concept, but the whole thing was grounded in Disel's own love for the fantasy genre. Long before Dungeons & Dragons: Honor Among Thieves made waves in 2023, Diesel met with screenwriter Cory Goodman (who had previously written The Searchers-inspired vampire thriller Priest), and the pair bonded over their shared love of D&D. The result was a movie inspired by Diesel’s own character Melkor the Witch Hunter, "a name that obviously I stole from The Silmarillion," Diesel added — and who hasn't stolen from Tolkien for D&D? With plenty of rich fantasy lore at its center, this fast-paced, visually stunning, and creature-heavy action flick is 106 minutes you won't mind spending with Vin Diesel.

The Last Witch Hunter may not have been well-received by critics or audiences, but the action is great and the special effects are nothing to write off, either. Even if Diesel's performance leaves a bit to be desired, it's hard to ignore the care and personal connection he put into bringing Kaulder's story to life. Perhaps what comes as the biggest surprise, however, is that The Last Witch Hunter is returning for more. Vin Diesel is working on a sequel, one that is set to bring even Michael Caine out of retirement to reprise his role as Dolan 36. Will it be able to live up to the wackiness of the original? Who knows, but considering many of us wish movies like Van Helsing had gotten their own sequels back in the day (or at least a television spin-off), those who enjoyed this supernatural thriller will certainly be happy.

So, if you're looking for some mindless entertainment to enjoy on an otherwise free evening, give The Last Witch Hunter a go. It's an oddball take on the genre, but one with a rich mythology and some impressive visuals that make it worth the watch. Although Halloween is over, it's still fall, meaning that now is just as good a time to return to this strange supernatural world than now — especially now that you can stream it for free.

Awesome repositories are no longer awesome

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

On GitHub, you will find a lot of repositories with the prefix 'awesome'. They usually contain curated knowledge and resources related to a certain domain, such as awesome-python (see, e.g., this repository for a list of awesome repositories). I used to see these awesome repositories as potential goldmines of good content. However, nowadays I see most of them as irrelevant and useless. There are two reasons for this, and they are both related to AI.

First, as many of these awesome lists get older, they contain a lot of outdated material if they are not actively maintained. You are in most cases better off simply asking a decent AI model for inspiration or the material you need. That is, I find it difficult to believe that most of these awesome lists are of interest to people anymore. If they remain useful, it will mostly be for agents looking to find and check relevant resources.

I had a quick look at the awesome-R repository. The repository was last updated more than a year ago, and most of the resources included in the overview are either outdated or will not be of general interest to people using R. One of the first links in the repository is an overview of the top 50 downloaded packages - as of 2016! This is simply not a good resource if you want to explore R in any shape or form.

Second, a lot of these awesome repositories are being flooded with AI slop. Go to any random awesome repository these days and you will most likely find a lot of pull requests suggesting the most generic tools and resources. For example, take a look at this awesome-free-online-tools repository. It is a small, unknown repository with 13 stars but 91 open pull requests at the time of writing. I did not look at all of these pull requests, but the ones I looked at all suggested generic vibe-coded tools.

I found this resource because of a pull request made to one of my repositories, a simple project where I store material I like related to statistics. The suggestion was to add StatOrigin ("a free, open database of industry statistics"), and once I had a quick look at the resource and the other pull requests made by the user, I could easily close the PR. This user alone has created at least six vibe-coded websites (StatOrigin, ScanChess, Sticker Printer Hub, QuizQuip, LiquidityPools, DefiLPKit) and submitted more than 100 pull requests to various awesome lists to add these websites.

It did not take me long to find examples where these tools were merged into the relevant repositories (see, e.g., here, here, and here). However, I also found examples where people did the basic work of checking out the material, such as here, where the PR was closed with the following explanation: "There is not yet sufficient evidence of qualified educator review, independent validation of the educational content, or demonstrated learning outcomes." Good stuff!

My point is not that these awesome repositories are now all bad. They can still be awesome. But as a general approach to finding good resources, awesome lists are less awesome than they used to be. As AI gets better at finding and creating resources (and creating pull requests for said resources), these awesome repositories will become less relevant and less useful.
