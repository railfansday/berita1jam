What Are Local Movie Theaters Showing on Election Night?

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

What does an audience need from its movie theater on election night?

A provocation? A distraction? Something… else? Across our semi-united, Letterboxd-loving nation, new microcinemas are opening up, old movies are selling out, and local programmers are giving film fans tons of exciting reasons to hit the box office.

Also breaking, I guess: the U.S. midterms. (Go vote!)

This election cycle, the future of Hollywood remains an open question, as does the strength of our democracy. With control of Congress at stake, voters are debating what they want their country to become — and, in many cases, what they’re afraid it already is — to spectacular and exhausting effect. Boarding yourself up in a movie palace like one of Joe Dante’s gremlins seems sort of reasonable under the circumstances. Thick walls, emergency snacks, and no checking your phone makes buying a movie ticket on November 3 an intriguing proposition in the U.S. So, what’s showing in 2026?

IndieWire spoke with 14 theater operators, programmers, filmmakers, educators, and authors across 12 states, including California, Illinois, Massachusetts, Minnesota, Missouri, Nevada, New York, North Carolina, Pennsylvania, Tennessee, Texas, and Wisconsin. Their answers offer a weirdly hopeful view of communities looking for comfort, perspective, and somewhere to be when the results come in.

At Las Vegas’ Beverly Theater at least, the connection is intentional: Reese Witherspoon and Matthew Broderick’s “Election” shares the day’s lineup with “The Devils” and “The History of Concrete.”

“We program The Beverly as a reflection of what’s happening outside our doors,” founding creative director and CXO Kip Kelly wrote. “Politics has become such an absurd spectacle that a dark comedy about a high school student council election might actually be one of the more sophisticated conversations you’ll have that day.”

Meanwhile, at Nashville’s Belcourt, a popcorn bag oversees the transfer of programming power. The theater’s monthly Staff Picks series typically falls on the first Tuesday, with a name drawn from a bag determining the employee who chooses the film. This time, someone picked “Under the Silver Lake”: the L.A. conspiracy thriller that’s paranoid enough to make anyone start connecting the deep-state dots.

“It’s totally a coincidence,” programming director Toby Leonard told IndieWire, laughing on the phone. “We realized what we had done after the fact.”

AFS Cinema programmer Lars Nilsen was similarly blunt about his Austin theater’s approach.

“We just programmed it as a regular week.” Nilsen resisted recruiting “The Good, the Bad and the Ugly” into any kind of election commentary, instead describing its Italian vision of the Old West as “the kind of dream America that Italians thought they understood about America itself.”

That said, tonight’s gathering still matters to Nilsen — even when the movie AFS is showing has nothing to do with the election itself. He pointed to the Texas organization’s addition of a third screen as a vote of confidence in people continuing to show up for one another through repertory culture.

“I think very intricately and carefully programmed cinemas are one of the places where community is being reborn,” Nilsen said. “Thank goodness.”

For Harlem’s Maysles Documentary Center, building community and considering who gets to tell that community’s story is the program’s point. Executive director Kazembe Balagun deliberately selected “BLKNWS: Terms & Conditions” for an evening that’s ordinarily devoted to watching the news.

He said he wanted to ask: “How do we imagine ourselves, represent our communities and think about power beyond the ballot box?” Plus, “I just think it’s a dope film.”

The “BLKNWS” screening is followed by a neighborly gathering echoing the center’s other events, from World Cup sidewalk screenings to group viewing for the Knicks playoff games. “I think that it’s not just what we put on the screen, but also the rooms that we create,” Balagun said. “The party afterwards is intentional because right now we live in a time where our communities are so fragmented.”

At Pennsylvania’s Gap Theatre, Spectral Cinema programmers Adrianna Gober and Dan Santelli are confronting power as an ambitious intellectual team. Their 35mm selection, Bresson’s “The Devil, Probably,” follows a young man overwhelmed by disillusionment with the institutions around him.

“The film speaks so much to the feelings of alienation and powerlessness that a lot of people are grappling with right now,” Gober wrote. She described the struggle to find meaning and control as “a tug of war between hope and despair.”

Santelli sharply cautioned against allowing that despair to become another reason to give up.

“In the current political climate, it’s all too easy to fall into a feedback loop of despair and resignation, whereby the recognition of one’s apparent powerlessness becomes a justification for further inaction,” he wrote. He’s “less interested in offering audiences an opportunity for catharsis than one for reflection.”

Binance breach puts custody at center of crypto infrastructure trade

📅 2026-11-03  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The $6.3 billion Binance hack could accelerate a shift in how investors value cryptocurrency companies, putting a greater premium on custody providers and the infrastructure needed to securely hold and protect digital assets.

Much of crypto's evolution this year has involved companies moving past their reliance on trading volumes and transaction fees, to focus instead on building the infrastructure connecting digital assets with traditional finance. Last Thursday's breach adds a new dimension, bringing cybersecurity, custody, compliance and balance-sheet resilience into sharper focus.

Importantly, the Binance breach was not an indictment of crypto itself, or a case of leadership-driven fraud, as with FTX in late 2022. Instead, Binance's leadership moved quickly to contain the fallout, protect customers and commit the exchange's own resources to cover losses.

On Tuesday, Binance CEO Richard Teng told CNBC's "Squawk on the Street" that the company has replenished 98% of $6.34 billion in affected assets, and that it is still in the process of purchasing outstanding, more illiquid assets.

Management's response, alongside efforts elsewhere in the industry to boost confidence, helped prevent a security breach from escalating into a broader crisis of confidence. For example, Tether, Circle, Ripple and others moved quickly to freeze or restrict access to about $3 billion in stolen assets; Coinbase blocked transfers to compromised wallets and offered Binance direct assistance.

The hack could even help shape the next phase of the crypto infrastructure trade. The new winners may not just be the businesses with the most users, volume or assets under custody, but the ones that can also demonstrate they're able to protect those assets under extreme duress.

"Which company has the resources to invest in cybersecurity and protect customer funds? For a smaller company, it could be much harder because once these crypto companies become infrastructure plays, providing the well for tokenization … they need to have the compliance and control in place so that a similar hacking will not happen or can be minimized," Owen Lau, senior analyst at ClearStreet, told CNBC. "Larger companies have more resources that could give them some advantage."

While institutional investors have increasingly embraced digital assets, broader adoption depends on confidence that those assets can be securely held, accessed and transferred. The Binance breach highlights the importance of that infrastructure, and could increase demand for specialized custodians and platforms with established asset-protection capabilities.

Coinbase has operated a dedicated institutional custody business since 2018. Circle entered the custody business this year, after the U.S. Office of the Comptroller of the Currency granted it approval to operate as a trust bank. In the past few years, Ripple and Kraken have also launched custody businesses.

Still, smaller players compete in the space as well. Small cap crypto exchange stock Gemini Space Station has offered custody services since 2019. BitGo Holdings, founded in 2013, is one of the earliest players in institutional crypto custody and infrastructure, with a reputation for security and serving major institutions.

The shift could also drive consolidation as companies look to buy capabilities, deepen their infrastructure offerings and achieve the scale needed to compete, Lau said.

The more consequential structural shift may be the separation of custody from execution, according to Morgan Stanley.

"Crypto exchanges have historically combined trading, custody, and settlement within vertically integrated platforms, concentrating operational risk. Repeated security breaches could encourage investors to move assets toward independent custody while continuing to trade on CEXs," the firm's analyst Felix Stratmann said in a note Monday," referring to centralized cryptocurrency exchanges.

"This would preserve the role of centralized exchanges as liquidity and execution venues, while potentially benefiting regulated custodians, including banks, as institutional investors place greater emphasis on asset segregation, operational resilience, and investor protection," Stratmann said.

Although military helicopter appearances are a familiar feature of major sporting events in the United States, the incident raised questions about coordination between stadium personnel and military flight crews. Fortunately, the helicopter continued flying after the fireworks erupted, with no apparent damage reported.

Four US Army Sikorsky UH-60M Black Hawk helicopters from the 2-158th Assault Helicopter Battalion, part of the 16th Combat Aviation Brigade, performed the ceremonial flyover at Lumen Field. The aircraft approached the stadium in formation as part of the pregame celebrations before the Seahawks faced the Chicago Bears. According to MyNorthwest, the first three helicopters passed over the stadium before the fireworks display began. Video footage shared widely on social media shows fireworks launching from the stadium structure and exploding extremely close to the trailing helicopter. From certain camera angles, the pyrotechnics appear to pass alongside the aircraft's rear section, creating the impression that the helicopter might have been struck. The aircraft nevertheless continued its flight behind the other three helicopters, with no visible signs of damage.

The incident was also reported by the New York Post, which highlighted the frightening proximity of the fireworks and the reactions from spectators. While some footage appears to show fireworks passing directly alongside the aircraft, it remains unclear whether any projectile actually made contact. Neither an official explanation for the timing nor confirmation of an aircraft inspection was available in the initial reporting.

Fireworks are generally not compatible with aircraft operating in close proximity, especially during low-altitude helicopter demonstrations. Although a military helicopter such as the UH-60M is designed for demanding operating environments, its rotors, engines, flight controls, and other components remain vulnerable to foreign objects and external damage. A firework exploding near a helicopter could potentially expose its crew to burning debris, heat, and fragments, while also creating a distraction during a maneuver that requires precise aircraft control.

Sikorsky, part of Lockheed Martin, manufactures the Black Hawk, a twin-engine military utility helicopter. Its design emphasizes operational versatility, survivability, and the ability to continue flying under challenging conditions. However, these characteristics should not be mistaken for immunity to airborne hazards. Even a relatively small incident involving a rotor blade or an engine intake could have serious consequences, particularly when an aircraft is operating close to a crowded venue.

A historical example also shows why coordination between aircraft operators and pyrotechnics teams is essential. On July 23, 1982, a Bell UH-1 helicopter crashed in California during the filming of Twilight Zone: The Movie after special-effects explosions detonated too close to the aircraft. The accident killed actor Vic Morrow and two child actors, and injured six other people. According to the National Transportation Safety Board's investigation, debris and heat from the explosions damaged the helicopter's rotor blades, causing the tail rotor assembly to separate and the helicopter to descend uncontrollably. The investigation identified inadequate communication and coordination between the helicopter pilot and film director as contributing factors.

Its guidance on mass aircraft demonstrations further stresses the need for detailed flight plans, mandatory briefings, and geographical, vertical, lateral, and time separation between participating aircraft.

This makes coordination between flight crews, stadium production teams, and pyrotechnics operators especially important. Apple warns of risks related to AI agents behaving in 'unintended or unpredictable' ways

📅 2026-11-03  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Apple said in its annual financial filing on Tuesday that AI agents that can act in "unintended or unpredictable ways" could represent a threat to its business by opening the company up to lawsuits or hurting its reputation.

The new risk factors in Apple's filing show the disruptive potential of AI agents, which have rapidly become top of mind for Silicon Valley's most important companies.

"In particular, artificial intelligence agents and other technologies can take actions autonomously, including interacting with internal or external systems that in some cases they may not be authorized to access, executing or altering code, sending communications, and completing transactions," Apple wrote in the filing.

The company added that agents "may behave in unintended or unpredictable ways, including by taking actions that were not directed, instructed or authorized."

Earlier this year, OpenAI released Dots, and Meta introduced Muse. Both AI agents use advanced AI models to work autonomously for tasks. They can even email or remind users when new messages arrive.

Apple's disclosure comes after several episodes that involved rogue AI agents breaking out of their sandboxes to hack third-party websites, like the Hugging Face incident, which has drawn renewed attention from policymakers in Washington, D.C., and around the world.

Separately, the ability of agents to use a computer in human ways is forcing big companies like Amazon and JPMorgan Chase to come up with policies around agentic access to services for strategic or security reasons.

Last month, Apple announced changes to full disk access on Macs to reduce "risks related to this level of access" for AI agents installed on PCs.

"The Company's ability to oversee and control third-party artificial intelligence agents and other technologies may be limited," Apple warned in its filing.

Apple doesn't currently have an agentic AI. But when new CEO John Ternus was asked on Monday's earnings call about AI agents, he said that the company had "real room" to expand Siri's capabilities.

"As far as agentic capabilities, we think these are promising tools for users," Ternus said. "We just want to make sure that users are aware of what they are giving access to. We want to make sure this is done in a way that protects security and privacy."

This fall, Apple released Siri AI, a new assistant that uses the iPhone's local data storage to access data like calendars and emails, but Apple's Siri can't work autonomously for long stretches like Dots or Muse.

Ternus said Apple can't access private data on its devices, calling it a "core differentiator."

"If you think about it, so much of your personal data is on your iPhone, whether it's your contacts, your communications, health data, financial data, all of that," Ternus said. "Siri can tap into that and help you access it in a way that's very secure and private and that nobody else has access to."

Commercial AI agents like those from OpenAI and Meta ask for high-level access to the user's accounts like email in order to be more useful.

Apple indicated that the current regulations around AI and "super intelligence" are changing, unpredictable and that it could cost a lot of money to adapt to new laws governing AI.

"In addition, the Company is subject to the risks posed by ethical concerns and negative consumer perceptions related to automation, artificial intelligence and super intelligence, including perceived threats to safety or other societal harms or complications," according to the filing.

Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac rally as Ackman presses for conservatorship exit

📅 2026-11-03  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The midterm elections could be a turning point for the release of Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac from government control, according to billionaire investor Bill Ackman.

Investors may agree with him, if the rally in both stocks is any sign.

The Federal National Mortgage Association, known as Fannie Mae, and Federal Home Loan Mortgage Corporation, or Freddie Mac, have each jumped more than 60% from Oct. 14 to Monday's close. The stocks were both up roughly 3% on Tuesday.

Ackman, the founder of Pershing Square Capital Management, has long pushed for the two entities to be released from federal conservatorship.

"The reason why it's not being touched right now is, you know, midterms," he said in an interview with CNBC's "Squawk Box."

Ackman, who owns both names through Pershing Square, believes Freddie and Fannie have the potential to increase "10-fold from current share prices," he said in his quarterly letter to shareholders, issued on Monday.

Fannie and Freddie buy mortgages from banks and other lenders, and then package them into mortgage-backed securities, known as MBS. Their operations are critical to the housing market, providing liquidity to banks and creating a secondary market for MBS.

The entities were placed into conservatorship in September 2008, during the financial crisis. The stocks were delisted from the New York Stock Exchange in 2010 and have traded over the counter since then.

Ackman wants the conservatorship to end, arguing the entities would be worth multiples of their current valuation if they were freed from government control.

President Donald Trump has supported ending the conservatorship of Fannie and Freddie. But it remains politically and economically contentious.

The entities operate under strict capital threshold requirements and would need to be recapitalized, which could happen through issuing common equity. However, the Treasury Department would need to figure out an exit plan for its roughly $388 billion ownership stake in the entities through preferred securities and warrants.

Congress may also need to play a role, passing legislation to restructure the government's role in mortgage financing and home-loan guarantees.

Nonetheless, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent recently said it could happen before Trump's term ends, telling 2Way's "The Morning Meeting" podcast that the administration will tackle the ownership structure "next year, the year after." He said affordability will be guiding the decision.

One point of controversy is whether ending the conservatorship of Fannie and Freddie would increase the spreads on MBS over Treasuries, effectively making mortgages costlier. Bessent has sought to reassure the market that wouldn't happen.

"Whatever we do is going to be guided by making sure that the spread on [agency] mortgages … over the Treasury does not increase," Bessent said.

Ackman argues that privatizing the GSEs would help insulate them from political meddling. "Fannie and Freddie are these massive, enormously profitable institutions where whichever political party controls them…if they're still in conservatorship, can influence the outcome of elections," he told CNBC.

In one scenario, he explained, Democrats win the White House, conservatorship is still in place, and Democrats push Fannie and Freddie to lower credit standards in order to expand home ownership and affordability.

"If they're going to move more aggressively into borrowers with lower credit quality, for example, to achieve a political objective, that's going to have an impact on trillions of dollars outstanding mortgage securities," Ackman said. "It's going to have an impact on overall credit spreads."

MBS investors want certainty about how the entities will be run, he noted.

"That only gets resolved in a world in which they exit conservatorship, they become publicly traded companies that are owned by institutional and retail investors," Ackman said. "We set the rules. In conservatorship, the president can do anything."
