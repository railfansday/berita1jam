The Fifa hopeful hoping to take Infantino down with 'samurai spirit'

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Kozo Tashima is under no illusions it will be easy to take down Gianni Infantino - but the first serious challenger for the Fifa presidency says he will draw on his "samurai spirit".

First, the 68-year-old needs to get the endorsement of five member associations around the world.

The honorary president of the Japan Football Association said he has had "good replies" from those he has contacted since announcing his ambition to run in the March elections.

If he achieves the necessary endorsements, the next step is an altogether different proposition - toppling Infantino, who has been in charge of Fifa for a decade and told BBC Sport last month he believed he had the backing of the "vast, vast majority" of world football.

Tashima, who sits on the Fifa Council, acknowledged the scale of the challenge, but wants to bring "good change" to the organisation.

The former footballer says he is driven by disappointment in Infantino's recent actions, surrounding the cancelled suspension of United States forward Folarin Balogun at the World Cup and the abandoned plan to sell stakes in the World Cup to private investors.

In an exclusive interview with BBC Sport, he spoke of why he feels he cannot leave Fifa as it is.

"I know [Infantino] has a lot of supporters," he said. "Now I am alone and my budget is not enough but it is OK. My policy, my philosophy, I have to fight against him with my samurai spirit.

"I would like to make sure that the game of football is safeguarded and to be loved and enjoyed by everybody. We have to review the system in Fifa and build good governance.

"In the uncertain situation of the world, sports - especially football - can present good values like fair play, co-operation, hard work, integrity, and show the model of the society. So, first to review and rebuild good governance and make football accessible for all with the principle of football first."

Asked how confident he was in his ability to get the five endorsements, he said: "I have confidence. I already asked to some of the member association and [received] good replies so far."

He laughed when asked if he realistically hopes to win the election in Morocco in March, before saying: "Of course, of course yes, I hope I will win. It is not easy."

Tashima's campaign will centre on his philosophy for the role of Fifa president, which he described as one based on the "values of sincerity, soundness, fair play, and integrity".

Regarding the Balogun red-card incident, which involved US President Donald Trump calling Infantino to ask for the ban to be reviewed, Tashima said: "I have never seen the red card and the next game who can play.

"This is, I hope, never happens in the world football. He didn't explain about this issue, how to come and how [it came] to happen.

He added: "Most of the things are not answered yet by Mr President Infantino.

"With my experience in Japan Football Association, Asian Football Confederation, and Fifa, for some more than 20 years, I would like to bring good change if I am elected."

Last month, former Tottenham Hotspur defender Ramon Vega announced his intention to stand as a candidate in next year's Fifa presidential election.

He told BBC Sport he had received "more than five" endorsements, but declined to give details of the identities of the member associations backing him.

All candidates must prove they have played an "active role" in football for two of the last five years. Vega has said he is confident he is eligible to stand for the presidency as a shareholder of a club.

Vega has attempted to run for the presidency three times before but each time has failed to garner the necessary support of five associations.

Tashima can therefore be considered the first serious challenger to Infantino.

Flutterwave Named "Africa's Best Payments Infrastructure Provider" at Euromoney Transaction Banking Awards 2026

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

SAN FRANCISCO and LAGOS, Nigeria, Nov. 6, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Flutterwave, Africa's leading payments technology company, has been named "Africa's Best Payments Infrastructure Provider" at the Euromoney Transaction Banking Awards 2026. The recognition highlights Flutterwave's role in strengthening Africa's payments infrastructure, enabling businesses to transact across borders and participate more effectively in the global economy.

Presented by Euromoney, a leading international financial publication with over 50 years of experience, the Transaction Banking Awards recognise excellence across the global transaction banking and payments industry. The recognition is a significant achievement as Flutterwave marks its tenth anniversary, celebrating a decade of work to simplify payments, connect markets, drive ecosystem growth, and support business growth across Africa and beyond.

Since its founding in 2016, Flutterwave has worked to simplify moving money across Africa's diverse financial markets. By connecting local payment methods, banking networks, and international payment systems through a single platform, the company helps businesses accept payments, reach new customers, and expand across borders. Over the past decade, Flutterwave has grown into a financial technology platform trusted by approximately four million individuals and businesses, from small and medium-sized enterprises to global organisations. The company has processed more than one billion transactions worth over US$50 billion, reflecting the scale of its operations and the growing demand for reliable, interoperable financial infrastructure across the continent.

Olugbenga 'GB' Agboola, Founder and CEO of Flutterwave, said: "Being recognised by Euromoney as Africa's Best Payments Infrastructure Provider is a proud moment for everyone at Flutterwave, particularly as we celebrate our tenth anniversary. When we started Flutterwave in 2016, our ambition was to make it easier for African businesses to move money and participate in the global economy.

He continued: "Ten years on, we have built infrastructure that supports millions of businesses and connects African markets to the rest of the world. This award recognises the dedication of our team, the trust of our customers, and the partnerships that have helped us grow. It also reinforces why we started this journey. There is still significant work to be done to make payments across Africa simpler, faster, and more accessible. This is why we are continuing to evolve beyond payments, building a more comprehensive financial infrastructure that brings together payments, banking, stablecoin capabilities and other financial services to help businesses and individuals move, manage and access money more efficiently. We remain committed to building the infrastructure that will support the continent's next phase of economic growth."

Earlier this year, Flutterwave was recognised by CNBC and Statista as one of the world's top 250 fintech companies, while its Founder and CEO, Olugbenga Agboola, was named among the Top 100 Fintech Leaders by Fintech Magazine. These recognitions reflect the company's and its leadership's track record of payment excellence.

As Flutterwave enters its second decade, the company is expanding beyond payments to help businesses and individuals do more with their money. The acquisition of open banking company Mono and the securing of a Nigerian Microfinance Banking Licence mark important steps in this evolution, bringing payments, banking, and financial data capabilities closer together within its ecosystem.

Flutterwave is also advancing stablecoin-powered payment and settlement infrastructure, with strategic investments from Ripple and Circle reinforcing its ambitions in digital asset infrastructure. These capabilities are designed to support more efficient cross-border money movement and settlement, complementing the traditional payment networks that businesses rely on.

Alongside these developments, Flutterwave is expanding its multicurrency capabilities, helping businesses manage international collections and payments while serving customers across markets. Through its broader suite of financial products, the company is working towards a more connected platform where businesses can accept payments, access banking services, manage funds across currencies, and move money across borders with greater flexibility.

As Flutterwave looks to its next chapter, its ambition is to build on that foundation, bringing payments, banking, multicurrency capabilities, and stablecoin-powered infrastructure together to help businesses grow across Africa and the global economy.

DaVinci Resolve Now Supports Apple’s Brand-New Touchscreen Mac

Apple’s brand-new MacBook Pro with PROLED XDR display, the first Mac to ever feature a touchscreen, arrives today, and Blackmagic Design’s DaVinci Resolve 21 video editor is ready.

DaVinci Resolve 21.2 introduces many updates, fixes, and improvements, including full compatibility and support for the Apple MacBook Pro with PROLED XDR touchscreen. PetaPixel‘s Jaron Schneider reviewed the new MacBook Pro, awarding it a 10 and, of course, Editor’s Choice.

However, while the big highlight is the touchscreen, it is the fundamentally new Tandem OLED screen technology that truly impresses. Whether users touch it or not, it’s the best display ever in a laptop, absolutely smashing PetaPixel‘s display benchmark tests and delivering truly absurd color performance and consistency.

“This is a remarkable display,” Schneider writes, emphasis his. Between that beautiful display and the MacBook Pro’s class-leading performance in DaVinci Resolve, video editors should be beside themselves, whether they ever use touch inside DaVinci Resolve or not. But now, at least, they can.

The update also adds support for Blackmagic Design Gen 6 LUTs, workflow improvements for audio and multicam clips, support for EXIF data in PNG files, and a bunch of bug fixes. The full list of changes is available on Blackmagic’s support center, and DaVinci Resolve 21.2 is available now as a free update for all Resolve 21 users, including free users and those with the paid Studio version.

The Blackmagic Generation 6 Color Science LUTs and film looks are a notable update. This new color science arrived in September inside Blackmagic RAW 6.0, the company’s professional RAW codec. While Blackmagic’s own cameras utilize Blackmagic RAW (BRAW), some third-party cameras do as well via external recording, including Sony FX-series cameras. Blackmagic says BRAW is the “world’s fastest RAW format,” and now it’s even better and fully supported inside DaVinci Resolve.

Speaking of purchasing DaVinci Resolve 21, Blackmagic Design announced a limited-time special on its flagship video editing app a few weeks ago. DaVinci Resolve 21 Studio is currently 20% off through November 20, so video editors still have two weeks to take advantage of the deal. With the discount, DaVinci Resolve 21 (21.2 now) drops from $295 to $236.

By the way, the company’s own portable DaVinci Resolve Speed Editor, which comes with DaVinci Resolve Studio, is also 20% off right now. That knocks it down from $435 to $348, meaning that customers are basically getting the dedicated controller unit for $112.

Your JSON isn't broken — your strings are: escapes, BOMs and the 2^53 lie

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Posted on Nov 6 • Originally published at jsonformatter.app

A few weeks ago we published a 30-second audit for spotting JSON tools that leak your payloads. This post is about the other kind of pain: the times JSON "breaks" and it's not the JSON's fault at all — it's the strings, the invisible characters, or a number that silently lied to you.

These are the four failure modes we see most, and what to actually do about them.

JSON strings allow exactly: \" \ \/ \b \f \n \r \t and \uXXXX. That's it.

Test escapes round-trip instead of eyeballing: the JSON escape tool has a minimal mode and an ASCII-safe \uXXXX mode, and the unescape tool validates what comes out instead of silently returning garbage.

A file that looks perfectly fine in your editor but fails with Unexpected token ﻿ is almost always a UTF-8 BOM. The spec (RFC 8259) says producers must not add one — and explicitly lets parsers ignore it. Plenty of parsers don't.

Similarly, content copied from PDFs or Word documents picks up typographic quotes, en-dashes and non-breaking spaces (\u00A0) that are invisible in most editors but structurally fatal. A tree viewer that can display raw escapes makes these obvious in seconds — our JSON viewer does exactly that, and it all runs in your browser.

This is the nastiest one because there's no error.

JavaScript numbers are IEEE-754 doubles, so any integer above 2^53 - 1 (9007199254740991) can't be represented exactly. Parse 9007199254740993 with JSON.parse and you silently get 9007199254740992. No exception. No warning.

The fix is boring but non-negotiable: big IDs travel as strings. If you're auditing an existing integration, paste a real response into a strict validator — ours flags lossy integers before they reach your code.

The most confusing sight in a log: a value that looks like "{\"a\": 1}". That's a JSON document serialized as a string inside another JSON document — logs, webhooks and message queues do this constantly.

Strip one level of escaping, then parse again. For real payloads use something like fromjson in jq running in your browser — no terminal, no uploads, described in our post on client-side jq.

Every tool linked above is 100% client-side: paste your payload, and nothing leaves your browser tab. After the CodeBeautify leak (user-pasted payloads exposed through a public feed), "the tool never sees your data" stopped being a nice-to-have for us — it's the product. The full story is in the leak explained.

If you want the reference version of all of this — the full escape table, the JSON vs JSON5 vs JSONC vs NDJSON comparison, and the base64 alphabet traps — we put together a one-page cheat sheet you can keep open next to your editor.

Match types, negatives, keyword bids, and impression share – here's what carries over from Google Ads for a paid search team and what doesn't.

In TikTok’s Oct. 30 Q4 product preview, two impression share metrics and a set of ready-to-use keyword packs were given to Search Ads advertisers. I wrote about this preview on Nov. 2. The quick version is that a help page defined those metrics back in September, and the packs are a new addition to the Keyword Suggestion Tool.

The long version is what a paid search manager sees when they open the campaign type for the very first time. A paid search manager has seen match type names, negative keyword lists and the search terms report before as part of Google Ads management. Match type reporting will depend on the campaign objective. Bidding by keyword needs the Traffic objective (along with a Cost Cap bid).

In each of these areas, for that manager, it becomes an issue of whether they can continue using some old habits they’ve developed, or if they need to change how they’re setting up their account and creating their reports. Each of these areas is addressed in TikTok’s help center.

Search advertising on TikTok has evolved sequentially during a four year period with the help center separating each addition. Brooke Osmundson reported that TikTok ran a search ads beta with select partner accounts in March 2022. The Search Ads Toggle was then made available to users in 2023. This is currently referred to as Automatic Search Placement, which extends in-feed ads into search result pages. All new campaigns will automatically be enabled with Automatic Search Placement. As noted by the help center at TikTok, Automatic Search Placement is considered “not a traditional keyword-based search ads offering.”

The keyword product Search Ads Campaign went live in the U.S. on Sept. 24, 2024. The two supported objectives were Traffic and Web Conversion. Also included with this launch were the keyword suggestion tool and negative keywords. At TikTok World in June 2025, TikTok stated “Billions of searches are happening on TikTok every day,” “up more than 40% from last year,” according to internal global data provided by the company, and did not provide an exact number.

On May 13, 2026, TikTok announced Search Hubs. This is a brand-owned page displayed above the search results for an approved list of keywords. TopSearch, which has a help center page dated October 2026, is a top-of-the-page auction for the first sponsored position and is billed per thousand impressions when using the Reach objective. When an advertiser loses in the auction, their ad “does not move into a lower search ad placement.” On Oct. 5, 2026, TikTok announced “Search Ads Campaign, powered by Smart+,” however there was no information regarding rollout dates in the post.

Automatic Search Placement, Search Ads Campaign, the Automated Keywords setting within Search Ads Campaign, Search Hubs and TopSearch are separate products and options; however the focus of this comparison is the Search Ads Campaign. The campaign page lists Traffic, Sales, Lead Generation and App Promotion as objectives. Additionally, the bottom of the campaign page states “available in the US and testing in other markets,” while TikTok’s availability page, dated April 2026, lists 11 countries for Traffic.

Keyword matching types on TikTok are called by the same name and have the same format as with Google Ads. On TikTok’s match type page it is explained that there are broad, phrase and exact. Phrase and exact are defined on the meaning of the search rather than its word order, so a phrase match for “Luxury fragrance” will also match a search for a specific perfume name. TikTok’s keywords page states you can have up to 1,000 keywords per ad group, but recommends you have at least 20.

Negative Keywords in both platforms have the same three match options, and TikTok allows a maximum of ten thousand negative keywords per ad group. As for plurals and synonyms, both platforms require the advertisers to include these variations. The platforms differ on misspellings. Google’s negative keywords automatically account for them. However, according to its documentation, TikTok’s negative keywords “don’t block singular or plural forms, misspellings, and synonyms” and “You must add each variation separately.”

TikTok’s search terms report is located where an individual managing a Google Ads campaign would look for it. In addition, TikTok’s reporting guide, dated April 2026, lists performance reporting at the search term, keyword and ad group levels. Traffic campaigns support keyword-level bids under Cost Cap. According to TikTok’s optimization guide, if a keyword has no assigned bid, the bid for that keyword will inherit the bid from its parent ad group.

TikTok’s Keyword Suggestion Tool generates “monthly impression predictions” it says “are based on historical data and are for reference only.” TikTok’s Keyword Planner provides “search volume, competition, and scale and performance prediction.”

The Oct. 30 preview of Keyword Packs includes pre-crafted lists of brand and category keywords. These lists generate “daily-refreshed recommendations” which include “key signals like search volume, CPC, and competition.”

The Impression Share help page, dated September 2026, has definitions for Impression Share as “The percentage of eligible search results impressions your ads captured” and Top 1 Impression Share as the same share of first-position impressions. The preview states Impression Share and Top 1 Impression Share are available at the campaign, advertiser and keyword levels. A health module in an ad group at the time of creation identifies thin keyword lists, low bids and a daily budget less than five times the bid.

Keyword-level bidding is documented for Traffic campaigns using Cost Cap, and the optimization guide says it “is based on the cost cap bidding, so it shows only when there is a valid bid.” The guide covers Web Conversion only through Target CPA but does not include keyword bidding. There is also an additional reporting restriction on match type keyword reporting, this one is Traffic only, and nothing on the pages reviewed as of Nov. 4 covers either control for Catalog or App Promotion campaigns. TikTok’s Automated Keywords feature, per a help page dated March 2026, is enabled by default for SMB accounts which “have to opt out or turn the toggle off,” while Key Accounts have the setting switched off by default.

TikTok’s page says “You aren’t notified when new automated keywords are added to a campaign.” Automated Keywords may be controlled with the toggle switch, by pausing an individual keyword, or through the use of negative keywords, and TikTok says its own internal testing showed the feature “can drive 10% incremental conversions while meeting CPA targets.”

All ads are either videos, carousels or, for Sales/Travel & Entertainment advertisers, a Search Catalog format. TikTok’s optimization guide says relevance is scored for each keyword and creative pair. Google measures ad quality as well; therefore, it is not the creative that differs between platforms. The production does differ because a campaign needs creative that fits each keyword theme.

Where the campaign type is sold varies based upon the objective of the campaign. TikTok’s availability page has two different country lists for Traffic. The first list includes Brazil, Australia, and all other countries’ advertisers who are trying to reach users in the U.S., and the second list includes Japan but not Brazil, Australia or the rest-of-world provision. Lead Generation campaigns are listed as available in the U.S. only. TopSearch campaigns are available to “Select accounts in supported markets,” and there are 20 countries listed for TopSearch.

Top 1 Impression Share is a related measure to Google’s Search absolute top impression share. The latter counts the number of impressions from “the very first ad among top ads” against the estimated number of impressions the advertiser would have been eligible for out of all the top ads. While both TikTok pages define the base of their metrics differently, “eligible search results impressions” according to the help page and “eligible search auctions” according to the preview, there are no publicly available resources showing the two platforms count the same way.

TikTok’s reporting guide says that keywords and search terms with low conversions “cannot be disclosed individually,” which “may cause a discrepancy in total conversions.” Keyword reporting is described as “a landscape report to provide insights on top-performing keywords” and full analysis, it says, should be done at the ad group and campaign levels, and not at the keyword level.

The keyword report provides you with the best-performing keywords, but may not reflect your campaigns’ total conversion numbers.

On the same reporting guide page it states that advertisers are unable to report on search terms or keywords broken out by creative (because of privacy). Data for iOS14 SKAN (Apple’s SKAdNetwork) for conversion, CPA, and conversion rate for Search Ads “are not currently available for the search ads campaign.” Brand safety and viewability measurement from third-party sources on the results page “may be subject to limitations.”

In addition, search campaign metrics lag behind by about eight hours. The guide says “Partial conversions will be available to advertisers after two days” and “Most conversions will display after three days.”

The automated terms report displays all automated terms as a single line item, with a drop-down menu showing the ten highest spending terms, the Automated Keywords page states. In addition, a search term that matches without an explicit keyword can display as “unknown.”

Neither the Impression Share help page nor the reporting guide indicates how to separate lost impression share by budget and by rank. On their help page, they list budget, bidding, keyword coverage, creative relevance, competition and changes in search demand as possible things to look at when assessing lost impression share; and sort the first two (budget and bidding) by if you spent your entire budget. Google’s page, by contrast, splits lost share into separate budget and rank metrics.

Reddit announced a test of ads in its search pages on Nov. 3 with a select group of eligible advertisers. The announcement says ads “may appear based on a range of factors, including contextual relevance to a user’s search query,” using Reddit’s existing targeting. It does not describe keyword controls.

In March 2023, Instagram announced ads in search results, showing “in the feed that people can scroll when they tap into a post from search results.” Both platforms announced a placement, while TikTok documented its keyword tools, a distinction for search teams to work with.

When you initially open the TikTok account, one of the first things you’ll recognize will be the search terms report. A manager who runs weekly Google search term reviews already has the expertise for that review, the negative keywords, and the ‘unknown’ rows.

The second item to verify when opening the TikTok account is whether the Automated Keywords toggle is turned on. The feature is enabled by default for SMB accounts, and additions arrive without notice.

Your campaign objective follows your business goal. According to TikTok’s optimization guide, if you run a Web Conversion campaign that cannot build volume, you can “temporarily switch to the Traffic objective to build volume,” and return to Web Conversion once the campaign is stable. During the Traffic phase, you can view match-type reporting and use keyword-level bids under a Cost Cap bid. Both of these disappear when you return to Web Conversion. However, keyword targeting (and its corresponding negatives), along with the search terms review, remain throughout.

Producing assets that match the keyword themes means working with whoever makes the company’s TikTok creative. Keyword totals show the top performers, TikTok points overall analysis to the ad group and campaign, and iOS14 SKAN conversion data is missing, so a cross-platform report needs a TikTok search row that says what it can and cannot show.

eMarketer’s U.S. search ad revenue forecast “excludes display-oriented formats on social networks, even if triggered by search queries,” and its social network ad spending forecast “includes all the various formats of advertising on those platforms.” That is how eMarketer draws its lines. This is useful context for anyone comparing TikTok search spend with its reported figures, though it doesn’t clarify where a company records the expense.

TikTok describes TopSearch as a visibility product. Its help center lists “brand, category, competitor, seasonal, or tentpole queries.”

As such, competitor queries listed as part of TikTok’s own lists constitute a brand-defense use case to assess, not a budget line to assume.

Paid search skills apply directly to TikTok’s keyword campaigns. The objective, automation defaults, creative requirements and reporting limits determine how far they carry. Budget ownership is still a company decision.

The campaigns for holiday promotions currently being run will be the ones described through the Impression Share columns in Ads Manager, on accounts where it shows them.

In addition, this quarter there will be a keyword-level read of spend on accounts that have the metrics. All accounts that have Automated Keywords enabled will see how those terms performed in campaign reporting and in the search terms report.

Reddit’s test is one to watch for anything more on search-specific keyword controls.

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

At 5x forward earnings, Micron stock is too cheap to sell.

Micron (MU +7.01%) investors had a rough week, no doubt about it -- but things are getting better.

A major player in both DRAM and NAND flash memory production, Micron stock shed 26% through Thursday's close. As of 10:25 a.m. ET Friday morning, however, the stock is bouncing back 6.3%.

Election 2026 put Democrats in control of both the U.S. Senate and the House and installed Democratic governors in all four states where data center construction is most active. If investors are operating on the theory that Democrats as a whole are hostile to AI and data centers, then it makes sense to worry that data center construction will slow down, the sale of memory chips needed to build those data centers will slow also -- and Micron stock will fall.

But there's also a chance that none of this will happen -- or that if it does, it still won't hurt Micron that much.

After all, according to Citigroup, 2027 will see memory companies increase production by 22%, while demand grows by 26%, maintaining a supply-demand imbalance that should spur sales and keep memory prices high.

This morning, analyst KC Rajkumar at LYNX Equity Strategies seconded that sentiment, confirming that supply shortages, particularly in memory and flash markets, remain tight, and that Asian memory and flash markets aren't showing any drop-off in sales post-election -- at least not yet.

Granted, in the long term, there's a risk that the AI tide could turn against data centers, slowing Micron's growth. In the near term, however, at least through 2027, Micron's future looks secure. Trading for less than 5x the profit it's expected to earn in 2027, Micron looks almost too cheap not to buy.

AI memory management: from 30 KB to 11 KB without losing facts

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Posted on Nov 6 • Originally published at wallabytoken.com

The fact was on disk. Twice. Our first winback draft included a customer segment that our current records did not support. That was the moment we stopped treating this as a copy problem and started treating it as a memory architecture problem.

We found 30,195 bytes in our central L2 file: 28 completed items, 28 migration pointers, and a legacy error log. We rebuilt it into an 11,228-byte view with 33 facts intact. This is what changed, what we measured, and why deleting was not the point.

Disclosure: Wallaby provides inference-as-a-service for curated large language models. This is a field note from the memory system we use for our own work, not a claim about customer outcomes.

The relevant customer records were explicit: the proposed segment was not eligible for this campaign. Yet the first draft still had a "previously topped up, now inactive" segment. That segment did not match the people the message was meant to reach.

The correct state existed in two domain records. The draft had not used it. This was not merely stale copy. It was a retrieval and lifecycle problem: the right state was present, but the task that needed it had no reliable way to see it.

A better search engine might have found the right line. It would not have told us which line should still be visible, which domain owned it, or when it had expired.

The central file had grown to 179 lines and 30,195 bytes. The raw counts were useful because they showed that this was not simply a file with too many words.

The file was trying to be five things at once: a cross-domain baseline, a current work board, a domain-state copy, a history archive, and a routing index. It also held a sixth role, the old error register, which had its own proper home elsewhere.

This explained the bloat, but not the missed fact. For that, we had to look at the lifecycle.

Completed work was marked with a strike-through, not moved out of the current view. Migration to five domain READMEs had left behind repeated pointers. Our status checker still searched the old locations rather than all five domains. Dates told us when something had been written, not whether it remained true. And the old error summaries had never been formally retired.

The issue was not a lack of rules. The rules were spread across the system, but they did not form a usable page.

Our first temptation was to make retrieval smarter. That would have addressed the visible symptom and hidden the architecture problem.

We already had event logs, domain projections, an append-only write path, and a query command that returned JSON or one-line Markdown. We did not need another memory store, a vector index, or a new semantic query layer.

The central L2 became a shared current page for humans and AI, not a second task database and not a smaller copy of everything that had ever happened.

The implementation reused domain_state.py, domains.json, the existing event writer, and the existing tests. The only new pieces were a composition entry point and a curated list of sections.

That preview command assembled the anchors into readable Markdown. It did not write L2 or create a second source of truth. Missing anchors failed closed. valid_until exposed stale states. Closed items left the preview after one shift.

The first preview had three anchors: the revenue baseline, current pricing, and the current product homepage. That was enough to prove the mechanism. It was not enough to replace the page.

Next, we migrated the current focus, in-flight work, recently completed items, and account states. The expanded list covered 33 display anchors. The candidate and production files then matched byte for byte, which let us check the replacement instead of assuming it was faithful.

A few implementation details mattered more than the framework:

We wrote the acceptance criteria before calling the work complete. They were intentionally unglamorous.

The projection and freshness tests passed 35 of 35. The wider domain-state regression passed 51 of 51. The index tests passed 10 of 10, and the unified-write integration tests passed 5 of 5. After we fixed the false positive described below, the domain-state suite was 54 of 54.

These are not claims about memory accuracy or business results. They are claims about what the implementation and fixtures demonstrated.

One remaining drift warning would have been easy to misread as a migration failure. It pointed at or-application in the growth README.

The current state was fine. The warning came from an older activation log containing or-application as its anchor. Our legacy-anchor scanner was treating an audit trail as if it were a duplicate current state.

We changed the scanner to exclude proj:activation audit views from the legacy-anchor check. The check still fails on a genuine old anchor outside the projection. It just no longer mistakes an audit log for one.

That small fix produced drift=[] and brought the domain-state suite to 54 passing tests. More importantly, it kept the distinction between a current-state projection and an append-only audit record. Confusing those two would have recreated the original retrieval problem in a new form.

We have not measured a change in customer conversion, memory quality, or long-term maintenance cost. A smaller file is not automatically a better memory system. A passing test suite is not proof that the next real task will retrieve the right context every time.

The current evidence supports narrower conclusions. The central page is smaller. The facts we required are present. The preview updates from events. Closed items leave on schedule. Stale states fail visibly. The old write path still works, and the rollback file exists.

The next checks are equally concrete: whether new events update the central page without hand copies, whether closed items keep leaving on schedule, and whether a real task can answer the current baseline without opening unrelated domain files. If those checks fail, the response should be to inspect the curated list and read path, not to restore the old pile of narrative.

For us, the useful change is not less memory. It is less ambiguity about what is current, who owns it, and when it should stop being shown as current.

Our earlier posts cover the adjacent layers: why memory needs an audit between its log and current state, how we grouped 31 control points into 9 and compared shadow projections, and why a saved status is not evidence of a durable write. The broader upgrade and verification flow is in AI agent memory governance.

Jesus makes seven changes in first Portugal squad since Ronaldo’s retirement

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Syndicated reporting provided by Stats Perform and reviewed by the SoccerNews editorial team.

Jorge Jesus has announced his first Portugal squad since Cristiano Ronaldo's retirement from international football, with seven changes made to their Nations League party.

Ronaldo brought the curtain down on his glittering international career earlier this week, leaving as the leading scorer and appearance-maker in the history of men's internationals.

The 41-year-old scored 146 goals in 234 games after making his debut for Portugal in 2003, while also captaining the team to their first-ever major trophy at Euro 2016.

He also lifted the Nations League trophy twice in 2019 and 2025, and after Portugal's round-of-16 exit at this year's World Cup, many expected Ronaldo to hang up his boots.

Instead, he was convinced to stay on by the FPF (Portuguese Football Federation), only to leave the Portugal camp during the last international break after being left on the bench during a win over Norway in the Nations League.

Ronaldo was subsequently given a 30-day suspension by the FPF, but ahead of this month's meetings with Denmark and Norway, he decided to walk away from the national team, with FPF president Pedro Proenca promising to celebrate Ronaldo's contribution in the future.

"No tribute will ever be enough to thank him for everything he has done for Portuguese football, for Portugal," Proenca said before the squad announcement on Friday.

"We will do everything we can to honour the legacy he leaves us. Everything he has ‌done deserves to be honoured and celebrated."
