5 Great LitRPG Novels That Are Nothing Like ‘Dungeon Crawler Carl’

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

The LitRPG genre (short for literary role-playing game) has taken off like a rocket over the last few years. LitRPG has been around for decades, but the emergence of Matt Dinniman's bestselling Dungeon Crawler Carl series has seen it move from an underground niche to a genre with dedicated shelves at chain book stores. Dungeon Crawler Carl has the zeitgeist in a way we haven't seen in some time, and it is paving the way for countless others. We're already seeing other hits like He Who Fights With Monsters appear in places like Target and Walmart, signaling the LitRPG genre is continuing to expand its footprint.

One common misconception, however, is that Dungeon Crawler Carl is totally representative of the wider LitRPG genre. While the entire series is fantastic, it's a small slice of what is a surprisingly large genre pie. There are so many hidden gems within progression sci-fi and fantasy, and Dungeon Crawler Carl is just the tip of the iceberg.

So if you enjoyed your time in the dungeon with Carl and Donut, but you're looking to see what else LitRPG has to offer, here's a list of five awesome stories in the genre that are guaranteed to take you in completely different directions.

Matt Dinniman has often described Dungeon Crawler Carl as a horror story, and it's not difficult to understand why. The bigger universe epic happening throughout the series is haunting at times, and there are plenty of body horror motifs stuffed into the dungeon itself. That said, Dungeon Crawler Carl doesn't necessarily fit into the traditional horror genre box, but fans wanting old-school scares and classic horror archetypes do have another series they can turn to.

Rob M. Lastrel's The Game at Carousel series is a horror movie molded into LitRPG form. The first book, The Bystander, sees a group of friends arrive in the small town of Carousel, only to be brought into a survival game in which they all take on the tropes of horror movie characters. Those archetypes, including the Film Buff and Final Girl, evolve as the friends play into their roles, and they have to figure out how to use those tropes to survive an onslaught of killers.

This isn't the first time Tree of Aeons has come up here, and it probably won't be the last. That's because the concept of this book feels deliriously unhinged, but spaizzzer turns it into one of the most thoughtful and compelling narratives within the LitRPG genre.

The eight-book series follows a protagonist that is reborn into a vast world of magic and fantasy. Instead of becoming a great warrior or skilled sorcerer, however, Matt is reborn into this world as a tree. That's right, the main character is literally a tree, and the story couldn't be more enjoyable. As a tree, Matt finds himself amidst an ongoing war with demons, and he must figure out how to level up his seemingly limited abilities to help the warriors around him.

It lacks the bombastic energy of a series like Dungeon Crawler Carl, but Tree of Aeons replaces it with introspection and ingenuity. Not only is this series unlike Dungeon Crawler Carl, it's unlike pretty much anything you've ever read.

While it does share the similar comedic trappings of Dungeon Crawler Carl, A.P. Scribe's hit series is an adventure of a completely different scale. Instead of saving the entire human race by working through increasingly deadly levels of a galactic reality TV show, Dungeons & Deliveries is about a regular guy just trying to deliver pizzas on time.

In a fantastical version of Toronto, protagonist Alex is struggling to get by. Unable to get hired by any guilds or adventuring parties, he takes on a job delivering pizzas. Unfortunately for him, many of his clients turn out to be dungeon bosses, and he has to dive deep into their dungeons to get them their pies, trying hard just to make it back to work in one piece.

Hopping through worlds with a New York plumber makes for a fun and familiar adventure.

There are slice-of-life progression fantasy stories, which take away the massive stakes of books like Dungeon Crawler Carl and focus on the more everyday lives of characters instead. Then there's Heretical Fishing, which goes so much further than many of those other titles, creating what might be the heaviest "vibes only" saga in all of LitRPG.

It may not be for everyone, but reading Jobson's Heretical Fishing series often feels like sitting out on the peer and listening to the soft sounds of the water as a cool breeze blows against your face. It's as relaxing an experience as you'll find in LitRPG, and it stands out amongst the crowd for all the ways it forces both its characters and you to slow down a little bit.

If you want something truly out of left field, and not about a sentient tree, then you may want to turn to Cassius and Tang's Mimic and Me series. This hilarious and heartfelt saga is about a protagonist who is left on death's door at the very beginning of the series, and only remains alive when a seemingly monstrous mimic stumbles across him. The mimic ends up bonding with the character and, from there, it turns into a Venom-esque buddy trip through a LitRPG world.

The story is nothing like Dungeon Crawler Carl, but fans of the famous DCC audiobooks will be keen to check out those versions of Mimic and Me, as Jeff Hayes is a featured voice in the productions. Ryan H. Reid is the main narrator for the audiobooks and Dorrie Sacks is featured alongside Hayes.

Did a Narcissistic Boss Cost You Your Career?

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Posted November 18, 2026 | Reviewed by Monica Vilhauer Ph.D.

Prada was a 29-year-old registered nurse when she came to my office in tears, saying she hated her job and was having panic attacks. Sharon, the hospital’s human resources director, perceived that the trauma exposure in her work in the emergency room was the source of the panic attacks and sent her to specialized resilience training.

Devin, a 42-year-old software architect, came to my office with concerns about depression. He feared losing his job because he was too old and believed that new artificial intelligence programs were replacing him.

At first glance, their struggles seemed understandable, given the unique demands of their professions. The trauma exposure, aging concerns, and technological pressures were real. But more conversations revealed something deeper and more destabilizing: a high-achieving, abusive, and narcissistic boss.

The challenge is that organizations often view a high-achiever as the goose that lays the golden egg, making them revered and supported. But when that high-achiever also commits narcissistic abuse, they can be untouchable. And the fallout of their abusive tactics resides in the long-term wounds of countless victims.

In It's Not You: Identifying and Healing from Narcissistic People, Ramani Durvasula describes how many therapists are trained to address the person’s isolated symptoms and often miss the larger impacts of narcissistic relationships. She also echoes what Lundy Bancroft explained almost 25 years ago in Why Does He Do That? Inside the Minds of Angry and Controlling Men—the abusive person is a master of disguise and actually gains footing with the therapist, while the abused person who shows up as anxious and closed can get further gaslit and retraumatized by the therapist. Moreover, interventions focused on appeasement or better communication can reinforce and escalate manipulation and abusive tactics.

Now imagine that third party is HR, an executive coach, or an organizational consultant.

And imagine that the abusive person is exceptionally accomplished, charismatic, influential, and valuable to the organization.

Who gets believed? Who gets coached? Who gets labeled difficult? And who quietly disappears from the organization?

Given these complex dynamics, I was surprised to read an article in the Journal of Business Ethics suggesting that clear codes of conduct and consequences could help address narcissistically abusive supervision. The authors’ research revealed that leaders high in narcissistic rivalry employ abusive supervision when they perceive employees as deviating from their authority or organizational rules; yet they suggest: “It is essential that organizations recognize the importance of fostering a supportive environment in which both leaders and followers are equipped with the skills and knowledge to interact respectfully with each other” (Gauglitz & Schyns, 2024).

While rules for conduct can establish best practices and delineate what is prohibited, they may fail to address the hidden dynamics of manipulation, coercion, and stealth sabotage.

In Ten Keys to Staying Empowered in a Power Struggle, I explain how manipulative tactics often operate through confusing double-binds, intermittent praise, shifting expectations, selective memory, and gaslighting. These patterns can leave people questioning their judgment while the person causing harm appears credible and composed.

I have witnessed people who left jobs—and entire careers—without understanding how an abusive workplace dynamic eroded their confidence, health, and sense of agency.

An analyst made a deep cut to his price target on the coal company.

By Eric Volkman – Nov 18, 2026 at 8:09PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Peabody Energy ›

Investors struggled to find the energy to be positive about Peabody Energy (BTU -1.45%) on Wednesday. An analyst tracking the storied coal company made a significant cut to his price target, which dampened sentiment toward the stock. Its shares closed the day nearly 1.5% lower.

That move came before the market open and was made by Freedom Broker's Vitaly Kononov. He now believes Peabody stock is destined for $22 per share, down considerably from his former level of $30.

Despite the deep cut, Kononov remained bullish on the coal company's future, as he maintained his buy recommendation.

According to reports, the prognosticator's new take centered around Centurion, Peabody's flagship metallurgical coal mine in Australia. Kononov noted that the production outlook for the play has been cut three times so far this year. The analyst also considers coal prices to be vulnerable.

Centurion is rightfully considered to be Peabody's most important play, so how it develops will make a major difference to the company's results. What I'd be nervous about if I were a company shareholder is the future of the energy industry, an important client base for Peabody's thermal coal business, given Peabody's continued (and deep) involvement in the segment. For numerous reasons, the energy sector has become greener over the years, and I think that's a permanent shift.

I'm bearish on coal companies generally for this reason, and as such I'm bearish on Peabody.

Warren Buffett Expects Berkshire Hathaway (BRKA, BRKB) Stock to Drop About 50% Again Someday. Here's What He Says to Do Then.

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Berkshire's stock has already been roughly halved (or more) three times. That should give investors confidence.

By Selena Maranjian – Nov 18, 2026 at 8:05PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Berkshire Hathaway ›

When Warren Buffett talks, most savvy investors listen. Last year, in his now-annual Thanksgiving letter, he said:

Our stock price will move capriciously, occasionally falling 50% or so as has happened three times in 60 years under present management.

Buffett followed that sentence with this one:

Don't despair; America will come back and so will Berkshire shares.

Buffett was at the helm of Berkshire Hathaway (BRKA -1.71%) (BRKB -2.07%) for about 60 years, and grew the company by a remarkable annual average of nearly 20% over that period. Now 96 years old, he has since stepped away from that top job and is chairman emeritus.

Buffett actually detailed the three 50%-ish drops earlier, in his 2017 letter to shareholders, along with a 37% drop. I'm including that data in the table below. Note that it refers to the company's Class A shares, not the Class B shares that most shareholders likely own.

Both classes of stock generally move in tandem. As I write this, Class A shares are trading at around $878,500 apiece, and Class B shares near $587. Few of us could afford a share of Class A stock, which is part of the reason that Class B shares exist.

There are lessons to be drawn from that table that apply to much of our investing, not just to Berkshire Hathaway stock. For starters, note how the lengths of the declines vary -- from a few weeks to a year or two.

That's a great reminder that whenever the stock market corrects or crashes -- and the S&P 500 has actually been generally declining over the past month or so, down about 3% -- it may not stay down very long. (Berkshire Class A shares, meanwhile, are up about 17.5% over the past month!)

Note, too, that Berkshire shares and stocks, in general, tend to bounce back quite well, either quickly or eventually. Look at that 50.7% drop nearly 20 years ago, from $147,000 per share to $72,400. And look where the shares are now -- $878,500.

Miami Heat go all out with Vice City night ahead of 'Grand Theft Auto 6' release

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

MIAMI — In this corner of Vice City, at least it was dry.

Outside, torrential downpours that lasted all day and had threatened flooding throughout South Florida didn't deter thousands of fans from lining up outside the Kaseya Center for a Miami Heat-Milwaukee Bucks game that was rebranded to celebrate the midnight release of "Grand Theft Auto 6." The game is set in a fictional city named Leonida, otherwise known as Vice City, that is very closely based on Miami.

The video game franchise has a massive fan base and this one has been in production for more than a decade, leading to intense enthusiasm and anticipation for the latest release. Fans, donned in ponchos and raincoats being blasted by the wind, were lined up outside the Kaseya Center more than four hours before tipoff.

Inside, the vibe was euphoric and festive, and the interest in the activations was overwhelming. A custom court with electric pink and blue paint matched the pink at center court, where "Vice City" was painted in the video game's font.

Vice City-branded apparel was sold at several stores across the arena, and lines to get in to purchase items wrapped around more than half of the bowl. One fan said he had been in line for around two hours; he was still several hundred patrons away.

A store selling jerseys, the Heat Jersey Lab, was stocked only with special edition Vice City jerseys to commemorate the activation. With about 45 minutes to go until tipoff, some of the slots where jerseys had hung had been cleared out.

"This is a really unique night," Heat coach Erik Spoelstra told reporters before the game. "It's going to be a lot of fun. It's going to be a global game tonight because of the setup with GTA. I think it's awesome. I think it's fun. It's 10x to everything."

The activations were consistent throughout the night. Fast-food restaurants had been rebranded into ones made popular in the video game franchise; in sections 105, 120, 308 and 324 were "Burger Shots" and in section 325 was a "Cluckin' Bell," a fried chicken joint.

During stoppages in play, the jumbotrons above the floor played advertisements that promoted fictional businesses from the video game; late in the first quarter, one for "Status Auto Imports" was played, with the footage all being gameplay from Grand Theft Auto.

Even the Heat's public address announcer, Gale Nelson, introduced the team to fans as "your Vice City Heat." Later, throughout the night, entertainers did not mention Miami whatsoever, instead referring to the home city by the video game moniker.

"It's interesting," Bucks coach Taylor Jenkins told reporters before the game. "I'm not one to play Gand Theft Auto, never was. I was more of a sports video game guy. But anytime you can cross collaborate with sports and entertainment, I think it's a great opportunity to generate fandom for all parties involved.

"Driving to the arena, even with all the rain coming down, seeing all the people waiting outside, there's going to be a lot of electricity in here."
