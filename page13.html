House Republicans to elect a new leader as they look to rebuild from midterm defeat

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Rep. Jim Jordan, R-Ohio, left, and Rep. Steve Scalise, R-La., confer during a news conference at the Capitol on June 8, 2022. The two GOP leaders are competing to replace Mike Johnson as the top House Republican. J. Scott Applewhite/AP hide caption

Just one week after losing the majority in the midterm elections, House Republicans will meet behind closed doors on Tuesday to pick a minority leader for the next Congress.

The choice to succeed Mike Johnson, R-La., as the top House Republican will be between current House Majority Leader Steve Scalise, R-La., and Ohio Republican Jim Jordan, a co-founder of the ultra-conservative House Freedom Caucus.

The election — which will be conducted by secret ballot — represents a high-stakes decision for the party that will help determine which way it goes as it looks to regroup from a staggering defeat that saw Democrats flip more than 40 GOP seats en route to winning back the majority.

"I think what develops here will become a larger, external conversation about the direction of the Republican Party," said former House Speaker Pro Tempore Patrick McHenry, who held the gavel in an acting role during the tumultuous GOP speakership fight in 2023.

Republicans say they are ready to turn the page on their losses and start rebuilding to regain voters' trust. But first, they will have to decide between two candidates who would bring very different styles of leadership.

In Scalise, 61, House Republicans would be choosing a form of continuity — the No. 2 House Republican since 2023, and someone who has served in broader GOP leadership roles for more than 10 years. He's seen as more moderate than Jordan, and in a letter to colleagues announcing his candidacy, the close Trump ally said his approach to the job would be "conservative and consensus-driven."

"My number one goal as Republican Leader will be to win back the majority and I expect you to hold me accountable to that mission," Scalise wrote. "To succeed, we will need to be effective in both the oversight and legislative fights ahead, but we will also need to lay out a clear and optimistic vision for America."

"We have big tasks ahead, and tested leadership is needed now more than ever," he added.

In Jordan, 62, Republicans would be choosing a veteran of both the House Oversight and House Judiciary committees — two panels poised to play a central role in the new Congress as Democrats plan for a long list of investigations into President Trump and his administration.

A former college wrestling coach known for his pugilistic style, Jordan has pitched an aggressive posture to his colleagues, writing that "offense, not defense, wins elections."

"For too long, we have operated without a clear plan to survive the hour or the day in front of us. That will not cut it against an emboldened and energetic Democratic party," Jordan said in his letter to colleagues, taking a swipe at the House leadership team led by Johnson and Scalise.

"I am battle-tested on the oversight front and I know the Democrats' playbook," he added.

The contest is a rematch from 2023. Back then, Jordan and Scalise were vying to succeed Kevin McCarthy. After multiple candidates failed to secure enough votes, Republicans ultimately voted for Johnson.

Leslie Shedd, a Republican strategist, said the candidates will need to break through a process that can be like "family-on-family violence."

"It's really important … to lay out a very clear, substantive vision of how they plan to lead this conference out of what is now a desert," she said.

John Leganski, who was an aide to McCarthy, the former House Speaker, estimates more than 70% of the incoming House GOP conference was elected after Trump won his first term. As a result, they were largely made in his image or won because of it.

That means, Trump could wield a major influence over the direction of the House Republican conference as a result. So far, he's staying out of the race, though in 2023 he did endorse Jordan.

Leganski said that the size of Democrats' victory last week will limit leverage for Republicans under the eventual House Minority Leader when the new Congress is seated in January.

"I think whoever wins your job is to … number one, try to win back to the House," he said. "And number two, make life as difficult for the Democrats as possible, largely by voting no."

The short of it? These earbuds aren't cheap — but we totally get why.

All noise-cancelling headphones, including earbuds, have some passive noise cancellation. The budget-priced Warhammer Survivors brings a variety of unlocks and franchise fanservice, but feels a little unevolved for the bullet heaven genre.

Story isn’t a crucial component here, but there is some fun Warhammer lore to collect.

It’s unsurprisingly quite similar to Vampire Survivors with weaker feedback overall, but there’s a lot to unlock and some surprise elements further into the game.

Could have served as a DLC, but larger sprite sizes, greater map detail, and some visual surprises manage to outdo vanilla Vampire Survivors.

OST is repetitive and uninteresting, audio feedback is lacking.

Warhammer can seem intimidating from afar, but the many video games minted from Games Workshop’s tentpole wargaming IP have brought countless new fans into the fold. There have been over a hundred such video games thus far, with few as easy to pick up and play as Auroch Digital’s Warhammer Survivors, a new fully licensed riff on Poncle’s Vampire Survivors, the bullet heaven action-roguelite dynamo that set the standard for the subgenre.

Imprinting Warhammer’s extensive lore, dark oddball humor, and formative tropes onto Poncle’s dopamine-juicing treadmill sounds like an easy swish. And, while perhaps feeling a tad too similar to justify its own SKU, Warhammer Survivors delivers an eminently playable counterpart to its vampiric predecessor. Anyone fond of both the subgenre and the Warhammer brand will find a considerable distraction here, but the game needs another pass for polish, and I wish it simply added more to the formula.

Like Vampire, Warhammer Survivors dispenses with any attempt at storytelling and tosses you into the action with a choice between two campaigns: 40,000 or Age of Sigmar (read as: sci-fi or fantasy). Both “worlds” hold a troupe of 20 unlockable heroes with dozens of weapons and relics to stack and track and synergize. Pick a toon and map, mark up any mods, then head off to war, directing the non-reptilian part of your brain to other affairs, like a low-stakes Netflix series or comedy podcast.

If you’ve somehow managed to sidestep the bullet heaven survivors genre until now, let’s briefly dig into the basics. Each level presents an infinitely scrolling pixelated map, with hordes of spawning enemies swirling into your hero as you work to avoid contact damage and projectiles, all while hoovering up XP, coins, and power-ups. Maxing out the XP bar delivers a choice of various active and passive items, with select pairings producing a scripted synergy that overpowers the basic weapon at hand. You choose where and when to move, but attacks are always automatic and constant.

Elite minibosses materialize every few minutes and drop a treasure chest on defeat. Open it and endure a dizzying Vegas-styled jackpot celebration and stack of cash. Money accumulates over multiple runs and feeds a metaprogression menu that boosts your basic stats, so even failure leaves something behind in the tip jar. You’ll lose early and often, but those buffed stats eventually slacken the resistance and ticks up your win-to-loss ratio.

Ten minutes deep into a run, you'll be eradicating dozens of mobs in a blink with a kill-count into the tens of thousands, multicolored damage numbers snapping like popcorn, utterly incoherent in the fracas.

Warhammer Survivors’ metaprogression and a few of its power-ups are reskinned from Vampire Survivors outright. Still, most bullet heavens crib freely from one another—Vampire Survivors is itself a riff on Korean mobile game Magic Survival—and there are some fun irreverent hat tips to the wargaming pastime here. You can collect paint pot relics, wield Nuln Oil and dice as weapons, and some other cheeky references set the game apart from Vampire Survivors’ 8-bit horror theming.

Beyond that, the hero objectives, Extremis Bosses, and faction traits in Warhammer Survivors are all wholly unique and excellent additions to the format. Some heroes are unusual indeed and tap directly into the lore, like Knight-Commander Pask and his massive Leman Russ Battle Tank, and Commissar Sebastian Yarrick with his Power Klaw. Like any other weapons, these can be unlocked and equipped later on, regardless of your initially selected hero.

That plays into the game’s squad mechanics, though I found them somewhat frustrating in action. Essentially, if your chosen hero cannot equip a granted weapon, someone from the pool will be conscripted and added to your team to wield it, which can get clumsy to control.

For instance, the straight-shooting boltgun is hard enough to aim properly when it’s in your own hands, but having to angle its shots from a teammate trailing behind you is a pain. Additionally, when your screen is packed to the rafters with targets, it's nearly impossible to keep track of your own five-man squad.

I found the game’s two eras to also be unbalanced, with early game progress on the 40k track much slower and more difficult than Age of Sigmar. Maybe that’s due to a better selection of power-ups in the latter, but I’m not entirely convinced, and I’m certain the game will go through a few rounds of balancing updates after launch to account for it.

I enjoy the way some levels feature environmental mechanics, like tunnels that hide items or elevators to carry you between different map layers. There should be many more of these quirks, as it's strange to wander battlefields peppered with massive turrets in the 40k era while none of them can be used, and 90% of the game's background content is mere set dressing; Vampire Survivors’ own Ode to Castlevania DLC featured a map with much more interactivity.

Speaking of that DLC, Warhammer Survivors starts you off with a similar map function, but this one is pretty much a basic pause menu radar for items. You can zoom in or out to get an idea of where relics and pickups can be found and track them with onscreen indicators, but there’s no landscape detail, nor can you even scrub through the map itself. Since most optional level objectives involve collecting a distant hero or relic, you’re forced to use that shoddy map a lot.

Perhaps most importantly, the game's feedback is far inferior to Vampire Survivors, which may largely be due to the sound. Warhammer Survivors’ audio mixdown is a problem, and I couldn't effectively balance out the SFX and music in settings, eventually opting to silence one or the other. That ultimately wasn't a great loss, as most of the music amounts to heavily-quantized militaristic industrial loops that overstay their welcome fast, though the ambient menu themes are decent enough.

Speaking of menus, the UI in Warhammer Survivors could have done with its own corrective pass (not that Vampire Survivors is much better). Controller input is especially scatterbrained; for instance, pressing A starts a selected level, but doesn't if you're checking off a mod, and you swap worlds with the Start button but then quit the game from the worlds menu with B. This all isn't impossible to manage, but peculiar.

I’ve never understood the fullscreen edge transparency in Vampire, but Warhammer Survivors actually blots out those edges with a skulls texture for some reason. The game’s overhead POV also often works against, as enemies love choking up doorways and walls, which renders them functionally invisible. Player projectiles evaporate on the edges of these surfaces, while enemy shots phase right through. Again, not unmanageable, but this will probably lead to a few frustrating deaths.

The game’s many objectives do dangle carrots aplenty, a boon in any bullet heaven. However, I wish there was a way to index and sort the game's 200+ challenges to the 40 characters they relate to. Additionally, the objectives menu provides no additional info or any explanation of the related rewards, which just seems like a missed opportunity. This could have functioned as its own wiki—like the Bestiarium, which is the best collection of Warhammer lore in the game—but it's just a minimalist list to chew through.

We cut bullet heaven games a lot of slack as a matter of course, and Vampire Survivors is a perfect example. As inspiration to countless others, it’s handily been outplayed at its own game time after time; Brotato shrunk the play space and filed down the progression structure, and Everything is Crab re-expanded that map, evolving the genre’s most basic active/passive upgrade design. Megabonk made traversal equally important to DPS, and Boneraiser Minions used a necromancer concept to present a kind of RTS/autobattler/survivors hybrid. And so on.

Considering these examples, Warhammer Survivors is a few days late and dollars short, albeit at a budget price. For those of you who’ve fully rinsed Vampire Survivors… Well, first off, that’s simply an impressive feat; well done. But secondly, here are another 40 heroes to max out, with hundreds of objectives to clear. Consider your bullet heaven buffet fully restocked.

Patriots keep losing ground in the AFC East in Week 9

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Coming off a Super Bowl berth, the New England Patriots entered the 2026 season with some high expectations. Those have not changed, but Sunday's 45-27 loss to the Green Bay Packers showed that meeting them might become even more of a challenge than initially anticipated.

What will happen over the coming weeks and months remains to be seen, of course, but for now let's focus on the concrete impact that defeat had on the standings.

The Patriots defending their AFC East title will be a challenge, even though they own the head-to-head advantage over the Bills courtesy of their Week 4 victory in Buffalo. However, what good is that advantage if you cannot get even in the standings? And with New England falling to 5-4 and the Bills winning their seventh game on Monday night against the Vikings, doing so will be tough.

Not only will the Patriots need the Bills to start dropping some games to overcome their current 2.5-point deficit, they themself also cannot allow many more slip-ups. Considering that five of their final eight opponents currently reside in either the AFC or NFC playoff pictures, that is far from a given.

Elsewhere in the AFC East Week 9 was business as usual. The Jets lost 35-12 to the Chiefs and are inching closer toward rebuild category once more, while the Dolphins — whose lone win this season came against New York — were once again unable to make enough plays to prevent a 19-9 loss to the Lions.

On the bubble: 8. Cleveland Browns (5-4), 9. Las Vegas Raiders (4-5), 10. Baltimore Ravens (4-5), 11. Houston Texans (3-5)

Not yet eliminated, but let's be real here: 12. New York Jets (2-7), 13. Indianapolis Colts (2-7), 14. Miami Dolphins (1-7), 15. Tennessee Titans (1-7), 16. Los Angeles Chargers (0-8)

Even though they were dominated on Sunday, the Patriots still remain in the driver's seat for a playoff spot in the AFC. They do have multiple advantages over the only other 5-4 team in the conference, the Browns.

New England's conference record is superior, while it also owns the strength of victory and strength of schedule tiebreakers at the moment. With no head-to-head matchup on the schedule, those tiebreakers might decide who makes it to the postseason when all is said and done.

Of course, there is still a long way to go before any conversations about the playoffs become serious. For the time being, though, the Patriots are still in control of their own destiny even if they ended up ceding more ground to the other wild card competitors.

On the bubble: 8. Seattle Seahawks (5-4), 9. Los Angeles Rams (5-4), 10. Dallas Cowboys (4-5), 11. New Orleans Saints (3-5), 12. Tampa Bay Buccaneers (3-6), 13. Atlanta Falcons (3-6)

Not yet eliminated, but let's be real here: 14. Arizona Cardinals (3-6), 15. Washington Commanders (2-5-1), 16. Philadelphia Eagles (2-7)

Losers of back-to-back games following their 6-0 start, the Vikings dropped all the way to the sixth seed in the NFC on Monday night. They are still well-positioned for a playoff push, however, as is the rest of their division: all three wild card spots are occupied by NFC North teams, as is the No. 2 seed.

The Seahawks and Rams, meanwhile, are both outside the playoff picture despite their winning records. The fourth seen, meanwhile, is occupied by a 3-5 Panthers team leading the worst division in football.

Grey’s Anatomy Season 23 Is Officially One of the Shortest Seasons Ever

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

If there's one thing Grey's Anatomy fans have learned over the past 21 years, it's that you can never really have too much time at Grey Sloan Memorial Hospital. Between the medical emergencies, complicated romances, and the seemingly endless collection of disasters that somehow manage to find their way into Seattle, the ABC drama has rarely struggled to fill a season. In fact, some of its earlier installments stretched beyond 20 episodes, giving audiences months of weekly chaos.

Unfortunately, anyone hoping for another lengthy season this year is going to be disappointed, because one of the show's longest-serving stars has finally confirmed what fans were worried about. Chandra Wilson, who has portrayed Miranda Bailey since the show's 2005 debut, has confirmed on the Chicks in the Office podcast that Grey's Anatomy Season 23 currently consists of just 13 episodes, making it the third-shortest season in the series' history.

During the podcast appearance, Wilson explained that the show's upcoming 500th episode has become a major talking point among the cast and crew. And while the current episode order might make that milestone seem a little further away, she has a plan for getting there.

Yeah, we talk about 500. 500 is a really good number, and I had, like, kind of worked out how we were going to do it. I said, so we’re doing 13 this season. If we implode that and actually do a 13 and a 14 in a 2-hour season finale, that’ll put us at 480 at the end, right? So, now we only need 20, and we could do, like, a full 20-episode season next season instead of 13, and that would be 500 at the end or do 19 and start season 25 with [the 500th].

The medical drama stars Ellen Pompeo (Old School) as Meredith Grey, Chandra Wilson (Philadelphia) as Miranda Bailey, James Pickens Jr. (The X-Files) as Richard Webber, Kevin McKidd (Rome) as Owen Hunt, Kim Raver (24) as Teddy Altman, Caterina Scorsone (Private Practice) as Amelia Shepherd, Camilla Luddington (True Blood) as Jo Wilson, Anthony Hill (Watchmen) as Winston Ndugu, and Adelaide Kane (Reign) as Jules Millin.

Created by Shonda Rhimes, Grey's Anatomy first premiered in March 2005 and has since become one of the longest-running scripted primetime dramas in American television history. Grey's Anatomy Season 23 airs Thursdays at 10 p.m. ET on ABC, with episodes streaming the following day on Hulu. The season currently has an order of 13 episodes.
