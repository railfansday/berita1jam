He Cleaned O’Leary’s Boots at Leeds - Now Lewis Hamilton and Drake Buy His Clothes

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Leeds United reached the semi-finals of both the UEFA Cup and Champions League in successive seasons in 2000 and 2001, in what was one of the most successful moments in the club's history.

Leeds had players like Harry Kewell, Mark Viduka, Jonathan Woodgate, David Batty and Rio Ferdinand playing at the highest level, with the latter duo eventually earning moves to Real Madrid and Manchester United respectively in their careers.

The Whites were finally beaten by Valencia in the last four of Europe's elite club competition in 2001, having beaten AC Milan, Lazio and Deportivo La Coruna on their way to the semis.

The Yorkshire outfit also had many players coming through their academy system looking to become the next Alan Smith or Robbie Keane in the first team, with Aaron Lennon and James Milner two players who emerged into the first team in the years following.

Rav Matharu was in the age group with Aaron's older brother Anthony, signing a professional contract at 17 years of age, having been born in Leeds and then progressed through the ranks as a Leeds-mad boy.

There were inevitably extra eyes on him as a young Asian player, as both he and Harpal Singh reached the fringes of the first team at the same time. But he says he never got close to making a first-team appearance for Leeds, having also failed to appear for the reserve side, which often consisted of first-team players too.

Matharu said: "I don't think I got close. Maybe I'll tell people differently in the pub after a few drinks, but I don't think I was near to breaking through to the first team at Leeds.

There were internationals playing in the reserves, it was hard to progress. I remember being on the bench for the reserves against Sheffield Wednesday, and that was probably the closest. Chris Waddle was playing for them and I was watching him, remembering idolising him at the 1990 World Cup.

"It was hard to break into that team because it was doing so well. There were internationals in the reserves like Jason Wilcox and David Hopkin. It was a club where you had to do exceptionally well to even get on the bench of the first team."

After leaving Elland Road, Matharu admitted that he began to fall out of love with football after trials with other clubs, and after failing to find a suitable next home, he began to work in retail - and never looked back.

Speaking about his next steps, he said: "I always used to draw. I was into clothing and footwear and designing. It seemed like something I could do. I really wanted to learn more.

I went to Loughborough University to do a foundation degree in art and design, and was then offered a place at London College of Fashion. I went there for two weeks but dropped out because the expense was too much. I moved back to Leeds and worked in retail for three years, but I always felt I had to do something more with my life. So I picked up my studies at Leeds College of Art.

"I didn't know how to sew. I didn't know how to cut patterns. But I knew I had a good eye for fashion and clothing, and from a young age I'd always wanted to know how it was put together."

After moving to London to pursue a dream of working in fashion, he was let go from his role as Head Designer at House of Billiam, which forced him to set up his own business.

He added: "When I moved to London I always had the intention of starting my own brand. The name was born at uni; a lot of people cut patterns with scissors, but I would always use a scalpel, and my tutor would constantly tell me I was like a surgeon. I'd built a small website and registered the name clothsurgeon, but didn't really do anything with it until it was time to do my own thing."

Clothsurgeon has gone from strength to strength, with Matharu explaining exactly how the process works.

"We concentrate on quality fabrics, quality production. You can come in and create a wardrobe of quality clothes.

We offer a full bespoke service where you can make whatever you want, timeless silhouettes with a contemporary edge that can last you a lifetime. Beautiful overcoats that will not go out of fashion in your lifetime. A bomber jacket, a suit, whatever it is.

His clothes have become renowned for offering unique details, taking off massively since October 2012.

Since then, Matharu's clothes have been purchased by the likes of Kendrick Lamar, A$AP Rocky, Drake, Sir Lewis Hamilton, Ian Wright OBE, Kevin Hart and Eric Cantona.

The brand has also included collaborations with Coca-Cola, Nike and Selfridges, while he became Savile Row’s first bespoke streetwear store.and the first south Asian designer to open on the Row.

It's been quite the change of direction from cleaning O'Leary's boots at Leeds, but Matharu is thriving.

The Packers have a bye in Week 11. Under the Collective Bargaining Agreement, the players are entitled to four consecutive days off.

Coach Matt LaFleur told reporters on Monday that, after discussions with the NFL Players Association, the Packers have decided to give their four days off following the first game after the bye.

It makes sense; there are fewer total days between the Packers’ Week 10 game and their Week 12 game (10) than there are between the Week 12 game and the Week 13 game (11).

The situation flows directly from the decision to play a Wednesday game, on the night before Thanksgiving. It removes the traditional two-week break, cobbling together a pair of mini-byes.

For the players, the break likely will coincide with Thanksgiving weekend, possibly giving them Thursday, Friday, Saturday, and Sunday off.

New-Home Buyers See Lower Rates as Builders Offer More Incentives

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

A young couple surveys the construction of their new home.

Despite continuous woes in housing construction, homebuyers may see some deals on the table if they buy a new home vs. an existing home.

With affordability having become a large problem for the new-home industry, builders have been offering incentives, price cuts and more to buyers in order to complete sales on their homes.

In October’s existing-home sales report, NewHomeSource and Zonda Chief Economist Ali Wolf even said that, in her opinion, new homes continue to offer better options for consumers with more sales at lower prices, a trend that will likely continue in the new-home market through the end of the year, as builders work to meet their year-end goals.

“As we watch existing home sales next month, buyers will still see more options and incentives for new homes in the fall and winter, making it the prime season for new-home shopping,” she continued.

Now, a new report from Realtor.com® has found that buyers of new homes are also seeing reduced mortgage rates.

The report found that new-home buyers saw an average starting mortgage rate of 5.01% in Q3, compared with 6.15% for existing-home buyers. This translates to about $258 less per month in interest during the first year of homeownership (on a $400,000 home purchase with 10% down).

While some of these rate reductions are only temporary (via rate buydowns), the report also noted that the fixed-rate mortgage also saw a lower rate for new-home buyers. Specifically, new-home buyers saw an average rate of 6.19%, compared with 6.68% for existing-home buyers. This translates to about $117 less per month in interest for the 30-year loan term (on a $400,000 home purchase with 10% down).

“Builders have adjusted to a market where affordability is still a major hurdle, but that adjustment isn’t always visible in the listing price,” said Realtor.com Senior Economist Joel Berner. “Reduced mortgage rates can help make a new home more affordable for buyers while allowing builders to hold asking prices relatively steady.”

“A price cut tells you the seller’s first number did not work. It does not tell you what the home is worth,” says one real estate professional. “Some reductions create a legitimate opportunity. Others simply bring an unrealistic price closer to where it should have started.” Read more.

To appoint the next comptroller general of the United States, follow the law

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The new approach being employed to appoint the next Comptroller General of the United States should raise a red flag. The Government Accountability Office (GAO), which is headed by the Comptroller General, plays an important role in helping Congress discharge its responsibilities under Article I of the U.S. Constitution, particularly in connection with oversight of the Executive Branch.

We were shocked to learn that U.S. Sen. Rand Paul (R-KY), Chairman of the Senate Committee on Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs (HSGAC), recently and spontaneously requested that names be submitted to him, under a short time constraint, to be recommended to the President for nomination as the next Comptroller General of the United States.

Under the GAO Act of 1980, a 10-member statutory bipartisan and bicameral Comptroller General Selection Commission was created. Chairman Paul is a member, since he is Chair of the HSGAC, but he is not the Chairman of the Commission. That Commission is required to jointly develop and transmit a list of at least three qualified nominees to the President. That is the law, and that is how the process of appointing the Comptroller General has been conducted since 1980.

The current vacancy for Comptroller General of the United States began on Dec. 30, 2025, and yet the Commission and its members have never met.

Equally shocking is that Sen. Paul took the liberty as HSGAC Chairman to insert an empty placeholder for an unnamed nominee for Comptroller General of the United States for a Nov. 19, 2026, Committee confirmation hearing.

Again, Sen. Paul did so even though the statutorily required Comptroller General Selection Commission has never convened or submitted a list of candidates. Then, on Nov. 16, President Trump submitted a nominee for confirmation. (We don’t want to name the candidate since we do not want to make it a personal issue.)

The General Accounting Office Act of 1980 included a revised method for appointing the Comptroller General. It recognized that the position was in the Legislative Branch and that GAO was the primary agency the Congress uses to conduct oversight of the Executive Branch. Therefore, it provided for a bipartisan and bicameral commission to recommend candidates to the President for possible appointment.

It also provided for a 15-year non-renewable term with extremely difficult removal requirements to help ensure the independence of the Comptroller General. This 15-year term is the longest for any appointed position in the federal government.

To be clear, the 1980 law doesn’t say in so many words that the President must pick from the Commission’s list. The Senate report on the bill said he could, “in his discretion,” look elsewhere, but should give the Commission’s recommendations “great weight.” You can’t weigh a list that doesn’t exist. Every Comptroller General nominated since 1980 came from a Commission list, and the law’s answer to a President who wants other options is to ask the Commission for more names, not to skip it. Henry Wray, a former GAO attorney who helped develop the 1980 law, says the current unprecedented process “violates both the letter and the spirit of the law.”

It is extremely important for Congress to follow the statutory process. Dave Walker and Gene Dodaro (two of the coauthors of this piece) were unanimously confirmed under this process in 1998 and 2010, respectively. Both of these appointments were made under the statutory commission method, which has worked like a charm.

With regard to potential candidates, any Comptroller General should be a highly qualified executive with relevant past experience who will operate in a professional, objective, fact-based, non-partisan, non-ideological, and non-personal manner. The Comptroller General must be independent from the Executive Branch, which the GAO audits, investigates, and evaluates on behalf of the Congress and the American people. In addition, any Comptroller General must have broad bipartisan support. In fact, every one of the past eight Comptrollers General was confirmed with overwhelming bipartisan support. All of these factors are of critical importance for the Comptroller General to be credible and for him or her, and the GAO, to be effective.

Since Senate Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs Committee Chairman Paul and President Trump have not followed the statutory requirements for the appointment of the Comptroller General, the Nov. 19, 2026, confirmation hearing should be canceled, and the normal appointment process should be adhered to.

Lindye Galloway Brings Intimacy to a 10,000-Square-Foot Home

At 10,000 square feet, a home can easily feel more expansive than inviting. For a new residence in Dana Point, California, interior designer Lindye Galloway approached that challenge through a careful balance of natural materials, layered textures, and sculptural furnishings. The goal was to create a home that felt as warm and comfortable as it was refined, with a relaxed atmosphere suited to everyday family life.

Galloway drew inspiration from the architecture and natural surroundings, aiming to create a timeless home. The interiors are organized around texture and natural materials in a soft, understated palette. Warm woods, soft neutrals, and natural stone run through the rooms alongside linen, wool, and mohair.

Tactile upholstery and darker accents contrast with the lighter woods and stone, adding depth and dimension to each room. Furniture was selected for comfort and sculptural appeal, with clean lines and soft, organic forms, giving rooms a relaxed, lived-in quality.

The attention to detail extends to the home’s fixtures and finishes. Hardware from Rocky Mountain Hardware and Nanz is complemented by mirrors from Claude Home and Croft House, while lighting comes from Ravenhill, Atelier de Troupe, and Roll & Hill.

The kitchen, primary bathroom, and living room carry the same combination of wood, stone, and natural fibers. Beyond the main rooms, the property includes a separate guest house with its own bedroom and an indoor-outdoor living area. Galloway extended the same material palette to these spaces, creating continuity throughout the property.

Rather than relying on dramatic gestures, Galloway lets the interplay of materials, textures, and forms define the interiors. It’s an approach that brings warmth and intimacy to a home of considerable scale.
