France sees new round of Yellow Vest, student protests

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Although fewer protesters turned out in comparison with previous weeks, several cities saw clashes between police and demonstrators.

Fewer protesters than expected turned out for the fourth weekend of mass demonstrations in France, although clashes with police were reported in several cities, the Interior Ministry said on Saturday.

Authorities had expected up to 170,000 people to protest nationwide, including 60,000 in Paris.

The ministry reported a slightly lower nationwide total of 146,000 people, out of which some 46,000 took to the streets of the French capital, where the afternoon was reported to have remained mostly calm, with some isolated incidents reported in the evening involving the deployment of tear gas.

Protest organizers gave a higher nationwide count of over 250,000 protesters, which is nevertheless fewer than the 320,000 to 500,000 that were reported to have turned out last Saturday.

Clashes between police and protesters were reported in Toulouse, Marseille, Lyon and Bordeaux.

The Interior Ministry reported some 350 arrests nationwide by the evening in connection with the protests.

Students and labor unions had called an authorized central Paris march "against the high cost of living and in support of young people."

In Paris, an authorized march proceeded peacefully from Republique to Nation, but tensions erupted after police said some 200  "violent individuals" attacked law enforcement after a man was arrested for setting fire to a trash can, according to Paris' police headquarters.

Separately, the country's so-called "Yellow Vest" movement — which gained notoriety eight years ago by violently protesting a proposed fuel tax hike rejected invitations to join the student rally.

Instead, the "Gilets Jaunes" held a banned event at Paris' Place de la Concorde, close to parliament and the presidential Elysee Palace. Two of its leading figures, Benjamin Ball and Solene Azelma Sigaux, were arrested and taken into custody.

Ahead of Saturday's protests, French Interior Minister Laurent Nunez said 70,000 police and security forces would be deployed across the country over the weekend, adding that authorities will "be ready" for "rioters and looters."

The large police presence comes on the heels of weeks of mass demonstrations that have at times grown extremely violent, most notably on October 17, when France's worst rioting in years resulted in more than a billion euros in damage to private and public property.

On Thursday, police made over 600 arrests during a nationwide strike that included blockades and other protest action.

Authorities say that, for the most part, protests have remained peaceful.

But there have continued to be numerous incidents of violent clashes with police, as outgoing President Emmanuel Macron and his government struggle to address public discontent over the rising cost of living.

Although Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu is scheduled to meet with trade and employee unions on Monday, organizers say the only thing that will quell anger will be for the government to bow to their demands.

"The only thing that will bring the protests to an end is a response to the demands that have been put forward for several weeks by workers, civil servants, young people and pensioners," according to an inter-union coalition statement published on Friday.

This latest round of protests began with students calling for improvements to the national education system and unions joining in to call for wage hikes and tax reforms.

Macron's government has said it intends to raise the minimum wage as well as increase funding for schools and teaching staff. But it finds itself in a tight fiscal spot as debt — currently 119% of GDP — continues to run out of control in the EU's second-largest economy.

Man Utd 'Make Enquiry' to Sign Morgan Gibbs-White as Fernandes Replacement

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Manchester United have a decision to make on the future of captain Bruno Fernandes in the coming weeks and months.

At the end of last season, it would have been a no-brainer for Man United to hand the Portugal international a new contract after a stunning season in which he recorded the most assists in a single Premier League season.

The 32-year-old has looked a completely different player this term though, and as well as being unable to replicate that form, he also made the disappointing decision to speak out publicly against manager Michael Carrick's tactics following the 4-1 defeat to Como in the Champions League.

Carrick dropped Fernandes to the substitutes' bench for the following game against Bournemouth in the league, which they lost 3-0, failing to bring him on even when they needed a goal.

Fernandes came back into the starting XI for the games against Chelsea and AS Roma, scoring a penalty at the second attempt against the Serie A outfit as the Red Devils came from 2-0 down to win 3-2 and send Old Trafford into raptures.

It was only the third game of the season that Fernandes had made an attacking contribution, with a goal and an assist, with his only other goals and assists coming in the club's other two victories against Ipswich Town and Sabah.

The facts from this season are that when Fernandes isn't on it, neither are United, and it has left club chiefs with a decision to make.

It seems likely they will exercise the option to extend his contract by 12 months whatever happens, in order to protect his value, but giving him a longer-term deal is more of a conundrum.

According to TEAMtalk, they are already considering potential replacements for their skipper, with Nottingham Forest's Morgan Gibbs-White one player they are said to hold an interest in.

United are going to find it difficult to find someone who has been as influential as Fernandes has been for them when the time comes for him to leave the club, but Gibbs-White could be an interesting option.

TEAMtalk report that the Red Devils have already made enquiries as they assess the situation of the Forest star, and consider their long-term midfield and attacking options.

Unsurprisingly, the performances and attributes of the England international have caught their eye, especially after a stunning 2025-26 campaign for the Reds.

Gibbs-White's record (Premier League 2025-26)

Gibbs-White gives off Fernandes vibes in a number of ways, not least the fact that he is Forest captain and is highly influential to their attacking play.

Because he is six years Fernandes' junior, he also has the ability to cover more ground in the middle of the park more consistently, and his goal record is particularly impressive.

Based on last season, United would probably want more assists from Gibbs-White if he was to genuinely become the Portugal international's replacement at Old Trafford, but that could come.

The issue the Red Devils may have is how they prise the 26-year-old away from the City Ground, given the player was on the verge of joining Tottenham Hotspur in the summer of 2025, before Evangelos Marinakis personally intervened and convinced his captain to stay with a bumper £150,000-a-week contract.

Spurs are also still interested in Gibbs-White, ahead of a potential 2027 battle for his services.

What is clear is that whoever lands him in the future, they will be getting a player who looks ready to make a big impact at the biggest Premier League clubs.

Worse Than Porro: De Zerbi Must Drop Kudus After Leeds 1-0 Tottenham

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Tottenham Hotspur may have felt that Jayden Bogle should have been sent off in their 1-0 defeat to Leeds at Elland Road on Saturday lunchtime, but it shouldn't deflect from what was a poor display from the in-form visitors.

Tottenham came into the clash in Yorkshire on the back of three successive Premier League victories, but in what was a pretty poor top flight game, they still looked second best compared to Daniel Farke's men for the majority of the game.

Spurs should have been full of confidence in the match before the two-week international break, but instead it was Noah Okafor's late winner that separated the sides.

The north London outfit will argue that they should have been playing against 10 men for the majority of the game after Bogle's first-half challenge on Mathys Tel, which went unpunished despite a four-minute VAR review.

That was a decision that former Premier League referee Mike Dean disagreed with on Sky Sports' Soccer Saturday, as he said it should '100%' have been a red card, especially after Jean-Philippe Mateta was sent off for something very similar last weekend.

A number of Spurs players simply didn't turn up at Elland Road, including Pedro Porro and Omar Marmoush.

The former struggled to deal with Gabriel Gudmundsson from a defensive point of view throughout the game, while he offered little threat in attack either.

The Spaniard was also fortunate not to see red following a challenge on Lukas Nmecha, having been harshly booked for an earlier tackle that led to De Zerbi being booked.

Meanwhile, Marmoush largely continues to flatter to deceive in a Spurs shirt following his summer move from Manchester City, and he offered little in the centre-forward position and on the left, where he moved after Tel had to be substituted.

Those two weren't the only poor performers on the day though, with Mohammed Kudus another to struggle badly.

While Porro and Marmoush were notably below-par in Yorkshire, Kudus produced one of his worst performances in a Tottenham shirt against Leeds.

The Ghana international started on the right wing against Farke's side, but he offered very little and was unable to provide the outlet they needed in the second half.

The 26-year-old was eventually replaced by Luca Williams-Barnett in the 83rd minute, and was handed a 3/10 rating by the Standard for his display.

Kudus is known for his ability on the ball but his dribbling was worse than normal, while he could only provide one accurate cross from seven attempts as Tottenham pushed for a goal.

To only win three out of the 13 duels he faced sums up the bad day at the office he had, and based on this display he will be fortunate to keep his spot in the XI against Ipswich after the international break.

While De Zerbi has made no secret of the fact that he is a big fan of Kudus having tried to sign him for Brighton previously, he can't continue to start matches if he performs like that.

The Italian boss has other options to pick from too with Luca Williams-Barnett, James Maddison and Dejan Kulusevski all on the bench on Saturday, while Savio could be back in action after the international break after injury.

Tel could well be missing for some weeks after leaving Elland Road on crutches, but it doesn't mean that Kudus will start again against the Tractor Boys.

Based on this display, he should be dropped regardless.

Tottenham have some strong youngsters coming through.

Liverpool 'Contact Agent' Over Surprise El Hadji Malick Diouf Transfer

Liverpool are eyeing a shock move for a Premier League star, according to reports, with FSG potentially realising the mistake they may have made.

Andoni Iraola's side could be in the market for a new left-sided defender in January with Milos Kerkez struggling for form since his £40million move from Bournemouth in 2025.

Kerkez shared responsibilities with Andy Robertson for the left-back role last season, and although the Scot has since moved on to sign for Tottenham, Kostas Tsimikas has returned from his loan at Roma and came in to start the 3-2 defeat at Fenerbahce on Wednesday.

While Kerkez is still Andoni Iraola's first-choice in that position, the Reds don't appear to be convinced about the Hungarian with alternatives eyed ahead of the January transfer window.

Pundit Alan Shearer certainly isn't, claiming that he believes Robertson is better than both Kerkez and Tsimikas.

Now, according to Swiss outlet 4-4-2.ch, Liverpool are plotting a potential move for El Hadji Malick Diouf at Brentford.

Diouf for Brentford in the Premier League

A fee of between £41.7million-£50m has been discussed, though the ex-Slavia Prague man has a contract to run until 2031 and Brentford might want more.

As well as Liverpool, Chelsea are said to be in the market for Diouf, according to the same report, with Xabi Alonso searching for a like-for-like replacement for Marc Cucurella, who left to join Real Madrid in June.

The 21-year-old joined Brentford in a £40m deal from West Ham in August, but has struggled for regular game time since. Indeed, Diouf has started just one Premier League match for the Bees and that came in the 2-2 draw at Aston Villa last month. The Senegalese was an unused substitute in the 2-0 win against Nottingham Forest last time out.

He was a standout performer for West Ham last season, despite the Hammers' relegation from the Premier League. It was no surprise to see him move back to the Premier League in the summer, with Brentford picking up his services despite interest from elsewhere.

But an exit from the Gtech Community Stadium has now been mooted, with Keith Andrews preferring Keane Lewis-Potter and even Aaron Hickey at left-back, meaning Diouf is third-choice.

Should Liverpool sign Diouf, he would add pace and an extra attacking threat down the left side. His athleticism is his biggest asset, and he is more than capable of getting up and down the flank.

Diouf has a high ceiling, and his former manager Nuno Espirito Santo heaped praise on him during his time at West Ham. “He’s a beautiful player," the Portuguese boss said in November 2025.

"He’s so sensitive, and the way he arrived here - I think he’s living a dream. He has a lot of talent, but he has things to improve. He’s like a diamond you have to polish. We have great responsibilities, but we have to enjoy what we do, and I think Malick does that.”

Taylor Sheridan’s 4-Part Crime Epic Has the Mob Boss Tony Soprano Could Never Be

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Starring Sylvester Stallone in his first leading role in a television series, Taylor Sheridan's Tulsa King follows a Mafia capo released from prison after 25 years. It's clear that a man who has spent so much time away from the rest of the world will struggle to adjust, but Tulsa King captures this spirit amazingly well, with Stallone serving as the figure who must cope with change. The problem is that getting a Mafia capo, himself a product of his time, to evolve can't be settled in the blink of an eye. Through Stallone's performance as Dwight "The General" Manfredi, Tulsa King adopts a different approach from the many mafia movies and shows audiences are familiar with.

Tulsa King isn't the story of a man who rises in the ranks to attain glory — something that has been depicted countless times. It is the story of a Mafioso who knows how to build a strong criminal empire but doesn't know exactly how to go about it in a setting very different from what he's familiar with. Sheridan's crime epic manages to give the "rising mafia boss" trope new colors, breaking the mold one stereotype at a time while also shattering streaming records in the process.

Like most mafia story protagonists, Tulsa King's Dwight Manfredi isn't the guy from the streets trying to climb the ladder to the inner circle of the criminal underworld. Instead, he's a 75-year-old veteran toughened by his life in the mafia and time in jail. After spending a third of his life behind bars on behalf of his boss, Manfredi expects a royal welcome when he's released. Instead, Don Charles "Chickie" Invernizzi (Domenick Lombardozzi), the son of Manfredi's boss, quickly reveals that his reward for celebrating a silver jubilee in jail is a trip to Tulsa, where he must establish a new base for the family. Manfredi, aware of the hay to reap in New York, realizes that the offer at its best is merely a banishment, away from where the real action and money are.

The legendary star likens the show's latest twist to 'Game of Thrones.'

The first few episodes of Tulsa King depict Manfredi struggling with the changed rules of the era. For a man who hasn't been in touch with the outside world for so long, getting out after such an extended period almost equals a rebirth. From opening a bank account to understanding how Uber works, there's a lot to Manfredi's journey of exploration as he gets introduced to the power of credit cards and the internet. While he still prefers to stick with age-old principles such as "cash is king," he is smart enough to use the resources at his disposal for his benefit. After all, you don't survive with a criminal record, which began even before the Beatles became famous, without being street smart, and the character's attempts to acclimate make for some truly joyful moments in the show.

With its setting, Tulsa King sets itself apart by ditching the city whose landscape has provided the setting for all legendary mafia sagas from The Godfather to Goodfellas, which is also referenced by Manfredi's right-hand, Tyson (Jay Will), in Episode 3. Not only does Manfredi need to adapt to the changes of the time, but his attempt to build his empire has to begin with decoding new unspoken rules.

From getting a free ride from the local car dealer to becoming the unofficial owner of a local dispensary that sells weed, Manfredi gets ahead merely on the back of his confidence and a bit of jaw-cracking violence. Tulsa proves to be a blessing in disguise to help him start out again with possibly minimal resistance, and at his own pace. In a city that doesn't wholly understand how the mafia operates, Manfredi's swagger makes room for hilarious encounters. The fact that he brings a money-making dispensary under his control with the help of just a water bottle emphasizes both Manfredi's criminal capabilities and the town's lack of caution in its daily business.

Tulsa King deviates from the trend of making mafia stories about blazing guns and tough guy acts. In this aspect, this show knows it's catering to an audience who's probably interested in watching for the legacy of the actor at its helm. While it'd be interesting to see the action star getting a beating, Tulsa King begins by placing Stallone's Dwight Manfredi as the undisputed king of the land — one who's jovial, sensitive, and dangerous all at once.

As a result, the Stallone-starring crime drama doesn't feel restricted by the legacy of what has come before (looking at you, Tony Soprano). Much like Dwight Manfredi, Tulsa King remains aware of the past but doesn't shy away from accepting a lesson or two from the present — and in doing so, the series offers a protagonist that doesn't fit into any classic mob boss mold.

Ex-refs chief drops Dan Burn disallowed goal verdict as Newcastle footage re-analysed

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Dan Burn's goal for Newcastle against Fulham was rightly disallowed for Yoane Wissa's foul on Timothy Castagne.

That is according to former PGMOL chief and FIFA referee, Keith Hackett, who has exclusively told Football Insider that Rob Jones made the correct call to stop Newcastle opening the scoring in the first half.

Newcastle were held to a 1-1 draw at Fulham on Saturday as Rodrigo Muniz's flicked goal earned a point shortly after Jacob Murphy's stunning 80th-minute opener.

The visitors felt they should have been ahead in the 33rd minute as Burn struck from Murphy's dangerous cross.

Newcastle's captain had peeled away in celebration against his old club before Jones blew his whistle to signify a foul in the build-up spotted by an assistant referee.

Magpies fans flocked to social media following the controversial incident, but seemingly did not have any grounds to complain.

Burn was able to drift into the box and poke Murphy's fizzed cross past Bernd Leno at the near post just after the half hour mark.

Zcash’s new security fund is 99.7% old money

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

A November 7 mainnet snapshot puts the historical seed at 99.7% of the reserve, with 1.12 ZEC accumulated under NU7’s fee rules.

Zcash’s new reserve for future block rewards is still overwhelmingly inherited value. About 99.7% of its balance in an early post-NU7 mainnet observation on November 7, 2026, came from previously unclaimed subsidy and fees, leaving just 1.12 ZEC accumulated under the new rules.

At 05:08:38 UTC, the mainnet API for Zakura’s NU7 monitoring dashboard reported a Network Sustainability Mechanism, or NSM, balance of 369.7 ZEC at block 3,547,068. Its status data showed NU7 active.

ZIP 237, the specification governing the reserve, sets the historical seed at 368.58445520 ZEC. Subtracting that seed leaves 1.11895065 ZEC of subsequent accumulation, or about 0.3% of the observed total.

An early mainnet snapshot separates the historical seed from new fee accumulation, then traces the fee path into future block subsidies.

The distinction matters for Zcash’s future security funding. NU7 has established a way to recycle transaction fees into later block subsidies. Its opening accumulation provides an early measure of contributions; assessing future security support requires a much longer record of fee demand.

The historical seed consists of subsidy and transaction fees that miners left unclaimed in coinbase transactions before NU6. ZIP 237 brings that fixed amount into NSM accounting. It therefore gives the mechanism a positive starting balance before any new fee contribution arrives.

For the rules deployed at NU7, ZIP 237 registers transaction fees as the reserve’s only contribution mechanism. ZIP 259, the deployment specification, excludes ZIP 233’s separate removal mechanism and ZIP 234’s alternative issuance design.

There is also no reserve reissuance at the observed height. Taken together, those rules mean the excess over the historical seed represents accumulated fee contributions under the specified accounting. The 1.12 ZEC figure follows from the reported reserve balance and those accounting rules.

The NSM balance itself is public consensus accounting for value outside issued supply. Distribution follows the protocol’s future subsidy rules.

ZIP 235 directs 60% of each block’s aggregate transaction fees into the NSM. The contribution is rounded down once per block to whole zatoshi, Zcash’s smallest unit; miners receive the remainder, including the rounding difference.

Scheduled subsidy remains separate from this fee allocation. The mechanism temporarily removes fee value from circulation and returns it through future block subsidies.

The delay is substantial in block terms. ZIP 259 assigns mainnet activation to block 3,543,405, while reserve reissuance starts at block 8,939,664. ZIP 237 sets the additional NSM subsidy to zero before that later height. Reaching that block threshold determines when payouts begin.

Once reissuance starts, each block adds a small fraction of the preceding reserve balance to the scheduled subsidy, rounded up to a zatoshi. The existing halving schedule remains in place.

ZIP 237 illustrates the scale with 100,000 ZEC removed from circulation: that amount would initially contribute 0.01375 ZEC per block under the post-NU7 formula. This hypothetical illustrates the payout rule; the actual starting payout will depend on the reserve accumulated by then.

Funding streams also receive a share of future reissuance. ZIP 237 applies funding streams’ prescribed percentages to the combined scheduled and additional subsidy. The reserve mechanism and discretionary developer grants are different arrangements.

Before payouts begin, the fee-funded portion grows with cumulative fee spending. More time permits more contributions if fee-paying activity continues; elapsed time alone supplies no funding.

That makes the useful follow-up measure the amount added beyond the fixed historical seed over clearly dated intervals. A growing total can otherwise obscure how much of the reserve still comes from inherited value.

The opening snapshot cannot establish a sustainable annual funding rate, predict future usage or show whether eventual support will be sufficient for network security. Those questions depend on fee demand over time and the balance available when gradual reissuance begins.
