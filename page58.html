Last month we got news that Wing Commander 3, the FMV space-combat sim with perhaps the highest concentration of Mark Hamill in any videogame, would be getting a remaster. Producer Jim Vessella is heading up the project, and revealed it during a presentation at the Portland Retro Gaming Expo.

What he did not reveal, it turns out, is that five other Wing Commander games—plus Road Rash—would be getting their own dolled-up re-releases, and that they'd all be dropping on Steam, GOG, and the EA App today as part of a larger bundle of 25 "EA Classics". Here's the full list of games that are dropping. Anything labelled "Anniversary Edition" is an updated, remastered version of the original game. Anything not labelled that is simply a re-release.

There's Demand to Remake Final Fantasy 6 from Within Square Enix, Says Series Boss

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Could a remake of Final Fantasy 6 happen at some point? The idea's been floating around for a while, but we're now, apparently, at a point where Square Enix employees want to see it.

This is according to series veteran and current brand manager Yoshinori Kitase, who specifically notes that there's an internal demand for said remake while speaking to IGN.

The outlet asks Kitase for his thoughts on additional remake projects now that Final Fantasy 7 Remake's trilogy is coming to a close, and while he certainly doesn't commit to a definitive answer, his mention of Final Fantasy 6 is enough to raise eyebrows.

Kitase says that while he personally doesn't "have any particular feeling towards any kind of remake" at this moment in time, and that he doesn't believe he'll work on one "anytime soon", there's demand "inside the company".

"Inside the company and also among the Final Fantasy fans, there's been many people asking for a remake of Final Fantasy VI, so who knows?," he comments.

To me, this seems like the kind of seed that could sprout into something down the line. It's easy to forget that companies as big as Square Enix are seeing constant churn in terms of development teams and project leads; all it takes is for someone to gather support and pitch this potential remake at the right time.

Interestingly, though, it does sound like Kitase's priorities lie elsewhere. At the end of the interview, he says that he'd like to spearhead something with a focus on "battle action", but "without losing the characteristics of a Final Fantasy story".

After a 16-year long dev period, Faraway, a shooting star arcade game about siphoning the energy of other stars, is out now

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Lifestyle  |  Read Source →

Lord almighty, video games take a while to make, 'ey? Especially if you're just one person working on it by yourself. Such is the case with Faraway, out today, an arcade game that reminds me of the early days of PS3 indies "about a shooting star that creates constellations to stay alive," all made single-handedly by creator of cult-classic mobile game Eliss, Steph Thirion. Did I mention that Faraway took 16 years to make? Like I said, game development is a lengthy process.

Looking at Faraway, and knowing how long it took to produce, it doesn't surprise me to come from a dev who managed to release a mobile game in the early days of the platform, long before free-to-play got its chokehold on the industry. It also really does feel like it should be sitting amongst the likes of PixelJunk, this thing should be something I'm desperate to play on my PS3 while I wait for my controller to charge.

As mentioned, you are a shooting star, a "spark meant to shine and vanish in a blink." But still you persist, connecting to and wrapping yourself around starts to "harvest their energy," in turn lengthening your own life, and of course, in arcadey fashion, racking up a nice score. It looks like game design as poetry, everything you're looking at procedurally generated to bring freshness to every run. I can fully envision this as a "one more run" kind of arcade game, the best kind, especially in an age long past the need to shove in another of your hard earned coins.

Faraway conceptually charms me because it taps into a style of game we don't get to see much of any more. What I'm saying is, I'm just a teensy bit sick of how many roguelikes there are, but that doesn't mean I don't want a game I can just hop in and out of at my own whims. Sure, arcade games might not be in vogue, I just miss them a bit. In any case, Faraway is available to pick up on Steam right now. Seems like it'll be worth a punt!

‘Wild Horse Nine’ Review: Martin McDonagh’s Prickly but Unconvincing CIA Satire Recasts ‘In Bruges’ as the Stuff of a Classic Western

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

[Editor’s note: This review was originally published on September 3, 2026 during the 2026 Venice Film Festival. Searchlight Pictures will release it in theaters on Friday, November 6.]

Another melancholically splenetic Martin McDonagh jag about a banter-mad pair of murderers who go on a boys trip to purgatory (where they search their souls as they wait for death to find them), the prickly but unconvincing “Wild Horse Nine” strains to translate the post-Guy Ritchie bark of “In Bruges” into the language of a classic American Western. In this case, an American Western set on Easter Island and backdropped by Augusto Pinochet’s rise to power in the fall of 1973.  Related Stories ‘North of North’ Season 2 Review: Netflix’s Defiantly Nice Arctic Sitcom Is for the Warmhearted F**k-Ups ‘Hen’ Review: A Cockeyed Comedy Sees Europe’s Migrant Crisis Through the Eyes of a Chicken

McDonagh’s decision to recast his same old script against the backdrop of such a real and terrible event grants this new telling a welcome sense of scale, both in terms of its characters’ morality, as well as in regards to their greater place in history. But that decision is a double-edged sword that cuts the film off at the knees. Where “In Bruges” kicked off with the killing of a fictional altar boy, “Wild Horse Nine” is overshadowed by the deaths of thousands upon thousands of very real people, and that reality bleeds into the edges of the frame even when the seriocomic absurdity of McDonagh’s writing demands a world of pure invention.

Does the overlap make “Wild Horse Nine” feel glib? Yes, and thank god for that — if not for glibness the movie would lose the majority of its few solid laughs. The problem is that it also makes “Wild Horse Nine” feel as transparently manufactured as the CIA-backed overthrow of a foreign democracy. I can accept that an inexperienced hitman might have a dark night of the soul after shooting an innocent child during confession. By contrast, I struggle to believe that a veteran CIA agent who openly brags about his involvement in the Kennedy assassination would have a come-to-Jesus moment after orchestrating the demise of Salvador Allende. McDonagh has some good fun wrestling with whether or not we should care about such a mottled change of heart, but the point is gradually rendered moot in a movie whose emotions are less credible than its conspiracies.

The original Company man, Chris (John Malkovich, more locked in than he’s been on screen since “Burn After Reading”) is a natural hinge for the off-kilter comedy that McDonagh builds around him, in that he can be a joy to watch even when you don’t buy a word he’s saying. More than just an ordinary CIA agent, he’s the living personification of America’s efforts to destabilize foreign governments across the 20th century. He was close with Patrice Lumumba until the day the Congolese leader was executed by a firing squad. He was in Cuba to spur on the exiles who failed to overthrow Fidel Castro in 1961. He was even in Dallas, USA on a Friday afternoon in November 1963, doing things his ornery boss MJ (Steve Buscemi) would much prefer he didn’t tell you about.

He traveled to all of those places, and got all of those people killed, with the blithe imperiousness of a flag waving in the wind. Chris says that he’s killed 68 people over the course of his career, and he offers that information as easily as a rumor — in a way that makes it sound like the number could be half that, or double, and it wouldn’t make a difference.

Recently, fate has blown Chris and his office husband/platonic life partner/exasperated straightman Lee (McDonagh vet Sam Rockwell) down south to Chile, where the two of them have been hard at work orchestrating the September 11th coup against the democratically elected socialist president, knowing full well that it would plunge the country into decades of brutal dictatorship. Chris appears to delight in sowing chaos, and the fact that he gets to wear a cute little Panama hat while he does it only boosts his joie de vivre.

But Chris is fraying at the seams in ways that we can’t see at first — ways that will only begin to reveal themselves when he and Lee decide to celebrate their imminent success with a sightseeing trip to Easter Island, a short 2,300 miles off the Western coast of Santiago. There are still a couple of months to go before the coup, but the wheels are in motion, and there’s little for these men to do for now besides fly to Rapa Nui, look at the “big heads,” flirt with a couple of beautiful young leftists (the prissier but gentler of whom is played by “Ema” mega-talent Mariana di Girolamo), and mordantly reflect upon their role in the awfulness Pinochet will soon inflict upon his own people. That task is complicated and confused by the fact that Chris seems to be losing his memory, which casts Lee in the role of his peevish but loving caretaker.

“Wild Horse Nine” is only the second feature that’s ever been shot on the ultra-remote soil of Rapa Nui (Kevin Reynolds’ 1994 epic “Rapa Nui” is credited as the first), and McDonagh rightly exalts in the fact that he’s brought Hollywood to the end of the world. The first half of his movie largely adheres to the fiction that Chris and Lee are just on Easter Island to fuck around, which offers a semi-relaxed opportunity for us to take in the sights. Ben Davis’ sun-bleached cinematography renders the hills and beaches of Rapa Nui with the veneer of a lost idyll, the ancient moai statues assuming an especially sacred charge when contrasted against such profane characters.

Chris bemusedly laughs the statues off with ethnographic glee, mocking one of the shorter figures as a dwarf (in case the “In Bruges” déjà vu wasn’t already strong enough), but it’s clear that he’s shaken by the fact that some of them almost 750 years old. That they’ve stood there in silent tribute to Polynesia’s ancestors as empires rose and fell on faraway shores, each of them a graveyard of forgotten people just like him. By the same token, it’s similarly clear that he’s trying to get at something in his own mind as he and Lee listen to Judy Collins in their rental car, argue over whether or not any Nazis made it into heaven (among other even more aggressively screen-written subjects), and whisper sweet nothings to the wild horses that run about the island, but the specific nature of that something is hard for him to pin down.

Malkovich has the time of his life with that search for Chris’ last speck of humanity, which shines up from the bottom of his soul as his mind continues to erode. At once both insidiously relaxed and also choked with Catholic guilt, Malkovich’s performance marries the nonchalance of a cowboy to the paranoia of a bounty target. One minute he’s waxing poetic about dinosaurs, and the next he’s rigging his luggage with explosives (maybe) and checking the “Memento”-like reminder notes he’s slipped into his jacket pocket.

“Wild Horse Nine” loves Chris too much to pretend like we’re not supposed to, but Malkovich never asks for our sympathy because he knows that he’ll get it anyway, and not just because the purpose of Rockwell’s character is to ensure that we do. It’s Chris’ job to make rooting for the bad guys feel like the right choice. It’s the cornerstone of America’s post-war foreign policy.

So while Chris might be cut from the same cloth as John Wayne (to the extent that Monument Valley is revealed to be a place of great importance to him), his arc is aimed in the opposite direction of the one that Ethan Edwards traces across “The Searchers”; Chris is a major asshole this movie gradually puckers into a wayward hero, and while it’s all too easy to root for him to complete that transformation before time runs out, at no point does “Wild Horse Nine” sell us on the premise that it would mean anything in the end — even to him.

The movie, blessedly, isn’t Catholic enough to offer Chris a big moment of divine expiation; in lieu of building to a grand confession, he and Lee simply run afoul of their CIA higher-ups until their PTO turns into a matter of American national security. Guns are fired. Hostages are taken. The word “fuck” is used like a punctuation mark, and regrets are muttered into the wind long after there’s any way for them to matter.

It all gets a bit too busy and madcap as “Wild Horse Nine” — freighted with sadness during even its cheekiest bits, as is McDonagh’s way — scrambles to make up for the general listlessness of its first half. Never as funny as it is sardonic, and always reaching so hard to be more of both, the film’s most successful moments are mined from the ambient tension between memory and erasure.

Chris, we begin to suspect, would do anything to forget what he’s done, and his own mind is graciously willing to get him halfway there. The rest of the CIA will do anything it takes to make sure that Chris doesn’t share whatever it is that he still remembers. Surrounding them on all sides are statues that predated America by several hundred years, and will likely continue to watch over Rapa Nui long after our empire has been wiped away like a tear in the rain. In “Wild Horse Nine,” American interventionism is less effective as the set-up for some jokes than it is as their punchline.

If you're thinking of picking up any big-ticket items soon—whether for yourself or as a gift for someone on your list— I would advise you to wait until you see what retailers have in store for the holiday sales season, which will kick off in earnest in mid-November. Walmart's Cyber Monday Deals Event: Nov. 30-Dec. 2

On Nov. 5, 2002, NASA's old Galileo spacecraft was almost out of fuel. But the Jupiter mission still had some science to do. Galileo flew by a small moon called Amalthea. Amalthea is small, but it's interesting for scientists.

The Jupiter moon Amalthea is the reddest object in our solar system. It also generates more heat than it gets from the sun. Scientists think Amalthea creates electrical currents as it orbits in Jupiter's huge magnetic field. Near this little moon, strong radiation from Jupiter is dangerous for spacecraft.

The flyby caused some problems for Galileo. The spacecraft went into safe mode only 30 minutes after going past Amalthea. NASA said it was ready for problems, because Galileo was on its last legs, and the radiation near Amalthea was strong. However, NASA quickly fixed the problem and got Galileo working again.

Less than a year later, Galileo threw itself into Jupiter's atmosphere. The spacecraft was crushed by the high pressure in Jupiter's atmosphere, but its data still survives on Earth.

The Galileo spacecraft was running on cosmic fumes by the time it reached Amalthea and the flyby would be the last time the probe would ever swing by one of Jupiter's moons.

While it may not look like it in Galileo's black and white images above and below, Amalthea is tinted red due sulfur contamination on its surface. The moon is small, about 168 miles long, and orbits about 112,468 miles (181,000 km) from Jupiter (much closer to the planet than the Earth and its moon).

Galileo flew within 99 miles of Amalthea during its flyby, which also marked the closest Galileo would get to Jupiter before its death dive in September 2005.

"Scientists studying data from NASA's Galileo spacecraft have found that Jupiter's moon Amalthea is a pile of icy rubble less dense than water," NASA announced in May 2005

"Amalthea is throwing us a curve ball," Torrence Johnson, a study co-author on the Almathea flyby and project scientist for the Galileo mission at JPL, said in 2005. "Its density is well below that of water ice, and even with substantial porosity, Amalthea probably contains a lot of water ice, as well as rock."

Originally known as Jupiter V (because it was the fifth Jupiter moon discovered after the so-called Galilean moons Io, Europa, Ganymede, Callisto), Amalthea was named after naiad, or water nymph, from Greek mythology.

It is fitting that Amalthea was the last of Jupiter's moons to be visited by the Galileo spacecraft. The moon was also last Jupiter moon to be discovered by visual observations when it was first disovered by astronomer Edward Emerson Barnard in September 1892 using the Lick Observatory telescope in California.

The Galileo spacecraft ultimately crashed into Jupiter on Sept. 21, 2005, less than a year after its Amalthea flyby.

After more than a decade of waiting, gamers will finally get to play Grand Theft Auto VI in just a few weeks. GTA 6 officially comes out on Nov. 19, 2026.

However, that's still too far away for some. GTA fans want to play the game now, or at least as soon as possible. So, when is that? Is there early access for GTA 6?

If you're hoping to be among the first, unfortunately, it's too late for that. Some lucky video game reviewers are reportedly already playing the game in anticipation of their coverage being published alongside its release. The embargo for GTA 6 reviews lifts one day before the release date, on Nov. 18. Some reviewers received their codes late last month, and more are going out to gaming and tech news outlets now and over the next couple of weeks.

What about the average consumer? How can they play the game at the earliest possible date?

Unfortunately, there is no early access for GTA 6. However, if you pre-order the digital version of the game, you'll be able to download GTA 6 on Nov. 10, a full 9 days before its release.

Now, the pre-order won't let you play the game on Nov. 10. The game will remain locked until Nov. 19. But GTA 6 is a massive download. It's reportedly 212 GB. That could take hours to download and install based on your internet connection. By pre-ordering, the game will be ready to play on your gaming console at midnight local time on Nov. 19, giving you the ability to jump into the game the moment it's officially released.

Still not good enough for you? What if you could trick your console into thinking you're in a country where the GTA 6 release comes roughly 18 hours earlier? Well, you can. Change your console region settings to New Zealand and connect to the internet through a VPN, and you'll be able to play GTA 6 on Nov. 18.

Or you can always hop on a flight and take a trip to New Zealand yourself.

Ex-Man United star Clayton Blackmore is forced to apologise for encouraging Mason Greenwood to 'come home to us' after winger beat Liverpool with Fenerbahce

📅 2026-11-05  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

By BEN WILLCOCKS, ASSISTANT SPORTS EDITOR

Published: 13:06 EDT, 5 November 2026 | Updated: 13:28 EDT, 5 November 2026

Former Manchester United footballer Clayton Blackmore has apologised after being criticised on social media for asking Mason Greenwood to 'come home' to Old Trafford.

Greenwood, 25, played a pivotal role for Fenerbahce during their 3-2 victory over Manchester United's archrivals Liverpool in the Champions League on Wednesday, scoring the Turkish giants' first-half equaliser.

The English forward broke through the ranks as a youngster at Manchester United and looked destined to become a key long-term player, but his career spiralled after he was arrested and charged in 2022 with suspected attempted rape, controlling and coercive behaviour and assault causing actual bodily harm.

Although those charges were subsequently dropped by the Crown Prosecution Service in February 2023, United decided Greenwood could not continue to ply his trade at Old Trafford after undertaking an internal six-month investigation.

Since his exit, which came after disgruntled Manchester United supporters groups heaped pressure on the club to cut ties with the forward, Greenwood has represented Spanish club Getafe on loan, French giants Marseille and Turkish side Fenerbahce, who he joined this summer for a reported €39million (£33.27million).

Blackmore, who played for Manchester United between 1982 and 1994, was met with backlash on Thursday after he heaped praise on Greenwood for his role in Fenerbahce's 3-2 win over Liverpool.

Mason Greenwood (centre) celebrates after scoring in Fenerbahce's 3-2 win over Liverpool

The 62-year-old Welshman apologised after his post was met with a furious backlash online

'Well done Mason Greenwood beating Liverpool. Come home to us please,' the Welshman, 62, posted on X.

Two hours later, after receiving a slew of disparaging comments on the platform, he said: 'Sorry I've offended anyone but I heard he had been cleared of any wrongdoing I apologise to everyone I've upset with my comments like I said I thought he had been cleared of everything and he’s had a baby with his girlfriend. Big sorry.'

Blackmore had previously come under fire for expressing his sadness that both Greenwood and Angel Gomes, another former English prodigy who was sold to Lille by Manchester United in 2020, no longer play for the club.

When the duo were both playing at Marseille last season, Blackmore said on X: 'Good to see Angel Gomez and Mason Greenwood score Marseille in first 26 mins. Sad they're not still at United.'

After landing himself in hot water with the post, he clarified to those commenting: 'Sorry I understand what you're saying but sometimes everything you hear is not always the truth why did his girlfriend stay with him I'm just looking at the football side.'

He later doubled down and wrote: 'Sorry didn't see or hear any of that I will do my home work next time just looking at it from the football side sorry if I've offended anyone especially his girlfriend and her family.'

While there was noisy resistance to Greenwood joining Marseille in the summer of 2025, he found no such opposition in Turkey, where one prominent journalist claimed 95 per cent of the population would not know or care about his past.

He didn't face any questions about the dropped charges or his public image in his first press conference and outside of brief post-match comments, has not conducted any interviews of note.

Greenwood has been hiding from the limelight a record signing would usually bask in, especially in the football-crazed city of Istanbul.

He found a home in a district called Sarıyer, a favourite among footballers, who largely live in gated communities.

Greenwood is said to count team-mates N'Golo Kante and Marco Asensio as neighbours and was seen in a rare online post after securing his new home, posing with the all-female team of estate agents who helped him with the purchase.

The former United starlet completed his move to Turkish giants Fenerbahce in the summer

Since the big-money move, Greenwood has only posted a handful of times on his Instagram profile, all of which were generic action shots after matches.

His latest offering came after Fenerbahce's stunning win over Liverpool, as he posted a gallery of photographs celebrating in the club's yellow and black jersey.

His partner, Harriet, 26, is also selective with how much she shares from their new life.

She only has four posts in total, with around 20 photos in each 'round-up', offering a glimpse into the high-life, though Greenwood himself only appears twice.

One of the images is of him looking exhausted in hospital at the birth of their second daughter in 2023 and another shows him holding hands with their eldest, Summer, who is three.
