Dan Campbell provides promising update after Kerby Joseph’s 1st game back

📅 Nov 23, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Last week, Detroit Lions coach Dan Campbell said the real tell about Kerby Joseph's return to the lineup will be how he feels after the game. The knee injury that held him out for over a year wasn't going to be as much of a problem on Sunday as it may be after playing on it for three hours.

But Campbell provided some great news on Monday. The Lions coach said Joseph came out of Sunday's win over the Tampa Bay Buccaneers with no cause for concern the day after.

"I think pretty good here. There's no red flags," Campbell said. "So that's a good sign."

Joseph played 46 defensive snaps on Sunday as part of Detroit's three-safety looks vs. the Bucs. He regularly manned the deep section of the field, while Brian Branch dropped down to nickel, and veteran Chuck Clark roamed the entirety of the field. While Joseph finished with just two tackles, Campbell said there were ups and downs to his afternoon.

"He'll be better for those reps, just like Branch was, to get his first reps of the season," Campbell said. "So, I thought he did some good things. There are things that he's going to only just get better at now that he's been out there. I thought it was pretty good. When you're able to get he and BB on the field and you've got Chuck out there, it's a pretty good unit."

After the game, Joseph told reporters he felt no discomfort in his knee for the entirety of the game.

"Every time I come out here, I feel like myself," Joseph said. "I don't know why you keep asking that. I'm going to always be me. I'm going to forever be me."

At this point, it appears Joseph is on track to play on a short week against the Chicago Bears, but we'll know more when the team drops their first injury report of the week on Monday. Detroit is only expected to conduct walkthroughs this week, so participation levels will be estimated.

The 5 Most Unwatchable James Bond Movies, Ranked

📅 Nov 23, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

The James Bond franchise has long stood as one of cinema’s most defining blockbuster titles. From the outset, way back when it started with Sean Connery in the lead role in 1962’s Dr. No, it has epitomized the suave spectacle and sophisticated charm of high-octane spy thrills. Ranging from Goldfinger to Skyfall, the saga has delivered some of the most exhilarating spy-action movies ever made, but any franchise that features 25 movies is bound to have a few misfires and, unfortunately, 007 is no different.

When the franchise is at its worst, it reduces its tropes to being crude caricatures of the genre. The gadgets become goofy, the Bond girl an affront to any form of progressive femininity, and the story often suffers most under the strain of forced stylistic choices, laughably cartoonish action sequences, and a sense of antagonism that is utterly ridiculous. Ranging from early errs that were painfully anachronistic even when they were released to more modern missteps, these blundering Bond movies represent cinema’s greatest franchise at its unwatchable worst.

Released 12 years after Connery's last Bond outing—1971’s Diamonds Are Forever—and marking just the second and latest non-Eon-produced Bond movie ever made, Never Say Never Again emerged amid a time of chaos and calamity for the franchise. Roger Moore’s ongoing tenure as the official 007 had begun to falter, and while his worst movies mark some of the franchise’s lowest points, they are made watchable through a sense of characterful campiness and, in some cases, a certain so-bad-it’s-good appeal. Neither of those attributes can be applied to Never Say Never Again, which sees a 53-year-old Connery return to the role to deliver what is effectively a tame rehash of Thunderball.

The lack of originality in the picture is only worsened by the fundamental Bond staples it was deprived of. Given it wasn’t an Eon Productions project, it never had access to the iconic Bond theme, the opening gun-barrel shot, or John Barry’s signature score. Without these qualities, the film lands with a certain flatness. It lacks authentic action-thriller energy, and its efforts to at least modernize the character by implementing ‘80s trends like arcade-style dueling only make it all the more outdated today. It isn’t the worst or most egregious Bond movie ever made, but it is among the most banal, and that pervasive sense of muted mediocrity makes it an unwatchable snoozefest in 2026.

Quantum of Solace is as bland and banal a Bond movie as has ever been made. The follow-up to Daniel Craig’s dashing debut in Casino Royale undoubtedly fell victim to the 2007/08 writer’s strike in Hollywood, making it indistinguishable from any run-of-the-mill, B-grade spy thriller. It lacks the ruthless hard-edged grunt of its predecessor, the snappy stylistic flourishes of its successor, and refuses to implement anything in the way of camp charm or thematic prowess. Even the villain’s environmental slant is ill-explored, making him an odd and forgettable concoction of timely ideals and traditional egomaniacal Bond villainy.

It is ultimately a Bond movie that lacks an identity. It doesn’t strive to be anything unique, refusing to stretch beyond the safe limitations of the franchise at its most middling and mediocre. To say it is an outright terrible film would perhaps be going too far, it just stands as a flick that has no aspiration of what it wants to be. Again, the writer’s strike of the time was definitely a limiting factor on what Quantum of Solace could achieve, but for a Bond movie to be plain boring is unforgivable. Even many of the worst installments of the franchise have their goofy eccentricities that at least give them something, but Quantum of Solace lacks anything memorable, and it derides viewers of any reason to want to rewatch it.

Most, if not all, of the original Connery Bond films have elements that haven’t aged all too well. In the best of them, it’s easily dismissed as a sign of the times, but in the lesser entries of his tenure as 007, it’s a bit more grating to watch. His fifth outing, You Only Live Twice, is the epitome of this notion. It lacks the pizzazz and pop of Connery’s earlier, more iconic movies, and it seems as though Connery himself knows it more than anyone. His performance is uncharacteristically wooden, a stilted cutout of a character where his charisma and quick-wit ought to be.

Bereft of Connery’s best work, the film struggles to apply wallpaper over the narrative cracks and biting insensitivities. Roald Dahl’s screenplay leans heavily on racial stereotypes—an idea that reaches an ugly crescendo with Bond’s infamous “yellowface” disguise as a Japanese man—and his endeavor to up the ante comes at an emotional cost. The movie is a big, drab blockbuster with no feeling, powered by senseless villainy and over-the-top theatrics. Upon release, You Only Live Once was a limp entry to the franchise, but today it is a bitterly hard-to-watch mishit that serves as a painstaking procession of every nasty trope and tendency the original Bond pictures had.

2006’s Casino Royale is a true masterpiece of action cinema, slick sophistication, and gripping high-stakes drama that finds the perfect balance between exhilarating set piece and character-charged intensity. The 1967 interpretation of the Bond story, however, is not. The unlicensed Bond adaptation follows a retired 007 (David Niven) as he is forced to re-enter the field to bring down the evil international crime faction SMERSH, which has been behind a recent series of deadly attacks on MI6 operatives. In an effort to mislead the criminal syndicate, several others also pose as agent 007, including baccarat master Evelyn Tremble (Peter Sellers) and Bond’s own former secretary, Miss Moneypenny (Barbara Bouchet).

The idea of lampooning the spy genre was good in theory, but the execution of 1967’s Casino Royale leaves much to be desired. Its story is a chaotic mess that is completely incomprehensible and never comes close to filling the film’s bloated 131-minute runtime, while on-set conflicts saw the movie pulled in several different directions, making the final product intangible. Even a supporting cast including Orson Welles, Woody Allan, and original Bond girl Ursula Andress are incapable of elevating the film beyond its pitiful nature. It was admonished by the masses upon release, and it has only gotten worse over the years.

The Bond franchise has had its fair share of mishits over the decades, but none have been quite so disastrous and agonizing as 2002’s Die Another Day. It marked an unfortunate end to Pierce Brosnan’s tenure as 007, which started out so well with GoldenEye giving a clear intent of suave style and gritty action. Three films later, the Bond saga had regressed to uninspiring uncertainty. Die Another Day is an awkward, disjointed conglomerate of the litany of Bond styles that came before it, and every tonal shift and plot twist it aspires for falls flat on its face.

The opening sequence has a certain gruffness to it, with Bond being tortured by the North Korean army over the course of 14 months, but whatever intrigue that hard-edged intro may have had immediately dissipates as the movie pivots to cringe-worthy campiness. Not only is Die Another Day’s narrative tempo off beat, its reliance on appalling CGI and ridiculous concepts to carry some of its action sequences is enough to leave viewers in hysterics. It marks an undisputed low-point for the franchise, a painful depiction of 007 at its absolute worst. The only saving grace of the movie is that it was so bad it forced producers to take an entirely new approach four years later when the saga was re-ignited with Casino Royale.

An analysis of subreddits flagged most users weighing in on AEO tools as likely promoters. Among the rest, accuracy was the top feature complaint.

Graphite flagged 55% of the users who gave an opinion of an AEO tool across 54 subreddits this year as likely working for or promoting a marketing software product.

Among the users it didn’t flag, the accuracy of visibility scores drew more unfavorable opinions than any other product feature.

The growth agency picked subreddits where AEO comes up regularly and found 5,990 posts and 25,392 comments from January through September that mention a tool. OpenAI language models labeled the mentions, the opinions and the likely promoters.

The agency helps companies with AEO and SEO and says it doesn’t sell AEO tools or accept payment to be mentioned. Its CEO, Ethan Smith, is an investor in Peec AI, one of three tools tied for the highest score in the analysis.

Graphite flagged 1,298 of the 2,365 Reddit users who gave an opinion of an AEO tool or a marketing platform’s AEO features.

A user was flagged when the agency’s model put the chance at 50% or more that their posts promoted a marketing product they build, own, work for, sell or partner with. That includes SEO, AEO, sales and web analytics tools. An agency’s description of its own client work did not constitute promotion.

To test the model, the company manually inspected 43 of the users it had flagged. Ultimately, 12% of them disclosed being promoters, while 62% were found to have promoted a product but failed to identify themselves as such; the remaining ones were judged non-promoters. The write-up also says that some of the models’ labeling decisions could be incorrect.

Four types of likely promoter Reddit accounts were identified. Each type exhibited a pattern across several posts or comments.

One account was used to recommend the same lesser-known tracking service on eight posts and comments. In January this user claimed that they switched from a competitor, and 11 days later they commented about possibly purchasing the product.

Another account made reference to a little-known product in four comments with well-known tracking services over the course of eight days.

A third account posted three separate times for the same product within 12 minutes.

Over six weeks, 38 accounts recommended the same product in 52 comments, each using the phrase “from what I’ve read” in its recommendation. That phrase appears with any other tool no more than twice.

Of the opinions regarding the accuracy of AEO tools from users who weren’t flagged, 143 were negative while 27 had positive views. No other feature elicited as many negative opinions. These users provided opinions on 44 tools, for a total of 1,557 opinions.

Graphite’s chart compares praise and criticism across features after excluding opinions from users it flagged as likely promoters.

According to the write-up, users frequently view visibility scores as estimates due to variation in AI answers among different runs, locations and users. Several have advised fellow users to “treat them as trend, not truth” and to verify the information through manual review.

The citation data, or the citations used by AI systems as source material for their answers, was generally viewed positively by users. Price received more user opinions than any other attribute and most of those opinions were negative, at 182 negative versus 98 positive.

The agency also evaluated the 11 tools which generated sufficient opinions using a 0-100 point scale; a score of 50 represented neutral. Each of the 11 tools scored between 54 and 66 points. The authors also said the ranges around many of the tools’ scores overlapped and therefore, the differences in scores could lack statistical significance.

Graphite’s count puts a number on how many Reddit users weighing in on AEO tools may be promoting marketing software. Before a tool recommended on Reddit goes on your shortlist, check the account’s history for repeat plugs and a story that changes from post to post.

Graphite’s tool scores reflect opinions from January through September, and the company says their order could change as more opinions come in. Legendary Critical Role Cast Members Officially Return With a Star-Studded New Guest Cast

📅 Nov 23, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Aabria Iyengar and Travis Willingham return in Campaign 4

It’s hard to believe the entire Critical Role table for Campaign 4 hasn’t been together since the Convergence arc in June. Since then, the Soldiers, Seekers, and Schemers tables have remained separate. Or, more accurately, the Seekers and Schemers tables have remained separate.

Since Critical Role Campaign 4 episode 31, "Trick of the Light," Critters haven’t seen hide nor hair of everyone’s favorite orc Druid, Thaisha Lloy. Nor have they received any confirmation about when, exactly, Travis Willingham will return with a new character, after his Paladin, Teor Pridesire, met an unfortunate end in episode 30, "Here in the Dark."

Luckily, the wait is finally over. Critical Role announced on Nov. 23 that its eagerly anticipated Soldiers table, featuring Aabria Iyengar as Thaisha, will return on Dec. 3 and Dec. 10 for a two-part Soldiers table arc. And she won’t be alone.

When the three tables split off in June last year, it seemed that the Soldiers table would consist only of Thaisha. In an interview with Polygon, Iyengar confirmed she wasn't sure at the time whether Thaisha’s story would be told through cold openings or off-camera. Later, however, the Critical Role team decided to give the Soldiers table a mini-arc following Thaisha as she explored her future on the druidic path. But she would not be doing all of that on her own.

“We’ve got some amazing guests, Celia Rose Gooding and Abubakar Salim," Iyengar told Polygon. "Two people who I have admired for so long; it was so fun to finally sit down and play with them. And, of course, the return of Travis Willingham and his new PC.”

Willingham has been absent from Critical Role’s Campaign 4 since his katari Paladin, Teor Pridesire, died in episode 30. Teor was killed inside House Tachonis’s Obrimus Manor during a truly heartbreaking moment for the fans and the cast.

Gooding and Salim have both guest-starred on Critical Role before: Gooding appeared in Exandria Unlimited: Divergence as the half-elf Rei’nia Saph, while Salim has played various roles, most recently the fungril Berserker Mercy in Age of Umbra: Sallowlands.

Though Iyengar couldn’t reveal what characters Willingham and the other guests would play in this two-episode arc, she told Polygon they were all “deeply connected and bound by a similar purpose.”

“Dol-Runja is the seat of the orcs and the druids in Kahan. So it's important and interesting to see multiple facets of that.”

Since June, the Seekers and Schemers tables have been dealing with assassination attempts at the theater, watery heists, and literal hell. When we last checked in, the Seekers table — with the help of several NPCs like fey pixie Clauny the Rose, talking dog Dame Morgaine, and scoundrel Pimley Morn — successfully rescued their ally turned into stone, Ulbid Morn.

On Dec. 3, Critters will reunite with Thaisha, who set off for the city of Dol-Rungja. There, she hopes to speak with the Druidic people and persuade them to abandon their neutrality and help fight back against the Sundered Houses.

Whether that will work out for Thaisha remains to be seen. The last time she encountered other druids, a few of them weren’t too pleased with her role in bringing the Necromancer Wizard, Occtis Tachonis (Alexander Ward), back to life as a Hollowed One. Given that she’ll have to answer for it to very important people, including her grandma, we expect the orc druid to be put through the proverbial wringer over the course of her two-episode arc.

Aabria Iyengar, Travis Willingham, and guest stars Celia Rose Gooding and Abubakar Salim begin their Soldiers adventure on Dec. 3.

Fourth Circuit Upholds Virginia's Law Terminating Agreements to Help With Immigration Enforcement - but Strikes Down anti-Mask Law

📅 Nov 23, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Mostly law professors | Sometimes contrarian | Often libertarian | Always independent

The ruling on Section 287 agreements overturns an earlier district court decision.

In September, I wrote about the the federal district court decision striking down Virginia's new law terminating Section 287(g) agreements, under which various state and local law enforcement agencies agree to help with federal immigration enforcement. Today, in United States v. Virginia, the US Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit reversed that decision. But, at the same time, in a 2-1 ruling, they struck down Virginia's law barring law enforcement officers (including federal ones) from wearing masks except in relatively narrowly specified circumstances. Here is an excerpt from the court's opinion (written by Judge Heytens) on the 287(g) issue:

We vacate the preliminary injunction that bars enforcing the 287(g) Law with respect to existing agreements. The district court concluded the Federal Government was likely to succeed on the merits because rendering such agreements unenforceable violate both the intergovernmental immunity doctrine and the Contracts Clause. We disagree. The intergovernmental immunity doctrine does not apply because the 287(g) Law neither directly regulates the Federal Government nor prevents private parties from contracting with it…. The 287(g) Law also does not violate the Contracts Clause for two separate reasons: (1) because the existing agreements expressly permit unilateral termination by the non-federal party; and (2) even if they did not, the Commonwealth would have constitutionally valid justifications for requiring state and local officials to terminate or renegotiate any such agreements.

Later in the opinion Judge Heytens expands on why Section 287(g) termination does not violate the Contracts Clause:

We conclude the Federal Government cannot satisfy that test for two different, yet complementary, reasons. First, all the agreements covered by the district court's injunction expressly permit the Commonwealth's agents to unilaterally terminate without cause, which means the 287(g) Law does not "substantially impair" (Sveen, 584 U.S. at 819) a contractual relationship at Step 1. Second, even if the agreements did not so provide, neither the Commonwealth's agencies nor those of its localities may contract away "an essential attribute of [the Commonwealth's] sovereignty." United States Tr. Co. of N.Y. v. New Jersey, 431 U.S. 1, 23 (1977). Here, that attribute is the Commonwealth's power to decide whether and on what terms to assist with the ongoing enforcement of federal law.

I largely agree. In my post on the district court decision, I offered some additional reasons why terminating these agreements doesn't violate the Contracts Clause.

The anti-mask law presents more difficult issues because - as the majority emphasizes - it directly regulates federal law enforcement officials. On the other hand, the law imposes the same constraints on state and local officers, as well, and therefore doesn't discriminate against them. On balance, I agree with Judge Robert Bruce King's dissent:

At bottom, a single ICE officer should never be able to override a duly enacted statute of the Commonwealth of Virginia in the absence of some federal law, regulation, or other valid supporting authority. Consider what purportedly conflicts with the Mask/Identity Statute. A federal statute? None. A treaty of the United States? None. A federal regulation or executive order? None. A written policy located anywhere within ICE or the Department of Homeland Security? None has been identified. What we have today is a single sentence in a Declaration asserting that individual ICE officers possess the discretion and ability to conceal their identities when they alone deem it appropriate. No supervision is at all necessary or required and the agency itself has no involvement in the ICE officer's daily decisions on whether to wear a mask. I cannot and will not accept the proposition that individualized discretion, untethered to any identifiable source of federal law, enjoys constitutional supremacy over a duly enacted statute of the Commonwealth.

As the majority recognizes, federal law enforcement officers are not generally exempt from state law while on the job. For example, they must obey state traffic laws when driving their vehicles. A valid federal law could override a conflicting state law. But here, there is no such overriding federal law, or even a regulation (though I'm not convinced the latter alone would be enough). Just the preferences of ICE agents. Those preferences are not enough to secure an exemption from state law.  Federal law (when constitutionally valid) is supreme over state law. The preferences of individual federal officials or agencies are not.

Your home may be full of wildlife you’ve never noticed

📅 Nov 23, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

A survey of indoor spaces across the U.S. and Canada recorded nearly 2,100 species, from familiar house spiders to an alligator in a garage.

Homes across the United States and Canada are full of wildlife.

A continent-wide survey has found nearly 2,100 species of wild organisms living indoors, mostly insects and spiders, plus centipedes, snails, fungi, and many animals most people have never heard of.

One record is an alligator that turned up in somebody’s garage.

This is the largest study of indoor wildlife so far. Nearly all of it is built from photos taken by ordinary people.

The study was led by researchers at North Carolina State University and Colorado State University.

Over 60 co-authors contributed, including museum curators, university entomologists, and independent experts from across North America and Europe.

Ecologists have spent decades cataloging life in rainforest canopies and on the deep sea floor. The places where most of us spend most of our time have received far less attention.

“While researchers have focused significant attention on a variety of natural ecosystems – from rainforest canopies to the deep sea – we know remarkably little about the ecosystems of manmade, indoor environments,” said lead author Bradley Allf from Colorado State.

“Our goal with this work was to treat the indoors, at the continental scale, as an ecosystem that we wanted to explore as ecologists,” he explained.

Earlier surveys of indoor life either covered much smaller areas or relied on DNA traces, which can’t always pin down exactly which species was present.

This is the first survey of indoor life spanning the United States and Canada.

The team launched a citizen science project called Never Home Alone on iNaturalist, a website where people share photos of the living things they come across.

Participants were asked to photograph wild organisms in their homes, offices, and other indoor spaces. The rule for “wild” was simple: anything that was neither a pet nor a potted plant.

Submissions came from at least five continents. This study focuses on North America.

About 3,000 people contributed 23,782 observations, from every US state and every Canadian province. Most were taken in homes, but some came from offices, schools, and airports. The records cover almost three decades, from 1996 to 2024.

Every observation was checked by specialists. Dozens of experts identified each organism as precisely as they could.

“This work underscores the value of natural history expertise and the ability to identify species, which is a skillset that is less common among biologists than it once was,” Allf said.

About 95% of the observations were arthropods, the group of animals with jointed legs and hard outer skeletons. Insects made up 60% of all records, mostly beetles, flies, moths, wasps and ants, and true bugs.

Spiders accounted for 30%. Animals with backbones made up only 4%, most of them reptiles and mammals.

The rest included fungi, plants, earthworms, snails, small crustaceans, and even single-celled organisms.

Eight species were recorded more than 200 times each.

In order, they were the Asian lady beetle, the brown marmorated stink bug, the house centipede, the varied carpet beetle, the long-bodied cellar spider, the triangulate combfoot spider, the bathroom moth fly, and the eastern parson spider.

“The organisms we found ranged from Asian lady beetles – which were the most commonly found species – to an alligator that unexpectedly turned up in one person’s garage,” Allf said.

“But one of the things that was striking was how common predator species were – including hundreds of different spider species.”

Half of the top eight are hunters, and some of them do useful work.

“One study participant noted that they view jumping spiders as working animals, because the spiders play an active role in reducing the population of flies and other pests that they don’t want in their home.”

Senior author Rob Dunn from NC State pointed out that most people share their homes with these animals every day and have no idea what they are.

“Another thing that is striking about this work is that most people could look over a list of the most commonly found species and wouldn’t know what most of those animals even are – and yet they are around us, all the time,” he said.

“For example, I suspect most people are unfamiliar with comb-footed spiders, but we have more than a thousand observations of this family of spiders.”

“This is a stark departure from what we would have expected historically, when our ancestors would have been more aware of the living things around them.”

“That’s something we lost. And this is a first step toward regaining that understanding.”

The list has a very long tail. About 52% of the species were recorded indoors just once.

The true total is almost certainly higher than 2,100, because 44% of the observations couldn’t be identified down to species.

The authors also point out the dataset’s limits. Nobody controlled how hard each person looked, so large, eye-catching animals are probably overrepresented.

And not every animal that ends up indoors actually lives there. Some had simply wandered in and become trapped.

The survey also shows how little is known about even the most common indoor animals.

“It could inform future research in many ways,” Dunn said.

“For example, house centipedes were the third most commonly found animals, and yet I’m not aware of anyone who is studying them. That’s worth exploring.”

Some of these animals might even turn out to be useful.

“For example, we now know that camel crickets carry a microbe that can break down paper products. That could be useful. What else might be out there, in our own homes?”

“Once we know what’s there, we can begin asking additional questions,” Dunn said.

“Why are these organisms there? What are they doing? Are they beneficial?”

“But knowing what’s there is the first step. And it’s exciting because these are the animals we’re around all the time, but we just overlook.”

The scientists want to investigate how the climate outside shapes what turns up inside. They also plan to figure out which species have actually adapted to life in buildings.

Every record has a date and a location, so the dataset can also serve as a baseline for spotting new arrivals or disappearances in the future. The data are free for anyone to use.

The project is still running, and anyone with a phone can contribute.

“People can still submit observations to Never Home Alone. The more data the project collects, the more insight we’ll have on the unique and growing ecosystem of the great indoors,” Allf concluded.

The study is published in the journal Ecology.

Finding Port Gelhorn's missing tourists and the serial killer in Grand Theft Auto 6 takes you on a twisted journey around the town, one with few guideposts and a lot of chances to get stuck. This is a quest where the storytelling, more than anything else, is the reward.

Below, we explain where to find Gelhorn's missing tourists in GTA 6 and complete the serial killer side mission.

You'll first come across this exploration mission in the Starlet Motel office after Chapter 2 starts. Lucia can examine a set of missing persons posters on the wall next to Linda's desk. There's a tip line with a number to call, but it just takes you to a voice mailbox.

And that's the only hint to go off of, as the poster info gives you no clues and nowhere to look. There might be other clues hidden somewhere around town, but we didn't find them. Instead, we stumbled across the quest's starting location by accident. It's an old bunker-like warehouse across the street from Moses' garage, next to the scooter store. You can approach the front for a prompt to open the door, but it won't work. Walk down the right side of the warehouse, and look for a hole at the base. Crawl inside.

Despite how creepy the location is, there's nothing inside to harm you, though there is a jump scare midway through. Enter the red room and the trophy room beyond it, and interact with the photos there. You'll see locations showing each of the missing tourists, and these photos are stored in your phone gallery if you want to view them later. The quest prompt changes in the map menu, too, with a new prompt for each photo.

You can track them in any order. We're just listing them in the order we found them. We recommend tackling this part of the quest during the day, just so it's easier to make out landmarks.

The junkyard is where you'll find Susanne Keeble, and it's next to the Pay 'n' Spray shop in central Gelhorn. You're looking for a pink car, and if you're having trouble finding it, activate focus mode. The car will stand out as the only interactable object in the area. Report the body to the tip line after you find it.

Donald Miller's body is out at Wild Hog Point, the marshes outside Port Gelhorn. Head toward the tree with no leaves near the center of the marsh, and look in the water nearby to find a car. Interact with it, report the body, and move on. Someone picks up the line this time.

Patrick Murray is the final tourist, and his body is in a trailer at the far north end of Port Gelhorn in an abandoned trailer park near a water tower. The trailer in question is the blue one. Head inside, and pull up the floorboards near the door to discover Murray's body. The clipping in the trophy room mentioned a reward for information about him, though, for reasons you'll soon discover, the tip line operator isn't ready to pay out.

The tip line man asks you to return to the warehouse after finding the third body, and the quest can change depending on whether you go alone or with your partner. We played as Jason and went with Lucia, though who takes the lead doesn't matter. Your partner stays outside, and tense events unfold inside, after which the culprit dies and the quest ends.
