Lamar Jackson's latest injury raises obvious questions about his future in Baltimore

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

With quarterback Lamar Jackson’s ninth NFL season possibly over after three full games and two first-half exits, obvious questions have emerged regarding his future in Baltimore.

The facts fuel the speculation. He’s under contract for one more season, and the Ravens can’t use the franchise tag or the transition tag to limit his ability to become an unrestricted free agent in March 2028, which is only 16 months away.

If Jackson wants a fresh start at age 31, he’ll be able to have one.

The Ravens made it clear in January that they wanted to extend his contract by the start of the new league year in March 2026. It didn’t happen, for reasons that never became clear because neither Jackson nor the Ravens (at Jackson’s behest) share information about the negotiations.

Before the season began, it seemed that a new contract wa just a matter of time. The Ravens surely would like to do it before the start of the new league year in March, when Jackson’s cap number spikes to $84.34 million. Although the team has the right to do another restructuring that would reduce the 2027 cap number, that would make the dead-cap reckoning even larger as of 2028.

A trade would avoid the final $52 million in cash and cap charges, but the cap realities would make a post-June 1 move the far better option. And there have been whispers of at least one team doing advance homework on a potential pursuit of Jackson in 2027.

The overriding question is whether Jackson’s lingering availability issue changes things for the Ravens. For now, there’s no reason to think it has. When the dust settles on the season, the Ravens will have to revisit that question.

But that will lead to another question. What would they do at quarterback without Lamar Jackson? That simple reality prompted them to give him a market-level deal in 2023, and to want to do it again.

As long as that desire to avoid a future without Lamar Jackson remains, there’s no reason to think he won’t be back in Baltimore in 2027.

The 10 Greatest Cozy Fantasy Books of All Time, Ranked

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

The term "cozy fantasy" as a recognized, distinct subgenre of fantasy was only coined around 2020, mostly driven by the desire for emotional escapism and comfy entertainment during the COVID-19 pandemic and subsequent global lockdown. However, its ancestors and most foundational roots can be traced much further back, retroactively making many great classics some of the best cozy fantasy titles in history.

Whether we're talking about one of those hyper-influential classics like The Hobbit, or about a modern gem like Emily Wilde’s Encyclopaedia of Faeries, one thing remains certain: for people who love fantasy as a whole, cozy fantasy can be a delightful blast. It's all about comforting, relatively low-stakes narratives, as well as emotional slice-of-life moments favored over epic wars or world-ending threats.

Written by Ursula Vernon under the pseudonym T. Kingfisher, A Wizard's Guide to Defensive Baking was a major critical and commercial success upon release, winning awards as prominent as the Locus Award for Young Adult Fiction. Its story follows Mona, a teenage baker witch who discovers a dead body in her aunt's shop and must use her powers to foil a city-wide conspiracy and defend her home.

It's one of the best fantasy books of the 2020s so far, and with its being published right at the peak of pandemic lockdowns, it was one of the first books to be labeled as cozy fantasy by social media as the term was being born. Striking the perfect balance between warm domestic comfort, comedic charm, and real emotional stakes, it's an unforgettable quirky YA novel that even older fantasy readers love.

Heather Fawcett's popular debut novel, Emily Wilde's Encyclopaedia of Faeries, launched the Emily Wilde series, one of the best cozy fantasy novel series to date. Its story follows the titular Emily Wilde, a grumpy Cambridge professor who travels to a remote sub-Arctic village to research elusive faeries, only to have her fieldwork upended by a charming academic rival who turns out to be a secret fae prince.

The book makes flawless use of the epistolary format, making its whimsical folklore elements feel all the more genuine and enhancing the warmth of its cozy atmosphere. It's dark academia meets cottagecore, with a delightfully smart protagonist and a wintry aesthetic that makes the heat of every fire, the hug of every wool blanket, and the steam of every mug of hot cider feel as comforting as the real things.

The first installment in Olivia Atwater's Regency Faerie Tales trilogy, the Regency romantasy delight Half a Soul is one of the best fantasy books published since 2020. Its story follows Dora, a girl cursed by a faerie to be unable to feel or express emotions after losing half her soul. So, she travels through London with a royal magician to reverse her condition and solve a magical plague.

At first, the book was little more than a modest self-published release, but the cozy fantasy phenomenon on BookTok during the pandemic lockdowns gradually turned it into a huge success, leading Orbit Books to acquire and publish it in 2022. It's just that good: with its uniquely compelling heroine and her fun dynamic with the Royal Sorcerer Elias, Half a Soul is a celebration of empathy, kindness, and whimsy.

Perfect for people who aren't really looking to commit to a new fantasy series and would rather just enjoy a standalone cozy fantasy title, Sangu Mandanna's The Very Secret Society of Irregular Witches should be a joy to read. It follows Mika Moon, a lonely witch who breaks the strict rules of her society by posting online magic videos. The videos lead her to being hired to teach three unruly orphan witches at a remote estate, where she unexpectedly finds family and love.

It's the ultimate "found family" cozy fantasy book, a novel that's just about as close as you can get to a proverbial warm hug on a cold, rainy night. Full of low-stakes domestic magic and imbued with a bright, colorful atmosphere that makes the whole story feel safe and inviting, it's a book all about the deeply relatable theme of overcoming isolation and finding a place where it feels like you belong.

The first book in T.J. Klune's Cerulean Chronicles is The House in the Cerulean Sea, a YA story about a government caseworker who oversees magical youth. He's sent to evaluate a remote island orphanage housing six classified children — including the Antichrist — where he unexpectedly finds a family and a home. As great as its sequel is, it doesn't get much cozier than this.

House in the Cerulean Sea is a deeply emotional tale about acceptance and found family, full of romance, low stakes, emotional warmth, and a gentle narrative. All in all, it's one of the best fantasy books of the past 10 years, built on the foundations of a protagonist whose redemption arc is as satisfying as it is moving. The magical children are irresistibly sweet, the romantic elements work wonderfully, and the message against prejudice and institutional oppression is profoundly powerful.

If there's any one author who has done more than enough to be worthy of the title of "best fantasy writer currently working," it's the infinitely prolific Brandon Sanderson. The first of the four secret novels that Sanderson wrote during the COVID-19 pandemic, Tress of the Emerald Sea is one of the author's best books to date, following a young girl who stows away on pirate ships across the ocean to rescue her beloved from an evil sorceress.

Though an epic adventure is what fuels the narrative of Tress of the Emerald Sea, it's nonetheless the coziest piece of fantasy that Sanderson has thus far written. Whimsical, stylish, and deeply comforting in spite of its epic narrative, this first installment in the Hoid's Travails series (part of Sanderson's overarching Cosmere universe) wears its cozy Princess Bride influences on its sleeve.

As far as modern books that were specifically written under the newly-coined "cozy fantasy" umbrella go, it doesn't get much better or more iconic than Travis Baldree's Legends & Lattes. A finalist for the 2023 Nebula and Hugo Awards, this is the story of a battle-weary orc retiring from her adventures and moving to the city of Thune, where she accomplishes her wish of opening a coffee shop.

It's the ultimate cozy fantasy premise, and the way it's executed is every bit as warm as the silky lattes served at Viv's café. Proof that you don't need sky-high stakes in order to craft a high fantasy story that's thoroughly engaging, Legends & Lattes is bolstered by Baldree's beautifully tactile and comforting prose, the heartwarming "found family" story, and the welcoming subversions of traditional Hero's Journey heroic fantasy tropes.

British writer Sir Terry Pratchett was one of the most beloved satirists in the modern history of English-language literature, revolutionizing comedic fantasy with his massive Discworld series. Going Postal is the 33rd book in the series, and it follows a condemned con artist who's spared from hanging and forced to revive the city's ruined post office, while battling a ruthless communication monopoly.

It's one of the most fun fantasy books of all time, and it's all anyone should need to read in order to understand why Pratchett is such a beloved figure—though it'll inevitably become tempting to pick up some other Discworld installment. Going Postal may predate the birth of the cozy fantasy trend, but its masterclass in satirical humor, quirky industrial fantasy, and cozy approach to sociopolitical critique make it an essential ancestor for the subgenre's modern form.

Diana Wynne Jones' Howl's Moving Castle achieved modest acclaim and success among children's fantasy fans and critics back in 1986, though it would be the release of Hayao Miyazaki's 2004 anime film adaptation that would really push its reputation into the stratosphere. Its story follows Sophie, an 18-year-old who's turned into an old woman by a witch. She takes refuge in the castle of a vain wizard named Howl, where she bargains with a fire demon to break their curses and untangle a web of magical chaos.

It's one of those perfect fantasy books that turned into even better movies, and another one of the most important and noteworthy precursors of the cozy fantasy subgenre. Long before the term was coined, Jones established this blueprint for whimsical domestic magic, eccentric found families, and reluctant yet undeniably witty heroes. From the vibrant characters to the titular castle itself, everything about Howl's Moving Castle inspires a joyful sense of awe.

Even though it was The Lord of the Rings that would go on to enshrine J. R. R. Tolkien as the single most important and influential fantasy author of the 20th century, it was the smash hit that was The Hobbit that put him on the map as a master of the genre. It's the tale of the hobbit Bilbo Baggins, who's recruited by a wizard and thirteen dwarves to embark on a dangerous quest.

Even though The Hobbit was written nearly a century before the coining of the "cozy fantasy" label, and some purists may be scandalized by the mere proposition of considering it the grandfather of all cozy fantasy tales, it's nevertheless a subgenre that fits it too well to ignore. One of the most perfect fantasy books of all time, The Hobbit's story may have danger and adventure, but it's also the ultimate ancestor of the cozy fantasy category. Bilbo as a home-loving reluctant hero, Bag End as the coziest space in all of fantasy, and a story that celebrates the power of friendship and hospitality all make this the most foundational pillar of cozy fantasy.

The interaction came a day after he produced an impressive 74 off just 51 balls against New Zealand.

Kohli's selections threw up a few surprises, with the batting great repeatedly favouring innings that might not immediately come to mind when recalling his most memorable performances.His first choice was his 35-run contribution in the 2011 ODI World Cup final against Sri Lanka. He preferred that knock over his unbeaten 133 against Sri Lanka in Hobart in 2012, when India chased down a target of 321 in just 40 overs. He also overlooked his 85 against Australia in the 2023 World Cup opener, an innings played when India were struggling at 2/3 while chasing 199.Kohli continued to back his World Cup final contribution when it was put up against his 122-run effort against England in 2017. That century came during a challenging chase of 351, with India reduced to 63/4. His unbeaten 160 against South Africa in Cape Town in 2018, where he scored 100 runs through running between the wickets, also failed to make the cut.However, when asked to choose between his 35 in the 2011 World Cup final and his performance in the 2013 Champions Trophy final against England, Kohli eventually picked the latter.The 43-run innings came in a rain-affected contest reduced to 20 overs, with India in trouble at 66/5. Kohli is the second-highest run-getter in the history of the format, with 15,183 runs from 318 matches and 306 innings at an average of 58.84. He has registered 55 centuries, a record in ODI cricket, along with 80 fifties.In T20 Internationals, he has scored 4,188 runs in 125 innings at an average of 48.69, including one century and 38 half-centuries. He ranks third on the all-time run-scoring list in the format.Across all three formats, Kohli has amassed 28,601 international runs in 566 matches and 633 innings at an average of 52.86. His overall tally comprises 86 centuries and 149 half-centuries, with his highest individual score being an unbeaten 254.

League One's bottom side Bromley have named former MK Dons and Oxford United boss Karl Robinson as their new manager.

Robinson, 46, who has been out of work since leaving Salford City in June, has signed a "long-term contract".

Byron Webster, who took interim charge following the sacking of Andy Woodman last month, will stay at Bromley as part of Robinson's coaching staff.

The Ravens are three points from the safety line in League One after winning promotion to the third tier for the first time in their history last season.

"Karl brings an elite level of experience, tactical acumen, and a proven track record of competing at the sharp end of League One," chairman Robin Stanton-Gleaves told the club website.

"His relentless energy matches our ambition perfectly, and we are unified in our vision to drive this football club forward into an exciting new era.

"I led a full recruitment process, which began the day after our previous manager's departure. From the outset, my intention was to find someone who had worked at this level and had the experience and qualities to take Bromley Football Club on the next stage of its journey.

"It was important to take the time to work through that process thoroughly, and I am delighted that it has brought us to Karl."

Robinson's first game in charge will be Saturday's FA Cup first-round meeting with Rotherham United, who are managed by his former number two at Salford, Alex Bruce.

Woodman, who was dismissed on 22 October, guided the club to two promotions in three seasons but left after two wins in the first 10 games this term.

Robinson spent more than six years in charge of MK Dons, which included a 4-0 League Cup win over Manchester United in October 2014, and promotion to the Championship seven months later after finishing runners-up in League One.

The team suffered immediate relegation and he was sacked by the Dons in October 2016.

Robinson then spent 16 months in charge of Charlton Athletic, departing by mutual consent in March 2018 and immediately being named the new Oxford United boss.

He twice took the U's to the League One play-offs, before losing his job in February 2023.

After a spell as assistant manager at Leeds United, Robinson took over at Salford City in January 2024, and last season led the club to their highest ever finish, of fourth in League Two.
