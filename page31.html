Urban Outfitters stock fell 11% after hours following record sales

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Net income rose to $134.1 million from $116.4 million as comparable retail sales grew 4.4% in the quarter

Urban Outfitters reported record third-quarter net sales of $1.67 billion, up 8.9% from a year earlier, and its stock fell 11% in after-hours trading on Tuesday. The stock traded at $75.98 during that session.

Net income was $134.1 million, or $1.54 per diluted share, compared with $116.4 million, or $1.28 per share, in the prior-year quarter. Gross profit rose 9.6% to $617.6 million. For the nine months ended Oct. 31, net sales rose 10.2% to a record $4.81 billion.

Retail segment net sales rose 6.1%, and comparable retail net sales rose 4.4%, with mid single-digit growth in both store and digital sales. Comparable sales increased 8.5% at FP Group, 5.8% at the Urban Outfitters brand and 1.7% at Anthropologie Group. Subscription segment sales climbed 28.0%, and wholesale segment sales increased 18.8%.

Comparable retail sales growth was 8% in the year-earlier quarter.

Earnings per share topped the $1.52 that analysts polled by FactSet expected, while revenue matched Wall Street expectations, according to The Wall Street Journal.

Total inventory rose 10.1% from a year earlier. The company attributed the increase to higher net sales and the timing of inventory receipts.

"Our teams delivered another standout quarter, achieving record third-quarter sales and earnings per share," Chief Executive Officer Richard A. Hayne said in a statement. "Every brand and segment contributed positively to our third-quarter performance, underscoring the strength of our portfolio and disciplined execution across the organization," Hayne said.

In August, the company reported record second-quarter net sales of $1.66 billion, up 10% from a year earlier. It described that period as its eighth consecutive quarter of record sales and profits. Hayne said after first-quarter results in May that the company's largely higher-income customer base had remained engaged despite economic uncertainty.

Scientists find “field” of washing machines in ocean during shipwreck hunt

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Scientists were left stunned after finding a “surreal” site of kitchen appliances at the bottom of the ocean, all while they thought it was part of a shipwreck.

Oceans are, notoriously, one of the last places on Earth that haven’t been fully explored. The depths of some make it impossible for researchers to actually get a clear picture of what is lurking down there, even with all the money in the world being thrown behind some explorations.

Back in 2017, a group of researchers from NOAA, the US National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration, had been using a ROV (remotely operated vehicle) in the Gulf of Mexico while looking for a shipwreck.

However, instead of finding ancient treasure, they stumbled upon a field of kitchen appliances on the seafloor, and couldn’t quite believe it.

In footage only released this year, almost a decade on, scientists from NOAA talked through their findings, with one expert calling it a “bit surreal” to see.

“We are at 1.5km in the deep sea, and here we are moving through a field of chest freezers, fridges, and washing machines,” they added.

Another joked that they’d “return this to the manufacturer and ask about the warranty,” when looking at one of the submerged washing machines.

The debris had been found as the researchers picked up a “sonar target,” which they suspected to be a shipwreck. Instead, they found a 40-foot-long shipping container with a hole on the outside of it. It is certainly a unique find.

Debian wakes up wearing Windows XP's actual skin

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Win4Wine runs the genuine Explorer shell atop Linux

Win4Wine is one of the more remarkable and creative hacks we've read about in recent years. It runs Windows XP's genuine Explorer shell through patched Wine on Debian 13, with a custom window manager integrating Linux applications into the desktop.

As its GitHub repository explains: "The taskbar, Start menu, desktop and folder windows are XP's real ones, and Linux applications get XP's frames and taskbar buttons."

This impressive, possibly demented project has English installation instructions and a README, although its design documents are in Japanese. We found Mozilla's built-in translation handled those pretty well.

Its developer goes by miKuta0407 on GitHub, their homepage, and X.

Also note, you're going to need some Windows installation media for the full experience. The README says: "No Microsoft files are included. Only the files that are needed are extracted locally from your own ISO."

It supports Windows XP Professional SP3 installation images in 32-bit Japanese or English editions, with an experimental option for Windows 7 SP1 x64. Without either Windows image, it can download and use ReactOS's free software shell instead, for what it terms "a Windows 2000-like classic look." In our view, that sounds much better anyway.

Before any former Windows users cry hurray and reach for an optical drive, this is not some ready-to-run project. The top of the README warns: "This is a personal, experimental project, not a finished product for daily use." The developer has tested it only on a fresh Debian 13 installation and recommends a VM or spare machine, rather than an existing working desktop, which sounds like an excellent precaution to us.

Also, before you ask, yes, it was vibe-coded with Claude.

We found ourselves reminded of another crazy-but-is-it project that we wrote about back in January: What if Linux ran Windows... and meant it? Meet Loss32. The Loss32 proposal was even more extreme. It suggested replacing essentially the entire Linux userland, so it would be a Linux kernel running Wine with a Windows desktop and Windows apps.

However, Mikuta-san was way ahead of us – lower down, the README mentions and even links to Loss32. Saying that, since it launched at the end of 2025, the bottom of the Loss32 web page shows a couple of updates, reporting on delays in getting it working. That, to be honest, did not shock us, but we still admire the let's-do-this spirit. The developer also seems to have got slightly distracted by a previous project, touchHLE: high-level emulator for early iOS apps.

Loss32, in turn, reminded us of Neptune OS, an attempt to give seL4 a Windows personality transplant. We wrote about that back in 2022, and work on it has continued: the project page shows updates in recent months. The team has also pushed out preview releases in 2022, 2024, 2025, and most recently in September.

Published: 08:22 EST, 25 November 2026 | Updated: 08:31 EST, 25 November 2026

A smile did eventually spread across the face of Erling Haaland when dissecting Manchester City’s draw with Napoli, stretching their worrying run to one victory in five games.

Not for the first time in recent weeks, he had appeared morose and despondent, radiating an air of gloom, talking about how the team are ‘questioning anything’ amid these results. That’s not exactly inspiring confidence among their supporters.

But just as City’s captain for the night was about to finish his media duties before heading home, one final question of his last interview focused on Sunday’s clash with Arsenal and a rivalry with a ‘certain centre half’. Haaland’s mood changed instantly.

‘It's always good to play against the best teams,’ said the Norwegian, breaking out into a smirk as he allowed the impending battle with Gabriel to percolate.

‘Arsenal were the best team last year and one of the best in the last five years now, so it's fun to play against them and it’s always fun to play against Gabriel.’

There was, at last, some positive emotion. Is Gabriel and that playground scrapping what he needs to re-energise? We’ll find out in a few days.

Erling Haaland appeared morose and despondent following Man City's draw with Napoli, suggesting he was 'questioning everything' amid their worrying run of form

Haaland did raise a smile about the prospect of his impending battle with Arsenal defender Gabriel this weekend

The Man City star has shown increasing dejected body language during games and has spent too much time recently with his head bowed and hand waving at team-mates

Haaland talked about City needing to be at their maximum to take anything from the Emirates this weekend. Whether that is ‘fun’ or not is a different matter entirely, but what Enzo Maresca must demand now is all of them doing more. Even with 14 goals already this term, that includes Haaland.

He is leading from the front off the pitch. Standing in front of the cameras more than anyone else, facing up to the dipping form and taking on those responsibilities without complaining. Yet City need more of that leadership between the white lines from somebody who has drawn a blank in four of his last five club matches, despite notching three times over the international break. Too much head bowing and hand waving at the moment.

The 26-year-old is admitting that he has to give extra, too. And believes that the team are generally creating enough chances for him, even if the freewheeling attacking abandon of early season has vanished.

These results are clearly affecting Haaland’s mood and that can be self-perpetuating. He talks about giving the fans something to hang on to and how failing to reverse this run is hurting him. That is evident by watching his increasingly dejected body language during games and the frustration which was seen when he exasperatingly gestured at Abdukodir Khusanov as the defender dithered with a routine throw-in during Saturday’s draw against Fulham. It wasn’t the picture of a man happy with his lot.

Only Haaland will know whether that general angst is contributing to a lack of intensity in his own performances, which seemed fine for a well-oiled Norway in the Nations League less than a fortnight ago. Nobody has mentioned the striker carrying a knock, although Maresca has appeared focused on offering him rest whenever possible over the past three months.

There is undoubtedly something proving an obstacle for him. He’s insisted that it isn’t the guilty verdicts and listening to him, the gripes and disappointment look centred on how City’s lack of control in games is unsettling them. It also cannot help that Haaland will feel aggrieved to have seen a goal on Saturday chalked off for a foul on Bernd Leno and then not awarded a penalty for grappling on Tuesday night.

If both or even one of those go his way, the interview that sparked concern with fans – one conducted almost immediately after full time when emotions are at their highest – is unlikely to come out the way it did. And the outlook looks less foggy. But as it is, City will have to cling on to the idea that a sullen Haaland is at least a Haaland who cares. And a Haaland who cares must surely dig them out of this hole, even if he cannot do that alone.

Raheem Sterling's chaotic descent into insanity is a product of the cocooned football world where stars can't handle rejection, writes IAN HERBERT - now he must listen to those around him if he wants to salvage his career

Published: 08:21 EST, 25 November 2026 | Updated: 08:23 EST, 25 November 2026

Raheem Sterling wore black to his sentencing hearing today, just as he had when he arrived at Basingstoke Magistrates Court two months ago and admitted to 90 minutes of terrifying rush hour driving, with canisters of nitrous oxide on the passenger seat beside him.

He was visibly more subdued this time, wearing the look of a man who’d spent two months contemplating the prospect of a jail stretch, whilst the contemporaries he once stood with in the England squad, Harry Kane, Jordan Henderson and Jordan Pickford, were continuing to hit the heights.

Imprisonment was only ever a narrow possibility under the sentencing guidelines for dangerous driving, given the miracle that Sterling did not cause serious injury or worse as he zig-zagged between lanes on the M3 and M4 that morning last May. Critically, he was not found to be under the influence of nitrous oxide. A motorist who drove in equally erratic fashion and initially ignored police on Cheshire’s M56, in a BMW which had only three tyres, was handed a suspended sentence earlier this year.

But while the mitigation preceding the magistrate’s decision that Sterling would walk free - his 12-month prison sentence is suspended for 18 months - garlanded him for his charity work and promotion of racial equality, don’t let it be said that his conduct that day was nothing less than a disgrace. And that the tetchy hyper sensitivity Sterling displayed with the police officers who arrested him revealed a shocking lack of contrition and shame.

Sterling has had his challenges, of course. The early death of his father and a nomadic London childhood spent helping his mum on cleaning jobs. But then he disappeared into the cocooned existence of the football academy world, full of the little princes who have people fawning over them, telling them how good they are and throwing obscene amounts of money at them.

Raheem Sterling's conduct the day of his car crash was nothing short of a disgrace

Sterling's meteoric rise has meant he does not have the tools to deal with rejection

Kane has flourished in a way that Sterling hasn’t because he was forced to learn the hard way, deal with rejection and develop a sense of perspective. The princes are at the mercy of the hangers on and leeches, around them for a cut of their next big pay cheque. It was Sterling’s misfortune to select Aidy Ward as his agent – the man who conducted ugly, public contract negotiations with Liverpool which included declaring that Sterling wouldn’t be staying at Anfield even if he were offered nearly £1m a week. The reputational damage that caused Sterling should have seen him sack Ward on the spot.

The problem for those whose adolescence takes place in football’s gilded cage is that they combust and can’t hack it when the going gets remotely tough. For Sterling, read Dele Alli, Kalvin Phillips and Aaron Ramsdale.

But what makes Sterling’s personal disintegration so needless is that he really has had positive influences around him in the background. Friends who were there when he wanted to develop a voice to speak against racial discrimination in football. There when he wanted to establish a Foundation to help the kind of London teenagers that he used to be.

He lost those influences during the chaotic descent into the insanity of that spring morning when he went berserk in his car. Some of them have described to me how he flatly refused to respond to calls and went into ‘oblivion mode’ in the hours after his arrest. It seemed like he was seeking escape from any vestige of the game which is all he has known.

England captain and Sterling's former team-mate Harry Kane has thrived due to the early setbacks in his career

The tragedy is Sterling has a strong support network who want the best for him

The bleak reality for Sterling is that his career in football is surely over now. Yes, he could pick up the thread in some place below the Premier League, though his unhappy experience at Feyenoord – where there was a prurient fascination with his struggles – suggests that he will face the same relentless scrutiny wherever he goes. In the modern world, more gratuitous pleasure than ever is taken in the fall of those who were once great. It will be a circus if he pitches up somewhere new.

But the evidence of that work with boys in London suggests that he has far more of a hinterland to turn to than Alli and his ilk, if only he can re-establish those positive influences and seek help.

A moment in time, precisely two years ago, proves what his future might look like. At a meeting room in King's Cross, Sterling sat with a group of seven young men, all of whom were deemed at risk of drifting into knife crime and who had been sent along to his foundation by Brent's youth offending service.

After an initial 15 or 20 minutes when the boys seemed blinded by the superstar in their midst, they became more comfortable, seeming to feel he understood the likes of them. There were smiles as he described the various school social groups he knew. 'The cool group, the middle of the road group and the geeks,' as he called them. The 'geeks' always seemed to be a bigger part of his world when exams approached, he said with a grin. The room was wreathed with laughter, then.

The group session evolved into several one-to-one conversations between him and the boys who'd been more reticent. It was then that he pointed out the value of allowing outside mentors to help you, if your home life is challenging. 'Having someone else involved changed the course of my life,' Sterling told them.

Now is the moment when Sterling needs others to help change the course of his own life and if he accepts that, he could play a part in public life again. First comes the challenge of rousing himself from any lingering pity, emerging from the celebrity bubble and appreciating that he is supremely lucky to remain a free man after those abominable, mindless few hours in his souped-up car.

He also suggested that certain founders might head back to crypto after they "recognize" that Anthropic or OpenAI could "usurp" what they have built.

Hayes said the biggest frontier AI labs are so large and capable that it is hard to "build a real AI business." In his view, "most" founders "raise a bunch of money and then light it on fire."

He expects the AI trade to run into "serious headwinds" during the latter half of 2027, as leading labs start to owe payments on leases for computing capacity and similar commitments that cannot be canceled. Anthropic projects around $451.6 billion in obligations for compute and infrastructure through 2030, and about $70.7 billion of that falls due by the close of September 2027, according to its prospectus.

"Do they have the money? We're going to find out," Hayes said.

Bitcoin slid to a low of $55,142.16 last Saturday and has barely bounced back since. It has dropped for seven weeks in a row, a run that has happened only twice before in its history, according to CNBC. The cryptocurrency peaked at a record $126,279.63 in October 2025.

Hayes called the current market moves a "correlation-led liquidation." Bitcoin and gold have both stayed tied to the Nasdaq so far, he said. "Stuff goes down, you sell crypto first because it's easy, and then you sell everything else," he said, according to CNBC.

Hayes said his preferred scenario is a bitcoin bounce from roughly $55,000 even as the Nasdaq continues to slide. That split would indicate markets are drifting away from a general sell-off and toward pricing in "reflation," as the Federal Reserve supplies additional liquidity, he said.

Once that pattern shifts, Hayes said, Zcash would draw his interest, a token he characterized as a "high beta bitcoin" that places more emphasis on privacy. He named ENA, the token of Ethereum-based platform Ethena, as the other holding he is watching. A bitcoin rally could strengthen returns from funding rates and the futures basis, which would swell Ethena's income and give its USDe synthetic dollar broader appeal, he said.

Hayes had predicted in October that the AI data center buildout would overshoot and crash, with bitcoin rising afterward. He said then that data center providers would seek payment when new capacity is delivered, a point he placed in late 2027 or 2028.
