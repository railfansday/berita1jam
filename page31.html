Oregon QB Dante Moore ruled out for rest of 2026 season

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Oregon Ducks quarterback Dante Moore will miss the rest of the season, coach Dan Lanning announced Monday night.

"I'm not anticipating him being back this season," Lanning said.

"Really got clarity for us this past week, for him," Lanning added. "And I think if it were up to Dante in any shape, way, he would want to be out there playing for the guys. But, unfortunately, it's not really up to Dante. It's not really up to me. We can control the setting and practice where he's able to do things for us, but that's not always the case in the game."

Lanning said he expects Moore, who has run scout-team offense in practice, to fully recover.

"That hasn't changed," Lanning said. "Definitely feel that way, but for him to be out there on the field for us right now, that's not gonna happen."

Lanning added that Moore would remain involved with the team.

"He's still going to be a big part of this team and helping us move forward and find opportunities for us to be better," Lanning said.

The announcement comes days after the reeling Ducks lost 21-10 at Illinois on Saturday in the third straight game Moore missed while in concussion protocol.

Before the Illinois game, Moore told The Oregonian/OregonLive that he felt good but didn't know if he'd return this season and was taking it "one day at a time."

He then began throwing passes on the field in shorts and a sweatshirt.

Saturday's loss all but knocked Oregon (4-3, 2-2 Big Ten) out of the national playoff picture and led Lanning to fire offensive coordinator Drew Mehringer on Sunday.

Without Moore, Oregon scored 42 points over three games and lost twice while rotating between backup quarterbacks Dylan Raiola and Brock Thomas.

Moore was injured on Sept. 26 at USC while sliding to end a 10-yard run to the USC 13-yard line. As Moore began to slide, Trojans linebacker Desman Stephens II hit him in the head with his shoulder.

Oregon quarterback Dante Moore, center bottom, takes a hard tackle by lSouthern California inebacker Desman Stephens II, center top, during the first half of an NCAA college football game, Saturday, Sept. 26, 2026, in Los Angeles. (AP Photo/Mark J. Terrill)AP Photo/Mark J. Terrill

Moore was taken off the field in a cart and loaded into an ambulance.

Following Moore's injury, Raiola entered the game and led the Ducks to a 41-27 win.

Moore entered the concussion protocol but returned to practice the following bye week.

Speculation was that Moore would return once he cleared the protocols. But each subsequent availability report listed Moore as out.

On Oct. 10, Oregon lost 17-15 to UCLA, with Raiola struggling through three quarters before being replaced in the fourth quarter by Thomas.

Thomas started the following week against Nebraska, but he played poorly before Raiola entered the game in the fourth quarter and led the Ducks to a 17-14 comeback win.

Oregon's realistic national playoff hopes remained alive at 4-2 — until the loss to Illinois.

At 4-3, the Ducks must win their final five games to reach 9-3 and hope enough playoff contenders finish with three losses so the Ducks can receive a berth.

If Moore enters the 2027 NFL Draft, his announcement will end his brief Ducks career, during which he led the program to one of its best seasons.

Oregon quarterback Dante Moore walks off the field after the No. 5 Ducks fall to the No. 1 Indiana Hoosiers, 56-22, in the Peach Bowl at the College Football Playoff semifinals from Mercedes-Benz Stadium in Atlanta on Friday, Jan. 9, 2025. Sean Meagher/The OregonianSean Meagher/The Oregonian

Lanning said he wasn't sure what Moore's plans were after this season.

"That's not fair for me to say or speculate," Lanning said. "Obviously, he'll have a decision again at the end of the season and what that looks like. I think we all can acknowledge that Dante is a great player and he will get an opportunity to get drafted. But what that looks like in the future, I think that's a decision for a later date. I don't know where his mind is at on that. We haven't had a conversation. That's not where my focus is at. I don't think that's where his focus is at right now. There's a reason you get time to make that decision later on."

Moore committed to Oregon in the summer of 2022. Later, he flipped to UCLA in December of 2022 rather than back up Ducks quarterback Bo Nix in 2023.

That summer, Moore learned that his mother had been diagnosed with cancer, adding to the pressure he was feeling while trying to perform as a highly touted freshman.

Moore had a rough freshman season at UCLA. His midseason struggles led Chip Kelly to bench Moore.

Following the season, Moore entered the transfer portal and committed to the Ducks in December 2023.

Moore transferred to Oregon to develop while backing up the team's new starter, Oklahoma transfer Dillon Gabriel.

That plan paid off in 2025. Moore passed for 3,565 yards, 30 touchdowns and 10 interceptions while leading the Ducks to a 13-2 record and the playoff semifinals, where they lost to eventual national champion Indiana.

Moore, a projected first-round pick in the 2026 NFL Draft, returned to Oregon for his senior season to keep improving with a team that returned eight defensive starters and was expected to contend for a national championship.

Oregon quarterback Dante Moore (#5) celebrates with running back Tradarian Ball (#4) after Ball's touchdown as the No. 21 Ducks face the Portland State Vikings in a non-conference college football game at Autzen Stadium in Eugene on Friday, Sept. 18, 2026. Sean Meagher/The Oregonian

Oregon entered the season ranked No. 2 — the highest preseason ranking in program history — and defeated Boise State, 34-27, in the opener.

Moore passed for a career-high 378 yards with three touchdown passes.

The season began to unravel the following week when the heavily favored Ducks lost 39-31 at Oklahoma State.

The following week, Oregon won 84-0 over Portland State. Next came the USC game.

The Ducks, who host No. 20 Northwestern (6-1, 3-1), could continue to start Raiola or turn to former four-star recruit Akili Smith Jr.

Chances are, Lanning will go with whichever quarterback he believes can win games.

In the offseason, Oregon is expected to seek a quarterback in the transfer portal. With high expectations at Oregon, Lanning likely won't bank on Raiola or Smith as the starter in 2027.

Oregon has 3 quarterbacks who could start Saturday. Illinois has a freshman who's never started before

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

The Oregon Ducks will face a team on Saturday with an even less desirable quarterback situation.

Illinois (3-3, 1-2 Big Ten) quarterback Katin Houser suffered a broken hand that will sideline him for four to six weeks during a 38-37 overtime win over Michigan State on Oct. 10.

Oregon has played its last two games without starting quarterback Dante Moore, who suffered a concussion in the second quarter of the Ducks' 41-27 win at USC on Sept. 26.

However, while Oregon can turn to junior Dylan Raiola — a Nebraska transfer with 23 career starts — and senior Brock Thomas, Illinois is relying on freshman quarterback Michael Clayton II, who will make his first start against the Ducks.

A positive for Illinois is that Houser was injured before the team's bye week, giving coach Bret Bielema two weeks to prepare Clayton.

"It was just good timing," Bielema told reporters during his Monday media session.

Illinois is off to a disappointing start after going 9-4 in 2025 and 10-3 in 2024. The defense is below average, while the passing combination of Houser and wide receiver Collin Dixon is one of the nation's best. Dixon is tied for third nationally with nine touchdown receptions.

Illinois head coach Bret Bielema celebrates their overtime win over Michigan State as he walks off the field after an NCAA college football game, Saturday, Oct. 10, 2026, in East Lansing, Mich. (AP Photo/Lon Horwedel) AP Photo/Lon HorwedelAP Photo/Lon Horwedel

Now Illinois heads into Saturday's game against a resurgent Oregon defense, trying to get Clayton up to speed.

His performance against MSU elevated expectations.

Houser left the game late in the third quarter with Illinois leading 23-10. Clayton completed 6 of 8 passes for 88 yards and two touchdowns. The final touchdown came in overtime on a 25-yard pass that put Illinois up 38-31.

Sophomore quarterback Carson Boyd also played. He rushed once for seven yards but did not attempt a pass.

Bielema and offensive coordinator Barry Lunney Jr. both said Boyd could play against Oregon (4-2, 2-1), but Clayton is the starter.

Usually, Bielema said, his starting quarterback gets a lot of rest during the bye week while the staff works on player development. Given Clayton and Boyd's inexperience, both got valuable reps in practice last week.

"I think those two guys in particular took advantage of every rep," Bielema said.

Bielema has leaned heavily on transfer quarterbacks during his six years at Illinois, including Houser. But that could change if Clayton, a former three-star recruit, earns the job.

"I've said several times that hopefully the next starter comes from within our program," Bielema said.

Lunney said Clayton will be more prepared than he has in the past given the circumstances.

Michigan State Spartans defensive back Charles Brantley (4) tackles Illinois Fighting Illini wide receiver Collin Dixon (17) in a Big 10 football game at Spartan Stadium in East Lansing, Mich. on Saturday, October 10, 2026.Isaac Ritchey | iritchey@mlive.com

"He knows he's going to play now," Lunney told reporters.

"We feel like we have another guy on campus in that room that can help us win and we're very confident in that," Bielema said.

Oregon coach Dan Lanning said it's difficult to scout Clayton given his limited body of work. Oregon has watched some of his high school game film. But mostly, Oregon will focus on Illinois' system.

"There might be wrinkles, but very rarely do people change the identity of their offense based on a change of the position," Lanning said. "So you prepare and then you make sure you have answers for what you think that skill set is."

If Moore doesn't return this week, Raiola and Thomas will compete for the starting job against Illinois.

Raiola replaced Moore at USC and thrived in the win, but following a bye, struggled for three quarters during a 17-15 loss to UCLA on Oct. 10. Thomas performed better in the fourth quarter and almost pulled out the victory.

Thomas started Saturday's 17-14 home win over Nebraska, but struggled for three quarters before Lanning turned to Raiola. He led the Ducks from a 14-6 deficit to a win with a four-yard touchdown pass to tight end Jamari Johnson with 28 seconds remaining.

Lanning said he's confident in all four quarterbacks, including sophomore Akili Smith Jr., who had a play package ready for Nebraska if needed.

Illinois is most familiar with Raiola, who, as a Nebraska freshman in 2024, passed for 297 yards, three touchdowns and one interception in a 31-24 overtime loss to Illinois.

"Raiola is obviously a very accomplished player," Bielema said. "Kind of almost unusual. You got to almost prepare for possibly three quarterbacks on Saturday. But we'll kind of handle that during the course of the week and see where that goes."

Dame Time is back in Portland — and the Blazers look dangerous

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

A new head coach will roam the sidelines. A franchise icon will make his long-awaited return. An All-Star will begin his redemption tour.

And a new ownership group will watch its first season opener from Paul Allen's former seats along the Moda Center baseline.

The Blazers open the 2026-27 NBA season Wednesday night against the Phoenix Suns — a team they upset in April in the play-in tournament — and if you're just now tuning in for the start of the regular season, well, there's a lot you missed.

RELATED: Suns vs. Blazers predictions, odds and best bets: Dame is home, and the model likes Portland on opening night

The Blazers are expected to unveil a new starting lineup that features a pair of notable All-Star additions: Damian Lillard and Ja Morant. The backcourt duo is joining a fivesome that includes returning starters Deni Avdija, Toumani Camara and Donovan Clingan. It's a versatile, star-studded lineup that features three All-Stars, a former All-Defensive Second Team selection, one of the best young centers in the NBA and loads of scoring potential.

Micah Nori, the Blazers' first-year head coach, said he plans on using a 10-man rotation to start the season, which will allow the Blazers to capitalize on their depth and versatility. In addition to the starters, Jrue Holiday, Scoot Henderson, Sidy Cissoko, Robert Williams III and Vit Krejci are expected to open the season in the rotation. The group could change depending on matchups and Williams' availability — he did not play in back-to-backs last season — and it's likely that Micah Potter, Branden Carlson and Yang Hansen will occasionally see spot duty, as well.

The Blazers stabilized their turbulent coaching situation in June, hiring longtime NBA assistant Nori to replace interim coach Tiago Splitter. Nori, who interviewed for head coaching jobs seven times before finally landing his first chance, is entering his 29th season in the league and is universally revered in NBA circles for his positive approach, ability to connect with players and sharp basketball mind. Early indications have been encouraging, as the Blazers players seem to love him and the front office works well with him. The Blazers' coaching position had been embroiled in controversy, of course, as former coach Chauncey Billups — who was placed on indefinite leave by the NBA — is facing federal felony charges related to money laundering and wire fraud conspiracy for his alleged involvement in a rigged underground poker game.

In a stark contrast to recent seasons, the Blazers are constructed around their offense rather than their defense. The roster is unbalanced, featuring five dynamic ball-handlers and playmakers (Lillard, Morant, Avdija, Holiday and Henderson), a solid center combination (Clingan and Williams) and little wing depth. As a result, the team is expected to play fast and free under Nori, bringing lots of speed, passing, cutting and flow. Nori said he hopes to rotate his players liberally in shorter shifts during games — perhaps as short as three minutes — which will enable them to play with maximum effort before giving way to teammates. The interior defense is strong with Clingan and Williams patrolling the middle, Camara remains one of the NBA's best and most versatile defenders and Cissoko will be asked to guard one-through-five. Holiday is still capable of locking down opposing guards and Avdija has pledged to be more of a two-way threat now that he doesn't have to serve as the team's primary ball-handler. But the backcourt combination of Morant and Lillard, while lethal on offense, is not imposing on defense and relatively small (both stand 6-foot-2).

Portland defied pundits last season, winning 10 of its last 14 regular season games to secure a spot in the play-in tournament, where it beat the Suns in Phoenix to earn the seventh seed in the Western Conference. The San Antonio Spurs dismissed the Blazers with a gentleman's sweep in the playoffs, but the strong run — combined with the additions of Lillard and Morant — has raised expectations in Portland. How much? Camara said in training camp that the Blazers' goal is to win 50 or more games and secure home-court advantage in the postseason. It won't be easy. The league is as deep and talented as ever, and new rules surrounding the lottery should discourage teams from tanking, meaning most of the league will be chasing wins instead of lottery ping-pong balls late into the season. It seemed like a third of the league had given up last year by the All-Star break.

As the Blazers' new-look lineup works to build chemistry and a first-year coach settles into a new role, the schedule will be challenging. Six of the first eight games — and 12 of the first 17 — will come on the road and include a slew of playoff contenders, including the Spurs, Oklahoma City Thunder (twice), Minnesota Timberwolves (twice), New York Knicks, Philadelphia 76ers, Denver Nuggets and Los Angeles Lakers (twice).

The most uplifting part of opening night figures to be the return of Lillard, the franchise icon who is back after spending two seasons in Milwaukee and one season on the injured list. The nine-time All-Star and franchise leader has not played in an NBA regular-season or playoff game since April 27, 2025, when he suffered a torn left Achilles tendon. And he has not played with Portland since March 22, 2023, six months before it traded him to the Bucks. Lillard showed no ill effects from his injury during the exhibition season, when he played in three of the Blazers' four games and averaged 17.3 points, 2.7 assists and 22.7 minutes. If anything, he looked like the same old Dame, swishing three-pointers, creating off the dribble, initiating offense and drawing defensive attention. Lillard joked earlier this week that he's "not an old man," but also acknowledged that he isn't likely to play upward of 38 minutes a game like he used to and may even take a night off here and there for rest, most likely on a random back-to-back. His addition not only should help cover up two of the Blazers' biggest weaknesses last season — three-point shooting and shooting in the clutch — but also inspire a thirsty fan base, which has rarely loved a player more. All the while, Lillard says he intends to play with more "joy" than ever.

10 takeaways from Oregon’s loss at Illinois and firing of OC Drew Mehringer

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

CHAMPAIGN, Ill. — The Oregon Ducks (4-3, 2-2 Big Ten) suffered their worst loss of the season at Illinois on Saturday.

The 21-10 defeat came against a marginal Illinois team that started a true freshman at quarterback, rushed for 225 yards, and held Oregon to two scoring drives.

As a result, on Sunday, Oregon coach Dan Lanning announced the firing of offensive coordinator Drew Mehringer.

What that means for the rest of the season remains to be seen. Mehringer had to contend with several key personnel issues, including starting quarterback Dante Moore missing three games while in concussion protocol.

Here are 10 takeaways from Saturday's game and the firing of Mehringer:

Criticizing play-calling is an armchair quarterback's delight, but doing so under Oregon's current circumstances is somewhat obtuse.

Few teams excel without their starting quarterback. The next factor is an offensive line that was clearly mismanaged in the offseason after losing three starters to the NFL. Next is inconsistent play from backup quarterbacks Dylan Raiola and Brock Thomas. Then sprinkle in losing top wideout Jeremiah McClellan (broken foot) after three games, starting wide receiver Evan Stewart (undisclosed injury) for the last two games, and two more rotational wide receivers heading into Illinois.

Does all of that excuse Mehringer for Oregon scoring just 42 points over the past three weeks? No. But those are a lot of obstacles for any offensive coordinator to overcome.

Lanning will probably announce his new offensive coordinator during his Monday press conference.

He could promote from within to get through the season and then conduct a national search in the offseason. Or he may have someone in mind to take over now and beyond.

Whatever Lanning decides, the next play-caller had better hope Moore returns, or little will change.

Lanning did well by hiring younger offensive coordinators, Kenny Dillingham and then Will Stein. But maybe it's time to bring in an accomplished, seasoned veteran coach to add a new perspective to a relatively young staff.

Someone with a track record of success as an offensive coordinator and maybe as a head coach.

Someone like former Oregon coach Chip Kelly, Northwestern's offensive coordinator. No, don't expect Kelly to want to return to Oregon.

But someone like him could fit perfectly.

After Saturday's game, Raiola was asked twice how difficult it was to build timing with the receiving crew, which lacked McClellan, Stewart, and freshmen Messiah Hampton and Jalen Lott.

Oregon Ducks QB Dylan Raiola (8) throws a pass to Oregon Ducks RB Dierre Hill Jr. (6) during a college football game between the Oregon Ducks and Illinois Fighting Illini on October 24, 2026 at Gies Memorial Stadium in Champaign, IL. (Photo by James Black/Icon Sportswire) (Icon Sportswire via AP Images)AP

Raiola avoided throwing anyone under the bus but acknowledged what the question implied. Passing games are built on timing. If the quarterback can't fully trust the receivers running the routes, synergy suffers and efficiency wanes.

That's less a knock on the receivers than an acknowledgment that it's easier to develop a connection with a team's more polished top receivers than with those thrust into action by circumstance.

At times, running back Dierre Hill Jr. played wide receiver alongside freshmen Hudson Lewis and Gatlin Bair. Neither had played much until injuries hit the group.

Lanning said Stewart would return. McClellan is out indefinitely.

Much has been made about the failed gadget play in the third quarter. Many have blamed Mehringer for the play-call and the negative result hurting momentum.

Oregon trailed 14-10, forced an Illinois punt and took over at the Ducks' 9-yard line. Five plays later, Oregon moved the ball to its 47.

Oregon lined up with four offensive linemen to the far left, with wide receiver Dakorien Moore behind the linemen. Three Illinois defenders lined up on that side with a fourth closer to the box.

Raiola checked to a run play and handed the ball to Moore on a jet sweep to the right, where the Ducks were outnumbered. The poorly-blocked play resulted in a one-yard loss.

After the game, Lanning was asked whether the failed play hurt the team at that moment.

"Yeah, it's a great question," he said. "It certainly wasn't successful. There's a pause in operation there, right?"

Yes, but who is at fault for the failed play? Is it Raiola for reading it wrong and not passing to Moore? Is it Mehringer for calling the play? Or is it Lanning for allowing the play to happen?

For that play to be called, it must be accepted by Lanning, practiced, reviewed on film and further stamped by Lanning to appear on the play sheet. Then, when Mehringer calls it, Lanning must let it go through. He can hear every play call through the coaches' headsets.

If Lanning didn't like the play, he could have vetoed it. He did not.

The concept is not much different from when Lanning chooses to go for two after touchdowns. If Oregon gets the favorable look it wants, it takes the shot.

That's what happened with the gadget play. But Oregon made the wrong choice and the play failed.

Regardless of the process, had Lanning not liked the play, he could have stopped it from being called.

Oregon gained 180 yards on the ground against a team that ranked 12th in the Big Ten in rushing defense, allowing 134.4 yards per game.

Still, too many drives started well before getting stuffed, putting more pressure on Raiola, who couldn't consistently deliver.

As a result, Oregon converted on just four of 15 third-down attempts.

With most of the team's top receivers out, the rushing attack needed to be special, and it wasn't.

The junior entered the game 9-for-9 on field-goal attempts and went 1-for-4. But two of his three misses came from beyond 50 yards.

Hurych missed his first attempt from 52 yards out. He made a 20-yard field goal in the second quarter to put Oregon up 10-0. He missed a 53-yarder with 6:04 to go in the fourth quarter and later missed a 47-yarder with 52 seconds to go.

"He's human," Lanning said. "The reality is, those are long kicks. Some of those are really tough. The wind's a little bit different in this stadium. I think we put them in a position that wasn't the easiest to operate, and we've been really fortunate this year."

Oregon's defense kept the Ducks in the games against UCLA and Nebraska until the offense started to come alive.

Oregon lost to the Bruins 17-15 and beat Nebraska 17-14.

On Saturday, the Ducks led 10-0 late in the second quarter. At that point, nobody knew Oregon wouldn't score again. But the defense's job was to keep dominating, just in case.

Illinois Fighting Illini RB Aidan Laughery (21) runs the ball into the end zone for a touchdown during a college football game between the Oregon Ducks and Illinois Fighting Illini on October 24, 2026 at Gies Memorial Stadium in Champaign, IL. (Photo by James Black/Icon Sportswire via Getty Images)Icon Sportswire via Getty Images

Illinois running back Aidan Laughery got loose for a 75-yard touchdown run, making it 10-7 with 2:15 left in the half. Illinois had rushed for just 14 yards before that run, but finished the game with 225 rushing yards.

That production took pressure off freshman starting quarterback Michael Clayton II, who threw for two second-half touchdowns.

A dominant defense doesn't let that happen against a team it has on the ropes.

The loss was more about the Ducks' offense than the defense. But the defense got sloppy—several defenders made mistakes on Laughery's long run—revealing a lack of four-quarter focus.

"In moments today, they played really well as well," Lanning said of his defense. "But ultimately, they still had 225 yards rushing, or whatever. The final total was there. We can play better than that."

Many fans frustrated with the quarterback play have called for Smith to get a shot. The sophomore former four-star recruit flashed some skills against Portland State and again last spring.

Oregon quarterback Akili Smith Jr. (#11) looks downfield for an open receiver as the Ducks hold their annual spring football game at Autzen Stadium in Eugene on Saturday, April 25, 2026. Sean Meagher/The Oregonian

Is he ready to take on a Big Ten team? Only the coaches know for sure. They see him in practice every day.

Even if the Ducks have no shot at the playoffs, Lanning will likely play to win, not to develop. That means he has to believe Smith can win before Smith gets on the field.

However, if Moore does not return, Lanning should at least give Smith some game reps. Lanning said he had a Smith package ready for Nebraska, so we know Lanning has some plays he believes Smith can run effectively.

Truth be told, what do they have to lose? Raiola has been so inconsistent that giving Smith a series or two couldn't hurt much.

Understandably, Oregon left Illinois dejected. It will be interesting to see how the team responds. The loss probably ended the Ducks' playoff chances.

Although a 9-3 team could qualify for the playoffs given the level of national parity, Oregon winning nine games appears unlikely.

Oregon Ducks Head Coach Dan Lanning walks onto the field following a college football game between the Oregon Ducks and Illinois Fighting Illini on October 24, 2026 at Gies Memorial Stadium in Champaign, IL. (Photo by James Black/Icon Sportswire via Getty Images)Icon Sportswire via Getty Images

With many players returning this season to make a national title run and key players with NFL futures to consider, it's conceivable that many players shut down emotionally for the season.

It's doubtful that many marquee players have any interest in playing in a random bowl game. Those headed to the NFL or the transfer portal wouldn't play in a bowl game anyway.

If the team loses its zeal, the Ducks could fail to win seven games in a full season for the first time since 2016, when Oregon went 4-8.
