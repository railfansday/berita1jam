Miles Morales’ New Spider-Man Redesign Released By Marvel

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Lifestyle  |  Read Source →

Miles Morales gets a new Spider-Man costume that complements his iconic black-and-red suit with extra accessories. Miles Morales achieved the remarkable but overlooked feat of turning his sleek black-and-red Spider-Man costume into an unmistakable pop-culture staple nearly three decades after Peter Parker’s legendary black symbiote suit first debuted in 1984's Secret Wars. Creating an alternate Spider-Man uniform that stands on its own without feeling like a temporary variant is notoriously difficult, yet Sara Pichelli’s 2011 design immediately resonated with a new generation of readers. The striking contrast of matte black fabric accented by crimson webbing gave Miles a distinct identity that stands apart from Peter’s classic red-and-blue attire.

Not content to stop at a single memorable color scheme, Miles Morales has pushed Spider-Man fashion into bold territory with his many streetwear accessories. Miles' Spider-Man: Into the Spider-Verse look became an instant phenomenon after it layered an oversized hoodie, basketball shorts, and Air Jordan 1 sneakers directly over his spandex suit. In the comics and games, Miles has continued to experiment with standout looks, including his high-collared tenth Anniversary pink-and-navy streetwear suit, his Vibranium armor gifted by Black Panther, the Bodega Cat backpack outfit, and sleek cyberpunk or symbiote-inspired ensembles that reflect his Brooklyn roots and evolving heroic confidence.

Miles Morales has arguably many more options for creative costume redesigns than Peter Parker, and Marvel's new variation of his suit proves it.

After a first wave of gorgeous character redesigns, InHyuk Lee's Streetverse variant covers return with a new batch of stylish Marvel characters. Black Cat wears a fancy all-white outfit with long black boots, the Punisher dons a beautiful black-and-red coat that makes him look like a vampire hunter, and Miles Morales shows off a multi-layered Spider-Man costume. Clearly, InHyuk Lee's new wave of Streetverse designs are geared specifically for winter, and Miles Morales proposes the most practical outfit of all.

Peter Parker has famously worn civilian clothes over his Spider-Man suits, particularly during the cold New York winter or during tough flu episodes. Both The Amazing Spider-Man 2 and Spider-Man: Brand New Day adapted these visual twists, while Spider-Man: Into the Spider-Verse integrated Miles' hoodie into his first official Spider-Man costume. Perhaps in the future, Earth-616's Miles could put even more stylish twists on the classic winter Spider-Man outfit trope, potentially drawing closely from InHyuk Lee's Streetverse design in Marvel Comics canon.

Miles Morales’ Spider-Man: Into the Spider-Verse costume and InHyuk Lee’s acclaimed Streetverse variant design succeed as a natural streetwear extension of his personality. Into the Spider-Verse's oversized hoodie, basketball shorts, and sneakers over Miles' classic black-and-red spandex tells an immediate story about a teenager still growing into his heroic role and refusing to abandon his everyday style. InHyuk Lee’s Streetverse cover artwork takes the same concept and adds sharp urban tailoring that complements Miles’s signature crimson-and-black palette. Meanwhile, Insomniac’s similar attempt at an "Evolved Suit" in Marvel’s Spider-Man 2 clashed with Miles' style, as the jarring electric-blue-and-white toothpaste color scheme and the open-top mask felt out of place.

Through twenty years of cinema, Spider-Man's iconic red and blue suit has seen many variations. Each one is unique in its own way. What are they?

Jackets, hoodies, sneakers, and layered streetwear accessories work so effortlessly on Miles because they capture his youthful energy and his artistic background. Unlike Peter Parker, whose classic superhero aesthetic grew out of 1960s acrobatic simplicity, Miles belongs to a modern era where fashion, music, and self-expression go hand in hand. The addition of a cropped jacket and a pulled-up hood breaks up the rigid uniformity of traditional superhero spandex and gives his agile movements extra visual momentum in both comics and animation, as long as those layers enhance his iconic palette instead of burying it under distracting and unnecessary details.

China attackers deployed AI agents that autonomously found and exploited a 0-day to take over a university server

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

'Agents kept adapting for nearly a full day without human direction,' Amazon CISO said

Suspected Chinese spies deployed AI agents which worked autonomously to find a zero-day in an open source application and then abuse the flaw to break into university networks, gaining full control of a server, according to Amazon CISO CJ Moses.

The China-linked attacker used Cairn, an open source, penetration-testing framework that has become increasingly popular with cybercriminals who use the AI tool to carry out multi-stage intrusions with no human assistance.

In this case, the agents found and exploited a zero-day MONAI Label, an open-source medical imaging AI application. Amazon notified the project's maintainers of the vulnerability, and they released a fix and a security advisory.

On September 10, Amazon's Threat Intelligence Team found a server with a publicly hosted instance of Cairn that provided visibility into the campaign targeting universities in East Asia, outside Mainland China, Moses said in a Friday report shared with The Register.

The campaign occurred over a period of 48 hours beginning on September 8, and the prompts were written in Simplified Chinese, with timestamps in executed scripts configured to China Standard Time.

Before the AI agents found and exploited the zero day, however, they tried to break into another university’s Redis server over about 16 hours and 148 sequential actions, including abusing Redis's replication feature to load unauthorized code, escaping its scripting sandbox, writing to its configuration files, and corrupting its memory.

None of these were successful, and when those actions failed, the agents also attempted a Log4Shell injection against a different service, a server-side request forgery (SSRF) attack against a vulnerability in a co-located Qdrant instance, and several credential sprays against PostgreSQL, SSH, and other tools.

Meanwhile, a separate operation running for more than 6 hours targeted a university GPU server with another credential-spray attack, compiling a list of 80 real user names found across the school’s public websites, academic papers and other documents.

"Neither operation succeeded, yet together they show that the agents kept adapting for nearly a full day without human direction," Moses wrote.

Then the AI agents found the zero-day path traversal vulnerability in MONAI Label, and this time they were able to gain access to the server.

"The agents identified the application's framework, mapped its API endpoints, and found an upload endpoint with a filename parameter," Moses wrote. They tested that parameter for path traversal, and the parameter was vulnerable. In about 11 minutes, the agents went from mapping the application’s API to arbitrarily writing files on the server."

Later, the agents abused the same flaw to execute a Python reverse shell running as root.

In another session, after the agents discovered the zero-day, the human operator provided them with a proxy that tunneled into a second university network, and connected to a server running the same vulnerable code.

Amazon's discovery follows an earlier report by Gambit Security's threat intelligence team that also documented a Chinese operator using Cairn to carry out at least 105 attacks, and managing to compromise, "to varying degrees," at least 27 companies.

This campaign also occurred in September. In addition to Cairn, the attacker used two other open source AI harnesses to steal credit card records and other sensitive data from a Fortune 500 hospitality company, a major US airline, a large private US industrial supplies distributor, and a US online fashion retailer, and other companies.

"Together, these cases show that this autonomous AI agent framework is being adopted in both espionage and criminal operations," Moses wrote. ®

To deploy an AI-built app to your own server, you do five things: get the code into a Git repository, find out whether it is static files or a server, decide where its backend lives, build it into a container image with the right environment variables, and run that image behind a reverse proxy that serves your domain over HTTPS. Lovable, Bolt.new, Replit, v0 and Claude Code all take this path; what differs is the stack each one produces and the backend it leans on.

Every builder fact below comes from the builder's own documentation, checked on 11 November 2026. The Dockerfiles and the Caddy and Docker Compose files in the builder guides were built and run in Docker the same day, on Node.js 22, against fresh apps from the Vite, TanStack Start and Next.js starters and a small Express and PostgreSQL app.

Every builder hosts what it builds: Lovable publishes to lovable.app with custom domains and a managed backend, Bolt has Bolt Cloud hosting with .bolt.host addresses, Replit publishes to its own Google Cloud infrastructure, and v0 publishes to Vercel. Lovable's own docs recommend starting on Lovable, syncing the code with Git, and moving parts only when you hit a real constraint. Stay where you are when:

Move when you need something the builder's hosting does not give you: a fixed monthly price, data in a region or on hardware you choose, other services on the same machine, root access, or an app that no longer depends on one vendor. The comparisons of your own server against Vercel and against hosting platforms in general go through the trade-offs.

Then clone the repository and run the app on your own computer. If it does not start locally, it will not start on a server, and a local run shows you every environment variable it expects.

Open package.json and look at the dependencies and the build and start scripts.

Build-time public variables. Vite exposes variables that start with VITE_ to the browser and replaces them in the code at build time; Next.js does the same with NEXT_PUBLIC_. Their values end up inside the JavaScript every visitor downloads, so:

Runtime variables. Everything else, read on the server while the app runs: DATABASE_URL, API secrets, session secrets. These go into the container's environment on the server, from a file that never enters the repository. A server-rendered app can need a public value twice: a Lovable TanStack Start app reads its Supabase URL and key as VITE_ values in the browser and again as SUPABASE_URL and SUPABASE_PUBLISHABLE_KEY on the server.

Collect both lists before you build. Lovable keeps its public Supabase values in the repository's .env; Bolt keeps environment variables in .env but its secrets only on Bolt's servers; Replit's secrets must be re-entered; v0 keeps everything in the Vercel project's settings.

What breaks after you move an AI-built app, and why

Lovable apps, and Bolt apps whose database you claimed into Supabase, sign users in through Supabase. Its Site URL is the default redirect address that confirmation and password-reset emails use, and its Redirect URLs list says where users may be sent after sign-in. In a Supabase project of your own, both are on the dashboard's URL Configuration page; for Lovable Cloud, Lovable's hosting guide says only to add the new address to the auth provider's allowed redirect URLs.

OpsCrew is an open-source tool that takes an app from its GitHub repository to a hardened server on your own cloud account. It builds the container image in GitHub Actions with its own Dockerfile for Vite and Next.js apps and Node.js servers such as Express (or yours, if the repository has one), and handles VITE_ and NEXT_PUBLIC_ values as build-time values that are public by nature. It deploys to a new server on Hetzner Cloud or DigitalOcean, or to a server you already have, buys or connects your domain on Cloudflare, and runs a database next to your app when you want one, or keeps the hosted one you already use. Electric Vehicles Now Make Up Nearly 1/3rd of Global New Car Sales: Study

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The conversion of the automotive industry over to electric vehicles, sold as a foregone conclusion by many car brands in the early 2020s, may have largely stalled out in America, but the picture is far different globally. According to data from the Commonwealth Bank of Australia, EVs now account for 31 percent of all passenger vehicle sales.

And the global increase came despite falling sales in China. The major driver of electric vehicle sales has been Europe; European EV sales have risen 37 percent year over year so far in 2026, climbing to 43 percent of the new vehicle sales market. CommBank data also showed substantial growth rates in countries such as Brazil (EV sales up 164 percent, to a 13 percent market share) and India (EV sales up 76 percent, to a 9 percent market share). Even in Japan, where manufacturers have been notably EV-reticent, sales jumped 80 percent year-over-year to 5 percent of the market.

Meanwhile, electric vehicle sales have declined in the United States this year. According to Cox Automotive data, EV sales made up 6.4 percent of the new car market in Q3 of 2026, down from more than 10 percent in Q3 of 2025. Fuel prices have been elevated this year, but that has led Americans to gravitate toward hybrids rather than fully electric vehicles. Total electrified vehicles now account for nearly 25 percent of the American market.

Much of the decline has been a direct result of Trump administration policies. Last year, the U.S. eliminated a $7500 federal tax credit for electric cars, which—due to a commercial vehicle loophole—made them especially cheap for buyers to lease; the 10 percent share of sales in Q3 2025 came from a glut of sales as buyers rushed to take advantage of the tax credits before they expired. Congress is also considering imposing an annual federal tax on electric cars.

The Trump administration has also effectively eliminated enforcement of America’s CAFE standards, which would have effectively forced manufacturers to sell EVs to avoid hefty fines. American laws and tariffs also effectively prohibit importing affordable Chinese-made EVs, which have been a major reason behind the sales increase in Europe.

Similar maneuvers are increasingly common across the AI data center market as companies look for ways to deal with ballooning debt balances.

That's obviously quite difficult if they're in space. Meerkats cross the desert together, but what happens when one finds itself alone?

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Travel  |  Read Source →

Researchers tracked 20 meerkats in the Kalahari Desert to see how they navigated familiar territory after being separated from their groups.

Meerkats leave their burrows together each morning and forage as a group. How well can one find its way alone?

In South Africa’s Kalahari Desert, researchers carried 20 adult meerkats up to 0.9 miles from where they caught them.

After release, 18 reached a familiar spot or found their group. Fourteen headed first to the burrow where the group had slept.

The team found no clear advantage for older or more experienced animals.

The researchers suggest that daily group travel may give meerkats enough experience to navigate alone.

The study was led by Mathilde Martin of the University of Zurich. The team worked alongside the Kalahari Meerkat Project, which follows about 15 wild groups accustomed to people.

The project is run from the Kalahari Research Centre, which is known as the KRC.

“At the KRC, our work with meerkats allows us to distract them with a water bottle and fit their collar in a few seconds while they stand upright drinking; a mere formality for them,” Martin said.

A GPS tag on each collar logged the position of the animal once every second. A radio transmitter was also fitted, along with a small audio recorder for the meerkat’s calls.

The team tested a total of 20 adults from five groups between January and March 2025. Fifteen of these animals were male, whereas only five of them were female.

Each meerkat was caught while its group was foraging and was released up to 0.9 miles (1.5 kilometers) away.

Such a distance is greater than the ground usually covered by a meerkat group in a whole day.

The release sites were located inside the territory of each group. The team chose areas that the group had not visited for at least 30 days.

Homing experiments of this kind have previously been carried out on honeybees and desert ants. Highly social animals that travel in tight groups have rarely been tested in the same way.

This is because the knowledge of one animal is difficult to separate from the decisions of the group.

Eighteen of the 20 meerkats left the release site. The sleeping burrow, the capture spot or the group itself was reached by all 18 of them.

Some of the meerkats departed almost at once, whereas others circled the release site for nearly 90 minutes.

The area covered by them ranged from half an acre to almost 17 acres (2,050 to 68,200 square meters).

A meerkat rarely strayed from its course once a direction had been chosen. Sixteen of the animals aimed for a fixed place.

The sleeping burrow was visited first by 14 of those 16.

On average, the straight-line distance to the target amounted to 82% of the distance actually walked by the meerkat.

The release areas had not been visited by the group for a month. The team therefore concluded that the meerkats had been navigating from long-term memory.

Two males never left the release site, and they were carried back to their groups after 90 minutes. They were no younger or less experienced than the others.

The authors suspect that stress kept them there, and not a lack of knowledge.

Some meerkats had been expected to navigate better than others. Subordinate males sometimes leave the group and go alone in search of mates.

Other females are occasionally driven out for a while by the dominant female.

Solo trips of this kind could be counted as practice. The researchers also considered the age of each animal, together with the time it spent on guard duty.

Attention was given as well to how often it scent-marked the territory or dug at shelters.

None of those traits predicted how long a meerkat explored. Neither did they predict how accurately it set off or how straight it travelled.

No clear difference was found between males and females either, although only five females took part.

The authors think that the explanation lies in the territory itself. It is small and stable, with 0.3 to 0.6 miles (0.5 to 1 kilometer) covered by a group daily.

The whole territory has probably been crossed several times by each member by the time adulthood is reached.

The authors wrote that pigeons of different ages are likewise able to find their way home equally well.

On the way home, the meerkats spent about two-thirds of their time searching for cues. Most of the remaining time was spent moving.

Few of them stopped to eat, and those that did only grabbed millipedes crawling on the sand.

The animals stood upright or climbed a bush or tree trunk in order to scan the landscape. Overall, they spent 10 times longer on looking than on sniffing.

This balance between looking and sniffing was altered late in the trip. By that stage, a meerkat had reached its target and was searching for its group. Sniffing then took up more than 20% of its cue-searching time.

Along the way, meerkats were often seen sniffing around boltholes. These are the small shelters that the animals dig across their territory. An earlier experiment had found that meerkats remember where their boltholes are.

The authors suggested that boltholes may serve as landmarks in a meerkat’s memory of its territory.

The search was not ended by reaching the burrow, because the group could have moved on since the capture.

Thirteen of the 20 meerkats rejoined their groups on their own. The slowest took almost three hours.

The burrow or the capture site was reached by five others. The group had nevertheless not been found by them when the three-hour limit ran out.

These animals were caught again and returned by the team.

The researchers could not explain why some meerkats succeeded while others did not. Success was not predicted by a straighter trip to the burrow.

Nor was it predicted by the number of scent marks left behind by the group.

The tracking of a group was compared by the authors to a predator following its prey. The target keeps moving and its route is not known to the searcher.

Twenty animals make up a small sample, as the authors wrote. All of them were subordinate adults older than 10 months, so pups and dominant animals were not tested.

Every release was also made inside familiar territory. The results therefore do not show how a meerkat would manage in a place it does not know.

Two reasons for looking around could not be fully separated either. A lone meerkat that scans the landscape may be checking for predators as much as searching for landmarks.

If many group members know the territory, the next question is who leads when the group moves.

The authors want to know whether certain traits influence which animal takes the lead. The physical needs of an animal and its social rank are two examples.

Co-author Benjamin Robira said the team now wants to understand what makes up a meerkat’s mental picture of its territory.

The researchers also intend to study how the animals get from one point to another.

The full study was published in the Journal of Experimental Biology.
