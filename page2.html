Jadan Baugh helps No. 20 Florida beat Florida State for third straight time, 26-10

📅 Nov 28, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Jadan Baugh became the first Florida tailback with consecutive 1,000-yard rushing seasons and scored on a 3-yard run to help the No. 20 Gators beat Florida State 26-10 on Friday.

Baugh had 77 yards on 19 carries for the Gators (8-4), who have won six of eight games against the Seminoles (5-7).

Aaron Philo completed 9 of 12 passes for 127 yards for Florida before leaving because of an injury. Backup Trammel Jones Jr. had a 1-yard touchdown run but also had a fourth-quarter interception.

The Seminoles, down to their fifth running back due to injury, managed just 98 yards on 37 carries.

Ashton Daniels completed 18 of 32 passes for 183 yards and two interceptions for Florida State. He left the field for part of a series due to injury and was replaced by Kevin Sperry, who had a fourth-down 9-yard touchdown pass to Landen Thomas.

Micahi Danzy had a 64-yard catch-and-run to set up a Florida State field goal.

Florida: The Gators forced seven punts and recorded a fourth-quarter interception to wrap up their third straight victory in the series.

Florida State: The Seminoles couldn't build many drives on offense and they couldn't flip the field as the punters averaged 32.9 yards, forcing the defense to protect short fields.

As digital transformation and artificial intelligence redefine corporate operations and personal finance, choosing between service giant Accenture (ACN +0.56%) and software leader Intuit (INTU +0.08%) represents a fundamental choice for investors.

Accenture focuses on global enterprise consulting and technology implementation, while Intuit dominates the tax and accounting software market for individuals and small businesses. Both companies leverage massive data sets to improve their platforms, but they operate with different growth profiles and capital structures.

Accenture serves approximately 9,000 clients, including about 75% of the Fortune Global 100 and 500 companies. This broad reach across the tech stocks landscape is supported by 18 key technology ecosystem partners that generate around 65% of its total revenue. The company maintains deep roots with its clients, as 85% of its top 200 commercial partners have worked with the firm for 20 years or more.

In the 12 months ended Aug. 31, 2026, Accenture reported revenue of about $74.2 billion, representing 6.5% growth compared to the prior year. The company delivered GAAP net income of approximately $8.4 billion during this period. This steady growth reflects a consistent demand for digital transformation services across its five major industry groups, including communications and financial services.

Accenture maintains a debt-to-equity ratio of about 0.4x, which compares its total debt to shareholder equity. As of its August 2026 balance sheet, the current ratio is approximately 1.4x, indicating it can cover short-term liabilities with short-term assets. In the fiscal year ended Aug. 31, 2026, free cash flow was about $11.6 billion, representing the cash left after capital investments.

Intuit serves about 93 million customers through its integrated financial technology platform, which includes household names like TurboTax and QuickBooks. The company provides essential tools for tax preparation, small business accounting, and credit monitoring to a global user base. Its strategy focuses on an AI-driven ecosystem that automates business workflows and financial management for consumers and mid-market enterprises.

In the 12 months ended Oct. 31, 2026, Intuit achieved revenue of about $21.9 billion, which marked a 12.8% increase over the previous year. The company generated GAAP net income of approximately $4.8 billion during this same period. These results highlight the performance of its financial technology ecosystem, particularly as it expands into larger enterprise markets while maintaining its core consumer base.

Intuit reported a debt-to-equity ratio of about 0.4x, while free cash flow reached about $8.7 billion in the fiscal year ended July 31, 2026. As of its July 2026 balance sheet, the current ratio of approximately 1.5x suggests it can meet short-term obligations. Note that stock-based compensation represented roughly 23.3% of operating cash flow, which inflates reported cash generation since SBC is a non-cash expense added back in the cash flow statement.

Accenture faces intense competition from global technology providers like Amazon (AMZN +3.96%), offshore IT firms, and specialized consultancies. The company also manages significant cybersecurity risks, as sophisticated attacks targeting sensitive client data could damage its reputation and financial standing. Additionally, uncertain economic conditions may lead clients to reduce or defer spending on major digital projects.

Intuit faces aggressive competition from both established entities and emerging start-ups, including potential government-funded tax software alternatives. The company also relies on third-party relationships with financial institutions and digital platform stores like those operated by Apple (AAPL +0.83%). Disruptions in these partnerships or shifts in generative AI accuracy could harm operations and customer trust.

Accenture offers a lower P/S ratio, measuring price against sales over the past twelve months, while Intuit features a lower Forward P/E using future earnings estimates.

I'd go with Intuit. Millions of people and small businesses count on its software every day for taxes and bookkeeping, from TurboTax to QuickBooks, and Intuit has built AI into those tools to handle chores like payroll, paying bills, and sending invoices. What impresses me is the momentum: Sales rose 12% year over year in its latest quarter, and customers can already use QuickBooks right inside popular AI chatbots.

Intuit does have things to work through, though. Its growth is expected to slow to about 8% to 9% this fiscal year. Even so, its AI push gives it room to keep growing.

To be fair to Accenture, it's a giant at helping companies put AI to work, and it returns lots of cash to shareholders. But analysts keep asking whether AI will let clients pay for fewer consulting hours, and Accenture says prices fell in many areas last quarter. Only about 3% of last year's 5% growth came from its own business, and it expects just 3% to 6% growth in the year ahead.

Intuit, meanwhile, is growing faster and turning that growth into even bigger profit gains. For long-term investors, a software leader with momentum like this makes Intuit the better buy for 2027.

The holiday movies have officially taken over the 2026 TV schedule, and I could not be happier. Between Hallmark’s Countdown to Christmas lineup, Netflix’s releases, and even Hulu’s 12 Dates of Christmas, the options have been endless. Now, Prime Video is getting in on the fun with the release of Clashing Through the Snow. As of writing this, the festive flick is sitting in the number three spot on the streamer’s trending charts, but don’t let the popularity fool you. Some fans are really upset that the film featured a character asking ChatGPT for relationship advice, and I agree with their outrage.

The romantic comedy follows rival book editors Hannah (Michelle Randolph) and Ryan (Christopher Briney) who, after learning their jobs were in jeopardy unless they could wow their boss, set out to land major book deals. When weather and a series of misfortunes halt their respective travel plans, the two begrudgingly agree to road trip to New York together. The proximity allows them to bond, and sparks start to fly, complicating their career ambitions.

Clashing Through the Snow is overflowing with witty banter, memorable scenes, and hilarious and endearing side characters, which is why it was so upsetting when Ryan opted to ask ChatGPT for relationship advice instead of calling his friend Walter (Andrew Schulz), who had already given him advice earlier in the film.

Things weren’t much better over on Threads, where fans also made their distaste for the inclusion abundantly clear:

I don’t know what’s more upsetting: the fact that the film had a Ryan ask ChatGPT for advice or the fact that he was a senior book editor with human friends he could have turned to.

What might be the most infuriating thing is that apparently there was a version of Clashing through the Snow where Ryan did turn to a human instead of ChatGPT. At least that’s what Carlson Young, the film’s director, told Deadline before defending her decision to go with the scene in the film, saying:

I personally thought that him talking to Chat was so Ryan. It made me laugh so much. And the amount of times that I’ve caught my husband… I’m like, ‘Are you asking ChatGPT a question? Like for real? Are you serious? ‘And he is serious. It just felt really modern and so of the times, and something like his character would actually do. And I like a lot the responses that Chat has for him. It just made me laugh.

Maybe I missed something, but I can’t wrap my head around hopeless romantic, pragmatic Ryan being the character who would turn to ChatGPT to ask for serious relationship help. That seems like a job he would have wanted a human who can experience actual love to help with, like Walter or even some random stranger he could have stumbled upon.

As upsetting as it is to see an amazing character turn to AI for help, I don’t think one scene should tarnish the rest of the movie. Clashing Through the Snow is a swoonworthy and hilarious rom-com that does deserve its flowers. Dare I say it might be Briney's best rom-com role (yes, even better than his portrayal as Conrad Fisher in The Summer I Turned Pretty).

Mike Macdonald on Sam Darnold, when healthy: "I think he's done a good job"

Quarterback Sam Darnold is back as Seattle’s starter this week, after missing two games due to quad and rib injuries. It’s an opportunity to improve on what has been a challenging start to the 2026 season.

On Friday, coach Mike Macdonald was asked to assess Darnold’s performance when healthy.

“I think he’s done a good job,” Macdonald said. “As a football team, we’ve been good enough to go 6-4, and we want to get better. Yeah, he’s right there with everybody else.”

Well, he’s not right there with the player who replaced him. Drew Lock has started three games and played all but one drive of another. His record is 4-0. Darnold is 2-4.

And their numbers are starkly different. Darnold has thrown 10 touchdown passes and nine interceptions. Lock has thrown seven touchdown passes and no interceptions.

Lock’s passer rating is 125.6. Darnold’s is 79.5.

Lock’s games have come against the Patriots, the Cardinals twice, and the Raiders. Darnold has faced the Commanders, Chargers, 49ers, Broncos, Chiefs, and Bears — a much tougher slate of opponents.

The Seahawks won a Super Bowl with Darnold. That counts for something. Still, they’re 6-4. They have an opportunity to get a seventh win on Sunday, with Brock Purdy out for the game against the 49ers.
