If you need to change the Wi-Fi password, check who is connected or update the firmware, the login below is the way in.

The full model guide, with the steps and fixes in order, is on WiFi Router Zone.

Once you are in, the useful pages are the wireless settings, where you change the network name and Wi-Fi password on each band; the DHCP client list, which shows every device on your network; and the management or firmware section, where you check for updates and reboot the router. Change the Wi-Fi password first if the router is still on its factory settings, and write the new password down before you save it.

The page never loads. Make sure you are connected to the DIR-806A itself, not mobile data or another network, and check the address — D-Link uses 192.168.0.1, not 192.168.1.1. On Windows, ipconfig shows your real gateway on the Default Gateway line. Note that dlinkrouter.local only works while you are connected to the D-Link router. The same check-the-gateway-first habit applies to every brand — see our Hathway router login guide for another worked example.

Should You Buy Dutch Bros Stock Before the End of November?

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Does the calendar matter for Dutch Bros investors? Not really, but the price might.

I can't quite shake Dutch Bros (BROS -2.16%). The stock has spent most of 2026 sliding downhill. On Nov. 17, it sits near $35 a share, about 43% below where it started the year. Every time Dutch Bros' stock looks like a bargain, it finds a new floor to fall through. Meanwhile, the drive-through coffee shop chain keeps opening locations like it gets paid per ribbon-cutting.

So, should you buy before the end of November? Let me spare you the suspense: the calendar doesn't care. There's no earnings report due before the month runs out, and the next one isn't expected until February 2027. If you buy Dutch Bros stock, you should do it because of the price and the business, not because November is running out of days.

The third-quarter report gave Wall Street plenty to grumble about. Revenue jumped 30%, which sounds great until you lift the lid. Systemwide customer visits grew a whisper-thin 0.4%. Nearly all the same-shop sales growth came from people paying more per order, not from more cars pulling up to the window.

In other words, Dutch Bros' famous round-the-block car lines stopped growing longer in recent months.

Management also trimmed its full-year outlook. It now expects at least $2.1 billion in revenue and roughly $380 million in adjusted earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization (EBITDA), after calling for up to $2.13 billion and $390 million in August.

Here's what keeps me interested. Dutch Bros ended September with 1,273 shops and plans to open about 250 more in 2027. Management says it has already lined up the real estate for 2,029 shops by 2029.

I appreciate punny business goals, but it's also a downright ambitious target. Staying on track to meet it is an impressive feat, and Dutch Bros did it again in Q3 2026.

After the 2026 sell-off, Dutch Bros trades at about 30 times forward earnings. That's a steep multiple for a restaurant chain, but a more modest one for a company growing revenue near 30% a year.

If you have a long investing horizon and a sturdy stomach (more coffee might help), a 43% price drop for a business growing this fast deserves a closer look. November isn't a deadline for buying Dutch Bros stock, but it does look tempting right now.

How to Maintain Long, Healthy Hair Over 50, According to Experts

From top treatments to when to plan your next trim, here’s everything to know

Every product on this page was chosen by a Harper's BAZAAR editor. We may earn commission on some of the items you choose to buy.

There’s something undeniably chic about a head of long, luscious hair. Think glossy, waist-skimming, bouncy, voluminous lengths that make extensions an option rather than a long-term commitment. What’s more, long hair provides the ultimate canvas for experimentation, with styling possibilities that feel limitless.

As hair matures, however, maintaining the same level of shine, strength, and vitality can require more attention. As texture changes and dryness increases, the key is to adapt your routine to give your longer lengths the care they deserve. “Long hair can look beautiful at any age, but keeping mature hair glossy and healthy-looking is all about replenishing moisture and maintaining shine,” says celebrity hairstylist Naomi Brooks. “I always recommend prioritising hydrating products that help smooth the hair cuticle, improve softness, and enhance shine, alongside regular trims to keep the lengths and ends looking healthy.”

If you’re considering making the transition and letting your bob grow out, look to a host of celebrities for inspiration. From Demi Moore and Naomi Campbell to Andie MacDowell and Claudia Schiffer, countless iterations are proving just how effortless long hair can look on women over 50. As Brooks explains: “Demi Moore is a fantastic example of how beautiful long hair can look at any age. Her hair is consistently glossy, healthy-looking, and full of movement.”

While genetics certainly play a role, Moore’s glossy lengths are also the result of great care and strategic products. Her hairstylist, Dimitris Giannetos, revealed on social media that he often relies on Kérastase’s Gloss Absolu Glaze Drops Anti-Frizz Hair Oil to reduce frizz, boost shine, and ensure hair remains sleek without looking too heavy. Indeed, experts recommend investing in a high-quality hydrating treatment to ensure mature hair is treated with extra care. “Mature hair can become drier, finer or more fragile over time, so maintaining moisture and shine is important,” says Warren Edwards, creative director at Warren & the Hare.

Additionally, for those with gray or silver hair, session stylist Danilo Giangreco recommends incorporating a clear gloss into your routine to enhance shine. This can also help counteract any unwanted brassiness, ensuring hair appears brighter and healthier.

Another expert-approved tip is to keep heat styling to a minimum where possible. If you do rely on hot tools, applying heat protectant can help shield strands from damage while preserving moisture, strength, and shine.

Finally, contrary to popular belief, long hair thrives on regular maintenance. Though it may seem counterintuitive when trying to grow your hair, routine appointments are essential to keep split ends at bay and prevent the ends from looking too thin or tired. In turn, this helps keep long, mature hair looking fuller, healthier, and overall more glossy.

OpenBot: How to Run a Team of AI Agents on Your Computer

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Posted on Nov 17 • Originally published at blense.tech

Everyone has been part of a school group project where each member wrote their section alone at home without talking to anyone else. The night before the deadline, someone pasted everything into a single document. The result was an academic Frankenstein: the introduction ignored the conclusion, every section cited sources differently, and arguments contradicted each other right on the page.

Despite rapid progress in language models, most of our day-to-day work with AI still repeats that exact dynamic. We open a chat window, ask a question, copy the response, and close the tab. The next second, all project context and reasoning vanish.

It feels like hiring a brilliant freelancer with severe amnesia: they have to relearn your entire business from scratch before every single task.

For one-off questions, that transactional relationship works fine. For ongoing workflows—code maintenance, product launches, support triage, or operational monitoring—the real leverage comes from accumulating context over weeks. That is the shift OpenBot, a source-available project by Synthetify Labs, puts into practice by replacing the disposable chatbot with a resident operational team right on your computer.

OpenBot's core difference isn't launching yet another proprietary model. The desktop app is built on a local-first architecture and follows a Bring Your Own Plan model: instead of charging token markups, it connects to the AI subscriptions and API keys you already pay for—or to local model servers running on your own hardware.

Rather than relying on ephemeral cloud threads, every team member gets a continuous technical environment on your machine built on four pillars:

In practice, the source-available codebase in nightly-labs/openbot (under the PolyForm Noncommercial 1.0.0 license, free for personal use on macOS, Windows, and Linux, with remote access via web, iOS, and Android) turns AI from an isolated utility into a permanent deskmate.

With context preserved on disk, agents no longer live in solitary tabs—they coexist in shared channels, much like a #launch room, except populated by humans and models from different vendors working side by side.

In the same thread, an agent backed by Codex can investigate a bug, another on Claude Code writes tests or reviews the patch, while a third on Gemini or Grok organizes release notes. The ecosystem also supports OpenCode (including free models with no account required), Cursor, Cline, Muse Code, Pi, Inception's Mercury models, any agent compatible with the Agent Client Protocol (ACP)—such as Goose, Qwen Code, and GitHub Copilot CLI—and 100% local models via Ollama and LM Studio.

Working together in a persistent space unlocks a decisive shift: strict specialization. Instead of a generic assistant doing everything adequately, the official OpenBot Marketplace catalog features 27 agents with narrow scopes and explicit boundaries:

Putting brilliant specialists in the same room creates an obvious risk: without a protocol, everyone talks and edits files at the same time.

To prevent collisions, OpenBot channels run on crash-safe FIFO message queues (First In, First Out) with strict state control. At any given moment, there is only a single task owner.

A real workflow shows how this engine turns without human micromanagement:

An assistant that only acts when you type a command still demands constant mental overhead to remember, prompt, and follow up. To remove that friction, OpenBot attaches scheduled routines directly to resident agents.

Think of them as classic cron jobs, except they wake up the full language model with access to local context, accumulated history, and authenticated browser tabs. Chief of Staff wakes on weekday mornings to cross-check your calendar, emails, and commitments and post a ~25-line briefing before your workday starts. QA Tester runs morning smoke checks against production, while Ops Digest sweeps Notion, Airtable, or Trello boards on Friday afternoons to flag overdue items.

Yet the most mature engineering detail in this architecture is the silence rule, implemented through the routine_no_update function. In immature automations, if nothing broke overnight, the bot still sends a cheerful message saying everything looks fine—flooding your phone with operational noise.

A well-designed routine works like a smart alarm clock: it wakes up on its own, reads your emails, checks your flight status, and only wakes you up if boarding has been canceled.

When an agent runs its routine in OpenBot and finds no anomalies or relevant updates, its instructions tell it to call routine_no_update. The channel stays completely silent, protecting human focus.

Giving background agents access to local directories, a terminal, and an authenticated browser raises an inevitable security question. OpenBot's technical documentation is candid about this: after initial consent, local agents run with danger-full-access permissions and approvalPolicy: never inside the local environment.

Model ML raises $250M to automate investment banking grunt work

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Model ML, a London-based startup that uses AI to automate work traditionally performed by junior bankers and consultants, including building financial models, preparing pitch decks, and writing due diligence reports, has raised a $250 million in Series B funding at a $1.5 billion valuation. The new round, which comes a year after its $75 million Series A round was announced, was co-led by General Atlantic and earlier backer QED Investors.

Brothers Chaz and Arnie Englander co-founded the three-year-old startup after previously co-founding two other companies, including Fat Llama, a peer-to-peer rental marketplace acquired by Swedish rival Hygglo for $41.5 million in 2022.

Model ML develops AI agents that integrate with clients’ systems, data, and tools, including Excel and PowerPoint. The startup says its agents understand the full context of deals that bankers and consultants are working on, and can automatically update financial models and presentations when new developments affect their analysis.

Other startups automating the routine, repetitive tasks for investment bankers and management consultants include Hebbia and Rogo, which raised $160 million at a $2 billion valuation in April.

Anthropic and OpenAI have also built tools for financial professionals, but Model ML says it runs on open-source models and that doing so allows it to operate at about 2% of the cost of frontier models while delivering faster results.

James Bond Director Reveals The Most Important Quality Of Robert Pattinson's 007

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Now that Robert Pattinson has officially been cast as James Bond, Amazon MGM's plans for the franchise are coming together. The new owners of the Bond IP are clearly sparing no expense for the upcoming "Bond 26," hiring a major director in Denis Villeneuve to shepherd the project, which will now be led by one of the biggest names in Hollywood. After the missteps that were Daniel Craig's last few films in the tux, it seems Amazon is aiming to re-establish Bond movies as must-see cinematic events. For Villeneuve, however, the star power of his Bond actor is less important than the ability to project the energy of a cold-blooded killer.

In an interview with GQ, the "Dune" director was asked what he looks for in a 007 actor. "A first quality is that I need to believe that this man is a killer," he replied. "Because more than a spy, Bond is an assassin, and I need that kind of strength. I need that kind of anger deep in the eye. It's not everybody who has that." Those who doubt whether Pattinson can do so need only take a glance at the casting announcement video, in which the actor's Bond emerges from the darkness aiming his Walther PPK and wearing a cold, resolute expression on his face. There's a definite edge to his brief appearance in the clip, and you can see why Villeneuve was impressed.

Denis Villeneuve unpacks the process of casting the newest James Bond: Robert Pattinson. https://t.co/DLgqqUErYa pic.twitter.com/FWB1l6r2L8

That said, it seems Pattinson wasn't actually the first choice for Bond. According to a recent report, Villeneuve initially settled on "Wake Up Dead Man: A Knives Out Mystery" actor Josh O'Connor, who ultimately dropped out and cleared the way for Pattinson. But that will almost certainly prove to be a positive thing.

Robert Pattinson's casting as James Bond breaks a 60-year tradition of the franchise introducing audiences to relative unknowns in the lead role. But that's a good thing, as Bond is in need of revitalization following the debacle that was 2021's "No Time to Die." Pattinson's star power will help restore the franchise's popularity. But the "Batman" star is more than just an A-lister with a built-in audience. He's easily one of the most talented actors of his generation, having demonstrated an extraordinary ability to completely transform on-screen. As Bond, he'll no doubt exude exactly the kind of subtly sinister energy Denis Villeneuve is looking for and which has been one of the most important aspects of the character since his introduction.

In 1962, "Dr. No" kicked off cinema's most enduring franchise by introducing audiences to Sean Connery in the lead role. Rather than casting an established star with the kind of refined energy you'd expect of a gentleman spy, producers Albert R. Broccoli and Harry Saltzman hired a working class former footballer from Scotland, and it was a masterstroke. 007 debuted on the big screen with an edge that only Connery could bring to the role, and that's been an integral aspect of the cinematic incarnation of the character ever since. Sure, director Terence Young helped round off some of Connery's rough edges, but he'd be nothing without the sense of danger the Scotsman brought to the screen. Thankfully, it seems both Villeneuve and Pattinson recognize that, and it's yet another aspect of this casting that bodes well for "Bond 26."

Denis Villeneuve clearly values the killer instinct aspect of James Bond above everything else. But the director has plenty of other requirements for the role. As he went on to tell GQ, "I need someone who has that kind of unapologetic panache, someone that [...] when he comes into the room everything starts to gravitate around him, that kind of strong charisma." It's a foundational aspect of Bond and a characteristic every actor to portray the spy has possessed. But that's not all Villeneuve is looking for.

The French-Canadian filmmaker went on to say he also looks for "sensuality" in his Bond actor, as well as "dark humor" and an ability to take "pleasure into being in deep s***." It's standard stuff for Bond, but it's reassuring to know Villeneuve has such a solid grasp of the fundamentals. Beyond that, the director described someone "strong, muscular, someone that is a force," as well as an individual with a "deep intelligence in the eyes" and who is "always ahead of us." Sounds about right to me. Thankfully, Robert Pattinson can fulfill every one of these requirements, which makes him one of the most exciting of all the James Bond actors.

Thus far, Bond fans have reacted to Pattinson's casting with positivity, even while there's been the predictable objections regarding his "Twilight" years and physicality. At this point, however, the British star has long since proved his ability to deliver consistently electric performances that always surprise. There's no reason to think his Bond portrayal will be any different. Heck, Villeneuve was convinced, and he clearly seems to know what he's talking about.

Denver-based Frontier Airlines has rapidly cut a total of 16 international routes from its network this year, with Atlanta (ATL), one of the airline's major bases, losing a total of eight routes overall. These network changes have taken place between March and August 2026.

Data from Cirium, an aviation analytics company, shows that the budget airline has reconfigured its international operations. It is redeploying aircraft on more lucrative domestic or other international routes following a difficult year for airlines in the face of high fuel prices and other operational demands. This data has been reviewed against 2026 and 2027 flight schedules.

Frontier is the second-largest operator at Atlanta. However, it is still just a marginal player when compared to Delta, which calls Atlanta home. Frontier operates around 6% of all flights from Atlanta, according to the airport's website. The airline trimmed eight nonstop international routes from the airport in April of this year.

These affected services to Puerto Vallarta (PVR), San José del Cabo (SJD), Santo Domingo (SDQ), Nassau (NAS), Aruba (AUA), Providenciales (PLS), St Maarten (SXM), and San Pedro Sula (SAP). This leaves Frontier with a network of 40 domestic and six year-round international destinations from Atlanta.

The remaining five year-round services from Atlanta to Cancún (CUN), Guatemala City (GUA), Montego Bay (MBJ), San José, Costa Rica (SJO), and San Salvador (SAL). There is also a seasonal link to Punta Cana (PUJ).

Earlier this year, Denver (DEN) lost two international services served by Frontier. The airline withdrew its operations to San José del Cabo (SJD) from the Colorado airport in March, while Denver to Puerto Vallarta (PVR) lost its international link in April. This was followed by Charlotte (CLT) also losing its international route to Cancún (CUN) after the airline pulled service on the city pair in April.

This leaves the airline with no international routes from Charlotte. Now, it only has domestic flights to Baltimore (BWI), Chicago–O'Hare (ORD), Dallas/Fort Worth (DFW), Denver, Detroit (DTW), Fort Lauderdale (FLL), Houston–Intercontinental (IAH), Miami (MIA), New York–LaGuardia (LGA), Orlando (MCO), Philadelphia (PHL), San Juan (SJU), and Tampa (TPA).

Frontier's international route from Raleigh/Durham (RDU) to Cancún was also lost in May. Meanwhile, Orlando's link to St Maarten (SXM) was axed in summer, after the airline pulled the pin in August.

Atlanta loses multiple leisure routes while competitors like Delta expand to absorb sunseeking travelers from the hub.

The decision to consolidate a number of international routes was also seen in both Chicago and Miami, with Frontier terminating two services from each city. Chicago Midway (MDW) lost its service to Cancún in April, while O'Hare (ORD) lost its link to Punta Cana (PUJ) in the Dominican Republic.

This leaves Frontier with 23 domestic routes from O'Hare, and a further nine domestic routes from Midway. Frontier is the second-largest airline at Chicago Midway, the city's second busiest airport after O'Hare. While Southwest operates more than 91% of all flights at Midway, Frontier served over half a million passengers between June 2025 and May 2026, accounting for 3% of all traffic.

Packers fan kicked out of Lambeau Field for spreading loved one’s ashes after loss

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

A Green Bay Packers fan was ejected from Lambeau Field after spreading a loved one’s ashes onto the field following the team’s loss to the Minnesota Vikings.

The incident occurred on Sunday, November 15, after Green Bay suffered a 30-10 defeat at home. Police were called to the stadium after a spectator was spotted dispersing what authorities later confirmed to be cremated human remains.

According to local news station WBAY, officers spoke with the individual and confirmed that the ashes belonged to a loved one. The fan was subsequently removed from the stadium for violating local regulations.

Under Green Bay Municipal Code 24-117, spreading human ashes onto the playing field is considered unlawful conduct at a public event.

The Packers addressed the incident in a statement to WBAY, acknowledging that some supporters want to honor deceased friends and family members at the stadium while emphasizing that the practice is prohibited.

“While we understand the desire of some fans to have their ashes, or those of friends or family, spread at Lambeau Field, it is against the law and prohibited at the stadium,” the team said.

Rather than scattering cremated remains, the organization encouraged supporters to consider alternative ways of remembering loved ones, including its commemorative brick program.

“To honor a loved one’s legacy appropriately at Lambeau Field, we refer fans to the commemorative brick program. We also suggest they can coordinate tributes in other appropriate ways with their funeral home,” the Packers continued.

The program allows supporters to purchase personalized bricks honoring individuals, creating a permanent tribute at the historic stadium without violating its regulations.
