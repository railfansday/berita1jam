We've Witnessed the Stock Market Do This 19 Times in 76 Years, and Equities Have Been Higher One Year Later 100% of the Time

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Past events have a knack for forecasting the future on Wall Street.

This has shaped up as another stellar year for the iconic Dow Jones Industrial Average (^DJI +0.49%), benchmark S&P 500 (^GSPC +1.31%), and growth-focused Nasdaq Composite (^IXIC +1.60%). The artificial intelligence (AI) infrastructure build-out, better-than-expected corporate earnings, and record S&P 500 share buybacks have collectively lifted all three stock indexes to several new highs.

But recent volatility also suggests that equities may be on shaky ground. Just two days after the midterm elections, on Nov. 5, the Nasdaq Composite logged its largest-ever single-day point decline (1,422 points).

While headwinds are clearly mounting on Wall Street, historical precedent offers a different take.

Election Day went, more or less, as the prediction markets had forecast. Democrats swept both houses of Congress, setting up a scenario in which few major spending bills will be signed into law from January 2027 through January 2029.

But there was one surprise on Election Night that clearly unnerved professional and everyday investors. Democrats flipped five governorships previously held by Republicans (with two races still uncalled by the Associated Press, as of Nov. 9), including in Texas, Ohio, and Georgia. These three states are critical to the AI data center build-out. Whereas President Donald Trump favors as little regulation as possible for AI, most Democratic lawmakers want to limit data center expansion and ramp up the vetting of large language models.

Gina Hinojosa may not be as bad for Texas data centers as it looks. Her plan asks for: no subsidies, union labor, no NDAs, and proof household power and water bills won't rise. Much of that is already underway: Amazon, Google, Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI, Oracle and xAI pledged in March to bring their own power and pay for transmission Microsoft pledged to pay full property taxes without seeking tax breaks Nvidia is working with Vistra, NextEra and Constellation on data centers that cut power use when the grid is strained

The stock market entered 2026 at its second-highest valuation in history. A market that's priced for perfection can ill afford a slowdown in the AI infrastructure build-out.

The ideological differences between President Trump and the Democrat-led Congress will likely also make debt-ceiling negotiations more challenging.

If the Dow, S&P 500, and Nasdaq Composite are to reach new highs, they'll have to climb the proverbial wall of worry.

Although no predictive tool can ever guarantee what the Dow, S&P 500, and Nasdaq Composite will do over the short term, some past events have a knack for foreshadowing the future. One, in particular, has a flawless track record, dating back to 1950.

According to data from Morgan Stanley (MS +1.63%), the S&P 500 has been higher at the six- and 12-month marks following each of the previous 19 midterm elections since 1950.

Carson Group's Chief Market Strategist Ryan Detrick also notes that November has been green for the S&P 500 100% of the time following midterm elections over the last 76 years.

Despite all the pre- and post-election noise that investors have been attempting to digest, history shows that stocks consistently head higher after midterm elections, regardless of how the political puzzle pieces are arranged. This data serves as a good reminder that factors such as corporate operating results, capital-return programs, management decisions, and innovation matter far more in the grand scheme of things on Wall Street than the makeup of Congress over the next two years.

Will there be stock market hiccups in the second half of Trump's term? More than likely, yes.

But have the midterm elections materially changed the long-term outlook for equities? A resounding no.

Crown Agents Bank strengthens interest rate risk management with ALMIS

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

EDINBURGH, Scotland, Nov. 12, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Crown Agents Bank, the UK-regulated bank providing cross-border payment solutions across the Global South, has strengthened its interest rate risk management through its partnership with ALMIS International Ltd. (ALMIS). This follows the introduction of a multi-currency interest rate swap hedging programme that has materially reduced the bank's interest rate sensitivity.

Using Cobalt, the ALMIS Treasury Management System (TMS), together with ALMIS Hedge Accounting, Crown Agents Bank introduced interest rate swaps for the first time, moving from vendor selection to live hedging in around four months.

ALMIS was chosen following a competitive selection process, enabling the bank to extend its hedging toolkit beyond bonds to interest rate swaps. The partnership has since expanded, with Crown Agents Bank now working with ALMIS to bring high-quality liquid assets (HQLA) fixed income assets onto Cobalt.

With a core banking system that continues to serve its payments and FX business well, the bank needed a modular solution that could add interest rate swap and hedge accounting capability alongside its existing systems, rather than a wholesale replacement. ALMIS offered the modular fit, speed to implement and cost-effectiveness the bank needed. By year end 2025, the bank had its interest rate swap programme in place, hedging its structural interest rate exposure.

Aasif Sarigat, Banking Director, Crown Agents Bank:

"Introducing interest rate swaps for the first time was an important step in strengthening how we manage risk across our balance sheet. We needed a solution that would let us hedge our interest rate exposure more effectively, work alongside our existing systems rather than replace them, and give our teams the capability to run a more sophisticated hedging programme. ALMIS did all of that, and we were live within four months."

Jake Rose, Chief Commercial Officer at ALMIS International:

"Introducing interest rate swaps for the first time is a significant step for any bank. Crown Agents Bank set a clear objective and a tight timetable: get the hedging right without disrupting its core banking platform. Their team's focus made that pace possible. It shows how banks can add specialist treasury capability as they grow, one piece at a time, rather than replacing everything at once."

The partnership demonstrates how banks can strengthen risk management and introduce new treasury capabilities through modular technology, without the cost and disruption of replacing existing core banking systems.

ALMIS International helps banks and building societies manage their balance sheets with confidence. Its cloud-based platform brings Treasury Management, Asset and Liability Management, Regulatory Reporting and Hedge Accounting together, running them all from a single source of data and giving finance, treasury and risk teams one consistent view of the balance sheet.

With more than 30 years' experience, ALMIS is a specialist in balance sheet management, supporting more than 60 banks, building societies and financial institutions globally.

For more information about ALMIS visit https://www.almisinternational.com/, and follow it on LinkedIn

Crown Agents Bank is a UK regulated bank providing cross-border payment solutions that power opportunities across the Global South. Driven by its purpose to deliver prosperity in the markets it serves, the bank combines nearly 200 years of heritage with proven expertise in complex cross-border payments and specialist market knowledge, helping organisations and institutions move money with confidence across complex and underserved markets.

Its network connects clients across the public, private and development sectors to more than 120 countries and currencies and over 800 currency pairs. Built through long-standing relationships with central banks and other local partners, the network opens access to harder to reach markets and helps ensure the flow of funds to and within the Global South.

The St. Mary Catholic Central student grew up as a volleyball player, but started playing golf with her grandfather, Jerry Marcero, the summer before her freshman year.

"I learned from my Papa," Tolliver said. "We would go out every Sunday (to Sandy Creek Golf Course). He was a really big into it. He used to be the manager out there for a long time."

On those lazy Sunday afternoons, Tolliver got to spend quality time with her grandpa and learn about golf.

"He taught me lot about the mental part of it – how to be able to stay positive," she said. "It was really uplifting."

Tolliver put those lessons to good work during her senior season at SMCC.

She showed amazing consistency with every 9-hole round between 42 and 46 and all but two 18-hole rounds between 85 and 88.

Thanks to Papa, her greatest improvement came on the mental side.

Tolliver showed her ability to do that during a key dual meet against Grosse Ile.

"I just try to stay calm," she said. "I don't even check scores throughout the match. I just make sure to focus on one shot at time. Yeah, I like to celebrate good shots, but don't get too down about bad ones."

Tolliver also is the starting shortstop for SMCC's softball team.

"I got cut from my travel (softball) team," she said. "That was a good thing. It gave me a lot more room for golf. I still play (softball) in the spring, but not the summer."

Her proudest moments on the golf course this fall were team achievements.

"I am really excited about this year," she said. "Last year the team did so well. We had high hopes this year. We (the four SMCC seniors) wanted to finish it out strong. We all love each other and were so close.

"I am really happy to win the league and the county. It was awesome. We fell short at regionals (tied for first, but lost tiebreaker), but I am glad we did well at state."

All five SMCC players improved from the first day to the second at state. Tolliver shaved three strokes off her score.

"It's kind of intimidating at state," she said. "I have never played with those girls before. But all of them were great. It was nice to get to know them and make friends from other schools."

Perhaps the biggest individual thrill for Tolliver was shooting a season-best, 18-hole round of 85 at the Huron League meet to nip Flat Rock's Elyse Rogier by a stroke for medalist honors.

Fittingly, that event was held at Sandy Creek – the place where she learned to love golf.

"He came at the end," she said. "I was really glad to see him. That is a special place for us."

Tolliver doesn't plan to play golf in college. The daughter of Leah and Nicholas Tolliver of Newport will concentrate on studying nursing.

But she will find time for some rounds with Papa.

Cortaca Jug rivalry returns to Yankee Stadium. What to know

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

For the second time in four years, one of college football's biggest rivalries will be played at one of pro sports' most recognizable venues.

Unbeaten SUNY Cortland will face Route 13 rival Ithaca College in the annual Cortaca Jug game at 1 p.m. Saturday, Nov. 14 at Yankee Stadium in the Bronx.

Cortland, three years removed from a national championship, brings a 9-0 record into the contest and is ranked ninth in Division III by D3football.com and 10th by the American Football Coaches Association. The Red Dragons finished with a 7-0 record in Empire 8 play with last week's 42-24 victory over SUNY Brockport, securing an automatic berth into the NCAA Division III Tournament while extending their conference winning streak to 40 games.

Ithaca is 7-2 and comes off a 14-10 loss to Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute in a game that decided the Liberty League title. The Bombers' seven-game winning streak came to a close and they ended up 6-1 in league play.

This regular-season finale will be the third meeting between the teams in the New York City area. Ithaca beat Cortland, 32-20, in front of an announced Division III record crowd of 45,161 fans at MetLife Stadium in East Rutherford, New Jersey in 2019. In 2022, Ithaca beat Cortland, 34-17, in front of 40,232 fans at Yankee Stadium.

"Cortland and Ithaca both help set the standard in Division III athletics, and our Cortaca Jug rivalry demonstrates that shared excellence," Mike Urtz, Cortland's athletic director, said in a press release when it was announced the rivalry would return to Yankee Stadium.

"It's been called the 'biggest little game in the nation' for a reason, and it's fitting to be played in one of the most iconic sports venues in the entire world."

More: This Elmira native 'enjoyed every minute' of 50-year coaching career

The Ithaca-Cortland rivalry dates back to 1930, when Cortland topped Ithaca by a 12-0 score. Ithaca won last year's meeting, 26-21, at home.

This will be the 67th Cortaca Jug game and the 84th all-time meeting between the schools. Ithaca leads the series 44-36-3.

The Cortaca Jug made its debut in 1959, the first Cortaca Jug game. There are now three Cortaca Jugs after the first two ran out of space to chronicle scores.

Nicholas Lang, an all-state pick in football and basketball at Union-Endicott, is the top receiving weapon for the Bombers with 45 catches for 712 yards and seven touchdowns this season. Lang, a senior, caught touchdown passes of 44 and 7 yards in last year's triumph over Cortland. He has 108 yards and a touchdown on the ground this season.

IC quarterback Michael Reed has completed 134 of 222 passes for 15 touchdowns, with four interceptions. He is second on the team with 516 rushing yards on 102 carries, including seven touchdowns.

Chris Scully is the Bombers' top rusher with 516 yards and 12 touchdowns on 102 rushes.

Red Dragons quarterback Mike Rescigno, who starred at Arlington High in New York's Section 1, has rushed 120 times for 902 yards and 10 touchdowns. The junior's passing numbers include 115 completions in 182 attempts, with 16 touchdowns and four interceptions.

Ethan Gallo is second on the team with 587 rushing yards on 118 attempts, including 14 touchdowns.

Four players have at least 300 receiving yards, led by Jacque LaPrarie with 19 catches for 415 yards and six touchdowns.

Aidan Lochner leads the defense with 62 tackles, and Joe Galea has a team-high 5 1/2 sacks.

Along with Lang, other Section 4 high school alumni on Ithaca's roster are defensive back Cooper Hughes from U-E, receiver Jack Gannon from Vestal, and defensive lineman Lucas Boccasini from Owego Free Academy.

Section 4 players on Cortland's roster include receiver Will Brainard from Trumansburg, offensive lineman Talan O'Connor from Groton, tight end Evan English from Owego, and running back Jay Miles from Dryden.
