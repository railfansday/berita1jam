Beto O’Rourke Says He’d “Do Anything” for James Talarico and Gina Hinojosa—Including Work for Them

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

After the shocking Democratic victories in Texas last week, it was hard not to look over the shoulders of James Talarico and Gina Hinojosa and see the shadow of Beto O’Rourke, whose losses for Senate in 2018 and governor in 2022 had become cautionary tales of Democratic hopelessness in Texas. And perhaps proof that nice guys couldn’t win. But in the years after those failed runs, O’Rourke never really went away. He launched a voter-registration group called Powered by People, punched out missives on Substack, livestreamed his thoughts, and quietly advised his party from the background.

“Should I have talked to Joe Hagan when he was on my front porch in 2019?” O’Rourke says now, looking back at the cover story I wrote, in which he proclaimed he was “just born to be in it,” a premature declaration that almost immediately sank his prospects. “I’m glad I did. I’m grateful for everything that’s happened, because look where we are. It’s a beautiful new day in America.”

O'Rourke taks the stage during the Fight the Trump Takeover rally at the Texas Capitol in August 2025.

Like Stacey Abrams in Georgia, who lost her own race for governor but used her visibility to build a stronger coalition, O’Rourke can claim some credit for setting the table for Democratic success in Texas. Talarico won his first race, for a Republican-held state House seat, on O’Rourke’s 2018 wave. And as it turns out, O’Rourke himself weighed another Senate run this year. When Talarico asked whether he’d run, O’Rourke said only if Talarico ran for governor instead, eventually stepping aside after concluding that the moment called for “something and someone new.” When he launched his Senate campaign, Talarico said he wanted to replicate O’Rourke’s “energy.”

O’Rourke, meanwhile, became a behind-the-scenes player: Powered by People was one of four partners in the Texas Democrats’ $30 million coordinated campaign, and it says it reached more than 110,000 new and young voters in Texas this cycle. Ken Paxton noticed and tried to put O’Rourke “behind bars” for funding the Democrats who fled the state to block its Donald Trump–backed redistricting.

O’Rourke says the wave cascaded so far down the ballot in Texas that a candidate who tried backing out of her campaign before Election Day ended up winning. “At that point it was too late to take her name off the ballot,” he says. “She didn’t campaign, just won her election. That speaks to the magnitude of the wave, but also to the value of having a fighter on every front. Somebody said to me, ‘A wave’s great, even better if you have a surfboard.’”

Over an hour-long conversation, O’Rourke parses the political revolution in his home state, suggests how Democrats can hold on to their land grab, and, perhaps unsurprisingly, looks ahead to another run for office.

It’s insane having Texas Republicans say, if you’re Muslim, regardless of whether you’re an immigrant, you should be deported from this country. That’s just not who we are.

O'Rourke speaks during the Fight the Trump Takeover rally, where 5,000 people attended. Attendees protested proposed redistricting, immigration enforcement, and other Trump-backed legislation and proposed changes.

When I ran for governor, 9 million registered voters didn’t cast a ballot. It’s not because they don’t care; it’s not that they’re lazy. This state makes it harder than any other to participate, so we’ve got to try that much harder. I knew it was a matter of time and effort. The greater the effort, the shorter the time. But did I know it would be 2026? Did I know it would be every statewide office and the state House? Fuck no, man. Nobody knew that.

Ken Paxton called you a “repeat loser” and tried to prosecute your PAC. He lost by 12 points.

We’ve spent more than $600,000; we have Powered by People defending our organization, defending me. He accused me of bribery. He tried to remove the license for Powered by People to do voter-registration work in the state of Texas. He promised to lock me up, to put me behind bars. The 15th Court of Appeals [judges], all of whom were appointed by Greg Abbott, so none of them friends of mine, have ruled against Paxton for attacking somebody for exercising their First Amendment rights. Do I think that I’m going to get that money back? I don’t know.

Couldn’t have happened to a better person.

Did Talarico and Hinojosa acknowledge your role, or did they keep their distance?

You’d have to ask them. I got a really nice message from James on election night, and I had a chance to campaign with Gina in the closing days of the election. That’s not an easy choice for Gina, right? Both of them did an exceptional job of being themselves and not seeming to be beholden to anybody. They invited nobody in from outside of Texas, and we all understood: This is their show. So getting to campaign with Gina here at the University of Texas at El Paso, at the University of Houston, it was really nice to hear her acknowledge the work that so many of us have been doing, especially all these volunteers who’ve been out there, whose names most people will never know.

I’ve known them both for a decade. James and I were on the campaign trail together in 2017 and 2018, when he was running his first race for state House in Williamson County. Somebody just sent me a picture of the two of us at an event in Williamson in 2017. These are really fucking good human beings, and they’re also really good at understanding power and how to win it. It is so rare that those two things come together.

O'Rourke speaks during a "Fighting Oligarchy" event in Houston, Texas on Oct. 20, 2026.

Are Chuck Schumer and Hakeem Jeffries the right leaders for this moment?

Chuck Schumer absolutely is not. Early 2025, [Elon] Musk is running wild with DOGE, Trump is steamrolling every center of power. They’re all bending the knee—the Ivy Leagues, the big law firms, the media companies. Hakeem marshaled every Democratic vote in the House but one to stop Trump, DOGE, and Musk, and the deal with Schumer was going to do the same. Then, inexplicably, Schumer bent the knee as well. In that moment, it was absolutely over for me.

Schumer deserves so much credit and praise for his work over the decades. But he doesn’t deserve to be majority leader at this major turning point for the country, not when you have James Talarico, Abdul El-Sayed, or Jon Ossoff. We have an embarrassment of talent in the Senate right now, and we should use it. The age when any American cares what the majority leader reads on the Senate floor is over. I don’t even know what his message is, and that says something.

Hakeem Jeffries hasn’t had the chance to lead as Speaker. We were elected in the same class in 2012, and I’m grateful that he stood strong early in the Trump administration. I wish he could communicate more effectively than he has so far. Maybe there’s a scenario where he’s Speaker, but he taps the far better communicators in his caucus, like Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez, to do more of that work, and he’s focused on getting the legislative results.

Eleven House Democrats missed the vote to impeach [Pete] Hegseth, enough to have changed the outcome. What happened there?

Eleven is a big number. That certainly invites some conspiracy thinking, or, maybe just as bad, the idea that these folks aren’t up to the job. It’s really important that the Democratic leadership in the House address this directly, explain what happened, and take ownership and responsibility for it.

You’ve said Democrats wasted their power in 2009 and 2021, and that next time they should “drive that car like we stole it.” What happens in the first 100 days?

Right off the bat, you have to make our lives better in a way that we can feel. In Texas, that’s not hard, because we are in such a fucking hole. Raise the minimum wage from $7.25. Expand Medicaid when the federal government’s paying 90 cents on the dollar. Stop this voucher thing that is draining tax dollars from local schools, and pay teachers so they don’t have to work a second or third job.

Nationally, you have to do the same. The response to this from many is that Trump has the power of the pen and will veto that. Make him veto that. I don’t like anything about him, but I don’t know that there’s a more successful political operator than Trump. I don’t think there’s someone who reads power better than he does. Maybe there are deals to be had. Maybe you force the veto, and you set up a clear contrast going into 2028. At a minimum, you have to get caught trying.

The huge issues that Democrats have absolutely failed to lead on, we have to lead on now, so that would include immigration and that would include democracy. Immigration was arguably the issue that voters made their decisions on in 2016 and 2024, and if we continue to hope that it’s just going to solve itself, instead of making tough choices and using political capital on this, then it’s going to be our demise in future elections as well. [On democracy], there’s no democracy to restore or save. There is a democracy you can build anew. Getting corporations out of politics; what just passed in Montana is potentially a wonderful precedent from which we could build. Getting a voting rights act passed again. [Pushing] the popular-vote compact; I think we have enough trifectas now. I’m probably in the minority in the Democratic Party, but I’m a big believer in term limits. People give a speech and are like, “We’ve got to overturn Citizens United,” or, “We’ve got to do away with the Electoral College.” That’s a great applause line, but when is that ever going to happen?

O'Rourke spoke in October 2026 as the fight for the Senate came down to the wire.

The big dig on Democrats is: You talked a great game on immigration, then you won the White House; you had the House and the Senate. You didn’t do anything. You talked a great game about voting rights. You had the trifecta, and you didn’t do anything. Now we have the power. We’d better deliver, or watch out.

I’m surprised I’m not hearing more from you about prosecutions and subpoenas. You spent the last two years warning about fascism and calling for accountability.

That’s got to be part of it too. The law is king in this country; no one’s above it, no one’s below it. We’ve allowed the precedent, through the Epstein crimes, that you can be rich enough and well-connected enough to rape and traffic girls and women with complete impunity. I spent a lot of time with young people doing this voter-registration work, and that is probably the number one thing I hear from them for why they don’t vote: This shit doesn’t work. Ghislaine Maxwell [went to jail], but no man who actually did this has gone to prison or even faced trial.

Then there’s the corruption that flows from the president through his family, through his Cabinet officials, through the oligarchs [with whom] he surrounds himself. If we let this stand, much the way that we let stand his crimes against this country on January 6, 2021, that sets the most dangerous precedent. Look at the Roman Republic. It wasn’t Caesar crossing the Rubicon. It was Sulla turning his armies against Rome, and there was never a consequence or accountability for that, so the precedent was set and Caesar picked that up. [Editor’s note: O’Rourke is an avid fan of the podcast The Rest Is History.] There will be a more capable, powerful demagogue than Trump—he or she may be a Democrat; he or she may be a Republican—who will use that precedent unless that door is closed. I know this is an unpopular thing to talk about within our party, because folks want to turn the page. But that keeps biting us in the ass. Everyone who went to prison for [January 6] was pardoned by this president.

But if you spend the next two years investigating him and prices stay high, Democrats are cooked.

That’s exactly right. That’s why I said you’ve got to deliver for people, and they have to feel it immediately. Democrats are responsible for so many good things that are both slow-working and expressed through the tax code, like the child tax credit, and for which they don’t claim credit. I was in the Rio Grande Valley after 2020, doing voter registration, knocking on doors, and talking to this guy who voted for Trump. They really reflect my values and my interests. No. Totally understandable, and it’s a recognition of how politics and power typically work. When you allow these things to stand, they become the precedent. But if there’s accountability, then that becomes the precedent upon which future leaders will be able to act. Talarico is masterful at saying that: Their corruption is costing you and your family. So many examples in Texas. I am the last guy to give Talarico advice, because he’s just been so freaking smart and amazing. Euclid's First Data Release: A Stepping Stone to its 3D Map of the Sky

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

The ESA's Euclid has already teased us with some early results. In the past it's given us a small, 60 square degree of the sky created with a few days of observations. Those images showed individual targets from the Horsehead Nebula to the Perseus cluster of galaxies.

But now we have data from more than one year of the telescope's observations, named DR1-Foundation. DR-1 Foundation covers a sky area as large as about 9,500 full moons, and captures light from more than one billion objects. It took about 13 months to gather all this data.

Understanding dark energy and dark matter means studying as much of the Universe as possible. The effects of dark energy and dark matter are felt at large scales, and by constructing an image of galaxy superclusters and the filaments they exist on, as well as the voids between those filaments, Euclid will lead to new understandings of both dark energy and dark matter.

There are other efforts to map this structure, some dating back decades. DESI, the Dark Energy Survey Instrument, has created the largest 3D map so far, but Euclid's will be larger. Euclid's will have more depth and volume, and since it's space-based while DESI is ground-based, it won't have to deal with atmospheric interference and will have a higher resolution.

This data release is from the Euclid's Wide and Deep Surveys, two surveys that will form the backbone of its 3D map. Even though the data covers 1900 square degrees of the sky, they only represent about 14% of the sky the space telescope will cover in its six-year mission.

This image shows Euclid's Wide Survey in light yellow and its Deep Survey in darker yellow. The Deep Survey are targeted regions within the Wide Survey. They're overlain on a star map from Gaia and a dust map from Planck. Image Credit: ESA/Euclid/Euclid Consortium/NASA; ESA/Gaia/DPAC; ESA/Planck Collaboration. Licence: CC BY-SA 3.0 IGO or ESA Standard Licence

The Wide Survey is Euclid's primary campaign and will cover almost half of the galactic sky. It's an imaging and spectrographic survey in visible and near-infrared. It's the telescope's main cosmology survey and will cover a large volume to constrain dark energy and dark matter.

The Deep Survey serves a different purpose. It'll find fainter objects like dwarf galaxies, low-mass galaxies, and tidal tails, as well as high redshift galaxies and quasars. It will serve to calibrate the Wide Survey, and will provide its own, separate science based on its higher density of sources.

Basically, the Wide Durvey provides the greater volume, and the Deep Survey looks more closely and gives more depth. The two combined are needed for a cosmological survey.

Part of the release includes Euclid's image of the core of the Messier 81 galaxy group. This image shows part of that, centered on the Messier 81 galaxy, the group's largest member. The smaller NGC 3077 is on the left, which is also a member, as are many other small members spread across the image. Wispy blue clouds, sometimes called 'galactic cirrus clouds' are also visible. An irregular dwarf galaxy is also visible in diffuse blue to the left. Image Credit: ESA/Euclid/Euclid Consortium/NASA, image processing by J.-C. Cuillandre and E. Bertin. Licence: CC BY-SA 3.0 IGO or ESA Standard Licence

Euclid also delivered an image of NGC 1291, a stunning ring galaxy about 33 million light years away. It's also called the Golden Eye galaxy, and it has an inner ring and an outer ring, as well as an inner bar. It's inner and outer rings may be because of a merger with a gas-rich galaxy in the past.

This is Euclid's image of NGC 1291, a ring galaxy that faces us head-on. It's about 33 million light years away, and highlights Euclid's power. Image Credit: ESA/Euclid/Euclid Consortium/NASA, image processing by J.-C. Cuillandre and E. Bertin. Licence: CC BY-SA 3.0 IGO or ESA Standard Licence

Euclid also captured images of Abell 3128. It's 800 million light years away and contains hundreds of individual galaxies. Massive clusters like this one are important probes for dark energy and dark matter, but especially for dark matter. They serve as important gravitational lenses and reveal a lot about where dark matter is concentrated. They can help cosmologists test Lambda-Cold Dark Matter, which is the standard Big Bang theory of the Universe. They also help Euclid calibrate itself.

This is the Abell 3128 cluster, part of the Horologium supercluster. Horologium is home to more than 30 smaller clusters, and Abell 3128 is one of its richest members. Abell holds spirals and ellipticals, and a gravitational lens is in the middle, warping and magnifying the light from a distant background galaxy. The image shows only a fraction of Abell 3128, and also part of another galaxy cluster named ACT-CL J0330-5227; while in reality the two clusters are separated by a huge distance of around 5 billion light-years, they overlap here in our line of sight. Image Credit: ESA/Euclid/Euclid Consortium/NASA, image processing by J.-C. Cuillandre and E. Bertin. Licence: CC BY-SA 3.0 IGO or ESA Standard Licence

We're only at the appetizer of the visual and scientific feast that Euclid will deliver. By the time it's completed, its 3D map of the sky will be visually entrancing and full of science. It's already discovered a new comet, the most ancient quasar in the Universe, and new images of the Milky Way's over-populated center.

It's off to a strong start and will only get better.

Zidane: ‘Italy played better in France, but they won tonight’

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Zinedine Zidane feels Italy ‘played better in Paris, but they won this evening’ when inflicting his first defeat as France coach, and defends Chelsea’s Malo Gusto from criticism.

Les Bleus had got off to the ideal start at San Siro when Michael Olise pounced on a Gianluigi Donnarumma howler playing out from the back.

Francesco Pio Esposito equalised, but they felt that when Sebastiano Esposito blazed a penalty wide, it would be a turning point in the match.

Instead, the Azzurri got back on their feet quickly, winning 3-1 with Davide Frattesi and another penalty, this time converted by Pio Esposito.

“We had a good initial approach and held the ball well, but after the break we lost our way, especially defensively,” confessed Zidane in his press conference.

This was an unusual situation, as before tonight France had not scored in the first half in any of their last nine matches.

“We stayed in the game, did good things in possession, but we fell away in the second half, especially after going 2-1 down. It is part of the process.”

France were decimated by injuries, missing Inter’s Marcus Thuram, Juventus defender Pierre Kalulu, Roma midfielder Manu Koné, Kylian Mbappé, Ousmane Dembélé, Aurelien Tchouameni, William Saliba, Jules Koundé, Maxence Lacroix, Maghnes Akliouche and Esteban Lepaul.

Malo Gusto had terrible problems at right-back dealing with Nicolò Cambiaghi first, then Federico Dimarco.

“Your job is to analyse what you need to analyse, but my focus is on recharging the batteries for the next game,” replied Zidane to questions about the Chelsea defender.

“We are all united, we didn’t defend well in general as a team and need to improve. I won’t change players over one game. Malo Gusto gave away a penalty, but it can happen.”

Olise scored the opener, but then made little impact as the match wore on at San Siro.

“You cannot always play at the top level for 90 minutes. Olise had some good performances, he wasn’t the only one who fell away in the second half, the whole team did.

“We wanted to win the game tonight to ensure we had first place on Sunday, now we must win the final match against Turkiye, but that was the objective anyway regardless of this evening’s result.”

Italy are the only team who Zidane has been unable to defeat as France coach, earning a 1-1 draw in Paris and this 3-1 victory.

“The most important thing is to create chances, and we did hit the woodwork twice. I am not disappointed, I would’ve been if we had not had such good possession play. What we need to analyse is that we weren’t at the right level off the ball.

“I think Italy had a similar performance to in Paris defensively, which is what they do best. I must admit, I thought they played better with the ball at their feet in the first game, but they won tonight and that’s all.”

Mbappé was not even on the bench this evening, but could he recover in time for the final group game with Turkiye on Sunday?

“We hope so, that is the plan and why he has not gone back home. We just decided not to risk it tonight over a slight problem.”
