Winter Weather Hits EV Range Hard. One Automaker Handles It Better Than Tesla

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Browse this and many other websites without banner ads, personalized tracking and video ads.

With the first signs of winter making their presence felt in some regions, it’s handy to remember that electric cars lose range when temperatures drop considerably. It happens across the board, but some automakers and models handle the cold better than others, and a new study of over 59,000 EVs from Recurrent shows which battery-powered cars retain the most range when the thermometer reaches 32°F (0°C).

This year, Tesla has been dethroned in the automaker rankings by an unexpected competitor, though the American EV manufacturer still holds the first two spots when it comes to individual models.

Polestar, the Swedish-headquartered, Chinese-owned company that’s no longer allowed to sell cars in the United States after its inventory dries up, surpassed Tesla in the EV winter range rankings. The company’s models retain, on average, 88% of their range at 32°F (0°C) compared to ideal conditions, and 83% when the thermometer reads 20°F (-7°C).

Meanwhile, Tesla, which has historically been the top performer in Recurrent’s rankings because of its early investments in heat pump systems and other winter optimizations, is second on the list this year, with an 85% average range retention at 32°F (0°C) and 77% at 20°F (-7°C).

Audi completes the automaker podium, with the German marque’s EVs retaining 79% of their range at 32°F (0°C) and 71% at 20°F (-7°C), according to Recurrent.

At this point, it’s worth noting that the comparison point for these figures is not the official EPA range figure, but the longest range achieved by a particular EV in ideal conditions, as recorded by Recurrent’s system. As a reminder, the company gathers real-world data from EVs through owners who are willing to connect their cars to Recurrent’s servers.

This year, Toyota is at the bottom of the list, with its EVs retaining just 70% of their summer range at freezing temperatures, with Mercedes-Benz not far ahead. Rivian ranked sixth, with an average range retention of 78% at 32°F (0°C), ahead of Hyundai and Porsche, but below Jaguar and Nissan.

When looking at individual electric models, Tesla still has the lead, with the heat pump-equipped Model S ranking first. The discontinued electric sedan retains 90% of its summer range at 32°F (0°C), followed by the soon-to-be-discontinued Cybertruck, with an average retention rate of 89%.

Despite Tesla losing to Polestar in the overall rankings, the Model S still has the lead in the models list.

Next up is the Polestar 2, the entry-level sedan that put Polestar on the map. The four-door EV can still deliver 88% of its summer range at 32°F (0°C), followed by the Tesla Model X with 87% and the Tesla Model 3 with 85%.

The popular Hyundai Ioniq 5 is 10th on the list, with an average range retention rate of 81%, below the Ioniq 6, Audi e-tron GT, Tesla Model Y, and Chevrolet Silverado EV, but above the Audi e-tron, Rivian R1S, and Nissan Ariya.

The pre-facelift Toyota bZ4X is at the bottom of the list with 70% of range available when the temperature is 32°F (0°C), ahead of the Mercedes-Benz EQS, Chevrolet Blazer EV, Mercedes-Benz EQS SUV, and Cadillac Lyriq. Interestingly, the Volkswagen e-Golf, which is over two decades old and doesn’t have a battery thermal management system, ranked above Cadillac’s Lyriq.

On average, the 43 electric models included in Recurrent’s dataset retain 78% of their range in freezing temperatures compared to ideal conditions. That said, the best EVs out there top out at 90% range retention, while the bottom of the pack gets only 70%.

A heat pump makes a world of difference when it comes to boosting winter range. Compared to traditional resistive heating systems, an EV equipped with a heat pump will have 8% more range in the winter, which can make the difference between getting home and calling a tow truck.

Speaking of home, home charging and preconditioning the car’s interior before setting off will also preserve precious miles when it’s time to go somewhere with a thick coat on. By using electricity from the grid to heat up the cabin, the battery will last longer before needing a recharge.

Flipkart CEO Kalyan Krishnamurthy to step down next week amid IPO uncertainty

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Kalyan Krishnamurthy (pictured above), who steered Flipkart through Walmart’s acquisition in 2018 and helped build it into one of India’s biggest e-commerce companies, is stepping down as chief executive next week after more than 11 years with the company.

On Wednesday, Flipkart said it would announce Krishnamurthy’s successor in the coming weeks, and chief product and technology officer, Balaji Thiagarajan, will lead the company until a replacement is found.

A Walmart spokesperson told TechCrunch that Krishnamurthy’s last day at Flipkart will be December 4.

Krishnamurthy joined Flipkart in 2015 from Tiger Global, one of the company’s early investors, and became its CEO in 2017. He remained at the helm after Walmart acquired a controlling stake in the company for $16 billion in 2018.

Under Krishnamurthy, Flipkart said its business grew more than five-fold. The company has more than 500 million registered users and over 1.4 million sellers, including those on Shopsy, its low-cost shopping platform. The company’s marketplace offers over 150 million products across more than 80 categories.

Flipkart has of late moved into newer segments, including quick-commerce through Flipkart Minutes and, more recently, food delivery through a venture called Eat-in by Minutes. The expansion is targeted at Indian consumers who are increasingly turning to quick-delivery services for everything from groceries to electronics.

Thiagarajan joined Flipkart in September last year to lead its product and technology teams. Before that, he had founded Kasu.ai, a Bay Area startup working on AI assistants for corporate finance. He also previously worked at Google, Microsoft, and Uber in senior engineering positions, per his LinkedIn profile.

Krishnamurthy’s exit comes amid uncertainty around Flipkart’s IPO. In March, the company moved its headquarters from Singapore to India as part of its preparations to go public, but in May, Walmart reportedly told Flipkart to delay its IPO and focus on becoming profitable first.

Walmart declined to comment beyond its announcement when asked what prompted Krishnamurthy’s departure, and whether the leadership change would affect Flipkart’s IPO plans. Flipkart did not respond to a request for comment.

Founded in 2007 by Sachin and Binny Bansal as an online bookstore, much like its bigger rival Amazon, Flipkart eventually expanded into other categories. Under Krishnamurthy, it has grown into India’s largest online retailer by gross merchandise value, ahead of Amazon.

“Both Flipkart and Walmart are better because of Kalyan. We are grateful for his leadership, the governance he instilled and his many contributions to the company,” Chris Nicholas, president and CEO, Walmart International, said in a statement.

The Lord Of The Rings: The Rings Of Power Season 3's Surprising Finale Cameo Explained

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Spoilers are never late, nor are they early, but arrive precisely when they mean to. This article discusses major plot details from the Season 3 finale of "The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power."

Sometimes, in the darkest moments life has to offer us, all we need is a friend. Throughout the most recent episodes of "The Rings of Power" Season 3, the gentle wizard Gandalf (Daniel Weyman) has struggled to temper his hasty inclinations and listen to what fate has in store for him in combatting the rise of Sauron's (Charlie Vickers) evil in Middle-earth. The solution? Repeatedly, he's told that his purpose isn't to fight and defeat evil, whether it be the Dark Lord himself or simply the Dark Wizard known as Pallando (Ciarán Hinds). Instead, he's to save those seduced by the darkness — or, as he eloquently puts it in the finale, "to inspire good" in others.

He learned that lesson in the first two seasons of "The Rings of Power," in fact, thanks to one diminutive friend and ally: the Harfoot Elanor "Nori" Brandyfoot (Markella Kavenagh). Unfortunately, these proto-Hobbits have been missing in action throughout Season 3, but that finally changes once Gandalf finds himself imprisoned in Mordor. After rebuffing Sauron's offer of one of his remaining rings of power, the wizard suddenly wakes up inside a cell with Nori at his side, claiming to have been there for weeks. Obviously, this is yet another of Sauron's deceptions, and Gandalf quickly sees through the façade.

Still, it's undeniably nostalgic to see Nori back again and playing a significant role in a story that seemed to have moved beyond her. Thankfully, this cameo isn't the last we see of her, either. Her next appearance is the episode's best, most poignant moment.

From horror to heartbreak to happiness, viewers were taken for quite an emotional ride in the Season 3 finale of "The Rings of Power." Nori's first appearance in that cell with Gandalf likely caught many by surprise, even if it seemed to be too good (or, in these circumstances, too bad) to be true. Muholi was born in 1972 in Umlazi, Durban, South Africa and lives today in Cape Town. They are a self-described visual activist, who, since the mid-2000s, have used photography to document the lives of LGBTQ+ people, initially in South Africa, where they were born.

Zanele Muholi, Diana Cristina Nascimento Ramos, Salvador, 2025, from Faces and Phases, ed. by Renée Mussai (MACK, 2026)

They have since expanded their project around the world and work in discrete, often ongoing series that have a common aim: to respond to violence and discrimination against queer and trans communities, to reflect their defiance and resistance, and to expand their visibility in visual culture in South Africa in particular but also far beyond.

The people they depict have, since the start, been far more than sitters or subjects; they are collaborators, participants who help define the way they are represented. Germany Agrees to Hand Over Photo Archive Chronicling a Family’s Persecution by Nazis

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Germany Agrees to Hand Over Photo Archive Chronicling a Family’s Persecution by Nazis

The German government has agreed to hand over an extensive photo archive to the Jehovah’s Witnesses that documents the persecution of a pacifist Christian family under the Nazi regime.

Jehovah’s Witnesses were persecuted by the Nazis for their religious beliefs, particularly their refusal to serve in the military, pledge allegiance to Adolf Hitler, or participate in Nazi organizations. An estimated 4,500 Jehovah’s Witnesses were sent to concentration camps.

According to a report by The New York Times, among those persecuted were Franz and Hilda Kusserow and their 11 children, who lived in Bad Lippspringe, a town in the German state of North Rhine-Westphalia. Two of the couple’s sons were executed after refusing military service because of their religious beliefs. The three youngest children were taken from their family and placed in a Nazi training school, where they were prohibited from contacting their relatives. Both parents and their remaining children were sentenced to prison terms.

One of their daughters Annemarie Kusserow was arrested by the Gestapo in October 1944 and remained a prisoner until May 25, 1945, after the war in Europe had ended. Over the following decades, Annemarie gathered and preserved a collection of more than 1,000 photographs, documents, drawings, letters, and reports documenting her family’s experiences.

In her 2005 will, Annemarie bequeathed the entire photo archive to the German headquarters of the Jehovah’s Witnesses. However, her brother Hans Werner Kusserow, to whom she had loaned the collection for research purposes, sold it to the Bundeswehr Military History Museum in Dresden, Germany, in 2009. The archive has remained in the German museum’s possession ever since.

According to the Jehovah’s Witnesses’ official website, the sale of the photo collection to a military museum was “especially troubling” given that two of Annemarie’s brothers had lost their lives because of their pacifist beliefs. The organization says the sale of the archive “resulted in years of legal battles.”

The Jehovah’s Witnesses filed a lawsuit to recover the archive from the Bundeswehr Military History Museum, which is operated by the German armed forces. On November 19, the museum and the religious organization reached an “amicable agreement” under which the collection will be handed over to the Jehovah’s Witnesses. Both parties have agreed to keep the terms of the settlement confidential.

According to The New York Times, Sebastian Stock, a spokesman for the religious organization in Germany, says that once the transfer is complete, the “Jehovah’s Witnesses will develop and present a concept for making the archive accessible for further academic research.”

In addition to digitizing the archive and making it available to scholars, the Jehovah’s Witnesses plan to display some of the documents in a new exhibition examining the history of the religious group in Central Europe.
