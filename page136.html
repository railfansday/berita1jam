Man United's reluctance to make big decisions has ultimiately cost them hugely financially, but despite having been burnt in the past, they have not learned from their mistakes.

Chelsea owner Behdad Eghbali is likely to escape punishment despite the discovery that he is behind a website selling Premier League tickets at hugely inflated prices.

That is according to former Everton chief executive and ex-Premier League board member Keith Wyness, speaking exclusively to Football Insider, who believes that the Clearlake tycoon has exploited a loophole.

Eghbali is the director of US-based company Victory Live, which owns the website events365.com, which has been found to be selling Premier League tickets at significantly inflated prices.

Wyness does not believe that the scheme will make substantial profit for Eghbali, and has pointed out that it is not worth the negative PR that comes with it.

Crucially, the tickets available on events365.com were for all Premier League clubs with the exception of games involving Chelsea.

The website have reportedly now removed all Premier League offerings, with Eghbali's scheme seemingly set to hit the buffers.

Everton’s former chief Wyness – who served as CEO at Goodison Park between 2004 and 2009 and now runs a football consultancy advising elite clubs – believes that Eghbali has likely dodged a breach of Premier League regulations by not selling inflated tickets for his own club.

He and the club's former chairman Todd Boehly have both been shrouded in controversy for their revenue-generating methods in recent years.

Speaking on the latest edition of Football Insider's Inside Track podcast, Wyness couldn't understand the benefit of this particular venture.

"Well, we've been down this road with Todd Boehly as well, if I remember. There was another ticket resale site," he said.

"There is, as I understand it, a slight distinction here that if you're not selling your own club's tickets, then it's not quite as clear in the regulations as to what happens.

"Look, it's just not a good look for Chelsea politically. I mean, we all know that there are a lot of questions around, you know, their spending patterns, et cetera, and there are UEFA issues.

"Why would you be messing around with something that I don't think is going to be that big a deal in terms of profit when politically and image-wise it's not the best for you?

"I just don't get it. It's something I would have certainly kept at arm's length rather than being that involved. So it's a question mark for me as to really understand the image implications and political implications going forward."

Wyness previously told Football Insider earlier this week that Chelsea's new VVIP dressing room experience is only likely to net them around £1million if they continue it for the remainder of the campaign.

The Blues have launched the scheme for their upcoming home clash with Leeds United, and it allows fans to lay out the kit before games and leave messages for their favourite players.

However, Wyness believes that the Blues' new package is "very close to the line" in terms of whether it could interfere with manager Xabi Alonso's matchday plans.

Chelsea's VVIP experience is available for £750 per person, and places for the Leeds game have already sold out in rapid fashion.

Mancini explains why he left Sebastiano Esposito out of Italy squad to face Belgium

📅 Nov 15, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Roberto Mancini gave a brief interview to Rai ahead of Italy’s Nations League clash against Belgium on Sunday, paying his respects to the victims of the Heysel disaster and explaining why he decided to leave Sebastiano Esposito out of his matchday squad.

Italy are in Brussels for their final fixture of the Nations League group stage on Sunday evening. The Azzurri top the group on 10 points ahead of kick-off, ahead of France on 10, Belgium on nine and Turkey on zero.

Italy require a draw to qualify for the Nations League quarter-finals and in all likelihood a victory to secure top spot. If Italy lose, they will require Turkey to beat Belgium in order to still qualify for the quarter-finals.

Head coach Mancini spoke briefly to Rai a few hours before kick-off. He says that his players ‘deserve’ qualification for the next round of the Nations League given the work they have put in, but does not need reminding that the job is far from over.

“The boys deserve to get through to the last eight of the Nations League because they’re doing an extraordinary job, but there’s still a lot to do,” said Mancini.

“France are among the top three sides in the world and Belgium are not far behind them,” he added.

When asked how to stop Belgium’s creator-in-chief Kevin De Bruyne, Mancini simply replied: “By not allowing him to play.”

As is customary for Nations League group stage fixtures, Mancini has whittled his list of players down to a matchday squad of 23. Davide Frattesi and Nicolo Cambiaghi have not recovered from the France game on Thursday night and have been left out of the squad.

The surprising omission is Sebastiano Esposito, who missed a penalty during Italy’s 3-1 victory over France on Thursday night. Samuele Ricci, Federico Gatti and Matteo Palma have also been left out of the matchday squad to face Belgium.

Asked if Seba Esposito had been left out as a ‘punishment’ for missing the penalty against France, Mancini replied: “Don’t make me laugh. It’s just a technical decision, he’s played in practically every game.

“It’s a shame that we won’t have Frattesi or Cambiaghi, who were decisive in the game against France. Dimarco can also play as an attacking winger.”

Lastly, Mancini was asked about his memories of the Heysel disaster in Brussels back in 1985.

“We will never forget a tragedy as immense as this,” said Mancini. “I remember everything from that day of May 29, 1985. I played for Sampdoria at the time, but I am a Juventus supporter.”

The Nvidia scandal has sent shockwaves through Silicon Valley as well as the political establishment

Alice Huang, a suspected China spy who has been linked to Nvidia's former boss Jensen Huang, left her executive role at Megaspeed in August last year, the company says.

Jensen Huang resigned as chief executive of the AI chip-making giant Nvidia after it emerged he had allegedly had affairs with Alice Huang as well as with one of his employees.

Megaspeed is the biggest buyer of Nvidia chips in south-east Asia and the scandal at Nvidia has raised questions about the possibility that high-end AI chips may have ended up in China despite US export restrictions.

The Singapore-based company said on Sunday it had launched an internal investigation into its business.

It said it was aware of reports "containing assertions about our business, supply chain and global operations that are not supported by verifiable evidence".

It added that it "conducts legitimate and lawful business" and has "retained external legal counsel to conduct a thorough internal investigation".

On Friday, US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent blamed fellow Trump cabinet colleague Howard Lutnick's commerce department for failing to properly oversee the export of high-end chips.

Bessent told CNBC that several agencies "were in favour of putting Megaspeed on the entity list for months... but Commerce has the pen... and you will have to ask them about their internal process".

The so-called entity list restricts US trade with overseas firms and individuals.

Lutnick has been called to testify before the Senate Banking Committee next month over his department's role in allowing Nvidia to export its advanced chips.

Other congressional committees have asked both Lutnick and his department to retain all records relating to Nvidia and Jensen Huang.

Megaspeed is suspected of giving backdoor access to Nvidia chips to Chinese companies via its data centres in Malaysia, Indonesia and Thailand.

Nvidia has been banned from selling its advanced chips to China.

While Singapore does not prohibit the transfer of advanced AI chips to companies abroad, such moves can breach US export controls.

Megaspeed said it "designs and maintains its global operational and supply-chain control frameworks to comply with all applicable laws and regulatory regimes". It said this includes "US export control requirements and local jurisdiction rules in every market where we operate".

China has denied that Alice Huang is a spy and warned the US not to use the scandal as a "pretext for imposing a technological blockade" on AI technology.

Robert Christgau and Carola Dibbell Talk New Documentary ‘The Last Critic’ and Staying Crazy in Love

📅 Nov 15, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The Last Critic is one of the year’s most fascinating rock documentaries — even though it’s not about a musician. It’s a portrait of the legendary writer Robert Christgau, the self-proclaimed “Dean of American Rock Critics.” He was one of the pioneers who invented pop-music criticism in the Sixties — but six decades later, at 83, he’s still at the top of the game. He began his Consumer Guide column in the Village Voice in 1969, and has kept cranking it out ever since, now on Substack. For generations of music fanatics, Christgau’s voice hits like Bob Dylan or Neil Young — two artists his age who are still doing it with the same fiercely independent spirit he brings to his writing.

The debut feature from director Matty Wishnow, The Last Critic won the jury prize at the South by Southwest and Oak Cliff Film festivals, as well as the 2026 Screen Dallas grand prize. It gets its New York premiere tomorrow. The film, which is co-produced by longtime Rolling Stone contributor and editor Joe Levy, goes beyond music, because it’s also the portrait of a marriage. Christgau and his wife, novelist Carola Dibbell, have been together more than 50 years. Over the decades, they’ve shared an intellectual life, raised their daughter, collected way too much vinyl, and — the weirdest part — remained crazy in love.

Christgau has a fearsome rep for never pulling his punches, with his own savage wit. Lou Reed spent a chunk of his live album Take No Prisoners complaining about getting a B+ grade for Street Hassle; when U2 won the Grammy Award for Album of the Year in 1988 for The Joshua Tree, Bono jokily groused in his speech about his band’s reviews in the Village Voice. Christgau’s inspired more songs attacking him than any other journalist, from Sonic Youth (“Kill Yr Idols”) to Public Enemy (“Bring the Noise”).

But there’s something obsessive about his lifelong quest to chronicle the music, still going strong after 60 years — he’s heard more albums than anyone on the planet. In one of the most surprising moments, the legendary songwriter Randy Newman recalls their friendship, and admits he wrote a song about Christgau and Dibbell, “Lost Without You,” inspired by the closeness of their marriage. It’s the kind of movie where you come for the rock, stay for the romance.

Rolling Stone met up with the couple at the same East Village pad they’ve called home for 50 years. The place is packed with albums, books, art — the first thing that greets you at the door is a painting of the Shirelles.
