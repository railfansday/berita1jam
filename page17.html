Over the past year, UnitedHealth Group has conducted a series of independent reviews of its practices as part of a broader turnaround effort at the company.

The healthcare giant released the last of these analyses on Thursday, digging into the processes it uses to establish medical policies. The reviews were conducted by FTI Consulting and Rubrum Advising, a provider-led firm with expertise in medical coverage policies.

While the auditors dove into the processes the insurer uses to develop its medical policies, the report doesn't examine the application of those polices and any impact on coverage denials, an area where UnitedHealthcare has drawn significant public scrutiny.

The report concluded the company's methods "were strong overall and that the medical policy development process reflected numerous best practices."

For instance, UnitedHealthcare's Medical Policy team uses a structured and standardized process to establish medical policies, rooted in evidence-based and stakeholder-informed approaches. Clinical evidence comes from nationally recognized authorities, and epidemiologists play a key role in the review process, per the report.

In addition, the analysts concluded that the team is well-staffed with qualified workers and operates under a strong governance model. Medical policies are built "using systematic evidence review, evidence-based tools and industry best practices," and UnitedHealthcare is transparent about posting these policies and making updates.

All policies are reviewed at least once per year, the report found.

CEO Stephen Hemsley said in a letter to investors that the company will continue to conduct such analyses.

"We will continue to subject key practices across UnitedHealth Group to independent review, share results publicly and make changes where needed," Hemsley wrote. "This work reflects our broader commitment to transparency, accountability and continuous improvement."

While the report concluded that overall processes in place at UnitedHealth were up to snuff, the consultants did offer multiple areas of improvement for the company, too. For instance, the analysts said that the team could improve its documentation practices, including how it details criteria for policy development.

Plus, the report suggests that UnitedHealthcare review how it documents vetting criteria for internal and external subject matter experts. The company could also do more to formally document training and education for members of its governance committee.

Medical policy documents can be lengthy and complex, so the consultants recommended the insurer consider developing a template that standardizes the written format for each policy, which would make it easier to understand. For example, the template could make it easier to connect the evidence summary to the coverage rationale.

"While these gaps do not diminish the overall strength of the program, addressing them would further enhance transparency and consistency and provide opportunities for continued refinement," the analysts wrote.

DuPont Partners with Microsoft and NobleAI on Agentic AI for R&D

DuPont announced on November 12, 2026 a research collaboration with Microsoft and NobleAI that will broaden access to AI and machine-learning tools for its scientists and engineers, with the goal of reducing the time needed to deliver customer solutions.

Each company contributes a different component. DuPont supplies expertise in advanced solutions and applications; NobleAI provides Science-Based AI modeling through its Visualizations, Insights and Predictions (VIP) Platform; and Microsoft contributes Discovery, its enterprise agentic AI platform for R&D. DuPont’s stated goal is to help teams assess potential solutions faster, guide experiments and accelerate cycles of learning.

The proposed workflow separates research orchestration from scientific modeling. Aseem Datar, Microsoft’s corporate vice president of product innovation for Microsoft Discovery & Quantum, described Discovery as an agentic environment that connects R&D teams’ data, models and know-how for scientific discovery. NobleAI CEO Alex Wang divided the software roles between Discovery’s planning and orchestration of research and VIP’s predictive and optimization modeling.

Wang described a workflow in which DuPont teams would use their existing data, expertise and processes to explore more ideas in silico, meaning through computer simulation, before concentrating physical testing on the candidates most worth pursuing. That sequence places AI earlier in candidate selection: models help narrow the options, while physical testing remains part of development rather than being displaced by simulation.

NobleAI’s modeling approach is designed for situations where industrial data is limited. The announcement describes Science-Based AI as integrating scientific principles throughout the modeling workflow, VIP as turning limited industrial data into predictive models, and NobleAI as one of Microsoft Discovery’s first launch partners. That relationship brings the modeling approach into agentic workflows for industrial R&D.

DuPont is linking the technical work to a company-wide AI rollout model. Chief Information Officer Matt Abbott said, “This collaboration reflects how we are scaling AI at DuPont, with security and governance designed in from the start, so what proves out in one lab can be put to work across the company.”

That approach treats a successful laboratory result as a candidate for broader use, with security and governance forming part of the deployment design. Chief Technology Officer Marty DeGroot said the combined tools are expected to accelerate development, shorten time to market and expand innovation’s impact across DuPont’s portfolio. DuPont serves healthcare, water, construction and industrial markets, giving that portfolio-wide ambition a broad potential scope.

The practical test is whether this combination can shorten development cycles while producing results that hold up outside the initial laboratory. If it does, the governance model Abbott described would provide a route for turning a successful local workflow into a broader R&D capability across DuPont’s portfolio.

House Ways and Means ranking member Richard E. Neal and Senate Finance ranking member Ron Wyden opened an investigation on November 12, 2026 into the Trump administration’s approval and taxation of Nvidia H200 AI-chip sales to Chinese companies, including a 25% tariff, and demanded that relevant records be preserved.

The letter to Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick sets out a transaction chain: H200 chips manufactured in Taiwan would be imported into the United States for a security review, assessed the tariff, licensed by Commerce for export, and then shipped to China. That sequence is central to the inquiry because it combines an import tariff with permission for an onward export.

Section 232 of the Trade Expansion Act of 1962 permits presidential action against imports determined to threaten national security. Neal and Wyden argued that using it in this arrangement appears to create an unconstitutional export tax, effectively taking a cut of Nvidia’s China sales while benefiting Nvidia and the Chinese government and undermining U.S. security.

Their committees’ jurisdiction over taxation and tariffs gives the investigation a trade-law focus alongside the export-control questions. The inquiry therefore concerns both whether the chips should reach Chinese buyers and how the administration structured and taxed the transactions.

Commerce is being asked to preserve material concerning its Section 232 semiconductor-import investigation and resulting tariffs, its review and approval of Nvidia’s export-license applications, and its communications with or about Nvidia, former chief executive Jensen Huang, and their agents or representatives. The request covers existing and future records and includes a Section 232 report that, according to the letter, Lutnick allegedly sent Trump on December 22, 2025.

The lawmakers warned that federal criminal laws prohibit destroying records relevant to congressional investigations. Their demand makes the department’s internal deliberations, including how buyers were reviewed and how the tariff was constructed, part of the new inquiry.

Licensing diligence is another focus. Citing press reports, Neal and Wyden said Commerce approved sales to Chinese companies with government and military ties, including cutout companies with no physical presence at their listed addresses. They also repeated reports that Commerce suspended a separate investigation into Nvidia customer Megaspeed after outreach from Nvidia.

The letter, citing press reports, describes Alice Huang as a Chinese executive in a close relationship with Jensen Huang and as the leader of Megaspeed, a company suspected of smuggling Nvidia chips into China. U.S. intelligence officials identified her as a Chinese intelligence asset and concluded that she funneled information to the Chinese government and facilitated Chinese access to advanced Nvidia chips.

The letter says Trump and Lutnick were reportedly briefed on that intelligence, but Trump nevertheless granted Nvidia permission in December 2025 to sell H200 chips to China in exchange for a cut of export sales.

Neal and Wyden said the approval came only after Jensen Huang and Nvidia directed at least $26 million toward Trump’s personal priorities, including the White House ballroom. They questioned whether, and to what extent, political considerations improperly influenced the administration’s policy decisions.

A separate November 9, 2026 letter from House Foreign Affairs Democrats Gregory W. Meeks, Gabe Amo, and Sydney Kamlager-Dove sought an immediate halt to advanced AI-chip exports to China. They also asked Commerce to explain what officials knew about the reported intelligence connections, when senior officials were informed, and why exports continued.

That earlier appeal emphasized the chips’ importance to AI capability, military modernization, and economic competition. It warned that once those capabilities reach China’s government or entities acting for it, the consequences could be nearly impossible to reverse.

The earlier letter centered on stopping future exports. Neal and Wyden’s demand also puts the approval process itself under scrutiny, including whether tariff powers and political considerations displaced national-security judgments.

Unemployment Labs Reports Blackwell Patterns in Chinese AI Models

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

An Unemployment Labs analysis by Will DePue, published November 12, 2026, found initialization patterns consistent with NVIDIA’s export-controlled Blackwell GPUs in six open-weight models from DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, MiniMax and Ant Group.

The analysis associates DeepSeek-V4-Flash and MiniMax-M3 with initialization on B200 or B300 chips, and DeepSeek-V4-Pro, Kimi K3, Kimi K3.1-Pro and Ling-3.1-flash with GB200 or GB300 chips. The matches identify the hardware layout behind the starting weights; whether those chips also performed training is a separate inference.

Successive releases place the apparent changes in time. DeepSeek-V3 produced a Hopper reading in December 2024, followed by the two Blackwell-reading V4 models in April 2026. Moonshot AI’s Kimi K2.5 read as Hopper in January 2026 before K3 in July 2026; MiniMax-M2.7 and M3 followed the same pattern in April 2026 and June 2026, respectively; Ant Group’s Ling-3.0-flash and Ling-3.1-flash did so in August 2026 and October 2026.

The pattern was not universal. The newest readable models from Alibaba, Zhipu, Tencent, Xiaomi and StepFun produced Hopper rather than Blackwell readings.

The technique begins with weights, the numerical parameters that training adjusts. Before training, the PyTorch/cuRAND setup examined uses the Philox generator to produce repeatable values from a seed. GPU threads distribute those values among weight positions, and a sufficiently large initialization uses 2,048 threads per streaming multiprocessor (SM). Changing the SM count therefore changes where the same generated numbers land.

In the configurations tested, Hopper H100 SXM, H200 and H800 chips have 132 SMs and 270,336 threads; B200 and B300 chips have 148 SMs and 303,104 threads; GB200 and GB300 chips have 152 SMs and 311,296 threads. A reading identifies one of these groups, not a specific chip within it.

Weights reserved for words or symbols absent from training can preserve their initial values almost exactly, while trained weights may retain a weaker statistical relationship to those values. The investigators select a seed and layout on one set of weights, then test that unchanged combination on a separate set excluded from the search.

Under its statistical model, the report estimates that roughly 6 trillion seed-and-layout combinations would produce an expected strongest chance match of about 8 sigma, a statistical measure of distance from random variation. The strongest results for the identified models range from 49 to 1,385 sigma, and every individual test included reaches at least 20 sigma.

DePue’s physical-chip reproductions provided another check. Using DeepSeek’s recovered seed, an H100 reproduced DeepSeek-V3’s preserved values but not V4-Flash’s, while a B300 did the reverse. On the B300, unmodified PyTorch reproduced 461 of 469 selected V4-Flash rows with correlations above 0.99, including 445 complete bit-for-bit row matches. A rented GB200 reproduced all 13 preserved Kimi K3 rows with Moonshot’s seed; changing either recovered seed by one produced no matching rows.

The report says the method agreed with all 24 cases where a developer publicly named a specific model’s hardware, while cautioning that 11 involve step-zero checkpoints and related models are not independent. That makes the comparison a consistency check rather than a measured accuracy rate.

Kimi K3.1-Pro uses K3’s seed and may be a K3 checkpoint with further post-training. Assuming the initializing hardware also performed training, DePue estimates five distinct final runs at roughly 500 to 3,000 Blackwell GPUs each, generally over two to three months. The calculation divides training compute by chip throughput at 35% utilization, using DeepSeek’s disclosed token counts and weight-based token estimates for the other models. Applying the same arithmetic to DeepSeek-V3 produced 2,000 to 2,500 chips, compared with DeepSeek’s disclosed 2,048.

A match concerns initialization alone; it cannot inventory every processor used during later training. A lab could create initial values on one system and transfer them, mixed clusters could leave only some hardware traces, and a Hopper result does not exclude subsequent Blackwell training. Nor can weights establish chip ownership, location or acquisition. DePue’s report notes that Blackwell systems can be rented outside China and says this evidence alone establishes neither smuggling nor an export-control violation.

AMD’s MI300X is the identified hardware alternative. It normally uses 304 processing units but can be divided into two groups of 152 or configured to use 148, reproducing the layouts in physical tests. For DeepSeek-V4-Flash, 363 of 377 preserved values that differ between vendors matched NVIDIA and none matched AMD. That numerical test did not settle the other five models, although the report considers a specially configured AMD cluster very unlikely.

The report states that B200, B300, GB200 and GB300 exports to China require a U.S. license and that no such licenses have been reported; it also says the MI300X is controlled. The method itself requires public weights and a recognizable hardware-dependent initialization pattern. CPU initialization leaves no GPU layout, and Baidu’s PaddlePaddle generator does not carry the examined chip layout.

The analysis gives policymakers technical evidence of hardware access without supplying an enforcement conclusion: public weights can expose an initialization pattern, but determining whether export controls were breached would still require evidence about where the hardware was used, who owned it and how access was obtained.

It was Dave Talbott’s first year hunting a small farm in southeast Ohio. The property, which is owned by a family friend, has rolling hills and steep hardwoods and is scattered with hayfields.

“I never walked the farm until the week just before bow season opened,” says Talbott, who lives in Logan. “I found good deer sign, with lots of buck rubs on trees.”

Talbott didn’t set any trail cameras or bait. Instead, when the season opened, he just walked the woods, scouting and hunting different locations. He used a lightweight tree saddle and climbing sticks to check areas quickly and quietly.

“I sat in six different trees over 12 hunts, moving according to wind and weather conditions,” says the 26-year-old, who works in heavy construction.

On the morning of Nov. 7, he hunted a pinch point where he knew a few does were bedded.

“But I didn’t like the place and started walking the farm again when I climbed down that morning.”

It was the first cold day of autumn, cloudy and with temperatures around 40 degrees. Talbott located a couple of fresh scrapes, and climbed into a nearby tree around 1:30 p.m. to hunt that afternoon. He didn’t see much until about 4:30. That’s when he spotted a big deer headed his way along the field edge.

“I knew he was big, and a shooter, and it all happened very fast.”

When the buck stepped behind some brush 15 yards from Talbott, he drew. The deer stepped into an opening, and Talbott released his arrow.

Talbott saw the deer run about 30 yards and, pouring blood, enter the timber toward a distant hayfield. Then it disappeared over a hill.

“I was shaking uncontrollably when I called my wife, dad, and best friend [Brady Garren] and told them what had happened,” Talbott says. “Dad and Brady got there after sunset to help me look.”

Carrying flashlights, the three men followed a blood trail for about 150 yards. But the trail petered out. Then they heard thrashing in a nearby ravine. Thinking it might be Talbott’s buck, they backed out and called in Triple BC Deer Drone Recovery for assistance.

“They got there with a drone a couple hours later,” says Talbott. “They flew the drone to where we last found blood and in just a minute the buck was found in the ravine, but its head was up.”

They waited an hour and flew the drone again but saw just a slight movement from the buck. An hour later when they flew the drone a third and final time, they saw the buck was dead.

By then, around 11:30 p.m., more friends had arrived and seven people went into the ravine to help haul out Talbott’s huge buck.

“I knew he was a great buck, but I had no idea he was that big until I walked up to him,” Talbott says. “I was beyond ecstatic.”

It was after midnight by the time they field dressed the buck and dragged it 600 yards to the truck. They loaded it and drove to a friend’s cooler.

The next day Talbott’s wife, Alison, took photos before they caped the buck and processed the meat. The buck has 16 points and is an estimated 5.5 years old.

DOJ’s Operation TorNado Arrests 354 and Seizes $234 Million in Cash and Crypto

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The Justice Department and Europol said Thursday that Operation TorNado has produced 354 arrests and the seizure of more than $234 million in cash and virtual currencies, in what the FBI called the largest takedown yet of dark web drug vendors and buyers.

The announcement came from the department’s Joint Criminal Opioid and Darknet Enforcement team, or JCODE, an FBI-led group created in 2018. Officials said Operation TorNado is the sixth, and most extensive, internationally coordinated action since then, and that it builds on last year’s Operation RapTor. The sweep ran from May 2025 to October 2026.

Europol, which coordinated work with agencies in 14 countries, published a parallel statement the same day. The operation targeted vendors and buyers who had been active on five dark web markets already seized by law enforcement: Archetyp, Bohemia, Nemesis, Kingdom and Tor2Door. Officials also identified accounts on DrugHub and on forums, single-vendor shops and encrypted messaging services.

The seizures included about 5.7 metric tons of illicit drugs worldwide, among them fentanyl and other opioids, methamphetamine, heroin, cocaine, ecstasy and MDMA, plus 38 pill presses and 209 firearms. Neither the Justice Department nor Europol split the $234 million between cash and cryptocurrency, and neither named bitcoin or any other coin in the official totals.

Europol put the European share at more than €60 million in cash and cryptocurrencies, alongside 950 kilograms of drugs and 57 firearms, and the U.S. share at more than €150 million, or $166 million, in cash and cryptocurrencies, plus more than 4.8 metric tons of drugs and 152 firearms. Those regional money figures still combine cash and crypto. Europol said its forensic and blockchain analysis teams traced illicit transactions as part of the work.

The closest official window into how the vendors were paid comes from the cases the Justice Department highlighted and from court papers posted with the announcement.

Alexander Green, 32, of Jacksonville, North Carolina, was arrested in June and charged in the Southern District of Ohio with conspiracy to distribute controlled substances, including fentanyl. The department said he allegedly operated a vendor called STARLABS on DrugHub, which it described as a Monero-only dark web market, and that the group has been linked to two overdose deaths. A criminal complaint says undercover purchases from STARLABS were made “in exchange for cryptocurrency.” If convicted, he faces up to 20 years in prison.

In a separate Florida arrest, brothers Stanislav Chernyshov, 30, and Vladislav Chernyshov, 35, are charged in the Eastern District of Virginia with conspiracy to distribute Schedule I synthetic opioids. Prosecutors say they allegedly ran the vendor “BarbaraWhite,” selling counterfeit oxycodone pills laced with nitazenes and shipping more than 1,000 parcels across 49 states. A search-warrant affidavit describes bitcoin transfers tied to that account and the Nemesis market. They each face up to 20 years if convicted.

Operation TorNado led to 132 arrests in the United States, 59 in the United Kingdom, 45 in Hungary and 41 in Germany, along with arrests in France, New Zealand, the Netherlands, Slovenia, Austria, Ireland, Czechia, Australia and Finland. A number of investigations are still ongoing to identify the people behind remaining dark web accounts, the department said.

FBI Director Kash Patel called Operation TorNado “the largest interagency takedown of Dark Web drug vendors and buyers to date.” IRS Criminal Investigation Chief Jarod Koopman, whose agency was part of the JCODE group, put the financial work at the center: “Criminal networks may operate across borders and in the shadows of the Dark Web, but they cannot operate without money.”

Federal prosecutions are underway in more than 20 districts. An indictment or complaint only alleges that crimes were committed, and the defendants are presumed innocent until proven guilty.

What the announcement does not settle is how much of the $234 million was cryptocurrency, and how much of that was bitcoin. Officials have not said. The record on Operation TorNado shows a combined cash-and-crypto seizure, a Monero-only market in at least one featured case, and bitcoin transfers in court papers, without a breakdown of the total.

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Follow Healthline on our socials for all things health.

Wintertime can bring everyone down with its colder weather and less sunlight. But for those with eczema, this time of year can wreak havoc on their skin, too.

That’s the case for Grammy-winning singer, actress, and Destiny’s Child alum Kelly Rowland, who has been living with eczema since her 20s. During the winter months, she often dreads the cold.

“[You] don’t know what the environment is going to give you—because mine is also environmental—or what the weather is going to give you,” Rowland told Healthline.

Cold weather and indoor heating can reduce humidity, drying out the skin and triggering or worsening eczema flare-ups.

“[What we know] about the skin barrier is that it really is like a gate or wall against the outside world,” Camille Howard-Verovic, MD, a dermatologist at Active Dermatology, told Healthline.

In healthy skin, the barrier controls what enters and leaves the skin. However, for people with eczema, Howard-Verovic said they have skin barrier dysfunction, which allows substances such as allergens and irritants to penetrate the skin.

Cold weather can increase transepidermal water loss, a process in which moisture escapes through the skin’s outer barrier and evaporates into the surrounding air.

“People with eczema have a tremendous amount of transepidermal water loss, so you’re just drier,” said Howard-Verovic. “[You’re] not holding onto the water as well, so that causes the itching and the rash that we feel when we have eczema.”

Rowland first noticed eczema flares behind her ears as well as on her elbows, arms, and the back of her knees.

“[It] looked like a rash, so that’s what I thought it was all this time, and I dealt with it for so long until after I had my son and then it started to get worse on my face,” she said.

She eventually went to a dermatologist who diagnosed her with moderate-to-severe eczema, a chronic inflammatory skin condition that can cause intense itching, painful skin cracking, thickened skin, and redness or swelling.

When Rowland learned that eczema can be genetic, it made sense to her.

“I remember seeing big rashes on [my mom’s] neck and her arms…so she had it, and I guess I was the lucky one,” said Rowland.

At first, she tried eliminating foods that she thought might be triggers. She also bathed in oatmeal.

“[I] literally have had a tub full of oatmeal, like literally went to the store to get store-bought fresh oats,” she said. “I tried everything, and it doesn’t help until you go talk to a doctor about it.”

At first, she tried prescription topical medications but found them cumbersome.

“I don’t have the time, if I’m being honest, and we live in a world that is moving so fast…I started buying like three tubes of whatever it was I was using and losing it; it was just too much for me,” Rowland said.

Rowland said she found relief with Ebglyss, a prescription injectable medication for moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis. She has also partnered with Eli Lilly and Company, its manufacturer, to raise awareness about eczema.

Rowland said she’s sharing her experience to help others living with eczema, including people she’s met who have experienced greater challenges managing the condition.

“One lady was telling me about how she’s having a tough time [looking] in the mirror cause she doesn’t know who’s looking back at her, and she saw her skin before eczema and after,” said Rowland.

When she first had symptoms, she said she felt embarrassed, too.

“[Because] it was just in the middle of my arm space and…[if I] put out my hand to shake someone’s hand, you don’t know if somebody’s judgy,” she said.

Rowland hopes being open about her condition will encourage others to advocate for themselves and talk with their doctor about treatment options.

Because eczema can look different on deeper skin tones, Howard-Verovic noted that a dermatologist can help make the correct diagnosis.

“Some people may not even know that they have eczema,” she said. “We know that eczema is a rash; it can appear red on certain skin tones, and it can be grey or white on certain skin tones, so it can look a little different.”

In addition to medication, Rowland said managing stress helps reduce her eczema flare-ups.

“Before [when] I went on tour, because I was under so much stress, it flared up like crazy, like you wouldn’t believe,” she said. “We were in the back trying to figure out how to cover certain things up, and we figured some of it out, and some of it was really tricky, but what helped was when I actually just calmed down…not that it helped overnight, but it did just kind of bring the flare down.”

She said red light therapy, feeling grounded outside without her shoes on, getting vitamin D from natural sunlight, and cooking are ways she reduces ongoing stress.

“Cooking calms me when my kids aren’t here, and I have a really good [Zen] playlist on,” said Rowland. “[If] you ask me a question [in those moments], I might give you my heart; I’m just so happy.”

She also tries to cut back on sugar, which she believes triggers flares.

Another way to reduce flares, according to Howard-Verovic, is to avoid hot showers, especially in the winter.

“[We] know eczematous skin likes lukewarm water,” she said.

Because Howard-Verovic and her daughter both live with eczema, she set a rule about when it’s time to get out of the shower.

“[If] you see condensation on the bathroom mirror, you’ve spent too much time in the shower,” said Howard-Verovic. “[That] really hot, hot water feels really good in that moment…but when you get out all that water evaporates, and there’s no moisture in the atmosphere, and it dries your skin out, and now your skin is unhappy again.”

If you prefer to take baths, she said use gentle cleansers.

“[There] are bleach baths for really, really bad flare-ups,” she noted.

Diluted bleach baths are sometimes recommended for people with eczema, but they should only be used according to a healthcare professional’s instructions.

Scout Motors has 212,000 vehicle reservations as $2 billion South Carolina plant nears completion

📅 2026-11-12  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

BLYTHEWOOD, S.C. — Electric vehicle startup Scout Motors has notched roughly 212,000 reservations for its upcoming EVs as the company nears completion of its new $2 billion plant here, CEO Scott Keogh told CNBC.

The reservations are for the Volkswagen-backed company's Terra pickup truck and Traveler SUV, which are expected to start full production next year before deliveries begin to customers in 2028.

Reservations do not necessarily translate to sales, as the U.S. automotive industry has learned during the past decade, especially with highly anticipated all-electric trucks such as the Ford F-150 Lightning, Tesla Cybertruck and others that garnered strong interest that didn't lead to meaningful numbers of purchases.

Keogh said he expects Scout could roughly double those reservations by the time customer production begins, but he said the more important thing is to convert early interest into actual sales as production nears.

"It's been a consistent drumbeat," Keogh said during an interview at Scout's plant site outside Columbia. "I think we could easily be up over 400,000 with reservations without doing too much effort. The big thing for us is how much of the initial production can we 100% presell."

Keogh said achieving an order book of 12 to 18 months "would be great" and is something he thinks the company can do before production. Scout on Thursday said plant construction is "in its final phase."

Scout's vehicles will be offered as all-electric versions as well as electric hybrid variants, known as extended-range electric vehicles, or EREVs, that include an internal combustion engine as a backup generator for the electric motors and batteries.

Scout, a former American vehicle brand from 1961 to 1980, was expected to exclusively offer EVs in a bid for the German automaker to expand its presence in the U.S. However, slower-than-expected adoption of EVs and higher costs led the company to decide to offer EREVs as well.

Keogh said extended-range and SUV models are dominating reservations thus far, with roughly 87% choosing EREV models and 75% being SUVs.

The CEO also said the company is drawing interest from its target markets, which include owners of off-road vehicles such as the Ford Bronco and Jeep Wrangler as well as full-size pickup trucks and U.S. EV leader Tesla.

Given the interest signaled by reservations, Keogh said the company is expected to launch with the EREVs first, followed by the EVs. He also said the company plans to launch with mid-level models first, a deviation from many companies that begin with their highest-priced models.

Both the Traveler and Terra are expected to start at under $60,000. The EREV models are expected to feature more than 500 miles of range, according to the company, compared with 300 miles of range for the all-electric models.

"The No.1 thing for me is I want to go get the best volume as soon as possible," Keogh said. "In my mind, it's how quick can you get to 100,000 units? It's at 100,000 units where you can start to punch through."

His comments come months after Rivian Automotive received criticism online for launching its R2 SUV with its highest-priced models first, despite that being a common practice across the automotive industry.

Keogh said Scout's South Carolina plant is about 85% complete, but supporting infrastructure and a $300 million supplier park continue to be installed.

Reservations for Scout's vehicles initially opened in October 2024, when it revealed the Traveler and Terra, which feature sleek, yet rugged, exteriors that pay some homage to the brand's historic models.
