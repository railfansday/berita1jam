Warren Buffett Just Gave Away $5.5 Billion of Berkshire Stock. He Says His Confidence in Berkshire Is "As Strong as Ever."

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The rest of his shares are set to leave his hands by 2034. Does this matter for the stock?

Warren Buffett gave away another big part of his wealth on Monday, Nov. 16. The chairman emeritus of Berkshire Hathaway (BRKA -1.02%)(BRKB -1.03%) converted 6,150 Class A shares into 9,225,000 Class B shares and donated them to four family foundations. At Monday's closing price, the gift was worth around $5.5 billion.

This gift is over three times the 2.7 million Class B shares he gave the same four foundations last Thanksgiving. Buffett also paired it with a short note to shareholders.

"You heard plenty from me in September about my confidence in Berkshire. That confidence is as strong as ever," Buffett wrote.

Berkshire's Class B shares finished at $592.85 on Monday, just under Friday's record close of $592.94. The stock is up around 18% in 2026 as I write.

Warren Buffett. Image source: Getty Images.

This year's note is much shorter than last year's, and most of it is about kindness, not business.

On Berkshire itself, Buffett just pointed back to September. That month, Berkshire announced that he had become chairman emeritus and that his son Howard Buffett had taken over as chairman.

In a letter put out with that news, Buffett wrote that he was more confident than ever in the future. In other words, Monday's gift isn't a vote against the stock.

After Monday's gift, Buffett holds 182,140 Class A shares and 1,162 Class B shares. His stake's worth around $162 billion at Monday's prices, or about 13% of Berkshire's market value.

It's all spoken for. In July, Buffett wrote that he hopes his children can "carry out the disposal of my shares" by Dec. 31, 2034. Either way, he said, the shares will reach the four foundations by then.

Showing how much the giving has picked up, Buffett's two gifts this year come to around 21.2 million Class B shares -- 12 million in July and about 9.2 million on Monday. The four family foundations got around 5.6 million Class B shares from him in two gifts in 2025. This year's total is almost four times as big.

Buffett explained the faster pace last year, saying that his children are all past normal retirement age and that he wants them to finish the work themselves. Of course, he added that the acceleration "in no way reflects any change in my views about Berkshire's prospects."

The foundations aren't built to sit on the stock. Buffett's 2006 pledge letter to Howard's foundation, for example, said there was no need to build large reserves, because new gifts would come every year. The Susan Thompson Buffett Foundation, the biggest recipient, paid around $1.5 billion in grants in 2025 alone.

It's fair to expect most of Buffett's remaining stake to be sold over time. Spread evenly through 2034, around $162 billion comes to about $20 billion of stock a year. That might seem like a lot. But Berkshire bought back around $14.4 billion of its own stock through September, including about $9.6 billion in the third quarter. Monday's 9.2 million shares also equal only about two days of normal trading in the Class B stock, based on its average volume over the last three months.

True, the buybacks might slow. Berkshire only repurchases shares when its CEO thinks the price is under the company's intrinsic value (an estimate of what the whole business is worth). And the stock is now near a record. But selling spread across gifts planned and announced years ahead arguably isn't likely to move the stock much by itself.

The price matters more. Berkshire's third-quarter operating earnings (which strip out the swings in its stock portfolio that hit net income) dropped about 6% year over year.

The largest drag was insurance underwriting, where profit slid about 43%.

But the stock now trades at about 27 times operating earnings over the past four quarters, up from about 22 times operating earnings at the end of September.

Ultimately, I don't see anything in Monday's gift or letter that should worry a Berkshire shareholder. For a new buyer, though, the record price is the bigger issue. I still think Berkshire is worth a look for the long run, but I'd be more comfortable paying under $593 for it.

7 Greatest Action Movies That Are Also Biopics, Ranked

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

It is not often thought of to associate action films with biographical dramas, but the two can intersect more often than not. There is a lineage of classic epics that are at least in part inspired by real stories, and many of them have been embellished over time. While many of these action epics are more based on religion than they are on history, which is the case with films like Ben-Hur or The Robe, they certainly kicked off an interest in using stories passed down by generations as a creative influence.

It can sometimes be hard to define a film if it does not entirely qualify for both categories. Lawrence of Arabia is a classic biopic that contains a lot of action, but might not have enough for it to be fully defined as an “action film.” Similarly, an action classic like Gladiator is loosely based on a true story, but contains too many factual inaccuracies for it to realistically be considered a biopic. Although the two definitions can be fungible, they crossover more times than some might expect.

Escape From Alcatraz was one of the greatest collaborations between Clint Eastwood and director Don Siegel, who had previously made the first Dirty Harry film together in addition to several other Westerns. The film is based on the true story of a 1962 prison escape in which three inmates on Alcatraz Island devised a scheme to break out of the notoriously secure facility. Eastwood stars as the ringleader of the group, and the supporting cast included Danny Glover in his first film role.

Escape from Alcatraz offered insights into the most mysterious prison in the United States, and attempted to depict it with more accuracy than the more sensationalist productions of films like The Rock. Although Eastwood had already built up a screen persona of someone who plays tough, morally flexible characters, Escape from Alcatraz saw him effectively mirroring a figure from the recent past, giving one of his best performances from a film that he did not also direct.

Bloodsport was widely marketed as being based on a true story, even if the facts regarding Frank Lux have been heavily debated, and in all likelihood somewhat fictionalized. There are nonetheless a number of great biopics that play fast and loose with the facts, and the inaccuracies involving Lux’s records do not prevent Bloodsport from being one of the most compelling martial-arts spectacles of all-time.

Jean-Claude Van Damme is not exactly an actor with a tremendous amount of range, but his skills as an actual martial artist made him perfect to star in this gritty thriller about a secretive fighting ring that accepts the best candidates in the world. Van Damme would go on to star in several other martial arts films, but the simplicity and unabashed brutality of Bloodsport made it a cult classic within the subgenre of late ‘80s fighting movies.

Braveheart is one of the breakaway successes of the ‘90s as it became a word-of-mouth hit in the summer of 1995 that turned into a true sensation, winning the Academy Award for Best Picture alongside a trophy for Best Director for Mel Gibson. Although the film has been heavily debated by historians for many of its perceived inaccuracies about the actual revolution by William Wallace, Braveheart certainly captures the sentiment of the Scottish rebels who were determined to be freed of English rule. Although Gibson’s only directorial credit up until that point had been the earnest drama The Man Without A Face, he certainly was able to transition into making a sweeping historical epic.

Braveheart captures the spirit of war in all of its ugliness in a successful way, as it offers maximum bloodshed without lionizing the act of violence itself; the story of the revolution begins as a simple act of revenge on the part of Wallace, and it blows up into a full-on political siege. While Gibson is by no means a subtle filmmaker and there are a number of rather obvious allusions to Christinaity, they felt less glaring back in 1995 when he had not yet reiterated his prior work.

Rescue Dawn is a curious project from Werner Herzog, who had previously made a documentary in 1997 about the same subject, Dieter Dengler, a German-American pilot who was shot down in the middle of the Vietnam War and forced to survive imprisonment and isolation. Although Herzog’s documentary had effectively captured the events of Dengler’s life and pointed to the context of why his rescue was so challenging, the narrative film is a more visceral experience that explores the minutia of being lost in the wilderness. The most important decision was casting Christian Bale to play Dengler, as at this point he had already attracted renown for his ability to physically transform.

Bale perfectly captures the spirit of a patriotic man who is hollowed-out by dispirited bleakness, and completely gives himself over to the part. Although Bale ended up getting the most attention, Rescue Dawn features an extraordinary supporting cast that includes brilliant supporting performances from Steve Zahn and Jeremy Davies.

The Revenant was based on the somewhat obscure story of Hugh Glass, a tracker in the early 19th century who was abandoned by his fellow men during a mission and left to die after his own son is killed by the deceitful con artist Fitzgerald (Tom Hardy). Given that it was an Alejandro Gonzalez Inarritu film, there is a tremendous amount of artistry relating to pain and suffering by means of the natural world; Leonardo DiCaprio completely transformed in an unrecognizable performance to play Glass, who is nearly killed by a bear before he grows up the strength to make the long journey in order to kill Fitzgerald.

The Revenant is an untraditional film for everyone involved, as it saw DiCaprio destroying his star persona to play a humbled man who calls upon ethereal strength in order to seek balance. It’s a revenge thriller that questions the very nature of vengeance, and it uses Glass’ violent odyssey as a means of exploring all the evil that exists within the American West, including the genocide of indigenious people and the destruction of nature. Rarely has a film of 156 minutes felt so relentlessly compelling, which is in large part due to the Oscar-winning cinematography of Emmanuel Lubezki, who used natural lighting to make the depiction of the Great Plains even more authentic.

Hacksaw Ridge was a comeback vehicle for Gibson after a period of Hollywood exile that briefly made it seem like he was going to be taken seriously again, at least until he started making more garbage like Flight Risk. Although it is unfortunate that Gibson seems intent on burning more bridges instead of actually seeking forgiveness, Hacksaw Ridge is a strong example of what he can do when he’s trained on telling an inherently compelling historical story that has strong moral takeaways. The film explores the plight of Desmond Doss, a drafted man in World War II who is played by Andrew Garfield. While Doss’ religious principles prevented him from touching a weapon, he agreed to serve the army as a medic, and saved countless lives in the process.

Hacksaw Ridge is well-drawn as a three-act hero’s journey arc that dedicates its first third to showing Doss’ upbringing, its second section examining the conflicts he came into with the most belligerent members of the army, and a stunning finale that includes some of the most gripping World War II combat footage ever brought to the screen. Although the film is stunningly violent in a way that does not shy away from the sheer brutality of wartime activity within the Pacific Theater, Garfield gives a deeply sensitive and moving performance that embodies the messianic themes at play. The entire cast is quite strong, with Hugo Weaving in particular giving an unforgettable performance as Doss’ father.

Napoleon is an interesting historical epic that was many years in the making, given that there have been many attempts to bring to life one of the most famous military leaders of all-time. Ridley Scott obviously has a vested interest in telling grandiose historical stories, and Napoleon allowed him to focus solely on the career of one man in a way that he wasn’t able to with more ensemble-based epics like Kingdom of Heaven and The Last Duel. The surprise of Napoleon is that Scott was more interested in the perception of a historical icon than nailing all of the facts; Joaquin Phoenix does not look or sound anything like the real Napoleon (and doesn’t even attempt to do a French accent), but the film still works as a harrowing character study.

Napoleon explores the extent of ego, as it uses the framework of a “great man” biopic to question why such vain men seek greatness. As is often the case, Scott made a much longer director’s cut that is superior.

The Last of Us is a TV show that has meant a lot to a lot of people, as it has successfully translated the emotional core of the hugely popular video game and built on the complexity of the character relationships. The final season, anticipated to debut in 2027, is sure to be emotional for fans, too. So it’s not at all surprising to hear that the cast is emotional as they get to the end of filming and will have to say goodbye to each other.

An HBO show that got the world talking from the moment it debuted, the post-apocalyptic drama started by following Joel (Pedro Pascal) as he escorted the teenage Ellie (Bella Ramsey) across a devastated United States ravaged by a fungal infection that had turned people into violent creatures. With Season 3 delving deeper into the journey of Joel’s brother, Tommy Miller, actor Gabriel Luna not only has to say goodbye to a character he’ll know much better, but he’ll also be leaving the cast and crew behind after bidding them an emotional farewell. Here’s what he told Entertainment Weekly about the emotions that are already starting to swirl:

Oh man, a lot of emotions are going around. But right now I think we’re all too tired to feel them. [Laughs.] Because it’s just massive. Obviously I can’t give anything away, but people who’ve played the game know the level of the scope of what we’re dealing with here towards the end of the story. I’m so proud of Bella [Ramsey], Kaitlyn [Dever], and Kyriana [Kratter], who’s joined the cast, for being the tip of the spear and shouldering the weight of this production and what it requires from them.

If you played The Last of Us games, you knew that Abby would be seen on the show as a villain after killing Pedro Pascal's Joel. No matter her motivation or the extent of her own trauma, it would be challenging for viewers to understand the actions of someone taking out such a beloved character. And then, the Season 2 finale ended with the focus shifting to Kaitlyn Dever's Abby, who will take center stage in The Last of Us Season 3.

In a world like the one portrayed in The Last of Us, where everything is heightened as you just try to survive and not get killed or infected, emotions understandably run high for the cast helping bring that world to life. And even though Tommy comes in and out of the story, The Terminal List actor can’t help but be impressed by how the Ellie, Abby, and Lev actors are handling everything required of them, as they head closer to the finish line. He went on to say:

The bulk of my work was back in the summer, and then I’d go back and forth and come in for two, three weeks of time, but they’re here every day and doing the lion’s share of the work.

Since the Naughty Dog video game-turned-TV series is a massive production, it sounds like there’s going to be a prolonged series of goodbyes taking place as storylines are wrapped up and actors depart. Gabriel Luna not only has to process the emotion of saying goodbye to his own character and wrapping up a story that he likely feels very connected to after three seasons, but he also has to handle the departure of each of his co-stars. That’s a lot of emotions for the Dexter: Resurrection star, who will probably be exhausted by the time it’s all said and done. He pretty much said just that:

We’re getting to the end of it, and people are starting to wrap, and we’re giving them their flowers, and then they’re heading off, and one by one, the family is dwindling down to just the final few. We’re about to get a welcomed break and respite from what we’ve been doing, and then we have to come back and finish strong after the holiday. We’re so excited for this thing to come out.

I can only imagine the tears that will be flowing and the emotions that will be felt as the final days of the HBO series come to an end, and everyone says their final goodbyes. Fans anxiously anticipating the third season will have to pack their patience pants while waiting for details on when it will officially premiere. In the meantime, the first two seasons of The Last of Us are streaming with an HBO Max subscription.

The First Original Movie of the Decade To Make $500 Million Is Officially on Netflix

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Hannah covers horror, sci-fi, and games at Collider, where she writes news and features, reviews new releases, and interviews the people who make them. She’s helping build out Collider’s gaming coverage, with Pokémon as one of her specialties. If she isn’t writing about a monster, she’s probably watching one on a tape from her VHS collection.

On its second weekend in theaters, one horror movie made $6.7 million more than it had on its first. That’s the kind of jump you might expect from a movie that opened in a few cities and then went nationwide, but Curry Barker’s horror movie was already in wide release. Its first weekend saw $17.2 million in earnings, followed by $23.9 million the following weekend, a 39% increase. The entire movie was filmed on a $750,000 budget, so even numbers from the first two weekends indicated an incredibly successful directorial debut for Barker.

What's most impressive is that Barker had a truly unique idea to sell with Obsession. A guy named Bear (Michael Johnston) breaks a trinket called the One Wish Willow and asks it to make his friend Nikki (Inde Navarrate) love him more than anything in the world. If you’ve ever spent five minutes trying to explain a horror movie to somebody without giving it away, you can probably see the advantage of having a setup that fits in a sentence.

As of November 17, Obsession is streaming on Netflix in the U.S. Its arrival on wide streaming comes with plenty of anticipation after it became Hollywood's first original movie of the 2020s to gross $500 million worldwide, a milestone the movie reached in August. Obsession became the fifth horror movie to gross $500 million worldwide, joining It (2017), The Sixth Sense, I Am Legend, and World War Z.

Barker told The Playlist that Netflix offered to buy the movie after its 2025 Toronto International Film Festival premiere and put it straight on streaming. However, Curry ultimately went with Focus Features, who acquired Obsession and released it theatrically in May. The movie eventually made more worldwide than any Conjuring movie, then went to Peacock for its first subscription streaming run. With Obsession finally reaching Netflix, it has already become the kind of hit a studio can spend years trying desperately to pull out of an established franchise.

The wish is what gets people in the door, but Inde Navarrette is the reason it works for a whole movie. Bear (Michael Johnston) already knows Nikki well enough to tell when she’s acting like herself, which makes him a lousy person to trust when he decides things are going fine. He wanted her to love him, so every time she gives him another reason to be scared, she’s also giving him a reason to stay. There’s an obvious question in the title about how far Wish Nikki will go once the wish takes hold, but it could also be said that the title actually represents Bear's obsession with Nikki and unwillingness to let Nikki go. After all, Bear is the true villain of the movie.

Obsession is available to stream on Netflix.

Did Nicolas Cage Really Gain 60 Pounds to Play John Madden? He Says...

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Nicolas Cage's transformation for Madden—the biopic in which he plays the legendary NFL coach while costarring with Christian Bale—included putting on 25 pounds plus prosthetics.

Nicolas Cage refuses to fumble a role by not looking the part.

After all, the National Treasure alum put on weight in addition to wearing a heavy bodysuit while portraying famed Oakland Raiders coach John Madden instead of relying on "a frozen mask of prosthetics."

"Sort of a hybrid of gaining weight—I gained about 25 pounds and added some prosthetics to achieve a 60-pound look," Cage exclusively told E! News' Will Marfuggi at the Madden premiere. "Enough of myself where I could still be expressive."

And though the dramatic body transformation brought the Longlegs actor closer to walking in on the late 85-year-old's cleats, that doesn't mean Cage didn't put his own artistic spin on the character.

"I'm not an impressionist. I lean more towards being a surrealist," the Oscar winner explained. "I'm trying to find the energy, the imagination, the heart of the man and dig deep within and try to inform it with something that is emotionally accurate."

Still, the 62-year-old—who attended the film's debut ahead of its release on Prime Video and in select theaters Nov. 18 with wife Riko Shibata, 31—studied Madden's "cadence and some of his gestures" to bring authenticity to his interpretation of the NFL legend.

In his words, Cage's game tape ahead of shooting entailed "a lot of reviewing interviews that he gave, and watching his announcing, and watching some old footage of him on the football field."

GTA 6's Release Will Stress The Internet Just As Everything's Falling Apart

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Rockstar's game arrives amid major network outages

In early November, some internet service providers warned their customers that pre-downloads of Grand Theft Auto 6 were causing network slowdown issues for everyone on the service. That problem became visible before millions of gamers tried to log into the same game simultaneously. A long list of recent outages and issues across industries reliant on tech and network services now makes it seem likely that the biggest game launch of all time won't go down smoothly.

If you've ever been around for the release of a big new game, you know that day-one network issues are fairly common. Some hiccups are worse than others, of course, but most people are probably already bracing for some potential hitches when we're talking about the biggest game launch of all time. What makes GTA 6 different from other game releases is its scale and timing.

The highly anticipated sequel first began causing issues for some ISPs when Rockstar opened preloads for the 212 GB game on Nov. 10. At the time, some providers claimed that the game was directly responsible for never-before-seen traffic peaks so high that they congested the proverbial internet tubes for everyone else.

Rockstar undoubtedly foresaw that the problem could occur or worsen by launch day. The studio dropped a couple of patches in a staggered rollout prior to launch. The release cadence made it less likely that GTA 6 fans could overload Rockstar's, PlayStation's, and Xbox's servers at the same time. Service providers have also been gearing up for Nov. 19, with at least one ISP advertising that it would offer increased download speeds specifically for GTA 6.

Yet network issues of some sort, both in the game and the internet at large, may be unavoidable on Thursday. Recent trends show that many online services are already hanging on by a thread. Earlier in November, flights across the country were halted due to apparent issues with aging routers that could not handle the amount of data shared by newer pieces of equipment. During that same week, Microsoft products like Outlook and Excel went dark for nearly 24 hours because a faulty gateway was overwhelmed by the amount of data passing through it. On Nov. 16, customers were unable to access accounts at major banks like Chase or through payment services like Zelle.

On Nov. 17, Apple users reported widespread issues with iCloud, AppleCare, App Store, and Apple Music. Apple has not revealed the cause of the issues, noting only that outages are slowing down services. On that same Tuesday, news broke that the United States' national security network also went down after weeks of wonkiness.

The quick succession of these instances has led to conspiracy theories online that they're the result of cyberattacks. So far, there's no evidence to support such suspicions in relation to recent newsworthy outages. It's probable that the specific causes of all of these events vary widely, but there's an overarching theme among all of them. Online services, by and large, are built on fragile interconnected systems. Some of those systems rely on old hardware and software that are buckling under normal but increasing strain. Even modernizing systems are not immune — those efforts may actually be responsible for the sudden tech difficulties.

Kris Beevers, CEO of a network and infrastructure company, told the BBC on Monday, “I expect we’ll see more meaningful outages in the infrastructure that essential parts of society depend on in coming months as the invisible infrastructure that our world depends on becomes more strained and visible.”

Since GTA 6 is already responsible for an internet-wide slowdown by some ISPs, it may not be a bad idea to preload the game and its updates ASAP. The same advice applies to those who have purchased physical copies — the game has to be downloaded from Rockstar. Nov. 19 will still probably be rocky for the game if not for the entire internet. But hey: if GTA 6 took over a decade to get here, what's another day?

General-purpose robot maker Genesis AI reels in $500M at $3.5B valuation

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Genesis AI SAS today announced that it has raised $500 million in funding from a group of prominent investors.

Premji Invest, Khosla Ventures and Eclipse led the Series A round. They were joined by the venture capital arms of Nvidia Corp., Advanced Micro Devices Inc., Zoom Communications Inc., Samsung Electronics Co. and Ericsson. Former Google Chief Executive Eric Schmidt contributed as well along with other tech executives.

Paris-based Genesis develops a general-purpose robot called Eno (pictured). Unlike humanoid robots, it moves using a wheeled base rather than legs. The wheeled base can adjust Eno’s height using a mechanism that consists of two large panels connected by a hinge. The module enables the robot to adapt its configuration to each task.

Eno is powered by a custom artificial intelligence model called GENE. Genesis says that the algorithm is easier to train than traditional robotics-optimized neural networks, which can translate to higher output quality.

The dataset on which an AI model is trained must be tailored to the robot it’s intended to control. If a company is building an AI model for a four-legged robot, the training dataset must include footage of four-legged robots. The more specialized the robot, the more difficult it is to collect the necessary training data.

Genesis has come up with a workaround to that challenge. Eno’s palms and fingers are based on a custom design closely modeled after the human hand. As a result, the robot can be trained on demonstrations in which humans perform tasks with their hands. That kind of training data is much easier to produce than footage of robots performing work.

Eno’s robotic hands have 20 degrees of freedom, which means they can perform that many motions. The human hand has 27 degrees of freedom. Additionally, Genesis covered the hands with a soft material designed to mimic skin.

The GENE foundation model that powers Emo can perform tasks comprising dozens of steps. Genesis has released demo videos that show the system playing the piano, cooking and solving a Rubik’s Cube. According the company, that versatility enables Eno to operate in not only industrial environments but also homes.

“We’re working to build the world’s best general-purpose robot,” said Genesis co-founder and CEO Zhou Xian. “By building it all ourselves, we can obsess over every detail and improve the entire robot faster in one closed loop. This funding will accelerate our efforts to deploy our robots in the real world at scale.”

The company will use the capital to hire more AI researchers and roboticists. Additionally, Genesis will step up its training data collection efforts in a bid to expand its AI model’s capabilities.
