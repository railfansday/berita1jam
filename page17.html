EU plans €50 billion sovereign tech equity fund to boost quantum technology

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The European Commission proposed a tech fund with a target size of €50 billion to boost quantum computing efforts. The bloc aims to keep up with the US and China in the global race.

The European Commission proposed a €50 billion ($54.73 billion) equity financing fund on Wednesday to boost artificial intelligence and quantum computing in Europe.

The draft "Quantum Act" aims to help Europe compete in a race against the United States and China.

"Quantum technologies will shape the future ‌of industry, security and innovation, and they will have a ‌profound ​impact on our societies. Europe needs to accelerate now," EU tech chief Henna Virkkunen said in a statement.

"We want to build a fund and our target scale is now €50 billion," she said, calling it a "sufficient starting point."

Virkkunen introduced the officially named "Sovereign Tech Equity Fund" while presenting new rules to support Europe's quantum technology sector.

The new rules will push public authorities to favor European companies when giving public contracts and funding.

The European Union (EU) also wants to reduce its dependence on third countries amid concerns that US companies could use so-called "kill switches” to disable their products in Europe.

Quantum computing technology is still at the early stages of development, with both the US and China competing to achieve dominance in the field.

While classical computers process data using bits — which are either 0 or 1 — quantum computers use qubits (quantum bits), which can exist in a blend of both states at once.

By using qubits, these machines could eventually solve complex problems much faster than today's supercomputers.

The EU expects the quantum computing to lead to breakthroughs in several areas, including drug development, earlier medical diagnoses or super-efficient batteries and even AI development.

Although quantum technology offers significant potential, it also poses cybersecurity risks, as it could undermine encryption and other safeguards for digital data at a time when governments are battling AI-driven cyberattacks.

Around one-third of the world's quantum companies ⁠are ​based in the European Union, according to ​the Commission. Last year, public funding for quantum technologies reached €8.6 billion in the bloc, compared with €5.3 billion in the United States and €13.6 billion in China.

However, European quantum start-ups have attracted €1.1 billion in private funding so far, compared with €7 billion in the United States.

The EU launched its "quantum Europe" strategy in July 2025, aiming to be a global leader in quantum technologies by 2030.

The bloc is already a global leader in quantum research but it falls short when it comes to transforming this into viable commercial opportunities.

The draft legislation must be negotiated with EU member states and the European Parliament in the upcoming months before it can be law.

Trump is proposing new tariffs on China, the E.U., and 12 other economies

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The proposed Section 301 duties target industrial overcapacity, with China facing a rate that could rise to 40%

The Trump administration proposed new tariffs on China, the European Union and 12 other economies on Wednesday, arguing that the countries singled out maintain more factory capacity than they need and that this weighs on U.S. business. The 14 economies received advance notice of the actions.

The proposal covers Bangladesh, Cambodia, China, the EU, India, Indonesia, Japan, South Korea, Malaysia, Singapore, Switzerland, Taiwan, Thailand and Vietnam, according to Bloomberg.

China would face a 20% rate in addition to the 25% duty that took effect in 2018, according to CNBC. That rate could climb to 40% if Beijing does not honor its commitments under the trade truce reached in October 2025 in Busan, South Korea.

Goods from the EU, Japan, South Korea and Switzerland would face 15% rates that could rise to 20%, according to Bloomberg. Indian goods would be taxed at 17% if New Delhi seals a trade agreement with Washington, and at up to 25% under the proposal.

The new duties would not be piled onto the forced-labor tariffs of 10% or 12.5%. For goods that qualify under both, the larger of the two rates would be charged, according to Bloomberg. Oil and gas, food the U.S. cannot grow domestically, and goods already hit by sector-specific duties, such as pharmaceuticals and critical minerals, would be exempt.

The proposal will go through a period of public input and could be revised. A final determination might land in January at the earliest, according to Bloomberg. Comparable steps targeting Mexico and Norway were omitted from the proposal, though officials are still weighing them.

China's foreign ministry said on Wednesday that "the so-called 'overcapacity' does not really exist," according to CNBC. The trade truce between Washington and Beijing runs out in January unless both sides renew it, according to Bloomberg.

The administration had been preparing to raise duties on the targeted economies after a Section 301 investigation that the Office of the U.S. Trade Representative opened in March. U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer has said state backing has left those economies with more industrial capacity than they need, which lets their companies undercut competitors abroad on price and hurts American manufacturers.

Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974 gives Washington a way to retaliate against overseas commercial conduct it judges unfair or a burden on U.S. commerce. In February, the Supreme Court invalidated the duties President Donald Trump had levied under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act, according to CNBC.

After the ruling, Trump invoked Section 122 of the Trade Act to levy a worldwide tariff of 10%, according to CNBC. He later swapped that tariff for duties of up to 12.5% covering 60 economies, imposed through Section 301 on the grounds of forced-labor practices. Those duties face legal challenges, according to Bloomberg.

Einsia AI Open-Source RoboUniverse-10K, the First 10K-Scale RL Environment Suite for Agential Robotics

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The open-source project includes 10,807 physics-interactive simulation tasks and a 500-task benchmark; fine-tuning improves task success from 0.8% to 21.2%.

SINGAPORE, Nov. 25, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Einsia AI today announced the release of RoboUniverse-10K, an open-source suite of 10,807 interactive physics simulation environments designed for embodied AI agent training. Developed in collaboration with researchers from Tsinghua University, Peking University, the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign (UIUC), the University of Hong Kong (HKU), University College London (UCL), and the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill (UNC-Chapel Hill), the project aims to address a fundamental bottleneck in embodied AI: the lack of scalable, physically realistic, and verifiable training environments.

The release also includes RoboUniverse-Verified, an independent benchmark comprising 500 held-out tasks. Using approximately 17,000 multi-turn interaction trajectories collected from the environments, the team fine-tuned a Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-based model, improving its task success rate from 0.8% to 21.2%. Experiments across additional simulation benchmarks and on a real Franka robot further demonstrate the potential of the approach.

Large language models have benefited from scaling training data across the internet, while coding agents have advanced through executable environments built from real-world software repositories, such as SWE-Gym and SWE-smith. Embodied AI faces a more complex challenge: agents must interact with physical environments governed by realistic dynamics, while relying on accurate feedback to determine whether their actions have succeeded.

Yet simulation environments have traditionally been expensive and labor-intensive to build, limiting the number and diversity of available tasks. Without reliable physics and task verification, agents may also exploit simulation flaws, such as passing through objects or using unrealistic movements to obtain rewards without actually completing a task.

RoboUniverse-10K addresses these challenges by turning real-world robot manipulation videos into executable and verifiable simulation environments.

Turning Real-World Robot Data into 10,807 Interactive Environments

The project covers both single-arm and dual-arm manipulation tasks. Its single-arm environments draw primarily from DROID, alongside more than ten datasets including BridgeData V2, BC-Z, RT-1, and RoboCook. Dual-arm tasks incorporate data from RoboDesk, RoboCOIN, GM100, and RoboMIND2, among others.

To ensure that the dataset reflects genuine task diversity rather than superficial variations in object appearance or placement, the team established a five-level deduplication framework covering robot configuration, task family, individual task, scene layout, and manipulation trajectory.

The resulting dataset contains 10,807 distinct tasks, including 10,307 training tasks and 500 held-out evaluation tasks. Its scale is approximately 30 times that of RoboCasa365, with dual-arm tasks accounting for more than 60% of the collection.

At the core of the pipeline is an automated Real-to-Sim workflow powered by Coding Agents. Using tools such as SAM 3.1 for segmentation and DA3 for depth estimation, the system extracts task-relevant information from real-world videos. Coding Agents then construct simulation scenes, reference solutions, and task verifiers in Isaac Sim, iteratively refining the environments through comparisons between real footage and simulated execution.

An independent QA Agent reviews the source videos, simulation replays, and state logs to assess physical fidelity, reset reproducibility, and verifier robustness. Environments containing issues such as object penetration, teleportation, or false-positive success detection are rejected before entering the dataset.

Evaluating Agents Without Privileged Information

RoboUniverse-10K also introduces a strict execution-and-verification architecture compatible with Harbor, isolating the agent's execution environment from the simulation backend and task verifiers.

Agents can interact with the environment only through five public interfaces. They cannot access hidden object coordinates, reference solutions, or verification logic. Simulation time advances only when actions are executed, not while the agent is reasoning or analyzing visual observations. The environment provides no intermediate progress rewards and does not allow agents to reset tasks themselves. Each task permits only one attempt, with success determined independently by the verifier based on the final physical state.

These constraints are designed to ensure that agents complete tasks through visual perception and action control, rather than relying on privileged information or shortcuts unavailable in the real world.

Benchmark Results Reveal Key Gaps in Embodied Agent Capabilities

The team evaluated 11 leading closed-source and open-source models on RoboUniverse-Verified. GPT-6.2 Astra achieved the highest success rate at 85.8%, followed by Claude Opus 5.5 at 76.8%. GPT-6 Astra, GPT-6.1 Sol scored approximately 50%, while the remaining seven models each achieved no more than 10%. The strongest native open-source baseline reached 10.0%.

Further analysis identified three major capability gaps.

First, grasping remains a fundamental challenge. Models with overall success rates below 10% achieved an average success rate of 32.1% on non-grasping tasks, such as pushing and pressing, but only 2.7% on grasping tasks. By comparison, the four leading models achieved an average of 63.2% on grasping tasks.

Second, flexible-object manipulation remains difficult even for frontier models. While leading models improved by 35â38 percentage points on rigid-object tasks, their performance on cloth manipulation improved by only around three percentage points, with success rates remaining between 28% and 41%.

Third, precise control after contact is a major bottleneck. Approximately 80% of failures among leading models occurred after initial contact with an object. In one cup-lid assembly task, failed attempts showed an average offset of 6 mm and a tilt of 5.6Â°, exceeding the tolerances of 2 mm and 4Â°. In another task, some models applied excessive force to remote-control buttons, reaching 70â138 N.

These findings suggest that the next stage of embodied agent development will require more than improved visual recognition and task planning. Reliable grasping, contact-aware control, and the ability to respond to continuous physical feedback remain critical challenges.

Fine-Tuning Improves Task Success from 0.8% to 21.2%

To test whether RoboUniverse-10K can translate into measurable capability improvements, the team conducted a post-training experiment using Qwen3.6-35B-A3B as the base model.

The researchers freezing the visual encoder and alignment modules while fully fine-tuning the language backbone. The resulting model, RoboUniverse-35B-A3B, achieved a task success rate of 21.2% on RoboUniverse-Verified, up from 0.8% for the base model.

Performance on single-arm tasks increased from 2.0% to 40.5%, while success on pick-and-place tasks rose from 0% to 62.2%. The model also achieved an 8.3% success rate on dual-arm tasks, where the base model had previously recorded no successful completions.

The fine-tuned model also completed tasks in three additional simulation benchmarks: LIBERO-MAX, RoboCasa, and RoboTwin 2.0 where the base model had failed to complete the corresponding tasks.

In a real-world experiment, the model was deployed on a Franka robotic arm and successfully completed a whiteboard drawing task not included in its training data. Given a reference image, the robot drew the Einsia logo while adapting to an unknown whiteboard height through visual observation, trial movements, and continuous correction.

Building Scalable Infrastructure for Embodied AI

RoboUniverse-10K, including its environments, benchmark, and related fine-tuned model, is now open source. The release is intended to provide researchers with a reusable foundation for embodied agent training, evaluation, and further research.

While the current post-training results are based on supervised fine-tuning rather than reinforcement learning, the project demonstrates the potential value of scalable, physically grounded environments for improving embodied agent capabilities.

Just as large-scale internet text helped advance language models and real-world code repositories provided training grounds for coding agents, RoboUniverse-10K aims to establish scalable physical-world training infrastructure for embodied AI, helping the field move toward an era of environment scaling.

Through the development of authoritative model benchmarks and research into advanced AI training methods, Einsia AI aims to expand the boundaries of intelligence aligned with the real world.

Christie's announced today it has acquired the British auction house Dreweatts, a move that confirms the enduring appeal of antiques and older collectibles, particularly as profit margins in other parts of the auction sector remain squeezed.

Both businesses will run as before, Christie’s confirms, with no staffing changes expected as a result of the deal. Drewatts will retain its name and continue to be run “independently” by its chief executive, Henry House, and chairman Will Richards, said Christie’s president, Europe, Middle East and Africa Anthea Peers in a statement. Dreweatts will continue to operate from its salesroom at Donnington Priory in Newbury, Berkshire, with no sales expected to move to London.

Drewatts, founded in 1759, is best known for country house and estate sales, and its specialist departments covering furniture, Old Masters, ceramics, jewellery, silver, clocks and wine. It holds around 70 sales a year. In 2025, it posted a record total auction turnover of £37.56m (£47.3m with fees)—a 26% increase from its 2024 results. Both Christie’s and Dreweatts are privately owned and do not disclose their profits.

A display ahead of the sale of Patrick and Amabel Lindsay’s collection at Dreweatts

The acquisition effectively sees Christie’s backtrack its 2017 decision to close its South Kensington venue specialised in antiques, known as Christie's South Kensington or CSK. This decision was taken as the market for postwar and contemporary art was at its peak, and as the trade chased global expansion and billionaire tastes at the expense of localism and conoisseurship.

Today, those sales of Modern and postwar trophy works might generate records and headlines, but the overheads for winning these prize consignments devours profit margins. Indeed, many leading industry figures criticised Christie’s decision to close CSK as a shortsighted one that haemorrhaged its strongholds of specialist knowledge and staff.

Christie's president Bonnie Brennan says in a statement that Dreweatts possesses “particular strength in a part of the market that is highly complementary to our own. Most importantly, our clients collect across categories and at every price point, and together we have the opportunity to provide them with an even broader and more complete level of service.”

This marks the second major acquisition of a smaller auction house by Christie’s in recent years, following the 2024 buyout of the classic car vendors Gooding & Company.

Christie’s acquires Dreweatts from the advisory firm Gurr Johns, which bought the regional auction house in 2017 for £1.25m, as The Art Newspaper reported at the time. “I believe that Christie’s is the only business with the capacity, experience and shared culture to fulfil the further potential of Dreweatts,”, Gurr Johns chief told the Financial Times.
