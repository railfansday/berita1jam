UnitedHealth releases independent audit of its medical policy development practices" style="max-width:100%; height:auto; border-radius:8px;" loading="lazy">

Over the past year, UnitedHealth Group has conducted a series of independent reviews of its practices as part of a broader turnaround effort at the company.

The healthcare giant released the last of these analyses on Thursday, digging into the processes it uses to establish medical policies. The reviews were conducted by FTI Consulting and Rubrum Advising, a provider-led firm with expertise in medical coverage policies.

While the auditors dove into the processes the insurer uses to develop its medical policies, the report doesn't examine the application of those polices and any impact on coverage denials, an area where UnitedHealthcare has drawn significant public scrutiny.

The report concluded the company's methods "were strong overall and that the medical policy development process reflected numerous best practices."

For instance, UnitedHealthcare's Medical Policy team uses a structured and standardized process to establish medical policies, rooted in evidence-based and stakeholder-informed approaches. Clinical evidence comes from nationally recognized authorities, and epidemiologists play a key role in the review process, per the report.

In addition, the analysts concluded that the team is well-staffed with qualified workers and operates under a strong governance model. Medical policies are built "using systematic evidence review, evidence-based tools and industry best practices," and UnitedHealthcare is transparent about posting these policies and making updates.

All policies are reviewed at least once per year, the report found.

CEO Stephen Hemsley said in a letter to investors that the company will continue to conduct such analyses.

"We will continue to subject key practices across UnitedHealth Group to independent review, share results publicly and make changes where needed," Hemsley wrote. "This work reflects our broader commitment to transparency, accountability and continuous improvement."

While the report concluded that overall processes in place at UnitedHealth were up to snuff, the consultants did offer multiple areas of improvement for the company, too. For instance, the analysts said that the team could improve its documentation practices, including how it details criteria for policy development.

Plus, the report suggests that UnitedHealthcare review how it documents vetting criteria for internal and external subject matter experts. The company could also do more to formally document training and education for members of its governance committee.

Medical policy documents can be lengthy and complex, so the consultants recommended the insurer consider developing a template that standardizes the written format for each policy, which would make it easier to understand. For example, the template could make it easier to connect the evidence summary to the coverage rationale.

"While these gaps do not diminish the overall strength of the program, addressing them would further enhance transparency and consistency and provide opportunities for continued refinement," the analysts wrote.

Which states plan to opt into the federal school choice program?

States have until Jan. 1, 2027, to tell the IRS they will join the Education Freedom Tax Credit program.

More than half the states have indicated to the IRS that they will participate in the new federal Education Freedom Tax Credit program that begins Jan. 1 and is projected to direct billions of dollars in taxpayer donations to private and public school services through student scholarships.

Even though rules for the program are not yet finalized by the U.S. Department of the Treasury and the IRS, states and the scholarship-granting organizations that will manage the scholarships can rely on proposed and temporary guidance as they prepare for the program's launch.

Only states that opt into the Republican-led program can participate, and in addition to the 30 states that have formally told the IRS they are interested, at least two other states — New York and New Mexico — have publicly announced they will also take part in the first national school choice program.

The midterm elections saw five state governorships — Georgia, Iowa, Nevada, Ohio and Texas — flip from Republican to Democrat and some state legislative chambers also gain Democratic majorities. This blue wave could affect which states ultimately participate in the federal school choice program.

States must inform the IRS by Jan. 1 of their election into the program and finalize their decision to participate by Feb. 15 with their submission of approved scholarship-granting organizations. Those SGOs will manage and distribute student scholarships.

Rights on the Ballot Alongside US Congressional Elections

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Education  |  Read Source →

Click to expand Image A voter picks up an "I Voted" sticker after casting their ballot in the US midterm elections on November 3, 2026. © 2026 AP Photo/George Walker IV

Alongside the high-profile congressional and gubernatorial races decided during the United States midterm elections on November 3, human rights were at issue in several state and local ballot measures across the country. 

In Virginia, voters adopted a measure to automatically restore voting rights to people with felony convictions upon release from prison, ending a lifetime ban that had been in place since the Jim Crow era.

Arizona voters rejected and Wisconsin voters approved measures to end diversity, equity, and inclusion practices in education, employment, contracts, and administration, mirroring Trump administration initiatives to dismantle programs to address ongoing impacts of historical discrimination based on race and ethnicity. Vermont voters approved an amendment providing equal protection to individuals regardless of their race, ethnicity, sex, religion, disability, sexual orientation, gender identity, gender expression or national origin.

Meanwhile, Missouri voters rejected an effort to restrict the right to abortion care and measures to safeguard abortion rights were successful in Nevada and Virginia but failed in Idaho.

Ballot measures on transgender rights also showed mixed results. In Colorado, voters adopted a measure to ban gender-affirming surgeries for youth under 18. In Missouri, one of 27 US states to have a ban on gender-affirming care for youth, voters opted to keep the ban out of the state’s constitution.

Virginia voters approved an amendment formally lifting the state’s obsolete ban on same-sex marriage and affirming that two adults may marry regardless of sex, gender, and race.

Immigration-related measures were also on several ballots. Colorado voters rejected a measure that would have required state and local law enforcement to coordinate with federal immigration authorities in some circumstances. Voters in Honolulu, Hawaii were consulted on a measure requiring their police department to ensure the constitutional rights of all persons regardless of immigration or citizenship status, and work with federal agents only when required by statute or a court order, though the results are still to be decided. And voters in Chicago, Illinois, said they agreed that the city should seek financial compensation from the federal government for harm caused by immigration enforcement operations.

The US Congressional elections may be the biggest story of the week, but voters took up some important human rights issues directly in these initiatives and the impacts will be felt for years to come.

What Does an Attention Deficit Really Mean?

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Education  |  Read Source →

Posted November 6, 2026 | Reviewed by Tyler Woods

Most of us have experienced what it’s like to struggle to pay attention to something. I’ll offer a recent example of my own.

My mind wandered further and further from the focus of the long academic meeting I was attending. I looked around at the others in the room. They seemed to be listening—or at least staring at the screen.

We were in a plush venue just off campus, the sort we often hire for these long meetings. Rain pounded on the glass roof. Beyond it, the grounds were lush and green.

A young waiter, about the same age as my son, clinked some glasses, replenished the water, juice and coffee, and left a tray of small chocolate brownies and fresh fruit at the back of the room.

I remember how I wanted one of the brownies and some plump green grapes, still shining with moisture. But walking to the back and helping myself felt rude. Everyone else seemed to be managing to pay attention. Weren’t they?

It felt as though I had lost all ability to focus.

But my attention hadn’t disappeared. I could focus on the rain, the trees, the grapes and the brownies. I could attend to them effortlessly. But I couldn’t keep my mind on the sixth presentation of the day.

I tried to care about what was being said. I really wanted to. But I simply couldn’t.

Something inside me had stopped cooperating. I’d reached a point of attentional saturation. It was a little like realising you’re full and that each extra mouthful feels forced.

I longed to get up, walk outside, or breathe fresh air. I wanted to think about something other than the presentation. I felt as though I was dying of boredom.

I felt resentful that I was expected to keep listening, but also guilty that I couldn’t find the attention the speaker seemed to deserve. What was wrong with me? Why was I so inconsiderate?

Perhaps you can remember similar experiences. Encountering situations in which our capacity to ‘pay attention’ is stretched is so often part of modern life.

I wondered what it reflected about me. Was I bored? Had the subject failed to interest me or feel important enough? Was it about motivation? Was I tired? Had I simply reached the limit of what I could sustain that day? Or was I reacting to an expectation that I should or must keep listening?

The idea that something can be ‘wrong’ with our ‘attention’ is now woven into the cultures and institutions in which we live. But psychologists don’t really use the term attention to describe one clearly defined ability.

In a 2019 paper, psychologists Bernhard Hommel and colleagues describe the common idea of attention as the mechanisms that help us make efficient use of our limited capacity to process, store and retrieve information. That sounds straightforward.

But the word is used as a proxy for a wide range of abilities and experiences:

These are all capacities that might be (and often are) referred to when someone uses the word ‘attention.’ However, Hommel and colleagues argue that it is highly unlikely that the same set of functional or neural mechanisms would be involved in and responsible for each of these. They also caution that dividing attention into neat, separate functions can obscure what those functions have in common too.

They’re all examples of "attention," but they might not share the same underpinning anatomical or neurological roots. This matters more than you might think. Their argument has implications beyond the lab.

For example, if attention describes different things, what are we saying when we tell someone to “pay attention”? Furthermore, what are we referring to when we say that someone has an “attention deficit”?

What’s more, when someone struggles to stay with a task, the difficulty may indeed involve attention. But it may also involve motivation, intention or emotion. Perhaps the task feels pointless, the person doesn’t want to do it, or they are bored. The label 'attention' alone doesn’t tell us which of these is at play in a situation.

Psychological labels deserve care and scrutiny, no matter how deeply entrenched they might be. A label can help us recognise a pattern. But it can frequently give us the impression that we understand more than we do.

The authors of the paper above ultimately concluded that we’d be better to drop the term 'attention' as a label for a specific functional or neural system, because, they argue, it doesn’t actually identify a single functional or neural system. This is significant.

If attention were no longer treated as the label for a single mental or neural system, what would we make of things like Attention-Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD)? The name itself seems to point to a deficit in a particular capacity we call 'attention.' But if there is no single system called attention, then what, precisely, is deficient in something like ADHD?

This doesn’t necessarily make ADHD imaginary, or the difficulties people experience less real. But it does make the label seem less like an explanation than it first appears. Perhaps it names a recognisable pattern of difficulty, while leaving the processes, circumstances and histories that give rise to it in any one person wide open.

<a href="https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/providers/cms-proposes-rollbacks-excessively-burdensome-conditions-participation" hreflang="en">CMS proposes rollbacks of 'excessively burdensome' Conditions of Participation</a>

📅 2026-11-06  |  Category: Education  |  Read Source →
CMS proposes rollbacks of 'excessively burdensome' Conditions of Participation" style="max-width:100%; height:auto; border-radius:8px;" loading="lazy">

The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services plans to trim several “unnecessary” requirements for hospitals and other providers participating in Medicare, including emergency services readiness requirements and staff training for obstetrical services that had been brought during the tail end of the Biden administration.

The proposed rule’s Conditions of Participation (CoP) rollbacks, shared late Thursday (PDF), followed a “comprehensive review” CMS conducted to identify and eliminate “obsolete, outdated and excessively burdensome regulations.”

Removing these, the agency estimated, would save $320 million and free up 3.26 million hours of staff time for provider organizations.

Among the chief changes is a repeal of requirements finalized in the 2025 Outpatient Prospective Payment System (OPPS) and ASC Payment Systems rule that tasked hospital and critical access hospital emergency departments to be “continually prepared” to provide emergency screening or stabilization regardless of whether they provide relevant specialty services, which were made effective in July 2025.

The 2025 rule paired those with baseline staffing and care delivery standards, annual staff training and maternal quality assessment and performance improvement (QAPI) requirements around obstetric care, which are currently set to take effect on Jan. 1, 2027. CMS also proposed eliminating those requirements this week.

CMS under the prior administration had said these requirements would improve safety, quality and the timeliness of care delivery for pregnant women.

On the other hand, CMS' new proposed rule looks to add a new obstetrics requirement for hospitals and critical access hospitals tied to the collection of birth tissue, “including standards for informed consent and policies and procedures to support families requesting their birth tissues for cultural, religious or personal reasons,” per a fact sheet.

Other proposed changes to the CoPs include clarification revisions to policies regarding distance-related eligibility for critical access hospital designation and to implement an exemption from the distance eligibility requirements for some critical access hospitals.

Long-term care facilities would also see changes around COVID-19 vaccination education for staff and uptake for residents, as well as exemptions for existing facilities from newer room occupancy and bathroom facility standards. Among other changes are removals or slim downs of respiratory illness data reporting requirements for multiple types of provider facilities.

The proposed rule will be open for public comment for 60 days. Among these, CMS said it is interested in hearing from stakeholders on additional requirements for participating providers, including flexibilities for physician supervision of certified registered nurse anesthetists, expanding the scope of practice of nurse practitioners and physician assistants in long-term care facilities, and reducing barriers for new transplant programs to achieve initial certification.
