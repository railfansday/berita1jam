FA considered legal challenge to stop Gianni Infantino standing in Fifa election

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

The Football Association considered a legal challenge to Gianni Infantino's eligibility to stand for a fourth term as Fifa president but decided it would not succeed, its chair Debbie Hewitt has said.

Hewitt, a Fifa vice-president and Fifa Council member, was not on the Council in 2022 when it took a decision to discount Infantino's first term from 2016 to 2019 from the three-term limit set out in Fifa's statutes, effectively clearing him to stand again next year.

The FA was asked by human rights group FairSquare's Reboot campaign to challenge Infantino's eligibility, but as first reported by the BBC, Hewitt has responded to say the FA had already considered it and decided against it.

"The FA has explored the possibility of challenging Mr Infantino's eligibility to run for a further term as Fifa president but does not consider that such a challenge would secure the outcome sought," Hewitt wrote in a letter seen by the Press Association.

"Ultimately the process followed Fifa's governance and led to an outcome where Mr Infantino's part term was disregarded for the purposes of maximum term limits. Having considered the process in detail, we do not believe that a challenge would succeed."

The FA declined to comment on the letter, but it is understood the exploration of a legal challenge to the term limits took place when other efforts to extract information from Fifa about the controversial World Cup private investor plan and the Folarin Balogun disciplinary case had been exhausted.

The FA was the only national association which objected to changes in Uefa's statutes in 2024 which have now allowed Aleksander Ceferin to stand for a fourth term as its president next year.

Infantino has stated he intends to stand for a fourth term at the Fifa Congress in Morocco in March and appears well placed to win, with the deadline for candidates to be submitted having now passed.

Gianni Infantino (right) next to FA CEO Mark Bullingham (PA)

Uefa said in August it had lost confidence in Infantino over the private investor plan and would work to identify a credible candidate to stand against him.

On Monday it was announced French Football Federation president Philippe Diallo had the backing of national associations on the Uefa executive committee and European members of the Fifa Council, including Hewitt.

She intends to travel to Zurich to scrutinise documents related to the World Cup private investor plan, following an offer to do so made by Fifa's secretary general Mattias Grafstrom on 2 November.

However, that trip is understood to be dependent on the conditions Fifa seek to impose and the terms of any non-disclosure agreements it insists must be signed.

Fifa confirmed on Thursday morning that three candidates had been proposed by the national FAs – Infantino, Diallo and Kohzo Tashima, the honorary president of the Japanese Football Association.

Fifa said the candidates' names would now be submitted to its electoral committee for eligibility checks, and that national associations would be notified no later than 18 February on the final list of admitted candidates.

Tzolis on Arsenal’s title quest: It’s a marathon

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Syndicated reporting provided by Stats Perform and reviewed by the SoccerNews editorial team.

Christos Tzolis knows that Arsenal's quest for glory on multiple fronts this season is a marathon, and not a sprint.

Arsenal went into the international break five points clear at the top of the Premier League.

They have suffered just one defeat in the top flight all season, and after a shock loss to Fleetwood Town in the EFL Cup on October 27, the Gunners bounced back by beating Liverpool, Slavia Prague and Hull City.

Tzolis, who joined Arsenal from Club Brugge in the summer, insists Mikel Arteta's team are taking it slow and steady.

"It's like a marathon," he told Arsenal's official website.

"It's not because you win one game that everything is OK, it's a very tricky league. Every team is very good and the quality is very high so you have to be focused and alert for every game.

Next up for the Gunners is a trip to St James' Park to face Newcastle United.

Arteta's next win will be his 159th as a manager in the Premier League. It will take him above Mark Hughes (158) into outright 10th for wins by managers in the competition's history.

"It's having the mentality, even before the game, to go in with the mindset that you're going to win, even if the performance is not that good," Tzolis said when asked what it takes to be a winner.

"It's about feeling that you can always find a solution, always to take the three points, because that's what matters.

"If you go with the right plan and the quality we have, that makes it even easier to win the game. But even when you struggle, it's about having the winning mentality in your mind and having everybody – even the coaching staff – on the same page, to do everything to get the win."

It feels like just yesterday that we were coming out of drafts and Week 1 was imminent.

You're doing everything you can to get the players you want while trying to shore up any weak spots on your roster. Once the trade deadline passes in your league, the best avenue for making those changes is the waiver wire, where the pickings are much slimmer. Either way, now is probably the best time to conduct a sweeping assessment of your roster and do what you have to do. That's where we come in: to help you make heads or tails of some of the options here. We'll do that for all 26 teams playing this week.

Implied Point Total: Texans 28.5, Colts 16

After trading away both Josh Downs and Keenan Allen at the trade deadline last week, it makes at least some basic sense that the Colts would lean more heavily into their running game with their best offensive player, Jonathan Taylor. What nobody saw coming was how heavily Seth McGowan would factor into the equation as well.

The run game vastly overshadowed the passing attack, as Daniel Jones and the Colts had only 22 pass attempts compared to 29 called runs. Of those runs, 28 went to Taylor (16) and McGowan (12). Both averaged well over five yards per carry, with Taylor notching 83 yards and McGowan rushing for 77 yards while hitting paydirt with a touchdown in the second quarter. With the Colts trotting out what amounts to a Week 18 or even preseason lineup consisting of Laquon Treadwell, Deion Burks and Anthony Gould as their most-frequently used wide receivers, leaning much more heavily into the run will take some pressure off Jones and the receivers to make plays.

Notably, Taylor ran routes on 80% of dropbacks, in stark contrast to McGowan's 8%. This feels like an extreme version of the early-season Rams split between Kyren Williams and Blake Corum. Against a team like the Dolphins, a split like this can be successful. Against the Texans on the road in Week 11? That's another story.

Fantasy Advice: Having to split carries brings Taylor down to high-end RB2 status, while McGowan is certainly flex-worthy if this volume holds.

Well… Warren caught two passes for one yard. Not good. Warren actually had three times as many rushing yards (three) on his lone carry as he had receiving yards. While this is only one week of data, Warren's fantasy production has primarily come through receptions, with a low aDOT (4.6 yards this season), rather than downfield opportunities or touchdowns. He has two touchdowns this season, with neither coming since Week 2. While Warren should have some solid games for the rest of the season, his stock in this barebones offense has to dip. It's fair to say that Warren has fallen outside the top 12 at the position.

Fantasy Advice: The Texans have been one of the better teams against tight ends in fantasy this season, allowing the sixth-fewest fantasy points to the position. Given the issues the Colts have had on offense, Warren is difficult to count on as a locked-in starter right now. Recently, though, the truth has become unavoidable: Tom Brady is pretty good at his job. Gone are the days of the best quarterback of all time being hopelessly outmatched on TV. What has changed is that Brady now sounds like an actual human being.

Brady's early struggles in his current job, which he started in 2024, are the stuff of broadcast legend – a way to remind people of how humbling a new gig can be even for a talented, beautiful superstar. At the beginning of his run, Brady had a penchant for waiting too long to deliver points that were fine but not especially insightful for a man whose brain is a football supercomputer. The pregnant pause followed by an observation like "they are bringing all six, and they can only block five of them" was typical.

When Christian McCaffrey scored on a 66-yard catch and run in the fourth quarter, Brady quickly telestrated the play and showed how Dallas's pressure package left McCaffrey in a perfect position to run an angle route against Von Miller, who is known for rushing the QB rather than for his ability to cover a player as elusive as McCaffrey.

"He's one-on-one with Von Miller because they're pressuring on the inside," Brady said. "That's not the assignment Von Miller's looking for." Indeed it was not, and the understatement was perfect. The success isn't entirely surprising. A man who was pathologically dedicated to becoming the most effective quarterback of all time was never going to enjoy being seen as a failure as a broadcaster. Uncensored AI models are thriving on code hosting platforms

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Uncensored large language models (ULLMs) capable of producing harmful content are readily available at popular model-hosting websites, calling into question AI industry commitments to safety.

Researchers affiliated with University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign in the US and Nanyang Technological University in Singapore have analysed 22,096 ULLMs collected from five major LLM hosting platforms.

As described in their paper, "Characterizing the Ecosystem of Open-Source Uncensored Large Language Models: Supply Chain, Governance, and Risks," authors Zilong Lin, Zichuan Li, Xiaojing Liao, and XiaoFeng Wang found that uncensored LLMs are easy to obtain from hosting platforms like US-based Hugging Face, Kaggle, and John Snow Labs, and China-based OpenCSG and ModelScope.

What's more, these unrestrained models have been deployed through applications, GitHub projects, and model routing platforms like Open Router, and users of those apps or services may not be aware of the nature of the underlying LLM.

"Uncensored LLMs generally refer to LLMs whose content filtering or refusal safeguards are absent, removed, or weakened, allowing them to respond to requests that aligned models may refuse," the paper explains.

Zilong Lin, lead author and assistant professor of computer science at the University of Missouri-Kansas City, told The Register that open source LLM hosting platforms like Hugging Face play a similar role to code hosting services like GitHub, GitLab, and Codeberg in the open source community by enabling sharing, modification, and redistribution of code artifacts.

"However, unlike traditional software, whose behavior can often be examined through source code analysis, the safety alignment of LLMs is difficult to assess directly from their model files," Lin explained. "Evaluating whether an LLM has adequate safety safeguards typically requires deploying the model and testing its responses to potentially harmful prompts, which involves substantial computational and financial costs."

Lin said this makes the identification and assessment of LLM safety particularly challenging, particularly given the large number of models available. That's why he and his colleagues chose to study the space through publicly available model metadata.

Even popular commercial LLMs that are purportedly "aligned" – to some standard not everyone accepts – may respond to prompts with content deemed harmful. The US Government Accountability Office just released a report that found Claude, Grok, ChatGPT, and Gemini can be coaxed to provide information that would help with the planning of a terror attack.

To highlight the consequences of AI advice, the GAO points to news reports indicating that generative AI helped enable the creation of an explosive device in a January 2025 attack on a Las Vegas hotel and the planning of a February 2026 mass shooting in British Columbia, Canada.

The issue with open weight LLMs is that anyone with modest technical skills (or just cluelessly assisted by a compliant LLM) can apply techniques like toxic training/fine-tuning (TTFT) or de-aligned training/fine-tuning (DTFT) to datasets that haven't been safe, in order to produce models with reduced or removed refusal behavior. Examples include toxicqa-Llama2-13B and WizardLM-7B-Uncensored. A more recent technique called refusal-direction ablation (or abliteration, e.g., Daredevil-8B-abliterated) dilutes refusal training to make the model less likely to refuse requests.

Uncensored (or simply less restrictive) models have legitimate uses, sometimes ironically because commercial models are insufficiently permissive and refuse legitimate security analysis. Hugging Face, for example, had to resort to using Z.ai's less rule-encumbered GLM-5.2 to analyze the hacking of its infrastructure by OpenAI's irresponsibly unsupervised AI agents.

But ULLMs can just as easily be misused to provide guidance about illegal activity, assist with malware development, or emit content that's harmful, malicious, or manipulative in some way.

"We identified more than 11,000 ULLMs appearing across multiple platforms, with Hugging Face serving as the dominant upstream source," Lin said in a blog post.

"This movement exposes an important governance gap: models can travel more easily than the information needed to assess their risks. Only about 13 percent of the ULLMs we identified carried moderation warnings, and security threat indicators were not consistently preserved across platforms.

"This creates a supply chain problem: a model can be copied across platforms while the information needed to understand its risks is lost along the way."

The ULLMs were evaluated for text output and not images. The issues raised by text-to-image LLMs, such as the ability to generate illegal images (e.g. CSAM), were beyond the scope of this particular study.

"The uncensored LLMs we evaluated were generally more likely to comply with harmful requests and generate harmful content than non-uncensored models," Lin said. "Our evaluation covered 11 categories of harmful requests, including child abuse, illegal activities, and fraud.

"However, the ability of an individual model to generate harmful content varies depending on several factors, including its training data, development methods, and the extent to which its refusal direction has been ablated."

A small portion of these ULLMs (27 of them, which have been downloaded 136,000 times) exhibited threat indicators from security scans. Many of these, authors say, had signatures associated with malware, exhibited suspicious binary patterns, or contained unsafe deserialization mechanisms that could allow arbitrary code execution.

The number of ULLMs has been rising rapidly, according to the paper, from about 100 per month in May 2023 to more than 1,000 per month presently. And some of these models have been downloaded millions of times. The uncensored model dolphin-2.9.1-yi-1.5-34b has more than 53 million downloads, and more than 4.8 million just last month.

The authors note that many ULLMs violate the licensing terms of the models on which they've been based, not that source code licensing has had any meaningful impact on the behavior of the AI industry.

Asked whether the safety concerns arising from the availability of ULLMs might prompt a replay of the 1990s when authorities tried to limit the availability of cryptographic code, Lin said, "It is difficult to predict whether model abliteration tools will face similar legal challenges.

"As shown in Section 5.1 of our paper, refusal-direction ablation (i.e., abliteration) appeared in 2024 and quickly gained popularity within the open-source LLM community. Within approximately two years, a growing ecosystem of publicly available tools had emerged to support this technique.

"In the meantime, techniques for both removing and mitigating LLM safety alignment have also become active research topics in academia, with many researchers releasing open-source tools and artifacts to support their studies."

Dealing with the risks posed by uncensored models isn't easy because it depends on divining the intent of the user's prompt. When is a question about terrorism intended as a plan and when is it guidance for writing a thriller? When is a question about hacking intended for theft and when is it cybersecurity education?

The current approach taken by commercial labs — limiting powerful models to favored paying insiders and offering hobbled models to the rest — doesn't work for those left out. Applying some similarly censorious regime to open weight models isn't likely to achieve any more success than efforts by law enforcement to make cryptography available only to "the good guys."

Lin expects that greater transparency, auditability, and verification of models will help.

"As discussed in Section 8 of our paper, our approach to identifying uncensored LLMs relies on publicly available model metadata, such as model cards, to identify models that explicitly disclose their uncensored characteristics," he said. "Our findings demonstrate the value of such transparency for identifying potentially unsafe models.

"Regulatory frameworks have also increasingly emphasized AI documentation and transparency.
