Middlesbrough signed Rokas Pukstas from Hajduk Split for £4.3m in the summer transfer window. Boro saw off interest from Birmingham City to land the attacking midfielder, but he has not had the impact hoped with only two senior appearances before injury hit.

The slow start is an obvious source of frustration, but one man backing Pukstas to come good is fellow summer signing and compatriot Sebastian Berhalter. As quoted by The Northern Echo, he expressed that Pukstas needs time to adjust and develop at only 22 before making a promise to the Riverside Stadium faithful.

“Once he gets back from his injury, he’s only going to come back better,” Berhalter said. “He works so hard; he’s one of the hardest-working guys I’ve ever met and he’s going to be just fine, I promise you that.”

Pukstas’ two substitute appearances have brought just 12 minutes on the pitch for Middlesbrough. He was unused on the bench in four Championship games before his hamstring injury hit, so supporters haven’t really had a chance to see what he’ll bring to Kim Hellberg‘s side.

The 22-year-old mainly operates in attacking midfield, offering a box-crashing threat as he powers into the box with well-timed runs to have efforts on goal. He has played in a deeper role before too though, as his physical traits translate to duel success and ability on the ball means he can be trusted in possession.

His well-rounded skillset and physical profile mean he is ideal for Hellberg’s flexible tactics. Expect to see him covering plenty of ground and popping up in both thirds, much like his supportive compatriot Berhalter.

His age means there is plenty of time for Pukstas to grow too. He needs time to get back up to fitness and settle into the Boro side, but Berhalter’s faith is well placed.

Understandably. Pukstas’ limited impact has brought some pessimism from sections of the Middlesbrough support. The fact he barely played before picking up an injury means they are yet to see why £4.3m was spent on him.

However, the success of Boro’s recruitment means that they have good reason to have faith he will come good on his price. Berhalter’s message should only increase the confidence that the American ace can deliver, but it also serves as a timely reminder that he may need time to show his best as he develops as well.

At present, the expectation is that Pukstas will not be able to return from his hamstring injury until December. Hellberg will likely offer a fresh update ahead of Boro’s clash with Bristol City this Saturday.

Circle Internet Group vs. Strategy: Crypto's Brutal Sell-Off Is Exposing a Critical Difference Between These 2 Stocks

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The cryptocurrency downturn is putting two fundamentally different business models to the test, raising questions about which offers investors a more sustainable path to long-term returns.

The ongoing cryptocurrency bear market is exposing fundamental differences between businesses operating in the digital asset economy. Circle Internet Group (CRCL -5.24%) and Strategy (MSTR -6.34%) offer investors two distinct ways to gain exposure to the industry, but their financial performance depends on very different factors.

Circle is building the financial infrastructure behind digital payments through its USDC (USDC -0.01%) stablecoin, generating revenue primarily from interest earned on its reserves. Strategy, meanwhile, has transformed itself from an enterprise software company into a Bitcoin (BTC +0.06%) treasury business, leveraging capital markets to accumulate increasingly large holdings of the cryptocurrency.

While both companies stand to benefit from the long-term adoption of digital assets, their contrasting business models, capital structures, and exposure to cryptocurrency volatility could have significant implications for shareholder returns heading into 2027.

With services spanning 185 countries, Circle is positioned to benefit from the growing integration of blockchain technology into traditional finance. Unlike many cryptocurrency businesses, Circle does not depend directly on rising digital asset prices to drive revenue. Instead, it earns the majority of its revenue from interest generated on USDC reserves.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached nearly $3 billion, representing year-over-year growth of approximately 25.7%. Net income totaled $462.9 million, translating to a profit margin of roughly 15.4%. The expanding use of stablecoins for payments and financial settlement provides Circle with a structural growth opportunity that extends beyond speculative cryptocurrency trading.

As of December 2025, Circle maintained a debt-to-equity ratio of 0.0 and a current ratio of 1.0. The company generated approximately $529.7 million in free cash flow during fiscal 2025. However, stock-based compensation represented 104.4% of operating cash flow, warranting closer scrutiny of the quality of its cash generation.

Although Strategy continues to operate its enterprise analytics software business, its investment thesis increasingly centers on accumulating Bitcoin. The company utilizes a combination of common stock, preferred securities, and debt financing to expand its Bitcoin holdings, providing shareholders with exposure to the cryptocurrency through a traditional equity structure.

Over the latest 12-month reporting period, revenue reached approximately $495.1 million, representing year-over-year growth of 4.2%. However, Strategy reported a net loss of approximately $13.7 billion, primarily reflecting declines in the fair value of its Bitcoin holdings. The disparity between revenue and net income illustrates how much Bitcoin's price movements now overshadow the financial performance of its software business.

As of December 2025, Strategy maintained a debt-to-equity ratio of 0.2 and a current ratio of 5.6. Despite these seemingly favorable balance sheet metrics, the company recorded negative free cash flow of $112.5 million in fiscal 2025. With limited internal cash generation, Strategy remains heavily dependent on external financing to support its Bitcoin accumulation strategy.

One of Circle's primary risks is intensifying competition. Efforts to formalize stablecoin regulation, most notably through the GENIUS Act, lend legitimacy to the industry but also open the door for banks, payment processors, and fintech companies to issue competing stablecoins, potentially eroding Circle's market share and profitability. Circle is also sensitive to interest rates, as falling short-term Treasury yields could reduce reserve income unless offset by growth in USDC circulation.

Strategy faces a different set of risks. The company frequently issues new shares to fund Bitcoin purchases, which can dilute the ownership of existing shareholders. While selling new shares has historically worked in Strategy's favor during bull markets, a prolonged bear market makes this approach less effective. As Bitcoin's price declines, Strategy risks having to issue additional shares to fund further purchases, potentially diluting existing shareholders without delivering a corresponding increase in per-share value.

While Circle appears more reasonably valued based on its P/S ratio, Strategy currently offers a lower multiple relative to its future earnings estimates.

Valuation metrics include sourcing from Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) and may differ from other data providers.

Investors should note that the Forward P/E compares the current stock price to future earnings estimates. The P/S ratio measures the company market cap against sales over the past twelve months.

Despite Circle's relatively expensive forward price-to-earnings ratio of 53.2 compared to Strategy's 6.5, I believe Circle offers the more compelling investment opportunity heading into 2027. Strategy's seemingly attractive earnings multiple warrants caution, as its profitability is heavily influenced by fluctuations in Bitcoin's price rather than the underlying performance of its software business. Circle also trades at just 5.4 times sales, compared to Strategy's 93.1, although traditional valuation metrics are less useful when evaluating a Bitcoin treasury company.

Ultimately, the deciding factor for me is the sustainability of each company's business model. Stablecoins represent one of the most practical applications of blockchain technology, with opportunities extending across cross-border payments, commercial transactions, and institutional financial settlement. Circle can capitalize on this growing adoption regardless of whether Bitcoin enters another bull market.

Strategy could deliver substantial returns if Bitcoin rebounds in 2027. However, its dependence on capital markets and potential shareholder dilution introduce additional risks that investors would not encounter by simply owning Bitcoin. While Circle faces competitive pressures and a demanding earnings valuation of its own, I prefer its ability to generate revenue from the broader adoption of digital assets rather than relying on their price appreciation.

It's been a hectic international break for Blackburn, who went into it sitting 13th in the Championship with 21 points from 15 games. They've punched above their weight all year and have exceeded plenty of pre-season expectations so far.

Toth has helped Rovers stay in games, and at Bristol City especially, win them, making seven saves at Ashton Gate and preventing 2.56 goals in a classic 1-0 away win. His contract is up at the end of the season, so his long-term future is up in the air, but in the meantime, he's an important player at Ewood Park.

So, the possibility of losing him for their biggest game of the campaign on Sunday at Turf Moor against a hapless Burnley side would be a massive blow.

Toth's performances in a Blackburn shirt have led to him nailing down the number one shirt for Hungary. He played every minute in their Nations League campaign, including Tuesday night's draw against Northern Ireland.

That point was enough to see ex-Rovers boss Michael O'Neill lead his side, filled with EFL talents such as Justin Devenny, who scored the equaliser against Hungary, Pierce Charles, Terry Devlin, Isaac Pirce and others, to top Group 2 of League B in the Nations League.

Toth and his Hungary side can still earn promotion to League A of the Nations League, with their second-placed finish in their group securing a play-off spot. They'll battle Slovenia, Bosnia and Herzegovina and the Republic of Ireland in March.

From intricate braids and gravity-defying knots to elaborate, architectural chignons, the red carpet has seen plenty of sculptural hair looks as of late. Leave it to Hunter Schafer to offer a decidedly cool-girl interpretation of the trend.

Attending the London premiere of Blade Runner 2099 on Tuesday evening, Schafer—styled as always by Dara—wore a custom ivory Celine ensemble comprising a strapless satin bustier dress, with a dramatic train trailing behind her, layered over tailored tuxedo trousers for that nonchalant elegance. Her beauty followed suit. Working with her go-to hairstylist Rena Calhoun, Schafer wore her honey-blonde lengths in softly tousled, loose waves, parted loosely at the center and falling around her shoulders. From the front it was a classic and romantic, and a rather effortless-looking hairstyle. It’s another clever beauty moment in what’s proving to be an excellent press tour for Schafer. What’s so great about Hunter Schafer’s latest look is its restraint. While everyone else is twisting, sculpting, and shellacking their hair into elaborate works of art, Schafer makes a case for something more low-key. Sometimes the most interesting beauty detail is the one you almost miss.

Democrats win Alaska Senate race two weeks after midterms counting began

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Mary Peltola defeated the state's Republican incumbent after a controversial race

Democrat Mary Peltola has won Alaska's hard-fought Senate race, completing a sweeping victory for the party in the US upper house in the midterm elections.

The win gives the Democrats a 56th Senate seat following a tumultuous and controversial election in Alaska, which featured alleged spoiler recruitment efforts and unprecedented court battles.

It took two full weeks of counting to call a winner in the 3 November poll due to the logistical challenge of collecting ballots from across the state's remote expanse.

It is the ninth Senate seat the Democrats have taken from Republicans in the midterms, giving the party control of the US Congress for the last two years of Donald Trump's presidency.

The Democrats also won back the House of Representatives, the lower chamber.

Peltola defeated the incumbent Republican senator, Dan S Sullivan, who is yet to comment.

In her victory statement, Peltola said the "status quo in DC is not working for Alaska".

She recalled speaking to "elders in tears about empty fish racks" and to parents in rural Alaska choosing between heating their homes and putting food on the table.

Peltola is the first Alaska Native elected to the US Senate, and is the first Democrat to win a US Senate seat in Alaska since 2008.

Donald Trump won the state by 13 points in the 2024 presidential election, but defeat for the Republicans in this year's US Senate race in the state caps off a string of losses in the midterms for the president's party.

Many voters across the United States cited cost-of-living issues as a factor that affected how they cast their ballots.

Peltola's campaign had come under fire after the New York Times, external reported that her team worked with people who tried to recruit so-called spoiler candidates in an alleged attempt to confuse voters.

The alleged scheme led to a candidate named Daniel J Sullivan - a complete political unknown residing in a small coastal town who shared a name with the state's current US Senator and incumbent, Dan S Sullivan - contesting the race.

This smell startup says its AI nose is as good as a human’s

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Osmo’s smell model is already being used in the fragrance industry. But are medical diagnostics and threat detection a sniff too far?

In a lab near Newark Airport in New Jersey, a team of around 30 experienced human sniffers are slowly working their way through every odor on Earth. Their careful notes—“nutty,” “peachy,” “marine”—are cross-referenced with a chemical analysis to create a rich set of training data for what AI startup Osmo calls the world’s first foundation model for smell. The company’s goal, says founder Alex Wiltschko, is “to give computers a sense of smell.”

“Scent molecules are really the only language most species on this planet can speak,” he says. “Most species can’t communicate with sound or visually, so it truly is Earth’s original language. And it’s incredibly powerful for us, from a memory and an emotional standpoint. You can smell something once and remember it for the rest of your life. You can smell something on the street and be pulled back to high school.”

In the last few months, Wiltschko claims, Osmo’s model has achieved “human-level olfactory intelligence.” The company’s definition of this is that the model is about as good as the average person at detecting and discriminating between smells, and about as good as a junior perfume designer at creating them. It says its technology is already being used in the fragrance industry to help brands design and manufacture new scents. Last month, the company says, Osmo’s AI model outperformed in-house perfumers at one of the world’s largest consumer goods companies. Wiltschko believes it could be deployed eventually for threat detection at airports, for example, or diagnosing diseases.

Wiltschko spun the startup out of his work at Google Brain, the search giant’s former AI research lab, in 2023, and he is running the same playbook as AI startups in other domains: Throw a lot of data at difficult problems, and see what happens. The challenge with scent, Wiltschko says, is that there was a dearth of good data. Before Osmo, there were fewer than 200,000 human descriptions of smells in existing databases, by his count. Plus, the chemical sensor devices needed to collect data are expensive, and the fragrance industry is secretive about its work on olfactory science.

A lot of the company’s work has been in creating its own dataset of more than 10 million human labels and 800 million “scent tokens,” which describe the exact molecules and concentrations that go into creating specific odors. Osmo uses spectrometers and other chemical sensors, running around the clock, to break down the molecular makeup of everything from fruit and flowers to pantry  staples and organs. A team of around 70 trained data labelers in the US and overseas spend their days sniffing things and writing down what they smell like, using a standardized fragrance vocabulary.

Mapping out scents in this way is throwing out some unlikely parallels. In the same way that changing the order of words can drastically influence the meaning of a sentence, rearranging scent molecules in different proportions can drastically alter the smells humans perceive. “The scent tokens that define the smell of a newborn baby have massive overlap with the scent tokens in a fresh plate of cooked spaghetti,” Wiltschko says. (Specifically Barilla brand spaghetti, he says, which perhaps raises some questions for the people at the pasta factory.)

The company also creates its own scents, using a smell-printing robot the size of a school bus that mixes ingredients according to precise formulas to make samples for Osmo’s own research and fragrances for paying customers. It can design and print scents to order based on a ChatGPT-style prompt. Wiltschko flipped open his laptop to show me how the AI model worked. He opened a text box on screen, and I asked him to generate a fragrance that smelled like a Portuguese custard tart (we were in Lisbon for Web Summit), aimed at 18- to 23-year-old men. The system spent a few minutes cogitating, then generated three options for potential scent profiles that matched the brief (creamy, coconutty, fruity) and the molecules needed to create them. If I’d continued with the ordering process, my signature L’Eau de Custard would have been ready about a week later.

Osmo’s technology has already been used to create retail fragrances, including for giants like Procter & Gamble. The Gates Foundation, which invested $5 million when Osmo launched, later gave the company a $3.5 million grant to assess molecules that repel disease-carrying insects, and Osmo identified eight substances that were more repellent than DEET. Osmo has also worked with StockX, the online clothing marketplace, on using smell to spot counterfeit sneakers by detecting differences in the chemical composition of the materials used in the shoes.

But not everyone is convinced by the technology or the company’s big claims. Avery Gilbert, a smell scientist and author of What the Nose Knows, has been sharply critical of Osmo on his Substack, arguing that some of its marketing claims are undocumented, and that its patent filings still rely on human sensory panels and perfumers, rather than AI. “This heavy reliance on human sensory judgments seems odd for a company premised on super-cool AI,” he wrote in March. A representative for Osmo did not respond in time for publication.

Michael Schmuker, a former professor of neural computation at the University of Hertfordshire who has previously consulted for Osmo, says the company’s technology is an achievement. With colleagues, he created an open-source, reverse-engineered version of Osmo’s 2023 model, but it lacks the reams of data the company has collected since.

But even with all this smell data, Osmo may be missing some of the factors that make human olfaction unique, Schmuker says. “Smell is super complex. Context is very important for smell. The same molecule may evoke a different association depending on context,” he says. “[The smell of] a French cheese like Camembert is partly the same molecule as in used nappies. You don’t confuse the two because of the context.”

In a controlled setting like a fragrance lab, this lack of context may not matter so much. But it could have an impact if Osmo’s technology becomes, as Wiltschko suggests, the “olfactive channel” for AI world models. Imagine a machine that could sniff out spoilage in food on a production line, or detect tumors through smell the way dogs have been trained to do in pilot studies.

For that to happen, sensor technology will need to get a lot better, Schmuker says. The models will need to keep improving. Medical diagnostics is the biggest prize.

“There’s a lot of history of companies that have gone directly at that mountain peak and, unfortunately, not been successful, because it is very expensive and it is very difficult,” Wiltschko says. "So what we’re doing is building this foundation at the foothills of all these mountains. We’re sending expeditions up to the mountains, doing explorations, and basically smelling the whole world.”

“Good TV is very connected to good sex; it shows that you are free, and this freedom is very important to us,” said Czech producer Karolina Zalabakova at Tuesday night’s TV Beats Awards ceremony in Tallinn, Estonia, where her project “Intimacy on Prescription” scooped the Council of Europe’s coveted €50,000 ($56,000) Series Co-Production Development Award.

In her acceptance speech last night, the emotional Zalabakova hinted both at the topic of her show – sex therapy in 1970s Czechoslovakia – and at her government’s current attempt to crush media freedom through an unpopular reform of its public service media. In a totally different genre, the Irish-Canadian spy thriller “Tanner” created by Keith Hodder, brought home both the Göteborg Film Festival’’s TV Drama Vision Select Award and the Conecta 2027 Award.

Produced by Bernie Hoban and Darach Ó Tuairisg of Fíbín Films (“Boglands”) in co-production with Canadian powerhouse Rhombus Media (“The Sympathizer”), the show turns on a former Irish intelligence officer who returns home to investigate a missing teenager and uncovers a tech-powered conspiracy.

A fourth industry award – the Midpoint Talent Award – was handed out to “The Inheritance”, produced by Belgium’s Panenka (“This is Not a Murder Mystery”). The psychological thriller, set against the world of animal welfare and hunting, is penned by Alexandra Jans and Jan Van Dyck.

At the closing ceremony of the 6th TV Beats series market, part of the Industry @ Tallinn & Baltic Event, co-heads Petri Kemppinen and Roosa Toivonen also handed out to ‘the ‘Queen of Nordic drama’ Piv Bernth (“The Killing,” “Blackwater”), the TV Beats Honorary Award, which she shared with the creative trio of the German hit “Babylon Berlin”: Tom Tykwer, Achim von Borries and Henk Handloegten.

Thanking DR “for teaching me everything I know,” “The Killing” creator Søren Sveistrup and actress Sofie Gråbøl among others, she confirmed that she will be closing her current outfit- – ITV Studios’-backed Apple Tree Production – in the New Year and deliver to the Danish pubcaster a last show [“The Best of Families”]. “That closes a cycle and it’s a really good point to start [a new one],” she said.

Commenting on this year’s slate of 10 TV projects selected from 50 submissions across Europe, Begg said “the quality of the pitches was the testament of the quality of the event attended by a mix of top tier, emerging and seasoned producers.”“I’ve been at TV Beats since its inception, and this edition was by far the strongest,” said Cia Edström of the Göteborg Film festival’s TV Drama Vision.

Nearly 30 series at different stages of production were showcased during the 6th edition of TV Beats which reflected not only the giant leap forward quality-wise of drama making in the Baltics, but also the ability of the event to attract top-of-their-class producers, distributors, creators and programmers from around the world.
