2. Bundesliga preview: 1. FC Heidenheim vs. Hannover 96

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Travel  |  Read Source →

The 2. Bundesliga provides an intriguing encounter on the Ostalb, as Heidenheim get set to host Hannover.

Both sides have experienced contrasting fortunes in recent weeks, with Heidenheim struggling to rediscover their early-season form, while Hannover will be looking to build on their first league victory under new head coach Sandro Wagner.

With just four points separating the teams ahead of the 11th matchday, Sunday's meeting presents an important opportunity for both clubs to strengthen their position in the standings.

Heidenheim will be determined to get their promotion challenge back on track after suffering a 2-1 defeat away to Karlsruher SC last weekend.

Frank Schmidt's side appeared to be heading towards a point after Alessandro Vogt equalised late in the contest, only for the hosts to snatch all three points with a stoppage-time winner.

The result extended Heidenheim's winless run in the league to five matches, with their last victory coming against Holstein Kiel back in September.

Despite their recent difficulties, the recently-relegated side remain sixth in the standings with 15 points from their opening ten fixtures. Their home form has also been a positive, with Schmidt's side unbeaten at the Voith-Arena this season, recording three victories and two draws from their five matches.

Sunday's encounter also carries additional significance following Schmidt's announcement earlier this week that he will leave the club at the end of the 2026/27 campaign, bringing an end to his remarkable spell of almost 20 years in charge.

Speaking ahead of the Hannover fixture, Schmidt acknowledged the quality within the opposition ranks but expects an intensely contested encounter.

"Accordingly, Sunday will be a real battle," Schmidt explained, highlighting the importance of intensity, second balls and winning individual duels.

The Heidenheim coach also admitted that his team had been overrun in certain situations during their defeat to Karlsruhe, insisting that his players needed to rediscover their sharpness in training before returning to home action.

With their unbeaten home record on the line, Schmidt will be hoping his side can produce a response and return to winning ways.

Hannover travel to Heidenheim with renewed confidence after finally securing their first league victory under Sandro Wagner last weekend.

The Lower Saxony outfit came from behind to defeat Dynamo Dresden 2-1, with Husseyn Chakroun and Marcel Hartel both scoring within seven second-half minutes to turn the contest around.

The victory was particularly important following a difficult start to Wagner's tenure, which had seen Hannover struggle to translate their attacking potential into consistent results.

Having collected just 11 points from their opening ten league matches, Hannover currently occupy 11th position, leaving them four points behind Sunday's opponents.

Wagner will be encouraged by the character his players demonstrated against Dresden, particularly after falling behind during a difficult opening period. However, their away form remains a significant concern, with their only victory on the road this season coming against Darmstadt. They have lost their other four away fixtures.

Speaking ahead of the trip to Baden-Württemberg, Wagner was full of praise for the opposition, describing Heidenheim as a "top team in the second division".

The Hannover coach also highlighted their distinctive playing style, organisation at set-pieces and attacking presence inside the penalty area, acknowledging that his side face a significant challenge.

Wagner will also be hoping to get more out of Benjamin Källman, who has struggled to replicate the goalscoring form that saw him register 14 goals last season. The Finnish forward has yet to find the net in the current campaign, although his manager remains pleased with his overall contribution.

Nevertheless, last weekend's comeback victory provides Hannover with an opportunity to build some momentum, and another positive result would represent a significant step forward under their new coach.

Heidenheim have historically enjoyed the better of this fixture, winning six of the ten previous meetings between the clubs, compared to Hannover's four victories. Interestingly, none of their ten encounters in the 2. Bundesliga have ended in a draw.

The hosts will also take confidence from their recent record at the Voith-Arena, having won each of their last four home meetings with Hannover.

Their previous encounter came during the 2022/23 campaign, when Heidenheim secured a convincing 3-0 victory away from home, with Tim Kleindienst among the goalscorers.

Hannover's last victory against Heidenheim came in August 2021, when they recorded a 1-0 home success.

Heidenheim have relatively few injury concerns heading into Sunday's fixture, with Luca Kerber the only confirmed absentee.

The midfielder continues to struggle with muscular problems and will not be available for selection. However, Schmidt confirmed during Friday's press conference that the remainder of his squad had been involved in training.

Hannover have a slightly longer injury list, with Lars Gindorf, Benedikt Pichler, and Karl Steinmann all ruled out.

There is more encouraging news regarding Husseyn Chakroun, who suffered a shoulder injury during the Dresden encounter. Wagner confirmed that the attacker would be included in the travelling squad, although a decision on whether he starts or features from the bench will be made closer to kick-off.

Non soltanto una sfida sul campo. Inter e Como, avversarie domenica a San Siro, rappresentano anche due modelli economici differenti: da una parte una società storica impegnata nel risanamento dei conti, dall'altra un progetto ambizioso sostenuto da importanti investimenti. Un confronto tra sostenibilità e crescita, come evidenzia l'analisi della Gazzetta dello Sport.

Numeri che raccontano una strategia orientata a crescere rapidamente, rafforzando la rosa di Cesc Fabregas e investendo anche nelle infrastrutture e nell'area commerciale. All 8 Episodes Of Netflix's R-Rated Thriller Miniseries Deliver Peak Psychological Horror

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Some of the greatest thrillers carefully transcend the screen and invade a viewer's personal space. They introduce audiences to such bizarre yet intimate scenarios that it becomes hard not to wonder if their hard-hitting terrors could someday happen in the real world. One of Netflix's better psychological thrillers is driven by the same principle, allowing it to stay with one long after the credits stop rolling.

Written by Son Ho-young, The Frog is relatively obscure compared to most mainstream thriller shows on Netflix. It follows a relatively simple premise where a mysterious visitor enters the lives of ordinary unsuspecting people before unleashing complete chaos. However, what starts as a simple psychological thriller soon turns into a tale of extreme obsession and manipulation.

The Frog's early appeal comes from its portrayal of quaint rural settings. It primarily unfolds in the brightness of the summer in seemingly safe and peaceful environments. This, however, is cleverly juxtaposed by the gripping Netflix K-drama's portrayal of how one can fatefully find darkness even during the brightness of the day and the peace of the night.

There is a famous Korean proverb that goes something like: "A stone thrown carelessly can kill a frog." The proverb is where the show gets its name from as it serves as the primary driver of its story. More often than not, in typical psychological thrillers, a game of cat-and-mouse unfolds between a protagonist and an antagonist.

The protagonist usually makes a mistake or lapse in judgment, which triggers an antagonist to chase them.

In Netflix's The Frog, however, characters do nothing to provoke "monsters." They are portrayed as the proverbial "frogs" who merely become targets and victims due to bad timing. By showing how one literally has to do nothing to attract trouble, The Frog instills a kind of existential dread that most psychological thrillers lack.

Instead of merely unfolding a thrilling game of cat-and-mouse, The Frog also uses one's denial and remorse as a narrative device. It shows how one does not merely become a "frog" when directly confronted by a villain. Even the agonizing struggle to overcome denial and accept misfortune can turn an individual into a "frog," trapped in the crossfire of someone else's wrongdoings.

The Netflix psychological thriller succeeds because it remains less fixated on a conventional battle between good and evil. Its primary emphasis remains on the psychological paralysis experienced by an innocent bystander trying to pretend nothing happened. With its more grounded portrayal of psychological horror, The Frog completely immerses viewers into its drama.

Even the show's characters seem so realistic and ordinary that it becomes hard not to see yourself in their place.

There is a sense of passiveness to The Frog's exploration of suspense and high-stakes psychological terror because of how it captures the consequences of inaction rather than blatant immorality. However, owing to its unique approach to the genre, The Frog risks feeling a little too uneven and detached from its own core story at times.

For the most part of its run, however, the Netflix thriller does a great job of making up for its flaws with its compelling character beats, brilliant performances, and incredible atmospheric contrast. Not to mention, its brief 8-episode runtime makes it perfectly bingeable.

2. Bundesliga preview: VfL Bochum vs. Karlsruher SC

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Travel  |  Read Source →

The meeting presents an important opportunity for both sides to strengthen their positions in the 2. Bundesliga table as the season progresses. Bochum will be looking to make home advantage count, while Karlsruher SC travel to North Rhine-Westphalia hoping to come away with a positive result.

Bochum head into Sunday's encounter with renewed confidence after ending their difficult run of league form with a 2-1 victory away to Energie Cottbus last weekend.

Uwe Rösler's side had endured a five-game winless run in the league before travelling to Cottbus, but Christian Rasmussen's late winner secured three valuable points and lifted the club to 11th in the standings.

The victory was particularly important given Bochum's struggles in front of goal this season. Having scored just eight times in their opening ten league matches, they currently possess the lowest-scoring attack in the division. However, their recent cup victory over Mainz and the comeback success against Cottbus suggest that Rösler's team are beginning to find some momentum.

Rösler is nevertheless expecting a difficult afternoon against a Karlsruhe side whose intensity and attacking movement could cause problems. Speaking ahead of the fixture, the Bochum coach described Sunday's encounter as a potential 50-50 contest and warned that the visitors are one of the most intense teams in the division.

The German side will need to pay particular attention to Marvin Wanitzek, who Rösler identified as Karlsruhe's standout player, highlighting his ability to find space and influence matches through both open play and set-pieces.

Karlsruher SC travel to Bochum looking to build on an encouraging 2-1 victory over 1. FC Heidenheim, which brought some much-needed stability to their start under new head coach Maximilian Senft.

The Baden-Württemberg outfit had endured a difficult spell before welcoming Heidenheim, having suffered a heavy 6-1 defeat away to Magdeburg immediately after an impressive 4-0 home victory against Darmstadt.

However, goals from Tim Civeja and Marvin Wanitzek secured a deserved victory over the recently-relegated side last weekend, with Wanitzek's superb free-kick proving decisive.

Despite that result, consistency remains a significant concern for Senft. Karlsruhe have collected 11 points from their opening ten league matches, leaving them level with Bochum, although their inferior goal difference places them two positions below Sunday's opponents.

Their away form is another area requiring improvement, with the visitors yet to record an away victory in the league this season, collecting just two points from their four matches on the road.

Senft acknowledged that his team must become more consistent when discussing their recent performances, explaining that the squad are still developing under his new tactical approach.

"We are coming out of the Heidenheim game strengthened, particularly because of the way we performed tactically and mentally," the Austrian coach explained. He also stressed his desire to deliver positive performances more frequently rather than continuing to experience such significant fluctuations.

Karlsruhe will therefore be hoping that last weekend's victory can provide the platform for a more convincing away performance, particularly against a Bochum side who have also struggled to find consistency this season.

Historically, Bochum have enjoyed the better of this fixture, recording 23 victories compared to Karlsruhe's 16 across 64 competitive meetings, with the remaining 25 encounters ending in draws.

However, the more recent record at the Vonovia Ruhrstadion offers encouragement for the visitors. Karlsruhe have avoided defeat on each of their last six visits to Bochum, who have not beaten them at home since a 1-0 victory in May 2014.

Interestingly, 13 of the 24 meetings between the sides in the 2. Bundesliga have ended in draws, highlighting how closely contested this fixture has traditionally been.

The previous meeting came on the final day of the 2025/26 campaign, when Bochum claimed a 2-1 away victory thanks to Moritz-Broni Kwarteng's winning goal.

Bochum will be without several players for Sunday's encounter, with Enis Cokaj, Jean Manuel Mbom and Oliver Olsen having returned to training but not yet being available for selection.

Babis Drakas and Philipp Strompf are also not ready to return, while Berkan Taz missed one training session earlier in the week. However, Rösler confirmed that the remainder of his squad had completed full training.

The Bochum coach also suggested that changes could be made to his attacking partnership, with Philipp Hofmann having played a significant number of minutes recently. Charlie Patiño and Jarne Steuckers are among those pushing for starting opportunities following encouraging substitute appearances.

Karlsruhe, meanwhile, will again be without defensive regulars Marcel Franke and Sebastian Jung, who are both unavailable through injury.

New signing Héliton has completed his first full week of training since the previous international break, although Senft indicated that he is more likely to be considered for a brief appearance rather than a starting role.

Paul Scholl is consequently expected to retain his place in the back three after impressing during the 2-1 victory over Heidenheim, while Tim Civeja will also hope to keep his place following his first goal in the second division.

Kepa Arrizabalaga set for emotional Athletic Club homecoming at San Mames as Arsenal backup goalkeeper is called up by Basque Country to face Venezuela

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Arsenal goalkeeper Kepa Arrizabalaga is poised for a poignant return to former club Athletic Club's San Mames stadium after earning a call-up from the Basque Country representative team. The shot-stopper has been named in the Euskal Selekzioa squad to take on South American visitors Venezuela during next weekend's international friendly in Bilbao.

Osasuna manager Jagoba Arrasate has included the Ondarroa native as one of the marquee names in his 22-man selection for the clash on Saturday, November 14. Currently serving as understudy to David Raya at Arsenal following previous loan stints with Real Madrid and Bournemouth, Arrizabalaga originally departed Bilbao for Chelsea in 2018. That €80 million move to Stamford Bridge remains the world-record transfer fee ever paid for a goalkeeper.

Joining Arrizabalaga in the squad as players plying their trade outside Spain are Udinese forward Unai Gomez and Bolton Wanderers attacker Gaizka Larrazabal, while Osasuna stopper Aitor Fernandez provides competition between the posts. Arrasate has once again leaned heavily on a familiar domestic core drawn from Athletic Club, Real Sociedad, Osasuna and Deportivo Alaves.

In defence, Athletic's Dani Vivian, Hugo Rincon and Johaneko Louis-Jean are joined by Sociedad trio Jon Pacheco, Igor Zubeldia and Aihen Munoz alongside Osasuna's Inigo Arguibide and Malaga's Einar Galilea.

Midfield options feature Athletic's Mikel Jauregizar, Sociedad's Benat Turrientes, Alaves pair Pablo Ibanez and Ander Guevara, Osasuna's Asier Osambela and Sevilla's Jon Guridi, while Athletic star Oihan Sancet, Osasuna's Kike Barja and Racing Santander duo Inigo Vicente and Asier Villalibre complete the attack.

Organised by Basque Football Federation president Xabier Goni, the fixture marks the representative team's first appearance since defeating Palestine 3-0 in front of a record 51,396 crowd on November 15 last year via goals from Unai Elgezabal, Gorka Guruzeta and Urko Izeta. Next Saturday's contest carries deep cultural weight as it celebrates the 90th anniversary of the first autonomous Basque Government formed under President Jose Antonio Aguirre in 1936.

Aguirre subsequently established the Euzkadi team in 1937 to tour Europe and the Americas during the Spanish Civil War. Venezuela also holds a special bond with the region after welcoming thousands of Basque political exiles fleeing Francisco Franco's dictatorship.

Arrrizabalaga will hope to earn rare starting minutes in familiar surroundings next weekend before returning to north London to resume his backup duties under Mikel Arteta after the international break. On the pitch in Bilbao, Euskal Selekzioa will look to preserve their 100 per cent historical record in what will be their fourth meeting against Venezuela. The hosts previously beat the South Americans 4-3 away in San Cristobal in June 2007, 3-1 at the old San Mames in December 2010 and 4-2 at Mendizorrotza in October 2018.

I started again, cleared my save file, and it turns out I've completely forgotten most things about the game - which is by no means a complaint! I'm rather enjoying making the little discoveries that are either brand-new to me, or ones that make me go 'AH-HA, I remember you' (in the case of Gloom Hands, the reaction was 'NOPE, NOPE, NOPE, NOPE.')

I've also had another surprise. I play in handheld mode mostly, and ToTK was all looking a bit blurry on the Switch 2's screen. So I got the upgrade pack and suddenly everything became beautiful again - Link was in focus, the individual blades of grass in Hyrule Field were clearly whipping in the wind, and the crispy snow around Rito village almost sparkled. I don't often get upgrade packs for games I already own, but this one was worth it.

I suspect this won't be the last time you see me yapping about Queen's Domain here. I've already enthused about it elsewhere on the site this week, but I've managed to squeeze in a few more hours since then, and it continues to enthrall. Here's a small moment from my most recent go: I'd been fumbling about the Tower of Knowledge for a while at this point, dying slightly more than I'd like to those jabby gargoyle gits and lumbering chimera things, so I thought I'd have a little wander away from the violence and have an explore. That was when I noticed the gap on the bookshelf, wide enough to slot in the mysterious Ratty Bug Compendium I'd been wondering about ever since I picked it up elswhere some five hours early.

Moments later a secret passage had opened, leading out to a balcony overlooking the ocean. Treasure! Exciting! But just as I'm about to go back inside, I notice a little outcrop on the exterior of the tower, seemingly reachable with my sword's magic dashing power. So over I go, discovering an otherwise inaccessible route into an inner chamber. There, I find a lever, which I excitedly pull, only to be rewarded with... nothing at all. Until five minutes or so later, when I discover a secret panel has now opened up on the other side of the tower, granting me access to the treats within. In isolation, it's not a remarkable gaming anecdote, but Queen's Domain is just crammed with these little incremental secrets and surprises, full of clever design that makes exploration feel so rewarding. I've already spotted more enigmas waiting to be cracked, and if Queen's Domain keeps distracting me like this, I might never escape its pull.

Liverpool have lost a lot of truly great talent over the last few years. Not all of it has been choice, either, though some of it absolutely was. Mohamed Salah leaving this year, for instance, was down to the club as much as the player.

There have been plenty of sales, too. Curtis Jones departed for Inter in the summer while homegrown talent like Caoimhin Kelleher and Jarell Quansah were both sold to starting positions elsewhere.

But then there are the ones that aren't by choice. Trent Alexander-Arnold was offered a new contract but rejected it to join Real Madrid. One year later, Ibrahima Konaté did literally the exact same thing.

And it likely won't stop there. That doesn't mean players will leave next year to join Real Madrid (but don't rule that out) but there are stars who could get their heads turned.

Bayern Munich were surprisingly linked with a move for Rio Ngumoha in the summer. They were supposedly willing to put up quite a bit of cash to make it happen, though Liverpool never entertained the idea.

But Christian Falk reports that they haven’t actually stopped looking. He claims that Bayern remain in talks with representatives of Ngumoha, though not directly the player himself.

They’re eager to find the player who could take over from Luis Diaz on the left wing. Ngumoha is certainly a fit for that - even if a successor to the Colombian is the exact same role that Liverpool want him for.

Now, the reason these talks are important is that Ngumoha’s contract expires in 2028 and Liverpool are in their own talks to get a new one signed. If they fail, there will be immense pressure to sell next summer while they can still get a fee for him.

That then becomes even more complicated because Chelsea get 20 per cent of any sale. Essentially, there’s no good outcome here other than Ngumoha re-signing with Liverpool.

Bayern are going to do all they can to make sure that doesn’t happen, though. We’ll probably refrain from predicting how this one goes, given the exits over the last couple of years, but we know Liverpool are pushing hard.
