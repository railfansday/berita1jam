Taylor Swift and Karlie Kloss Attend Travis Kelce’s Chiefs Game in Kansas City

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The longtime friends reunited to watch the Chiefs take on the New York Jets at Arrowhead Stadium on Nov. 8.

Taylor Swift had some familiar company as she cheered on husband Travis Kelce and the Kansas City Chiefs on Sunday (Nov. 8).

The 36-year-old pop superstar watched the Chiefs take on the New York Jets at Arrowhead Stadium in Kansas City, joined by longtime friend Karlie Kloss. The supermodel, 34, was also among the guests at Swift and Kelce’s July 3 wedding at Madison Square Garden in New York City.

“Listen, I try to protect the privacy of my friends’ personal lives,” Kloss said during a July appearance on the Today show. “But what I will say is that they’re an amazing couple and I’m so happy for them.”

Swift and Kloss appeared in good spirits as they watched the Chiefs take the field for their ninth game of the season. The “Shake It Off” singer wore a red sweater as she cheered on Kelce alongside her parents, Andrea and Scott Swift. Sunday’s matchup also marked a career milestone for the tight end, who made his 200th NFL appearance.

Swift and Kloss first struck up a friendship in 2013, eventually becoming close enough to share the cover of Vogue in 2015. Their relationship later became the subject of speculation when they were seen together less frequently. In August 2023, Kloss attended Swift’s Eras Tour show in Los Angeles.

Sunday marked Swift’s fourth appearance at a Chiefs game this season. She previously attended the team’s Oct. 18 home game against the Los Angeles Chargers, watching from a suite alongside musician Este Haim and basketball star Caitlin Clark.

Earlier in the season, Swift attended the Chiefs’ Sept. 14 game against the Denver Broncos, where she was spotted with actor Tom Cruise. She returned to Arrowhead Stadium on Sept. 20 for the team’s matchup against the Indianapolis Colts.

Swift’s Oct. 18 appearance at Arrowhead Stadium came just one day after a surprise appearance at the 2026 Academy Museum Gala in Los Angeles, where she delivered the live debut of “The Fate of Ophelia” during a short set. The event honored filmmaker John Carpenter, Colman Domingo and Charlize Theron.

Crystal Palace To Sack Pierre Sage

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Crystal Palace have decided to sack manager Pierre Sage following their 3-1 home defeat to Liverpool on Sunday afternoon, as per The Athletic.

Sage was only appointed as Eagles boss over the summer, replacing Oliver Glasner, who left for Nottingham Forest following a successful two-and-a-half year stint in South London. The French coach was sounded out by the Selhurst Park hierarchy after leading Lens to a second-place finish in Ligue 1 last season.

Despite these impressive credentials, Sage hasn't been able to elicit strong performances from his players at Palace, who currently sit bottom of the Premier League after 10 matches.

The Conference League champions have won just two league matches under their new boss, and Palace co-owner Steve Parish has subsequently opted to part ways with the 47-year-old manager.

Lasting until November, just 16 games, Sage will only narrowly surpass Frank de Boer as the shortest-serving manager in Palace's recent history.

The Dutchman famously lasted just four matches at the start of the 2017/18 season, after failing to pick up a single point during his short-lived tenure in the Selhurst Park dugout.

The final straw for Sage was seemingly Palace's dire defeat against Liverpool on Sunday afternoon. Palace will now search for a successor in the upcoming international break, as they desperately look for a coach who can take them away from a relegation battle, whilst coping with the demands of Europa League football.

There have been calls for Eddie Howe to replace Sage, although the former Newcastle boss is reportedly in line to succeed Michael Carrick at Manchester United, if and when the Red Devils manager is dismissed.

Meanwhile, former Ipswich Town boss Kieran McKenna is said to be in the running. The Northern Irishman has been out of a job since stepping down from his role at Portman Road at the end of last season, having led the Tractor Boys to another promotion.

Aston Villa Director Calls Out Roy Keane’s Man Utd Equaliser Verdict

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Aston Villa director of football operations Damian Vidagany has slammed Roy Keane's assessment of Patrick Dorgu's equaliser being allowed to stand in a 4-4 draw against Manchester United.

Unai Emery's side were heading towards all three points when second half stoppage time arrived but Joshua Zirkzee's 90+2nd minute strike gave United hope. They capitalised with a late corner with Zion Suzuki missing the ball and Dorgu heading home, much to Emery's frustration.

The Villa boss seemed to take issue with the manner of the goal when he stormed down the tunnel after watching the ball find the net. There are question marks over United goalkeeper Senne Lammens potentially obstructing Suzuki when the Japanese shot-stopper attempted to claim the ball in midair.

Emery has since explained that his actions were a response to spurning a victory in the dying embers rather than the goal itself. Vidagany has vented his frustrations with Dorgu's header along with Roy Keane's analysis after the match.

Keane spoke on Sky Sports about Lammens' possible involvement in the goal and whether he felt the Belgian had leaned into Suzuki. VAR had checked and cleared this and the former United captain agreed with this verdict: "Football is a contact sport. I have no problem with it."

Vidagany wasn't happy with Keane’s narrative of “contact sport” and posted his thoughts on X:

To put in context this convenient narrative of “ contact sport” we’ve been told by Referees during the summer that this season to go to unbalance or push the GK on purpose on the six hard box was not going to be allowed anymore. Lammens not even look at the ball, went straight to do a contact with the hip over Zion. Having said that we played good Match and we must do better than last 6 minutes. We will do and we will improve. But the rules are the rules and must be respected. That is a fact.

Lammens doesn't fully make contact with Suzuki but rather puts his back to the goalkeeper, who fails to collect the ball. Former Villa winger Ashley Young agreed with Keane and said that he didn't feel there was enough in it.

Emery did suggest in his post-match interview that Villa would have been awarded a foul for Lammens' actions in other leagues. He said that this was an 'adaption' to the Premier League for Suzuki to deal with players 'pushing and disturbing' him.

The Premier League operates under IFAB rules and the guidelines regarding impending a goalkeeper make for interesting reading. It states that if an attacker moves into the path of a goalkeeper to block their line of movement without attempting to play the ball, it is an offence.

Impeding with contact includes body checks, pushing or backing into the keeper and in this case it could be determined that Lammens is guilty of the latter offence. The rule also determines impeding without contact to be directly stepping into their path to force them to stop or alter direction.

The Villa boss lost his head after throwing away a win.

Referees also strictly enforce protection of goalkeepers jumping to catch or punch the ball. This is why Vidagany's dismissive response to Keane claiming football is a contact sport holds merit.

When the World Doesn't Say Thank You

Posted November 8, 2026 | Reviewed by Jessica Schrader

For a while, one of the most reliable relationships in my life was with a traffic sign.

It was one of those electronic billboards you see on the outskirts of villages that nags you for how fast you're driving. Go too fast and the numbers flash red. Keep to the speed limit and you're rewarded with two words of appreciation:

I became a bit too fond of that sign. Embarrassingly, some days I would deliberately crawl past it, making sure I was going at exactly the right speed, just to receive its validation. And it delivered. Every time.

“You're most welcome,” I'd think, feeling absurdly pleased with myself.

Now, you might reasonably wonder why a mature woman would get quite so much satisfaction from being thanked by an illuminated slab of roadside equipment.

I'd recently returned from Switzerland, and was trying to find my feet in a community where I'd once felt very much at home. Much had changed in my absence, and cultivating a sense of belonging was proving difficult.

I was living alone, feeling bereft, and some days the only living creature I spoke to was an elderly cat. Not to be too judgy, but although he was selectively affectionate, his vocabulary wasn’t up to much. So the traffic sign became an accidental but welcome addition to my social circle.

I knew perfectly well that it was only thanking me for driving at the appropriate speed, but I looked forward to those two little words dropping into my day. It felt good to be acknowledged and, truthfully, I wanted some reassurance that I hadn't disappeared.

I've been reflecting on that period recently, and about how much we rely on other people to remind us who we are. It's a backdrop we barely notice when life is flowing along reasonably well. We have our familiar routines, people to talk to, and places to go. There's a sense of belonging that we probably take for granted until our circumstances change.

I think this is one of the reasons loneliness can be so unsettling. Alongside the sadness of having nobody to share things with, there's the strange experience of chalking up an entire day without anyone witnessing it.

You might have achieved something substantial, or white-knuckled your way through a thoroughly miserable morning. Maybe you made a particularly earthy soup with the leeks from your garden, and nobody noticed.

One unremarkable Tuesday, I remember calculating how long it would take my neighbors to realise I hadn’t collected the milk from my doorstep. Three days, I wagered.

Having written a book called Change Your Words, Change Your World, I've spent much of my working life exploring how language affects the way we feel about ourselves, so I’m quite familiar with the idea of self-worth. In theory, at least. In practice, knowing something intellectually doesn't necessarily mean you're proficient at applying it when life gets difficult.

Like you, no doubt, I've caught myself looking for digital reassurance. A message from someone I care about can lift my spirits enormously. Equally, the absence of a reply can fill my head with squirrels and I’ll waste a couple of hours wondering what I've done wrong.

Wanting to feel appreciated is human. The trouble is that other people are unreliable sources of reassurance because they have their own lives to get on with. Sometimes they’re just busy running a taxi service for their kids and don’t get your message until bedtime. Other times they’re having enthralling conversations with their own cats.

Meanwhile, we're wondering whether we've somehow offended the entire human race. It's exhausting. And I wonder how much of that exhaustion comes from constantly looking outside ourselves for something we might be able to offer ourselves.

We’re often much kinder to other people. If a friend tells us she's had a dreadful day, we don't usually ask her to produce evidence; we listen, sympathise, maybe make her a cup of tea. However, when it comes to our own shortcomings, we can be surprisingly dismissive.

Psychologist Kristin Neff has spent years researching self-compassion, including how we can learn to respond to our own challenges with the understanding we'd offer a friend.

It sounds perfectly sensible, doesn't it? But when you’re feeling lonely or discouraged, do you comfort yourself, or spend time forensically investigating what you’ve done to end up that way?

This year I’ve decided to take a different approach. Whenever that familiar feeling of being disappointed or overlooked colors my perspective, I pause and acknowledge what's actually happening.

Is it possible I was hoping someone would call, or do I just need a hug? There's often a little relief in admitting that, even if nothing changes immediately.

It doesn't always work, of course. Some days I'd like the universe to send me a personalised letter of appreciation, preferably on headed paper.

I wonder what I’d say now to the woman who used to drive past that traffic sign. I'd tell her I understood why she was lonely, and that it was perfectly reasonable to miss the life she'd had, even though she might not believe me.

Perhaps that's the thing about learning to validate ourselves. It's easy enough to understand the idea, but quite another matter to feel it. I don't think I've entirely worked it out yet, but I’m getting better at noticing when I’m waiting for someone else to tell me I’m doing all right.

It still feels a little unnatural, but then so did developing an emotional attachment to a traffic sign. I still smile whenever I drive past one and receive my THANK YOU. And I always make sure I'm going at the right speed. It would be a shame to disappoint an old friend.

Neff, K. D. (2003). Self-compassion: An alternative conceptualization of a healthy attitude toward oneself. Self and Identity, 2(2), 85–101. https://doi.org/10.1080/15298860390129863

"It depends on whether or not they sign a very strong agreement," he told CNN on Sunday. "The answer is highly unlikely it's going to be necessary."

At the same time, Trump restated that the US military was "ready" to launch a new round of strikes and could "finish [Iran] off."

Strange Materials Make Waves Behave in Ways That Defy Expectations

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

Researchers have uncovered an unusual wave phenomenon that links engineered materials to principles found in ocean physics.

Waves bouncing around an irregularly shaped space typically follow increasingly unpredictable paths. Scientists have now shown that certain materials can produce the opposite effect, guiding waves into stable, repeating patterns instead of chaotic trajectories.

Researchers at the Advanced Science Research Center at the CUNY Graduate Center (CUNY ASRC) discovered this behavior in hyperbolic materials, whose unusual properties restrict the directions in which waves can travel. Their study, published in Nature Physics, identifies structures called “hyperbolic wave attractors” that could eventually help engineers control light, sound, and electromagnetic signals.

“This work shows how geometry and the properties of a material can work together to produce wave behavior that is both surprising and useful,” said Andrea Alù, the study’s principal investigator, director of the Photonics Initiative at the CUNY ASRC, and Distinguished Professor of Physics at the CUNY Graduate Center. “By understanding how waves organize themselves in these hyperbolic media, we can begin to explore new approaches to controlling energy, information, and communication signals in complex environments.”

In ordinary materials, waves generally reflect at the same angle at which they strike a surface. Repeated reflections inside an irregular enclosure can produce chaotic motion, a phenomenon studied through a classic physics problem known as a dynamical billiard.

Hyperbolic materials behave differently. Named after the mathematical curve called a hyperbola, these materials direct waves along specific paths and alter how they reflect from angled surfaces. Inside an irregular cavity, these unusual reflection rules can cause waves to converge toward organized trajectories.

“Normally, we expect a complicated cavity to produce complicated, chaotic wave patterns,” said Simon Yves, a postdoctoral researcher in Alù’s lab and a first author of the study. “Here, the opposite happens. The unusual propagation and reflection of waves create a strong geometric organization, producing well-defined paths that can persist across a broad range of wavelengths.”

The researchers found that repeated reflections can guide waves into closed trajectories that remain stable across different scales. This behavior arises partly from broken mirror symmetry in the reflection process, which causes wavelengths to progressively shrink as waves travel through the cavity.

These wave attractors also exhibit handedness, meaning their paths circulate clockwise or counterclockwise. Their stability and direction depend on the geometry of the cavity and the material’s unusual wave properties. The team demonstrated the phenomenon using a mechanical metamaterial engineered to control vibrations.

“The most exciting aspect of these results is that they connect a simple geometric idea with a rich set of wave phenomena,” said Enrico Renzi, a doctoral student in Alù’s lab and a first author of the study. “We can observe how the waves become organized, and we can connect that organization to properties such as stability and handedness. This gives us a framework for designing wave behavior rather than simply observing it.”

Related wave attractors occur in oceans, where internal waves traveling through water layers of different densities reflect unusually from underwater slopes. These reflections can concentrate energy along closed paths and contribute to wave turbulence.

The new study demonstrates a similar effect in an engineered solid, allowing researchers to investigate the underlying physics in a controlled laboratory setting. The principles could also extend to hyperbolic phonon polaritons, hybrid light and matter excitations found in certain crystals, including hexagonal boron nitride and molybdenum trioxide.

These materials can confine infrared light to extremely small regions, making them promising candidates for nanophotonic technologies. Potential applications of hyperbolic wave attractors include compact optical circuits, optical signal separation, particle trapping, analog wave computing, and sensitive biological sensors, although these uses have not yet been demonstrated.

“This research opens a path toward engineering stable and robust wave patterns in systems where waves would normally be expected to rapidly become chaotic,” Alù said. “Our goal is to understand how these effects can be harnessed to create new functionalities for photonics, phononics, and wave-based technologies.”

Reference: “Hyperbolic wave attractors” by Simon Yves, Enrico M. Renzi, Sander A. Mann and Andrea Alù, 28 September 2026, Nature Physics. DOI: 10.1038/s41567-026-03453-7

The research was supported by the Simons Foundation and the National Science Foundation Science and Technology Center “New Frontiers of Sound.

Air Algerie Business Class Airbus A330neo Review: Utterly Awful

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

For the first long haul segment of my Orient Express adventure via Africa trip, my first class quest to Japan, I flew Air Algerie’s Airbus A330-900neo business class on the 6hr50min flight from Montreal (YUL) to Algiers (ALG). I had heard almost entirely negative things about Air Algerie, which intrigued me, naturally! Well, now that I’ve flown Air Algerie I can confirm that everything I heard was true, and then some.

I don’t want to be dramatic — obviously flying business class is better than economy — but honestly, just about everything sucked. On the plus side, the seats as such were fine, but that’s where the good news ends. The food was not good, and took an insanely long amount of time to be served. There were no amenity kits… or menus… or meal choice… or alcohol… or diet soda. If the crew were any more indifferent, they would’ve been invisible.

I wouldn’t fly Air Algerie again unless I absolutely had to, which… I can’t imagine that’ll ever be the case. Honestly, Air Algerie made me yearn for my recent flight on Turkmenistan Airlines, which was at least fun.

Air Algerie is a pretty isolated airline, which perhaps reflects Algeria overall — the country just isn’t interested in visitors, and getting a visa requires jumping through hoops.

So Air Algerie doesn’t have any useful partnerships when it comes to earning or redeeming miles. As a result, booking a cash ticket is just about the only option, unless you’re a frequent flyer with the airline. Air Algerie tends to have rather steep business class fares, despite its lackluster product.

However, I did manage to find a rather attractive one-way business class fare. I booked the following in business class for $1,171 in cash:

10/03 AH2701 Montreal to Algiers departing 4:10PM arriving 5:00AM (+1 day)10/04 AH4000 Algiers to Tunis departing 7:10AM arriving 8:30AM

The first segment was operated by an Airbus A330-900neo, while the second segment was operated by an Airbus A320 that was wet leased from Avion Express.

I had flown into Montreal the same day from Miami (MIA) on Air Canada Rouge, so I had an extended layover. I was at the Air Algerie check-in counter before it opened at 12:30PM, and there was already quite the queue of passengers waiting. Unsurprisingly, and as is very common for North Africa flights, people had a ton of checked bags.

It took me around 30 minutes to get checked in, since there was one counter for business class, and there were half a dozen people in front of me. At first I was asked if I had a visa for Algeria, though I explained I was in transit, which seemed to catch the check-in agent off guard. Air Algerie is clearly targeted at the local community, and doesn’t see much international connecting traffic.

Security was a breeze, and Air Algerie sends its business class passengers to the National Bank Lounge, which is the contract lounge in the international portion of the terminal. I won’t do a full review, but it was actually a pretty pleasant facility, with plenty of seating, lots of natural light, and it wasn’t very crowded.

The food selection was pretty average, with a handful of hot options, plus cheese, cold cuts, etc. There was coffee, tea, wine, beer, and soft drinks. I made the mistake of saving my hunger and thirst for onboard the Air Algerie flight!

My Air Algerie flight was departing from gate A58, with boarding scheduled for 3:10PM, a full hour before departure. I was at the gate early enough to see the inbound aircraft arrive, and goodness, what a stunner it was!

The Montreal route is primarily served by an A330neo with the registration code 7T-VLB, which is in a special livery celebrating the country’s football team. Looking at the plane was honestly the best part of the flight experience, unfortunately.

The boarding area got very full before departure, with lots of passengers in wheelchairs, and lots of crowding around the gate. Boarding ended up starting at 3:20PM, with those needing extra time, as well as business class passengers, being invited to board first.

I can’t describe how excited I was to step onboard, as I always love trying new airlines! I wasn’t fully prepared for the level of indifference shown by every part of the experience, though.

Before I get into this review, I should mention that Air Algerie has recently started taking delivery of Airbus A330-900neos as part of its fleet modernization initiative. However, the airline isn’t just taking delivery of these planes factory fresh, but has also acquired a couple secondhand from Air Belgium, which went out of business in 2024.

Air Algerie maintained the Air Belgium interiors — they also have staggered seats, but it’s a slightly different configuration. I boarded through the second door on the left, and then turned left into the business class cabin.

Business class takes up the entire space between the first and second set of doors, and consists of 30 seats, spread across eight rows, in a 1-2-1 configuration. These seats are from the Stelia Aerospace Solstys line, so they’re typical last generation staggered business class seats. When it comes to the seat details, they have 44″ of pitch, 21″ of width, and convert into 77″ flat beds.

On the plus side, it’s really easy to photograph Air Algerie cabins, since crews are nowhere to be seen. 😉

If you’re traveling with someone, you may appreciate the sets of center seats in odd numbered rows, as these are “honeymoon” seats, which allow you to sit really close to your companion. Meanwhile in even numbered rows, the seats in the center section are further from one another and really close to the aisles, so those would be my least preferred seats in this configuration.

Along the windows, seats alternate between being closer to the aisle and closer to the windows. In odd numbered rows, seats are closer to the aisle. What I particularly don’t like about these staggered seats is just how exposed some of them are to the aisle, as you really have no separation.

In even numbered rows, seats are closer to the windows, and these are the best seats in the house, in terms of offering some separation from the aisle, and an easy view out the window.

I assigned myself seat 6A, the window seat on the left side in the third to last row.

These staggered seats have a console to the side of the seat, and on the side of that is a panel with seat controls, an entertainment controller, and a small armrest.

There’s an exposed storage area above the panel, where you can store things, or even hang the headphones. As you can see, the seat just wasn’t well maintained, and also wasn’t clean, as it also needed a good wiping down.

In terms of charging, the AC power outlet and USB-A outlet were located underneath the seat. There was no USB-C or wireless charging, unsurprisingly.

Looking at the front of the seat, the tray table is stored behind the seat in front, and can be extended by being lowered and then moved over. One of the nice things about these tray tables is that you can angle them away from you, so it’s possible to get up during the meal service.

The entertainment system is in a fixed position and is located above the ottoman. The monitor here was 16″ and was touchscreen, but wasn’t terribly high definition.

As much as there are many aspects of this seat I don’t love, I do like how big the footwell is, which is great for those of us with big feet. It’s not necessarily that comfortable when you’re laying sideways in terms of space for knees, but still, there was a great amount of space here.

One other interesting note is that there were no overhead bins in the center of the cabin, so the overhead bins along the windows got pretty full, since the A330neo doesn’t have huge bins. On top of that, there were no individual air nozzles above seats, sadly. That was a problem on this flight, given how warm the cabin was kept.

All-in-all, Air Algerie’s business class hard product is okay. The cabin maintenance could definitely use some help, but honestly, this is the least of Air Algerie’s problems.

The Air Algerie business class entertainment system on the A330neo is pretty awful. For one, there’s not even a map feature. I’m sorry, I’m not sure the last time I flew an airline with seat back entertainment that didn’t have a map feature. What’s going on there?

The system did have some movies, TV shows, and music, but it was a really lackluster selection.

In total, there were 100 movies and 36 TV shows, but it definitely felt like the number of “popular” options was limited.

The airline presumably went with the cheapest entertainment package available. For example, when it comes to sitcoms, there was one episode of “Fresh Off The Boat,” and it was the pilot episode from 2015. Yikes.

When it comes to Wi-Fi, that’s something that just isn’t offered by Air Algerie. Funny enough, these ex-Air Belgium A330neos have Wi-Fi installed, but the airline just chooses not to use the system. So expect that you’re going to disconnect when flying with Air Algerie.

When it comes to amenities in Air Algerie business class, they were also very limited. The highlight was the pillow and blanket, which were at the seat upon boarding, but not exactly very nicely presented.

Anyway, this was the highlight of the amenities, and while they were nothing amazing, they were at least decent.

Headphones were also passed out well after takeoff, though the quality of them was very poor. This was closer to what you’d expect in economy than business class.

There were no amenity kits, or anything else to speak of. I don’t remember the last overnight business class flight I’ve taken where I wasn’t offered an amenity kit?

Despite the very drawn out boarding process, the crew was nowhere to be seen for most of boarding. Finally at 4:10PM, right at departure time, they came around with pre-departure drinks. There was no “hello” or “welcome onboard,” but instead, just the tray being held out with orange juice or water. At least it was served in glassware… yay?

Boarding this Air Algerie A330neo took an incredibly long time. Every seat was taken (business class seemed to be occupied mostly by operational upgrades plus friends and family). There was just endless confusion, and the crew’s only involvement with things was to occasionally check on people they knew and have chats with them, while everyone else was ignored.

Boarding was also rough because it seemed like the APU was broken, so the cabin was incredibly hot. During boarding, I had asked the flight attendant if I could use the lavatory (I try to do that as a courtesy, even though it showed as unoccupied), and she responded “wait.” I checked with her again some time later, and she again said “wait.” I didn’t end up getting to go before departure… oh well!

Our departure time came and went, and about 20 minutes later I asked a flight attendant if we were delayed. She didn’t understand my question, but after rephrasing, her answer was “no.” The door ended up closing at 4:35PM, 25 minutes behind the scheduled departure time.

At that point the manual safety demonstration began, which was exclusively performed in French and Arabic, and not in English. In fairness, I was probably the only person onboard who didn’t have French or Arabic as my first language. I should also mention there were no safety checks during taxi — some people had their seats reclined, plenty of people still had their (glass) pre-departure beverages, and the guy across from me didn’t even have his seatbelt on.

We began our pushback at 4:40PM, and then at 4:50PM we began our taxi.

While taxiing (and turning a corner), the captain made his welcome onboard announcement at 4:55PM, telling us “this is captain, good morning, taking off shortly.” Okay, we’ll ignore the “morning” part (I’ll assume it just means he’s well rested!), but I assume at Air Algerie the captain is also the one taxiing, right? If so, that’s a lot of multitasking, especially when you’re actively turning.

But hey, credit where credit’s due, this guy was good at turning corners. When we got to runway 24R at 5PM and were cleared for takeoff, we didn’t slowly make the turn and then accelerate. Instead, we started accelerating pretty aggressively in our turn, and were going over 15 knots, while turning at a 90 degree angle. Seriously, check the Flightradar24 data.

We had a very powerful takeoff roll and the captain pulled up on the sidestick as if we were in a fighter jet. The views on the climb out were gorgeous — what a lovely afternoon!

We made a sharp turn after takeoff, and had some nice views of the airport thanks to that.

The seatbelt sign was turned off around five minutes after takeoff. A little while later, we started to get a beautiful sunset view as well.

Dinner was served after takeoff in Air Algerie business class, but my gosh, what a bad and drawn out meal it was. I can deal with bad food or slow service, but bad food, bad drinks, and slow service? BLEH. The first thing to understand is that there were no menus or choices. You’re just served stuff, and it’s a “take it or leave it” situation.

Service began 50 minutes after takeoff, when warm towels were distributed.

Then around 15 minutes later, tablecloths were handed out. These felt more like large, flimsy napkins, than actual tablecloths, but whatever, no biggie.

Another 10 minutes later, the first course was served. The actual food was a little smoked salmon, some egg, some olives, etc. It didn’t taste good at all, but what was worse was the presentation. It’s as if they just threw a bunch of packaging on the tray, and put zero effort into how it’s presented.

I couldn’t help but do their job for them, and make it look a little nicer, because it’s just embarrassing. Oh, and bread is just served by hand out of the basket. Nice.

I kind of loved the Air Algerie branding on the cutlery, but that’s the same cutlery you have to use for the entire meal. For that matter, the cutlery was downright sticky when I removed it from the packaging, so clearly it just hadn’t been cleaned properly.

Drinks were brought out separately from the meal, but they might as well not have bothered. I didn’t realize that Air Algerie is a “dry” airline — it hadn’t even made my list of airlines that are dry. As it turns out, this is only a fairly recent development, as the airline switched to being dry a few years ago.

It’s not only that they don’t have alcohol, but they don’t even have any diet sodas. They have juice or full calorie soda. There was also a huge language barrier here:

“Drink?”“Do you have wine?”“Soda, juice.”“Do you have Diet Coke?”“Coke?”“No, Diet Coke.”“What?”“Coke Zero? Diet Pepsi? Pepsi Max?”*stares blankly*“I’ll have a water, please.”*serves me Sprite*

Another 15 minutes later the main course was served. Again, there was no option, and it seemed to be some sort of a swordfish, or something. As someone who typically loves fish (and orders it way too often on planes), I found this to be way overcooked, dry, and completely tasteless. If you’re not going to offer people a choice of meals, what a random thing to serve!

After an additional pause, the crew came around with cheese plates. Next up, two whole apples were served, which… I mean, I can’t blame them for the excessive amount of food, serving cheese, fruit, and dessert. But still, this very much feels like an afterthought. I also couldn’t help but wonder if those apples had been washed, given that we’re talking about an airline that serves bread by hand.

Lastly there was a little dessert tart trio. I tried to order a tea without caffeine, but that was a challenge:

“Coffee or tea?”“Do you have any tea without caffeine?”“Coffee or tea?”“Tea please, but no caffeine.”“Coffee?”“Tea, please.”

The entire meal service took close to three hours. Nothing about it was good. The food wasn’t good. The service was terrible. I mean, everything was just so poorly timed. There was never even the offer for more drinks (even just bottled water), and when I did ask, it took them 15 minutes and multiple reminders to actually get some more water.

I just don’t get it. As another example, they served the cheese plates, and then 10 minutes later they came around with more bread, after just about everyone had finished their cheese course. I’m exhausted writing about this. I need a drink.

The Air Algerie A330neo business class lavatory is located at the very front of the cabin. One lavatory really isn’t enough for a full business class cabin plus the crew, so there was often a wait. While there are two lavatories behind the cabin, they’re shared with economy passengers, so there’s usually a line.

The lavatories were fine. Toward the end of the flight they definitely weren’t clean, but to be honest, I’ve seen much worse lavatories on airlines in the region, so I’m hardly going to complain.

By the time the meal service was complete, we had well under four hours remaining to Algiers. So I reclined my seat into bed mode, and tried to get a bit of rest. The bed was okay, and again, compared to the rest of the experience, it was one of the better things.

The issue is how hard it was to actually get rest on this flight. The lights were fully on until after the meal service was complete, so at that point it was under four hours until our arrival in Algiers.

Then the cabin lights were once again fully turned on exactly two hours before arrival. So for those keeping track at home, on a 6hr50min flight, the lights were dimmed for around 1hr50min. I’m not sure I actually slept for a minute. The cabin was really warm, and with the lack of individual air nozzles, I just couldn’t get comfortable.

It absolutely boggles my mind why the crew has to start the pre-landing service a full two hours before landing, when the extent of the breakfast is a single tray with a small side of fruit, a small piece of cake, and a croissant. Seriously, this is what everyone is being woken up two hours before landing for?!

Credit where credit’s due, the drip coffee with milk was drinkable, and perhaps the culinary highlight of the flight. Getting a refill was beyond my abilities, but I sure did savor that one cup!

I understand that North African countries are often known for warm hospitality in a non-transactional setting, where there’s a genuine warmth and generosity to hospitality. Clearly that doesn’t apply in a transactional setting, though, and certainly not on Air Algerie.

I don’t want to say the crew was rude — that wouldn’t be fair, and I didn’t even communicate with them enough to draw that conclusion. They actually seemed to be very nice in how they chatted with their friends who were in the cabin.

As a customer, though, everything about the service was terrible. The crew had no warmth, no attentiveness, they were incredibly disorganized and slow, and the service flow just made no sense. If I were to train a crew on how to provide good service in business class, I’d have them experience this flight, and then have them do exactly the opposite. I think I’ve ragged on them enough.

You’ve gotta love how the refreshing towel has Air Algerie’s logo, and then says “Always caring for you.” If this is what care looks like, I’d love to see what not being cared for looks like!

I was excited when my Air Algerie flight was slowly coming to an end. At around 4:20AM Algiers time the seatbelt sign was turned on, and the crew prepared the cabin for landing. Now, that didn’t actually involve doing any security checks, but they made sure passengers were aware there would be no more service.

We ended up touching down at 4:50AM, and it must’ve been one of the smoothest touchdowns of my life, as you could barely feel it. From there, we had a short five minute taxi to a remote stand, where we pulled in at 4:55AM.

It’s very odd how an airport that has so many empty gates and that’s rather underutilized sends its “flagship” aircraft to remote stands, but whatever.

Upon deplaning, there wasn’t so much as a “goodbye” from the crew. There also wasn’t any priority deplaning for business class, or a separate business class bus. It was quite a long drive to the terminal, and we got there at 5:15AM, where my interesting transit experience started…

I highly recommend flying Air Algerie business class… if you want to be reminded how every other airline doesn’t actually suck that much, by comparison. Air Algerie’s A330neo business class hard product is okay, and is the least of the issues.

The problem is everything else, from the completely indifferent crew, to the unnecessarily drawn out service, to the lack of meal options, amenity kits, menus, diet soda, alcohol, etc., to the subpar entertainment and lack of Wi-Fi… the list goes on and on. And that says nothing of the bizarre elements of the flight, like no English safety demos, no safety checks before takeoff or landing, etc.

What’s sad is that I would’ve actually had a more favorable impression of the flight if they just said “this is a sleeper flight, and we have no inflight service.” Truly, no service would’ve been better than this. I’m happy I had the chance to fly Air Algerie, but you know, I think I’m good for the time being!
