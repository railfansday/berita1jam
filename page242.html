'Who let the dogs out?' Jameis Winston busts out hilarious cadence

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Leave it to Jameis Winston to allow Thursday night's game to go to the dogs.

The New York Giants veteran backup quarterback, who now doubles as something of a short-yardage specialist, tried to draw the Washington Commanders offside with 3:27 remaining in the game when the NFC East-leading Giants faced a fourth-and-1 from their own 11-yard line.

Lined up in a bunched formation, Winston began yelling out signals, but Washington held its ground. When that didn't work, the No. 1 pick of the 2015 NFL Draft invoked the Baha Men with this cadence: "Who let the dogs out?Who, who, who, who, who?" ... the latter portion naturally sounding like barking.

But the Commanders didn't bite, and the Giants had to punt.

Washington quarterback Jayden Daniels threw a seemingly crucial interception four plays later, but the Giants went three-and-out on the subsequent drive − that punt proving costly, as the Commanders scored what turned out to be the winning touchdown of what turned out to be a 24-21 triumph, one that likely saved their season, on the following possession.

Winston did convert a 1-yard sneak on fourth down in the second quarter for a first down but couldn't do the same on the play prior to his cadence hijinks. He's rushed 20 times this season for a meager 62 yards but has picked up 12 first downs.

US disrupts North Korea's $400 million crypto scam, FBI says

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The US has seized servers linked to a North Korean scam that used fake video meetings and deepfakes to steal more than $400 million in crypto, and has moved to seize $1.2 billion that was taken from Binance.

The United States Justice Department said Thursday it had seized cloud infrastructure used by North Korean hackers in a video-call scam that has stolen more than $400 million (€361 million) in cryptocurrency over 18 months.

It also said it had court approval to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion taken in the recent Binance hack.

The Federal Bureau of Investigation calls the scam "FaceStealer."

According to the Justice Department, hackers sent meeting invitations from accounts taken over from earlier victims. Once a target joins, the hackers record their webcam video and play a prerecorded clip of a previous victim the target knows, so trusted contacts appear to be present. The FBI says hackers are also starting to use AI-generated deepfakes of well-known crypto figures.

The hackers then stage a fake technical error and offer a link to "fix" it, which installs malware, the department said. In some cases, they waited months, or over a year, before stealing funds.

After that, they use the victim's contacts to find new targets.

Most targets work in crypto and other internet-related fields, and the hackers are increasingly going after open-source software developers, apparently to enable supply-chain attacks, the department said.

Under a federal warrant, the FBI seized more than 900 webcam videos of victims.

"If you can't imagine a scenario where you might fall prey to it, you are not thinking creatively enough," said Patrick Grandy, who heads the FBI's Los Angeles field office.

The FBI ties FaceStealer to a hacking cluster known as Sapphire Sleet or UNC1069. It is separate from the North Korean military hackers blamed for the Binance theft.

In the Binance case, the FBI said the group known as TraderTraitor stole more than $6 billion (5.4 billion euros) on or around October 29. AP put the figure at about $6.3 billion (5.7 billion euros) and called it the biggest crypto heist on record. US agencies and private firms have since frozen more than $2.7 billion (2.44 billion euros), the department said.

Authorities usually file a forfeiture claim in court, after which a judge decides who has a rightful claim to the recovered funds, AP reported.

"North Korea's government bankrolls its rogue regime and illicit weapons program through cryptocurrency theft,"  said M. Scott Proctor, the department's principal deputy assistant attorney general for national security.

North Korea has denied involvement in the Binance hack.

Rosalia Makes History With Third Album of the Year Win at the Latin Grammys

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Rosalía just made Latin Grammys history — again. The Spanish singer won the Album of the Year award for her 2025 LP Lux on Thursday, marking the third time she’s taken home the biggest honor of the night and making her the first woman to win the award as a lead artist that many times.

Rosalía couldn’t contain her emotions during the big moment, already in tears as she walked onstage to receive the award from presenter Juanes. The Colombian rocker’s presence made her especially grateful as she reminisced how he helped her during the beginning of her career. “The fact that you’re the one giving me this award is so powerful,” the Spanish singer told Juanes.

“For me, Lux has been a leap into the abyss. I think it’s really important to speak your truth and that everyone else can share their truth. This album has been mine,” Rosalía said in her speech. She ended by honoring how her mother raised her. “Thanks, mama, for making me this way. I’m going to bring this Grammy to your house. I don’t know if another one is going to fit in your house, but I’ll bring it to you anyways and see if you make room.You always find a way for anything,” she said.

Released last fall, Lux saw the Spanish singer flex her power as a trained conservatory musician as she blended classical music, opera, and 14 different languages into a spellbinding project that tackles theology, love, and womanhood. Rosalía took the all-encompassing energy of the album and channeled into the Lux world tour this year, making for a unique spectacle complete with a full-blown orchestra. Managing Offshore Wind in the Face of Uncertainty

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

This report lays out key adaptive management principles and recommendations for regulators.

Despite current headwinds from the Trump administration, offshore wind energy is essential for transitioning away from harmful fossil fuels to a clean energy economy. Offshore wind could provide more than three times the electricity required to power the United States, giving the country an opportunity to fight climate change, reduce local and regional air and water pollution, and grow a new industry that supports thousands of high-quality careers.

While the need for clean energy is urgent, we can and must ensure that all offshore wind in the United States, including floating offshore wind systems, are developed responsibly and in a manner that avoids, minimizes, mitigates, and monitors for adverse impacts to biodiversity and coastal and marine ecosystems. “Adaptive management” is crucial for meeting the dual goals of facilitating a rapid transition to renewable energy while minimizing impacts to the ocean in the face of uncertainty—and continuing to do so over time.

This report explores how adaptive management can be implemented in offshore wind development, provides examples of its use in other energy sectors today, and offers clear recommendations for agencies and policymakers.

Thought-Watching for Athletes and Others

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

After years or decades, problems and patterns can become deeply entrenched. Be a fly on the wall during a sample couples therapy session and witness the slow, deliberate work of changing those patterns for a more enduring love.

Posted November 12, 2026 | Reviewed by Margaret Foley

Let’s begin with a challenging drill: What thoughts and feelings appear as you read the words “Let's begin with a challenging drill?” Do you have a sense of “Let’s do this!” or “Ugh, I’m not in the mood”? Are you curious? Excited? Nervous? Simply notice whatever thoughts and feelings show up without judging them as good or bad, right or wrong.

OK, now here’s the challenge. Get four large paper clips and your cell phone or a stopwatch. Separate the paper clips and set them on the table. Before you begin, take 10 slow, deep breaths. Then time yourself and, using only your dominant hand (the hand you write with), link all four paper clips together end to end in a chain. Most elite athletes can do this task in 30 seconds.

Before you begin, though, there is one additional part to the exercise: While you connect your paper clips, do your best to notice your thinking. When you are ready, start your timer and begin. Stop the timer when you have linked all four paper clips.

What did you say to yourself, either internally or out loud? Were your thoughts kind or unkind? Helpful or unhelpful?

Do you have thoughts like these when you are training and competing? Or when you are doing homework, taking a test, working on a project, or talking to someone you are interested in?

If some, or a lot, of your internal dialogue is unkind, unhelpful, and untrue, please know you are not alone. Most people have this negative internal chatter and self-­critical play-­by-play. I fondly call it Unkind Mind.

As author Grace Lichtenstein writes, Unkind Mind is our real opponent: “Your opponent, in the end, is never really the player on the other side of the net, or the swimmer in the next lane, or the team on the other side of the field, or even the bar you must high-­jump. Your opponent is yourself, your negative internal voices.”

Sports psychologist Keith Kaufman has conducted multiple research studies regarding the benefits of mindfulness in sports. His research supports a primary premise of my book A Still Quiet Place for Athletes: that with practice you can learn to watch Unkind Mind without believing it, taking it personally, or letting it prevent you from fully expressing your talent. "Athletes can learn to recognize their mind wandering toward thoughts about limitations during competition (e.g., 'I can’t sustain this pace') but then mindfully label these experiences as just thoughts, let them go and bring attention back to a present moment anchor like their breathing. Through this process, athletes can free themselves from judgments that might unnecessarily determine their limits."

And everyone—athletes, business people, performing artists, health care providers, young people, parents—can benefit from learning to observe their thoughts without believing them or taking them personally.

During the next week, practice simply noticing your thoughts without believing them or taking them personally. See if it helps you in your daily life—in training and competition, at work or school, on stage, or in your relationships.

Five thoughts from Cisco’s first quarter of fiscal 2027

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

GUEST COLUMN by Zeus Kerravala

Cisco Systems Inc. opened fiscal 2027 with a quarter that should put any question about whether artificial intelligence will act as a massive tailwind for it to rest. Revenue and earnings hit records, growth rates were the best Cisco has posted in more than 15 years, and management raised its full-year outlook and AI infrastructure revenue target.

While Cisco has delivered a string of solid quarters, some investors are concerned about the durability of the growth. After listening to the analyst call, I maintain that accelerated growth is what Cisco watchers should expect, as AI will act as a catalyst for several years. Here are my five thoughts, plus the one metric I think investors should care about most:

Asked why this isn’t simply the top of a hyperscaler spending cycle, Chief Executive Chuck Robbins (pictured) cited five growth drivers: hyperscale AI, the campus refresh, the “post-Mythos” security upgrade cycle, sovereign infrastructure and enterprise hybrid data centers. “All five of these are multiyear, multibillion,” he said. “And they’re all in relatively early stages.” In particular, the enterprise opportunity is in its infancy. Using the baseball analogy for “what inning” we’re in, the pitchers are still warming up, and enterprise is what Cisco does best.

Though hyperscalers have been driving AI growth across the industry, only one of the five trends Robbins highlighted was related to that group of companies. Chief Financial Officer Mark Patterson reinforced the point by breaking down the guidance raise, noting that a large share came from Cisco’s core, non-hyperscale business. “It’s not just campus,” he said. “It’s broad-based, and the core continues to do quite well.”

The market still views the enterprise as a slow-refresh business. The call made a strong case that it’s about to become the bigger part of Cisco’s AI story.

During the investor Q&A, a data point was worth highlighting. Robbins said that of the campus switching Cisco sold this quarter, “only 9% was really coming from the new portfolio.” Despite multiple quarters of strong campus growth, the installed base has barely moved to the next generation.

Two forces will push this base to accelerate an upgrade. One is agentic AI, which Robbins said is driving “modernization for bandwidth reasons” because agents generate traffic that campus networks were never designed to handle. The other is the security fallout from frontier models such as Anthropic’s Claude Mythos — the Mythos moment. “We’re beginning to hear from customers who are attributing orders to early Mythos implications,” Robbins said, noting that European service providers are upgrading managed customer premises equipment.

Robbins added that customers “got to get the architecture exactly right.” That suggests a long, deliberate redesign rather than a buying spree, which is characteristic of a multiyear cycle.

Cisco has long been criticized for building its own silicon as the industry shifted to merchant silicon. Robbins said that outside of optics, the hyperscale AI business is “100% Silicon One-based platforms.” The P200 continues to win scale-across deals, and the G300 is now in customer labs.

Asked why Cisco keeps winning those deals, Robbins kept it simple: “It’s really about performance and power. And there’s just this whole focus on diversity of silicon in the space.” The second point is the more strategic one. No hyperscaler wants a single supplier for any critical layer of its AI fabric, and Cisco is the credible second source.

The bigger long-term play is the enterprise. Silicon One is already shipping across parts of the enterprise portfolio, and Robbins said the target to convert all of it by fiscal 2029 “is still on track.” That gives Cisco a single silicon architecture from the hyperscale data center to the branch, with cost and roadmap control competitors can’t match.

The reality is that at Cisco’s volumes, it makes no sense to buy merchant silicon. Not only can it build it cheaper, but it can also optimize it specifically for its own features and capabilities.

A theme running through the call is that AI is decentralizing. “Our enterprise customers will be running AI applications in the cloud, but they’re also investing heavily in private data centers,” Robbins said. “They want to run open-weight and open-source models in their own data centers. They want to train on their own data in their own data centers.”

Sovereignty is driving in the same direction, with Cisco extending its Sovereign Critical Infrastructure portfolio from Europe to Canada and preannouncing India. Robbins noted that Cisco is “one of the few companies that has maintained a robust on-premises portfolio.” For years, analysts treated that on-premises focus as a liability. In a world where countries and regulated industries want operational control, it has become an advantage.

Equally important is what Cisco isn’t chasing. Robbins called neocloud exposure “relatively immaterial,” adding that “we’ve been very conservative relative to the credit situations.” That’s a disciplined choice. Cisco is growing into the safest parts of the AI build-out rather than financing its riskiest buyers.

Given the number of neoclouds, one could look at them as a massive pot of gold, but they often required vendor financing. If the neocloud isn’t successful, it’s the financing organization left holding the bag. This was the case with CLECs in the Internet days. Cisco was conservative then and will continue to be.

Security has been the weak spot in Cisco’s story, so this quarter’s progress matters. Robbins said core security orders, excluding Splunk, were “the best it’s been in three years,” with firewalls, Zero Trust secure access, and AI Defense all gaining customers. Patterson said Cisco is “a little bit above the expectations that we set out” and feels “good about turning the corner.”

The refresh cycle drives near-term numbers, but the strategic prize is trust for AI agents. Robbins said enterprise conversations now center on “agentic security, agentic identity, agentic observability, as well as the new security architecture needed for the AI era.” The network is where agent traffic flows, making it the natural enforcement point. Cisco owns more of that network than anyone.

Investor concerns received clear answers. On margins, Patterson said, “gross margins are pretty much right on target,” and on supply, “we’re seeing not a lot of issues at all.” Robbins added that Cisco is growing revenue “with less headcount than we had at the beginning” two years ago.

The number I’d watch most closely isn’t AI revenue, campus orders, or gross margin. It’s the growth of Cisco Cloud Control. Robbins mentioned it almost in passing, noting thousands of sign-ups and integrations with AI Canvas, Cisco IQ and Cisco Model Foundry. Most people will think of Cloud Control as a network management tool, but that badly understates its potential.

Cloud Control is now Cisco’s core product. The best analogy is VMware vCenter, the data center’s control point that turned a collection of products into a sticky platform. Cheaper, sometimes free hypervisors never made a dent in VMware’s share because the operational control gained from vCenter far outweighed the few bucks saved on hypervisor licenses. Cloud Control is broader and potentially more impactful because Cisco is positioning it as the control point for enterprise AI, not just the network through the following capabilities.

Cisco’s numerous network and security competitors have their own agentic operations platforms, and many beat Cisco to market. However, Cisco played the long game. In my conversations with Chief Product Officer Jeetu Patel, he has been emphatic about how important Cloud Control is to Cisco’s vision of shifting to a platform company. It enables customers to manage the underlying infrastructure, including third parties, through a single dashboard.

If Cloud Control becomes the enterprise IT console for managing AI-era infrastructure, Cisco stops competing on boxes and starts competing on control. Hardware cycles always ebb and flow, so always being best of breed is impossible. Whether this one becomes a transformation for Cisco depends on whether customers run their environments on Cloud Control. My advice to Cisco is to start disclosing active usage, partner integrations, and attach rates for the platform.

My advice to investors is to start asking for them. That number will tell you what Cisco is worth five years from now.

‘Boston Blue’ Brings Back a ‘CSI’ Legend, but Its Biggest Problem Remains

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Jeffrey is a freelance features writer at Collider. He is an MPA-accredited entertainment journalist and a Tomatometer-approved critic based in the LA area. He graduated from the University of Texas at Austin with a Bachelor of Science in Radio, TV, & Film and a Bachelor of Arts in Theater.

The hit CBS series Boston Blue just pulled off a huge coup in the casting department. Emmy Award-winning actress Marg Helgenberger, a television legend and veteran of shows like CSI, will return to the network in the hit Blue Bloods spin-off. Helgenberger will make her series debut in the upcoming Season 2 episode "Bloodlines" on November 13, portraying Gwen Silver, the estranged biological mother of main cast member Sarah Silver (Maggie Lawson). Helgenberger makes a great addition to the series and the overall Blue Bloods saga. However, despite the stupendous casting choice, the show's biggest problem still needs to be addressed.

Boston Blue serves as both a continuation and spin-off of its flagship series Blue Bloods. Much like its predecessor, it showcases a multi-generational family of law enforcers with the Silvers. The main connective tissue between the two shows is Blue Bloods veteran Danny Reagan (Donnie Wahlberg) and his son, Sean Reagan (Mika Amonsen, who took over the role from Andrew Terraciano for the new series). Danny uprooted his life to move to Boston to mentor Sean in his own career as a police officer, while Danny also receives a fresh start in a new city, seeking to climb the ladder in his career. Boston Blue struggles to find a balance between these two narratives.

A great detective is really just a personality with a method attached, which is why we keep remaking the same four of them. Here is yours, what it says about you, and the case for them being the best in the business.

You let people think you are not a threat and then you simply never go away. Columbo is the only major detective show that tells you who did it in the first ten minutes, because the pleasure was never the puzzle, it was watching a rich man slowly realise he is in trouble.

You are the most interesting person in the room and you know exactly how to use it. Rian Johnson built Blanc as a gentleman detective who is also an outsider everywhere he goes, which lets him be charming at people who would never otherwise talk to him.

Everybody tells you everything, and you have never once asked them to. Jessica Fletcher is a crime novelist from a small town in Maine who is so warm and so obviously trustworthy that suspects confess to her essentially as a social courtesy.

You expect to be lied to and you are almost never disappointed. Chandler wrote Marlowe as a man with a rigid private code in a city that has none, which is why he takes the case, solves it, gets nothing out of it and takes the next one.

As great as it is to see a multi-ethnic Jewish family celebrating Shabbat in a weekly broadcast television series, it's strange that the Reagans are constantly present for the dinners. The Reagans are Roman Catholics, and although they respect the Silvers' religious practices, they aren't Jewish themselves. In Blue Bloods, the Reagan family would occasionally invite guests to their Sunday dinners, but those were rare exceptions rather than the rule.

Danny and Sean appear to join the Silvers for Shabbat dinner on a weekly basis. It’s great that the Reagans and Silvers have a close friendship and bond, but they haven't known each other too long. Since they aren't related, it doesn’t make sense that they join the Silvers for Shabbat dinner all the time. Shouldn't the Reagans set up their own Sunday dinners instead? It comes across as if the writers are attempting to forcibly mimic the weekly family dinner dynamic from Blue Bloods in Boston Blue, where it makes less sense.

One area that Boston Blue appears to be working on in Season 2 is establishing the hierarchy of the BPD and City Hall, a narrative structure that the series has struggled with before. Thus far, the series has introduced the BPD's chief, Lanigan (Ron Lea), who has appeared sparingly, but the commissioner has yet to appear. Beginning with its first season, Blue Bloods did a much better job of demonstrating the hierarchy of everyone's roles in the NYPD, including Frank Reagan's (Tom Selleck) relationship with the mayor’s office, Erin working in the District Attorney's office, Danny operating as a detective, and Jamie Reagan (Will Estes) as a patrolman.

Across all these characters and subplots, the series presented a strong overview of the Reagans and their career dynamics; yet, in Boston Blue, it feels like we’re two seasons in and still playing catch-up. It derives from the series struggling as a continuation of Blue Bloods while trying to forge its own identity. Boston Blue shouldn't copy the weekly family dinners simply because the Blue Bloods made them famous. Although improvements have been made in Season 2, with storylines involving BPD’s superior officers, such as Chief Lanigan and Sean and Jonah's new sergeant, Betina Ryan (Sydney Ozerov-Meyer), it would improve the show to include additional storylines showcasing the command structure of the BPD, as well as Mae Silver's (Gloria Reuben) office and their relationship with Mayor Jim Shaw (Brian Goodman).

Boston Blue sometimes appears at odds with its format, trying to balance the traditions and characters of Blue Bloods to lure in longtime fans of the original series, while still maintaining strong narrative focus on its new characters and family. It's simply not necessary for the Silvers and Reagans to be joined at the hip every week. Separate dinners would work fine, and maybe Sean and Danny could have their own Sunday dinners with Sean's girlfriend, Penny (Xochitl Gomez), at some point? Also, why not bring Penny to New York City to introduce her to the Reagans, or have the entire family visit Boston to set up a Sunday dinner with Sean, Penny, and Danny?

Marg Helgenberger joining the show is a positive development because it helps flesh out Sarah Silver's character arc and her relationship with her mother, whom we've never seen before. As a multi-ethnic blended family, the Silver family presents a strong family dynamic that's unique to Blue Bloods, and the show should explore the family's distinctness more in future episodes. This will further reinforce Boston Blue's fresh new storylines, rather than trying to come across as Blue Bloods-lite or Blue Bloods: The Sequel.

Scott Bakula's role in Star Trek: Enterprise as Archer ushered in a new era for the franchise aboard the Enterprise NX-01, with the doggo Porthos being a somewhat out-of-place addition to the crew that completely changed the dynamic of the show and the captain's character.

Archer's relationship with Porthos brought a touching and wholesome element to the franchise, as the loyal beagle was a constant source of companionship for the captain through thick and thin, with their bond providing some of Enterprise's most endearing moments. Now, more than two decades after the series concluded, Scott Bakula's Captain Archer is officially returning to Star Trek this week for an overdue reunion with his adorable four-legged sidekick.

Wizards of the Coast has found an adorable way to translate Archer and Porthos' relationship into the new Magic: The Gathering crossover releasing this week. The new "Captain Jonathan Archer" card brings Scott Bakula's Enterprise captain back to the franchise, but easily the best part of the comeback is that his faithful canine companion automatically joins him.

Archer is a rare green 3/3 Legendary Creature costing three mana to play, including one green, but when he enters the battlefield, he creates Porthos, a legendary green 1/1 Dog creature token. For those unfamiliar with Magic, tokens are essentially game pieces created through other cards' abilities that serve as creatures, artifacts, or other permanents on the battlefield, functioning much like regular cards despite not being part of a player's main deck.

Porthos even has his own collectible token card, so players can actually reunite the adorable duo on the battlefield without having to draw and cast two separate characters. It's an incredibly fitting tribute to their relationship in Enterprise, where Porthos was Archer's constant companion (despite having no official Starfleet duties). Even better, the pair actually complement each other through Archer's second ability, Federation.

By spending one green mana and tapping Archer, players can give a creature they control +X/+X until the end of their turn, with X being the number of different creature types among their non-Borg creatures.

Archer counts as both a Human and Officer, while Porthos adds Dog to the mix. Together, they provide three distinct creature types, so Archer can actually turn his adorable 1/1 companion into a formidable 4/4 creature. It's a clever translation of the partnership and a great nod to longtime Enterprise fans.

Archer is included as part of the main Star Trek expansion (TRK), available through boosters, while Porthos is included as a separate collectible token card. The crossover also includes four themed Commander decks: Federation Fleet, Landing Party, Klingon Fury, and We Are the Borg, as well as unique scene cards featuring mosaic-style themed sets from across the franchise, such as Unimatrix Zero.

Magic: The Gathering x Star Trek officially releases Friday, November 13 to celebrate the franchise's 60th anniversary. However, it's worth noting that Wizards of the Coast has warned that certain products, including Commander decks and select bundles, are experiencing shipping delays and may arrive after the official launch.

Protein Startups Are Selling Lots Of Snacks And Drinks. Our Sample Tasting Found Most Neither Great Nor Horrible

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

In a perfect world, we would all eat meals and snacks that are convenient, taste great and provide optimal nutrition.

In reality, however, there are tradeoffs. We crave ice cream, chips and candy bars, which make carbs and fat easy to over-consume. Meeting protein goals, meanwhile, can require conscientious effort, particularly for those, like your author, who aren’t keen on eating lots of meat.

Fortunately, as we chronicled earlier this week, both startups and established food brands are making it simpler than ever to up protein intake. From sodas to snack bars to ice cream, it’s now possible to get a fix of this coveted macronutrient with foods and drinks more commonly associated with sugar, starch and fat.

The looming question is: Do they taste good? To find out, we hit up a local Target for a selection of protein foods sold by venture-backed startups. I and a 10-year-old then subjected them to a taste test.

The verdict? Most of this stuff is indeed edible and drinkable, albeit almost never nearly as tasty as the more protein-deficient alternatives, with just one exception.

No. 1: David Vanilla Bean Frozen Dessert: Anyone who buys or consumes this particular ice cream alternative will be doing so for the nutritional content, not the taste. A single pint packs in a whopping 30 grams of protein and has just 210 calories. The texture is also on point, creamy and easy to spoon, with a distinct vanilla flavor.

That said, you can’t hide the fact that this is not a product made with your typical ice cream ingredients. Those who don’t like artificial sweeteners or the flavor and texture of whey protein will want to pass. For myself and my co-taster, however, it was a reasonably appealing eating experience. Not as good as traditional ice cream, but not bad for a high-protein food.

Made by: Medici Brands (David parent company).

No. 2 SkyPop Protein Soda: The nutritional stats for SkyPop Protein Soda are also remarkable. A 12-ounce can has just 45 calories, with 10 grams of protein and a single gram of carbohydrates. It’s also properly fizzy, and makes a satisfying pop when you open the can.

But what about flavor? Our local store only had the Ice Pop flavor in stock and it was, in both tasters’ view, not horrible but not something we’d plan to buy regularly. I could see consuming it when on a clear fluids diet, as is the case before some medical procedures, as it’d be one of the few available sources of protein.

No. 3: Khloud Protein Popcorn: Unlike many of our other featured foods, Khloud Protein Popcorn isn’t a super protein-rich offering. A serving contains 7 grams of protein and 160 calories, most of which come from carbs and fat. Still, its protein count is higher than other packaged popcorn, which typically has maybe 2 protein grams in a comparable serving.

Overall, the tasters’ conclusion, from our trial of White Cheddar Khloud, was that this is a decent snack food. The flavor of milk protein and whey protein concentrate was palpable, but not overwhelming. It’s not exactly a snack food to savor, but for something to casually munch at one’s desk or while watching a show, it served its purpose.

No. 4 Mosh: In the third season of “The White Lotus”, Mosh co-founder Patrick Schwarzenegger plays a fitness and finance bro who makes a daily high-protein shake that other family members find revolting. Perhaps this was inspired by Mosh, the brain-supporting protein bar startup he launched with his mother, Maria Shriver.

The Mosh bar, which we purchased in the peanut butter cup flavor, crams 20 grams of protein, 6 grams of fiber, plus brain-supporting ingredients like creatine and lion’s mane, into a compact, shelf-stable bar. It’s an impressive feat. Yet while our brains’ may have appreciated the nutrition and supplement boost the Mosh bar provided, our taste buds decidedly did not.

I’d put Mosh in the functional food category. It’s something that you might eat if you wanted that particular nutrient mix. But it’s not something to consume for pleasure.

No. 5 Cadootz Crackers: We tried two varieties of Cadootz Crackers — cheddar and sea salt — and walked away neither enthused nor repulsed.

For people interested in a gluten- and seed oil-free small snack cracker with about 5 grams of protein per serving, Cadootz rises to the occasion. It’s got a nice crunch and decent flavor.

As someone who’s not particularly seeking to avoid gluten and seed oils or to get a modest protein bump from my crackers, I’ll probably pass on follow-on purchases. But for its target audience, Cadootz should serve well.

No. 6 Frozen One: For those who thought the David frozen dessert was a marvel of protein engineering, the folks at Frozen One would like a word. Their protein ice cream has a whopping 40 grams of protein in each pint.

Yes, but how does it taste? Not that bad, according to our two-person tasting team. We tried both the chocolate and apple pie varieties, preferring the former. Like the David offering, Frozen One tastes like a product made with milk protein concentrate and sugar substitutes. You won’t mistake it for Ben and Jerry’s. But if the goal is to consume a steak-like quantity of protein in the form factor of a pint of ice cream, it does its job.

No. 7 Brami Protein Pasta: We saved the best for last. That, unexpectedly, was Brami Protein Pasta, a noodle made from semolina and lupini beans that boasts about 70% more protein than standard pasta and more than double the fiber.

With about 12 grams of protein for a 200 calorie serving, Brami isn’t a dense protein source. But, with the right topping, it’s enough to transform a typically starchy noodle meal into something reasonably protein rich.

Most notably, however, Brami tastes quite good, with no discernable weird aftertaste or texture issues. I made both standard and Brami noodles to taste the difference. For seconds, I went with the Brami.
