Hosts got through to Airbnb on their gripes and Airbnb bent. But many of the policies that stoked host anger, such as a limit on guest checkout chores, a ban on contracts, and collecting guest IDs, remain in place.

Airbnb is giving hosts more control over their house rules after they pushed back on a policy, announced in October, that limits what they can ask of guests. Under a House Rules policy that was updated November 3, hosts can now set checkout rules guests are expected to follow, such as putting dirty dishes in the sink, and can require a separate rental agreement when their insurer demands one.

A general restriction on contracts between hosts and guests, however, and a prohibition against collecting guest IDs — both of which made hosts angry — remain in place.

The restrictions that drew the most attention in the mainstream media will still be in effect starting November 20: Hosts still won’t be able to ask guests to wash dishes or strip beds. Airbnb also laid out, for the first time, when it will review listings for compliance and how hosts can appeal.

Airbnb has partially walked back a House Rules policy announced in October after hosts objected publicly, but the core restrictions take effect as planned on November 20. Under the November 3 update, what Airbnb had called "Requests" — instructions guests were only "encouraged" to follow — are now "additional rules" that guests "are expected to comply with," covering items such as putting dishes in the sink, gathering used towels, and sorting recycling. Hosts can also require a separate rental agreement when an insurer demands one, alongside existing exceptions for laws, homeowners associations, and building rules. Airbnb published a review timeline for the first time: listing reviews begin November 20 and run through March 2027, with at least 20 days to fix a flagged rule and one appeal per rule, supporting documents allowed. The limits that drew the most attention remain: no checkout chores such as washing dishes or stripping beds, no host-imposed fines, no rules added after booking, and no collecting guest IDs, emails, phone numbers, or signed agreements absent a legal, building, or insurer requirement. Fantasy Football: Target share for Justin Jefferson with Kirk Cousins back, plus more key stats from Week 10

In fantasy football, you have to look back to move forward. Fantasy points are the goal, but how a player gets there is the key. Analyst Joel Smyth breaks down 8 numbers to know from Week 10 in order to win your matchup in Week 11 and beyond.

Touches for Emmett Johnson in Week 10, the fourth-most on the week. Johnson took full advantage of Kenneth Walker III being absent for the week, taking on 22 of the 24 RB carries and all seven of the RB targets. The rookie became a 1:1 replacement and a week winner for those who picked him up this weekend. He should be kept no matter the availability of Walker in Week 11, and can even be used as a flex next weekend, with Walker likely being limited against a Cardinals defense that will be significant underdogs in Kansas City.

This should be a wake-up call for the importance of fantasy insurance RBs late in the season. Bye weeks are largely behind us, the waivers are shrinking and the upside for top RB2s is clear. It was very well known that if Walker was out of the picture, even for a game, Johnson would become the favorite for workhorse volume. The rate of late-round running backs posting RB1 numbers doubles over the last month of the season, making now the next best time to pick one up for your bench.

Targets and routes for Josh Downs in his first game as a Kansas City Chief. It's a new day for the young WR, going from a struggling offense led by Daniel Jones to an immediate season-high performance on eight routes with Patrick Mahomes. Downs was not only the go-to guy when on the field, but also capitalized on his opportunities with four receptions, 53 yards and a touchdown.

The Chiefs brought in Downs to help as a desperately needed man-coverage beater, but also as a downfield target so far. Three of Downs' five targets were 10+ yards downfield, tying a team high in such targets out of the gate, catching all three. Rashee Rice will get a lot of the work around the line of scrimmage while Downs can continue to help Mahomes push the ball downfield after starting dead last in air yards per attempt. That usage wasn't working with Jones in Indianapolis; however, the QB upgrade often fixes that.

Targets per route for Chris Olave the last two weeks when Jordyn Tyson has been on the field. Before Tyson's return, his 25.9% targets per route was one of the best in the NFL, leading him to WR1 numbers. The two-week sample becomes more dangerous in my eyes with it not being just okay production, but dominant target volume in a WR's first two career games. This offense has been perfect for Olave because of the volume, rather than being a top-10 offense. Tyson being targeted on 38% of routes so far is a major problem, not that it will stay that extreme, but that his routes are still limited.

The Saints will have weeks where their high-volume passing attack will be enough to satisfy both, but there will be more days where Olave isn't saved by top-five WR volume as the Saints' offense struggles as a whole.

Snaps for Travis Etienne Jr. in his return. The playing time can certainly increase, but the three-RB committee looks strong and much tougher to overcome than simply one RB. Audric Estime still played a major role, leading the backfield with 27 snaps. Kamara was third, but did have four of the six red-zone snaps, and Etienne at least led in touches (11).  Even worse than the volume issue is the efficiency issue. The Panthers entered 31st against RBs, it can't get much better. The Saints helped that ranking improve on Sunday with 17 carries for 24 rushing yards. They had negative yards before contact, zero carries over five yards and combined for 59 scrimmage yards. Although the schedule will have some bright spots, the New Orleans backfield is likely to continue to be messy, splitting three ways in an offense that ranks 27th in rushing yards per game. ​

Target share for Justin Jefferson in his first game with Kirk Cousins this season. Jefferson benefitted from T.J. Hockenson and Jordan Addison being unavailable, but almost everything else was positive for future outlook. His 13 targets and nine receptions were immediate season highs, with Minnesota having its second-highest scoring game on the season, resulting in an expected 23.9 fantasy points, the fourth-highest on the week. The production as a team was there and should show up stronger in the fantasy box score soon. In terms of fit, it was a great start to the change.

Cousins loves to target receivers on in-breaking routes and over the middle of the field compared to Kyler Murray, and that showed immediately. Jefferson was targeted five times over the middle of the field, over twice as much as his average to begin the year, an area of his game that has lost the most value since Sam Darnold left town. The beginning of Cousins saving this offense and Jefferson's fantasy season look promising.

Of the Colts RB touches went to Jonathan Taylor on Sunday, lower than it's been on average the last few weeks. Not according to plan! What was expected was that now that the Colts have no reason to save Taylor, and they don't have a first-round pick to tank for, their star RB would go back to getting 85%+ of the workload. Seth McGowan thought otherwise. The RB split in Indianapolis stayed nearly the exact same as the previous few weeks, resulting in Taylor being vulture-picked in the red zone and posting a very modest fantasy performance given the opponent. This was Taylor's best opportunity the rest of the season, and was largely wasted without the high-end RB volume we're used to seeing. He's talented enough to overcome low volume, or a low offense, but not many can do both. A road matchup versus a Texans' run defense on Thursday Night Football will be a much more difficult task at hand.

Targets for Trey McBride on Carson Beck's 18 passing attempts Sunday, his lowest in a game since 2024. The worry wasn't necessarily that Beck wouldn't target McBride as often, but that a high target share wouldn't equal high targets. McBride's 28% target share is pretty normal for the fantasy superstar; it just doesn't work on only 34 offensive plays with 11 completions. The Cardinals aren't just on their third-string QB, but their WR5 is the new WR1, their offensive line is banged up, their top-five drafted RB is on IR and it's showing on the scoreboard. After taking five sacks, Beck only completed a pass on 45% of his dropbacks, much below Jacoby Brissett's 58%, or even Gardner Minshew's No. 1 rate in a small sample. The volume will come back at times when the offense can sustain more drives, but it's just the start of the issues, with scoring upside and receiving efficiency also raising concerns.

The snap differential between Zach Charbonnet and Jadarian Price in Week 10, favoring Charbonnet. After a 50-50 split the last two weeks, the veteran made a real leap over the rookie, resulting in 2x as many touches (20-10). When Price was on the field, he was very involved in the offense, touching the ball on 10 of his 15 snaps; it's just hard to get much more when you're barely on the field. The Seahawks relied on Charbonnet in the passing game, running 15 routes versus Price's six, and turned their first-round pick into a worse version of 2025 Kenneth Walker III. Yes, he got the TD, but the score only resulted in 10.9 points and modest hope for future volume. Price has averaged under 10 fantasy PPG since returning, with the worst part being that it was versus a very friendly schedule. Upcoming, the Seahawks have the bye week before San Francisco, the No. 2 team against rushing fantasy points.

The acting director of the National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases, John Powers III, will become the permanent director, the agency announced Monday.

NIAID has been without a permanent director since March 2025, when the Trump administration ousted Jeanne Marrazzo. Powers has been serving in an acting capacity since June. A physician-scientist, Powers has worked with NIAID since 1999 in several roles focused on clinical research.

The second Trump administration has brought a renewed spotlight to the infectious disease institute, in large part because of the president’s ire for former director Anthony Fauci, and because of unsubstantiated accusations that research funded by the institute in China played a role in creating the SARS-CoV-2 virus. It has also seen tumult in its leadership. After Marrazzo was ousted, Jeffery Taubenberger was appointed acting director. He unexpectedly stepped down, and several other leaders at NIAID were reassigned to other institutes or asked to resign.

You'd think something like Super Smash Bros. would be easy to sell. It's Pikachu, Link and Mario fighting each other – what's not to love? Well, creator Masahiro Sakurai has revealed it was a difficult pitch because of his platformer-inspired vision of the gameplay.

The longtime figurehead of the Smash Bros series recalled the conversations for Denfamicogamer. "Think about if Smash Bros. had normal fighting game rules," he says, translated by Nintendo Everything. "In a one-on-one battle, you'd have guard, attack, and special attacks, and once your energy is depleted, you're KOed and the match is over. I don’t think the original Smash Bros. would have been released like that."

He notes that if it had shipped with mechanics similar to Street Fighter or Tekken, it would've been seen as a "novelty" and not lasted the way it has. "I made the game confidently believing that the ingenuity of Smash Bros. and gameplay hooks of elements such as damage accumulation and positioning would be fun," he recalls.

"At the time though, it was criticized by people who couldn’t get into the differences it had from Street Fighter 2 and Virtua Fighter," he says.

I can believe it. The original Super Smash Bros. stood out like a sore thumb in the fighting game landscape. The series still does, to an extent, but between its own legacy and a number of clones and pretenders, it's not as singular within the industry.

Sakurai then doubles down on the trickiness of selling the core ideas internally. "But it’s actually very difficult to 'intentionally add ingenuity,'" he adds. "When there are no previous examples, it’s very difficult to explain to staff, and it’s also very important - and difficult - to think about how to aim for and proceed towards what we want."

At least we know he was successful. Craving your own path can be hard, especially when it involves pitting beloved characters against each other in a novel way.

Guy Ritchie & Jason Statham's Best Movie Together Is Officially Streaming For Free

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Guy Ritchie is a beloved director with an expansive filmography. He's arguably best known for his crime stories and stylish approach to storytelling. Those crime tales feature larger-than-life characters, with slick writing that's often endlessly quotable.

Lock, Stock & Two Smoking Barrels is a great example of the formula, and feels like the blueprint for several other Guy Ritchie projects. The movie is about four friends who lose a rigged poker game. They end up owing a massive gambling debt to Hatchet Harry (P.H. Moriarty), with just a week to pay it back.

While the movie does focus on how the friends intend to pay off their debt, Ritchie takes the time to flesh out so many characters. Rory Breaker (Vas Blackwood), for example, plays more of a supporting role, but is also responsible for one of the funniest speeches in the entire movie.

Lock, Stock & Two Smoking Barrels is also a remarkable project for Jason Statham, as it was his feature film debut. Since then, Statham has appeared in numerous other Guy Ritchie movies, along with dozens of other projects with other directors. He's widely regarded as a modern action star, and Snatch, arguably his best movie directed by Ritchie, is now free to stream on Pluto TV.

Jason Statham played Bacon in Lock, Stock & Two Smoking Barrels. He is a main character and one of the four friends who lost in Hatchet Harry's poker game. Statham also stars in Ritchie's 2000 crime caper, Snatch. He plays Turkish, an illegal boxing promoter working alongside his close friend, Tommy (Stephen Graham).

The movie has the same storytelling style as Lock, Stock & Two Smoking Barrels, with several tales intertwining into something far grander. Turkish and Tommy run into trouble when their prizefighter is injured. Unfortunately for the duo, they had a fight arranged with another promoter and feared crime boss, Brick Top (Alan Ford). He's furious the fight can no longer take place, and fully capitalizes on the situation.

Turkish and Tommy are at the center of the story, but they are just one part of what makes Snatch a classic. Vinnie Jones was also in Lock, Stock & Two Smoking Barrels, and returns as Bullet Tooth Tony. His story is only loosely connected to the illegal boxing plot, but he remains an unforgettable character, responsible for some ridiculously over-the-top speeches.

Lock, Stock & Two Smoking Barrels is an adored crime caper. It has such a well-crafted story that even non-crime fans will find something to enjoy, but Snatch is arguably even better. The smaller stories in Snatch fit together masterfully, and while Turkish and Tommy are the main characters, they're only part of what makes the movie so charming.

Another major element of Snatch is a diamond heist that occurs at the start of the movie. The diamond ends up in London, but its arrival also draws the attention of mobsters like Boris the Blade (Rade Sherbedgia). The dialogue throughout the entire movie is arguably wittier and funnier than its predecessor, and no character in the extensive cast feels underutilized.

There are plenty of serious moments, but some of the sharp-tongued exchanges are exceptional. Brick Top, in particular, is a treasure trove of quotes, and some of his speeches are arguably among the best quotes in Guy Ritchie's entire filmography.

Lock, Stock, and Two Smoking Barrels will always deserve a special mention for its originality. Snatch feels like it builds on the proven formula. The story is more complex, yet it's not convoluted. Many of the characters are wonderfully grandiose, and somehow still convincing. The soundtrack will always be a personal preference, but several music choices really are perfect in Snatch. This is particularly true for the climactic boxing match, as "Fin' in the Bushes" by Oasis leads us nicely to one of the greatest characters in the entire movie.

Without delving too deep into spoilers, Brad Pitt plays Mickey, a wild traveler in Snatch. Turkish sends Tommy to meet Mickey and complete a transaction for a new caravan. The deal goes south, but the duo discovers that Mickey is actually a bare-knuckle boxing champion. They want him to fight for them, but he's unpredictable and will only play along for a price.

Pitt's Mickey is unforgettable in Snatch. He certainly looks tough with his shirt off, but he steals every scene he's in with some of the funniest dialogue in the entire movie. His accent is over the top, and he's purposefully difficult to understand.

Best of all, Pitt seems to be enjoying playing the character. His mannerisms are believable for such an over-the-top character, and he's so likable because audiences never know what he'll do next. Mickey also becomes embroiled in Turkish and Tommy's situation with Brick Top, and his chemistry with the rest of the cast is exceptional. He's arguably the biggest star in the movie, with an incredible screen presence, and a perfect actor for the role.

Since Lock, Stock & Two Smoking Barrels, Jason Statham has become a staple of sorts in Guy Ritchie movies. Statham starred in 2005's Revolver after Snatch. He also starred in Wrath of Man in 2021, a notably more serious, gritty revenge movie, wherein Statham plays H, a mysterious man of few words who starts working for a security truck company. It's a high-risk job, but H proves deadly efficient. He also has a unique motivation for joining that we learn about as the story unfolds.

Most recently, Statham teamed up with Ritchie for 2023's Operation Fortune: Ruse de Guerre. The movie is a spy thriller, starring Statham as Orson, an elite agent. The story is notably less complex than Snatch, for example, but still boasts a brilliant cast, including Josh Hartnett and Aubrey Plaza. Hugh Grant is also unforgettable as Greg, a powerful arms dealer.

Audiences who enjoy Ritchie and Statham's collaborations will have to wait until January 2028 for their next fix. Details on their upcoming movie are scarce, but there are already some details about Viva La Madness to get excited about. Statham is reportedly the lead, and Vinnie Jones is also part of the cast alongside Camila Mendes and Jason Isaacs.

Interestingly, Viva La Madness is a crime novel written by J.J. Connolly. That novel is also a sequel to Layer Cake, which had a movie adaptation in 2004, starring Daniel Craig. Both novels are about a London criminal called X, though it's unclear whether the upcoming Viva La Madness movie will be connected to Layer Cake. Regardless, the plot and setting alone should be enough to get fans of Snatch and Lock, Stock & Two Smoking Barrels excited, especially given Jason Stathan and Guy Ritchie's involvement.

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Crystal Palace are Thomas Frank's third London club after previous spells with Brentford and Tottenham

"When the time is right I will look forward to my return as a manager, ready to embrace the job with great energy and dedication," said Thomas Frank in June.

That time is now after the Dane was confirmed as the new manager of Crystal Palace, who are two points and a goal difference deficit away from safety at the bottom of the Premier League.

Former boss Pierre Sage was sacked last week after just 16 league and cup games.

The role offers Frank an opportunity to rebuild his reputation after he was sacked by Tottenham in February after eight months.

Frank described it as "an honour" to be "in charge of this fantastic football club" as Palace announced his arrival.

"Crystal Palace is an iconic club in England and in the Premier League," Frank told Palace's website.

"I will do everything I possibly can to help and support the players and everyone around the place, and I'm really looking forward to meeting them."

Palace chairman Steve Parish said Frank was the "perfect fit for our club".

But how big is the challenge at Selhurst Park that awaits the man who made his name at Brentford?

"It's going to be a bit of a shock for Thomas Frank," former West Ham and England goalkeeper Rob Green tells BBC Sport.

"He had his success at Brentford - a very well-run club where players are brought in with a lot of thinking and planning to take over the players they sell for profit.

"They lose players and then have a mad scramble to get players in. They have a team that has been decimated by sales and not replaced by the same quality.

"Thomas Frank needs to find something within that team that builds and garners the belief that means that they can compete in the Premier League, because it feels a long way short of that right now."

Will Frank prove the right fit for Palace and haul the Eagles out of the relegation zone?

Since getting the sack at Tottenham after 26 league games last season, Frank has been out of management longer than he was in charge of Spurs.

In the summer he released a statement in which he said "there have been conversations and opportunities" to return to management, but that he had "decided not to rush into the next role".

Frank's name was heavily linked with Palace before they went for a head coach with no Premier League experience to replace Oliver Glasner - the man who delivered an FA Cup, a Conference League title and Community Shield before moving to Nottingham Forest.

"Palace had one of the best managers they have had, if not forever, silverware coming across the doorstep. Look at what they achieved last season and instead of building on that, they dismantled a team," said former Tottenham and England goalkeeper Paul Robinson.

Frank, who was part of BBC Sport's World Cup punditry team during the summer, will be coming into the job refreshed and with renewed energy and enthusiasm following several months out of the game.

Between his appointment as Brentford boss in October 2018 and the sack at Tottenham earlier this year, he managed without a break for seven years and four months.

Spending the past few months away from Premier League pressure has allowed Frank, in his own words, to "study, observe and learn from other leaders both within sport and beyond".

Will his positivity and passion rub off on a Palace team who have lost seven times in 10 top-flight matches?

Frank, who places a major emphasis on set-piece organisation, will have to hit the ground running at Palace, who have lost their past four league games.

With the Premier League season just 10 games old, Frank certainly has time to turn it around and will have targets in mind when the transfer window opens in January.

Sandisk Is Splitting Its Stock 8-for-1. Here’s the Number I’d Check Before Buying.

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Sandisk’s profit margin matters more to the investment case than its new share count.

A stock split always generates buzz. So, when Sandisk (SNDK -6.90%) recently announced an 8-for-1 stock split, investors cheered.

Yet while stock splits can make headlines, investors may want to focus on another figure -- its gross margin -- rather than its new, lower stock price. Here's why.

First, let's talk about stock splits. While they may seem exotic, they don't actually change anything fundamental about the business. The best analogy is culinary: If you take a whole, uncut pizza and cut it into eight slices, you've done nothing to change how much food you have. You've simply divided it up.

Stock splits work the same way. Fundamentals, like revenue, net income, and free cash flow, do not change when a company splits its shares. When Sandisk shares split on Nov. 20, no one receives anything for free; they will simply see what they already own split according to the 8-for-1 principle.

For example, say you own 10 shares of Sandisk today. As of Nov. 13, each share trades for around $858, for a total market value of $8,580. After the split, someone who owned 10 shares will own 80 shares, and each share will drop to $107.25 (assuming the price stays steady between now and then). The total market value of 80 shares at $107.25 per share? $8,580, the same as before.

Granted, the price of Sandisk shares will fluctuate between now and Nov. 20, but the principle remains the same. The market value of shares going into Nov. 20 will still equal the market value after the split; the only thing that will change is the share price.

Rather than focusing on the stock split, I think investors should take a closer look at Sandisk's gross margins. Gross margin is the profit remaining after deducting the cost of making a product.

Imagine Sandisk sells a memory chip for $100. If it took $15 to physically assemble the product, its gross margin on that product would be 85%. Bear in mind that the company doesn't get to keep the full $85 – it still has administrative and research costs, not to mention taxes.

However, gross margins are useful because they strip away these costs, allowing investors to see how profitable a business is without them. Sandisk's gross margins exploded since 2024, increasing from near 10% to around 85%.

SNDK Gross Profit Margin (Quarterly) data by YCharts

However, the big question is whether the company can maintain these incredible margins, or whether those margins will come crashing down.

For Sandisk, its gross margins are driven more by the price customers are willing to pay than by the input costs for its products. Therefore, it may be helpful to think of Sandisk's business like a hotel. The same rooms are far more profitable at $400 per night than at $150 per night, with many of the hotel's costs remaining steady.

Similarly, the supply-and-demand dynamics of the memory market have an enormous effect on Sandisk's gross margins. If memory demand were to pull back, perhaps as a result of slowing AI infrastructure investment, Sandisk's gross margins could tumble. On the other hand, if the company's long-term memory contracts prove durable, Sandisk's gross margins may remain resilient.

Stock splits alone do not make a stock an attractive investment. The split changes the unit size; profits determine the investment's appeal.

As a result, Sandisk must maintain its impressive gross margins in future quarters in order to justify its appeal. The stock already trades at a price-to-sales (P/S) ratio of 4.6. That's down significantly from its peak of over 25 in June, but it remains far above its 2025 average multiple of around 1.7.

The market has already priced a major step into Sandisk's profitability. If that fall doesn't materialize, the stock could be primed for a move higher. That's why I'm intrigued by Sandisk's gross margins, not its upcoming stock split.
