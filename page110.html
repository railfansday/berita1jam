An analyst made a deep cut to his price target on the coal company.

By Eric Volkman – Nov 18, 2026 at 8:09PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Peabody Energy ›

Investors struggled to find the energy to be positive about Peabody Energy (BTU -1.45%) on Wednesday. An analyst tracking the storied coal company made a significant cut to his price target, which dampened sentiment toward the stock. Its shares closed the day nearly 1.5% lower.

That move came before the market open and was made by Freedom Broker's Vitaly Kononov. He now believes Peabody stock is destined for $22 per share, down considerably from his former level of $30.

Despite the deep cut, Kononov remained bullish on the coal company's future, as he maintained his buy recommendation.

According to reports, the prognosticator's new take centered around Centurion, Peabody's flagship metallurgical coal mine in Australia. Kononov noted that the production outlook for the play has been cut three times so far this year. The analyst also considers coal prices to be vulnerable.

Centurion is rightfully considered to be Peabody's most important play, so how it develops will make a major difference to the company's results. What I'd be nervous about if I were a company shareholder is the future of the energy industry, an important client base for Peabody's thermal coal business, given Peabody's continued (and deep) involvement in the segment. For numerous reasons, the energy sector has become greener over the years, and I think that's a permanent shift.

I'm bearish on coal companies generally for this reason, and as such I'm bearish on Peabody.

When Warren Buffett talks, most savvy investors listen. Last year, in his now-annual Thanksgiving letter, he said:

Our stock price will move capriciously, occasionally falling 50% or so as has happened three times in 60 years under present management.

Don't despair; America will come back and so will Berkshire shares.

Buffett was at the helm of Berkshire Hathaway (BRKA -1.71%) (BRKB -2.07%) for about 60 years, and grew the company by a remarkable annual average of nearly 20% over that period. Now 96 years old, he has since stepped away from that top job and is chairman emeritus.

20 Most Universally Loved TV Couples

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

It's astonishing that romance series are considered niche, as almost everyone has opinions on the couples in the shows they watch. In fact, some of the most spirited TV discussions come from arguing about the best romance in a given series. It's been over 20 years since Buffy the Vampire Slayer ended, yet the Buffy-and-Angel vs. Buffy-and-Spike debates still rage on. Meanwhile, Gilmore Girls fans are no closer to reaching a consensus on Team Dean, Team Jess, or Team Logan than they were when the show aired in the 2000s.

But as fun as it is to put ourselves into staunch TV romance camps, it's just as wonderful when the stars align, and everyone can universally agree on small-screen pairings that are absolutely magical. This is no easy task — these relationships need enough ups and downs to keep audiences hooked, but not so toxic that they turn viewers off.

This can also change over time. Audiences who once eagerly tuned in to watch Ross and Rachel or Jim and Pam's relationship unfold have since reevaluated the couples and their romantic storylines as dysfunctional and grating. For a couple to be truly universally loved, they must still make their fans swoon.

The longest-running will-they, won't-they couple in television history, Olivia Benson and Elliot Stabler have been making audiences swoon for almost 30 years, all without ever having kissed — though they memorably came so close in Law & Order: SVU season 24. First meeting as partners in New York's Special Victims Unit, Benson and Stabler had instant rapport and crackling on-screen chemistry. Over the years, their loyalty and love for each other have only strengthened as they prove that sometimes the slowest-burning relationships are the hottest.

Most universally loved TV couples are popular because they're between two lovable people, but Blair and Chuck showed that you can still be a terrible person and have audiences root for your romance, because what's really important is being right for each other. Blair and Chuck are a perfect match in every way, from their raw ambition to their unbridled, animalistic mutual desire. One of Gossip Girl's harshest realities is that every character is actually awful, but these two scheming lovebirds make no apologies for it.

Forbidden love is always titillating, and it doesn't get much more forbidden than falling for a priest. However, while audiences may have been first hooked on the taboo element of Fleabag's season 2 romance, what really makes this relationship special is how vulnerable she and Hot Priest are with each other, thanks to their instant trust and connection. Going their separate ways in Fleabag's ending is so beautifully bittersweet because although they aren't together, their lives have been changed forever from having experienced the kind of love that they shared.

Willow and Tara were one of the first long-term Sapphic couples in television history, but their Buffy the Vampire Slayer romance is about so much more than breaking barriers. In a show full of toxic romances, these two stood out for forming a connection based on genuine warmth, affection, and empathy. Though this wasn't always maintained, as Willow's magic addiction temporarily tore them apart, it's clear that they belonged together, making Tara's "bury your gays" death one of the grimmest moments TV has ever seen.

Grand romantic gestures and tumultuous love triangles certainly make for great TV, but Janine and Greg show that in matters of the heart, it's often the little things that make the biggest difference, whether that's in drawing a couple together or keeping them apart. The Abbott Elementary characters' slow-burn road to romance was paved with small acts of kindness, like Greg being the only one to notice and compliment Janine's new hair, while their reasons for not leaping into a relationship were also grounded, like Janine needing time to be single as an adult. Ultimately, it all served to make their eventual, inevitable coupling feel earned.

Opposites-attract couples always have a fun frisson, but they carry extra emotional weight in teen dramas, where stepping outside your social circle and status is even more high-stakes. However, Seth and Summer's romance was so much deeper than just another nerd wooing the prom queen.

Teen dramas have always dominated television, but there are only a handful of titles that remain truly iconic in the age of modern streaming.

The O.C. characters' witty banter was bar none, and while it kept audiences entertained, what really made them root for these two as a couple was that they never stopped growing. Their wedding in The O.C.'s series finale was not only romantic but earned after four seasons of consistently being better for each other.

Parks and Rec is full of great couples, with pairings like Leslie and Ben or Ann and Chris also worthy candidates as one of TV's most universally loved couples. However, there's something even more special about two very different yet equally odd people not only appreciating but loving each other for their eccentricities. Because of this, April and Andy are free to be themselves, and their relationship is a wonderfully weird playground full of goofy inside jokes and alter egos. While they never take life too seriously, April's black-cat cynicism and Andy's golden-retriever enthusiasm help balance each other out.

Whereas many of TV's most famous LGBTQ+ couples have defining moments of tragedy, David Rose and Patrick Brewer are an ultimate celebration of queer joy. Though their relationship is hardly problem-free, it's always rooted in true love and pure happiness. Together, they are grounded and authentic, while their romantic moments, from Patrick publicly serenading David to their mountaintop engagement, are simply the best.

It's one thing to be a happy, healthy couple in the world of a low-stakes sitcom; it's another to maintain such a relationship when crises and trauma are an almost daily occurrence. However, Randall and Beth Pearson's marriage not only holds steadfast throughout six seasons of This Is Us, but it's a consistent bright spot in the tearjerking drama series. Throughout fostering and adopting a child, mental health struggles, and major career upheavals, the couple may experience friction, but they ultimately never lose their sense of playfulness, fizzy banter, or love for each other.

While Ross and Rachel were kept in perpetual will-they, won't-they purgatory throughout Friends' 10-season run, Monica and Chandler had a much more organic yet entertaining path from friends to lovers. What started as a shocking London hookup twist in season 4 grew into one of sitcom history's best partnerships thanks entirely to Courteney Cox and Matthew Perry's chemistry. Monica and Chandler experience huge struggles and don't always see eye to eye, but once they get together, there is never a question of whether they'll break up because it's clear that their love is so strong.

Avengers: Doomsday's New Runtime Report Is Disappointing For 1 Key Reason

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Avengers: Doomsday's runtime has reportedly been confirmed, and it comes with a disappointing update for one key reason. Fans have been waiting for a new Avengers movie for seven years. Honestly, the Marvel Cinematic Universe dropped the ball by not having an Avengers film in Phase 4, as that would have set up the Multiverse Saga in a major way, making new heroes important.

As it stands, several heroes introduced during the latest chapter of the shared universe have only appeared once so far and/or not interacted with characters from other corners of the MCU. Instead of making a smaller, controversial TV show out of the Secret Invasion storyline, Marvel could have easily used it for a grounded and intense Avengers movie to establish a new team.

That did not happen, but the MCU is now headed to one of the biggest events ever with the release of Avengers: Doomsday in theaters on December 18, a month from now. The upcoming Marvel movie not only features Robert Downey Jr.'s Doctor Doom in a major way, but also brings back heroes from all corners of the franchise, and even includes players from Fox's X-Men universe and other live-action Marvel franchises.

Avengers: Doomsday is huge, destined to eclipse the $1 billion mark at the global box office, and all eyes are on how the MCU movie will pull off, including so many moving parts. To that end, a lot of fans have been getting their hopes up when it comes to Avengers: Doomsday's runtime, but a new report comes as a hard blow.

According to Omelete, Avengers: Doomsday has a runtime of 2 hours, 29 minutes, and 36 seconds in its standard version. As such, the new Marvel movie clocks in at 149 minutes, which is a match for 2018's Avengers: Infinity War, but falls below Avengers: Endgame's 185 minutes, aka over three hours. This is a somewhat disappointing development, as Avengers: Doomsday is such a massive event and has so many moving parts that it merits having a runtime of around three hours to make sure every character arc is as fleshed out as it can be and there are plenty of engaging fight sequences.

After all, this is not only the first Avengers movie of the Multiverse Saga, but it will have to deal with explaining the multiverse's collapse and feature several teams interacting for the first time, including the Fantastic Four, the Avengers, the New Avengers, the X-Men, and more. Finally, Doctor Doom's origin story and motivations will also need to be explained by the MCU movie, given the little build-up to the character beforehand, as will the return of Chris Evans' Steve Rogers, which an Avengers: Endgame Encore post-credits scene already began to explain. Doomsday is a huge movie, but its runtime falls below Eternals and others.

The most likely explanation for why Avengers: Doomsday's runtime clocks in under three hours is that the MCU is following the formula that resulted in its biggest Avengers movie wins. At the end of the Infinity Saga, Avengers: Infinity War was a more fast-paced and action-packed movie than 2019's Avengers: Endgame, and so it did not need to have as big a runtime as the latter. Given the urgency of the multiverse's collapse and how different heroes are trying to save their respective Earths, it makes sense for it to be fast-paced and pack a lot into its runtime.

Why The World's Debut Boeing 747 Was Built In Half The Time Of Other Aircraft Of Its Size In 1968

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The Boeing 747 has earned its title as one of the most iconic airliners ever built for several reasons. It was the first widebody aircraft, easily distinguishable by a humped second deck, a key enabler in opening up aviation to the masses, and it enjoyed roughly fifty years of production. But equally as impressive as the Queen of the Skies itself is how it came about.

Boeing took the 747 from the drawing board to rollout remarkably quickly. That is, notably faster than the Airbus A380 program took to churn out its first model after officially launching, and quicker than both Boeing and Airbus took with their latest widebodies. What is more, Boeing pulled it off decades ago in the late 1960s.

Just 29 months separated the 747 project’s official launch and the rollout of the first model. Boeing announced on April 13, 1966, that it would build the 490-seat passenger transport that would come to be known as the 747. Exactly two years, five months, and 17 days later, on September 30, 1968, the first 747-100 ceremoniously rolled off the production line at a new purpose-built assembly plant in Everett, Washington.

Less than five months later, on February 9, 1969, the aircraft took off for its maiden flight. Test pilots Jack Waddell and Brien Wygle, accompanied by flight engineer Jess Wallick, took the controls, and the jumbo jet passed the major test with flying colors, paving the way for its introduction into service just under a year later in January 1970.

All told, that saw the Queen of the Skies go from a mere concept to carrying passengers in under four years. While that is no doubt a rapid turnaround by today’s standards, the reasons behind the pace are plentiful and clear-cut.

Behind it all was keen interest from Pan American World Airways, which had set out to source a jet two and a half times larger than the existing Boeing 707 against the backdrop of an increasingly busy market. Though the industry was mulling whether supersonic flight would come to dominate in the years ahead, there remained appeal for a huge, versatile airliner that could handle cargo operations rather than passenger services in the future, if needed.

As it turned out, Boeing had already looked into something similar, having lost out in a 1965 military transport design competition to the Lockheed C-5 Galaxy. Although the 747 was ultimately largely built from scratch, research into high-bypass engine technology and the thinking behind the 747’s distinctive hump can be traced back to the military project bid. As such, when Pan Am head Juan Trippe and then-Boeing president William Allen met that same year for initial discussions about the 747, something of a basis was already there.

It was Trippe himself who is said to have pushed so heavily for the 747. He had spent years beforehand attempting to open up aviation to the masses, overseeing the introduction of the world’s first economy fare in 1958, for instance. Given that an ambitious project like the 747 would only aid in reaching that goal, Trippe reportedly told Allen in 1965: “If you’ll build it, I’ll buy it.” By April 1966, the commitment was solidified through a $525 million deal — worth almost $5.5 billion today — for 25 of the new jumbo jets. This set an ambitious deadline of 1969 for the first delivery, though.

The 747 transcended aviation and became an iconic silhouette.

Nonetheless, Boeing did not waste any time in dwelling on the task at hand. After signing the contract with Pan Am, the design team of just a few hundred workers conceptualizing and writing up requirements for the 747 ballooned to 4,500 almost instantaneously. These workers, who would later become part of a group known as The Incredibles, were led by Joe Sutter, who would similarly earn the title of the Father of the 747 over time.

Sutter himself enjoyed a 40-year-long career at Boeing, although he recalled in a 2013 interview fearing for his job as he and his team rushed to get the 747 ready. Without public backing, Boeing found itself racking up debt on the back of the project, leaving Sutter under pressure to cut jobs. Instead, he requested hundreds more engineers. “I figured that was the day I lost my job at Boeing,” he said, “but I went to work the next day, and nobody said anything, so I kept working."

According to Boeing, some 50,000 employees ultimately ended up working on the jumbo jet over the course of its development cycle. Armed with an eye-watering 75,000 drawings, physical assembly of the first model got underway in September 1967, a year prior to its initial presentation to the world and only 16 months before the first flight. All of this, of course, required a specialized facility for production to actually be carried out.

Such a factory did not exist when Boeing first unveiled plans for the 747, so it had to be built almost in conjunction with the first model. Among the battles Sutter fought in his bid to steer the project to a timely conclusion was one over where his team would be based. Faced with the proposal of being sent to California, Sutter argued against it and insisted his engineers stay close to where those who would eventually build the aircraft would work.

This turned out to be Everett, which was far from Boeing’s first choice to house the 747 program initially, but prevailed on similar fears that key personnel would not relocate in great numbers to a new facility elsewhere. Regardless of where the 747 would be built, a new building would have to be constructed to accommodate such a vast aircraft and all the equipment needed to build it.

Under the tight deadline set by the deal with Pan Am, Boeing set about clearing forest and then building the facility in 1967. Work began just before assembly of the first 747 started, but the two efforts overlapped, meaning the prototype was under construction in an incomplete factory for a period. When the former was eventually completed, it took the crown as the largest building in the world by volume — a title it retains to this day and a reflection of the mammoth undertaking by Boeing to get the project off the ground.

The original designs for the Boeing 747 were made with a different purpose in mind. Find out how the 747 came to be as we know it today.

Naturally, such an effort required vast financial backing, and it took a monumental amount of risk on Boeing’s part to get the first 747 successfully turned around at such a pace. Boeing had secured $525 million — or $21 million per aircraft — under its deal with Pan Am. This covered barely half of the program's total $1 billion costas it transpired; however, adjusted for inflation, it would exceed $10 billion today.

As a result, Boeing faced financial pressure and was forced to turn to the banks, which explains Sutter being asked to cut engineers midway through the program. He said in 2013 that “it was a big gamble,” adding, “I think the only reason that it happened is that Bill Allen was a forward-seeking guy, and Juan Trippe from Pan Am also had that characteristic.”

Despite racking up debt, the gamble paid off, and the 747 was a resounding success. Not only did the first model get built and delivered on time, but the factory Boeing built for the jumbo jet has enjoyed decades of service since. Some 1,574 747s rolled out of the site over the life of the program, culminating in the delivery of a final 747-8 Freighter variant to Atlas Air in 2023. Notably, this saw production of the 747 end just a year before that of its primary challenger, the A380, despite its initial introduction decades earlier.

The A380 is an impressive feat of engineering in its own right. The industry moved in a different direction, and while Boeing hit a sweet spot with the 747, the appetite for huge quad jets was by no means as strong as it was at the A380’s commercial entry in 2007. All told, this saw the 254th and final A380 completed in 2021.

Airbus grappled with a string of delays between officially launching the A380 in December 2000, unveiling a prototype in January 2005, and then making the initial delivery to Singapore Airlines in 2007. How severely these impacted the A380’s long-term success is up for debate. Either way, the A380’s stretched timeline offers some context for how impressive Boeing’s effort was in getting the 747 up and running so quickly.

And it is not just the A380. Boeing itself took three years and six months to show off its first 777 after launching the program in October 1990. Its 787, despite going from concept to prototype in three years and three months, then took an additional two years and five months to first take off in December 2009. As for Airbus, the A350 and its answer to the clean-sheet 787 went from launch to initial rollout in six years and five months. Similarly, the 777X project took more than five years after launching in 2013 to produce a prototype, with certification still not secured today.

10 stocks we like better than Kimberly-Clark ›

Investors often seek stability in market giants that dominate daily life. Deciding between a consumer essentials powerhouse like Kimberly-Clark (KMB -0.84%) and a global restaurant titan like McDonald's (MCD -0.65%) requires weighing income against growth.

Kimberly-Clark provides essential hygiene products that consumers buy regardless of the economic climate. McDonald's leverages a massive franchise model to generate consistent cash flow through the fast-food market. Comparing these two involves looking at how defensive positioning in household goods stacks up against the high-margin, scalable nature of the global restaurant business.

Kimberly-Clark sells essential hygiene and health products to households and professionals among consumer staples stocks worldwide. Its brands, including Huggies and Kleenex, reach customers in more than 175 countries through supermarkets and e-commerce platforms. Walmart (WMT -0.13%) is its largest customer, accounting for nearly 16% of net sales, and customer concentration like this adds a layer of risk to the business.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached nearly $16.6 billion. The company reported net income of close to $3.3 billion during this same timeframe. Kimberly-Clark has total cash of $1.8 billion, with total debt of $6.5 billion. Over a 12-month trailing period, it has operating cash flow of $3.3 billion.

McDonald's operates a global fast-food empire by primarily franchising its locations to independent owners rather than running them directly. This model allows for high net margins compared to many traditional retail-focused businesses. It also maintains a delivery partnership with Uber Technologies (UBER -0.07%) to help boost volume and customer loyalty across more than a dozen global markets.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached approximately $27.8 billion, which shows revenue growth of nearly 5.8%. Net income for this period was roughly $9 billion, demonstrating the company's ability to turn sales into significant bottom-line profit. The steady expansion of the franchise system and delivery infrastructure supports these consistent financial results.

Kimberly-Clark faces intense competition from other branded household products and lower-priced private label manufacturers. It is sensitive to price changes in volatile commodities like cellulose fiber and petroleum-based materials used in manufacturing. Additionally, the company must manage the integration of its acquisition of Kenvue while navigating global regulatory and sustainability reporting requirements.

McDonald's deals with fierce competition and depends heavily on the financial health of its individual franchisees. Labor shortages and rising wage costs can pressure the system, while food safety concerns or public perception issues can damage the global brand reputation.

McDonald's carries a significantly higher premium than Kimberly-Clark based on both its forward price-to-earnings (P/E) and its price-to-sales (P/S) ratio, which measures market cap relative to sales over the past twelve months.

Both Kimberly-Clark and McDonald's are well-run companies, and while McDonald's is known for its singular brand, Kimberly-Clark is known for multiple brands, ranging from Kleenex to Band-Aid. That's because in tight economic times, Kimberly-Clark offers items people will keep buying, whereas people may limit how often they go out to eat. We are seeing a bit of that play out as McDonald's figures out how to keep prices lower amid rising costs. For its 2026 third-quarter results, restaurant sales in the United States decreased by 0.2%.

In comparison, Kimberly-Clark has more predictable demand, which is nice to have with all the uncertainty swirling around the markets. In the first nine months of 2026, Kimberly-Clark reported a 1% increase in net sales to $12.4 billion. That 1% increase may not sound exciting, but again, it's about consistency. Also, the acquisition of the consumer healthcare company Kenvue gives Kimberly-Clark even more essential products to sell, adding brands like Tylenol and Listerine to its portfolio. While consumers may cut back on going out to eat and ordering burgers and fries, they won't hesitate to buy Huggies or Tylenol if they need them.
