11 most valuable EFL Championship players and how much they earn

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

In recent years, the Championship has become a league that plays host to some incredibly valuable players, which now eclipse some of the top leagues across Europe.

Parachute payments and the money that comes from television rights means that top second tier clubs can keep most of their best players, and they can spend millions bolstering their squads each summer - figures are being spent that wouldn't have been dreamed of 20 years ago.

Here, FLW takes a look at the most valuable Championship starting 11 in a 4-2-3-1 formation, as per valuations made by Transfermarkt, and what they're all estimated to be earning in wages per week, with estimates taken from Capology.

A sufferer of back-to-back Premier League relegations with two different clubs, Mads Hermansen hasn't had too much joy in the last few years of his career.

The Dane was impressive enough with Leicester City to earn himself a £20 million move to West Ham last summer, but he spent half of his debut season at the London Stadium on the bench, albeit he ended it as Nuno's number one, and has been between the sticks throughout the current season.

With a market value of €15 million (£12.5m) and an estimated wage of £22,500-per week, the 26-year-old still holds value, but it's now below what was paid for his services last year.

The abilities of Jackson Tchatchoua are a bone of contention amongst Wolves fans, and he is now second-choice to Kieran Trippier when both are fully-fit and available.

The athletic Cameroon international arrived at Molineux last summer in a £10.8 million deal from Hellas Verona, where he was dubbed the fastest player in Serie A.

For all his speed though, Tchatchoua's performances have left a lot to be desired, and in the end he was part of last summer's recruitment drive which really didn't go down well on the whole.

Tchatchoua still holds a €12 million (£10m) market value though, which could see him be worth something in January if the club wanted to move him on, but is earning an estimated £65,000-a week in the Midlands, which could prove more problematic to shift.

He may not be the best at helping to keep goals out for West Ham at one end, but Konstantinos Mavropanos has certainly proved to be a goal threat in the Championship.

The Greek centre-half joined the Hammers for £19 million in 2023, but was part of a leaky defence last season that saw the Londoners drop into the Championship.

And whilst the defence hasn't been exactly watertight in the second tier, Mavropanos has been showing up at the other end of the pitch - at the time of writing, he's fired in five goals and has been showing up the club's strikers in a big way.

An €18 million (£15m) market valuation feels fair, with an estimated weekly wage of £25,000 feeling quite low, but there's likely going to be transfer interest in January - Besiktas are already being linked.

It was perhaps a surprise to some that Wolves managed to keep hold of Czechia centre-back Ladislav Krejci in the summer, but they're able to utilise him until at least January as a result.

Funnily enough, West Ham made a push to sign him late on in the transfer window, but with Wolves not wanting to sell to a divisional rival, and the player not wanting to play in the Championship, the deal was off - yet he remains in that league anyway.

Wolves had to pay £26 million for the centre-back from Girona after an appearance-based clause was triggered in his loan deal, and whilst he's an obvious good player for the Championship, a market value of €22 million (£18.3m) seems about right.

It would mean Wolves would make a financial loss if selling him for that price - he also comes with a chunky estimated weekly wage of £45,000.

West Ham youngster Ollie Scarles is the most valuable left-back in the division, according to Transfermarkt, at €12 million (£10m) - yet he's not even a regular starter at the London Stadium.

A Hammers academy product, Scarles played 13 times in the Premier League last season, and was expected to take big steps forward in the Championship under Nuno.

The 20-year-old did start the campaign in the starting 11, but has seen his game-time dwindle thanks to the presence of Nottingham Forest loanee Morato.

There's still time for Scarles to make an impact though this season, and given the home-grown rules for the Championship, he's unlikely to be leaving anytime soon, and with an estimated wage of £10,000-a week, he's one of the lower earners at the club.

On the other end of the spectrum, West Ham summer signing Arne Engels is estimated to earn a cool £50,000-per week - which is to be expected given his £22 million move from Celtic broke the Championship transfer record.

Has the Belgium international made enough of an impact to be worthy of that fee so far though? It's hard to say - he scored his first goal for the club in a 3-3 comeback draw against Cardiff City, and he's also notched a handful of assists, but he's not been a standout midfielder that looks far and away Premier League quality.

Scott Parker may be able to unlock another level to Engels' game, and with an estimated valuation of €25 million (£20.8m), there surely has to be more to come.

A tough-tackling midfield general who also possesses technique, it would've been a fair assumption that André would've signed for a top flight club over the summer, but to the surprise of many, he signed a brand-new contract at Molineux.

Like Engels, André's market valuation of €25 million (£20.8m) is in the right ballpark, and that will likely increase if Wolves win promotion back to the Premier League at the first time of asking - an estimated wage of £65,000-a week for the Championship though is a really steep one.

Bowen's nine goals in 2025-26 weren't enough to help keep the Hammers in the Premier League, but he committed his immediate future to the club in the wake of relegation, albeit there is a release clause that can be triggered next summer if they do not win promotion at the first time of asking.

Currently, Bowen is showing exactly what he's capable of at a lower level, but for an estimated £75,000-a week and a €30 million (£25m) transfer valuation, he's doing what is expected, and he's probably capable of even better - West Ham won't be entertaining selling him in January, but they might not have a choice in the summer.

He's looking every inch of the player that is estimated to have a transfer value of €22 million (18.3m), and he's probably worth more than that - his estimated wage of £25,000-a week is on the low side as well for Wolves' first-team squad.

Wolves didn't have much to cheer about last season, but the emergence of Mateus Mane in the first-team was one of the bright sparks.

US: Giant pandas from China make public debut at Atlanta zoo

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Ping Ping and Fu Shuang are the first giant pandas to be housed in Zoo Atlanta after a gap of two years.

Two giant pandas from China have made their first public appearance at a zoo in the US city of Atlanta as a queue of visitors lined up to see the bears.

In a treat for them and their fans, Ping Ping and Fu Shuang —  male and female, respectively — were seen munching on bamboo at Zoo Atlanta on Friday.

The six-year-old pandas arrived from China's Chengdu on September 27.

The FedEx flight they traveled on was dubbed the "Panda Express."

The panda duo had spent the last few weeks getting acclimated to their habitat.

Ping Ping and Fu Shuang have taken up residence in a panda enclosure at the zoo that had been vacant since 2024.

A 25-year-long partnership that saw Atlanta host four pandas on loan since 1999 ended in the midst of strained US-China ties. Seven panda cubs that were born at the zoo during that period now live in Chengdu, China.

That arrangement has been renewed under a 10-year loan agreement following a Washington summit between US President Donald Trump and his Chinese counterpart Xi Jinping.

China's ambassador to the US, Xie Feng, said at a press conference on Friday that the pandas' arrival "marks the renewal of a decade-long bond between China and Zoo Atlanta."

Raymond King, CEO of Zoo Atlanta, described pandas as "animals that have got a unique ability to inspire curiosity, instill passion, evoke joy, and unite people around the globe."

"This extraordinary spirit of partnership helps create a future for a species that is truly beloved the world over,"  he said as the two adult pandas made their debut.

China has a history of leasing the iconic pandas to other countries in a bid to build diplomatic ties, dubbed "panda diplomacy."

Cubs born in foreign zoos are typically sent back to China.

Meanwhile, unaware of the soft power they wield, Ping Ping and Fu Shuang, lounged about their new habitat on Friday.

The zoo recently renovated its panda facilities and has also ensured a long-term supply of locally grown bamboo for its newest residents.

Three US zoos now house the black-and-white mammals.
