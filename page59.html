Mittelstädt after MOTM-performance: "The result was extremely important to me!"

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Maxi Mittelstädt was named Man of the Match after Stuttgart's 1-0 victory over Werder Bremen, but the defender insisted that the individual award meant little to him compared to the result.

"Honestly, I don't care about being Man of the Match. The result was extremely important to me, especially with the international break coming up," Mittelstädt said after the game.

"Werder also played well, and we had to suffer a little towards the end. We're very happy that we managed to keep all three points here today."

Mittelstädt also reflected on his goal, admitting that he was fortunate to see the ball fall into his path. "I was a bit lucky that the ball came through to me. It was also partly instinct," he explained. "I've been training to get into those positions around the edge of the box and intercept balls like that. It's great that it worked out today."

The Stuttgart defender also had praise for Niclas Füllkrug, highlighting the striker's physical qualities and ability to hold up the ball.

"Fülle is incredibly strong. He holds the ball up and gets the better of opponents – he's really difficult to defend against," Mittelstädt said. "I'm glad we managed to deal with him today. He can start scoring again over the coming weeks."

Mittelstädt acknowledged that Stuttgart had expected a demanding afternoon against a strong Werder side. "We knew it might not be the prettiest game today. We knew we would have to fight hard because Bremen are a good team. We put a lot of energy into it."

Odegaard winner spares officials from another VAR row

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

An ultimately routine Arsenal win but, even by the standards of VAR, an utterly bizarre refereeing controversy. Mikel Arteta’s instant simmering fury at being denied a first-half penalty may also have turned into something rather more explosive had Martin Odegaard not later sent the champions six points clear with a ninth win in 10 Premier League games.

The big talking point arose with inadvertent theatre from referee Farai Hallam when Kai Havertz, who was clearly in an offside position, was equally clearly pulled down by Hull City defender Nobel Mendy inside the penalty area. Which comes first? The intervention of VAR suggested the latter. But Hallam duly went to check the pitch-side monitor before then announcing the former.

“After review, there is a holding offence by Blue No 32 on Red No 29,” he declared. Cue cheers from the home Arsenal crown. “However…,” he went on. “Red No 29 is in an offside position, so the final decision will be a free-kick for offside to Hull City”.

#ARSHUL – 42' VAR recommended an on-field review for a potential penalty to Arsenal for a challenge by Mendy on Havertz - but determined that Havertz was in an offside position in the build-up. REF: "After review, there is a holding offence by Blue No.32 on Red No.29. However, Red No.29 is in an offside position, so the final decision will be a free-kick for offside to Hull City".

It all sounded so decisive that most inside the Emirates instinctively thought the outcome reasonable. But then came a closer look at the rules. And Law 11.2 certainly did seem to support Arteta’s protestations: “In situations where a player in an offside position is moving towards the ball with the intention of playing the ball and is fouled before playing or attempting to play the ball, or challenging an opponent for the ball, the foul is penalised as it has occurred before the offside offence.”

Arteta clearly saw no value post-match in aiming much criticism at Hallam following a fourth 1-0 win in 10 league games but did still emphasise his surprise at the decision. “What do you think?” he asked one reporter. “I thought offside, but the law says that doesn’t matter,” said the journalist. “So not offside then,” said Arteta, who stressed that he needs to understand “what is overruling what in the decision-making” after a Premier League statement simply said that Havertz was deemed offside “in the build-up.”

At the very best, it all leaves further confusion and again opens refereeing body Pro Ref to the charge of inconsistency. Fabian Schär, for example, was awarded a penalty for Newcastle against Bournemouth in 2024, despite being in an offside position when his shirt was grabbed by Adam Smith.

Conversely, Dango Ouattara had a penalty overturned for Brentford against Leeds last season after going down under pressure from Gabriel Gudmundsson before being deemed offside. The crucial point seems to be whether the player has actually challenged for the ball at the time of the infringement and, in this case, the answer was surely “no”.

Premier League title triumphs, however, are often forged on forgettable wins in frustrating circumstances and this was ultimately a classic of the genre. With Noni Madueke continuing to step up superbly in the absence of Bukayo Saka, Arsenal’s combination of attacking persistence and defensive resilience was ultimately rewarded by Odegaard’s scrappy 72nd-minute shot. For young players, it was also every bit as educational as the VAR controversy might be for emerging officials.

Madueke had shot low to force a save by Hull goalkeeper Konstantinos Tzolakis, but Odegaard was somehow still first to the rebound, despite being the wrong side of the penalty spot and five yards behind Hull defender John Egan when the ball was struck.

“The way he scores, not the most beautiful, but the most effective,” said Arteta, who said that his team had sometimes tried too hard for a “perfect goal” and should instead be happy with something “more pragmatic”.

It was a well-judged assessment as Arsenal head into the international break with creative deficiencies, but still almost flawless in the cold hard currency of results. Three wins in three days, including at Anfield and in Prague, also underlines a certain champions mentality.

“I need to praise the players,” said Arteta. “It’s been very very demanding; very short time to recover with all the travel. The mentality is incredible. It’s still going to be super difficult. We can improve in a number of areas.”

When it was then put to him that Arsenal have been offside 35 times this season – with Havertz responsible for 15 of those – Arteta acknowledged that “timing” was one element. But, with Havertz targeted by some frustrated fans, he stressed that responsibility was always shared for those split-second moments by the passer and receiver.

He also singled out Madueke, again easily Arsenal’s most creative player, following his surprise omission from Thomas Tuchel’s England squad. “He went away, started to train, he came back and it was a different Noni,” said Arteta, of his reaction to also being out of the Arsenal team earlier in the season. “You could sense that he meant business and he wasn’t going to accept the situation and, since then, I think he’s been incredible.”

Richard Lloyd Dies: Guitarist With Influential Punk Pioneers Television Was 75

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Richard Lloyd, whose intricate guitar work for the pioneering punk band Television was a fiery counterpart to the sinewy stylings of the band’s co-guitarist and singer Tom Verlaine, died today of the bile duct cancer called cholangiocarcinoma at his home in Chattanooga, Tenn. He was 75.

His death was reported by his family to The New York Times. Lloyd had been diagnosed with the cancer in March.

With his Television bandmates Verlaine, bassist Richard Hell (later replaced by Fred Smith) and drummer Billy Ficca, Lloyd was among the innovative, even revolutionary, New York bands in the mid-1970s to establish the East Village dive bar CBGB as the epicenter of a new downtown music scene. Along with Patti Smith and the Ramones, Television led the way in creating and developing a new sound – defined later (and loosely) as punk – that was often as literate and playful as it was sonically powerful. Related Stories News Paul Howard Dies: Son Of Three Stooges' Moe Howard, Keeper Of The Flame Was 91 News Kimmarie Johnson Dies: 'Beverly Hills, 90210' & 'Dude, Where's My Car?' Actor Was 50

Formed in 1973, Television grew out of the teenage friendship of two Delaware boys, Thomas Miller and Richard Meyers, who by the early ’70s had changed their names to, respectively, Tom Verlaine and Richard Hell and set about developing the spiky guitar sound and keening vocal style (courtesy Verlaine) that would reach its apotheosis in the band’s 1977 masterpiece Marquee Moon. Watch on Deadline

By the time of their 1974 debut at CBGB, Verlaine and Hell had recruited Lloyd to be the group’s second guitarist, a move that gave Television its signature and distinctive dueling Strat-versus-Jazzmaster sound. Unlike the more power-chord-heavy guitar bombast of other bands on the scene, Television specialized in complicated, even byzantine interplay between the six-stringed instruments. And like Patti Smith, Verlaine sang lyrics that signified a devotion to poetry as surely as did his chosen name.

Born Richard John Alt on Oct. 25, 1951, in Pittsburgh, Lloyd grew up in Manhattan’s Greenwich Village and began playing guitar before he hit his teens. He became a regular in the audience of the famed Fillmore East rock palace, mesmerized by the music of The Grateful Dead and other bands of the era. He would be forthcoming about working as a male hustler to support himself during the lean years.

With an early residency at CBGB, Television quickly won over audiences as well as fellow musicians like Patti Smith, Lou Reed and David Bowie, who had caught the band during a trip to New York and set about spreading the word to anyone who would listen. Bowie would later record the group’s song “Kingdom Come” for his 1980 Scary Monsters album

The band’s initial line-up didn’t last long, though, as the old friends Verlaine and Hell began to bicker over everything from their music to the way it was performed on stage. Hell quit in 1975 and formed a sort of punk supergroup called The Heartbreakers with ex-New York Dolls members Johnny Thunders and Jerry Nolan. He eventually left that group to form Richard Hell and the Voidoids.

Hell was replaced in Television by ex-Blondie bassist Fred Smith (not to be confused with Patti Smith’s husband Fred “Sonic” Smith).

Despite its quick success, Television called it quits in 1978, in part because of Lloyd’s increasingly dire heroin addiction and mental health issues. Lloyd would release nine solo albums after Television, and he and Verlaine, despite a prickly relationship, occasionally reunited the band. Neither guitarist would ever match the success or influence of Television in its genre-building heyday.

Lloyd is survived by wife Sheila O’Keefe Lloyd; son Dylan; and brother Eric Lloyd.
