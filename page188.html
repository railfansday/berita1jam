Viktor Gyökeres the difference as Arsenal claim nervy win against Slavia Prague

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Another away day, another victory for Arsenal. This time, the much-maligned Viktor Gyökeres stole the show as poor Slavia Prague matched the longest winless streak in European Cup and Champions League history despite a spirited late comeback. That now stands at 23, equalling the mark set by AEK Athens and FCSB – formerly known as Steaua Bucharest – while Arsenal are now unbeaten in their last 10 away European trips. After last week’s wobble against Fleetwood, there was no prospect of Arsenal making the same mistake again as soon as Noni Madueke fired them ahead early on after being superbly set up by Gyökeres.

That was his third assist in his last five appearances, although Mikel Arteta will have been even more encouraged by the emphatic finish that had seemed to put the game beyond Slavia’s reach just before the break. While Kai Havertz remains his undoubted first choice, having another striker in form will only boost Arsenal’s challenge and it was noticeable that there was an extra spring in the 28-year-old’s step after his goal.

Slavia’s solitary victory in this competition came in their first-ever match in 2007 and, after losing 3-0 to Arsenal here exactly a year ago, the Czech champions knew their chances of avoiding the unwanted milestone were extremely slim. While Fleetwood’s giant-killing against the Premier League champions in the EFL Cup last week may have given them some hope of an upset, Arteta’s side has developed a killer instinct on their travels in Europe. Not since May 2025, in the second leg of their semi-final against Paris Saint-Germain, have Arsenal lost an away game. The 2-2 draw at Bayern Munich two weeks ago underlined their credentials as genuine contenders once again.

After their exploits at Anfield on Sunday that took them three points clear at the top of the Premier League, Arteta heavily rotated his side again with six changes. Martín Zubimendi was handed only his third start of a frustrating campaign for the Spain World Cup winner, while Gyökeres led the line for the third time in Arsenal’s past four matches.

The Sweden striker finished as top scorer last season with 21 goals and finally got off the mark against Fleetwood with a scrappy finish, although he has clearly been struggling for form and confidence. Yet there was no doubting the quality of his assist for Madueke’s goal after just eight minutes. David Raya’s long ball should have been dealt with by the Slavia defence but they made the criminal error of allowing it to bounce. That was the cue for Gyökeres to come alive as he beat his marker to the ball, chested it down and played the perfect pass for the England forward to run on to and score. Arteta’s reaction on the touchline suggested he enjoyed it too.

Mikel Merino was Arsenal’s hero here last year with two goals and he could have extended their lead on three occasions before the half-hour mark. The lively Madueke created his best opportunity after cutting the ball back to Merino but he could not beat Nazar Domchak in Slavia’s goal.

With Martin Ødegaard watching from the bench next to William Saliba, on his return from a long-term back injury, Arsenal lacked some of their usual fluency in attack. But Slavia were still no match for them and their task became almost impossible seven minutes before the break. Gyökeres exchanged a smart one-two with Merino inside the area before curling a brilliant shot into the far corner. It needed a goalline clearance from Michal Sadilek to prevent a rampant Arsenal inflicting more damage before half-time.

To their credit, Slavia started the second half with much more aggression after introducing their record signing Ange N’Guessan from the bench. The hosts came within a few centimetres of being awarded a penalty when Emmanuel Ayaosi was brought down by Ezri Konsa on the edge of the area. The England defender and Rice both found themselves in the book as Arsenal struggled to recapture their rhythm from the first half without ever looking in danger of conceding.

But that all changed in the last 20 minutes after David Jurasek let fly from long range and his effort was deflected just wide of Raya’s goal. From the resulting corner, Gabriel Magalhães somehow managed to clear two goalbound headers off the line in a matter of seconds. The Brazilian celebrated with a massive fist pump.

Mikulas Konecny should have done much better with another headed chance as Arsenal’s formidable defence was put to the test in the final 15 minutes. A spectacular save denied Oskar Kubiak as Raya managed to push the ball on to the post at full stretch. Slavia eventually scored the goal all their endeavour deserved in the final minute of normal time when Wiktor Nowak fired in at the far post. There were a few nervous moments in stoppage time and Arteta breathed a sigh of relief at the full-time whistle as Slavia were condemned to their fate.

Cardiff 3-3 West Ham: The Hammers came back from three goals down to secure a point in Parker’s first match in charge

Want to bookmark your favourite articles and stories to read or reference later? Start your Independent Membership today.

Scott Parker saw his West Ham side recover from three goals down to draw 3-3 at Cardiff in a thrilling first game in charge.

Jack Moylan’s first hat-trick in English football had Cardiff in cruise control at half-time and Hammers fans venting their anger.

But the Hammers – inspired by captain Jarrod Bowen – fought back in incredible fashion as Divine Mukasa, Arne Engels and Max Kilman netted to secure a point.

Parker was confirmed as Hammers boss on Wednesday morning – just 24 hours after Nuno Espirito Santo’s departure – but it was a tough watch for the former England midfielder initially as Moylan had Cardiff 3-0 ahead inside 39 minutes.

Moylan scored a hat-trick on his Republic of Ireland debut against Grenada in May, but this was his first club treble since scoring three for Shelbourne in May 2023.

The draw extends Cardiff’s unbeaten home league run to 10 games over the last two seasons, while West Ham occupy the final play-off place in eighth.

Nuno’s 13-month tenure had ended after the Hammers had won just twice in six matches and lost successive home games to Southampton and West Brom.

Taty Castellanos had hinted at better times ahead, forcing Nathan Trott in to early action after Bowen’s effort had deflected to him.

But the Hammers were behind after Alex Robertson found Moylan in the 11th minute. Moylan advanced to find the top corner of Mads Hermansen’s net with a ferocious strike from 20 yards.

Hermansen tipped aside Calum Scanlon’s cross and Will Fish headed over as Cardiff probed. There was no reprieve for West Ham when Perry Ng curled a ball over a static defence after 36 minutes.

Moylan cleverly slowed down for Morato to bundle him over, a clear penalty. Cardiff had missed their last two spot-kicks, but this time Moylan was entrusted with the responsibility and sent Hermansen the wrong way.

Angry Hammers fans responded with cries of ‘you’re not fit to wear the shirt’, and many of them appeared to be making a swift departure three minutes later.

Joel Bagan’s corner cleared Fish at the near post, but Moylan came in behind him to head home his sixth goal in five games, and his 10th of the season.

Parker sent on Mohamadou Kante and Joel Piroe for Soungoutou Magassa and Castellanos at half-time and there was instantly more conviction from the Hammers.

Bowen floated a delicious pass through after 58 minutes, and Mukasa lifted the ball over Trott and in off a post.

The deficit was reduced further seven minutes later when Piroe flicked on Bowen’s pass and Engels slid home his first Hammers goal.

Kante nearly toe poked the ball past Trott before Kilman touched home Bowen’s brilliant cross, and West Ham almost won it through substitutes Ezra Mayers and Tomas Soucek.

Durbin Is Retiring, Marshall Just Lost—Why The Bill Threatening Your Credit Card Rewards Isn’t Dead

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Dick Durbin is retiring and Roger Marshall just lost his Senate seat. The two most prominent champions of the Credit Card Competition Act, the law intended to regulate credit card interchange which will mean far fewer rewards for consumers, will be gone in January. The bill threatening the economics of card rewards, and the airlines that depend on them, has support from President Trump and Vice President JD Vance was an original co-sponsor so it may come back with his presidential campaign. But the thing about this legislation is that it’s more useful as a way to raise campaign money from both retailers and banks than as something Congress actually passes. Departures of Durbin and Marshall haven’t made the Durbin-Marshall bill go away. Durbin identified Vermont Senator Peter Welch as his successor on the issue. The Airline Lobby Is Right To Worry In this week’s Airlines Confidential, Airlines for America CEO Chris Sununu warns that state initiatives interfering with card fees would eliminate points-funded travel. Banks buy miles because rewards help attract customers and their spending. By reducing margins on that spending, it’s less valuable to spend to encourage consumers to use the product. In markets where government has gotten involved in interchange rates we’ve seen: smaller initial bonuses higher annual fees annual caps on awards award chart devaluations (if each mile is worth less, an airline can still award one mile per dollar on spend despite receiving less money for it from the bank) It also means less revenue for the airline. In the third quarter, American Express represented all of Delta’s profit. American Airlines is barely breaking even even with several billion net from Citibank. We’d have much shakier airlines and fewer flights, and aspirational leisure destinations like Hawaii in particular would see less air service. What The Credit Card Competition Act Actually Does The current Senate bill, S. 3623, would require covered issuers with more than $100 billion in assets to enable at least two unaffiliated payment networks. At least one would have to be outside of Visa and Mastercard. Merchants get to choose the routing. That’s meant to put downward pressure on merchant payment costs by changing who controls which network handles the transaction. Cards issued in a three-party system, such as American Express issuing its own cards, are exempt from the routing requirement. Merchants benefit from accepting cards. They get more sales, and higher average sales. It’s often less expensive to accept credit cards than to take cash (due to employee theft, incorrect change, higher insurance costs). Retailers want the service cards offer, but want the government to force Visa and Mastercard to provide it for less. Trump’s promise of $1,200 in annual family savings was completely made up and inconsistent with the bill. Currently a majority of interchange gets rebated to the customer. Regulation of interchange has never lowered prices to consumers. And card payments, funded in large part by interchange, provide services to consumers beyond just payments (and borrowings). They often fund consumer protections – not just stronger dispute resolution than debit cards, but also extended warranty, return protection, and travel protections. The Bill’s Strongest Value Is As A Threat Back in July 2023, I argued that this was an especially useful issue for opening contribution wallets on both sides. Retailers have a large, concentrated interest in lowering their acceptance costs. Banks, payment networks and airlines have a large, concentrated interest in preserving the existing economics. An unresolved legislative threat gives both sides a reason to pour dollars into campaigns and political committees. I’d still bet on another round of proposals, threatened amendments and lobbying. Durbin and Marshall are leaving but the retailers and banks with billions at stake aren’t. Politicians still want the money. Is It Over for DAPT After Left Atrial Appendage Closure?

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

— Antithrombotic monotherapy worked at least as well at 6 months

Antithrombotic monotherapy was similar to dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT) for 6-month outcomes after left atrial appendage (LAA) closure, the SIMPLAAFY trial showed.

The incidence rate of combined all-cause death, stroke, systemic embolism, or major bleeding at 6 months was 5.1% with reduced-dose non-vitamin K oral anticoagulants (NOACs), 3.3% with aspirin alone, and 6.9% with DAPT, reported Saibal Kar, MD, of Los Robles Hospital and Medical Center in Thousand Oaks, California, at the Transcatheter Cardiovascular Therapeutics annual conference hosted by the Cardiovascular Research Foundation.

Statistically, the strategy of reduced-dose NOACs was at least as good as DAPT (risk ratio [RR] 0.75, 95% CI 0.48-1.18), as was aspirin alone (RR 0.49, 95% CI 0.29-0.82).

"These findings support the simplification of antithrombotic management after uncomplicated LAA [closure] and may be individualized according to competing bleeding and device thrombosis risks and patient preferences," Kar said.

The study was simultaneously published in JAMA.

Among the secondary analyses in the trial, there was no excess stroke at 6 months with the aspirin or NOAC monotherapy regimens. However, bleeds alone gave both an edge over DAPT, while low-dose NOAC therapy stood out for a relatively low incidence of device-related thrombus (DRT) at 45 days (0.9% vs 3.4% with DAPT and 2.7% with aspirin).

As it stands, standard practice varies widely for antithrombotic therapy after LAA closure on account of a "lack of legitimate evidence" for guidance, said session discussant Mohamad Alkhouli, MD, of the Mayo Clinic in Rochester, Minnesota.

"Aspirin seems to emerge a winner," Alkhouli suggested, based on SIMPLAAFY, while he noted that "the jury is still out" for NOACs.

Complicating the picture is the ANDES trial, which found that after LAA closure, short-term NOACs were no better than DAPT at preventing DRT at 60 days, and were associated with even more DRTs at 1 year (5.2% vs 2.3%, P=0.154).

Kar said these data gave him pause on making a blanket recommendation for NOACs after LAA closure.

Meanwhile, 12-month results from SIMPLAAFY are still pending.

"The fact is that we really didn't know what to do with these patients," said Kar. "What we will do beyond 1 year, I don't know."

SIMPLAAFY included patients with nonvalvular atrial fibrillation who had the Watchman FLX Pro LAA occluder successfully implanted. The investigators randomized 1,857 patients 1:1:1 to aspirin 81 to 100 mg alone, initial DAPT for 6 months (followed by aspirin thereafter), or initial reduced-dose NOACs -- 2.5-mg apixaban (Eliquis) twice daily or 10-mg rivaroxaban (Xarelto) daily -- for 3 months (followed by aspirin thereafter).

Across the three study groups, mean age was 76 years, 43% were women, mean CHA2DS2-VASc score was 4.2, and mean HAS-BLED bleeding risk score was 2.6.

Acute events through 7 days were rare in general. Rates of clinically relevant nonmajor bleeding were 1.0% with aspirin alone, 1.6% with DAPT, and 1.5% with low-dose NOACs; major bleeding rates were 0.2%, 0.5%, and 0.2%, respectively.

The proportion of participants adhering to their assigned medications (for 80% or more days) was 87.9% with aspirin alone, 76.8% with DAPT, and 85.3% with reduced-dose NOACs. "Both monotherapy treatments were better tolerated than DAPT," Kar noted.

A subgroup analysis indicated that people ages 75 and older had disproportionately better outcomes on aspirin (RR 0.34, 95% CI 0.18-0.64) or reduced-dose NOACs (RR 0.53, 95% CI 0.31-0.93).

LAA imaging at 45 days suggested no differences in the Watchman device seal across groups.

Kar stressed that only uncomplicated LAA occluder implants were included in the trial, limiting the generalizability of the findings. Some high-risk populations were also excluded (e.g., those with severe kidney disease, recent stroke, myocardial infarction, or major bleeding).

Of note, there was no prespecified comparison of the two monotherapy arms.

Como led 3-0 at half-time with hat-trick hero Douvikas scoring two at that point before Paz's goal and his De Miguel tribute.

Soon after the interval the Serie A outfit crushed any notion of the hosts coming back as they plundered in five goals in a devastating 22-minute spell which included Douvikas completing his hat-trick. Lucas Da Cunha, Marc-Oliver Kempf, Mattia Liberali, Martina Baturina also got in on the act during that period.

Ruben Aguilar pulled one back for Lens before Matteo Papaccioli rounded off the rout in stoppage time. The 18-year-old Papaccioli, who came on in the second half, is a member of Como's youth team and hasn't even played in Serie A yet.

Wednesday's win means Como have broken many records in the Champions League.

The Italian side matched the eight-goal winning margins recorded by Liverpool and Real Madrid in their 8-0 home victories over Besiktas and Malmo respectively. They also became only the second team after Bayern Munich to score nine goals in a Champions League match.

Como followed their 4-1 victory over Manchester United with another emphatic performance, becoming the first team to score nine goals away from home in the European Cup since 1971 – Feyenoord beat Olympiacos Nicosia by this scoreline – as they climbed to fourth in the standings on nine points. The previous best scoring performance by an away club in the Champions League was Bayern Munich's 8-2 win over Barcelona at a neutral venue (Lisbon) in 2020.

Fabregas's side may have made Champions League record-equalling history, but Feyenoord hold the European Cup mark for most goals scored in a single match, having beaten Icelandic side KR 12-2 in September 1969.

Lens, who suffered the heaviest defeat by a French club in the competition, are 33rd with three points.
