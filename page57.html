Pokémon Gold & Silver Officially Return November 12 In New 30th Anniversary Collection

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Pokémon Gold and Silver have proven to be some of the best mainline Pokémon titles. Their combination of two distinct regions, coupled with a slew of exciting new Pokémon, phenomenal new locations worth exploring, and intriguing lore, made for an engrossing experience that few other Pokémon games have quite managed to surpass. Indeed, their remakes, HeartGold and SoulSilver, are often regarded as the best Pokémon games ever made, and it is likely it'll stay that way for some time to come.

So, it is not terribly surprising to see The Pokémon Company revisit these legendary games in a brand-new collection designed to celebrate the series' seemingly never-ending 30th anniversary. This new collection, while a tad pricey, is absolutely the perfect item for any fan's collection, as quite a considerable amount of care and attention has seemingly gone into its design. While it isn't the brand-new Pokémon Gold and Silver remasters some may have been hoping for, this new line of merchandise is sure to appeal to dedicated devotees.

Pokémon Gold and Silver have been given quite the spotlight in a brand-new Pokémon merchandise collection that features music box dioramas and paper recreations of iconic locations from across the series' history. The new collection sees routes from across all nine mainline games come to life in dioramas that also happen to play each route's respective track. For example, the Pokémon Gold and Silver diorama features Chikorita, Cyndaquil, Totodile, and Pikachu running about the tall grass in Route 29. The music box it is attached to, when opened, will play the Route 29 track.

In addition to the music box, fans can also pick up a paper recreation of the iconic Ecruteak City Bell Tower, which features the tower itself and its surroundings, including various NPCs and the player. Additionally, the top of the Bell Tower can be removed to reveal a recreation of one of its floors inside. Furthermore, there's a framed region map of both Johto and Kanto from Pokémon Gold and Silver. All of these products retain the original game's art style and aesthetic, just realized in 3D.

You can pre-order these items from the official Japanese Pokémon store, and they will be released on November 12. However, they are exclusive to the Japanese Pokémon store and won't be released internationally, at least for the time being. The music box diorama costs 12,100 yen, which works out to roughly $76. If you wanted to collect all nine, you'd have to spend around $684, so it certainly isn't a cheap collection.

The region map costs a whopping 16,500 yen, which is about $104, while the paper recreation of the Ecruteak City Bell Tower costs 2,750 yen, or around $17. Of course, each of these can be bought separately, allowing fans of just Pokémon Gold and Silver to buy their respective items from the collection exclusively.

If you are interested, you can check out each product via the official Pokémon store website. While it is a shame that these Pokémon Gold and Silver collectibles, and the rest of the set, won't be released internationally, it is great to see The Pokémon Company offer unique merchandise that goes beyond the expected.

American Airlines Has A Unique Airbus A321neo Subfleet That Unavoidably Breaks Daily Consistency

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Robin Guess | Shutterstock, Simple Flying

American Airlines operates two versions of the Airbus A321neo that look broadly similar to passengers but cannot be made identical. 10 aircraft acquired from Alaska Airlines have a different emergency-exit arrangement from the airline’s factory-delivered A321neos, a structural feature inherited from their original design. Even after American refurbished the former Alaska jets with its standard Oasis cabin, the fuselage layout prevented the airline from matching the larger subfleet’s seating capacity and seat locations.

The result is a small but persistent source of inconsistency across American’s narrowbody operation. Both versions are presented under the same 32Q fleet code, yet they have different total seat counts, exit rows, and cabin details. Passengers selecting seats based on a particular map may therefore find that an aircraft substitution changes the location of extra-legroom seating or places them in a different part of the cabin. For the airline, the challenge is not simply maintaining two interiors. It is operating a single fleet designation across aircraft whose underlying layouts cannot be standardized through a conventional cabin retrofit.

The subfleet traces its origins to Virgin America, which ordered the aircraft before Alaska Airlines acquired the carrier in 2016. Alaska inherited the Airbus jets as part of the transaction, despite operating an otherwise Boeing-focused mainline fleet. When Alaska later moved to simplify its operations around Boeing narrowbodies, it retired the 10 A321neos and sold them to American in 2023. Only four of the 10 aircraft entered service with Virgin America; the other six joined Alaska directly after the acquisition. By the time American took ownership, the aircraft were relatively young, averaging about 5.6 years old when Alaska retired them. Purchasing an existing group offered American access to additional A321neo capacity without waiting for new production slots, which were already constrained by strong global demand for the type.

The aircraft began entering American’s operation in spring 2024, initially flying from Miami with much of Alaska’s original cabin still intact. At that stage, they had 190 seats, including 16 in first class and 174 in the main cabin. The layout offered a more spacious experience than American’s standard A321neo, with greater seat pitch in several sections, but fewer first class places for complimentary upgrades.

American’s acquisition was therefore a practical fleet decision, not a plan to create a permanently distinct passenger product. The airline intended to bring the aircraft closer to its established domestic configuration. However, the jets’ original door arrangement would prove to be the limiting factor. The timing also offered an opportunity to add capacity relatively quickly. New aircraft deliveries require production slots, financing, and time to enter service, whereas the former Alaska jets were already built and had accumulated comparatively little operating history. Their acquisition helped American expand its narrowbody resources while it awaited additional Airbus deliveries. The trade-off was accepting a small group of aircraft whose design would complicate efforts to maintain a uniform cabin.

The core of the problem is the development of Airbus Cabin Flex, or ACF. Early A321neo aircraft were built with a door arrangement broadly resembling the previous-generation A321ceo: four main passenger doors on each side of the fuselage, including an additional pair positioned ahead of the wing. The 10 former Alaska aircraft belong to this earlier A321neo configuration. Airbus introduced Cabin Flex to give airlines more freedom over cabin layouts and passenger capacity. The redesign removed the second pair of full-size doors and introduced modified overwing emergency exits, alongside changes to the rear fuselage and door positions. This created additional usable cabin space and allowed airlines to install more seats while meeting evacuation requirements. Airbus said the ACF arrangement could support configurations of up to 240 passengers, depending on the chosen layout.

The revised design became standard on later A321neo production, but the former Alaska aircraft were built before that transition. Their physical structure cannot be converted into the later Cabin Flex arrangement through a normal interior refurbishment. Changing the exit layout would require extensive structural work, certification, and significant investment, making a full conversion commercially impractical for a group of only 10 aircraft. American’s larger fleet is made up of A321-200NX aircraft, while the former Alaska jets are classified as A321-200N models. The suffix reflects a difference in the airframe configuration, not merely the airline’s choice of seats or cabin finishes.

American Airlines has received four new Airbus jets in one week as its A321neo and A321XLR fleets expand.

American began bringing the former Alaska aircraft into its standard interior in 2024, with N950XV among the first to undergo the work in San Antonio. The retrofit replaced the inherited cabin with the airline’s Oasis-style arrangement, aligning the passenger experience more closely with other domestic A321s. However, the door configuration remained unchanged, limiting how many seats could be installed and where certain rows could be positioned.

After the refurbishment, each former Alaska A321neo has 190 seats. The airline’s 196-seat A321neo configuration has the same number of first class seats, but accommodates more in the main cabin. Six additional economy seats distinguish the larger group. The former Alaska aircraft effectively retain the capacity of American’s older A321ceo domestic layout, despite being newer-generation aircraft with more efficient engines. The original Alaska cabin had fewer first class seats and more generous spacing in several sections, but American’s standardization work increased first class from 16 to 20 seats and brought economy seating closer to the airline’s usual density. Passengers no longer receive the same broad spacing advantage associated with the aircraft’s initial configuration, but the aircraft still cannot carry as many passengers as the ACF-equipped A321neos.

American Airlines Airbus A321neo Cabin Comparison

For American, the six-seat shortfall is a permanent capacity constraint on each of the 10 aircraft. The figure is modest relative to the airline’s overall operation, but it illustrates how a seemingly small design difference can affect revenue potential over thousands of flights. There is also a cabin-planning consequence. American has to accommodate the same general product standards within a narrower seating footprint, rather than simply copying the exact arrangement used on its newer aircraft. The result is a familiar-looking cabin with a different internal map. Passengers may notice little difference in the overall appearance, but the location of individual seats remains tied to the original aircraft design.

The practical consequence is most visible when passengers select seats. American groups both A321neo versions under the same 32Q fleet code, so the booking process does not always make the underlying configuration obvious. Yet the exit rows occupy different positions because the doors and emergency exits are arranged differently. According to SeatCompare’s 2026 guide, the 196-seat A321neo has exit rows at 17 and 27. On the 10 former Alaska aircraft, the corresponding rows are 11 and 24.

The differences extend beyond emergency exits. The seat maps have different last rows, with row 37 on the 196-seat aircraft and row 36 on the former Alaska jets. Lavatory placement also changes the available seats near the rear of the cabin. SeatCompare notes that the port-side lavatory block removes different A-C positions in the two configurations, meaning the cabin columns do not line up in the same way from one aircraft to the other. Some seats are also narrower or have restricted recline, but the affected rows vary by version. As a result, a passenger comparing seat maps needs more than the aircraft name to identify the best option. The registration numbers N950XV through N959XV identify the former Alaska group.

Maintaining two cabin layouts within one fleet code creates work for American’s reservation systems, airport teams, and customers. Seat maps must accurately reflect the aircraft assigned to a flight, while operational substitutions can make a previously selected seat unavailable or alter its characteristics. While aircraft swaps are a normal part of airline operations, the fleet differences can cause some challenges.

There are also commercial reasons for American to retain the aircraft rather than retire or substantially rebuild them. They are comparatively young, use the same engine family as the airline’s other A321neos, and provide useful capacity on domestic routes. Replacing them would require acquiring aircraft or reallocating existing capacity, while a structural conversion would be costly and difficult to justify for just 10 frames.

American’s larger A321neo fleet is also expanding. ch-aviation reported in February 2026 that the airline operated 75 A321-200NX aircraft alongside the 10 A321-200N jets, with additional standard-range A321neos and XLRs on order. As new aircraft arrive, the former Alaska group will represent a smaller proportion of the overall A321neo fleet, but the configuration mismatch will remain as long as those 10 aircraft continue flying.

American’s former Alaska A321neos demonstrate how aircraft standardization has limits when airlines inherit equipment designed around a different specification. The Oasis retrofit brought their interiors closer to the rest of the fleet, but no cabin refresh can relocate doors or reproduce the Cabin Flex fuselage. Their six-seat capacity gap and alternative exit-row positions are therefore likely to persist.

As American receives more A321neo aircraft, the 10 jets should become a smaller share of the overall fleet. That may reduce how frequently passengers encounter the alternate seat map, but it will not remove the need for accurate aircraft-specific information. The most useful improvement would be clearer differentiation in booking tools, allowing customers to identify the 190-seat version before paying for a particular seat. For airlines increasingly selling individual cabin positions as products, reliable seat-map accuracy is part of the service itself, not merely a back-office detail.

TL;DR: European trading venue 21X and LayerZero say a private, permissioned deployment of Zero handled 10,000 order matches per second in a month-long technical pilot. The companies disclosed the result on November 4, reporting a 45-fold performance improvement against the EVM layer-two baseline they used.

European trading venue 21X and LayerZero say a private, permissioned deployment of Zero handled 10,000 order matches per second in a month-long technical pilot. The companies disclosed the result on November 4, reporting a 45-fold performance improvement against the EVM layer-two baseline they used. It is an unusually specific benchmark in a market full of throughput promises, although an internal pilot is not the same as sustained production trading under real market stress.

The partners say they ran a fully onchain central limit order book with an emphasis on US equity tokens. Matching orders onchain is technically different from recording a trade after an offchain matching engine has already decided the outcome. The reported throughput would therefore address a genuine challenge for blockchain-based venues. The private instance also matters: network membership and visibility can be controlled differently from a permissionless public chain. Comparing the result with public networks requires care because validators, transaction patterns, hardware assumptions and network conditions may not be equivalent.

Regulated markets need surveillance, market-access controls, records, resilience and procedures for trade errors. Some institutions are unwilling or unable to expose all transactional details on a public ledger. A permissioned environment can allow more predictable operations while still using programmable execution and a shared data layer. The trade-off is that permissioning changes trust and governance: someone must define who can participate, how upgrades occur and what happens when the network is unavailable. A fast benchmark does not settle those governance questions or prove that the system is suitable for every regulated market.

Performance comparisons are only as good as their baseline. LayerZero says the pilot was around 45 times faster than a referenced layer-two EVM setup. That is useful for the experiment's stated conditions, but it should not be generalized to all blockchains or all workloads. Throughput can vary when orders are cancelled, modified or subjected to complex compliance checks. Latency, finality, settlement integrity and recovery from outages matter as well. A trading venue would also need to demonstrate that market data and order priority remain fair at high volumes.

Tokenized securities are often described primarily as a custody or issuance problem. But active exchange trading introduces difficult requirements around price-time priority, matching, market making and operations during volatile conditions. The partnership suggests infrastructure providers are taking those less visible tasks seriously. The promise of running an order book onchain is more auditable shared state, but that benefit depends on execution design and on who can inspect the records. A private test is a starting point rather than proof of an operating securities market.

The next milestones are independent testing, disclosure of methodology, resilient deployment across multiple participants and approval for any relevant regulated activities. Real trading volume and incident history would be more persuasive than repeating peak performance figures. If 21X and LayerZero can demonstrate those outcomes, this could become an important example of high-throughput financial infrastructure. Until then, the strongest conclusion is that the companies reported a promising benchmark under controlled conditions.

Can the Rams really add a receiver to replace Puka Nacua?

The Los Angeles Rams will be without Puka Nacua for at least four weeks, and with the NFL trade deadline less that 24-hours away, should the Rams make a big-time trade for a wide receiver? Whether they do or not, there is no replacing Nacua, who's carried the offensive passing game since the Rams lost Davante Adams, who's also on IR.

If the Rams could make a blockbuster (or any) trade to save 2026, they would, but that may not be an option as the NFL trade period comes to its close. The Rams may choose to go with the group they have, injuries and all, and if they do, then they'll need to play better.

Change for the sake of change doesn't guarantee success. Though it could be naive of McVay to not make major changes on offense this season, the group of pass catchers we've seen may be the group going forward until guys get healthy, unless something changes, that appears to be the case.

L.A. has been rumored or speculated as linked to potentially trading for receivers like Josh Downs, Brian Thomas Jr. Stefon Diggs, George Pickens and more. Would any of these receivers do what Nacua does for L.A.'s offense?

The run after catch, the physicality, maybe. The relationship he's s built with Sean McVay, Matthew Stafford and the offense? Can any of the names above fill in well enough for both Nacua and Adams to make a trade worthwhile?

The Rams would have to make the trade to find out.

They could also trade for a receiver who struggles to connect and build chemistry with Stafford as L.A.'s veteran quarterback is struggling currently with receivers like Tutu Atwell, Jordan Whittington, Tyler Higbee and Colby Parkinson. Players Stafford has worked with for years.

Trading for a receiver like Thomas Jr. is splashy, but would he and Stafford work well together? Does he fit McVay's scheme? Would a player like Pickens fit McVay's culture? Are those receivers worth the Rams sending draft capital for?

Are there any receivers available for the Rams to get going into Week 10 that truly increase their odds at a playoff spot?

Going into their 10th game of the year, the Rams sit at 9th in the NFC playoff picture, below the Seattle Seahawks. Below the entire NFC North. Well below the 49ers who sit atop the NFC.

Injury is playing a role in the Rams struggles, but so is inconsistent offensive play and coaching. The defense for L.A. has overcome missing Myles Garrett, Trent McDuffie, Jaylen Watson, Aaron Donald, Nate Landman and more, but the defense for the Rams has been objectively better than their offense this year.

In Week 9, the Rams failed to convert a third down until the second half. The offense had Nacua on the field. Trade or not, the offense needs to be better.

Though it would be financially tough, the Rams could make a trade or add someone new. Just because they could, doesn't mean they should. And if they do, there is no guarantee that it would work. They can't trade for Nacua to save their season, and there is no recreating Nacua in the aggregate.

The Rams have options, but none of them may be able to help the Rams enough in 2026 to play past Week 18. The Rams had the entire offseason to seriously revamp or retool their receiver room. They didn't. Was that a feature or a glitch?

Due to injuries to Nacua, Adams and even Terrence Ferguson, L.A.'s passing game has been glitching, but the Rams went into this year thinking their pass catchers were a feature. At this point in the season, it may be too late for a trade to patch it. The Rams may just have to play better with who they have.

‘Walk to Stop the SERPent Pipeline’: Connecticut Groups Protest Proposed Eversource Pipeline

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

EAST HAMPTON, Connecticut — On Saturday, November 7, Sierra Club Connecticut supporters, other community groups, and allies walked for four hours across seven miles from Hurd State Park to Eversource Headquarters in East Hampton to protest the utility’s proposed Southeast Resilience Pipeline. Fifty people attended the Walk to Stop the SERPent Pipeline. Of the 50, 37 participated in the seven-mile walk to Eversource Headquarters.

Sierra Club and partner organizations have given the proposed Eversource pipeline the name “SERPent” to highlight its potential harm to public health, energy affordability, climate, and the Connecticut River which, if approved, the pipeline will run under.

“Prioritizing profit over the health of the environment is to our own detriment,” said Erin Day, Owner of Ten Summit Yoga and Earth Based Wellness. “Protecting our natural landscapes is essential, as the connection to the earth is what sustains our own well-being. Preserving this land from the pipeline is a vital step in honoring our relationship with nature and upholding our responsibility for a healthy future.”

“Building communities resilient to climate and economic crises requires improving people’s health and environments while preventing the danger and destruction posed by this pipeline and similar projects,” said Diana Martinez, of Chicks Ahoy Farm. “We can’t allow our futures to be sacrificed for corporate gain.”

“Eversource wants to build a pipeline under the Connecticut River because they make more money building pipelines than they do selling gas; they are a for profit company making a billion IN PROFIT per year so we know what their priorities are,” said Martha Klein, Sierra Club Connecticut and the No Pipeline Expansion Northeast Coalition. “Eversource is blocking the transition to the modern energy economy by expanding fracked gas infrastructure in our state, which locks us into methane use for decades. Burning fossil gas kills people, disrupts the climate and makes energy unaffordable, but it has been very profitable for Eversource. The time for change is here.”

“As young people, we are critically aware of the fallacies embedded in the short-term logic of fossil fuel infrastructure,” said Jo Almond, Sunrise Movement Wesleyan. “I grew up in a town with one hundred and forty-four active methane gas leaks; a result of relying on natural gas pipelines imposed by our utility company. I want to be part of the last generation who has to deal with the toxicity that fossil fuels introduce into our communities; this pipeline would guarantee that Connecticut’s youth will be growing up alongside poisoned water, air, and soil for generations to come.”

“Environmental racism harms all communities and it is important to hold corporations and utilities accountable for expanding poisonous methane gas into our communities and exposing us to environmental harms that exacerbate health, housing, education and economic barriers,” said Tenaya Taylor, Nonprofit Accountability Group.

The Sierra Club is America’s largest and most influential grassroots environmental organization, with millions of members and supporters. In addition to protecting every person's right to get outdoors and access the healing power of nature, the Sierra Club works to promote clean energy, safeguard the health of our communities, protect wildlife, and preserve our remaining wild places through grassroots activism, public education, lobbying, and legal action. BBC’s Near-Perfect Detective Thriller Teases Major Changes for American Remake

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Greer Riddell is a London-based film and television journalist writing about comedy, streaming and the evolving media industry in the UK and USA. She works at Collider, covering the latest in film, television and entertainment through interviews, features, reviews and industry coverage.

Her recent brand collaborations include HBO, Warner Bros., Netflix, Hulu, Sky, Disney+ and DC Studios. She has covered titles including Saturday Night Live UK, Rivals, Scrubs, Stuart Fails to Save the Universe, Ludwig, Supergirl and Stranger Things.

Her recent interviews include Florence Pugh, Mike Faist and Christopher Abbott for Netflix's East of Eden, Taika Waititi and Jenna Ortega for Klara and the Sun.

David Mitchell has made an unlikely detective out of a socially awkward, semi-reclusive puzzle setter in Ludwig, the BBC's hit murder mystery from Mark Brotherhood. With its combination of soft murder, a postcard-perfect British city and Mitchell in a role he seems born to play, the series has become one of the UK's most recent successes. Now, it's getting an American counterpart.

Kenton Allen, executive producer of Ludwig and CEO of Big Talk Productions, the company behind the series, spoke to Collider ahead of the Season 2 finale arriving on BritBox. He gave an update on what murder mystery fans can expect from Rabbit, the U.S. adaptation being developed for ABC by writers Humphrey Ker and Megan Ganz. But with the original series already finding an audience across North America through BritBox, why make an American version at all? And how much of Ludwig will make the journey across the Atlantic?

John Taylor lives a quiet, highly ordered life in his mother's house, rarely venturing beyond his familiar routines. He prefers the company of his books and puzzles, publishing his work under the professional pseudonym Ludwig. His identical twin brother, James, on the other hand, is a DCI in the Cambridge police force, married to Lucy (Anna Maxwell Martin), with whom he has a son, Henry. When James mysteriously disappears following his involvement in a murder investigation, Lucy turns to John for help finding him. Reluctant to get involved, John is eventually persuaded to take his brother's place at the police station, impersonating a detective while trying to establish what happened to James. However, his extraordinary ability to recognize patterns and solve complex puzzles proves surprisingly useful when it comes to solving murders, and he soon discovers that he's rather good at a job he has no qualifications for.

Britain makes more detective television than any country has any business making, and somehow almost all of it is good. Here is the one you belong in, what it says about you, and the case for it being the best of the four.

You want the tea, the gossip and the vicar behaving oddly. Midsomer is a county where the scenery is beautiful, the fete is on Saturday and the murder rate is roughly that of a small war, and absolutely nobody involved thinks this is strange.

You feel other people's grief as if it were handed to you personally. Broadchurch is less a whodunnit than a study of what a small town does to itself when it finds out that the answer was always going to be somebody everybody already knew.

You go through things rather than around them. John Luther is the best detective in London and a liability to everybody who works with him, and the show is completely honest that those two facts are the same fact.

You say the thing and you do not dress it up. Vera Stanhope turns up in a hat and a mac, is rude to almost everybody, and is the only person in the room prepared to ask the question that the family has been waiting years for somebody to ask.

The first season sees John navigate the increasingly complicated business of maintaining his brother's identity while investigating the circumstances surrounding his disappearance. By the Season 1 finale, the need for the impersonation has come to an end, but John's involvement with the police force doesn't. In Season 2, he continues helping the Cambridge detectives as a crime consultant, using his talents to solve a new murder each week while the wider mystery of James's whereabouts remains unresolved.

Alongside Mitchell and Maxwell Martin, Ludwig brings together familiar faces from British television for its weekly murder mysteries. Season 1 featured Sir Derek Jacobi as teacher Mr. Todd and Felicity Kendal as Lady Camilla Bright, while Season 2 welcomed Stephen Merchant as snooker player Danny Sloane in Episode 4. Rebecca Front and Doon Mackichan also appear as sisters whose mother is murdered at the theater. These guest appearances have made Ludwig a vehicle for seeing nostalgic names from British comedies in a completely different genre.

The show's success has extended well beyond its initial premise. According to the BBC, Ludwig Season 2 became 2026's biggest scripted launch across broadcasters and streamers to date, attracting 9.1 million viewers over 28 days. It also performed strongly on BBC iPlayer, where the opening episode attracted 5.5 million viewers, making it the second-biggest audience for the opening episode of a BBC comedy this year. The series has already been renewed for a third season, alongside a Christmas special. With those numbers, Ludwig has firmly established itself as a successful British detective series. So why does it need an American makeover?

The answer begins with understanding what makes Ludwig so appealing to international audiences, and why Allen believes some elements of the original are particularly well suited to export. Murder mysteries are hardly new for American television, with shows like Columbo and Monk helping establish the murder-of-the-week format long before John Taylor arrived in Cambridge. "There are some examples in the States now of this, and our inspiration for Ludwig," explains Allen.

However, he also believes the British setting is a significant part of what makes the series so distinctive. The production deliberately chose Cambridge as its backdrop and that sense of place is part of what Allen describes as "exportable UK" appeal. British murder mysteries have a long history of finding international audiences, and the combination of historic locations, familiar genre conventions and distinctive British talent gives them a particular appeal overseas. As Allen puts it, "Brilliant actors, brilliant writers, full stop. Our on-camera and off-camera talent is world-class." While the British setting and production talent might be part of the attraction, neither is necessarily something that can easily be recreated in an American adaptation.

Season 4 premiered in the U.K. last month.

Mitchell's portrayal of John Taylor is also central to the show's identity, and rather than attempting to find an American equivalent of Ludwig, the creative team wants to build something with its own personality. "Rabbit will be a brilliant cousin to Ludwig," Allen says. He believes the differences between the two countries will provide enough material to make the new series a distinct proposition, pointing to "where it's set and what the attitude is towards crime and crime prevention in North America" as important differences. "Our police don't carry guns," he jokes. "There's lots of cultural differences that I think will make an interesting companion piece to Ludwig."

Allen's thinking is that Rabbit should offer something familiar to fans of the original without becoming a direct copy. He points to the American adaptation of The Office as an example of how a British television concept can find an entirely new identity when moved across the Atlantic. Originally created by Ricky Gervais and Stephen Merchant, the BBC sitcom followed David Brent (Gervais) and the employees of the Wernham Hogg paper company in Slough, Buckinghamshire. When the concept was adapted for American television, Steve Carell took over as Michael Scott, managing the Dunder Mifflin paper company in Scranton, Pennsylvania. The very early episodes even borrowed directly from the British series, including the famous scene of Jim (John Krasinski) putting Dwight's (Rainn Wilson) stapler in jello. However, the American version quickly developed beyond those initial similarities. While the original British The Office consisted of 12 regular episodes and two Christmas specials, the American series ran for nine seasons and 201 episodes, eventually becoming a television institution in its own right.

Allen sees a similar opportunity with Rabbit. Rather than just reproducing the British series for a different audience, the intention is to take its central premise and explore what it could become in a different cultural setting. "I think that's what we're hoping to do with Rabbit, which is why it's called Rabbit and not American Ludwig," he explains. The important thing for Allen is that the new series has a reason to exist beyond the success of its predecessor. "It's seeing the fundamental setup through a different lens," he explains. "Nobody wants to make a facsimile of a show that we're making and is on air. So it has to have some reason to exist."

A large part of making that possible comes down to the writers involved. Rabbit is being developed by Ganz and Ker, two comedy writers with considerable experience across British and American television. Ganz previously worked on Community and Modern Family, earning an Emmy for her work, before becoming an executive producer on It's Always Sunny in Philadelphia and co-creating Apple TV+'s Mythic Quest alongside Rob McElhenney. Ker also has a connection to Mythic Quest, appearing in the series himself, and has worked closely with McElhenney through their shared involvement in football. Ker is the executive director of Wrexham AFC, the Welsh football club purchased by McElhenney and Ryan Reynolds. He subsequently became a familiar face to audiences through FX's Welcome to Wrexham and is also part of the writing team behind Saturday Night Live UK. The duo are also husband and wife.

Allen is particularly enthusiastic about what Ganz has brought to the project, praising her approach to making Rabbit feel connected to Ludwig without simply repeating it. He says that Ganz has found "a very cute, smart way of making a cousin rather than a copy." That distinction will be important for the adaptation. Ludwig already has an established audience, both in the UK and internationally, and its success on BritBox means American viewers can continue watching Mitchell's original portrayal of John Taylor. Rabbit doesn't need to replace that version or convince audiences that they should choose one over the other. Instead, the hope is that viewers who enjoy one will have another detective series to turn to, with its own characters, setting, and approach to crime.

Colin Farrell's Remake Of A '90s Sci-Fi Movie Is One Of Prime Video's Biggest Streaming Hits

"Total Recall" (2012) is directed by Len Wiseman, who most recently oversaw the 2025 "John Wick" offshoot "Ballerina" and previously helmed the "Underworld" films between 2003 and 2016. He is, unfortunately, also responsible for 2007's "Live Free or Die Hard," which, despite making the third spot on /Film's "Die Hard" movie ranking, we simply could have done without.

Unlike the 1990 original, "Total Recall" is set entirely on an Earth ravaged by chemical war, leaving only the United Federation of Britain (UFB) and the Colony (Australia) habitable. Whereas Arnold Schwarzenegger's Douglas Quaid had dreams of Mars, which turn out to be long-buried memories, Colin Farrell's counterpart finds himself experiencing nocturnal visions of being a secret agent. Bored of his factory job on The Colony, he enlists the company Rekall to implant artificial memories to match his dreams, which he soon learns are, in fact, memories of having worked as a UFB agent. Hunted by his former employers, he teams up with Jessica Biel's Melina, a member of a rebel group known as The Resistance. But as the pair try to outrun the UFB and their Chancellor Vilos Cohaagen (Bryan Cranston), the line between fantasy and reality becomes increasingly blurred.

Ahead of the film's debut, /Film asked: "Is 'Total Recall' the least essential film of the summer?" The answer, according to most critics, was yes. But it seems the Prime Video crowds are willing to give it another shot. Per streaming viewership tracker FlixPatrol, "Total Recall" is the No. 3 most-watched movie on Prime Video in the United States as of November 9, 2026. What's more, based on the film's trajectory thus far, it could be set for a chart-topping run. "Total Recall" debuted on the charts at No. 9 on November 7, 2026, before jumping to fourth the following day and hitting No. 3 the day after. By the end of the week, Farrell's forgotten remake might just hit No. 1 — not bad for a film with a 30% critic score on Rotten Tomatoes.

Both Philip K. Dick's story and the 1990 "Total Recall" film feature Mars. But the 2012 remake actually has more in common with Paul Verhoeven's rendition than the novel, using characters that only appear in the movie and adhering more closely to the big-screen storyline. That might have seemed like a reasonable approach, given how the first film was so successful. Unfortunately, Len Wiseman's effort didn't emulate the offbeat comedy of the original, which was so sharply rendered by Arnold Schwarzenegger and gave the film such a unique tone. Despite featuring some impressive action scenes, Wiseman's version also managed to feel like a generic sci-fi actioner, all of which combined to yield a less-than-impressive critical response.

That said, Roger Ebert was impressed enough to bestow three stars on the film — just half a star less than the 1990 version — and called it a "well-crafted, high-energy sci-fi." Still, even he had to admit that the film was one of several sci-fi movie remakes that should have never happened, ending his review by writing, "It never touched me emotionally, though, the way the 1990 film did, and strictly speaking, isn't necessary." Others were much more condemnatory, with David Sexton of the London Evening Standard giving the film one star and writing, "We used to do the future so much better. Let's not forget that. Let's forget this instead."

Normally, that should deter you from joining the "Total Recall" renaissance on Prime Video. But considering that, since the movie's debut, we've been subjected to an onslaught of streaming movie shabbiness, it might be worth a go to see if the remake really is bad or whether that's merely a memory implanted by the critics.
