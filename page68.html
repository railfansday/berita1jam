Sebastian Hoeneß left with mixed emotions after frustrating Galatasaray defeat

Stuttgart suffered their first defeat of the Champions League campaign on Tuesday evening, with two goals from Aleksey Batrakov inside the opening 15 minutes proving decisive in a 2-0 victory for Turkish champions Galatasaray.

However, the visitors were left wondering what might have been, having created plenty of chances throughout the contest.

Jamie Leweling remarkably struck both posts with the same effort before Galatasaray took the lead, while Deniz Undav also hit the woodwork after the interval, and Chris Führich eventually found the net, only for his effort to be ruled out for offside.

Speaking to DAZN after the final whistle, boss Sebastian Hoeneß struggled to hide his frustration at the manner of the defeat.

"I think we had a good chance to take the lead, a very good chance," he explained.

"We'd already had one or two really good breakthroughs down the wing before that. We got into the final third, we looked dangerous. I had the feeling that the game was just starting to swing our way.

"And then, in exactly that phase, looking back, you have to say that's where we lost the game. We conceded two goals.

Despite the early setback, Stuttgart did continue to threaten, particularly during a much-improved second-half performance.

Hoeneß was full of praise for his players' response as a result, but he admitted their failure to find a breakthrough remained difficult to accept.

"In the second half, I can only take my hat off to the performance the boys showed. They left everything out on the pitch.

“It would have been very interesting to see what would have happened if we'd scored a goal, and there were enough opportunities.”

"It's starting to add up a little. Dženan [Pejčinović] had another chance, Tiago Tomas had another chance. There were other really good opportunities as well.

"But of course, there were also those three or four minutes when we conceded the goals. From my point of view, they were Galatasaray's first shots on goal. I don't think Fabi [Bredlow] had anything to save before that. Both went in, and then it becomes incredibly difficult."

Nevertheless, Hoeneß was determined to recognise the efforts of his squad on their travels to Istanbul.

"I'm proud of the boys for the performance they showed, for the way they delivered what they'd set out to do in this atmosphere.

"On the other hand, when you look at how the game went, it's also very, very frustrating that we've lost."

Stuttgart remain on seven points from their opening four Champions League fixtures, having previously made an encouraging start to the league phase.

Apple warns of risks related to AI agents behaving in 'unintended or unpredictable' ways

📅 2026-11-03  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Apple said in its annual financial filing on Tuesday that AI agents that can act in "unintended or unpredictable ways" could represent a threat to its business by opening the company up to lawsuits or hurting its reputation.

The new risk factors in Apple's filing show the disruptive potential of AI agents, which have rapidly become top of mind for Silicon Valley's most important companies.

"In particular, artificial intelligence agents and other technologies can take actions autonomously, including interacting with internal or external systems that in some cases they may not be authorized to access, executing or altering code, sending communications, and completing transactions," Apple wrote in the filing.

The company added that agents "may behave in unintended or unpredictable ways, including by taking actions that were not directed, instructed or authorized."

Earlier this year, OpenAI released Dots, and Meta introduced Muse. Both AI agents use advanced AI models to work autonomously for tasks. They can even email or remind users when new messages arrive.

Apple's disclosure comes after several episodes that involved rogue AI agents breaking out of their sandboxes to hack third-party websites, like the Hugging Face incident, which has drawn renewed attention from policymakers in Washington, D.C., and around the world.

Separately, the ability of agents to use a computer in human ways is forcing big companies like Amazon and JPMorgan Chase to come up with policies around agentic access to services for strategic or security reasons.

Last month, Apple announced changes to full disk access on Macs to reduce "risks related to this level of access" for AI agents installed on PCs.

"The Company's ability to oversee and control third-party artificial intelligence agents and other technologies may be limited," Apple warned in its filing.

Apple doesn't currently have an agentic AI. But when new CEO John Ternus was asked on Monday's earnings call about AI agents, he said that the company had "real room" to expand Siri's capabilities.

"As far as agentic capabilities, we think these are promising tools for users," Ternus said. "We just want to make sure that users are aware of what they are giving access to. We want to make sure this is done in a way that protects security and privacy."

This fall, Apple released Siri AI, a new assistant that uses the iPhone's local data storage to access data like calendars and emails, but Apple's Siri can't work autonomously for long stretches like Dots or Muse.

Ternus said Apple can't access private data on its devices, calling it a "core differentiator."

"If you think about it, so much of your personal data is on your iPhone, whether it's your contacts, your communications, health data, financial data, all of that," Ternus said. "Siri can tap into that and help you access it in a way that's very secure and private and that nobody else has access to."

Commercial AI agents like those from OpenAI and Meta ask for high-level access to the user's accounts like email in order to be more useful.

Apple indicated that the current regulations around AI and "super intelligence" are changing, unpredictable and that it could cost a lot of money to adapt to new laws governing AI.

"In addition, the Company is subject to the risks posed by ethical concerns and negative consumer perceptions related to automation, artificial intelligence and super intelligence, including perceived threats to safety or other societal harms or complications," according to the filing.

Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac rally as Ackman presses for conservatorship exit

📅 2026-11-03  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The midterm elections could be a turning point for the release of Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac from government control, according to billionaire investor Bill Ackman.

Investors may agree with him, if the rally in both stocks is any sign.

The Federal National Mortgage Association, known as Fannie Mae, and Federal Home Loan Mortgage Corporation, or Freddie Mac, have each jumped more than 60% from Oct. 14 to Monday's close. The stocks were both up roughly 3% on Tuesday.

Ackman, the founder of Pershing Square Capital Management, has long pushed for the two entities to be released from federal conservatorship.

"The reason why it's not being touched right now is, you know, midterms," he said in an interview with CNBC's "Squawk Box."

Ackman, who owns both names through Pershing Square, believes Freddie and Fannie have the potential to increase "10-fold from current share prices," he said in his quarterly letter to shareholders, issued on Monday.

Fannie and Freddie buy mortgages from banks and other lenders, and then package them into mortgage-backed securities, known as MBS. Their operations are critical to the housing market, providing liquidity to banks and creating a secondary market for MBS.

The entities were placed into conservatorship in September 2008, during the financial crisis. The stocks were delisted from the New York Stock Exchange in 2010 and have traded over the counter since then.

Ackman wants the conservatorship to end, arguing the entities would be worth multiples of their current valuation if they were freed from government control.

President Donald Trump has supported ending the conservatorship of Fannie and Freddie. But it remains politically and economically contentious.

The entities operate under strict capital threshold requirements and would need to be recapitalized, which could happen through issuing common equity. However, the Treasury Department would need to figure out an exit plan for its roughly $388 billion ownership stake in the entities through preferred securities and warrants.

Congress may also need to play a role, passing legislation to restructure the government's role in mortgage financing and home-loan guarantees.

Nonetheless, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent recently said it could happen before Trump's term ends, telling 2Way's "The Morning Meeting" podcast that the administration will tackle the ownership structure "next year, the year after." He said affordability will be guiding the decision.

One point of controversy is whether ending the conservatorship of Fannie and Freddie would increase the spreads on MBS over Treasuries, effectively making mortgages costlier. Bessent has sought to reassure the market that wouldn't happen.

"Whatever we do is going to be guided by making sure that the spread on [agency] mortgages … over the Treasury does not increase," Bessent said.

Ackman argues that privatizing the GSEs would help insulate them from political meddling. "Fannie and Freddie are these massive, enormously profitable institutions where whichever political party controls them…if they're still in conservatorship, can influence the outcome of elections," he told CNBC.

In one scenario, he explained, Democrats win the White House, conservatorship is still in place, and Democrats push Fannie and Freddie to lower credit standards in order to expand home ownership and affordability.

"If they're going to move more aggressively into borrowers with lower credit quality, for example, to achieve a political objective, that's going to have an impact on trillions of dollars outstanding mortgage securities," Ackman said. "It's going to have an impact on overall credit spreads."

MBS investors want certainty about how the entities will be run, he noted.

"That only gets resolved in a world in which they exit conservatorship, they become publicly traded companies that are owned by institutional and retail investors," Ackman said. "We set the rules. In conservatorship, the president can do anything."

In The Citadel Project, "the last line of defense built by the Ancient Kings in an attempt to preserve what remains of humanity in the face of the Great Storm." Some kind of evil force is ruining it from the inside, and you have to make your way through a medieval megastructure to figure out what happened. I'm also a fan of your main weapon, a crossbow. Not enough games with crossbows if you ask me! Bruce Springsteen Urges Officials to Protect the Vote on Election Day: ‘Our Vote Is Our Voice’

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

"You have a solemn duty to ensure that our votes are securely counted," he said in a video message.

Bruce Springsteen has a message for election officials on Election Day.

On Tuesday (Nov. 3), the 77-year-old music icon and longtime political activist shared a brief video message on social media, urging “election officials and representatives of our government” to ensure votes are securely counted.

Standing in front of a stone wall and dressed in a flannel shirt, Springsteen delivered a direct appeal to those overseeing the electoral process.

“I’m speaking to election officials and representatives of our government on Election Day,” Springsteen said on Instagram. “You have sworn an oath to preserve, protect, and defend our Constitution. That means you have a solemn duty to ensure that our votes are securely counted.”

He continued, “Thomas Jefferson said, ‘Government requires a consent of the governed.’ You who represent us must never consent to any directives that undermine our constitutional right to a free and fair election.”

Election Day marks the 2026 midterm elections, with voters across the country casting ballots in congressional races that will determine control of the House of Representatives and Senate. Voters are also deciding a number of gubernatorial, state legislative and local contests.

“Our vote is our voice,” Springsteen said in conclusion. “And it’s up to you to ensure that our voice is heard. I wish you all a good Election Day, and thank you for your service.”

In the caption accompanying the 45-second clip, Springsteen thanked those voting on Election Day and encouraged his followers to continue supporting democracy by taking the Oath to the Constitution at oathproject.org.

Merck Mercuriadis, founder of the influential music catalog investment group Hipgnosis, took to the comments, writing, “The boss has spoken – for 50 years he has spoken truthfully about the cracks in ‘merica in order to help us become the best version of ourselves that we can be. Born to vote!!!!!!!!!”

This isn’t the first time Springsteen has spoken out about voting rights and election integrity. In 2012, the “Dancing in the Dark” singer-songwriter criticized voter suppression efforts in several states, writing that Americans of all political views should oppose what he described as “anti-voter, anti-citizen efforts.” He also raised concerns about the future of American democracy during the 2024 presidential election, when he endorsed Kamala Harris and Tim Walz.

A vocal critic of President Donald Trump and longtime Democratic supporter, Springsteen released his protest anthem “Streets of Minneapolis” earlier this year in response to the fatal shootings of Renee Good and Alex Pretti amid the administration’s immigration crackdown. He also performed the song during his recent Land of Hope and Dreams Tour.

During a January 2026 benefit concert in New Jersey, Springsteen also spoke out about the Trump administration, saying, “The United States, the ideals and the values for which it stood for the past 250 years, is being tested like it has never been in modern times. Those values and those ideals have never been as endangered as they are right now.”

On Monday (Nov. 2), Springsteen also threw his support behind Democrat Abdul El-Sayed in the Michigan Senate race.

“Dr. El-Sayed is a fighter for our American Constitution, he’s a fighter for our American Democracy, and he’s a fighter against the corrupt, lawless, and anti-American leadership currently in the White House,” he wrote on Instagram. “That’s why I’m in his corner 100%.”

Copycat Coopers Hawk Red Wine–Braised Short Ribs

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

This copycat Cooper's Hawk red wine-braised short ribs recipe delivers fall-apart-tender short ribs bathed in a luxurious sauce. It's like dining out in your kitchen.

One taste of the red wine–braised short ribs at Cooper’s Hawk is enough to understand why they’ve remained a bestseller. A generous portion of melt-in-your-mouth braised short ribs is stacked over a mound of buttery whipped potatoes, then drizzled with umami-rich braising liquid. When served with roasted vegetables and garnished with crispy fried onion strings, it’s a plate of comfort food that’s just as much a feast for the eyes as it is for the taste buds.

Whether you’ve been to Cooper’s Hawk and want to recreate the magic at home, or you don’t live near a Cooper’s Hawk location and want to know what all the fuss is about, this copycat recipe belongs on your must-try list. It’s the perfect recipe for chilly days when you want a meal that feels like a warm hug. It’s also impressive-looking and great for feeding a crowd, so keep it in mind as the holiday season approaches.

Cooper’s Hawk Winery and Restaurant is something like a more refined Cheesecake Factory that’s known for its wine rather than its cheesecake. Let me break it down. First, each chain greets diners by showcasing its specialties: Where the Cheesecake Factory has cases of cheesecake, Cooper’s Hawk has a well-appointed wine shop. Second, both are known for extensive menus that span a wide range of global influences and cuisines. Finally, both have a long list of signature dishes (we’ve replicated many Cheesecake Factory copycat recipes over the years) that keep customers coming back. These copycat Cooper’s Hawk red wine–braised short ribs, for example, are an ode to the dish that’s been on the menu since the restaurant opened its very first location in 2005.

Preheat the oven to 425°F. Sprinkle the short ribs with salt and pepper. In an ovenproof Dutch oven over medium-high heat, brown the ribs in oil in batches. Remove the meat and set it aside.

Add the celery, carrot and onion to the drippings, and cook them until they’re tender. Add the garlic, and cook for one minute longer. Add the wine and broth, stirring to loosen the browned bits from the pan.

Return the ribs to the pot. Add the parsley and thyme. Cover the pot and transfer it to the preheated oven. Cook the ribs for 2 hours to 2 hours and 30 minutes or until the meat is tender.

While the ribs are braising, in a small bowl, whisk all the butter ingredients until they’re blended. Transfer the mixture to a sheet of storage wrap, and roll it into a log. Refrigerate the butter until it’s chilled. Unwrap and slice it.

Place the hot mashed potatoes on a serving plate. Top them with the short ribs. Immediately top the ribs with a slice of Dijon butter and french-fried onions. Garnish the dish with additional fresh parsley, if desired.

Store any leftover short ribs in an airtight container in the refrigerator. Enjoy them within four days. Leftovers can be microwaved in a microwave-safe container, or they can be transferred to a Dutch oven and warmed in a 350° oven until they’re heated through.

Braised short ribs freeze like a dream. Store them in a freezer-safe airtight container and freeze them for three to four months. Defrost them completely in the refrigerator before warming them in the microwave or in the oven.

Short ribs are a heavily marbled cut of beef from the lower portion of the rib cage (the same area as the chuck and brisket). They have a robust, beefy flavor but contain a lot of connective tissue, so you need to cook them slowly with low heat for the meat to become tender.

Braising is one of the best (and most popular) ways to prepare short ribs. That said, you can make bone-in short ribs in a slow cooker or on a wood, pellet or charcoal smoker. To speed up the process, you can also make tender bone-in short ribs in a pressure cooker.

Cooper’s Hawk serves their short ribs with mashed potatoes and roasted vegetables, but that is far from the only pairing option. Serve them over a bowl of creamy polenta or on a bed of buttered egg noodles (similar to beef Stroganoff). For freshness, add a garden salad, grilled asparagus or creamed spinach for a more indulgent spin.

Greenland's parliament ratifies Trump's US security agreement

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

The US is set to expand its military footprint on the Arctic island, part of the Kingdom of Denmark. It follows pressure from Donald Trump earlier in the year which opened a major rift in the transatlantic alliance.

Greenland's parliament on Tuesday signed off on a trilateral security and military-access agreement with the United States and Denmark that paves the way for an expanded US' military presence on the strategically located and mineral-rich island.

The deal has alleviated pressure coming from US President Donald Trump, who earlier this year had repeatedly threatened to take control of the self-governing Danish territory, citing US security interests.

After months of negotiation the agreement was signed in September by Trump, Danish Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen and Greenlandic Prime Minister Jens-Frederik Nielsen on the sidelines of the UN General Assembly in New York.

The agreement allows for two new US military bases and Trump's "Golden Dome" missile defence system. It also keeps Greenland under Danish control, while reaffirming Greenlanders' right to self determination.

On Tuesday, shortly before the vote in the capital, Nuuk, Greenlandic Foreign Minister Mute Egede welcomed the agreement's ratification.

"We must accept that peace and stability are no longer something we can take for granted," Egede said.

In a first debate last month, a majority of Greenlandic lawmakers had already signalled support for the agreement.

Pele Broberg, chair of the pro-independence Naleraq party, questioned the deal's value for Greenland.

"We are looking for a better agreement that we can benefit from when we become an independent state. But no economic benefit has been gained from the agreement," Broberg said.

The agreement will come into effect after ratification by the Danish parliament, which is due to read the bill over two sessions in November. A vote is scheduled for November 19 in Copenhagen.

In January 2026, Trump began calling for the US to control Greenland, and threatened the use of military force and tariffs against US European allies if he did not get his way.

Trump had claimed that Denmark was not doing enough to defend its autonomous arctic territory from unspecified threats from China and Russia.

Trump's repeated threats damaged US ties with Denmark and rattled the US' European NATO allies.

On January 21, after Trump met with NATO chief Mark Rutte on the sidelines of the Global Economic Forum in Davos, tensions cooled after Rutte and Trump announced the "concept of a deal" allowing for expanded US defense access to Greenland, which would later evolve into the agreement signed in September.

Denmark had repeatedly said it was willing to discuss expanding the US military footprint in Greenland, while rejecting Trump's more extreme demand that Greenland become US territory.

Critics have pointed out the same deal could have been made without the extreme disruption caused by Trump's territorial and tariff threats.

Under a 1951 agreement, the US already has considerable military freedom of action in Greenland. After years of voluntarily drawing down its military presence, only the Pituffik Space Base in northwestern Greenland remains, which is used for missile defense and radar surveillance.

British fintech Revolut slashes AI coding costs 10x by cutting out Claude and going self-hosted

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Turns out cheap tokens get engineers doing things that save time, like debugging and tackling Jira tickets

Revolut says that in less than a year it's managed to slash its AI coding inference costs by roughly 90 percent by making self-hosted models its default instead of relying primarily on commercial services like Claude.

Speaking at the AI Engineer conference in New York last month, David Abitbol, ops and infrastructure lead at the British fintech, described how it saw a 60 percent improvement in coding time and a 70 percent improvement in lead time following its rollout of AI coding tools to roughly 2,000 engineers. Revolut initially adopted GLM-5.1 before moving to GLM-5.3, which it now serves using 56 Nvidia B200 GPUs.

Prior to its switch to GLM, Revolut had been experimenting with AI, deploying Gemini in early 2025 and Claude Code in early 2026. "We were highly skeptical about the cost of AI. We were seeing engineers spend $1,000 a month, $10,000 a month with not so much ROI, and so when we launched it we launched it with strict budget limits," Abitbol said.

Because of this, engineers only had access to a limited number of tokens, so the company began investigating self-hosting iyd own models. At $0.30 per million input/output tokens, self hosting GLM 5.1 cost about a tenth of using OpenAI's Codex or Anthropic's Claude Code. And, as it turned out, when tokens are cheap, you stop worrying about how many they use or what they use them for.

While Revolut did see improvements to coding speed, Abitbol noted the biggest bottleneck wasn't actually the code. Instead, he says the biggest gains came from letting the AI models deal with things like debugging and Jira and Confluence tickets. "When you have unlimited tokens, you're free to do that," he said. "You no longer have to save your tokens for the end of the quarter or the most difficult task."

Developed by Chinese open weights model dev Z.ai, GLM 5.1 isn't exactly a small model. At roughly 754 billion total parameters (40 billion activated for each token generated), the model requires roughly 380 GB of GPU memory just to hold the weights and another 6-26 GB per user to maintain context depending on precision.

To serve GLM-5.3 at scale, Revolut deployed 56 Nvidia B200 GPUs, equivalent in GPU count to seven eight-GPU DGX B200 systems. But as Abitbol noted, the greatest performance gains didn't come from adding GPUs, but rather tuning the model's hyperparameters along with switching to NVFP4 quantization, implementing disaggregated prefill and decode, and tuning context lengths.

One key takeaway was that supporting a one-million-token context window doesn't necessarily make such a large window useful or efficient. "Long context is a scam. You really need to be mindful of that," Abitbol said. "Even though you can serve one million tokens, 256 [thousand] is really what you need."

With these optimizations, Abitbol says that the company was able to achieve sub 3-second time to first token on context windows of around 256,000 tokens, and generation speeds of around 200 tokens a second, both of which he notes are the sweet spot for the company's software engineers.

"What you want to aim for is a sub-3 second time to first token. This is empirical evidence. As soon as you exceed 5 seconds, my Slack inbox starts to blow up," Abitbol said. "In terms of throughput, it doesn't really matter as much. We serve, in terms of interactivity, 200 tokens per second. People are fine [with that]. You don't need to go higher. The way people work has changed. We no longer look at the stream of thinking tokens. People run tasks in parallel and check in when the task is done."

And if throughput is a concern, he notes engineers who sign in after 5 pm are getting closer to 800 tokens a second.

However, it's worth noting that GLM hasn't replaced GPT or Claude entirely. The Chinese model is text only, so it isn't ideal for things like UI work. So instead, Revolut used a model router to direct vision queries to models like Gemini or GPT Luna while keeping code generation running on GLM.

"Open source is now a priority at Revolut, and our economics are favorable. The billing is flat, we don't have any bill shock at the end of the month, and the frontier is the exception," Abitbol said.

Having said that, Abitbol acknowledges that Chinese open weights models may be a concern for privacy and security reasons, but emphasizes that the major concern there is accessing them via APIs running out of China. When run on-prem or on regional cloud resources, there's really nothing to worry about. ®
