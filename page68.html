Record crowds, legends - Bordeaux bid to escape amateur game

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Bordeaux fans protested when the club were demoted to the sixth tier

The latest stop on Zinedine Zidane's homecoming tour was an important one for the new France head coach. When Les Bleus took on Turkey in Bordeaux on Sunday night, the ex-midfielder was returning to the city in which he spent four formative years of his playing career, between 1992 and 1996.

"I became a man in Bordeaux," Zidane fondly recalled in the build-up. France edged triumphed 2-1 with an uninspiring display, but it did not dampen the mood of the 40,000 fans inside the Stade Atlantique.

While the 1998 World Cup winner was not necessarily in familiar surroundings (the out-of-town stadium was built for Euro 2016, 20 years after his departure for Juventus), locals made every effort to make him feel at home, holding up a commemorative tifo before kick-off and chanting Zizou's name throughout.

Just a few miles up the road earlier that afternoon, Zidane's former club were playing their fifth match of the season in the sixth-tier Regional 1, in a north-western suburb of the city.

Thousands of Bordeaux fans had made the short away outing to Saint-Medard-en-Jalles' 3000-seater ground to watch their team seal a fifth win in five this season.

Girondins de Bordeaux, the six-time French champions, who were once regulars in European cups, now find themselves facing amateur opposition on a weekly basis.

Since 2022, the club has plummeted from Ligue 1 to the sixth tier through a combination of on-pitch struggles, mismanagement at all levels, and sanctions due to their financial troubles.

The club was taken over in 2021 by Gerard Lopez, who was previously in charge of Formula 1's Lotus as well as another French side, Lille.

While Bordeaux were already hamstrung by debts at that point, the situation on and off the pitch worsened under the Spanish-Luxembourgish businessman as the men's first team finished rock bottom of Ligue 1 in his first season.

A couple of seasons in Ligue 2 followed, before the club was docked down to the fourth tier in 2024. In their first National 2 season, they notably signed Andy Carroll but ultimately missed out on promotion.

The following campaign saw a repeat of that near miss, and the club was expelled from national-level football altogether this summer over their finances.

With Bordeaux left on the brink and whittled down to just eight full-time employees (down from around 250 a few years beforehand), Lopez sold up to Park Bench, a group of mainly British USA-based investors who also own Scottish side Dunfermline as well as a minority stake in Spanish second tier side Cordoba.

The supporters turned out in their numbers for the first home match of the campaign, which had been delayed amid the uncertainty over whether the club could continue running, against Stade Montois.

More than 11,000 tickets sold, a record crowd for that level of French football.

"140 years of history, we won't be stopped," read one of the banners held up by the Ultramarines fan group, who also made it a point to thank the seven first-team players who had stayed despite the club's double relegation.

The team is centred around a core of players with Ligue 1 and Ligue 2 experience. Steve Shamal, who scored a hat-trick in that 4-1 victory over Stade Montois, previously turned out for the likes of Caen and Annecy in the second tier.

"We know we should be higher up, but that certainty can also be a trap," said Shamal after the game. "We shouldn't think that it's going to be easy.

"We need to respect our opponents if we're going to put in good performances."

As the sixth tier of French football is regionalised, only the league winner in the Nouvelle Aquitaine region's top flight will earn promotion.

Their on-pitch success so far this season comes as little surprise given the gulf in quality compared to the rest of the league. Over the summer, Bordeaux notably managed to hold on to Europa League-winning full-back Almamy Toure, while former Belgian youth international striker Baptiste Guillaume also joined.

Several former players have also looked to get involved in Bordeaux's revival.

Benoit Tremoulinas, the former French international who was a part of the title-winning squad in 2008-09, notably announced that at the age of 40 he would be lacing up his boots again seven years after injury forced him to retire.

Paul Baysse, the former centre-back, is an assistant to head coach Arnaud Cormier.

Benoit Tremoulinas spent six years at Bordeaux, winning a league title and two cups, before also twice winning the Europa League with Sevilla

Perhaps the most significant endorsement the new owners could have hoped for was that of Alain Giresse, widely regarded as the club's greatest player. The Euro 1984 winner, who won three league titles while playing more than 500 times for the Marine et Blanc, was in attendance for the match against Stade Montois. He took the ceremonial kick-off before giving a full-time dressing room speech.

The new management unveiled their long-term plans for the club on Monday at Le Haillan, the city-owned training ground which includes a chateau that hosts the club's headquarters.

"We are humbled to have this responsibility," Park Bench investor Jeremy Green said.

"The club doesn't belong to Park Bench, it belongs to Bordeaux, it belongs to the 11,000 people who came to watch a Regional 1 game, to the players and to the community. We have a responsibility to rebuild this club.

"Our commitment to the community and to the players is that we will be fiscally responsible and patient.

"We're not a financial institution, we're a collection of individuals who bring with them business, sporting, analytical, and entrepreneurship skills."

Dominique Delport, the club's new president, revealed he knew Green and Twining through his wife, who studied at Oxford with them.

"We can learn from the club's golden years," the former journalist and advertising executive told BBC Sport, before adding that he would look to bring club legends to every league game.

Bordeaux won the Coupe de France as recently as 2013

While the club was sold for a symbolic euro, it did involve taking on long-standing debts which spanned transfer fees, stadium rent, and a compensation claim by former head coach Vladimir Petkovic.

Last Friday, the city's commercial court approved a request to restructure the debt as part of the club's continuation plan.

When Bordeaux were expelled from national-level football by the French FA at the start of the summer, the club used several avenues to appeal while the takeover was being finalised.

"We thought we had acquired the club in National 1 [the fourth tier]," Green and co-investor James Twining, told BBC Sport.

"Initially we walked away, but we were encouraged to come back," Green added. "We established that we were going to be able to restructure things and make it work.

"People around the club and in the local community encouraged us to try to find a way."

The six-time French champions have a long way to go before returning to the top flight.

"We're committing to a sensible financial structure, to a well-run club, and we're talking about 10 years to get this club back to Ligue 1," the new investors added.

A crucial step towards that long-term goal will be returning to the professional leagues, which would allow them to re-open the youth academy they were made to close when they dropped down.

Rebuilding the women's team, who were involved in the Champions League only a few seasons ago, is another one of the new owners' objectives.

Keeping the fans onside throughout the process will also be key, not least after the rift that opened up between groups during the previous ownership.

In any case, if the record-breaking turnout for Bordeaux's return to league action is anything to go by, their support seems unlikely to waver anytime soon.

Tame Impala Dominates 2026 ARIA Awards

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

With seven wins from eight nominations, Kevin Parker's Tame Impala trumped the 40th annual ARIA Awards, presented Wednesday evening (Nov. 18) at Sydney's Hordern Pavilion.

With seven wins from eight nominations, Tame Impala was no deadbeat at the 40th annual ARIA Awards, presented Wednesday evening (Nov. 18) at Sydney’s Hordern Pavilion. Explore Tame Impala See latest videos, charts and news

Kevin Parker’s psychedelic pop outfit snagged the biggest honors on the night, including album of the year for Tame Impala’s fifth studio album, Deadbeat; best solo artist; and song of the year for “Dracula.” On winning solo artist, the Western Australian singer, songwriter and multi-instrumentalist used the moment to unfurl the joke that was on everyone’s mind. ARIA has now put the argument to bed that, yes, Tame Impala is a one-man band. Actually, it’s not so clear-cut. Tame Impala has, in fact won for best group at the ARIAs on three occasions, in 2013, 2015, and 2020. Related Noah Kahan Reacts to 2027 Grammy Nominations Snub: 'I Was In My Feelings for a Sec' 'Grand Theft Auto VI' Revs Its Engines & Leads 2026 Game Awards Nominations: Full List Cody Johnson on Why He's Skipping 2026 CMA Awards: 'Going 100 MPH All the Time Isn't Healthy'

By snagging album of the year for Deadbeat, a record that went to No. 2 on the ARIA Albums Chart in 2025, but missed out on the crown and got a lukewarm review from some armchair critics, Parker got the last laugh. “I’m so grateful,” he said. “There are so many great f—ing Australian artists and so many great nominees.” Deadbeat “got off to a rocky start, to be honest…but I’m so proud of it. It’s getting the respect that it deserves, I think.”

As previously reported, Tame Impala collected the second annual Global Impact Award presented by Spotify. Savage Garden’s Darren Hayes was on hand to present the honor. “I’m so proud to be an Aussie artist, always,” Parker remarked. “Whenever I go overseas, I feel like I wear a badge on my shirt.” And, he noted, he’s “one of the lucky ones,” an artist who’s had an international career and just this week collected four Grammy Award nominations.

Another artist enjoying a global career is Ecca Vandal. As the Glastonbury Festival 2027 lineup dropped with her name on it, the Australian punk rock artist was converting four of her seven ARIA Award nominations, including the Michael Gudinski breakthrough artist, best independent release and best alternative release, in respect to her sophomore album Looking For People To Unfollow. “Not sure what breakthrough means,” she said after snagging the award named after Michael Gudinski, the late, great Mushroom Group founder, but “the biggest breakthrough for me was realizing I didn’t need to be anybody but myself.” The rising star also gave a shout out to those people who said “no” along the way, a burn which forced her to “dig deeper.” The naysayers, “you did me a really big favor.”

After collecting their first-ever ARIA No. 1 in August with their self-titled fifth album, DMA’S won a brace of ARIA Awards, including best group, while G Flip was also a two-time winner on the night (best video and best Australian live act).

K-pop superstars BTS won for most popular international artist, beating the likes of Taylor Swift, Olivia Rodrigo and Olivia Dean. Other winners included Bluey (best children’s album), Ninajirachi (Porter Robinson, for best dance/electronic release), Donny Benét (best jazz album), Emma Donovan (best blues & roots album) and Karnivool (best hard rock/heavy metal album).

If music was the glue that connected the night, artificial intelligence — or rather, the threat of it to the creative community — was the theme. On each industry table, reminder of the “Not AI’s to Take” campaign, while presenters including Spiderbait’s Kram, Tim Minchin, Tash Sultana and Elton John shared their thoughts on the matter. Sultana, the singer, songwriter and guitarist, offered a poem as she presented the independent category: “roses are red, violets are blue, if you support AI music, f— you.”

ARIA doesn’t support it. Earlier this year, the trade body announced it would ban from its official charts those recordings that featured mostly AI-generated music.

Performers at the 2026 ARIA Awards included Genesis Owusu; Blusher; Ecca Vandal; 3%, Scribe and Fred Leone; BOY SODA; and 2026 ARIA Hall of Fame inductees Spiderbait, which closed out the show with a medley of their hits including “Calypso,” “Buy Me A Pony” and “Black Betty.”

Among record labels, it was an especially solid outing for Sony Music which had its fingerprints on 18 of the 30 awards.

“What a night,” remarks ARIA CEO Annabelle Herd. “I am absolutely in awe of the incredible power of human artistry and Australian music. Tame Impala capping a sensational year on the world stage by sweeping the night on home soil, Ecca Vandal making her ARIA performance debut and walking away with four awards… then Sir Elton! I couldn’t be prouder to be the CEO of ARIA. Congratulations to all of our winners and a huge thank you to every artist who performed, you blew us away.”

The ARIAs, the Australian recorded music industry’s flagship awards ceremony, is supported by Destination NSW, in partnership with Spotify, and beamed out live on Paramount+ and broadcast on 10. Performances and moments will be available on the @ARIA.official YouTube channel.

Il Real Madrid blinda Arda Guler: rinnovo fino al 2032 per il giocatore turco

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Il Real Madrid guarda al futuro e mette un altro tassello nel proprio progetto. Il club spagnolo ha annunciato il rinnovo di Arda Guler, che ha prolungato il suo legame con la società fino al 30 giugno 2032. Una scelta che conferma la fiducia dei Blancos nei confronti del giovane talento turco, arrivato nella capitale spagnola nell'estate 2023.

Güler aveva appena 18 anni quando il Real Madrid lo ha acquistato, individuandolo come uno dei prospetti più interessanti del calcio europeo. In quattro stagioni con la maglia blanca il classe 2005 ha raccolto 126 presenze, contribuendo alla conquista di cinque trofei: Champions League, Mondiale per Club, Supercoppa UEFA, Liga e Supercoppa di Spagna. Un percorso inizialmente condizionato da qualche problema fisico e dalla grande concorrenza interna, ma che negli ultimi mesi ha visto il fantasista turco assumere un ruolo sempre più importante. Saudi Arabia’s Red Sea Film Festival Forges ‘Strategic’ Partnership With Cairo Film Festival

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Saudi Arabia’s Red Sea Film Foundation and the Cairo International Film Festival have announced what is being called a “strategic partnership” that will see the introduction of a dedicated Red Sea Film Festival award at the Cairo fest and other forms of collaboration dedicated to preserving the Arab region’s film heritage.

News of the agreement comes after the Red Sea fest — which is most prominent film event and market in the Arab world — canceled its sixth edition, which was scheduled to take place from Dec. 3 to 12. The decision was made following turbulence in Saudi Arabia due to an escalation in its conflict with Yemen’s Iran-backed Houthi rebels, who targeted several Saudi cities. The Red Sea fest has said it will return in the fourth quarter of 2027 with a “renewed vision and a program that continues to celebrate cinema and filmmakers from Saudi Arabia, the region and around the world,” according to a press release.

Starting in 2027, there will be a dedicated Red Sea Film Festival Award presented in Cairo to an outstanding film selected from that fest’s program. “The award will further strengthen ties between the two festivals while providing additional recognition for exceptional cinematic storytelling,” according to a statement.

“The agreement will also facilitate greater collaboration in film programming, creating opportunities for both festivals to showcase selected films from each other’s programmes, including productions supported by the Red Sea Fund,” the statement added. “This exchange will help broaden the reach of these films, connect filmmakers with new audiences, and encourage greater collaboration across the regional film industry.”

The Red Sea and Cairo fests partnership will also focus on film preservation and cultural exchange. The two entities will collaborate “on an exchange of restored films, with a dedicated weeklong screening program planned in Cairo following the fest’s 2027 edition,” according to the statement.

The Red Sea Film Foundation has supported the restoration of more than 20 films, including some of Egyptian cinema’s most celebrated works like Youssef Chahine’s “The Choice” (1971), “Watch Out for Zouzou” (1972) and “Love in Karnak” (1967).

“As Cairo International Film Festival marks 50 years since its founding, this partnership opens a new chapter of cooperation between two institutions committed to Arab cinema,” said Cairo fest chief Hussein Fahmy in a statement.

Commented Faisal Baltyuor, CEO of the Red Sea Film Foundation: “Egypt has played a defining role in the history of Arab cinema, and Cairo International Film Festival continues to be one of the region’s most important platforms for filmmakers and audiences alike. We are proud to strengthen our relationship through a partnership that reflects our shared commitment to supporting outstanding films, creating opportunities for filmmakers, and preserving our cinematic heritage.”
