Blizzard pulled out all the stops to ensure World of Warcraft: Forever survived a chaotic launch night. What was the company's secret weapon? Splurging an absurd amount of money on infrastructure made up of 3,924 virtual machines with 70,480 CPU cores and a whopping 251,136 GB, or 251 TB, of memory.

Praised by media and players as one of the smoothest launches in Blizzard's history, World of Warcraft: Forever requires significant computing firepower. 3,924 virtual machines work out to 18 cores and 64GB of memory per instance. Or, the company could be using a mixture of 16-core and 32-core nodes with slightly less memory.

To put the infrastructure's scale in consumer terms, we are looking at about 8,810 mainstream processors, or 7,848 high-end gaming systems with 32GB of memory each. In any event, the hardware is greatly improved compared to what World of Warcraft used to run on back in 2009.

In 2009, Blizzard said it used 10 data centers worldwide to host World of Warcraft. At the time, the infrastructure included 13,250 server blades, totaling 75,000 CPU cores and 112.5 TB of memory. By comparison, World of Warcraft: Forever leverages 6% fewer CPU cores but rocks 2.2X more memory. Modern processors are leagues faster than chips from 17 years ago. By today's standards, the bottleneck is memory capacity and bandwidth, which is why the infrastructure can run on fewer cores but has more than double the memory to compensate.

Blizzard does not specify the exact hardware that World of Warcraft: Forever runs on. However, if we were to translate the core specifications to, for example, AMD's EPYC 128-core Bergamo or 96-core Genoa chips, it could be anywhere between 275 and 365 2U server nodes, depending on which processor it uses. Therefore, World of Warcraft: Forever could be running on just eight to ten 42U server racks, a noteworthy improvement over 17 years ago.

Blizzard should have no trouble building the infrastructure for World of Warcraft: Forever. A ballpark estimate for about ten 42U server racks with EPYC chips is at least $15 million, even with today's memory pricing. But then again, since Microsoft owns Blizzard now, the company could just be tapping its parent's existing Azure platform. Technically, it is not free compute, but Blizzard would benefit from cost pricing and priority allocation of Azure's capacity.

World of Warcraft: Forever shows that a high-profile launch does not have to be a nightmare when companies are willing to invest in computing infrastructure. In this case, many would agree Blizzard is putting those 251TB to good use, although Sam Altman may say otherwise.

It’s not necessarily too late to plant amaryllis bulbs for Christmas, but timing does matter. Amaryllis typically blooms six to eight weeks after planting, so early to mid-November is your last chance to get bulbs in the pot if you want striking holiday flowers in time for the big day.

If you’re growing amaryllis for holiday flowers, choose the right type. Amaryllis usually refers to Hippeastrum, a South American native, not true Amaryllis, also known as belladonna lilies from South Africa. Most Hippeastrum bulbs sold this time of year are grown in the Southern Hemisphere and will bloom in six to eight weeks. Dutch-grown bulbs take longer, so avoid these.

Flowering also varies by the plant’s growing conditions, which means you have some control. While early or mid-November is the ideal time to plant a holiday amaryllis, you can do a few things to speed up the process if you’ve just missed the window.

Getting healthy holiday blooms isn’t just luck. How you choose and plant your bulbs is important. Start by selecting the best bulbs. Look for large, firm, and healthy bulbs without soft spots, damage, or mold. If you see a bulb that has the tip of a green stem emerging, grab it. This bulb has already begun to break dormancy and should flower sooner.

While unusual varieties can be expensive, you don't need to spend a lot of money on bulbs – Walmart sells preplanted bulbs for under $6.

Next, choose a good-quality potting mix, such as Miracle-Gro Indoor Potting Mix, available at Amazon, and a container with drainage holes that is only one or two inches (2.5 to 5 cm) wider than the bulb. Position the bulb in the pot so that the pointy end is up and one-third to one-half of the bulb is above the surface of the potting mix.

Water your planted bulb thoroughly, letting it all drain through, before setting it in a warm, bright spot. Keep the potting mix just slightly damp, never soggy. Amaryllis doesn’t need much water and can rot in soggy conditions.

If it’s already December, you won’t get a flower by Christmas from a dormant bulb. However, you can probably find a potted amaryllis that has already broken dormancy and has a stem. This plant will likely bloom by Christmas.

Wandrd’s Magnetic Camera Straps Are My New Favorite Accessory

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

More than a decade ago, Peak Design rethought the humble camera strap. This was a necessary development. When you buy a camera, manufacturers almost always toss in a chintzy, uninspired strap with their logo on it. These cheap straps have cheap mounting systems that make it hard to quickly remove the strap from the camera when you need to, like when you're attaching your camera to a tripod or gimbal and you’d rather not have a dangling strap whacking you in the face.

Peak Design solved this with its Anchor Links system, small circular nubs you leave attached to camera eyelets that make detaching or even swapping straps easy. The quick-connect mechanism is now the de facto industry standard and has inspired countless copycats.

Now, Wandrd has outdone that design using magnets.

“We're photographers ourselves, so we're constantly ideating, tinkering, thinking of ways that things can be better,” Ryan Cope, Wandrd cofounder, tells WIRED. “A camera strap like this has been at the back of our minds for a while.”

Wandrd—known for its camera bags—launched its Magnetic Camera Strap system on Thursday. It features a circular anchor similar to Peak Design, called “Torus Links.” The anchors attach to your camera’s eyelets and are rated to carry up to 198 pounds—so unless you're bench-pressing your camera, they'll do the job. If the cord does start to wear down over time, you'll first see yellow under the black fabric—a sign you should replace the Torus Link. If it's red, you should stop using the link immediately—a warning system borrowed from Peak Design. (Wandrd offers a two-year warranty.)

Unlike Peak Design's anchors, the Wandrd anchors look like donuts, with a hole in the middle. When you attach the strap, the embedded magnets guide the Torus Link into the strap's CoreLock mechanism, which pinches into the hole to secure the camera body. The magnets make connecting a strap easier, and they keep the strap fastened to the anchor, but you can’t just pull the strap off like you’re pulling a MagSafe accessory on your phone. You’ll need to use the strap’s dual-pinch mechanism to remove it from the Torus Link, a design that’s more secure.

The Torus Link is the donut-shaped object that attaches to your camera. On the right is the Magnetic CoreLock at the end of the strap. Pinch the top and bottom of this mechanism and let the magnets guide the strap into the Torus Link.

It’s a much faster way to connect and disconnect a strap, and that speed pays off in the field. This week, I had to photograph a product during a press briefing. Usually, I use Peak Design's Leash and, instead of taking off the strap, I bunch it up in my hand when it's not around my neck. It's not that it's hard to remove—it's just an extra step I have to think about. But Wandrd's magnetic system felt faster to unlock mid-shoot when I wanted to lower the camera for an awkward, low-angle shot, and reattaching the strap was practically instantaneous. I did the whole swap one-handed, with the magnets doing most of the work.

“I love magnets, man,” Cope says. “I love the simplicity they create—guiding things into place. You don't always have a place to put your camera down; I like that magnets help guide them and are a lot more secure, and they're also just fun.”

I have two Torus Links on my camera body, one on the right eyelet and another on the tripod baseplate. But my favorite configuration turns the strap into a single-point sling. The strap has a built-in “Torus Loop” on the buckle—clip one end directly into it to create a closed loop worn across your torso, leaving just a single magnetic connector to snap onto your camera. When you unclip the camera to shoot freely, the strap stays neatly in place across your body instead of sliding off your shoulder.

The nylon felt comfortable across my chest and near my neck—there's no padding, but that's intentional. Cope says Wandrd spent two years developing this custom “liquid nylon” tubular webbing that is softer along the edges, so it won't feel like the strap is cutting into your neck after a long day of wear. The lack of metal throughout the strap system also prevents the strap from scratching up your camera gear.

Best of all, you don't have to replace your strap to use Wandrd's system. If you have a strap you love—maybe a fancy leather strap someone gifted you—Wandrd is selling a DIY kit ($29) that lets you attach its Magnetic CoreLock system to the ends of your preferred strap. Otherwise, the strap kit comes in a 30-mm size ($69), a 15-mm “Lite" ($49), and a wrist strap ($39).

I don't know if Wandrd's system will see the same success as Peak Design's Anchor Links, but I don't see myself switching back anytime soon. Magnets are just too dang cool.

Sergej Milinkovic-Savic was the last to get as many in the first nine Serie A games back in 2022-23, with 10 contributions (three goals and seven assists).

Rabiot has been involved in a goal in each of his last five appearances, and the last Milan midfielder to do better in Serie A was Kaka in 2004 (seven). His opponent today will be Morten Frendrup, someone that Milan have been repeatedly linked with over the years. The Dane remains Genoa’s metronome in midfield, and the shield in front of the back three.

Summer signing Milutin Osmajić has been Genoa’s main threat in quite a low-scoring side, with four goals to his name in Serie A. He is keeping Lorenzo Colombo out of the side, though the Italian has provided a couple of assists for the Montenegrin when they have been fielded together in a front two.

After a disastrous performance against Ferencvaros, Koni De Winter will be hoping for a much better outing this afternoon. The Belgian has been in the headlines for rejecting a Belgium call-up meaning he will stay over the break, and he will aim to channel that same display against Marcus Thuram in the derby.

In addition to Rabiot, the other player to establish himself as a major threat for Milan is Diego Moreira. The Belgian has been directly involved in seven goals (four goals and three assists) in his first six Serie A appearances for the Rossoneri, and that came after a bit of a slow start too. He was decisive in the derby, with two assists and a beautiful free-kick goal.

He is expected to have quite a free-ranging role on the right, but what will be very important is getting players around Goncalo Ramos to try and unlock the Portuguese. So, Moreira will likely come up against the left-sided centre-back Johan Vasquez, someone who has become a mainstay in the Genoa side having entered his fifth season there. He does have three bookings in his first nine games, so that could be something to target.

Speaking of Goncalo Ramos, the concerns about his scoring form (or lack thereof) are beginning to grow into full-blown panic. He still has only two Serie A goals to his name so far, one of which was a penalty, and his overall involvement in the Rossoneri’s attacking play seems to be fluctuating between very little and non-existent.

Amorim and the team must get him firing, or it will continue to look like €70m+ spent very badly. Today will not be an easy duel for the former PSG man either as he faces Leo Ostigard, someone who will be well up for the physical battle. As we have seen, Ramos can get frustrated when he faces players who man-mark him, so he will have to be smart and sharp with his movement, ideally getting some more service too.

With an honourable mention going to Christian Pulisic vs. Alessandro Marcandalli, we had to end by mentioning Stephan El Shaarawy. He scored 21 goals in 83 league appearances for Milan, becoming a fan favourite during his time. The winger has also become the main creative force for the Grifone, having provided an assist in each of his last two league appearances.

Given his propensity to cut inside in order to create danger, both Mario Gila but especially Yunus Musah will have to watch the 34-year-old very closely indeed. Milan have been exposed in transition several times this season, and there is no doubt El Shaarawy would love to make a statement against his old club.

It's the Browns and quarterbacks, so people tend to get irrational. But there isn't a single team in the league that would bench its starter and go with a backup in this situation, so there is no reason for the Browns to be trendsetters.

Sanders gets the start today, because that is what the backup quarterback does when the starter is out. Then, as soon as the starter is cleared to play, things go back to the way they were.

The Browns need to win this game, and it doesn't matter how they do it as long as they accomplish that goal. But no matter what happens, the quarterback pecking order is established and is not changing.

3 Millionaire-Maker Quantum Computing Stocks

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The quantum computing market is full of exciting investments, but not all of them will pan out.

Quantum computing is an emerging industry that could have several huge winners in it. There are countless early-stage quantum computing stocks that I think could provide massive returns, as this technology is set to provide massive economic value once viability is reached. While that may seem like a far-fetched technology, the reality is many in the industry believe 2029 could be the year when viable quantum computing shows up.

That's right around the corner, so what stocks have huge potential? Let's take a look.

IonQ (IONQ +23.42%) is one of the first stocks investors think of when they hear about quantum computing. It was the first quantum computing stock to go public, and it has performed exceptionally well as a company, even if the stock is currently well off its all-time highs.

IonQ currently has one of the most accurate quantum computing technologies, with a 99.99% two-qubit gate fidelity score. IonQ has achieved this thanks to its trapped-ion approach to quantum computing, which yields excellent accuracy but at the cost of processing speed.

Next year, IonQ has plans to release its 256-qubit system, which could drive huge growth for the company as clients adopt early-stage quantum computing technology. That could be the event that is needed to turn IonQ's stock around and could kick-start huge returns.

Quantinuum (QNT +25.99%) is one of the newer quantum computing stocks on the public market. However, it's quickly emerging as a top option. Quantinuum just published results of 99.9995% logical fidelity, which isn't the same measure as IonQ's, but is still a very impressive result.

Quantinuum also uses trapped-ion technology like IonQ, which gives it an advantage in the accuracy measure of quantum computing. It believes it can achieve fault-tolerant quantum computing by 2029, and if it could do that, it would achieve impressive shareholder returns along the way.

Rigetti Computing (RGTI +19.29%) is taking a different approach than these two. It's focusing on superconducting computing, which hasn't yielded the greatest accuracy results. However, superconducting computing can operate at far greater speeds than trapped-ion computing, which will be a major advantage if Rigetti can get its accuracy where it needs to be.

Rigetti's two-qubit fidelity for its largest 108-qubit system is 99.1% (comparable to IonQ's figure) or 99.9% for a single qubit (comparable to Quantinuum's figure). As Rigetti's qubit size decreases, its accuracy goes up, but those systems are so small that doing anything useful on them isn't really viable.

Rigetti's goal is to produce a 1,000-qubit system with 99.9% two-qubit gate fidelity over the next three years, but that will be significantly smaller and less accurate than anything IonQ or Quantinuum is producing. However, because of Rigetti's speed advantage, it may be a longer-term quantum computing play that could make a comeback over the next few years.

We'll see how it pans out, but it's still a viable investment option.

One thing investors need to keep in mind with this trio is that there is no guarantee any of them will reach profitability. These are very high-risk stocks that could provide a high reward, but there's no guarantee either. There are countless impressive competitors in this space, many of whom have massive cash flows to fund quantum computing operations. These three are all upstarts and must continue to get funding from outside sources until they have a commercially viable product and business in place.

If they can make it to that stage, then they will be huge winners and a successful investment. But the reality is there will be a lot of failure in this industry too. Investors need to choose the best of the best, and these three represent that.

‘Rogue One’s Best Scene Was Actually the Worst Thing to Ever Happen To Star Wars

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Julio is a Senior Author for Collider. He studied History and International Relations at university, but found his calling in cultural journalism. When he isn't writing, Julio also teaches English at a nearby school. He has lived in São Paulo most of his life, where he covers CCXP and other big events. Having loved movies, music, and TV from an early age, he prides himself in knowing every minute detail about the things he loves. When he is older, he dreams of owning a movie theater in a small countryside town.

It was hard to contain the excitement viewers felt after walking out of Rogue One: A Star Wars Story when it first premiered in 2016 — especially after that ending. The movie was an undeniable game changer for Star Wars, and Darth Vader's hallway carnage scene felt like the crowning achievement. It's incredible, indeed, and it belongs in Rogue One right at that moment. Ten years later, it remains one of the franchise's finest hours, but it's also impossible to ignore how that hallway scene impacted not just the galaxy far, far away, but the fandom, too. From how stories are conceived to fan expectations, the overall effect of Vader's hallway scene has unfortunately not been as positive as one might expect.

It's undeniable that the hallway scene is one of the coolest things about Rogue One, and a top Darth Vader moment. The way he casually walks into that hallway, lets his mechanical breath fill the room, and ignites his lightsaber with perfect timing highlights how much of a drama queen he really is, and it's all for the benefit of the scene, really. It even started a franchise trend. However, all that goes so hard, some fans forget (or ignore?) that the scene's not really about him; it's about the rebels.

The purpose of the movie is to tell the story of how the rebels got the Death Star plans, using the Rogue One team to illustrate how much the Rebellion had to sacrifice to achieve that small, preliminary victory. Vader's role is to embody the abysmal difference between the Empire and the Rebellion's capabilities, in that sense, and that's what the hallway carnage is all about: even after all that, the Empire still has a literal Sith Lord to do what its whole army and navy couldn't.

The moment really was vital to Rogue One's story. It's supposed to highlight the constant sense of urgency inherent to being a rebel, because they are always much closer to defeat than to victory, regardless of how much they sacrifice and how righteous their cause is. It's also something that we all knew Vader could do, but never actually saw; such a moment could never fit within the Original Trilogy, for example. Unfortunately, what some viewers took away from the scene was a distinctly opposite impression.

It didn't take long for Vader's hallway scene to have its meaning twisted, however, with certain parts of the fandom seeing in it as something completely different than intended. The argument that it symbolizes what Star Wars could and should be has been repeated ever since: grittier and more adult (really meaning "violent").

Pretty much every franchise release after that has been divisive, regardless of purpose or whether they fit within these expectations. Vader was brought back in Obi-Wan Kenobi, just as vicious and even more important, but it was overlooked. The Acolyte appealed to Prequel Trilogy apologists and featured an equally awesome villain with The Stranger (Manny Jacinto), but, then, it fell to discourse. Even Andor, a direct prequel to Rogue One and the grittiest and most realistic work in Star Wars, wasn't enough — there was no Vader.

None of those are perfect, of course, but the fact remains that, after that hallway scene, fanboy discourse robbed Star Wars of its right to simply be fun. Some people saw it as an outlet for their worst opinions while ignoring what the scene really means, and with their sheer volume were loud enough to influence other parts of the fandom to agree with them. None of that is the scene's own fault, of course, but it's time these things were properly faced.

As Mon Mothma (Genevieve O'Reilly) brilliantly says in Andor Season 2, ignoring the truth means becoming "vulnerable to the appetite of whatever monster screams the loudest." The truth here is that Star Wars is in worse shape now than it was before Rogue One — not because of the quality of its products, but because it let itself be affected by those screaming loudly. Instead of taking risks and showing off personality, it now feels like the franchise is trying to draw as little attention as possible, to avoid this continuous backlash.

It's a pity, because Star Wars is such a big sandbox, it makes no sense to focus only on what you want to see and disregard the rest. What has become clear in the ten years since Rogue One is that people don't actually want "gritty, realistic, and adult" stories, they want killing, one-liners and gratuitous demonstrations of strength for its own sake. They saw the form of Vader's hallway scene and loved it, while ignoring all the meaning and substance behind it.

Again, that scene belongs in Rogue One, and it fits with Vader's overall character, but, even through that lens, it's still misunderstood. People see that and think Vader is a badass, but he really isn't; he's just super strong and easily manipulated. Considering all we know about him from the Prequels and the Original Trilogy, he is actually quite pitiful. His hallway scene doesn't exist in a vacuum, so it shouldn't be viewed that way, either. Before we get more of that, we should really understand everything it really means.
