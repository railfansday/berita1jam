What A Road Sign With A Black 'Y' Means (And Other Letters, Too)

📅 Nov 15, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

We have covered all manner of weird road signs on Jalopnik before, including some seriously goofy ones. While oddities from around the globe are fun to explore and explain, it is a little more relevant to touch on road signs that crop up on U.S. soil. Some of these will no doubt be familiar, but others may not, so there is good reason to delve into them and provide a quick explainer. The black "Y" sign was the one that initially piqued our interest, which, by the way, highlights that a Y-shaped intersection is to be found up ahead. That led us to look at other weird letter- and symbol-focused road signs drivers may come across.

The obvious candidates are "P" for parking areas and "H" that marks hospitals, which are universally understood, but throughout this article, we will also explore what those weird "T" or upturned "U" signs mean, in addition to winding "S" and plus-symbol signs. That includes where drivers are most likely to find them and how best to react when they do. Listed in no particular order, here are our picks of eight letter- or symbol-focused road signs that we thought are worth exploring in a bit more detail.

As already explained briefly above, a sign that depicts a capitalized "Y" is there to let drivers know of a Y-shaped three-way intersection or a forked road ahead. The sign will typically be a diamond shape with a yellow background, which means it's a simple warning sign alerting drivers that the road splits. The choices of where to go are obvious — either left or right — but what might be less obvious is how the right of way is determined in a three-way intersection that isn't controlled by any other signs.

The general rule is that drivers must yield to the car on the right. Then there's always the first-to-arrive rule, which works as you would intend — the first to arrive at the split goes first. Of course, some drivers will assume right of way when it is not deserved, or perhaps they just do not see the other car(s). In such instances, it's best to yield to the other vehicle even if you believe you should have the right of way to ensure everyone gets past the intersection without incident.

When a road splits like this, it pays to be especially cautious. Drivers may attempt to dart from one lane to the next when correcting a last-minute wrong decision, plus motorbikes or cyclists could be hiding in your blind spots, so try to ensure you have sufficient space around you if possible. The NHTSA highlights that roughly one third of all traffic fatalities occur at signposted intersections, so doubling down on cautiousness here is never a bad idea, no matter how simple it may first appear.

Surprise surprise, the letter "T" on a road sign warns of a T-shaped intersection — we would hope that you managed to work that one out. The road you are traveling on is about to end and merge with another. Once again, this is a classic warning sign finished in a yellow and black colorway, on a diamond-shaped sign that lets drivers know something's coming up and they will have to act accordingly.

In this instance, drivers must yield to traffic on the intersecting road — they don't have the right of way here — before heading either left or right. There will usually be a stop or yield sign prior to the intersection itself, and to avoid any frustrating bumps with other drivers making late decisions, it's best to get into the required lane early rather than last-minute. Thankfully, of the deadliest intersections on U.S. roads, not many are T-shaped, but the same rules as above apply here. Give yourself space, keep an eye on your blind spots, and don't rush. Oh, and don't forget to use your turn signals either, even if you are in a dedicated turn lane.

Again, we won't be handing out prizes to anyone who is able to correctly guess this one. A cross or "plus" symbol indicates that a crossroad is coming up, and just like the other aforementioned sign, this too will be depicted on a black and yellow diamond-shaped warning sign. Here, traffic can and likely will be coming from all directions, so it's imperative to take the time that's needed and approach it with care. Be aware that drivers on the opposite leg might try to cross ahead of you as you pull out, and be sure to check traffic from both left and right directions. As always, make sure to follow the instructions depicted on any regulatory signs posted in your direction, as these might detail give-way instructions.

There is a good case to make here in favor of the roundabout. In many ways, it is a far better design than the traditional intersection, with fewer conflict points, no left-turns across the flow of traffic, and lower speeds. Roundabouts might seem perplexing to some, but according to data from the Federal Highway Administration, they have been proven to reduce injury and fatal crashes by 76% and 90%, respectively, plus they are far more time-efficient than traffic signals, so perhaps road users ought to just get used to them.

OK, that's not strictly true. This sign, which isn't an "S" at all — it's just a winding arrow — signifies that a winding road is up around. In other words, the natural habitat for a Miata owner who's looking for some engaging roads to make the most of their drop-top weekend car. Of course, the sign isn't actually there to tell drivers that the road is a great place to go hooning. Instead, it lets them know the road ahead has several curves one after another and that they should approach that section of road with some caution.

In fact, these "S" signs will frequently be accompanied by another advisory speed sign that suggests the maximum safe speed for the section. Drivers would be wise to pay attention to these speeds, as around 25% of all fatal road incidents happen on a curved section of road, with common causes being cars either drifting off into trees, rocks, or other fixed objects, or indeed drifting across the center of the road and causing a head-on collision with other traffic.

Anyone who can't figure this one out should probably start taking notes here. An upturned U-shaped arrow crossed out in red, of course, means that U-turns are not permitted. Drivers should continue on, take a left or right turn if available, and find another suitable route. Exceptions will not be made for those in classic red-and-white Gran Torinos.

This is the first sign featured on our list that strays from that typical yellow and black warning sign colorway. Instead, this is a rectangular regulatory sign with a white background. These signs can crop up just about anywhere, but they can commonly be spotted in business districts, on narrow streets, or busier stretches of the nation's roads, such as on multi-lane highways and freeways.

For those who may question the legality of U-turns when this sign isn't present, drivers can usually perform such a move in these circumstances: They should be in the leftmost or center-left lane, signalling their intention, and keeping an eye out for traffic as per any other manoeuvre.

A sideways "T" sign indicates that a side road is coming up. Depending on which way the T is oriented, the sign highlights whether the side road is on the right or the left. If the sign instead looks like an F or an E, but with an extended top or bottom, this means that two side roads are coming up on the right-hand side.

Specifically, this sign will appear when the side roads on the right are particularly close to one another. The hazard this poses to drivers on the major road is quite obvious; they should be wary of traffic entering from the right, and should be prepared to slow down. The sign itself is a typical diamond-shaped yellow-and-black warning sign, which does not indicate right of way. Typically, those on the major road will have the right of way, while those on the indicated side roads will have to yield to traffic on the major road.

The sign for a roundabout shows three arrows forming a circle, and once again, it will be a yellow-and-black diamond-shaped warning sign. Roundabouts always run in a counter-clockwise direction in North America. They can have numerous lanes and are an alternative to crossroads. Here, the right of way rules are simple and uniform: Drivers must always give way to traffic on the left — in other words, the cars that are already on the roundabout.

Getting the right lane is crucial for avoiding accidents; the leftmost lane is used for turning left, whereas the rightmost lanes are used for turning right, and both can be used for going straight on. These are the rules on multi-lane roundabouts unless signposted otherwise — this way, traffic in either lane does not cross unnecessarily.

As for whether drivers need to use turn signals on a roundabout or not, the answer is yes. It's never a bad idea to let other drivers know where you are going; even if you are comfortable in your own abilities to get across safely, it's best practice to help other motorists out by indicating to them your next intended move.

Danielle Fishel charmed audiences for seven seasons while playing the TGIF fan-favorite character, Topanga Lawrence in Boy Meets World. Not only was the character notable for being involved in a TV relationship that defined goals, but Topanga was also loved for being smart and true to herself. While Fishel has taken great pride in her BMW work, she recently mentioned one regret related to her character that, as a fan, I completely sympathize with.

At the beginning of the ABC sitcom, Topanga was introduced as Cory Matthews’ hippie-like classmate whose otherworldly views always sparked great comedic moments. But by the second season, th flower child had an extreme personality change, where she transitioned to an extremely motivated student and later the object of Cory’s affections by Season 3.

In Fishel, Rider Strong, and Will Friedle’s new book, Book Meets World: The Definitive Inside Story of the Hit Sitcom Boy Meets World (via People), she got real about her character's evolution. She explained how she felt about Topanga's trajectory at the time compared to how she feels now:

Teenage Danielle was more comfortable being the more mainstream Topanga because they wrote her more like me. So I probably didn’t have a problem with it then. Now I think, what a gift to be given a character that has such a sharply defined POV, to be so unique and to have been such a good role model. I knew that she was special. But then, when it changed, and she kind of evolved, I don’t think I necessarily knew to mourn it.

I get what the Dancing with the Stars contestant is talking about. When Fishel previously spoke about Topanga’s personality shift, she admitted that she didn’t even notice the change at the time. Looking back, it’s true that the show was focusing a lot on Topanga’s evolving look, like in Season 2 when Shawn was staring at her chest or Cory fixated so much on her beauty in Season 3. I truly believe Topanga’s complete change was when she cut her trademark long hair and started to look more like many other woman of the ‘90s.

While no one formally told the Dish host that her character would deviate from her weird girl persona, she said she remembered hearing a conversation from the crew about wanting to make Topanga “more normal” for Cory. Yet, even in the Season 6 episode, "Resurrection," Cory talked about missing the old Topanga. I feel like Cory would have grown to like the more off-beat version of his eventual partner.

Something that's interesting is hearing Fishel mention that as a teen, she didn't really mind her character being more streamlined and fitting her own personality. She may not have mourned the transition then, but she certainly does now:

Now I see it, and I so mourn it. I would have loved even little flashbacks to it.

That would have been great. Fans see a flashback scene in “Resurrection” of when Topanga put lipstick all over her face but, in the later seasons, I would have liked to have seen Fishel's character retain traits of who she was early on.

At the same time, we can be thankful that no matter who Topanga grew up to be, she remained throughout the series in general. Some may not be aware that she was originally written to be a recurring character. As a result of her sticking around, fans got to see her transform into a confident young woman, who even became an incredible lawyer, wife, and mother in the spinoff series, Girl Meets World.

While Danielle Fishel now regrets having leaned into Topanga’s personality change, I'd argue that her performance on the show still deserves praise. And, of course, we can love the iconic character for who she was when she was first introduced as well as who she grew into by the show’s series finale. Regis: 3 Key Takeaways for Long-Term Investors

Regis's guest traffic fell 1.7% while franchisees closed 34 salons, leaving the turnaround's success tied to whether it can stop that erosion.

On its first quarter of fiscal 2027 earnings call, held on Nov. 5, 2026, Regis (RGS -1.38%) detailed its plan to boost guest traffic and stabilize franchisee operations. The salon franchisor is navigating customer traffic declines while managing debt reduction and store closures. Three things management said stand out.

Supercuts system traffic fell 1.7% during the quarter, making guest acquisition and retention a central priority. Management is expanding Choice Booking, an online scheduling tool that allows digital appointments alongside traditional walk-ins, after test locations saw higher customer visits and fewer online booking errors.

Management evaluated multiple refinancing options for its $121.1 million outstanding debt, including bank loans and private credit financing. Potential lenders requested additional quarters of operational progress before agreeing to new terms, prompting the company to delay refinancing until its prepayment penalty drops in June 2027.

Franchisees closed 34 locations in the first quarter, threatening recurring fee streams. To mitigate revenue erosion, management launched an artificial intelligence (AI) health dashboard to identify underperforming locations early and facilitate salon transfers to stronger operators.

Trans Catalina Day 2: Views for Days

📅 Nov 15, 2026  |  Category: Food  |  Read Source →

Despite being a late sleeper at home, I always seem to wake up early on trail. Today, I wake up before 6 am, but I force myself to sleep until 6:30. My campsite is just under 2 miles from Airport in the Sky, Catalina’s small airport. It has a small shop and offers breakfast, but it doesn’t open until 8:30 am.

I take my time packing up, enjoying the cool morning temperatures, finally heading out around 8:15 am. I am the first person in camp to leave.

Almost immediately I encounter my first confirmed sighting of the Catalina Quail. Two walk down the road in front of me as I climb the road out of Black Jack. I see countless quails the rest of the hike, almost always in pairs. It seems it may be mating season!

The trail follows a road before heading sharply downhill on singletrack. There seems to be no one else out enjoying the cool morning hiking hours. It is beautiful, the sun glinting off the ocean and the dew.

The trail dives into a valley before climbing to the airport. The valley is lush and green, and I even encounter a small, stagnant stream at the bottom — remarkable given the dry reputation of the trail.

What goes down must go up, though. After reaching the bottom of the valley, the trail immediately twists up towards the airport. I could see the airport from the top of the valley before descending, and it looks barely closer. The sun has come out and is beating down, even this early. This side of the valley is more exposed, with no trees and minimal foliage. The descent actually had a few trees, but it’s clear this ascent is closer to what the rest of the day will look like.

Airport in the Sky taunting me as I climb toward it.

Breakfast “sandie” – eggs, bacon, cheddar, avocado on sourdough.

I sit on the porch and enjoy the chance cool down, rehydrate, and fuel up. More and more people arrive on planes and from trail as the morning continues. It seems less crowded if you arrive for breakfast than for lunch. The line is much longer when I leave an hour and half after arriving.

My only regret is that I never got to meet the airport’s resident black cat.

As I head out, the sun is blazing in the middle of the sky, no cloud in sight. Today’s hike is much shorter and easier than yesterday’s, but the temperature is much warmer. As I scope out my path ahead, I see few trees and lots of road walking. It’s going to be a warm afternoon.

The trail alternates between singletrack and dirt road. I spend a couple of miles cruising along the wide, shadeless road before diving back into the landscape, but the road walk is not unpleasant. The views are perpetual and the track is relatively quiet. I quickly realize, though, that with minimal tree coverage, bathroom breaks require creativity or a lack of modesty — or both. The hillside often steeply climbs away from the road, making it even more difficult.

A lovely blooming cactus flower from along the side of the trail.

As I leave the road for singletrack, I notice movement on the side of trail. A small lizard freezes as I approach. I’ve noticed two distinct types of small lizards, but this one is my favorite. It has rainbow coloring, appearing almost to shimmer in the sun. I believe it is the common side-blotched lizard. I have already seen quite a few of them, and will see many more before the end of my hike.

The side-blotched lizard, known for the male’s throat colors indicating their mating style.

I continue to dodge more lizards under foot as I climb up a crest, and immediately my eyes are drawn forward. The trail winds along the very top of ridge before beginning the descent into Little Harbor. It’s rare for me to experience a trail on a ridge like this, and it takes my breath away.

The ridge walk is lovely, but the constant sun starts to make me weary. The temperature keeps increasing, no clouds or shade breaks to be found. I stop to sip my electrolytes and re-apply sunblock. Just ahead, at the bottom of this hill, I see a semi-shaded bench at a trail junction. It becomes all I focus on, my entire motivation for the remaining ridge walk and descent. Get. To. Shade.

The palm trees come into view, making me both smile (camp is near!) and chuckle (palm trees are not native). Mostly, I am thrilled at the view of trees. Trees mean shade. Shade means relief.

Even so, I stop and take in the view. It really is a picturesque sight.

Once at the campground, I head for Campsite #10, which turns out to be at the very front — right by the beach. Talk about incredible luck. When I reserved it, I was not sure what I was getting. A giant palm tree provides much needed shade over a fire ring and picnic table. A stream inlet burbles past, while straight ahead is the harbor itself.

I spend the rest of the afternoon exploring the camp and the beach. There is a water spigot in the next camp over, just like last night — though this isn’t typical. A row of porta potties on the road just outside the campsites serve as the toilets. Not my favorite, but at least there is a hand washing station!

I run into some fellow hikers I met at the shaded bench earlier. With wide eyes, one asks me, “Did you see the bison as you walked into camp?” Surprised, I reply that I did not. Apparently, it was lounging under a tree next to the trail into camp and I walked right past it. It must have blended with the dirt, obscured in the shade.

Despite missing the bison sighting, I still see quite a few new creatures throughout the afternoon and evening. I encounter more more Catalina quail as I walk around – click here for the video: Bobbing Catalina Quail. I see several Brown Pelicans diving for food near the shore.

What I believe to be a brown pelican hunting in the slough next to my campsite.

The real gem of wildlife for the evening, though, is the Catalina Fox. I say hi to the Isabellas at their campsite, and as we chat, a fox scurries past just behind the campsite. It slows and looks longingly at the picnic table, clearly familiar with human food, before finally disappearing into the bushes.

The Catalina Fox, staring longingly at our food. It looks a little like a stray dog.

Little Harbor is absolutely lovely. After dinner, I wander onto the beach to enjoy the sunset. The temperatures have finally cooled down, thanks in part to the cool ocean breeze. I enjoy it while I can.

Tomorrow is the day I am most nervous about. The temperatures are supposed to be highest tomorrow, and the official trail has a brutally steep climb and descent with zero shade, which I will face in the full afternoon sun. Somehow, though, I don’t feel too daunted. I feel excited, rejuvenated by my lovely evening. I knew the trail was short, but tomorrow is my last full day on trail. I can’t believe how fast it has flown by.

I crawl into my tent early, ready to get an early start to beat the heat tomorrow. Perhaps tomorrow is the day for my bison sighting!

For those who enjoy watching hardened gunslingers shooting their way to revenge, there are plenty of worthwhile options that can be streamed for free. Tubi, a streaming service known for not requiring a paid subscription to watch its content, hosts a vast library of Western movies.

Vengeance is one of the most popular themes of Western films. It's the driving force of so many stories in the genre. Because of how common it can be, a long list of these Westerns proved to be rather forgettable, somewhat standard entries into the genre. Others, however, stood out from the crowd to join the ranks of the greatest Westerns of all time, a distinction earned by the likes of Unforgiven, Hang 'Em High, The Searchers, and For a Few Dollars More.

Released in 2003, Open Range stars Western icons Kevin Costner and Robert Duvall as aging ranchers who resist when a gang of thugs led by an unscrupulous cattle baron tries to bully them off the land in the Montana territory. Open Range starts out as a story of resistance against a powerful force in a Western town, but when the villains of the film ultimately take a step too far, it evolves into a brutal tale of revenge.

The mysterious past of Kevin Costner's character comes into play here, with his skills as a gunfighter being key to their efforts to fight back. Their determination to not only hold their own ground, but also get revenge for their fallen culminates in a strikingly gruesome grand finale. Unlike many of the Westerns of the previous era, Open Range pulls no punches when it comes to showing its characters dying in grisly fashion. A big part of what makes The Forgotten Pistolero's revenge tale so interesting is the target of his crusade; Sebastian's own mother is one of the two main villains of the movie, setting up an emotional confrontation at the end of the story where the hero gets a chance to make her and her former lover pay for their crimes. Canada Goose: 3 Key Takeaways for Long-Term Investors

📅 Nov 15, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Store traffic is the open question: the retail network is where the apparel gains either hold up or stall.

On its second quarter of fiscal 2027 earnings call, held on Nov. 5, 2026, Canada Goose (GOOS +2.17%) announced a 5% revenue increase to 274 million Canadian dollars while expanding its gross profit margins. Long-term investors need to evaluate whether the luxury outerwear maker can build a sustainable year-round business while addressing headwinds in retail store traffic. Three things management said stand out.

Management expanded gross margins through improved sourcing, manufacturing efficiency, and higher pricing, rather than relying on short-term external boosts. These operational upgrades helped expand gross margins even as retail network costs increased.

Expanding beyond traditional heavy parkas creates a balanced business model that reduces reliance on cold weather. Sales growth in lighter apparel and rainwear indicates that retail customers increasingly purchase these products from the brand year-round.

Lower physical store traffic dragged down direct-to-consumer (DTC) comparable sales, which measure performance at locations open for over a year. Better online sales and store conversion rates partially cushioned the drop, but store foot traffic remains a clear challenge.

Wholesale orders are expanding, and management projects steady progress toward its annual profitability targets. Operational upgrades and pricing discipline support long-term margins while leadership tracks store traffic recovery.

Execution now centers on boosting store traffic and improving retail conversion during the peak winter buying season.
