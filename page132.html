Hollywood Has Flipped on GenAI, Says ‘Avatar’ VFX House Weta FX Chief: ‘If You Don’t Have an AI Strategy, Don’t Bother’

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Major Hollywood studios have gone from banning generative AI outright to shunning visual effects vendors that lack an AI plan, according to Daniel Seah, CEO of “The Lord of the Rings” and “Avatar” VFX house Weta FX.

Seah spoke to press in Taipei on Friday, after Weta FX signed a partnership with the Taiwan Creative Content Agency (TAICCA) at Taiwan Creative Content Fest (TCCF). Weta’s clients, he said, are mainly the Big Four studios and the streaming platforms. “We also got the memo from each of the studios, especially from their legal departments, saying that there is no such scenario that you can use any of GenAI for any of our work,” Seah said of early last year. By the middle of the year, several clients were allowing it, provided Weta got pre-approval and worked with only a small number of companies. Around the turn of the year, clients began asking about Weta’s AI strategy, mostly to find out how much money the technology could save.

“It’s, ‘If you don’t have an AI strategy, don’t bother to work with us,’” Seah said of the period since the third quarter of this year.

That shift drove Weta to look for a partner that could help it move faster on GenAI. It found one in Taiwanese animation and VFX outfit Moonshine Studio, which it has worked with for around six months. Seah said AI had become a national strategy in Taiwan on the back of the island’s semiconductor industry. Moonshine, without the client restrictions Weta faced, had been moving quickly as a result, he said.

Seah said Weta has no intention of using AI to replace any of its artists or employees. “It’s more like to enhance and increase more workflow for us,” he said.

Weta chief technology officer Kimball Thurston said the company has been building its own AI models for modifying and recreating the world, “not necessarily the like ChatGPT-type of model.” Work with Moonshine is likely to focus on the artist layer – what the AI world calls the harness, “which I think is the worst name they could have chosen,” Thurston said. The goal is a tool that lets people refine their stories across “whole timelines of long-form content rather than just 20-second TikTok videos.”

Thurston also said Weta uses outside video models such as Kling AI and ByteDance’s Seedance where clients permit it. “You kind of have to,” he said. “It’s not every studio allows us to, but they also came onto the scene and killed a bunch of competition, fundamentally.” Security rules mean such models must be hosted outside China, he added. That is achievable, he said, because ByteDance now operates globally.

Under the partnership, TAICCA and Taiwan’s Ministry of Culture will identify young talent and introduce them to Weta FX. Weta may offer them internships at its offices in Wellington, Melbourne and Vancouver. Seah said Weta recently announced it is likely to open a representative office in London too.

“There’s a Taiwan contingent at Weta in Wellington already,” Thurston said. He added that those artists were there before the new deal and are “integral members of the crews.”

Weta has no footprint in Taiwan. Seah called the island the ideal base for exploring Asia. He pointed to flight times of three to four hours to Southeast Asia, Japan and Korea, government backing and a pool of engineers and storytellers. Thurston said Weta has also spoken to people in Thailand and Korea. He also pointed to a build-out of immersive experiences across Asia, possibly via Singapore.

“It’s more, to me, about market enablement than it is about any particular location,” Thurston said.

“I think this is a key opportunity to move from technology hardware to technology applications – that is, software,” TAICCA chair Sue Wang added. She called the deal the biggest Taiwan has struck in the content industry so far.

Thurston said Weta started an open-source project around 18 months ago called the Rongotai Model Train Club, named after an area of Wellington. The project tracks refinement and LoRA training and writes that usage into image metadata. Anyone defining a model has to state which datasets were used. Thurston said the effort dovetails with work at the Visual Effects Society’s Technology Committee and at MovieLabs.

In the past year, Thurston said, Weta won a data center design award for the Southern Hemisphere. The award was for a cooling system that uses no water and is more power-efficient.

“I hate that a lot of companies are building up gas, basically gas turbines to power data centers,” Thurston said. “I think it represents bad engineering, speaking as a computer scientist.”

Weta VFX supervisor Sheldon Stopsack said the new tools have to fit into the studio’s existing pipeline, including its motion-capture work. “We want to be able to kind of shape the world the way the filmmaker envisioned it, we envisioned it, and, you know, have this creative control ultimately at the end of the day with any of these technologies,” Stopsack said.

“People still want to see the big star performing,” Thurston added. “They may be performing as an ape, or they may be performing as a Na’vi, or some other crazy creature that gets created, and that creature may be created using some technique, but people want to go see the actor perform.”

XRP Ledger's Biggest Lending Upgrade Just Got Closer

📅 2026-11-06  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The XRP Ledger (XRPL) is on the verge of a significant decentralized finance (DeFi) upgrade that could potentially open up new lending possibilities and liquidity for the XRP Ledger network.

According to Vet (@Vet_X0), a notable XRPL validator and community member, the much-anticipated Lending Protocol amendment has now gained two more Yes votes from validators, moving one step closer to activation.

The Lending Protocol amendment now boasts 21 Yes votes from a total of 35 trusted validators (60%). The amendment needs eight more Yes votes to reach the minimum threshold of 29 validators.

Reaching this threshold doesn’t mean that the upgrade will instantly go live. Instead, reaching the supermajority threshold will initiate the mandatory two-week activation period, which requires a minimum of 80% of validators to support the amendment.

The upgrade, known as XLS-66, is geared towards adding a native lending feature to the XRP Ledger.

In contrast to most crypto lending services, where borrowers are typically required to provide more collateral than the loan amount, XLS-66 is specially crafted to enable fixed-term and non-collateralized lending.

This will enable qualified borrowers to secure loans without the need to hold cryptocurrency collateral that exceeds the loan amount.

According to the official XLS-66 document, the Lending Protocol will enable the pooling of funds via so-called Single Asset Vaults, which can then be loaned to borrowers under set terms.

The proposed lending system involves three key actors: depositors, loan brokers and borrowers.

While depositors are in charge of depositing crypto or supported assets into a vault, loan brokers are tasked with brokering lending deals as well as evaluating borrowers.

For their part, borrowers would receive the loans and pay them back based on the set schedule.

The upgrade could potentially empower financial institutions and other entities to put capital to use on the XRP Ledger, as opposed to just holding or moving around assets.

It may also open up new prospects for earning interest on deposited assets in lending vaults. The amount of interest, however, would largely depend on the actual lending activity and risks.

According to the latest validator data, this amendment has reached 23 out of 35 votes. It still requires six more votes to hit the threshold.

Furthermore, the LendingProtocolV1_1 amendment, which offers upgrades including closed-ended vaults, updated interest accounting, etc., also currently has 21 Yes votes.

All three amendments are currently being considered, and none has yet reached supermajority.

The Institute for Supply Management’s (ISM) October services report, released Nov. 4, put its headline PMI at 54.5, down from September’s 54.9. The reading remained above 50, indicating that services activity was still expanding.

The monthly prices index also eased to 73 from 74, but its 12-month average rose to 69.2, the highest since April 2023. The longer view points to persistent cost pressure even as the latest monthly reading retreats.

Melissa Joan Hart Explains It All: Hot Flashes, Menopause, and Turning 50

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Follow Healthline on our socials for all things health.

Actor Melissa Joan Hart has a vivid memory of her mother standing in front of their open freezer to cool off. As a child, she didn’t understand why. Now, as a 50-year-old woman, Hart knows that her mom was experiencing hot flashes.

“[She’s] only 20 years older than me, so maybe I missed the part where she was going through [menopause], but there was no conversation about it,” Hart told Healthline. “I feel like it’s just becoming a hot topic with women, where…the taboo is going away from it now.”

Her own perimenopausal symptoms started with night sweats.

“[It] was really jarring, and the first few times, you’re not really sure what that was…do I need to turn the air down or what’s going on here?” Hart said.

Over time, she learned that she was experiencing moderate to severe hot flashes, which affect up to 80% of women during the menopause transition and can persist for 7 to 10 years.

Doctors categorize hot flashes as mild, moderate, or severe, said Diana Bitner, MD, OB-GYN, at Corewell Health.

“[So] if they’re mild, it’s a sense of warmth (maybe in the chest, or the neck, or the face). A moderate hot flash is where there’s associated sweating, and a severe hot flash is something that stops you in your tracks, wakes you up from the middle of the night,” Bitner told Healthline.

Hart has mostly experienced hot flashes while home or at her three sons’ activities, and they have yet to interfere with her work. However, according to research, one in three women report moderate to severe challenges in managing their work due to symptoms related to menopause.

“It’s just so sad that about 10% of women leave the workforce due to menopause,” said Bitner. “And so it can really affect our ability to perform at these key years of not only institutional memory, but also of earning power, and it can really affect the whole community.”

Bitner said women should feel empowered to bring up their menopausal symptoms or questions with their doctor.

“[This] is something that was taboo for so long…women didn’t really talk about this, whether it was because of embarrassment, or [they] weren’t sure if anyone was experiencing it,” she said. “[There’s] no reason, in this day and age, we need to suffer through it…it’s something that there are treatment options for.”

In fact, Bitner said providing specific details about their symptoms, such as how many mild, moderate, or severe hot flashes they’ve experienced over the past week, can help doctors better assess patients’ needs.

Hart plans to discuss treatment options for her hot flashes with her doctor soon. In the last few years, she said she has put emphasis on her midlife health.

“I just was sick of waking up with aches and pains in general [thinking], ‘is this the rest of my life?’” she said.

She started listening to self-help books in the car while driving her kids around and found ways to put herself first.

“My kids are a little bit older, so I kind of have that freedom to be able to do that,” she said. “[But] along with getting older, comes the menopause, comes that different time in life. And I think it’s a good time to reevaluate [how] our bodies can’t handle what [they] used to handle, and let’s figure it out.”

Hart has partnered with pharmaceutical company Bayer on its “Life Doesn’t Stop for a Hot Flash” campaign, which encourages women to discuss menopause symptoms with their doctors and promotes Lynkuet, a hormone-free prescription medication for moderate to severe hot flashes associated with menopause.

“It’s called ‘Life Doesn’t Stop for a Hot Flash’ campaign because it shouldn’t, because we should be able to push through, and this should be a time in our lives when our kids are getting older, and we’re able to kind of reinvent ourselves,” said Hart. “And I think it’s an exciting time, and it shouldn’t be something that we’re afraid of.”

She is embracing speaking openly about midlife health.

“I hit 50 this year, and it was, like, a huge milestone, and I think that’s something to be proud of, and not something to shy away from,” she said.

Hart also hopes to challenge the longstanding barriers women face in Hollywood as they age, including fewer opportunities for leading roles.

“[I] think we’re seeing that the generation before, and my generation, it feels like we’re pushing those boundaries…and we’re saying, you know, that women at this age have a lot to give, have a lot of information to share, have a lot of stories to tell and experiences and adventures still ahead of us, and, you know, we’re not ready to stop,” Hart said.

In everyday life, Bitner said that while some patients avoid talking about midlife issues like menopause with their doctors, she is seeing more patients who are confident being forthcoming.

As more women talk openly about menopause, she hopes to include men in the conversation, too. In fact, she invites her patients to bring their partners to appointments, so they can gain an understanding of menopause and the experiences their partners face, such as hot flashes, sleep issues, vaginal dryness, and discomfort or pain during intercourse.

“[Often], there’s, you know, comments that come up [from men who] say, ‘How do I talk about this without getting in trouble?’” said Bitner. “[We] want everyone to join this conversation…it doesn’t have to be just her issue because we know it’s not.”

XIIlab Approves KRW 10B Convertible Bond to Expand AI Data Center Business

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

South Korean AI infrastructure company XIIlab disclosed in a November 6, 2026 regulatory filing that its board approved a KRW 10 billion privately placed convertible bond for its AI data-center business. Subscription and cash payment are scheduled for November 16, 2026, so the disclosure records an approved financing, not a completed raise.

XIIlab’s announcement connects the proceeds to working capital for GPU-cluster construction and a broader operating model. The company plans to apply its AstraGo software to infrastructure it builds, pursue operations and management contracts, and generate recurring revenue alongside construction work.

The first-series bonds are bearer, unsecured and privately placed, with the full amount classified as operating funds. Both the coupon and maturity interest rates are 0%, no separate interest is paid before maturity, and principal is repayable at 100% of the electronically registered amount on November 16, 2031. Bonds held to maturity therefore retain a full repayment obligation despite the absence of coupon and maturity interest.

Conversion is set at KRW 8,106 per share and would produce 1,233,654 registered common shares if exercised in full. Investors can request conversion from November 16, 2027 through October 16, 2031. The bonds exclude a share-price-decline refixing clause, although separate adjustments apply to specified corporate actions, including below-market share issuance, mergers, capital reductions, stock splits and consolidations.

XIIlab or its designee can call up to 50% of each investor’s original subscription, capped at KRW 5 billion in total, at quarterly dates from November 16, 2027 through November 16, 2028. The company presents that option as a way to reduce potential dilution if its share price rises. Investors hold a counterbalancing put: they can request early repayment at 100% from May 16, 2029 and at subsequent quarterly dates. Taken together, the provisions balance potential equity dilution against a company-side purchase option and preserve a creditor route to seek cash repayment before maturity.

The planned subscriptions comprise KRW 8 billion from National Growth Fund sub-funds managed by Korea Investment Value Asset Management, DB Asset Management, Susung Asset Management and Orion Asset Management, and KRW 2 billion from other Orion-managed funds. XIIlab characterizes the National Growth Fund as a policy vehicle created to foster advanced strategic industries including AI, semiconductors and biotechnology, and places its AI infrastructure business within the fund’s principal investment targets.

The spending schedule assigns KRW 5 billion to 2027 and KRW 5 billion to 2028 and later, all for AI data-center-related business. XIIlab said it is securing construction working capital ahead of expected demand for newer GPUs, including Vera Rubin, and intends to expand GPU-cluster construction in its role as an NVIDIA Elite partner.

XIIlab’s AstraGo product documentation describes a Kubernetes-based platform for allocating GPU resources, running training and inference workloads, and managing resources and workloads in one environment. It supports existing NVIDIA GPU servers and specified Furiosa and DeepX NPUs, multi-server clusters, and on-premises or closed-network deployments. Pricing combines an annual subscription license with a per-node server installation charge, giving the operations plan a subscription-based commercial structure.

XIIlab launched AstraGo 2.1 on April 1, 2026, saying the release partitions one physical GPU across isolated inference workloads, runs training and inference in the same cluster, prioritizes resources for real-time inference, and separates allocation and usage histories by organization or service in a multi-tenant structure. Those are the operational functions XIIlab plans to develop further as it adds data-center management work.

TechCrunch reported on November 6, 2026 that Palo Alto Networks is in talks to acquire Clutch Security for $500 million in cash plus additional milestone-based payouts. The report describes discussions rather than a signed or completed acquisition, and neither company responded to TechCrunch’s requests for comment.

The person familiar with the discussions said Clutch was approaching $10 million in annual recurring revenue and had most recently been valued at $85 million in January 2025. TechCrunch characterized the proposed cash price as roughly 50 times that recurring revenue.

Clutch’s own January 30, 2025 Series A announcement says SignalFire led the $20 million round, with participation from existing investors Lightspeed Venture Partners and Merlin Ventures, bringing total funding to $28.5 million. The proceeds were designated for go-to-market expansion, research and development including Identity Lineage and Zero Trust controls, and customer-success staffing.

Clutch defines non-human identities as API keys, secrets, service accounts, tokens and certificates used across cloud infrastructure, CI/CD pipelines and AI-driven systems. These credentials give software access to enterprise resources, making their permissions and lineage central to agent security.

Clutch says its discovery product inventories agents across endpoints, SaaS applications, self-hosted infrastructure and cloud environments. It then maps each agent to its deployer, tools, authentication identities and reachable resources, producing a single inventory spanning all four environment types.

Clutch says its agent guardrails extend policies across that chain, covering the person, agent, tools, identities and resources. Its examples include restricting access to production infrastructure, protected branches, MCP servers and stored credentials. Enforcement can allow a tool call in development while blocking it in production, and each policy trigger and enforcement decision is recorded with lineage context.

That control chain closely resembles capabilities described in Palo Alto Networks’ fiscal 2026 annual report. Its Idira platform secures human, agentic and machine identities, while its AI Agents Security offering discovers agents, assigns identity attributes, limits access to task-specific resources, records activity and can suspend or revoke access. Secure AI Agents also scans SaaS, cloud and developer environments, places agents in a central registry, applies gateway guardrails and issues short-lived permissions that are revoked when a task ends.

The annual report also connects that portfolio to a series of acquisitions. CyberArk formed the identity-security platform, Koi added agentic endpoint security and enhanced Prisma AIRS, Portkey enhanced its AI gateway capabilities, and Console is expected to deepen agentic capabilities in Cortex. Clutch’s focus on discovery, identity lineage and context-aware enforcement therefore intersects with an existing product buildout spanning identity, endpoints and AI gateways.

Cyera announced on September 3, 2026 that it had completed its $1 billion acquisition of Oasis Security, combining its data-security platform with Oasis’s non-human-identity access-management technology. Oasis, founded in 2022, developed continuous, policy-driven controls over which workloads and agents can access systems, in what context and for how long. Cyera said the platform would operate as Cyera Identity, its dedicated identity pillar.

The Oasis deal supplies a completed comparison point for consolidation in agent identity; it does not show that Clutch will be acquired. For now, the reported talks pair close technical overlap with no signed agreement. Whether Clutch’s focused controls become part of Palo Alto Networks’ expanding identity portfolio remains the central open question.
