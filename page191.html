Marathoner Dies of Rhabdomyolysis: What to Know About Rare Condition

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Rhabdomyolysis, which caused the death of NYC marathoner Rachel Apa on Nov. 1, is a relatively rare condition, but here's what to know about what it is, who's at risk and how it becomes fatal.

When you think about athletes, you tend to think about stamina, strength and being in peak physical condition.

But anyone pushing his or her body to its limits also has to beware of overdoing it. The tragic death of Rachel Apa, a former college athlete who collapsed two miles from the finish line of the New York City Marathon, is an extreme case of a body fatally breaking down within a few hours, but a reminder that it can happen.

According to her father Steve Apa, Rachel died of rhabdomyolysis, a condition in which damaged muscle tissue breaks down and sends harmful proteins into the bloodstream.

“It’s unfathomable,” he told the New York Times. “Perfect health, perfect shape, 24 years old.”

Rachel, running her first marathon, had texted her dad after passing the 9-mile mark of the 26.2-mile route, which starts on Staten Island and finishes in Central Park, "I feel amazing. I've never felt this good."

Steve texted back from the family's home in Lewisberry, Penn., per NBC News, "Great, don’t overdo it. First marathon. This is a learning run. Don’t overdo it.”

Rachel had played field hockey for Bucknell University, was a regular exerciser and had trained for months ahead of the event.

“She was always on the go, always go," Steve said, "and it doesn’t matter if it’s school or sports or whatever it is. She was go."

So, how the heck does something like this happen?

Here is what to know about rhabdomyolysis, who is most at risk and more:

If you had never heard of rhabdomyolysis (or "rhabdo," as it's often abbreviated) you're hardly alone, as it's considered relatively rare, with about 26,000 cases reported in the United States each year, meaning it affects .0076 percent of the country's population.

According to the National Institutes of Health, rhabdomyolysis is a condition in which skeletal muscles break down and release intracellular components such as myoglobin, creatine phosphokinase (CPK), electrolytes and sarcoplasmic proteins into the bloodstream.

When toxic components of your muscle fibers enter your circulation system and kidneys, it can lead to acute kidney injury (AKI), electrolyte disturbances, and disseminated intravascular coagulation (a blood clotting disorder), per the NIH.

A "crushing injury" is the most common cause of rhabdomyolysis, according to the Cleveland Clinic, while overexertion (too much exercise without rest) and severe dehydration and overheating can also cause rapid muscle breakdown.

Cited causes of rhabdomyolysis also include certain medications, such as antipsychotic, antidepressant and antiviral medications, as well as statins; long periods of inactivity and physical conditions such as muscular dystrophy.

Endurance athletes and members of the military—i.e. those who engage in high-intensity physical activity—are considered more at risk, per the Cleveland Clinic, as are firefighters and others who work in hot environments and people over 65, as they're considered more prone to falls that could lead to injuries and prolonged immobility.

“Exertional rhabdomyolysis can occur after strenuous exercise and from high-intensity workouts during which the muscles are overused,” Niloofar Nobakht, MD, associate clinical professor of nephrology, told UCLA Health in 2023. “You can also get rhabdo from direct trauma, such as a crushing injury from a motor vehicle accident or a fall.”

But almost any intense activity can cause rhabdo, most commonly among people who are trying something new, according to Eric Rawson, chair of the department of health, nutrition and exercise science at Messiah College in Pennsylvania.

"You can be fit, and I can come up with a workout that you are unaccustomed to, and that could be what causes rhabdo," he told the NY Times in 2017, explaining why it's so important to start out at a beginner's level of a new activity before notching up the intensity.

Experiencing sore or weak muscles a few days after exercising, muscle swelling and abnormally dark or otherwise off-color urine can be signs of the condition.

The Cleveland Clinic notes that symptoms usually develop one to three days after a muscle injury, and persistent soreness doesn't always register as a potential life-threatening complication.

Rhabdomyolysis is survivable but requires prompt treatment, according to medical experts. Usually the condition is treated with fluids and electrolytes administered intravenously, while extreme kidney damage could require dialysis.

“We might start with an IV fluid and closely monitor electrolytes such as potassium, phosphorus and calcium," UCLA's Nobakht said. "Potassium and calcium are the two main electrolytes that play a significant role in recovering muscle function."

Witnesses said that Rachel Apa collapsed around the 24-mile mark of the New York City Marathon in Central Park. Three people, two of whom said they were doctors, took turns administering CPR, spectator Michaela Nicholas told the NY Times. New York Road Runners, which organizes the marathon, said event medical staff used a defibrillator on the fallen athlete before paramedics arrived and transported her to Mount Sinai Medical Center, where she was pronounced dead after six hours of attempted life-saving measures.

University of Michigan Health emergency medicine physician Dr. Bradley Uren told the publication that, for Rachel, "that worst case scenario happened just very, very quickly."

Omega-3 DHA Eases Autism Behaviors by Calming Inflammation

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

A preclinical study from Chongqing Medical University and Guangzhou Women and Children’s Medical Center demonstrates that docosahexaenoic acid (DHA), a crucial omega-3 polyunsaturated fatty acid, alleviates autism-like behavioral deficits in young rats exposed to early-life maternal separation stress.

Publishing in Pediatric Discovery, researchers revealed that daily DHA supplementation restored social interaction, curtailed repetitive self-grooming, and reduced anxiety. Molecular and network pharmacology analyses showed that DHA directly targets and inhibits the pro-inflammatory c-Jun N-terminal kinase (JNK) pathway while activating NRF2 antioxidant defenses within the prefrontal cortex, offering mechanistic insights into nutritional neuroscience approaches for neurodevelopmental conditions.

Source: Children’s Hospital of Chongqing Medical University / Guangzhou Women and Children’s Medical Center

Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) encompasses a heterogeneous continuum of early neurodevelopmental phenotypes characterized by persistent challenges in social communication, atypical sensory processing, and restricted, repetitive behavioral patterns. While the fundamental etiology involves complex polygenic and epigenetic architectures, mounting clinical evidence indicates that systemic immune dysregulation, neuroinflammation, and redox imbalances are common pathophysiological features in many autistic children.

Elevated circulating cytokines and depleted cellular antioxidant buffers create a neurotoxic cellular environment, frequently impairing neural plasticity and synaptic transmission within the prefrontal cortex (PFC), the cerebral locus governing executive control, social cognition, and repetitive impulse inhibition.

Docosahexaenoic acid (DHA), an essential long-chain omega-3 polyunsaturated fatty acid highly enriched in cerebral grey matter, is fundamental to synaptic membrane fluidity, dendritic arborization, and myelin sheath maintenance. Although dietary omega-3 interventions have shown mixed, exploratory benefits in small-scale human trials, the precise molecular mechanisms by which DHA could modify autism-related neural signaling remained elusive.

Now, a multi-method investigation published in Pediatric Discovery provides direct evidence that DHA mitigates autism-like behavioral phenotypes by dampening the hyperactive c-Jun N-terminal kinase (JNK) stress-signaling cascade and restoring antioxidant defenses in the brain.

The researchers, based at the Children’s Hospital of Chongqing Medical University and Guangzhou Women and Children’s Medical Center, evaluated the therapeutic potential of DHA using the neonatal maternal separation (NMS) rat model. In this established neurodevelopmental paradigm, rodent pups undergo three hours of maternal separation daily from postnatal day 1 through 21, followed by individual isolation housing until day 56—an early environmental trauma that reproduces core autism-like behaviors.

Rats administered daily DHA (80 mg/kg/day) demonstrated marked behavioral improvements between postnatal days 42 and 56:

To map the biochemical signaling networks mediating these behavioral recoveries, the team integrated network pharmacology with molecular docking simulations, Western blotting, and enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays (ELISA).

Network analysis revealed 13 core overlapping genetic targets between DHA bioactivity and ASD pathology, notably involving genes encoding tumor necrosis factor (TNF), interleukin-6 (IL6), superoxide dismutase 1 (SOD1), catalase (CAT), brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF), and peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor alpha (PPARA). Computational docking simulations predicted strong direct binding between DHA and key mediators, including JNK, CAT, SOD1, TNF-α, IL-6, and heme oxygenase-1 (HO-1), with binding affinities ranging from −2.93 to −6.02 kcal/mol.

Biochemical analysis of prefrontal cortex tissue validated these computational predictions:

The authors emphasize that while these findings outline a clear molecular framework connecting omega-3 supplementation to reduced neuroinflammation and improved social behavior, the results remain strictly preclinical.

Because the study utilized a neonatal stress-induced animal model, the results cannot be directly extrapolated to human clinical cohorts, nor do they suggest that omega-3 fatty acids can replace established clinical interventions, speech therapies, or behavioral supports.

Future translational research will require carefully stratified clinical trials with rigorous behavioral metrics and biomarker validation to determine whether specific neurodevelopmental subgroups—particularly individuals with verified elevations in baseline oxidative stress or cytokine dysregulation—can experience clinical gains from targeted adjunctive DHA therapies.

Docosahexaenoic acid ameliorates autistic-like behaviors by inhibiting oxidative stress and inflammatory response in neonatal maternal separation rats

Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) is a pervasive neurodevelopmental disorder characterized by impaired social interactions and communication, repetitive, or stereotyped behavior. Docosahexaenoic acid (DHA), an essential polyunsaturated fatty acid, has been demonstrated to exert anti-oxidative stress and anti-inflammatory properties, while also promoting myelin development and neural differentiation and development. However, it remains uncertain whether DHA can ameliorate autistic-like behaviors, and if so, the underlying mechanisms are still unclear.

Here, we established a neonatal maternal separation (NMS) rat model and treated it with DHA (80 mg/kg/day, i.p.). The results showed DHA treatment significantly alleviated autism-like behaviors in the NMS rats during their juvenile period. Subsequently, we employed network pharmacology analysis and molecular docking methods to screen potential targets of DHA in ASD therapy.

Through Gene Ontology, Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes, and Wikipedia enrichment analysis, we identified anti-oxidative stress, anti-inflammatory and JNK signaling pathway that might be associated with DHA-mediated improvement of autistic-like behaviors.

Furthermore, western blotting assays showed DHA significantly downregulated the expression levels of p-JNK and c-JUN, while upregulating the expression levels of NRF2, HO-1, SOD1, and CAT. In addition, enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay results showed DHA effectively reduced the production of pro-inflammatory factors, such as TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-1β.

Collectively, our study predicted and validated that DHA exhibits the potential to improve autistic-like behaviors induced by NMS in rats by suppressing JNK activation and inhibiting oxidative stress and inflammatory response. These findings suggest that DHA may be a potential therapeutic agent for the treatment of ASD.

Sam Darnold’s cratering play is a big picture problem for the Seahawks

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Seattle Seahawks quarterback Sam Darnold is a Super Bowl champion who played an important part in bringing another Lombardi Trophy to Seattle, and that can never be taken away.

Sam Darnold is also playing bad football.

You can blame the offensive coordinator, you can blame the offensive line, you can blame the lack of a running game, but Sam Darnold is playing bottom-tier football. His worst throw from Monday night's blowout loss against the Chicago Bears didn't even count, but it's emblematic of his incomprehensible decision-making.

Incidentally, the play design is pretty much DOA but there is no need to turn 2nd and 1 into a double coverage gamble.

Darnold's touchdown-to-interception ratio is barely 1:1 over his last 16 regular season starts. His mostly great playoff run is the break-up in what is otherwise a prolonged stretch of play that is far removed from much of his 2024 Minnesota Vikings season and the first 10-ish games of his Seahawks career.

(Editor's note: It's 344/551 and not 334)

There are no advanced stats that can positively spin Darnold as an above-average starter at the moment. He really wasn't playing particularly well for most of the back-end of 2025, but the running game finally sprung to life and Seattle's defense and special teams repeatedly either scored or gave the Seahawks extremely short fields.

The 2026 Seahawks are 4-4, which is not the end of the world, but how they've gotten to 4-4 is pretty much the worst case scenario in the short- and long-term. If Mike Macdonald's defense hypothetically had curiously dropped off to be a middling group and the Seahawks were losing shootouts while Darnold was putting up impressive numbers, I think we could live with that and acknowledge that defenses can be more volatile year-to-year than offenses. Darnold is heavily culpable for at least three of the four defeats, and even his best game against the Washington Commanders is significantly impacted by what was effectively a game-losing pick-six.

Darnold's contract is a great mid-level contract for someone with his track record and roughly two seasons worth of good productivity. It's also a contract that expires in 2027, and the current version of Darnold does not look like someone who should be Seattle's Week 1 starter, much less the starter in 2028.

Barring substantial improvement over the second half of the season, John Schneider has a major decision to make on Darnold, regardless of whether or not Brian Fleury is still the offensive coordinator. We've seen Darnold be very good when paired with the right play-caller, and I am not going to put the failures of this offense exclusively at his feet. But Darnold is in that tier of quarterback who needs way more help to be functional than the likes of Josh Allen or Joe Burrow or Patrick Mahomes. Every quarterback needs at least some semblance of foundational support to succeed, but Sam needs way more stuff than, say, prime Russell Wilson. That includes, seemingly above all else, having an experienced and effective play-caller who can play to his strengths and minimize his worst habits. Brian Fleury does not seem to fit the bill.

Darnold also needs play-action to work like we need air to breathe. When it's not working, Darnold has consistently been anywhere from average to ruinous in pure dropback situations. He needs an offensive line to protect him so well that his pressure rate is low, otherwise this is what you get.

Darnold with a clean pocket and/or the ability to get the ball out of his hands quickly can sling it with the best of them. When he is dealing with pressure in his face, he is barely a starting caliber quarterback. We have two years of evidence in a Seahawks uniform.

Play-action to be so insanely efficient that it overrides the lack of non-PA success.

Favorable game scripts such that his team is almost never trailing.

Related: A top-5 caliber defense such that his team is rarely trailing.

An offensive coordinator/play-caller who can scheme up easy-button throws for fun.

An offensive line that can pass protect competently.

An explosive running game in case none of the above works out or is figured out by NFL defenses.

There's nothing wrong with building a great environment around a competent but limited quarterback, but that also means a crevice turns into the Grand Canyon when said environment is even marginally worse. You can live with Darnold's turnovers if he's as efficient as he was in 2024 and 2025. That is not even close to what we're seeing in 2026.

The possibility also cannot be discounted that two years of film effectively exhausted all of the routes that the likes of Kevin O'Connell and Klint Kubiak could make Darnold thrive. As I wrote not too long ago, "With non-elite quarterbacks there are only so many levers you can pull before competent defenses respond." If Seattle sticks with Sam then it will likely need an(other) offensive coordinator to get him back on track, all while not being an immediate head coach candidate. This is the downside of having a defensive minded coach and your OC hires are either bad (see: Grubb, Ryan) or are so good that NFL teams take notice (see: Kubiak, Klint). The churn truly never stops unless you find that unicorn who can be a stable part of your organization while being no threat to hold a head coaching job.

Seattle's roster as a whole is a very good one; most of the players from last year's Super Bowl squad are still here. Any premise of Seattle having a sustained window of playoff and Super Bowl contention was contingent upon Darnold sustaining his success and mercifully giving us all a break from debating Seattle's next franchise quarterback. If Darnold cannot recapture even half of what made him go from draft bust to unlikely Super Bowl champion—this includes the rest of this year and (if applicable) his contract year in 2027—the Seahawks' long-term vision is suddenly a lot more complicated. Drew Lock is a free agent and frankly I don't want to bank on the QB revival thing yet again for a career backup entering Year 9. It's not like Drew himself hasn't had a history of being turnover prone with volatile week-to-week performances when he was a starter elsewhere. Jalen Milroe is a great unknown outside of preseason, and 2027 is a pivotal year for him to see if he's even ready to be an NFL starter. Other than that? Seattle might find itself once again dealing with major uncertainty at the most important position in the sport.

That's what stings the most, even coming off an unforgettable Super Bowl season. The Seahawks have their head coach, have a lot of hyper-talented, young core pieces under contract either on rookie deals or massive extensions, but quarterback has become a giant question mark now and therefore makes for a much murkier future than hoped.
