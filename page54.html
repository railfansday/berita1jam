Fourth Circuit Upholds Virginia's Law Terminating Agreements to Help With Immigration Enforcement - but Strikes Down anti-Mask Law

📅 Nov 23, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Mostly law professors | Sometimes contrarian | Often libertarian | Always independent

The ruling on Section 287 agreements overturns an earlier district court decision.

In September, I wrote about the the federal district court decision striking down Virginia's new law terminating Section 287(g) agreements, under which various state and local law enforcement agencies agree to help with federal immigration enforcement. Today, in United States v. Virginia, the US Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit reversed that decision. But, at the same time, in a 2-1 ruling, they struck down Virginia's law barring law enforcement officers (including federal ones) from wearing masks except in relatively narrowly specified circumstances. Here is an excerpt from the court's opinion (written by Judge Heytens) on the 287(g) issue:

We vacate the preliminary injunction that bars enforcing the 287(g) Law with respect to existing agreements. The district court concluded the Federal Government was likely to succeed on the merits because rendering such agreements unenforceable violate both the intergovernmental immunity doctrine and the Contracts Clause. We disagree. The intergovernmental immunity doctrine does not apply because the 287(g) Law neither directly regulates the Federal Government nor prevents private parties from contracting with it…. The 287(g) Law also does not violate the Contracts Clause for two separate reasons: (1) because the existing agreements expressly permit unilateral termination by the non-federal party; and (2) even if they did not, the Commonwealth would have constitutionally valid justifications for requiring state and local officials to terminate or renegotiate any such agreements.

Later in the opinion Judge Heytens expands on why Section 287(g) termination does not violate the Contracts Clause:

We conclude the Federal Government cannot satisfy that test for two different, yet complementary, reasons. First, all the agreements covered by the district court's injunction expressly permit the Commonwealth's agents to unilaterally terminate without cause, which means the 287(g) Law does not "substantially impair" (Sveen, 584 U.S. at 819) a contractual relationship at Step 1. Second, even if the agreements did not so provide, neither the Commonwealth's agencies nor those of its localities may contract away "an essential attribute of [the Commonwealth's] sovereignty." United States Tr. Co. of N.Y. v. New Jersey, 431 U.S. 1, 23 (1977). Here, that attribute is the Commonwealth's power to decide whether and on what terms to assist with the ongoing enforcement of federal law.

I largely agree. In my post on the district court decision, I offered some additional reasons why terminating these agreements doesn't violate the Contracts Clause.

The anti-mask law presents more difficult issues because - as the majority emphasizes - it directly regulates federal law enforcement officials. On the other hand, the law imposes the same constraints on state and local officers, as well, and therefore doesn't discriminate against them. On balance, I agree with Judge Robert Bruce King's dissent:

At bottom, a single ICE officer should never be able to override a duly enacted statute of the Commonwealth of Virginia in the absence of some federal law, regulation, or other valid supporting authority. Consider what purportedly conflicts with the Mask/Identity Statute. A federal statute? None. A treaty of the United States? None. A federal regulation or executive order? None. A written policy located anywhere within ICE or the Department of Homeland Security? None has been identified. What we have today is a single sentence in a Declaration asserting that individual ICE officers possess the discretion and ability to conceal their identities when they alone deem it appropriate. No supervision is at all necessary or required and the agency itself has no involvement in the ICE officer's daily decisions on whether to wear a mask. I cannot and will not accept the proposition that individualized discretion, untethered to any identifiable source of federal law, enjoys constitutional supremacy over a duly enacted statute of the Commonwealth.

As the majority recognizes, federal law enforcement officers are not generally exempt from state law while on the job. For example, they must obey state traffic laws when driving their vehicles. A valid federal law could override a conflicting state law. But here, there is no such overriding federal law, or even a regulation (though I'm not convinced the latter alone would be enough). Just the preferences of ICE agents. Those preferences are not enough to secure an exemption from state law.  Federal law (when constitutionally valid) is supreme over state law. The preferences of individual federal officials or agencies are not.

Everything We Know About Spider-Man & Wolverine Being In Avengers: Doomsday

📅 Nov 23, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

It's almost time for Avengers: Doomsday, and rumors swirl that Spider-Man and Wolverine will turn up. Marvel is determined to one-up even Avengers: Endgame, with this movie bringing back heroes from the last 26 years of Marvel blockbusters. The final Doomsday trailer divides its time between Steve Rogers and the X-Men, and even marked a franchise first: we finally got James Marsden's Cyclops saying an iconic line, "To me, my X-Men."

The X-Men, the Fantastic Four, and several different teams of Avengers are all going up against Robert Downey, Jr.'s Doctor Doom. But with the entire multiverse expected to end ahead of next year's Avengers: Secret Wars, we're all pretty certain there are going to be a lot more surprises. There have been particularly exciting rumors about both Spider-Man and Wolverine, but how likely are they to be true?

There's currently no evidence Tom Holland's Spider-Man is in Avengers: Doomsday. The core issue lies in the real-world, with the fact there are only small gaps in Holland's schedule where he could have been available for filming; he was occupied on The Odyssey through most of Doomsday's production, before moving on to Spider-Man: Brand New Day. If he does make an appearance at all, it would have to be a very small one indeed.

That said, there's intense debate about whether the end of Spider-Man: Brand New Day is actually Doomsday setup. It shows Ned Leeds' "Spider-Tracker" app losing Spider-Man completely, only to spot him in space. This is most likely a multiversal collision known as an incursion, where two different Earths collide. It's possible Holland's Spider-Man has been transported to another Earth, one that's about to impact Earth-616.

Tobey Maguire and Andrew Garfield both reprised their roles in Spider-Man: No Way Home, meaning they've already been part of the Multiverse Saga. It wouldn't be at all surprising to see them turn up; in fact, there have been rumors Doomsday kicks off with Maguire's Spider-Man killed in battle with the X-Men, who are fighting to save their universe. We'll have to wait and see whether that actually happens.

One thing's for sure: If Maguire or Garfield do return as Spider-Man in Avengers: Doomsday, it's likely to only be for a small role. The film principally involves three different Earths: Earth-616, the Fantastic Four's world, and the X-Men's. That already means Doomsday has a lot of cast to juggle (even more, given Doctor Doom may have been collecting people from other timelines as well). Many characters are likely to be relegated to cameos, just to make the story navigable.

Marvel is keeping very quiet about whether Hugh Jackman's Wolverine is in Doomsday. That said, the House of Ideas do seem intent on keeping Jackman on board till he's 90 (at least), and Deadpool and Wolverine brought the character into the Multiverse Saga. In this case, it feels likely Wolverine is being kept back as a surprise. But nobody will be shocked should Logan pop his claws and fight alongside the rest of the X-Men. Perhaps he's even one of the heroes who responds to Cyclops' call to arms in the trailer.

Speaking as a lifelong X-Men fan, I'm delighted to see other members of the team get the spotlight in Doomsday's marketing. Fox depended far too much on Wolverine's popularity, and it's been so good to see Marvel course-correct a little, choosing to emphasize the likes of Professor X and Cyclops. I'm sure there are going to be more X-Men surprises, too.

Oddly enough, though, there are actually quite a few heroes couldn't missing from Avengers: Doomsday's roll call. Sticking with the X-Men, two characters stand out: Ryan Reynold's Deadpool and Famke Janssen's Jean Grey. The former has already appeared in the Multiverse Saga, and even has connections to the Time Variance Authority. The latter is an absolute powerhouse (literally the Phoenix), so there's likely a story reason here. Perhaps Jean has already been killed (or recruited) by Doctor Doom.

Far too many heroes from Earth-616 are still absent. Brie Larson's Captain Marvel is surprising, again because she's one of the most powerful heroes in play. A multiversal event really should feature the "boss of space," so we can only hope she's going to turn up. Teyonah Parris' Spectrum was stranded in the X-Men timeline at the end of The Marvels, which should surely mean she's unwittingly triggering an incursion between Earth-616 and the X-Men universe. It would actually be rather odd for Marvel to ignore her in Doomsday.

The problem, of course, is that the MCU expanded a little too quickly during the Multiverse Saga. We were introduced to so many different superheroes; some are street level (such as Alaqua Cox's Echo), while others didn't prove especially popular (the less said about Secret Invasion's new Super-Skrulls, the better). Avengers: Doomsday already faces a huge challenge if it's to give due time to multiple Avengers teams as well as the X-Men, so some Earth-616 heroes are going to be neglected. Don't expect to see the Eternals anytime soon.

That said, Marvel did recently confirm two new heroes for Doomsday. Hailee Steinfeld is returning as Kate Bishop, meaning at least one of Marvel's two Hawkeyes (and, in this case, a Young Avenger in the comics) is due to appear. Meanwhile, VisionQuest's post-credits scene confirmed Doctor Doom has resurrected Elizabeth Olsen's Scarlet Witch, presumably confirming rumors Wanda Maximoff is one of the Latverian Witches we see in the recent trailer. If these two heroes are also back, then it's possible Marvel has quite a few surprises in the works. We'll find out more when Avengers: Doomsday finally releases in December.

Your home may be full of wildlife you’ve never noticed

📅 Nov 23, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

A survey of indoor spaces across the U.S. and Canada recorded nearly 2,100 species, from familiar house spiders to an alligator in a garage.

Homes across the United States and Canada are full of wildlife.

A continent-wide survey has found nearly 2,100 species of wild organisms living indoors, mostly insects and spiders, plus centipedes, snails, fungi, and many animals most people have never heard of.

One record is an alligator that turned up in somebody’s garage.

This is the largest study of indoor wildlife so far. Nearly all of it is built from photos taken by ordinary people.

The study was led by researchers at North Carolina State University and Colorado State University.

Over 60 co-authors contributed, including museum curators, university entomologists, and independent experts from across North America and Europe.

Ecologists have spent decades cataloging life in rainforest canopies and on the deep sea floor. The places where most of us spend most of our time have received far less attention.

“While researchers have focused significant attention on a variety of natural ecosystems – from rainforest canopies to the deep sea – we know remarkably little about the ecosystems of manmade, indoor environments,” said lead author Bradley Allf from Colorado State.

“Our goal with this work was to treat the indoors, at the continental scale, as an ecosystem that we wanted to explore as ecologists,” he explained.

Earlier surveys of indoor life either covered much smaller areas or relied on DNA traces, which can’t always pin down exactly which species was present.

This is the first survey of indoor life spanning the United States and Canada.

The team launched a citizen science project called Never Home Alone on iNaturalist, a website where people share photos of the living things they come across.

Participants were asked to photograph wild organisms in their homes, offices, and other indoor spaces. The rule for “wild” was simple: anything that was neither a pet nor a potted plant.

Submissions came from at least five continents. This study focuses on North America.

About 3,000 people contributed 23,782 observations, from every US state and every Canadian province. Most were taken in homes, but some came from offices, schools, and airports. The records cover almost three decades, from 1996 to 2024.

Every observation was checked by specialists. Dozens of experts identified each organism as precisely as they could.

“This work underscores the value of natural history expertise and the ability to identify species, which is a skillset that is less common among biologists than it once was,” Allf said.

About 95% of the observations were arthropods, the group of animals with jointed legs and hard outer skeletons. Insects made up 60% of all records, mostly beetles, flies, moths, wasps and ants, and true bugs.

Spiders accounted for 30%. Animals with backbones made up only 4%, most of them reptiles and mammals.

The rest included fungi, plants, earthworms, snails, small crustaceans, and even single-celled organisms.

Eight species were recorded more than 200 times each.

In order, they were the Asian lady beetle, the brown marmorated stink bug, the house centipede, the varied carpet beetle, the long-bodied cellar spider, the triangulate combfoot spider, the bathroom moth fly, and the eastern parson spider.

“The organisms we found ranged from Asian lady beetles – which were the most commonly found species – to an alligator that unexpectedly turned up in one person’s garage,” Allf said.

“But one of the things that was striking was how common predator species were – including hundreds of different spider species.”

Half of the top eight are hunters, and some of them do useful work.

“One study participant noted that they view jumping spiders as working animals, because the spiders play an active role in reducing the population of flies and other pests that they don’t want in their home.”

Senior author Rob Dunn from NC State pointed out that most people share their homes with these animals every day and have no idea what they are.

“Another thing that is striking about this work is that most people could look over a list of the most commonly found species and wouldn’t know what most of those animals even are – and yet they are around us, all the time,” he said.

“For example, I suspect most people are unfamiliar with comb-footed spiders, but we have more than a thousand observations of this family of spiders.”

“This is a stark departure from what we would have expected historically, when our ancestors would have been more aware of the living things around them.”

“That’s something we lost. And this is a first step toward regaining that understanding.”

The list has a very long tail. About 52% of the species were recorded indoors just once.

The true total is almost certainly higher than 2,100, because 44% of the observations couldn’t be identified down to species.

The authors also point out the dataset’s limits. Nobody controlled how hard each person looked, so large, eye-catching animals are probably overrepresented.

And not every animal that ends up indoors actually lives there. Some had simply wandered in and become trapped.

The survey also shows how little is known about even the most common indoor animals.

“It could inform future research in many ways,” Dunn said.

“For example, house centipedes were the third most commonly found animals, and yet I’m not aware of anyone who is studying them. That’s worth exploring.”

Some of these animals might even turn out to be useful.

“For example, we now know that camel crickets carry a microbe that can break down paper products. That could be useful. What else might be out there, in our own homes?”

“Once we know what’s there, we can begin asking additional questions,” Dunn said.

“Why are these organisms there? What are they doing? Are they beneficial?”

“But knowing what’s there is the first step. And it’s exciting because these are the animals we’re around all the time, but we just overlook.”

The scientists want to investigate how the climate outside shapes what turns up inside. They also plan to figure out which species have actually adapted to life in buildings.

Every record has a date and a location, so the dataset can also serve as a baseline for spotting new arrivals or disappearances in the future. The data are free for anyone to use.

The project is still running, and anyone with a phone can contribute.

“People can still submit observations to Never Home Alone. The more data the project collects, the more insight we’ll have on the unique and growing ecosystem of the great indoors,” Allf concluded.

The study is published in the journal Ecology.

The BepiColombo spacecraft has overcome a last-minute computer glitch to enter orbit around Mercury — the 2nd craft in history to orbit the closest planet to the sun.

When you purchase through links on our site, we may earn an affiliate commission. Here’s how it works.

After eight years slingshotting around the inner solar system, the joint European-Japanese spacecraft BepiColombo has finally entered into orbit around Mercury — becoming only the second mission ever to orbit the closest planet to the sun.

Mission scientists confirmed on Saturday (Nov. 21) that the spacecraft reached Mercury's orbit and is transmitting data back to the ground team.

However, the orbital insertion didn't go entirely as planned, European Space Agency (ESA) representatives said in a statement on Monday. Due to a software glitch, the spacecraft didn't fire its thrusters for the full 37 minutes required to slow its approach to Mercury.

While ESA investigates the issue and what it means for the spacecraft's trajectory, BepiColombo remains "safe," the statement noted. The spacecraft will hopefully be ready to begin a year of intensive science observations of Mercury starting a few months from now.

An illustration of BepiColombo approaching Mercury for orbital insertion.

Built jointly by ESA and the Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency (JAXA), BepiColombo is actually two separate probes, which are currently stacked one atop the other in orbit. The ESA-built orbiter, named the Mercury Planetary Orbiter (MPO), and the JAXA-built orbiter, the Mercury Magnetospheric Orbiter (Mio), carry separate science instruments, and they will soon diverge into separate orbital paths around the mysterious inner planet, operating as two probes gathering different streams of data.

A selfie of BepiColombo during a flyby of Mercury's north pole in 2025.

The pair of probes launched together aboard an Ariane 5 rocket in October 2018, and spent the following eight years on an interplanetary journey that took them past Earth, the moon and Venus. It even passed by Mercury itself six times. (During one of these flybys, in January 2025, Bepi snapped an incredible closeup photo of Mercury's north pole.)

This complicated cosmic choreography was necessary to prevent BepiColombo from picking up too much speed and overshooting Mercury's orbit, according to ESA. Mercury sits about 36 million miles (58 million kilometers) from our star, or roughly one third of the distance between Earth and the sun. That makes it very difficult for a spacecraft to reach Mercury without being captured by the sun's considerable gravitational pull and dragged off course.

Only one other spacecraft — NASA's MESSENGER probe — has succeeded in entering Mercury orbit before, circling the planet from 2011 to 2015 after its own seven-year journey through space.

Assuming ESA determines that BepiColombo is fit to continue its mission as planned, then it will spend the next few months getting both probes in position to start mapping Mercury in the greatest detail ever.

MPO and Mio are expected to separate in early December, with Mio entering a highly elliptical orbit around Mercury's poles. MPO will then take a few more months to fine-tune its trajectory, reaching a lower and more circular polar orbit in March 2027, according to ESA. Its science operations are expected to begin in April.

The sibling probes will each orbit Mercury several times a day, with MPO completing an orbit every 2 hours or so. Observing the mysterious inner planet from several different vantage points, the probes will make the most comprehensive map of Mercury ever. The mission's suite of science instruments will study the planet across the electromagnetic spectrum, taking observations in X-rays, gamma-rays and ultraviolet light, as well as collecting detailed measurements of the planet's magnetic field.

With this information, scientists hope to unravel many mysteries of the least-explored planet in the solar system. The new data should enable scientists to tackle myriad long-standing questions: What is Mercury's interior made of, and where does its magnetic field come from? How was the planet formed, and to what extent is it still tectonically active today? By making precise measurements of the spacecraft's orbit and position, a radio experiment will also provide a new test of Einstein's theory of general relativity.

Stay tuned for more updates from ESA about humanity's first visit to Mercury in more than a decade.

U.S. intelligence chief says more tech outages are inevitably coming

📅 Nov 23, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Over the past month, tech outages have disrupted work emails, delayed bank transactions, and temporarily grounded departing flights across the United States. Most of the biggest disruptions happened within six days of one another.

On Sunday, Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton said Americans should expect more disruptions like these, but not because of an incoming cyberattack; rather, because companies and government agencies are now in the process of updating aging technology.

"We are going to have glitches, gaps, interruptions in service," Clayton told CNN's State of the Union on Sunday, Nov. 24. "That is going to happen." He said much of the country's digital infrastructure was built over three decades or more and needs upgrades to run better and stay secure. (As one example, the FAA is still in the process of phasing out floppy disks, with a target of retiring the '90s-era technology by 2028.)

Clayton compared it to weekend maintenance at work: A company might take a system offline on Saturday and plan to have it back by 2 A.M. Sunday. If the team finds another problem during the upgrade, it might be Sunday afternoon before the system is back up. "We are taking more systems down and putting them back up than ever before," he said.

It was a lot to keep track of in six days: On Nov. 12, some Microsoft Outlook users couldn't open their inboxes or send email. A day later, the Federal Aviation Administration paused departures nationwide after air traffic control communications problems. Then, on Nov. 16, a software bug delayed Chase transactions, and a separate outage briefly paused United Airlines departures. The following day, several Apple services ran into problems, and the National Security Agency's classified internal network went offline.

As Mashable has reported, despite the odd timing, the outages were not related, and Clayton told ABC's This Week that they were neither coordinated nor related to state-sponsored cyberattacks either. He attributed the disruptions to system upgrades he called "absolutely necessary going forward." "And we’re going to see more of that," he said. He also said, "The need to improve our digital infrastructure is clear."

AI is identifying zero-day bugs in software faster than ever, and fixing those flaws safely takes time. Some zero-day bug bounty programs have been forced to shut down entirely, and earlier this year, the Zero Day Initiative reported bug reports had increased 490 percent year over year. Companies now have to move quickly to patch all the new bugs found by AI.

Microsoft President Brad Smith called the pressure to move faster part of the "AI ripple effects" on CBS's Face the Nation on Nov. 22 — companies want to fix flaws before attackers can use them. On older systems, Smith said, moving that quickly can leave engineers with less time to test what happens when a fix goes live.

Dmitri Alperovitch, chair of the Silverado Policy Accelerator, described what that looks like for the people doing the work. "I.T. teams, network teams are overwhelmed. They're working 24/7. They're making mistakes," he said on the same program. He expects more outages as teams work through the growing number of flaws they've found.

The National Security Agency's outage offers one example of how an urgent fix can disrupt service. The agency's classified internal network went offline on Nov. 17 during work after the agency installed a security fix for PHP, a widely used programming language, Clayton told Fox Business. Those same flaws, known as P2PSHELL, had already forced some website operators to take their sites offline in October while they installed the fix, as Mashable reported at the time. OpenAI said that its GPT-5.6 Sol model had found the bugs.

Microsoft, on the other hand, traced its outage to a software bug introduced in July that went unnoticed for months. It only became a problem in November, when Microsoft sent more Teams traffic to servers that also handle Outlook email. Some of those servers were already offline, and the extra traffic triggered the bug on others. When those servers stopped responding, Microsoft automatically sent traffic to the ones that were still working. They started to fail, too.

Smith said the broader work could take months. His advice? Everyone should install security updates as soon they arrive on a phone or laptop.

He went down with the knee injury in their loss to the Denver Broncos on Dec. 14, and has been sidelined recovering ever since. The Packers lost four straight games after losing Parsons, including a playoff loss to the Chicago Bears.

Parsons had 12.5 sacks in his 14 games with the Packers last season, and had accounted for a third of Green Bay's pressures before he went down. It was his first campaign with the team, too, after they acquired him in a deal with the Dallas Cowboys just days before their season opener.

Parsons was cleared to return to practice earlier this month and started his ramp-up while targeting Wednesday's contest at SoFi Stadium for his return. Clearly, that has gone exactly to plan.

The Packers will enter Wednesday's contest in Southern California with a 6-4 record, which has them in last in the NFC North but very much alive in the playoff conversation. They were off last week, but went into their bye fresh off a rough 30-10 loss to the Minnesota Vikings.
