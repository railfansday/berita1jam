US: Giant pandas from China make public debut at Atlanta zoo

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Ping Ping and Fu Shuang are the first giant pandas to be housed in Zoo Atlanta after a gap of two years.

Two giant pandas from China have made their first public appearance at a zoo in the US city of Atlanta as a queue of visitors lined up to see the bears.

In a treat for them and their fans, Ping Ping and Fu Shuang —  male and female, respectively — were seen munching on bamboo at Zoo Atlanta on Friday.

The six-year-old pandas arrived from China's Chengdu on September 27.

The FedEx flight they traveled on was dubbed the "Panda Express."

The panda duo had spent the last few weeks getting acclimated to their habitat.

Ping Ping and Fu Shuang have taken up residence in a panda enclosure at the zoo that had been vacant since 2024.

A 25-year-long partnership that saw Atlanta host four pandas on loan since 1999 ended in the midst of strained US-China ties. Seven panda cubs that were born at the zoo during that period now live in Chengdu, China.

That arrangement has been renewed under a 10-year loan agreement following a Washington summit between US President Donald Trump and his Chinese counterpart Xi Jinping.

China's ambassador to the US, Xie Feng, said at a press conference on Friday that the pandas' arrival "marks the renewal of a decade-long bond between China and Zoo Atlanta."

Raymond King, CEO of Zoo Atlanta, described pandas as "animals that have got a unique ability to inspire curiosity, instill passion, evoke joy, and unite people around the globe."

"This extraordinary spirit of partnership helps create a future for a species that is truly beloved the world over,"  he said as the two adult pandas made their debut.

China has a history of leasing the iconic pandas to other countries in a bid to build diplomatic ties, dubbed "panda diplomacy."

Cubs born in foreign zoos are typically sent back to China.

Meanwhile, unaware of the soft power they wield, Ping Ping and Fu Shuang, lounged about their new habitat on Friday.

The zoo recently renovated its panda facilities and has also ensured a long-term supply of locally grown bamboo for its newest residents.

Three US zoos now house the black-and-white mammals.

One of the biggest breakout stars in the world of literature in the last few years is Dungeon Crawler Carl. Matt Dinniman's fantasy series started life as a self-published passion project in 2019, but was subsequently picked up by Amazon and blew up massively around 2024. Now, in 2026, Dungeon Crawler Carl is a bona-fide hit franchise with multiple adaptations on the way alongside a ninth book.

I can see why Dungeon Crawler Carl resonates with audiences the way it has. The books feel like a traditional role-playing game come to life, set in a world where Earth has been destroyed by aliens and human survivors are forced into an 18-level underground labyrinth to compete in an intergalactic reality television show. It's a fairly simple pitch on paper, but the Hunger Games meets Dungeons & Dragons, vibes are boosted by the humor and world-building that make Carl and friends feel truly alive.

Now, Renegade Game Studios has developed a full tabletop adaptation based on the concept of Dungeon Crawler Carl, where one person acts as the game master, controlling the monsters and enemies while other players fight to survive. Of course, any good tabletop RPG requires miniatures, and there's a new set releasing this month, called Allies & Enemies.

"Enhance your gameplay of the Dungeon Crawler Carl roleplaying games with these 10 pre-painted Dungeon Crawler Carl miniatures," reads the official product description. "This set features many of the most dangerous and friendly characters from the bestselling LitRPG book series ready to use in your RPG campaigns or simply to collect! Inside you’ll find Brynhild's daughters: Hekla & Eva, Bianca & Prepotente and even Lucia Mar in both her day and night forms!"

Also releasing this mointh is the Mobs of Mordecai set, and the Unstoppable set, both of which feature their own unique pre-painted miniatures. The full RPG launches November 2026, and the 300-page rulebook is also available to pre-order now. The Dungeon Crawler Carl Roleplaying Game Core Rulebook is 300+ pages focusing on world dungeon floors 3 to 5. The book talks players through character creation, skills, spells, neighborhoods, saferooms, loot, mobs, and all the other essentials. You can build your own character from the ground up with nearly infinite combinations of backgrounds, skills, races, and classes, so there's plenty of room for a proper Dungeon Crawler Carl experience.

The next Dungeon Crawler Carl book, currently under the working title The Beautiful Place, doesn't have an official release date at the time of writing, but we do know it'll be the first part of an epic two-part conclusion, with the finale coming in book 10.

7 Great Netflix Shows You Can Only Watch Once

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

These seven Netflix shows are some of the best the streamer has to offer, but you'll probably only ever watch them once. There's a long history of TV shows that you would only ever want to watch once. Series that either don't have the same power on a second watch, or perhaps have a bit too much power.

Some series are reliant on a twist that loses its "oomph" once you know it. Some are dark referendums on real history that can be painful to acknowledge. Some just don't hold up as well the second time around. Whatever the reason, these seven shows are absolutely worth watching once, but just not twice.

BoJack Horseman is an adult animated comedy-drama that goes from mostly comedy with a sprinkling of drama at the beginning of its six seasons, to primarily a heavy drama with some dashes of comedy. The series stars the titular BoJack, an anthropomorphic (one of many in the world of the show) horse.

A former sitcom star, BoJack's life has been on a downward trajectory for years, exacerbated by his drinking, depression, and philandering. Now a washed-up former star, BoJack attempts to get his life back on track but keeps getting in his own way. Across six seasons, BoJack seems to break more relationships than he mends.

It's a very funny and clever series, and extremely relatable for anyone who has found themselves regretting their past actions. BoJack Horseman only gets darker as the seasons go on, and episodes about Alzheimer's, abuse, and self-harm become the norm. It's a great watch, but not one you'll quickly return to.

American Vandal is a highly underrated mockumentary series that parodied the popular true-crime documentaries of the era like Making a Murderer and Serial, the first of which premiered on Netflix. The two-season series is an anthology, and in each, a mundane crime or prank is handled like a notorious murder case.

In season 1, a prank leaves 27 faculty cars covered in graphic graffiti. In season 2, a cafeteria's lemonade is contaminated with maltitol. Both seasons utilize the same dramatic filmmaking, talking heads, closed-circuit camera footage, and foreboding storytelling of the true-crime TV genre.

Back when American Vandal came out, true crime was at the height of its popularity. Eight years later, the tropes that the show is poking fun at are a little less popular and relevant. This was a great series when it came out, but like a lot of satire, it loses its bite as the rest of the culture catches up.

Mike Flanagan's horror TV shows are all worthy of your time, though don't go into any of them expecting a rip-roaring horror filled with jump scares. His series are slow and thoughtful, quietly building dreadful atmospheres that make the few jump scares there are all the more impactful.

However, this does mean they can be a bit difficult to return to. Once you know what the shows are building towards, the slower parts get a bit more tedious. Even the best of Flanagan's miniseries, The Haunting of Hill House, suffers from this issue. It's a series dripping with atmosphere, horror, and sadness, and that makes returning to it difficult.

The miniseries is based on Shirley Jackson's story of the same name, though it plays with the structure of the story and adds an additional timeline that the show jumps between. It's a beautifully written plot, and Flanagan builds up some shockingly scary sequences all while keeping everything in the winding tale intelligible.

When They See Us is a four-episode true crime miniseries about the Central Park Five. In 1989, in Central Park in Manhattan, five young Black and Latino males were accused of raping and assaulting a white woman who was jogging in the park that evening. Despite little evidence, the five young men were charged and sentenced to prison for anywhere between seven and 13 years.

In 2002, DNA evidence exonerated the five men, and their convictions were vacated and they were released. The series begins with the events leading up to the assault; the police investigation into Kevin Richardson (Asante Blackk), Antron McCray (Caleel Harris), Yusef Salaam (Ethan Herisse), Korey Wise (Jharrel Jerome), and Raymond Santana (Marquis Rodriguez); and goes through their imprisonments and eventual releases.

When They See Us is a harrowing, brutal examination of the failures of the U.S. justice system, and the systemic racism that will feel all too relatable even to those living in 2026. It's a hard watch, one that forces the viewer to confront the realities about the inequalities we live with every day.

1899 is a multilingual German series from the creators of Dark, so you can guess that this historical drama is going to go in some weird directions. The show is set in the titular year and follows the passengers of the steamship Kerberos, bound from Southampton in the UK to New York City.

Throughout TV history, networks and streaming services alike have produced various great miniseries, but only some are perfect from start to finish.

On their journey, the Kerberos gets a message indicating its sister ship, Prometheus, has not sunk, as previously thought, and is stranded somewhere in the Atlantic. Upon meeting the survivors, the travelers on Kerberos begin to suspect that something very strange and very dangerous is going on.

1899 had the makings of a thrilling, mind-bending sci-fi series along the same lines as Dark, but the series was canceled after just one season. There's now basically no reason to ever rewatch the show, considering the ending in particular, which sets up many seasons' worth of plotting.

Archive 81 is a Lovecraftian TV show, with obvious influences from the master of cosmic horror. The series is based on a podcast of the same name, and takes place across two timelines. The first is set in 1994 and follows Melody Pendras' (Dina Shihabi) attempts to document the story behind an apartment building fire.

The second storyline takes place in 2022 and follows archivist Dan Turner (Mamoudou Athie), who is asked to restore Melody's videos by a mysterious organization. There are cults, creatures, secrets, and dark gods, and Archive 81 manages to weave them all together without losing the plot or the frightening atmosphere.

For most of the series, the viewers are unsure of how much of a Lovecraft show Archive 81 is, and that mystery keeps things interesting. However, towards the end of the series, all the mystery falls away. Once you know where Archive 81 is going, it loses a bit of its mystique, making rewatches less interesting.

Baby Reindeer is one of the best Netflix originals the streamer has ever put out. The seven-episode miniseries was created by and stars Richard Gadd, based on his one-man play, which was in turn based upon his real-life experiences. In the show, Gadd changes his character's name to Donny Dunn.

Donny is an aspiring stand-up comedian who makes the mistake of befriending one Martha Scott (Jessica Gunning), a former lawyer with a criminal record. The lonely and unstable Martha becomes obsessed with Donny, ruining his relationships and forcing him to confront an abusive episode in his past.

It is a fantastic series, in no small part due to Gadd's personal connection to the story. However, it can be a very difficult watch, particularly when it comes to the scenes of sexual assault, which are critical to Baby Reindeer. It's a Netflix series everyone should watch once, and follow up with a long, deep breath.

Jensen Huang Says AI Needs 1,000 Times More Power. Here's Why Oklo and NuScale Power May Still Suffer.

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Artificial intelligence remains desperate for more energy sources.

Despite a recent pullback, artificial intelligence (AI) stocks remain some of the market's best performers in recent years. And projections look very positive for additional AI spending and continued rapid adoption. Goldman Sachs now forecasts AI spending to surpass $1 trillion in 2026. Meanwhile, more than one-quarter of all U.S. businesses are now regularly using artificial intelligence products.

The recent pullback in AI stocks wasn't driven by uncertainty about demand. Instead, there are rising concerns regarding the pace of the global data center build-out. To scale adoption, more data centers will need to be built. Doing so, however, generally requires support from the communities these facilities will be located in -- support that is increasingly difficult to find. Plus, these facilities require substantial capital to build. The complete availability of that capital is currently in question.

"AI demand remains strong," affirms recent research from Barclays. "What the market is questioning is funding, as hyperscalers keep lifting capex while free cash flow evaporates."

Concerns about capital availability and public support for additional data centers are relatively new but intensifying. What isn't new is the market's concern over how AI companies will power their data centers.

"Capital is pouring into data center development, but there are real constraints on growth," McKinsey & Co. warns. "Incumbents can't meet demand for power."

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang agrees. "The amount of energy that we need for [AI] computing is likely probably 1,000 times more than we currently have," he said earlier this year.

It is this need for more energy generation capacity that has driven enthusiasm for nuclear energy stocks, including Oklo (OKLO +22.66%) and NuScale Power (SMR +16.25%). Both of these companies are developing small modular reactors, or SMRs. These systems can, at least on paper, be built faster with lower initial capital requirements than conventional nuclear power plants. That makes them an ideal fit for the AI industry, which needs to scale as much new power generation as quickly as possible.

This investment thesis -- that SMR stocks will benefit from the AI industry's desperate need for more energy -- has driven much of the bullish sentiment around Oklo and NuScale Power. Recent developments, however, have called the strength of that thesis into question.

Despite industry hype, Bank of America's estimates don't predict real-world SMR adoption will scale meaningfully until the 2030s or even the 2040s. That's a problem for NuScale Power and Oklo, given that both companies remain unprofitable and the full commercialization of their SMR designs may still be years away.

To sustain themselves financially, both Oklo and NuScale Power have sold large blocks of shares to raise cash, diluting shareholders. With capital and social support concerns on the rise, SMR adoption by AI and data center companies could be delayed, especially since lower-cost, lower-risk alternatives such as solar, natural gas, and wind remain accessible.

This year, shares of Oklo and NuScale are both down more than 60%. If the SMR build-out is stretched out, more stock issuances will be on the way, this time at rock-bottom prices. While I still believe in the SMR story over the long term from a business perspective, the investment case for SMR stocks is currently facing mounting uncertainty.

James was held to eight points as the Cavaliers routed the 76ers 119-95 in an NBA Cup group stage game.

The 41-year-old, a native of Akron, Ohio, who elected to sign with Philadelphia in the offseason, instead of returning to Cleveland for a third stint, was 3 of 12 from the field, including 0 for 4 on three-pointers, while he committed six turnovers in 31 minutes.

"Definitely not feeling a good rhythm offensively," James said. "We didn't look like much offensively. We had a couple spurts, but too many turnovers on my behalf."

James received a smattering of boos from Cavaliers fans every time he touched the ball during the first half.

And this time there was no video tribute to the four-time NBA champion - who won one of those titles with Cleveland in 2016. "At the end of the day, nothing can get as worse as December 2, 2010, from a reception standpoint," James said. "I've seen them near the depths of hell when it comes to reception here all the way to the heavens above. So anything in between, it's easy money."
