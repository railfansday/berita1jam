Mass Effect's Leviathan Gets A $300 Dark Horse Statue For N7 Day

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Turns out when a series is big enough, it takes more than canceling its next installment and closing the studio behind it to make it go away, especially when you make that baffling decision weeks before the day its fans use to celebrate it each year. That's why, even though we may never get another Mass Effect game, we have been hit with a wave of new Mass Effect merch in the wake of BioWare's closure.

The latest addition to that wave is a new statue created by Dark Horse Direct. The latest in a long line of officially licensed Mass Effect statues and figures created by the collectibles company, it's latest, and potentially final, release tied to the series depicts the iconic moment in Mass Effect 3 when Shepard comes face-to-face with Leviathan.

Filmmakers have been inside BioWare working on the doc for two years.

Dark Horse teased its new Mass Effect release earlier in the week, confirming that its imminent reveal wasn't a mistake, and that despite BioWare's closure, it wanted to give fans of the studio something to look forward to on N7 Day. The tease noted that the statue would get its full reveal at 9 am PT on November 6. However, that reveal appears to have happened a little earlier than planned, as pre-orders for the Leviathan statue are already live on the Dark Horse website.

That means we can now see the statue in all its glory. A stunning recreation of the Leviathan, a race that predates even the Reapers in the Mass Effect universe, but one that the apocalyptic enemies have no knowledge of since they are hidden deep beneath the waves. The statue's store page going live a little early also means we know all of the finer details about the statue outside of its design, including how much it will cost and when it's expected to ship.

The bad news is that if you want what might end up being one of the last pieces of official Mass Effect merch ever created, it's going to cost you $299.99. Considerably more expensive than most of Dark Horse's other Mass Effect figures, not only because it's larger (almost 14 inches tall) and more detailed, but also because only 500 of them will ever be made and sold.

Each of the 500 statues will be individually numbered, making every one of them unique. As is typically the case with high-end collectibles such as this one, you will have to wait a while once your pre-order has been placed. The statues won't start shipping until July 2027, with some potentially not arriving until September.

Mass Effect merch may be all we have to celebrate the now dormant, potentially dead series this N7 Day. Dark Horse's Leviathan statue joins a wearable Mass Effect helmet and three new additions to the series' Youtooz collection. The company that creates the figures revealed the Liara, Joker, and Mordin additions to its collection earlier this week.

A new report suggests that Mass Effect: Universe was still years away from launching after development paused to shift focus to The Veilguard.

Good Riddance: The Worst Airport Lounge In The United States Closes

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The Swissport Lounge at Chicago O’Hare Airport (ORD) Terminal 5 has just quietly closed. I’m about as sad as I’ll be on the day when Delta retires its latest Boeing 767-300ER…

Historically, Chicago O’Hare Terminal 5 has been a pretty lousy facility, though it has started to get better in recent years, thanks to an expansion to the terminal, and also thanks to the opening of new lounges.

Arguably the single most disappointing thing about Terminal 5 was the Swissport Lounge, which I’ve labeled the worst lounge in the United States — clearly many people agreed with me, since my review of the lounge had over 200 comments. It’s just truly one of those facilities that makes you wonder why we even bother using lounges.

The lounge had no windows, the most random collection of furniture, and a very limited selection of drinks and snacks. Unless you wanted to just guzzle alcohol, you’d be better off sitting in the terminal, in my opinion. Unfortunately for all too long, many premium airlines sent their customers here, given the lack of other lounge options. It’s quite underwhelming to be flying business class on a carrier like Etihad Airways or Qatar Airways, and then be sent here.

That brings us to the interesting update — the Swissport Lounge Chicago has closed as of October 31, 2026, so passengers are no longer being subjected to this facility.

It remains to be seen what happens with this lounge space, as that hasn’t been announced one way or another. In April 2025, Swissport’s head of North America lounges said that the company had “submitted a proposal to the Chicago Department of Aviation (CDA) for a multi-million-dollar renovation and expansion of our current Terminal 5 lounge at O’Hare International Airport.” However, I haven’t been able to find any updates on that, so I’m not sure what the latest is there.

It’s worth acknowledging just how much better the lounge situation at Chicago O’Hare Terminal 5 has gotten in recent years:

If we get another lounge at Terminal 5, that would of course be awesome, so I’m curious to see how this evolves. Chicago O’Hare is the biggest airport in the country to not have any sort of a credit card branded lounge, so I hope that eventually changes. The issue is the extent to which the terminals are dominated by their primary tenants (American and United), combined with the lack of airside connections to Terminal 5.

The Swissport Lounge Chicago O’Hare Terminal 5 has suddenly closed. I’m not sad to see the United States’ worst airport lounge close, though I am curious to see what is next for this space. Fortunately the Terminal 5 lounge situation has improved nicely in recent years, and there are now several solid new options. I do wish we’d still see a proper credit card lounge, though.

Several years ago, rummaging through the archives of the Academy of American Poets, I came upon a box labeled “Ballots 1950” — the record of the secret vote by the chancellors the year the Academy’s prestigious fellowship was awarded to E.E. Cummings, catapulting him into renown. The voting process is a black box — no one outside the Academy ever finds out who else is in the running and by how much the winner wins.

Leafing through the ballots, one other name appeared over and over, so much so that I was impelled to count.

Marianne Moore had lost by one vote, never knowing how close she had come. It would be many more years until, at 77, she was finally awarded the fellowship.

Long before that, before she won the Pulitzer Prize and the National Book Award (sharing a table with Rachel Carson at the ceremony), Moore had set down her views on writing in a series of essays later collected in the out-of-print gem Predilections (public library). Pulsating through them is a reckoning with the impossible task of the writer — to weave tapestries of truth and meaning from the tenuous thread of words on the ramshackle loom of language.

In an essay titled “Feeling and Precision,” Moore writes:

Feeling at its deepest — as we all have reason to know — tends to be inarticulable. If it does manage to be articulate, it is likely to seem overcondensed, so that the author is resisted as being enigmatic or disobliging or arrogant.

How we name what we feel is not so much a matter of our writing style as of our style of being, because in order to articulate something we must first apprehend it and we apprehend every smallest thing with the whole person — with the frame of reference that is our entire life, the sum of our experience and memory. When “one of New York’s more painstaking magazines” asked Moore to distill her poetic style into a formula, she fought back the “dictatorial” reflex to quip:

You don’t devise a rhythm, the rhythm is the person, and the sentence but a radiograph of the personality.

And yet a personality can write with more or less persuasion — that is, write more or less well — depending on what the person brings to the writing. In another essay from the collection, Moore identifies the three psychological elements necessary for persuasive writing: “humility, concentration, and gusto.” A generation after Mark Twain assured his friend Helen Keller when she was accused of plagiarism that “substantially all ideas are second-hand, consciously and unconsciously drawn from a million outside sources,” she writes:

Humility… is armor, for it realizes that it is impossible to be original, in the sense of doing something that has never been thought of before. Originality is in any case a by-product of sincerity; that is to say, of feeling that is honest and accordingly rejects anything that might cloud the impression.

By “concentration” Moore means a kind of discipline — annealing the essence of the sentiment by cutting away all superfluous explanations, elaborations, and distractions of stylistic posturing, being maximally truthfully in the most minimal way possible. Observing that there is always a “helpless sincerity which precipitates a poem” and that a good poem is always “a concentrate,” she writes:

Concentration — indispensable to persuasion — may feel to itself crystal clear, yet be through its very compression the opposite… I myself would rather be told too little than too much.

Long before we had the language of extrinsic and intrinsic motivation, of finite and infinite games, Moore uses a lovely word, now dusty, for that peculiar private zeal propelling all creative work with its twin dynamos of discipline and deliverance: “gusto.” Echoing Rachel Carson’s abiding advice on writing — “If you write what you yourself sincerely think and feel and are interested in,” she had counseled a young writer, “the chances are very high that you will interest other people as well.” — Moore offers:

Gusto thrives on freedom, and freedom in art, as in life, is the result of a discipline imposed by ourselves. Moreover, any writer overwhelmingly honest about pleasing himself is almost sure to please others.

She maps the fundamental relationship between the three:

Humility is an indispensable teacher, enabling concentration to heighten gusto.

When creating in integrity with these three values, it ceases to matter how the work is received because the process of locating and articulating the truth as you feel it, the world as you see it, is its own reward. In what may be the best advice I have encountered on how to orient to your own work, Moore writes:

There are always objecters, but we must not be sensitive about not being liked or not being printed… The thing is to see the vision and not deny it; or care and admit that we do.

Complement with Walt Whitman on how to keep criticism from sinking your soul and Mary Oliver’s advice on writing, then savor the moving story of how Marianne Moore saved a rare tree with a poem.

7-year-old Pokemon fan furious that Magikarp got an SIR card before Gyarados

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

A seven-year-old Pokemon fan has gone viral after demanding that The Pokemon Company give Gyarados the Special Illustration Rare card he believes the classic Water-type deserves.

Gyarados first appeared in the physical Pokemon Trading Card Game’s Base Set and has since received dozens of printings, including ex, GX, V, VMAX and Shining versions. However, collectors still have no physical Gyarados Special Illustration Rare, the full-art rarity marked by two gold stars in the modern era.

The complaint is especially focused on Magikarp’s Illustration Rare from Paldea Evolved. The Shinji Kanda card became one of the set’s most talked-about chase cards because its artwork hides a Gyarados in the background, making the contrast with its evolution’s lack of a SIR even more noticeable.

In the 53-second TikTok, the child asks, “Can somebody explain how Gyarados still doesn’t have a special edition Pokemon card?”

He points out that Gyarados has been part of the Pokemon Trading Card Game since the original Base Set and says the Pokemon has “loads of different cards over the years.” He then calls Gyarados “one of the coolest Pokemon ever” before turning his attention to Magikarp.

“Don’t get me wrong, I love that Magikarp card,” he says, arguing that it makes no sense for Magikarp to have such a striking Illustration Rare while Gyarados still lacks a Special Illustration Rare. He also pitches the kind of artwork he would like to see, imagining Gyarados bursting out of the ocean in a dramatic scene.

“Come on Pokemon, give Gyarados an SIR!” he concludes, before asking viewers which Pokemon should receive one next.

The clip was reposted by Guru Collects on X, where it was described as a child finally having “ENOUGH of the Gyarados disrespect.” The repost helped push the complaint beyond the original TikTok, with collectors joining the conversation over which overlooked Pokemon deserve premium artwork.

Pokemon TCG Pocket does include a Gyarados Illustration Rare, but that digital card is separate from the physical Pokemon TCG releases being discussed in the video. The Pokemon Company has not announced a physical Gyarados SIR, so the seven-year-old’s request remains a fan campaign for now.

US air traffic control is finally phasing out floppy disks after decades of use

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) has announced plans to phase out floppy disks at hundreds of US air traffic control facilities, replacing technology that has been in use for decades.

The outdated storage devices are still used to handle weather data and other critical information at more than 400 facilities across the country, despite floppy disks largely disappearing from everyday use more than 25 years ago.

The announcement comes as the FAA works to modernize the country’s aging air traffic control infrastructure, which continues to rely on technology dating back to the 1980s.

According to Reuters, the FAA plans to replace its floppy disk-based technology by the end of 2028 through the introduction of a new Enterprise Information Display System.

The system will replace four older platforms dating back to the 1980s, bringing important operational information together in one place so air traffic controllers can access updates more quickly.

“We’re moving away from outdated, manual processes and replacing technology that once relied on floppy disks with a modern system that allows controllers to instantly share critical flight alerts, restricted airspace schedules and pilot reports,” FAA Administrator Bryan Bedford said in a statement.

Floppy disks were once a standard method of storing and transferring computer data, but their use declined significantly in the early 2000s. Large-scale production of the storage devices largely ended in 2011.

The FAA’s modernization efforts extend beyond floppy disks. According to Reuters, the agency still relies on decades-old Compaq computers and has even purchased replacement parts through eBay to maintain its existing infrastructure.

Many air traffic control facilities also operate on separate analog computer systems that cannot communicate directly with one another.

Where To Upgrade Weapons In Phantom Blade Zero

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Phantom Blade Zero doesn't follow the RPG trend of having weapons in different tiers that you replace with newer ones. Instead, it lets you upgrade the weapons you're using to match the difficulty of the locations you're in. With this system, you can keep using the same weapon throughout the game if you'd like and just keep upgrading it to stay effective.

However, to upgrade these weapons, you'll need resources specific to the weapon type, similar to how you choose your skills. This means you can upgrade different weapon types, but because each type has finite resources, you need to choose which weapons go best together.

To upgrade your weapons in Phantom Blade Zero, you'll need to use the bells you use to upgrade abilities and fast travel around the map. At the bell, select Upgrade Equipment to open your inventory and choose what you want to improve. Hold the prompted button (X on PS5) to upgrade the weapon you choose.

From this menu, you can choose from your Main Weapons, Phantom Edges and Accessories, and they all get upgraded similarly. Choose the item you want to upgrade, and it will show you what resources you need. Although the resources differ, they all require Remnant Dust, just like upgrading your abilities.

For regular weapons, there are three different weapon types to choose from: One-Handed, Two-Handed and Dual-Wield. To upgrade these, you'll need Forged Metal, Twinned Metal and Broadplate Metal, respectively. You'll also need Solvent, which applies to any weapon you're upgrading.

However, as you level your weapons higher, you'll need to improve the materials, too. For example, for one-handed weapons, you can use Forged Metal to upgrade up to level 10, but you need Polished Forged Metal to upgrade from level 10 to level 20. Furthermore, you'll need Flawless Forged Metal to upgrade beyond level 20 to max level.

The same applies to the solvent you use to upgrade weapons. Solvent is used for upgrading weapons up to level 10, Concentrated Solvent gets you from level 10 to level 20 and Universal Solvent gets you the rest of the way to max level.

The Bashpole gives you one extra way to find hidden treasure.

Upgrading Phantom Edges functions the same as upgrading weapons, but requires different materials. Just like how weapons have three different types - One-Handed, Two-Handed and Dual-Wield - there are five different types of Phantom Edges: Martial, Destruction, Artifice, Ranged, and Summon.

The materials required for upgrading Phantom Edges are as follows.

Reverting weapons is one of the game's best features because it lets you freely try different weapons as you play. If you revert an upgraded weapon, it returns to level 1 and refunds all resources used to upgrade it. This means you aren't penalized for experimenting with different weapons.

However, this does get more complicated as you upgrade weapons to higher levels. For levels 1-10, reverting weapons is free, but for higher levels, you have to use a Reforge Talisman. Reforge Talismans are available for both weapons and Phantom Edges, and they are not refunded after you revert levels.

This means you need to be more thoughtful about which weapons you upgrade and revert once you reach higher levels. Luckily, by the end of the game, you'll already know which weapons work best for your play style, so you won't need to revert as much.

Upgrading Accessories is a similar process to upgrading weapons and Phantom Edges. Go to a bell and select Upgrade Equipment, then go to the Accessories tab at the top of the menu.

To upgrade Accessories, you need only one resource along with your Remnant Dust: Tainted Crystal Shards. You can find these around the world like the rest of the in-game resources, but you can not revert accessories like you can with weapons and Phantom Edges.

Once you use them, you can't get Tainted Crystal Shards back.

Soul Tempering is the highest level of upgrading that you can do in Phantom Blade Zero. To do so, you need to find materials throughout the game that are specific to each weapon or Phantom Edge. You unlock Soul Tempering only after you've spoken to Myst at the Order's tavern in Pangzhen.

To Soul Temper a weapon or Phantom Edge, you have to be in the upgrade menu. At the bottom left, a prompt will appear to open the Soul Tempering menu. When you press it, it pulls up the list of weapons or Phantom Edges you have and lets you Soul Temper them if you have the right resources.

You can find Soul Temper materials in various places, such as chests or by defeating bosses. You only need one of each material to Soul Temper a given item, and you can't revert the upgrade or do it more than once.

Strauss Zelnick, the head of Rockstar Games owner Take-Two, is known for his dedication to health and fitness. In 2018, he published a book titled Becoming Ageless: The Four Secrets To Looking and Feeling Younger Than Ever, and images of the executive looking shredded and lean at 8% body fat have circulated online. Zelnick has now spoken more about his health and fitness lifestyle, and offered some advice for people who want to get in shape and feel better.

Zelnick started off by telling Business Insider that he's good friends with Bryan Johnson, the billionaire who is trying to live forever, but Zelnick doesn't subscribe to the kinds of things Johnson is trying to do in terms of longevity in that regard.

Instead, Zelnick said he got some advice from his former doctor, the celebrity longevity doctor Peter Attia who appeared in the Epstein files, who told him he could help him with his healthspan but not his lifespan, and Zelnick was into that.

In 2016, Zelnick filmed a video for Business Insider where he showed off his workout routine--you can see it below.

Zelnick said his No. 1 piece of advice for anyone trying to improve their health is to avoid smoking altogether. "Smoking is the factor most highly correlated with a short, unhealthy, and frankly unhappy life. Don't smoke." Second, Zelnick said people should not abuse alcohol and drugs. "Those are the two most highly correlated factors with a bad old age, or no old age," he said.

After that, Zelnick said it's a good idea to get "some exercise," and that could be walking for 30 minutes four or five days a week. Zelnick said he goes way beyond this because health and fitness are his hobbies "but you really don't need to do that." Beyond exercise, Zelnick said he recommends that people eat a "responsible" diet, but it doesn't have to be "insanely healthy." The best practice for food, Zelnick said, is to simply not overdo it on unhealthy foods.

Another part of increasing your healthspan is getting enough sleep, and that should be about seven hours each night, Zelnick said. "If you're getting less than six hours of sleep a night, you're making bad decisions; that's what the math says," he said. "You are going to make bad personal decisions and bad professional decisions."

Having some kind of "emotional or spiritual life" is important, too, Zelnick said. "You have to have people you love and love you in your life, and preferably, you have to have a community around you," he said.

Also in the interview, Zelnick pushed back against taking peptides, which are popular in some fitness circles, but are largely unregulated.

"No. We have an FDA for a reason," he said. "Don't take something that is not double-blind tested. Even if it worked, do the stuff I do, forget the peptides, and you'll be in a better place."

Lastly, Zelnick said if people want to lose weight, they should consider taking some kind of GLP-1.

"Don't be grossly overweight. But in the world of GLP-1s, you have no reason to be," he said. "I am a huge advocate [for GLP-1s]. And no, there is absolutely no shame in taking them, whatsoever. If you're carrying extra weight, take a GLP-1. I happen never to have struggled with my weight, ever; I am blessed that way."

"I have this workout group, and I've had people show up who are heavyset, because we welcome everyone. So one guy shows up and he comes over and he says, 'I want you to talk to me about your diet, because I want to lost weight. I want to lose 50 pounds.' So I said, 'See, there are these things called GLP-1s. Go to my endocrinologist and get a prescription.' He said, 'Really? What about diet?' I said, 'That is literally like saying to me, I have a migraine, and I would say to you, forget about Tylenol, just meditate.' You take prescribed medications to help you live a healthier life. Of course."
