After Weil Gotshal’s collapse, law firm merger interest heats up

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Consultants Kent Zimmermann of the Zeughauser Group and Jeff Grossman of Oxford Strategic Legal Advisors both said they have seen merger interest increase, with Grossman noting that new candidates emerged after Weil, Gotshal & Manges’ collapse. (Photo from Shutterstock)

The fall of Weil, Gotshal & Manges is pushing law firm leaders to rethink strategy, and many are looking to find merger partners.

According to a Thursday story by Law.com, consultants Kent Zimmermann of the Zeughauser Group and Jeff Grossman of Oxford Strategic Legal Advisors both said they have seen merger interest increase, with Grossman noting that new candidates emerged after Weil Gotshal’s collapse.

“Many conversations were put on hold earlier this year to watch where the economy was going, how AI was going to effect the transformation of law firms,” Grossman told Law.com, adding that interest in mergers “was picking up over the summer, but now the pace is getting faster.”

“A large and growing group of firms want considerably larger balance sheets, greater long-term stability, more money to invest in talent and technology, and other advantages that come with scale,” Zimmermann added.

Lisa Smith of Fairfax Associates offered a slightly different read, telling Law.com that merger demand was already strong in 2026, and Weil Gotshal mainly added urgency for firms that were already considering deals.

“The lesson of Weil is that for firms that are looking to grow and looking to grow via merger or particularly for those starting to see some cracks in the firm, it’s important to go to the merger market while you’re still in a strong position,” Smith said to Law.com.

Smith suggested that firms should approach the merger market from a position of strength. Firms that wait until trouble sets in risk becoming what advisers call a “falling knife,” an unstable partner few want to take on.

“We’ve seen a number of occasions over the last few years where a firm needed to merge with a ‘white knight’ and can only get that when the firm is strong enough to be a desirable acquisition,” Smith said. “For firms that start to see signs that the future is going to be more challenging, it’s important to look at their options early.”

Workplace bullying damages health and drains productivity. For autistic employees, it perpetuates the cycle of abuse that starts much earlier. A 2026 review confirmed that autistic students experience disproportionately high levels of bullying, with most estimates falling between 40% and 70% (Heredia-Oliva et al., 2026), and it is a major source of stress for them.

The painful pattern then follows people into work. And many work environments prove fertile grounds for both overt (threatening, shouting) and especially covert (backstabbing, sabotaging,

In experiments, neurotypical people reported liking autistic people less than neurotypical people, with vague explanations like perceived “awkwardness” (Alkhaldi et al, 2021). Workplace stressors have the potential to magnify the dislike. Autistic employees, with their spiky profiles of abilities, are more likely to be on extreme ends of performance. High performers often experience veiled violence like sabotage or having crucial information withheld due to coworker envy; low performers face overt hostility, which makes their performance decline rather than improve (Jensen et al., 2014). Add autistic tendencies of direct communication, including possibly challenging inefficiencies and misconduct (Hartman & Hartman, 2024), along with a focus on work over socializing, and the target is set.

Autistic people's nervous systems often have less capacity to recover from social stress (Cheng et al., 2020), and autistic adults are already at elevated risk of developing mental and physical health issues due to a mismatch between their needs and the environments, sensory stress, effortful masking, and other forms of chronic stress (Botha & Frost, 2020; Dhanasekara et al., 2023). Past bullying also leaves a psychological trace: 87.4% of autistic adults showed signs of fear of being laughed at, compared with 22.6% of non-autistic adults (Leader et al., 2018).

Multiple vulnerabilities create compounding effects. Years of mistreatment result in hypervigilance and keep the body's threat response switched on. Each new injury lands on a system that never fully recovered from the last. To make things worse, the lack of resources often associated with unemployment, with only about 3 in 10 working-age autistic people in the UK employed (Buckland, 2024), leaves fewer options for recovery, seeking help, or leaving a toxic job.

Reporting bullying at work is generally a thankless task. Reports are often ignored or dismissed: in a nationally representative U.S. survey, 60% of employers reacted negatively when bullying was reported (Namie, 2021), and targets describe HR as regularly siding with bullies (Boddy & Boulter, 2025). Moreover, skillful manipulators, which many bullies are, can present themselves as victims.

Autistic people, however, face additional barriers to successfully reporting bullying and being heard.

Most organizations say they are committed to healthy workplaces, but their methods for handling bullying often make things worse. Those methods fail autistic employees and others dealing with trauma or overwhelm in at least three ways.

They are reactive. Action typically begins only after bullying is proven. But the first line of defense against bullying and any other hazard at work is prevention (Praslova et al., 2022). Preventing harm is both more ethical and more practical than repairing it.

They put the burden on the target. Targets are told to document what was said and done, when, and who was present, then discuss it calmly with a supervisor. How many people can realistically do this while doing their jobs, under stress, and dealing with trauma? The process appears to be designed to make it easy to reject the complaint because of the “tone” or the target “being emotional.”

They try to fix personalities. Bullies may act out their entitlement, fragile egos, and poor self-control, but bullying flourishes where the environment lets it go undetected and rewards it with resources or advancement (Praslova et al., 2022; Salin, 2003). Change the environment, and you are more likely to change the behavior.

Remove the rewards for bullying. Avoid internal competition for scarce resources. Distribute resources fairly and transparently, and balance individual rewards with team rewards. Realistic workloads reduce bullying used to offload work onto others, and transparency may take some of the fuel out of envy. Transparent decision-making makes rumors and backstabbing less likely to pay off (Praslova et al., 2022).

Set standards and train for them. A clear policy should state that bullying is not tolerated, and name its covert forms, not just its overt ones (Praslova et al., 2022). Train everyone in respectful communication, and train new managers specifically for complex interpersonal situations. Ideally, facilitate professional support from psychologists trained in workplace well-being.

Build civility and voice together. Some people mistake authenticity for a license to be cruel. Others try to buy “calm” by silencing people. Neither works. Bullying of autistic people is a predictable outcome of systems that fail to check assumptions, reward sameness and conformity, and make targets prove their own reality. Workplaces that are healthy by design, with transparency, civility, and voice, on the other hand, not only support autistic people, but make work better for everyone (Praslova, 2024).

Better educated than ever – yet Germany's Gen Z is being shut out of its own future

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Never before have young people faced so many challenges. Young Germans are asking: Will I be better off later in life than my parents are now?

It is becoming increasingly difficult for young people in Germany to find a job after finishing professional training or university. And finding an affordable place to live in a big city and finally starting to live independently is nearly impossible.

Nor do young people feel that politicians are finally taking action on issues that matter to them, such as climate protection. The climate conference in Antalya, Turkey, is a case in point. It therefore shouldn't be a surprise that young people across Europe are taking to the streets this Friday to make their voices heard. In Germany, Gen Z protests took place in 73 cities, coinciding with World Children's Day.

What particularly infuriates young people is being talked about rather than spoken to. Equally infuriating is seeing their protests branded as criminal before they have even begun, all because of the actions of a small minority.

One example from the political realm was when the youth wing of the conservative Christian Democratic Union/Christian Social Union (CDU/CSU) party in Berlin-Mitte suggested deploying the German army against demonstrations.

It can also be seen in the media, such as when Bild, Germany's highest-circulation tabloid, ran the headline: "Young, male, known to the police — who rioted after the demonstrations and where chaos threatens today."

"Young people are taking to the streets because they feel they are not being heard. And, of course, that feeling is strongly reinforced when the press devotes no more than a brief paragraph to their demands while focusing the rest of its coverage on how these protests could become problematic," says Erik Sczygiol, who until recently was deputy secretary-general of the Federal Conference of Student Representatives. "Of course, any potential security concerns need to be addressed, but they should not overshadow media coverage of the protests," he argues.

However, eighteen-year-old Erik Sczygiol is an exception. He was invited to participate in the launch of the World Population Report in Bonn alongside representatives of the United Nations Population Fund (UNFPA) and the German Foundation for World Population (DSW).This year's report is particularly noteworthy because it focuses on Gen Z. The report is titled "Succeeding Generations: Empowering young people to chart the future."

The United Nations surveyed 109,000 people across 73 countries. Some of the findings are encouraging. Young people worldwide are better educated than any generation before them. Today, 78% of adolescents complete at least upper-secondary education. Yet finding a job is becoming increasingly difficult, while informal employment is on the rise across the globe.

This economic uncertainty has far-reaching consequences for young people. According to the report, financial independence is often not achieved until one's mid-to-late twenties, and a stable partnership and parenthood are often not realities until one's early-to-mid thirties.

Nor do young people have a seat at the political decision-making table when it comes to shaping future reforms in the labor market, housing or climate protection. Florian Brod, 25, points out: "Almost half of the world's population is under 30, yet only 2.6% of parliamentarians worldwide are under 30."

Young people need and want change to protect their livelihoods and those of future generations. "Yet those who will have to live longest with the consequences of today's decisions are precisely the ones who have hardly any representation in the political decision-making process," says Brod.

At least Brod is somewhat closer to the political decision-making process. He has served as a Youth Delegate for Sustainable Development for almost two years. In this role, he represents the interests of young people in Germany at United Nations conferences. This is certainly an important step towards giving his generation a greater say in political processes.

Nevertheless, both Florian Brod and Erik Sczygiol, who represents students, have had similar experiences. They often participate in conferences and panel discussions and are invited to submit their opinions, but these invitations often amount to little more than symbolic gestures.

"Having access to an international conference is a first step. But without substantive briefings ahead of the conference, without involvement in preliminary negotiations and, in some cases, without access to negotiation texts, there is little opportunity for us to help shape the outcome. When young people see their demands making little headway politically while they are simultaneously excluded from the search for solutions, frustration inevitably builds," says Brod.

Sczygiol adds: "We often get the feeling at the Federal Youth Conference that we were invited simply to provide a nice photo opportunity. We may be sitting at the table, but often only once a bill has already gone through 17 different drafts and is just days away from its final vote in the Bundestag. By that stage, young people have virtually no say in changing anything, and their voices are barely heard. Their contributions are often simply dismissed with: 'Well, that's just typical youthful nonsense.' And that is something that really frustrates young people."

Medical student Amélie Belosevic knows that feeling all too well. She served as Germany's Youth Delegate at the World Health Assembly in Geneva this May. Twenty-five countries sent youth representatives, which Belosevic sees as a clear step forward. However, she has one criticism. According to the United Nations Population Fund, 90% of the world's 1.8 billion young people live in the Global South. Yet, the region was represented by only eight delegates. As a result, Africa, Latin America and large parts of Asia were left virtually voiceless.

Her demands for Germany include better job prospects for young people entering the workforce and sustainable pension systems 40 years from now. "For a very long time, people have said, 'I believe my children will have a better life than I do.' But that is no longer necessarily true. So it makes sense then that those who feel the greatest anxiety about the future are the people who have most of their life ahead of them. Sometimes, people don't understand that. We, young people, need to be able to believe in our future again!"

GTA 6 Players Have Already Discovered an Infinite Money Glitch

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

These get rich quick schemes have the fanbase divided

Many players have a habit of optimizing the fun out of video games, and that tendency has unsurprisingly found its way into Grand Theft Auto 6. Apparently, the game has many ways to “get rich quick,” and these exploitive strategies are going viral online.

In one method, the player drives to the Apollo Aggregates warehouse in South Vice-Dale County, where a drug deal went wrong. The building is full of narcotics, spare bills, and dead bodies, letting you easily pillage close to $8,000 worth of loot. After grabbing the goods, the player stores the contraband in their car, then saves and loads to teleport back to their house. At this point, they bring the contraband to Brian’s place to cash out. A YouTube video outlining the method claims you can make around $4,000 every five minutes, which is more efficient than nearly everything else in the early game.

In another strategy that falls more into the "glitch" category, the player robs the stores near Jason’s house, loses the cops, and then enters any story mission. After the story mission cutscene begins, the player selects Restart Mission in the menu and then pauses and quits. This causes all the stores the player just robbed to instantly reopen with new loot, allowing them to rob them again.

However, despite how efficient these early-game earning strategies are, many players are swearing off the temptation. “Nah I’m good! Didn’t wait 13 years just to glitch my way through the game,” one user wrote on X. “Top tip, if you just play the game the amount of money you can make is unlimited, no special trick needed,” a sardonic YouTube comment reads.

These users point out that earning so much money early undermines the game’s economy, ruins the gradual progression of unlocking better gear, and invalidates many of the game’s cash-earning side missions. On top of this, these methods directly counter the narrative flow of GTA 6, which is about Lucia and Jason attempting to scrape together some semblance of the fabled “American Dream.”

While some are baffled by why anyone would use such mind-numbing strategies, players are obviously entitled to play the game however they want. Still, it seems likely that Rockstar will patch the game soon to remove these methods, especially the second one, which seems to be a bug.

Rising memory costs are hitting mid-range devices the hardest. The latest report suggests Samsung will increase the price of its Galaxy A device series by 19% next year.

The report comes from Korean news outlet SEDaily. An industry source has reportedly stated that Samsung plans to increase the price of its mid-range phones starting next year. More specifically, the Galaxy A series.

The range between price jumps varies across a reported 8 Galaxy A series devices, but the highest value difference nearly hits a notable 19% increase. The largest jump reaches 18.6% for the Galaxy A17, which could increase from $269 to $319. It would no longer sit in a sub-$300 spot and wouldn’t be much of a value buy anymore.

Again, these are phones praised for sitting at a convenient price point. Samsung’s Galaxy A devices are generally good value and offer a lot of the best One UI features for a price that doesn’t come close to the current flagship selling points.

This comes after a report came out last month indicating Samsung cut production of its Galaxy A devices by “approximately 20%.” Memory makes up more of the overall component cost in mid-range devices. So the cost of the overall device is more influenced by memory prices than in a flagship, like the Galaxy S26 Ultra. Even with a specced-out build, there’s more dispersion of costly components, rather than one expensive component like memory taking up the bulk of the production price.

That cut is estimated to be around 10 million units, and this latest report only furthers the claim that mid-range devices are too costly to make for Samsung.

The opposite is true for the Galaxy S27 Pro and Ultra, which reportedly got the green light for increased production prior to launch. Price suggestions average an increase of $200 for the Galaxy S27 and Galaxy S27 Plus. As for the high-spec Galaxy S27 Pro and Ultra, they start at $1,399 and $1,499, respectively.

There was already a massive disparity between those devices and the increasingly validated Galaxy A devices, and the gap is shrinking.

The 6 Most Fun Things To Do in the First Few Hours of ‘GTA 6’

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Introducing Grand Theft Auto 6 is difficult, because there is so much to talk about, if you want to keep things broad. Broad is not the word of the day here, though. The only people who can go broad and talk about everything are reviewers who got a copy of the game some weeks early, and then the review embargo was lifted right as the game came out. Some of those people claim to have played GTA 6 for 100 to 200 hours, and many of them still claim they're finding new things. Some remain feeling overwhelmed, when it comes to talking about the whole thing and breaking down just what the game means. So, the person writing this… he’s only had the game for about two days. He’s devoted many hours of those two days to it, but he’s only about 11% of the way through the story. That does mean there won’t be spoilers here, at least as far as the narrative is concerned, but being in this position does mean only a few things can be talked about.

To spin something out of a lack of experience, though, how about this: GTA 6 is a slow-paced game at the beginning, not to the same extent as Red Dead Redemption 2, but it takes a while to really get going. But because it’s not as patient as Red Dead Redemption 2, there are still some fun things you can do once you’ve beaten the early tutorial missions (the ones that are brief and don’t allow freedom). The next few hours you spend with Jason, because the story kicks off with Lucia, the other protagonist, in prison (that was in the trailer! Not a spoiler). People have made fun of GTA 6 for having some tedious early missions, and also some activities that make you do chores and housework in preparation for Lucia getting out of prison, but alongside those slower parts, there are still some pretty wild things you can do in GTA 6 not long after you start playing the game for the first time. These are some of the most fun activities that are possible to do early in GTA 6, within the first area of the (absolutely massive) map, basically in the space between finishing the tutorial and seeing Lucia once she’s released from prison.

It’s tempting to go to Vice City early on, because that’s the big city in GTA 6. And that’s where 2002’s GTA: Vice City was set, so seeing how the city’s changed in 40 years (since that game was set in the 1980s, though if you want to get technical, they're not really in the same continuity anyway) is tempting. You can go there early, but you're not really given a reason to until later in the game, when the story takes you to other areas of the giant map. And the map is giant, because GTA 6 gives you various locations within the whole state of Leonida, which is the GTA universe’s version of Florida, so Vice City lines up with Miami. But there’s a lot to do in the first area of the game, beyond the early (and slow-paced) story missions.

There are also lots of places to go around Key Lento, but you can wander around the town for an hour or two and just get entertainment from that alone.

Key Lento is where the game wants you to stay for the first few hours, and even if it’s a small town, it’s got a lot of character and things to do. There are also lots of places to go around Key Lento, but you can wander around the town for an hour or two and just get entertainment from that alone. Some story requirements do get you interacting with some townspeople, including Mandy (pictured above), but there are things you can do by way of interacting with her and other people beyond the story that are interesting (Mandy’s memorable because of her strange dynamic with Jason, owing to him being with Lucia, but only kind of, since she’s in prison and all). And then your neighbor Shirley is an interesting character, too, asking you to get her rum and then steal a mobility scooter off another neighbor. And then there are all the other interactions you can have with NPCs who aren’t really characters, like the guy who you might see accidentally light himself on fire by spraying gasoline around at a fuel station, Zoolander-style. Just running into people around this small town is a blast, even if it’s so much smaller and low-stakes than what you'll ultimately get into later.

Well, you know, it’s a Grand Theft Auto game. There is, uh, “nightlife” to see. These parts of the game are controversial to some extent, but the first club you can go to that’s a bit risqué is a little more wholesome than similar clubs in past games. Unless you ask for a private dance, NINE1NINE is basically a normal nightclub, and going there as Jason, without Lucia, is pretty funny. You won’t have much money to spend, but you can get drunk and dance (and interact) with other people there to a greater extent than clubs in previous games. Everything is expensive, it's too loud, and you leave feeling dizzy and looking sweaty. In other words, it is a great simulation of going to an actual nightclub in real life.

It does involve venturing into Vice City, but NINE1NINE is the one nightclub you can get into without progressing through the story. And it’s a great showcase for the game’s lighting and capacity to have countless NPCs on screen at the same time. There is a real spectacle to the place, and sure, if you want to look at the risqué stuff, that option’s there as well (but not as out in the open, which is good to keep in mind if you yourself are playing GTA 6 out in the open… you know).

Like going into Vice City itself, to see a nightclub, maybe it’s better to wait a little while before doing too much by way of combat, but there are benefits to tackling some of the earlier Illegal Operations locations ASAP. If you have a gun, you can probably make some progress. Being stealthy early on helps, and then you can pick up better firearms from the first few people you take out.

Basically, it’s an adrenaline rush to take on gangs of criminals before you have many abilities and easy access to firearms. Further, if you do succeed in getting through some of these early gang hideouts, it’s the only real way you're going to find semi-advanced weapons or genuinely fast cars early on (and the latter is especially thrilling to take control of for the first time, well before any story mission lets you drive something so high-class).

This builds on that previously mentioned “meeting strangers” thing, which sure, was also a feature in previous Rockstar games, but it’s elevated in GTA 6 for sure. You can tell as much just from the early area of the game. It’s worth drawing attention to one particular encounter, though, because GTA 6 does not really waste time letting you get a glimpse of Bigfoot, or the Leonida equivalent, referred to sometimes as Skunk Ape.

He’s in the swamps around Key Lento, and look, this particular player hasn’t encountered him again, so there’s clearly more going on with him and his deal, but running into him just a few hours into the game is wild. The hunt for Bigfoot, or rumors around him being in some previous GTA games, has plagued players for years (see GTA: San Andreas, especially). But GTA 6 isn't afraid to let you see/investigate him almost right from the start. If the first 10% of the game can let you look into Bigfoot/Skunk Ape, it gets the mind racing about what weird secrets and unsettling easter eggs might be experienced dozens of hours into the game.

If the Illegal Operations side missions seem a bit daunting, one thing you should do (and get used to) is robbing places. Like, almost any building that can be entered has things you can steal in it, and there are so many more interiors than could be found in GTA 4 and GTA 5. GTA 5 did have those big heists, and you won’t be a stranger to committing crimes if you’ve played other GTA games, but the emphasis on all the smaller and spur-of-the-moment robberies in GTA 6 is something special, not to mention unique.

You do learn which kinds of stores are better to rob than others, and it’s always tense to go into a new one and scope it out, because they’ll usually have different backrooms and things like safes (and monitors where you can delete CCTV footage) in different places. You'll also be kept on your toes because cashiers react differently… like, one had a severe panic attack once, and that forces you to make a decision about helping him by getting his medical vape, or leaving him to pass out. And sometimes, you go to steal from a place and someone will already be in there trying to rob the place. For example, this player once had an NPC doing so with a banana instead of a gun, and you can embarrass him by telling the cashier that the amateur robber actually has a banana, sending the robber on the way before robbing the place for real with your gun… which is emotional whiplash for the poor cashier, but hey, it’s literally all in the game.

Driving around is what you do a lot in every GTA game, and GTA 6 being a leap forward technically makes going for aimless drives even more enjoyable and immersive. You do have to worry about fuel, but genuinely running out without a fuel station around is rare, plus you can get to most places without having to fill up. All the cars handle in interesting and different ways, and then driving is also the easiest way to get control of all the radio stations in GTA 6.

And the radio… well, yeah, like driving, listening to fake radio shows/news reports and real-world music is fun in every GTA game, but there’s a larger variety of songs here, and just an overall higher quantity of music. It would take hours upon hours of driving to hear every song, since there are more than a dozen radio stations, and some stations themselves have dozens of songs they’ll cycle through in random order. There are also all the songs written for GTA 6, and that’s impressive, plus in-game podcasts if you ever get sick of the radio stations, by any chance, but some of the most satisfying moments of the game’s first few hours involve driving and/or listening to music. Go figure.

Teenage Engineering announced what it describes as “a modern take on a classic CD player” with its new EP/BC–1 featuring a slab-like design that leaves the spinning disc exposed instead of hiding it under a lid or inside a slide-out tray. The company says the EP/BC–1 is the first entry in its new broadcast series of products and was designed to bring “physical audio formats into a modern context, evolved from a shared obligation within the company to preserve the formats they grew up with.”

The EP/BC-1 is available now through Teenage Engineering’s online store for $299. The CD player joins the company’s existing EP–series of audio devices and has the same dimensions as its KO II synthesizer that launched in 2023 so it can be paired with, and connected to, other Teenage Engineering gear including the recently-announced EP-35 battery-powered amp.

Although it’s designed to play a nearly 45 year old medium, the EP/BC-1 features modern conveniences like variable speed playback, cue markers, a jog wheel for forward and reverse search, and built-in beat detection. There’s a very retro-looking series of digital displays showing track, index, time information, and audio levels, and around the edges you’ll find both digital and analog audio connections including a headphone jack and a USB 2.0 port.

If you want to go completely wireless the CD player has a rechargeable battery and supports both Bluetooth connections to headphones and Auracast streaming to multiple devices that support it. The EP/BC-1 is compatible with standard CDs as well as discs full of MP3 files, and when using the former it can rip tracks to 44.1 Hz/16-bit WAV files. If you want MP3s or other formats, you’ll need to convert them yourself.
