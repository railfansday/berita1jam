Fantasy Football 2026: Week 10 preview — Start/sit and more

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Welcome to Week 10. The 18-Week NFL season is at its halfway point, and the trade deadline has passed. Most fantasy leagues have five weeks left before the playoffs start. Crunch time is here.

Six teams switched to a different quarterback in Week 9. It's one week later and five more new QBs are expected in Week 10, for Arizona, Baltimore, Las Vegas, Miami, and Minnesota. Three of those newbies will be making their first-ever NFL start. These QB replacements impact players at other positions. I'll highlight that as we go. Throw in a boatload of injuries and four teams on bye, and Week 10 could be a rough one for a lot of fantasy managers.

If you have a minute, check out the current wide receiver rooms for the Rams, Cardinals, and Colts. No, those aren't made-up names you can use for phony phone calls. They're real.

Every team in the NFC North is at least three games over .500. That's only happened in a division once, at any time, in NFL history. They all have at least six wins.

2. From the penthouse to the outhouse: All four teams in the NFC South have three wins. You can't even make this stuff up at this point.

3. No division has ever put all four of its teams into the playoffs, but if the season ended today, all four NFC North teams would get berths in the NFC.

4. Favored teams went 14-1 in Week 9, with only the Patriots losing.

5. The Broncos have won 16 consecutive one-score games in the regular season – the longest streak all-time.

6. The QB with the longest active win streak as a starter is…J.J. McCarthy, with six consecutive wins dating back to last season.

7. Christian McCaffrey caught the 37th touchdown pass of his career on Sunday, moving him past Marshall Faulk for most by a running back in the Super Bowl era.

8. The Raiders have given up 30+ points in six straight games, tying an NFL record.

9. Fantasy stat #1: Ashton Jeanty's receiving line on Sunday: Five catches for negative five yards.

10. Fantasy Stat #2: Both the Bengals and Packers have two wide receivers in the Top-8 at the position on a fantasy points per game (FPPG) basis.

On to week 10. Note: NE @ DET is being played in Munich. Set those lineups early! Good news for those on the West Coast – this is the last international game of the season.

Bye Weeks: Bears, Broncos, Buccaneers, Eagles

For those who are familiar with this column, you know the drill. For everyone else: The Rides, Fades, and Sleepers is an analysis of players that I think are primed for an especially strong or poor performance, often as compared to consensus expectations. This isn't a straight-up Start/Sit exercise. As a general rule, start your studs. I'll rarely list the most obvious names at a position as "Rides" because those players are almost always expected to have strong performances. You don't need me or anyone else telling you to start Jahmyr Gibbs. While this analysis is intended for season-long play, it works for DFS formats too. Half PPR scoring and Expert Consensus Rankings (ECR) are used for the column.

Each week, I'm picking a Ride, Fade, and Sleeper of the Week. The rules: The Ride of the Week can't be a truly elite option, the Fade can't be someone who nobody is starting anyway, and the Sleeper must be an actual sleeper, from down in the rankings.

Last week, my Rides, Fades, and Sleepers did well, with a lot more hits than misses, led by Fade of the Week Zay Flowers (3.8 fantasy points). You can check my work here.

Ride of the Week: James Cook (vs. NYJ). Maybe Cook should be listed among the elite options. He's quietly had a great season, with a touchdown in every week but one, but he hasn't had that monster game yet (he hasn't topped 22 fantasy points in a week). A surprising lack of work in the passing game, and Josh Allen gobbling up rushing TDs, has held him back. Well, I think this is the week. The Bills are 8.5 point favorites, and the Jets have a generous run defense. They've allowed the tenth most FPPG and fifth most rushing yards to opposing backs, plus 11 total TDs. Cook should smash this week.

Fade of the Week: Ashton Jeanty (@SEA). Jeanty hasn't topped 10 carries or 27 rushing yards in his last five outings, and he hasn't scored more than 13.5 fantasy points since his breakout in Week 1. Receiving work has kept him afloat, and I don't know how that's going to work in Fernando Mendoza's debut, which couldn't come in a worse spot than against Seattle. If the Raiders score ten points, I'll be surprised. Their implied total is 17 and I think that's unlikely. Jeanty is a clear sit for me this week.

Sleeper of the Week: DeMario Douglas (vs. DET). Douglas has emerged as New England's top receiver amid all the injuries, and they're going to need plenty from him against the Lions. He quietly put up three Top-12 weekly finishes at his position in the last five weeks. Detroit's defense has played much better of late, but that was against Miami and Minnesota. They're still allowing the seventh most FPPG to opposing WRs. This game has the second highest Vegas total of the week (51.5). Douglas is ranked outside the Top-25 receivers this week and that's too low – he's a solid WR2 this week.

Elite options this week – Josh Allen, Drake Maye, Brock Purdy, and Joe Burrow; the analysis starts below them.

Three QBs ranked in the Top-10 this week who I think will outperform:

Dak Prescott (vs. SF). The 49ers defense is playing well this season (ninth fewest FPPG allowed to QBs), but that doesn't scare me off this pick. The 49ers' offense is elite, even with all the injuries, and the Cowboys' "defense" isn't likely to offer much resistance. Dallas is going to need to score to keep up, and Prescott should be slinging it. The Vegas total for this game is one of the highest of the season (55.5).

Trevor Lawrence (@TEN). The risk here is that he won't need to throw all that much – which happened last week against the impotent Ravens. I'll take that chance. The Titans have allowed the fourth most passing yards in the NFL and they've also yielded three rushing TDs to opposing QBs. They also traded away their most impactful player in Jeffery Simmons. I think they'll keep the game competitive for a while. Lawrence should do enough damage before the game is out of hand.

Jared Goff (@NE) has to be licking his chops after what Jordan Love did to New England's secondary. It was big play after big play, and Goff can do that too with his excellent receiving weapons. I'm sure the Patriots will play better, but Goff was frustrated by Miami and Minnesota the last two weeks and is due for one of his big games.

Three others in the Top-12 who I like to beat their projections this week: Patrick Mahomes (@ATL), Tyler Shough (vs. CAR), and Jayden Daniels (@NYG). Daniels ran for over 100 yards and two TDs in the first meeting with New York (NOTE – bad weather could be an issue).

Bryce Young (@NO) is always a tough call. He threw three TDs last week, and on the season he's the QB11. But he's been highly inconsistent, with four games with 20+ fantasy points and three with fewer than 14. I like him this week, against a mediocre (at best) New Orleans defense that's bottom-third against QBs, and in a game that should be back-and-forth with plenty of scoring. He's ranked outside the Top-12 QBs this week and I think he'll have a Top-10 finish.

Jordan Love (vs. MIN) is playing out of his mind, and I wouldn't sit him unless you've got another really good QB. But Minnesota's defense is no joke, and on Monday night they held Josh Allen to a season-low 14 fantasy points. Love leads the NFL in passing yards, so this is strength on strength. The Vikings have allowed the fewest passing yards and FPPG to opposing QBs. They've also allowed a league-low five touchdown passes. This game is in Green Bay, which helps.

He's obviously familiar with the Minnesota offense but he just got there, and Green Bay's defense is playing well. They're allowing the seventh fewest FPPG to opposing QBs and have been getting a lot of pressure. Cousins is also not going to be asked to air it out all the time like he did in Vegas.

Dropping lower, here are some QB2s who fantasy managers could be considering this week, but that I'm avoiding if I can: Matthew Stafford (@ARI, he has no receivers, and the Cardinals held him in check the first time these teams met), Michael Penix, Jr. (vs. KC), Daniel Jones (vs. MIA), and Justin Herbert (@BAL).

Jonathan Taylor (vs. MIA) didn't get traded in the Colts' fire sale, and there will be matchups where game script is going to be an issue for him. This shouldn't be one of them. Miami's defense is playing hard, but they're starting a sixth-round QB making his first start which favors a good game script, and they've been tough to throw on so they get run on a lot. They're allowing the seventh most FPPG to opposing RBs. Start Taylor with confidence like you did earlier in the season.

Hark! A new Slay the Spire 2 patch is here! Or, well, it is if you're someone who's trying out the beta branches of the game. First up, a new character: The Fool! "Wah!! The first new character for Early Access arrives!" a Steam blog post explains. "A silly musician who wields a bow! How can such a whimsical creature survive within the Spire? Why, that's up to you! We expect to make a great number of content and balance changes before the Fool is ready for the main branch. Even more importantly, be sure to have fun figuring out all the strategies and story of this gently bard. It would be foolish not to!" There are also two new game modes to try out with this beta update. The first is Blitz, a "condensed and chaotic deckbuilding-focused mode." Here, the "Merchant's wares feel a bit off, the map is gone, and Gold no longer matters." You'll also find it comes with five levels of Ascension shared across every character, and you can unlock it by completing an Ascension run on any difficulty. The second mode is Dread, which as the name implies, is a "brutal" difficulty mode that you can only play if you've reached Ascension 10 on every character, including The Fool. You can expect a bunch of new balance tweaks and changes too. For starters, you'll find that rest sites can now appear a floor earlier every act. New events have been added to Underdocks and Act 2 and 3 too. Some nice quality of life alterations have been made, like how the character select screen for standard runs will start on whatever character you played as last, and controller navigation at the Merchant has been improved too. Cindy Holscher wins in Kansas, holding governor's office for Democrats

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

State Sen. Cindy Holscher has won the race for Kansas governor, keeping the office for Democrats in a state that leans Republican. Here she's seen speaking to reporters after casting her ballot during early voting at a polling location in Kansas City, Kansas. Jim Vondruska/Getty Images hide caption

Democratic state Sen. Cindy Holscher will hold onto the Kansas governor's office for her party after defeating Republican Ty Masterson, according to a race call by the Associated Press.

Masterson, the president of the state Senate, was endorsed by President Trump. Of the 36 elections for governor across the country, Kansas was seen as the most likely to flip from Democratic to Republican control.

The race call came after days of counting the provisional ballots and with local officials doing recounts in the closely contested election.

Current Democratic Gov. Laura Kelly is term-limited after serving for eight years in a state with a heavily Republican legislature and that usually votes for Republicans for other offices and in presidential elections.

Holscher, 57, ran a campaign with notes of populism, promising to fight "sweetheart deals" for billionaires. She tied Masterson to his work in the state Senate when it passed a tax cut under former Republican Gov. Sam Brownback that led to a series of budget crises and was later repealed.

Holscher opposed school vouchers, calling for more support for public schools, in contrast with Masterson, who backed school vouchers. She also called for a moratorium on new data centers and for legalizing cannabis as a way to generate state revenue.

Masterson, 57, called for lower property taxes and said faith influenced all his decisions, including in politics. He promised to be the "most pro-ag, pro-business, pro-oil and gas governor," the state has seen. He called Holscher, "the most radical, liberal, socialist Democrat ever" on a ticket in Kansas.

"He likes to throw around a whole lot of labels," Holscher responded. "You know why? Because that's all he's got."

8 Most Perfect Sitcoms of the Last 30 Years, Ranked

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

The sitcom genre is rife with some of TV's most unforgettable and entertaining watches, and over the last 30 years, viewers have been well-fed with an incredible variety of comedic masterpieces that have introduced iconic characters, redefined television humor, and delivered countless memorable moments that continue to keep audiences fantastically captivated even decades later. While the list is quite long, only a select few sitcoms have managed to stand out as perfect examples of the genre.

TV series like HBO's Veep, which turns the world of American politics into a nonstop series of embarrassing moments, and the long-standing audience favorite, The Office, which transforms the ordinary environment of a paper company into laugh-out-loud funny workplace comedy, are just two sitcoms that prove that over the last 30 years the genre has delivered brilliantly written and endlessly entertaining gems. Compiled on this list are such gems, sitcoms that effortlessly stand among TV's comedic masterpieces of the last thirty years.

This underrated sitcom is an entertaining combination of youthful energy and heartfelt parenting moments, giving it a special charm that allows the show to remain incredibly enjoyable even decades later. The Black sitcom One on One follows a single father, Flex Washington (Flex Alexander), whose comfortable bachelor life comes to an end when his teenage daughter moves in with him.

One on One is an early 2000s sitcom that captures the complicated but entertaining relationship between father and daughter pretty well. It's a show that is often outshined by bigger hits like Martin, Family Matters, and The Fresh Prince of Bel-Air, but it does deserve its flowers for offering audiences real humor and genuine warmth with a fun, familiar comedic premise. One on One even avoids relying on exaggerated situations just for comedy, instead finding humor in recognizable struggles between fathers and their daughters. It's an underrated gem that definitely stands as a worthy contender among some of the most entertaining sitcoms of the past three decades.

Archer stands as an outstanding animated sitcom of the last 30 years because it effortlessly blends ridiculous workplace comedy with the glamorous world of espionage. The series centers on elite spy Sterling Archer (H. Jon Benjamin), whose competence with fieldcraft and weapons is offset by recklessness, narcissism, and a catastrophic relationship with his mother and boss, Malory Archer (Jessica Walter).

Archer is a pretty underrated series—one worthy of the praise it's received. Its earlier seasons have achieved extraordinary verbal precision, offering viewers brilliantly timed jokes, razor-sharp dialogue, and an almost effortless ability to turn even the most ridiculous situations into pure comedic gold. What makes Archer especially impressive is how seamlessly its absurd humor works alongside its unforgettable characters, their constant bickering, and dysfunctional relationships, making it a great pick for this list of well-crafted sitcoms of the last three decades.

This HBO comedy is one of the most ruthless and verbally inventive shows ever made. Veep focuses on the vice president, Selina Meyer (Julia Louis-Dreyfus), whose desperate attempts to gain influence frequently result in mortifying disasters as she gradually realizes proximity to power can be more humiliating than possessing no power at all.

Veep is an exceptionally well-written series with vicious humor that has been dubbed one of TV's sharpest political comedies. In fact, the show is quite frequently praised as one of the clearest demonstrations of verbal comic engineering. From insults that are not just detachable one-liners but instead cleverly woven into the political rivalries and characters' relationships, to perfectly timed interruptions, rapid-fire exchanges, and laugh-out-loud uncomfortable conversations, Veep transforms almost every interaction into an opportunity for brilliant comedy. The outstanding show, with its clever writing, fantastic attention to comedic timing, and character-driven humor, makes it a perfectly crafted sitcom of the last 30 years.

Derry Girls is an underrated Netflix sitcom that deserves far more attention than it's received since its debut. Set in 1990s Derry during the Troubles, the series follows the self-dramatizing teenager Erin Quinn (Saoirse-Monica Jackson) and her friends, the anxious Clare Devlin (Nicola Coughlan), fearless Michelle Mallon (Jamie-Lee O’Donnell), cousin Orla McCool (Louisa Harland) and Michelle’s English cousin James Maguire (Dylan Llewellyn), as they navigate family expectations, school, friendship, and constant embarrassment.

Derry Girls absolutely refuses to make historical violence the sole subject of its story, yet somehow the series never allows viewers to forget that it's happening. The show is actually an extremely clever series that wields a combination of dark humor, raw writing, and a Northern Irish setting. Derry Girls is a truly rare gem that delivers jokes with confidence and speed—a genuinely overlooked sitcom featuring brilliantly performed comedy and a recognizable cultural setting, making it one of TV's most memorable shows in its genre over the last three decades.

Four Netflix characters, four completely different ways of showing up for somebody. This is the one who fits how you actually do friendship, and what having them in your life would be like.

You do not do friendship at half volume, and neither does he. What you want is somebody who says yes to the plan before you have finished describing the plan.

You are the friend who makes things happen, and so is she. Together you would be mildly exhausting to everyone around you and completely delighted with yourselves.

You have no patience for pleasant lies, and she has no ability to tell them. What looks like coldness from the outside is the most reliable friendship on this list.

You look after whoever is having the worst day. So does he. It is the least strategic way to move through the world and the best possible quality in a friend.

This recently made sitcom is a fantastic reboot of the 1987 hit series, and it has definitely begun to make its mark. The Netflix 2026 sequel, A Different World, centers on the daughter of the beloved characters, Dwayne Wayne (Kadeem Hardison) and Whitley Gilbert (Jasmine Guy), Deborah Wayne (Maleah Joi Moon), as she begins her own journey at Hillman College.

A Different World is an enticing mesh of contemporary student experiences and nostalgic connections. It's already very well-loved as a standout modern sitcom, praised for its lovable cast and relatable scenes. Rather than simply relying on familiar faces, the show instead uses its original legacy as the foundation for meaningful conversations and new comedic situations, offering something unique to this new batch of viewers tuning in. A Different World may be stemming from a truly beloved sitcom of the past, but it still manages to carve out its own place among the best of the best sitcoms.

Tittering on the thin line between sitcom and fun-filled drama, The Good Place brilliantly balances hilarious comedy with thought-provoking questions about life, morality, what it truly means to be a good person, and heartfelt storytelling. The series focuses on Eleanor Shellstrop (Kristen Bell), who dies and wakes up in the presumed "Good Place," only to quickly realize that she has been confused with a morally superior person and must learn from new friends how to be an actually good individual.

The Good Place somehow transforms moral philosophy into useful sitcom plotting rather than simply using it as decoration. With virtue ethics, utilitarianism, and deontology acting as a strong source for character conflict, while also providing truly hilarious situations, surprisingly emotional moments, and meaningful personal growth, the series is impressively entertaining. Of the last three decades, The Good Place defiantly claims a spot among some of the greatest sitcoms ever made.

The Office is a cherished and enduring sitcom whose characters gradually become far more than the subjects of workplace jokes. The Minimalist ’90s-Style Coat Will Be Everywhere This Winter

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

The return of ’90s fashion is hardly new territory for the Vogue Shopping desk. But if 2026 has proved anything, it’s that our collective obsession with the decade isn’t going anywhere anytime soon. This year, Carolyn Bessette-Kennedy fever swept the headlines for months, coinciding with the release of Ryan Murphy’s hit drama Love Story, which (albeit after a few costume department consultations) sparked a return to the late fashion icon’s off-duty photographs, in search of styling inspiration.

“The online deluge of CBK content isn’t just generating likes; it’s been driving sales, too—a quick browse of the websites of Massimo Dutti or Reformation will give you some idea of just how swiftly brands have moved to capitalize on consumer fascination with this uncomplicated, clean aesthetic,” wrote Vogue’s Emma Spedding in her recent story on minimalism’s continued grip on our autumn wardrobes. She outlined the single-breasted longline coat as a key piece in achieving the sought-after CBK look–alongside a pencil skirt, turtleneck vest and simple leather bag.

Such coats were a cornerstone of the ’90s collections of Calvin Klein, Jil Sander, and Helmut Lang, characterised by (but not bound to) clean lines, understated single-breasted buttoning, discreet masculine lapels and a long, lean silhouette. Usually rendered in a palette of black, charcoal grey, beige, camel, and chocolate brown.

CBK was frequently photographed on the streets of New York in versions crafted from both wool and lighter-weight gabardine, styled off-duty with straight-leg jeans, oval sunglasses and an Alice band by day, or draped over a simple LBD (probably Calvin Klein) with pointed black slingbacks (probably Prada) into the night. Kate Moss, too, favored the style in her early Soho days, often opting for a cashmere camel iteration.

For fall/winter 2026, the coat shape was a key style on the runway. Jil Sander, unsurprisingly, remains something of an authority on the subject, offering a multitude of interpretations, from extra-long and oversized to collarless and polished leather styles. Elsewhere, designers have found ways to disrupt the coat’s pared-back credentials. Dries Van Noten introduced a satin finish and statement pixelated graphic, while Miu Miu did what Miu Miu does best and took the familiar shape, rendered it in crumpled leather and added a fuzzy faux-fur trim.

Unlike more statement-making outerwear that often dominates the discourse, a well-cut, single-breasted coat does a lot with very little, whether layered over tailoring for the office, worn with jeans and loafers at the weekend or thrown over eveningwear as the nights draw in.

Look for a shape that falls below the knee, a relatively clean shoulder line and enough room to accommodate a knit underneath, without swamping you when worn with bare arms. Of course, wool and cashmere styles will earn their keep on colder days, but lighter cotton versions make a useful option for milder weather and transitional dressing.
