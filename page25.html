PSA Pilots Secure Contract Extension Through 2030

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

PSA Airlines’ pilot union leaders have approved an agreement that extends the regional carrier’s pilot contract by two years and locks in three rounds of pay increases.

The Air Line Pilots Association announced the agreement Wednesday. It extends the existing contract through April 1, 2030. The previous extension, ratified in 2022, had set an amendable date of April 1, 2028.

Current pay rates will remain in place until June 1, 2027, when the first increases take effect. Additional raises are scheduled for June 1 in each of the following two years. The deal also changes scheduling and work rules. ALPA’s announcement did not specify the size of the raises or detail the scheduling changes.

“When we asked our pilots what mattered most, the answer was clear: Protect our pay. This agreement does that,” said Capt. Chris Sabia, who chairs the union’s PSA Master Executive Council, in a news release.

ALPA represents approximately 2,260 pilots at PSA, which is a wholly-owned regional subsidiary of American Airlines.

How Stephen Miller Schemed His Way to the Top

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

President Donald Trump on Friday announced that Stephen Miller, one of his most powerful and politically aggressive aides, would be his next White House chief of staff following disastrous results for Republicans in the midterm elections.

Miller, whose titles until now were deputy chief of staff for policy and homeland security adviser, has orchestrated the most polarizing policies of Trump’s second term—most notably a brutal crackdown on immigration that has claimed the lives of US citizens.

Trump made the change public in a statement on his Truth Social platform, writing that Miller would take over from Susie Wiles on Monday—less than a week after Republicans lost the House and Senate in a searing rebuke of Trump’s policy agenda.

In recent days, Trump has privately blamed Wiles for the worst Republican midterm performance in decades, according to two sources familiar with the matter. Wiles submitted her resignation on Thursday, the sources say.

Trump broadly considered three candidates to replace Wiles, the sources say: special envoy Steve Witkoff, director of national intelligence Jay Clayton, and Miller.

But with the looming threat of congressional investigations starting next year, Trump settled on Miller, seemingly because of his zeal in defending the president and his deep loyalty. Miller has been with Trump since his first campaign and stood by him through his political exile in 2021.

Miller had made his interest for the job known inside the West Wing for weeks, ever since Wiles weathered a series of negative stories before the midterms that suggested her job was at risk, the sources say.

In recent months, Trump also started cutting Wiles out of certain decisions, which contributed to the sense among her detractors that she was on shaky ground, sources say. For instance, Trump reportedly hired Katie Zacharia as press secretary without consulting Wiles, though the White House pushed back on the claim.

With Wiles’ departure, White House aides were bracing for further personnel changes. Among other names, Wiles’ deputies James Blair, who put together the GOP midterm strategy, and Richard Walters are seen as vulnerable to being fired by Miller unless they resign first, sources say.

The changing of the guard also is likely to bring respite to defense secretary Pete Hegseth, who has had a strained relationship with Wiles as of late but is seen as ideologically aligned with Miller, sources say.

Hegseth and Miller worked closely last year when the Trump administration began a campaign of strikes against boats around Venezuela. Miller, who also leads the Homeland Security Council within the White House, orchestrated the campaign through the Pentagon.

Wiles leaves the White House as arguably the most successful chief of staff to Trump, who infamously cycled through chiefs in his first term. By comparison, Wiles kept Trump’s confidence from the start of his second presidential campaign in 2021.

Still, Wiles weathered a number of challenges in recent months. She underwent a successful course of chemotherapy, but also faced accusations that her daughter had received lucrative contracts in part because of the family’s connection to the president. (The White House and Wiles’ daughter’s firm pushed back on these claims.)

“Serving President Trump in the White House has been the honor of a lifetime,” Wiles wrote on X. “I’m so proud to have been a part of what we’ve accomplished together from securing our border and cutting taxes to strengthening our economy and putting America First.”

Ray-Ban Meta Audio review: Great specs. Shame about the price, though

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Ray-Ban Meta Audio glasses attempt to curb the controversy by removing the cameras, and while they’re still an excellent product, the price makes them harder to recommend.

This has been a bit of a rollercoaster year for Meta wearables. Meta’s VR division shrank considerably earlier this year, then later announced a massively redesigned follow-up that’ll launch next year. And while Meta has debuted dozens of new smart glasses styles throughout the year that have sold quite well, the company can’t seem to get away from controversy. That’s where Ray-Ban Meta Audio glasses come in.

Ray-Ban Meta Audio glasses do exactly what they sound like. It’s a pair of smart glasses powered by Meta AI and only includes the audio part of the Ray-Ban Meta experience. There are exactly zero cameras on these glasses, and the hope (obviously) is that they’ll provide a smart experience without the potential privacy problems. The problem, for me at least, is that they’re simply too expensive despite lacking standout features.

Now, that doesn’t mean Ray-Ban Meta Audio glasses aren’t worthwhile. They feature a much wider prescription range (-12 to +6) than the majority of Meta smart glasses models, which often cap out at -6 to +6. The battery life is also significantly better thanks to the lack of a camera and other improvements in the hardware. And, of course, if you want a pair of AI glasses but don’t want the camera, these are the only options from Meta.

If I were to choose between the two available models, I’d pick the Clubmaster style every time. Not only do they look better overall and feature the thinnest frames of any Meta smart glasses, but they also have substantially better nosepads that are adjustable and can be easily replaced.

The Burbank model looks just fine, but the nosepads aren’t the same, and the glasses feel more rigid overall (which I find less comfortable). Both models come in lots of colors with many different lens options, including clear, tinted, polarized, Transitions, and prescription.

Both models feature a touchpad on the outside of the right temple arm, as well as a small, circular button up top to activate Meta AI. You can alternatively use the “Hey, Meta” keywords to activate Meta AI. The button and touchpad can be disabled if you like. Only the touchpad has an alternative function, and that alternative function only controls music playback via Spotify or Amazon Music. Other Meta glasses offer far more options.

Both styles are the lightest Meta glasses available. Most Meta smart glasses with cameras weigh in around the 54g mark. Ray-Ban Meta Audio glasses weigh just 44g. They’re not as light as Even Realities G2, RayNeo iO, or MemoMind One, but 44g is still considered pretty light for smart glasses.

Ray-Ban Meta Audio glasses reuse the case from Ray-Ban Meta Gen 2, which looks more like a traditional Ray-Ban case rather than the foldable one that ships with Meta Ray-Ban Display or the 2026 Meta Camera Glasses. I don’t have an issue with Ray-Ban Meta Audio’s case at all, but I really enjoy the foldable one and wish it were included.

Audio playback on these is great, and while the speakers are identical to all other Meta glasses products, using them as spatial surround sound “headphones” while watching a movie with surround sound is a fantastic experience. They also have spatial audio recording and truly excellent noise cancellation thanks to the 6-mic setup.

But the lack of the newer processor from Ray-Ban Meta Gen 3 means the glasses don’t support the new hearing aid function. To me, that’s a massive missed opportunity since these are audio-only glasses. When I asked Meta about it, they only said it wasn’t available on Ray-Ban Meta Audio for now, so this problem could be fixed in an upcoming update.

And that’s actually where Meta’s glasses might shine brightest: software updates. The company has been exemplary with update cadence on all of its products, and its smart glasses constantly get new, innovative features that surprise and delight. While I’m a little underwhelmed with the number of features available on these glasses today, this product will likely look quite different a year from now, a fact that’s backed up by years of Meta significantly improving product feature sets over time.

If you buy these glasses today, know that you’re mainly investing in them for the future. They’re fine enough for today’s usage, especially if you’re interested in a hands-free way to use Meta AI products like Muse or just want an open-ear way to listen to music and take phone calls, but Meta’s penchant for continually adding new features is, undoubtedly, going to be the highlight for everyone who ever buys them.

Critical Role Officially Returns to Its Roots for New Campaign with Hololive

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

All the details about Shattered Legends, the new Critical Role x Hololive Daggerheart series

Announced in October, Shattered Legends is an upcoming miniseries featuring Critical Role and members of Hololive Production. Critical Role founders Matt Mercer and Marisha Ray will team up with Hololive’s Nerissa Ravencroft, Mori Calliope, Shiori Novella, and Gigi Murin​​​​ for a new adventure set in the world of Exandria, where the Critical Role story began with Campaign 1: Vox Machina.

Shattered Legends will premiere on Nov. 13 at 6 p.m. PT / 9 p.m. ET on Nerissa Ravencroft's YouTube channel and on Critical Role's streaming platform, Beacon. The six-episode actual play show will use the rules for Daggerheart, the tabletop role-playing game designed by Critical Role's publishing studio, Darrington Press. Mercer will take the role of Game Master, while Ray will sit on the players' side of the table along with the Hololive crew, helping them navigate a treacherous new adventure in Exandria.

Shattered Legends is set in the Shattered Teeth archipelago, located southeast of Tal'dorei, the continent where the first Critical Role campaign, Vox Machina, takes place. The adventurers will explore the island of Ubradar, and more specifically a mysterious location known as the Undermaw, "a massive sinkhole with an unknown darkness sealed below," according to a news release. If this reminds you of the popular anime Made in Abyss, the similarities don't end there. In the Shattered Teeth islands, time flows differently than in the outside world, just like in Made in Abyss' eponymous giant hole in the ground.

The characters and classes for each of the players in Shattered Legends have also been revealed. Marisha Ray plays tidekin witch Florence, "a moon witch of the sea who believes she guides the souls of lost sailors to the afterlife of the Sister Divas." Nerissa Ravencroft plays elf rogue Siliul 'Sil' Faeli, "a noble turned rogue and skilled tracker seeking refuge on Ubradar." Mori Calliope plays infernis bard Kaz Oogod, "the last of the Oogod clan, a young musician with a 'world-changing instrument.'" Shiori Novella plays fungrin druid Marchen 'March' Rosemyne, "an orphan searching for the Huntsman she lives with, aided by her connection to nature and a network of mushrooms." Finally, Gigi Murin plays faerie seraph Giryn, "a devoted follower of Mother Liopé on a mission to spread the Enclave’s teachings and witness the nature of life and death."

The Hololive cast members will appear with new virtual avatars based on the spectacular design of their characters, as seen in the presentation video below. Mercer and Ray will appear in the flesh, even if many fans are complaining this was a missed opportunity to give them a virtual avatar.

Shattered Legends will not end with the six-episode miniseries. A promotional tour will hit conventions in North America, Europe, and Asia, starting with a panel at Anime Frontier in Fort Worth, Texas, on Dec. 5.

If you miss Tal'dorei and the setting of the adventures of Vox Machina, or if you're just curious to see how a group of cheery VTubers will react to Mercer's evil Game Master shenanigans, tune in to Shattered Legends when it premieres on Nov. 13.

Like millions of other people, I’ve been eagerly anticipating Dune: Part Three, which is going to hit the 2026 movie schedule on December 18. The book-to-screen adaptation will serve as the third and final installment of director Denis Villeneuve’s trilogy that started way back during COVID, if you can believe it. The movie stars Timothèe Chalamet, of course, as Paul Atreides, just like the first two movies, and recently, Chalamet and his castmates sat down for an interview. He was asked about the ending of the movie, and his answer has me even more excited.

As with any source material as beloved as the Dune series of books, fans are usually nervous about an adaptation sticking the landing, and that’s certainly true of Dune: Part Three. Fans of the series know how the book ends, but will the movie change it? Improve on it? Screw it up? Chalamet was asked by one of his co-stars, Jason Momoa, in an interview with the cast in The New York Times about the ending, and Chalamet’s answer is ambiguous, but definite:

Absolutely. It didn’t feel like the bar dropped at all, Denis just changed the location of the bar. This movie is its own thing.

Now, calling the movie “its own thing” might make some book fans nervous, but that comes as a response from something another co-star, Robert Pattinson. It brings a little more context to what Chalamet said. Pattinson said:

The script was really cool, but it was quite complicated. And watching the film, it’s not about what I thought it was going to be about at all. It reads as much more of a grand political, moral fable, and the movie is so much more emotional.

This all stemmed from a question from The Times asking if the movie departs from the source material, the second book in the series, Dune Messiah. As a fan of the books (although I admittedly haven’t read Messiah in quite a few years), I welcome changes. I don’t want to stray too far, of course, but books and movies are very different mediums, and changes need to be made. The important thing is that the message of the book is translated to the big screen, and it sounds like, at least according to Chalamet, that Villeneuve’s done that.

Pattison calling it a political fable is exactly what I want, as that was ultimately the purpose of the author, Frank Herbert. The Dune series isn’t just a sci-fi story set far into the future, it’s meant to be a morality tale for us, here on Earth today (even if Messiah is almost 60 years old now). Ultimately, any concerns I had were put to rest as I was reading the interview when yet another A-list co-star, Anya Taylor-Joy, reminded us who is in charge here:

Denis is literally the biggest Dune fan of all time, so for that to be the person who’s steering the ship, you feel pretty good about it.

Feel good, I do. I think this is going to be epic in every way. In the meantime, now is a good time to fire up your HBO Max subscription to watch the first two Dune movies.
