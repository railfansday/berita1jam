Arsenal have now lost their last five WSL games at the Emirates. The results were yet another frustrating afternoon for the defending champions, who put in a strong display against Barcelona in Europe earlier in the week.

Arsenal started on a high note, creating some dangerous chances with their quick play. Emily Fox and Lisa Baum worked really well together down the right, and Ona Batlle had a great chance from the edge of the box.

As the pressure mounted, Fox surged forward and played a perfectly weighted through ball to Alessia Russo. The striker turned and played the ball back to Maanum, who rounded off her impressive comeback from injury by finishing.

Rodriguez also had to make a key stop before half-time as she got a hand to Daniela Arques Lazaro’s powerful strike to keep the scores level.

The Gunners continued their attacking play but were made to work hard by a high pressing side from London City.

But Arsenal still managed to create some chances. Caitlin Foord put Earps under pressure and Maanum and Baum combined before the latter saw her curl just wide.

Delphine Cascarino had the game’s defining moment of the second half, hitting the crossbar from outside the box. However, Arsenal were lucky to escape and Maanum came close to winning the game, hitting the woodwork from a dangerous counter.

Slegers brought on Kyra Cooney-Cross and Olivia Smith in the 72nd minute to provide some new energy. Smith looked lively upon entering the game, but Earps denied her efforts and Batlle’s cross.

Emily Fox had to be substituted due to injury in the final moments of the game, but Slegers later indicated that she would be okay.

The Gunners were unable to find a winning goal and will now turn their attention to their Champions League clash on Thursday with Lyon. They will then face West Ham at home in the WSL.

Thomas Frank is facing a difficult start to life as the new Crystal Palace manager as he prepared to take charge in South London.

The 53-year-old has been chosen as the man to replace Pierre Sage at after an agreement was reached at Selhurst Park, with a formal announcement expected soon.

The former Brentford boss has long been admired by members of the Palace board, who quickly identified him as their first choice to step into the dugout.

However, former Man United chief scout Mick Brown told Football Insider that Frank may have been set up to fail at Crystal Palace amid concerns about his appointment.

The Eagles had initially planned to hire their next manager as quickly as possible during the international break to prepare for a crucial run of fixtures on the horizon.

However, there are just four days until they visit Coventry City, and the boss is not expected to lead his first training session until later this week, raising red flags.

Brown told Football Insider on 16 November that Frank's appointment was delayed as the Palace board were surprised by his financial demands after his exit from Tottenham.

Meanwhile, negotiations over the manager's backroom staff are ongoing, despite the agreement with Frank, as they aim to back him to bring in his trusted allies.

Those delays, however, could cause further problems for Frank and Crystal Palace as they prepare for a battle to move away from the relegation zone.

"There is one big problem with all of these delays, though," Brown told Football Insider.

"It looks like Frank is going to be the next Crystal Palace manager, I wouldn't worry about that, but I would be concerned about how long he will have to get his plans in place.

"They'd hoped to do it quickly, it should've been done as soon as they decided to sack Pierre Sage, he could've come in with plenty of time to get to work.

"Now, it's only a few days until they face Coventry, and he's going to have very little time to start getting his ideas across before the game.

"That's not an ideal situation for any manager going into a new club, you need time and space to do your work, so he's been set up to fail a bit in that respect.

"It hasn't gone as smoothly as Palace had hoped, but that's the way things happen sometimes, so it's up to them and up to Frank to do the best with what they've got.

"Palace don't want to get sucked into a relegation battle, although it may be too late for that, but Frank must be given the right support to get things going in the right direction."

Frank quickly emerged as the front-runner to land the Crystal Palace job after their decision to sack Sage, having also been in the running for the job in the summer.

A number of other names have been assessed by the Eagles, including both Sean Dyche and Vitor Pereira, as they were keen to consider all potential targets.

Football Insider revealed on 15 November that Frank's Premier League experience and remarkable work with Brentford have made him the top choice for Palace.

The boss is also viewed as a good motivator, which is something Palace are searching for to improve their morale having dropped to the bottom of the Premier League table.

Crystal Palace hope Frank's reign will reflect the work he did with Brentford rather than with Tottenham, where he struggled, and believe he is the right man for the job.

Galaxy Outflows Likely Regulate Star Formation At ‘Cosmic Noon’ NASA’s Webb Reveals

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

Sitting here on the outskirts of our Milky Way Galaxy, itâs hard to imagine the galactic machinations that led to our goldilocks spot around a very welcoming yellow dwarf star. But for decades, astronomers have suspected that the internal dynamics of massive galaxies like ours play an outsized role in regulating star formation.

As observations by NASAâs James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) now reveal, massive galaxies have largescale gaseous outflows that greatly quench their star production.

And as the authors of a new paper in the journal Astrophysics and Space Science describe, JWST has observed a link between outflows from a massive galaxyâs central core and the quenching of star formation at so-called âCosmic Noon.â That is, roughly 8-12 billion years ago, when the number of stars forming per unit volume in the cosmos reached its peak.

Outflows are large-scale flows of gas, from inside to outside the galaxy usually caused by either supernovae or energetic outbursts from supermassive black holes.

These JWST observations have revealed that outflows are more abundant and more powerful than we thought, suggesting that the evolution of galaxies is strongly impacted by outflows, Sirio Belli, an associate professor at the University of Bologna, Italy and the paperâs co-author, told me via email.

Normally, galaxies are rich in gas, and when the gas is sufficiently cold, it collapses under its own weight and forms a dense cloud from which new stars form. But such outflows likely remove a large quantity of gas from galaxies, which are then unable to form new stars.

Growing supermassive black holes at the centers of galaxies drive powerful winds, blowing away gas that would otherwise form stars, Rebecca Davies, the paperâs lead author and an astrophysicist at Australiaâs Swinburne University of Technology in Melbourne, told me via email. With JWST, we can measure the amount of cold star-forming gas being ejected, Davies told me.

But JWST has also revealed that outflows powered by accretion onto supermassive black holes are linked to the rapid shut-down or âquenchingâ of star formation.

Quenching can happen if the galaxy runs out of gas or the galaxy stops being able to turn the gas into stars because the gas is too hot or turbulent to collapse, says Davies.

One possibility is that the supermassive black hole at the center of the galaxy releases a large amount of energy which removes and/or heats the gas and prevents the formation of new stars, says Belli.

As such massive galaxies age, their AGN become relatively quiescent, not unlike Sag A* --- our own Milky Wayâs supermassive black hole in our galactic center.

The Milky Wayâs black hole had a major flare just a few million years ago, smaller outbursts only a century or two ago, and it still flickers almost every day as hot gas swirls just outside it, says Davies.

A key open question is what triggers outflows powerful enough to quench galaxies.

Outflows are very common in massive galaxies at cosmic noon, but most of these galaxies are not quenching: the outflows simply keep the galaxies in equilibrium and prevent them from converting all their gas into stars too quickly, says Davies. Possible triggers of the most powerful outflows include galaxy mergers, or radio jets launched by the central supermassive black holes, she says.

As for how this stellar quenching might impact the formation of molecular clouds that spawn solar type stars and habitable planetary systems?

When galaxies are quenched, the formation of new stars and planets is shut down, with strong repercussions on the probability of developing habitable systems, says Belli. If the Milky Way had been quenched at Cosmic Noon, it would likely not have formed many new stars in the last 10 billion years, and our solar system which is only 5 billion years old would not exist today, he says.

But star formation can become too much of a good thing.

When star formation is too strong there are other problems, such as the large number of stellar explosions that could negatively impact the habitability of planets, says Belli. The connection between galaxy-scale processes and the habitability of planetary systems is only now being investigated, he says.

Former Ukrainian adviser Anton Gerashchenko shared images and video footage appearing to show extensive damage to the road section of the Crimea bridge, with remnants of the support arch and road section seen protruding from the water below.

The bridge, which has come under repeated attack by Ukrainian forces, was built by Russia after it invaded and annexed the peninsula in 2014, and was personally opened by Russian President Vladimir Putin in 2018.

While Ukraine has been trying to increase the cost to Russia of waging war economically, by hitting oil refineries and depots, striking the Crimea bridge is an attempt to increase the political cost on Putin.

The timing of the Crimea bridge strike comes at a significant moment, ahead of Putin meeting the leaders of China and the US. This strike could cloud his moment back in the spotlight.

While the long-term impact of the strike on was not immediately clear, the bridge has been used to move troops and supplies into Crimea and towards the front line.

The railway section of the bridge appears to still be intact but it has been damaged previously.

Russia's Investigative Committee confirmed structural damage, and said it had opened a "criminal case... on terrorism charges" after attacks by "Ukraine's armed formations".

The Crimea bridge (pictured in 2023) has been repeatedly targeted by Ukraine - one of the two distinctive white arches appears to have collapsed overnight

Monday night's strikes came a day after Ukraine began its most recent attempt to disable the Crimea bridge, in an attack which a Russian official said killed two and reportedly caused less significant structural damage.

Crimea's Moscow-backed governor Sergei Aksyonov confirmed on Telegram that traffic over the bridge had been suspended.

Zelensky had warned of major attacks in the days leading up to a meeting between Putin, Donald Trump and Xi Jinping in China's Shenzhen on Thursday.

The Northern Bridge (pictured in October) is closed after being targeted again by Russia

Some of Kyiv's above-ground subway services were damaged in the overnight attacks, officials said, after civilians were told to go to civil defence shelters.

Residents of Kyiv typically shelter in the city's underground stations during air raids.

Eight major bridges span the Dnipro River connecting the western and eastern parts of Kyiv, and an estimated three million residents rely on the bridges to travel across the city.

These latest Russian strikes will cause major disruption, with traffic already reportedly building in the capital.

Elsewhere, three people have been injured in a Russian missile attack in the Cherkast region, according to local officials.

Judge Acquits MIT Brothers in $25M Ethereum MEV Case, Ending Prosecution

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Judge Acquits MIT Brothers in $25M Ethereum MEV Case, Ending Prosecution

A federal judge in Manhattan has acquitted Anton and James Peraire-Bueno, the MIT-educated brothers accused of exploiting Ethereum's transaction ordering system to take $25 million from automated trading bots.

U.S. District Judge Jessica Clarke granted the brothers' motion for a judgment of acquittal on Monday, clearing them of wire fraud, conspiracy to commit wire fraud and conspiracy to commit money laundering, according to her ruling. The decision closes the criminal case without a second trial.

While the brothers' scheme "may have frustrated the spirit" of MEV-Boost, software that lets Ethereum validators outsource the ordering of transactions to specialized block builders, that was not enough to make out fraud, Clarke said. In a footnote, she added that the prosecution "raises serious due process concerns," though she declined to rule on that question.

Because the money laundering charge relied on the proceeds of the alleged wire fraud, it fell with the fraud counts.

The case stemmed from an April 2023 exploit targeting so-called sandwich bots, automated traders that profit from maximal extractable value (MEV) by placing their own trades on either side of other users' transactions.

According to the ruling, the brothers placed bait transactions designed to lure specific bots, then exploited a bug in a relay's code to see the bots' bundled trades before committing to a block. They then swapped the bait transactions for trades that sold the tokens instead, leaving the bots with about $25 million in losses in roughly 12 seconds. Authorities arrested the pair in May 2024, saying they spent months planning the attack and took steps to hide the proceeds.

At trial in Manhattan last year, an insider testified that the brothers had allegedly planned the attack, and the defense had earlier sought to block evidence of their Google searches. Clarke declared a mistrial in November 2025, after the jury deadlocked.

The case was closely watched in the crypto industry. Clarke's ruling described it as a "first of its kind" prosecution, a label the government itself used in its indictment.
