Here Are The Best Saves To Start In FM 27

If you're on the hunt for an exciting new challenge in FM 27, here are the teams we think you should manage (and why!).

If you're on the hunt for a new Football Manager 27 save to either push the limits of your ability or just be a fun romp to trophy after trophy, then we have a list of teams to consider.

These saves mix challenging relegation battlers with high-expectation teams, each with its own challenges. This is different from challenge saves, but each team comes with a few unique goals you'll need to achieve in order to be considered a success at the club.

Coventry City fell out of the Premier League in the 00/01 season and spent the next 25 years slowly declining to League 2, before an incredible run under manager Mark Robins brought them to within one penalty kick of returning to the Premier League. Robins would ultimately lose his job and be replaced by Chelsea and England legend Frank Lampard, who, in his first full season with the team, would win the Championship and return the Sky Blues to the promised land.

Your job is clear in FM 27. Save Coventry City from relegation, then attempt to build on that achievement to take them to European glory. Coventry City spent a lot of money on players in the off-season, so you don't have much of a budget to work with, and the squad is a huge mix of players. Young, raw talent like Caleb Yirenkyi and Sidiki Cherif, and experienced PL heads like Ethan Pinnock and Taiwo Awoniyi, are supported by unproven top-division talent like Bobby Thomas, Jack Rudoni, Milan van Ewijk, Carl Rushworth, and Tatsuhiro Sakamoto.

It's your job to find your best 11 as soon as possible and get into a good rhythm to scrape survival. However, the season kicks off with a brutal schedule, playing Arsenal, Man City, and Brighton in their first five games.

It's been a long, hard road back to success for Sports Interactive, but FM 27 is the game I hoped it would be and so much more.

We're heading over to the German leagues next, with Wolfsburg under heavy duress after relegation and under huge financial pressure. Following relegation from the Bundesliga for the first time in its history, Wolfsburg now has to fight back in a competitive 2. Bundesliga, or risk its financial problems swallowing the club into further dismay.

You're going to rely on a small squad and look to bring in cheap but talented players, or high-potential youth, to navigate the long season. Expectations are high despite the issues surrounding the club, so see if you're up to the task by taking over this former Bundesliga champion.

One of the most unique stories in football history, Portuguese second-division side Torreense actually won the Taça de Portugal last season, earning a spot in the Europa League while not being a top-division side. This means you'll need to balance European participation with trying to go as far as you can, while also aiming for promotion to Portugal's top division.

European participation is a hugely lucrative bonus, and it could fund the club for a few years if you manage to earn some wins. However, expectations are high for the league, so the last thing you want to do is put promotion at risk for the chance to participate in the Europa League or Conference League.

This is a hugely fun save to tackle, and it's interesting to see the player interest be quite a bit better for a second division club than you might expect, all thanks to the pull of European football.

Following a historically bad Championship campaign last year, and financial issues that would have killed most clubs in the same situations, Sheffield Wednesday find themselves in League One, and with a squad that isn't necessarily going to walk the league.

That's where you come in: this club is simply too big to keep slumming it in the third tier, so you need to return them to the Championship, and then ultimately to the Premier League, as quickly as possible.

You'd be relying almost entirely on club reputation to attract players, because the purse strings are tight, and the last thing you need is further points deductions ot undo any hard work.

Dutch Eredivisie side AZ Alkmaar are a bit of a wonderkid factory in FM 27, with at least one hugely talented player in five positions (Bendeguz Kovacs, Quenten Attigah, Andrea Natali, Kees Smit, Elijah Dijkstra). It's your job to raise them to their full potential and start a new era in Dutch football, taking over from the likes of Ajax, Feyenoord, and PSV Eindhoven.

Or, of course, you can maximize profits by selling these wonderkids to fund more established players and make a title push instead. The route you take is up to you, but one thing is certain: AZ are a very fun club to manage in FM 27.

A club with a growing reputation thanks to Manager/Owner Marc White, Dorking recently reached heights in the National League, but suffered relegation and found themselves back in the National League South, with a strong squad.

It's expected that you'll gain promotion back to the National League, but the expectations don't stop there: if you are successful, you'll be asked to reach the playoffs in your first season back.

The new Tactical Effectiveness feature makes managing teams in lower leagues a little bit harder, as ability is a factor in how players now adapt to tactics and roles, but if you keep things simple and make the right signings, you will find that this save can be a lot of fun for the first few seasons. Making the jump to the football leagues, however, is a different ball game, and that's where the real challenge kicks in.

Rainforest ferns from an ancient lineage have a surprisingly young family tree

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

An ancient lineage of rainforest ferns reveals how rising mountains and retreating wetlands may have shaped plant diversity across tropical America.

Some rainforest ferns belong to a lineage that stretches back to the age of dinosaurs. Yet roughly half its living species arose within the past six million years, as mountains rose and landscapes changed.

Researchers at the University of Turku and Aarhus University have reconstructed the history of tropical American Danaea ferns.

The findings draw on decades of work identifying ferns and establishing where they grow. That groundwork made it possible to investigate how the group spread and diversified.

“We have conducted extensive research at the University of Turku in order to understand the biodiversity of the Amazon rainforest,” said senior author Hanna Tuomisto, a professor of ecology.

“In the last 25 years, we have discovered and described 47 new Danaea species previously unknown to science, and have clarified the distribution and phylogenetic relationships of the species in the Danaea genus.”

“We have now been able to link this information to the geological and climatic history of tropical America.”

The team combined DNA evidence from 78 species with 2,289 verified records of where the ferns grow. These records helped reconstruct where ancestral ferns probably lived and the elevations they occupied.

The genetic analysis included 11 species that had not yet been formally described.

The researchers’ preferred estimate suggests that the Danaea lineage split from the lineage of its closest living relatives about 128 million years ago, during the Early Cretaceous.

The common ancestor of its surviving species lived much later, around 53 million years ago. The researchers traced that ancestor to Amazonia.

But an ancient lineage need not consist of ancient species. About half of today’s Danaea species originated during the past six million years.

This complicates a familiar explanation for tropical richness: favorable conditions allowed species to accumulate over immense stretches of time.

Persistence mattered, but the landscape also kept changing, creating opportunities for new species.

Fern diversity generally reaches its greatest heights in tropical mountain forests. Danaea follows a different pattern: Amazonia has consistently held its largest pool of species in the reconstruction.

“Mountain rainforests are home in particular to an abundance of epiphytes – plants that grow on top of other plants. Danaea ferns, on the other hand, grow on the forest floor,” said lead author Venni Keskiniva, a postdoctoral researcher at Turku.

“These research results are, in fact, similar to those obtained for trees, which are also the most species-rich in lowland rainforests.”

Where a plant lives within a forest may therefore help explain where its group becomes most diverse. Findings from one fern group cannot automatically describe them all.

The Andes still played an important role. Although Amazonia contained more species, the mountains became the leading source of dispersal to other regions.

Mountain formation affected far more than the slopes themselves. As the Andes rose, they changed rainfall patterns along with the soils developing across neighboring lowlands.

Vast wetlands once occupied much of western Amazonia. Their drainage near the end of the Miocene opened additional land for forest plants to colonize.

The resulting western Amazonian soils were generally richer in nutrients and more variable, than soils in central Amazonia.

Those differences may have encouraged plants to specialize in different environments.

The timing fits the fern history. Most new Amazonian species arising within the past six million years appeared in western Amazonia during the last three million.

New land in southern Central America also offered room for expansion. Arriving lineages frequently gave rise to additional species as the region’s landscapes developed.

The first mountain-adapted Danaea lineages appeared around 20 million years ago, following an intensification of Andean uplift. Several later spread into regions farther from the equator.

Their earlier move uphill may have helped them cope with the conditions they encountered there.

“As the plants had already adapted to the cooler climate of the mountains, it may have been easier for them to spread to areas where the temperature differences between the seasons are greater,” Keskiniva said.

Lineages that remained adapted to Amazonian lowlands tended to stay within that region.

The repeated sequence of mountain colonization followed by expansion suggests that climate tolerance influenced which routes were available.

The mountains could both separate populations and help others reach new habitats. Their role changed as the landscape rose and different fern lineages established themselves.

Assigning dates to this history was difficult. Many relevant fossils are fragmentary, and researchers disagree about where they belong in the family tree.

The team compared three dating approaches, which produced substantially different ages. It favored the reconstruction most consistent with other biological and geological evidence.

The dates remain estimates, and coinciding events do not establish a cause by themselves. Still, the findings connect the survival of ancient lineages with much younger episodes of diversification.

“The biodiversity in rainforests is the result of a long history, but its formation has also been greatly influenced by events that are relatively recent on a geological time scale,” Keskiniva concluded.

The study is published in the journal Frontiers of Biogeography.

Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge claims he has proved the Riemann hypothesis, one of mathematics’s most notoriously difficult problems, with the help of Claude, cracking a puzzle that has eluded some of the world’s best mathematicians for decades. If verified, it would mark yet another seismic achievement for AI in mathematics and add to the upheaval already unleashed by OpenAI’s previous, often inelegant forays into the field.

Alpöge’s solution has yet to be verified independently. Anthropic has not commented or involved itself in Alpöge’s announcement. In a document on his website, Alpöge says he texted Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei about the solution “late Sunday night” and suggests he held off publication until after the company’s IPO on Thursday.

The Riemann hypothesis is one of seven Millennium Prize problems, which are widely considered some of the most formidable open challenges in mathematics. First proposed in 1859, the Riemann hypothesis concerns the distribution of prime numbers and how zeroes of a function are involved. It’s regarded as one of the most important unsolved problems in mathematics.

A statement on Alpöge’s website says Claude was integral to developing the proof:

I believe I have, working with Claude, discovered a proof1 of the Riemann Hypothesis. In fact, the method easily extends much further, resolving the so-called Generalized Riemann Hypothesis, and I expect, with more time, the Extended Riemann Hypothesis too. Claude and I have also formalized the arguments without assumptions. It is natural to feel that this is an end, in my case a loss of a principal guiding light, but what I saw during the course of progress so amazed me that I believe it is one dawn across science of something far greater.

The Verge reached out to Anthropic but didn’t immediately hear back. Anthropic said it was working on the Riemann Hypothesis with Claude in August, but noted at the time: “We don’t expect that the techniques Claude used will lead to proving the Riemann hypothesis.”

Alpöge’s solution, presented in Lean, has only been published on his website and has not yet been publicly accepted by the Clay Mathematics Institute, which administers the Millennium Prize and offers a $1 million bounty for each problem solved. Under the institute’s rules, a proposed solution must be published and withstand at least two years of scrutiny before it can be considered for the prize, during which time it must have “received general acceptance in the global mathematics community.”

In the quarter-century since the prize was established, only one problem has been officially solved: The Poincaré conjecture, a topological problem concerning three-dimensional spheres. In September, OpenAI claimed to have cracked another, the Navier-Stokes problem, which concerns the flow of fluids.

OpenAI said it pursued Navier-Stokes after hearing rumors that rival researchers were closing in on a solution. Alpöge had been working on the problem with New York University mathematics professor Tristan Buckmaster. Buckmaster claimed OpenAI produced a solution for Navier-Stokes using the route he and Alpöge were working on with OpenAI’s Codex and Claude.

OpenAI spent millions of dollars and deployed 10,000 agents in the attempt. The result is widely considered credible, though it has yet to receive formal recognition. But the achievement has been marred by allegations of questionable research practices, including claims it used unpublished work from mathematicians who had shared their research with its AI tools. OpenAI has strongly denied wrongdoing, but this has done little to quell the uproar.

Though most of the spotlight has been on OpenAI’s movement in math, Alpöge said in July that he solved the Jacobean conjecture using Claude. The deluge of findings has left mathematicians scrambling to digest the material and figure out where they belong in what is rapidly becoming a profoundly different mathematical landscape.

Michelle Obama, Hillary Clinton, and Jill Biden Come Together at Gloria Steinem’s Joyous Celebration of Life

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Lifestyle  |  Read Source →

The former first ladies coordinated in elegant, high-fashion ensembles to honor the feminist activist

Gloria Steinem’s life, work, and legacy were celebrated this week, as many gathered at a memorial service for the feminist activist in New York City.

The celebration of life took place at the Park Avenue Armory and came two months after Steinem’s passing at age 92.

Among those who came together to pay their respects were former United States first ladies Michelle Obama, Hillary Clinton, and Jill Biden—as well as Chelsea Clinton, Diane von Furstenberg, Meg Ryan, Lucy Liu, Emma Watson, Marisa Tomei, and Mariska Hargitay. In total, there were about 1,000 invited guests, all who were encouraged to donate to Gloria’s Foundation, which aims to preserve Steinem’s Upper East Side brownstone on East 73rd Street as a permanent archive and center for feminist organizing.

Rather than a somber service, the memorial was a joyful, music-filled celebration titled “It’s Steinemite!” Cynthia Erivo sang John Lennon’s “Imagine,” Cyndi Lauper performed “Girls Just Want to Have Fun,” and Kathy Sledge of Sister Sledge closed the event with “We Are Family,” joined by Lauper and Gloria’s Choir.

For the evening, attendees coordinated in all-black ensembles—but it certainly wasn’t your average funeral attire. Clinton wore an elegant pantsuit—one of her favored looks. Obama was so chic and cool in a cropped blazer and a pleated skirt with a silky sash and golden accents. And Biden went for a fun, high-fashion ensemble that included Schiaparelli’s black Corsini suit jacket with asymmetric gold buttons.

If she could’ve looked into that crowd of incredible women, Steinem would’ve been endlessly proud.

The Apollo Dash is an excellent midrange electric scooter with power to get you up hills and enough range to get you around town. It also has great lights and turn signals. However, its lack of a suspension made it less comfortable to ride than the less-powerful Segway E3 Go.

If you live in a hilly area, you need an electric scooter that has enough muscle to get you to the top. However, if you’re a commuter, or just someone on a budget, you also need a scooter that’s relatively light and affordable.

The Apollo Dash hits those notes well. While its 600W motor won’t be competing in any Mr. Universe contests, it’s muscular enough to power you up all but the steepest inclines at a reasonable pace. It’s also very customizable via Apollo’s app, and even has Apple FindMy, so you can keep tabs on its whereabouts. Even better, you can often find it for around $500, making it very reasonably priced for what it offers.

But how does it compare to the best electric scooters? I tested the Apollo Dash for a couple of weeks to find out.

The Apollo Dash, which went on sale in 2026, is normally priced at $699, but for Black Friday and other sales, I've seen it as low as $529 at Apollo's site. If you can get it for that price, it's a pretty good bargain.

Thanks to its 600W motor, the Apollo Dash is pretty good at getting up hills. While it’s not a speed demon like the Apollo City, it did manage to zip me up a 10% grade at around 8 MPH, which is much better than the similarly-priced Segway E3 Go. On a 6% grade hill, the Dash dashed me up at around 12-14 MPH.

Apollo says that the Dash is good for up to 25 miles, using its lowest speed setting; that’s not for me. You can set the Dash to “Ludo” mode, which boosts its top speed to 22 MPH, but at that setting, the max range drops to a still-respectable 17 miles.

Of course, range depends on a lot of factors; after a 5.5-mile jaunt, I had 55% battery remaining, so realistically, I would be able to get a little over 10 miles before the Dash would need a recharge.

Apollo’s app gives you a ton of control over how you want the Dash to perform. For starters, you can set the speed for each speed mode, so if you want to cap Sport mode at 15 MPH, you can do so.

But, you can also adjust things such as the throttle response — how fast you want the scooter to start moving — as well as the amount of regenerative braking. You can also specify how long the turn signals remain on, and how long it takes to activate cruise control.

It’s a lot of granularity that in truth, I’ll rarely touch, but it’s nice to know that it’s there.

One of the things I worry about most when riding a scooter is that cars won’t be able to see me, especially around dusk. So I really like the very large lightbar that extends the length of the Apollo Dash’s downtube; it’s bright enough to really stand out.

If that weren’t enough, the Dash also has a dedicated headlight that’s also quite powerful, and you can adjust it, so that it hits the road where it’ll be the most effective for you.

While the Dash has 9.5-inch air-filled tires, its lack of suspension was definitely noticeable compared with scooters which had some sort of shock absorption. When I was riding over bumps and potholes, it was a much more jarring experience, especially when I was moving at full speed.

I was testing the Apollo Dash the same time as I was testing the Segway E3 Go, and there were a few things that seemed small, but that I appreciated on the Segway. For instance, the Segway’s handlebars are angled back slightly (Apollo’s are straight) and are about three inches longer, which gave me more control when going over bumps.

I also liked that the Segway’s turn signal switch is on the left handlebar. On the Apollo, the left turn signal is on the left, and the right is on the right, so you can’t activate the right turn signal if your thumb is on the throttle.

I’ve rode plenty of low-cost scooters that have struggled to make it to the top of hills in my town; the Apollo Dash is not one of them. It proved more than capable on my tests, and while it’s not going to win any long-distance contests, its range is more than suitable for a few runs to the train station or office and back.

I do wish the ride were more comfortable — the Segway E3 Go has it beat there — but as long as your commute doesn’t involve pothole-strewn streets, you should be ok.

If you want something with more zip, the Apollo Go has dual 750W motors, a higher top speed, and twice the range as the Dash; it’s also a few hundred dollars more. If you need a great mid-range scooter that punches a bit above its weight, then check out the Dash.

Privacy concerns around AI agents have prompted encrypted messaging app Signal to set new limits on screenshots. The company will prevent screenshotting in its iOS 27 app and “when AI assistants attempt to capture screen data on Android,” Signal President Meredith Whittaker wrote in a blog post Friday.

Whittaker noted that Google and Apple’s agentic features, including Gemini Intelligence and Siri AI’s Onscreen Awareness, compromise the privacy missions of third-party developers. These features observe what’s on a user’s screen as context for completing requests.

“Your sensitive data is being captured, stored, and made accessible to whatever local database or remote server an OS-level AI agent is using at that moment,” Whittaker said. “All of this is outside of Signal’s control once the data has been captured, and imperils the privacy of all the participants in a conversation.”

Signal also blocked screenshots last year in response to Microsoft’s controversial Recall AI feature, which could capture screenshots of Signal chats on devices running Windows 11.

Signal’s move is another reminder of how Big Tech’s AI strategy trickles downstream. As AI features have moved from services external to the OS (like chatbots from OpenAI or Anthropic) to further inside of it, third-party apps have to adapt to conditions that undermine their hard-won privacy infrastructure.

Just last week, researchers at Google DeepMind used AI agents to formally verify Signal’s code, building on Signal’s own work to essentially prove the app’s encryption works. That’s a high bar for a security guarantee within the app, but it becomes meaningless if an agent can simply grab messages off a screen.

Samsung is reportedly working on its own agent, Galaxy Claw, and Apple is developing a rival for Claude and ChatGPT as well.

Whittaker noted Signal’s screenshot restriction isn’t perfect, saying that AI agents can still access app data through other means, “such as retrieving content already present on a user’s device via the filesystem.” Earlier this week, a Reuters investigation supported by Signal and the AI Now Institute found that Meta’s Muse AI agent was using permissions from its initial setup in unexpected ways to access apps and data that a user had not explicitly connected it to.

Signal said its protections don’t currently work on MacOS.

“It is a serious problem that Signal – and application developers beyond – lack robust tools to prevent AI agents from undermining application-level privacy,” she added. “Even where we are able to detect and block AI data access, Google, Apple, Microsoft, and others do not provide ways for developers to easily restrict such AI data access without making drastic compromises to the core user experience.”

If you’d still like to take screenshots, you can simply disable the setting, though Signal’s announcement strongly advised against it.

“Doing so will potentially expose your (and your contacts’) Signal content to these AI agents, and break our privacy guarantees,” Whittaker said.
