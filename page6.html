Study Finds Artemis Astronauts Will Enjoy Moon-Grown Potatoes

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

By now, references to Mark Watney and how he grew potatoes in Martian soil have become a tired clichÃ©. Nevertheless, one can't help but recall the scene whenever news breaks about the latest experiment that examines the potential of growing plants in extraterrestrial soils. The latest comes from Oregon State University (OSU), where researchers have shown that potatoes grown in lunar soil simulant (amended with organic matter) are safe to eat and grow just as well as those produced on Earth.

This is good news for space agencies like NASA that are actively planning to send astronauts back to the Moon, and this time, to stay. Through the Artemis Program, crews will land around the South Pole-Aitken Basin and begin constructing a permanent base that will enable regular missions in the future (about one per year). China's National Space Agency (CNSA) and Roscosmos also plan on building a base - the International Lunar Research Station (ILRS) - in the same region by the end of the decade.

To support long-term missions far from Earth, where opportunities for resupply will be fewer and farther between, a degree of self-sufficiency is required. Whereas the International Space Station (ISS) is resupplied regularly and emergency supplies can be dispatched within hours, missions to the Moon will take at least three days to arrive. As such, any sustained program of exploration on the Moon must rely on In-Situ Resource Utilization(ISRU) as much as possible - i.e., using local resources for building materials and providing astronauts with water and food.

Research in the past few decades has led to methods for 3D printing habitats and other facilities using lunar regolith. This can be combined with a bonding agent to create "lunacrete," or sintered with microwaves to create molten ceramic. Either material is then deposited layer by layer by construction robots to form walls and other structural elements. While hydroponics remain a viable way to grow plants (as astronauts do aboard the ISS), the real question is whether Earth plants can be grown in lunar regolith and other extraterrestrial soils.

Led by Aymeric Goyer, a professor with OSU's Department of Botany and Plant Pathology, the Oregon team used lunar regolith simulants with different levels of vermicompost, a nutrient-rich organic soil produced when earthworms break down organic matter. With support from NASA's Johnson Space Center Simulant Development Lab, the team produced healthy potatoes with yields comparable to crops grown by farmers in Oregon (the 4th largest potato producer in the U.S.).

Goyer and his teams specifically selected potatoes because they produce more calories per square meter of soil than many other crops. They also offer substantial nutritional value: one medium skin-on potato (148 g/5.3 oz) provides roughly 110 calories, 26 g of carbohydrates, 3 g of protein, and 2 g of fiber. They are also naturally fat-free, gluten-free, cholesterol-free, and are therefore considered a practical staple food for long-duration space missions.

Since lunar regolith varies across the Moon, the researchers tested five simulants representing lunar mare (fine-grained basaltic soil) and lunar highland (feldspar-rich volcanic soil) regions. They also included soil from Hermiston, Oregon, which produces the world's highest potato yield. The results showed that potato plants grown in simulants containing 30% vermicompost grew as well as those cultivated in Earth soils. In other samples, plants grown without vermicompost were severely stunted and had underdeveloped root systems.

The "Moon Village" concept, conceived by MIT in collaboration with the ESA and Skidmore, Owings & Merrill. Credit: ESA/Skidmore, Owings & Merrill

Lastly, those grown with 5% to 15% vermicompost showed only partial growth. As Goyer said in an OSU press release:

We found that potatoes can complete their entire life cycle in simulated lunar soil when supplemented with organic matter. That's an important step toward developing reliable food production systems that could support long-term human exploration of the Moon.

The results also showed that the potatoes grown in lunar regolith simulants maintained their nutritional quality, with no statistically significant increase in naturally occurring potato toxins (glycoalkaloids) often associated with green discoloration. Lunar regolith still presents many challenges for plant growth, which include extremely fine-grained dust and elevated levels of heavy metals.

But the researchers are confident that adding organic matter helps bind heavy metals, preventing them from damaging roots, interfering with growth, or leaching into the potatoes themselves. In the future, astronauts could bring compost to the Moon, along with Earth worms, to create their own fertilizer, augmenting soil collected during surface trips to grow their food.

"We believe the compost helps balance some of the toxicity we see in lunar regolith by reducing plant exposure to harmful compounds," said David Handy, a postdoctoral researcher in Goyer's lab. Their results are reported in a paper, "Growth and molecular responses of potato to lunar regolith simulants," that appeared in npj Microgravity. Another paper, which reports the results of experiments involving 10 potato varieties to identify which grow best in lunar soil, will be released.

The 10 Best Fantasy Books in Literary History, Ranked

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Fantasy stories let audiences escape reality and journey to far-off lands and brand-new worlds. We’ve seen and felt these tangible worlds on screen, but many of the very best fantasy narratives began on the page. Though countless incredible fantasy books exist in literary history, these ten are simply the best.

For this list, we will include singular novels and larger book series because, well, together they complete a monumental tale. From Middle-earth to Westeros, Hogwarts to Narnia, these fantasy stories have played a critical part in our lives and pop culture at large. What are you favorite fantasy books?

For her debut novel, Susanna Clarke made a miraculous impression. Jonathan Strange & Mr. Norrell, set in an alternate 19th-century England during the Napoleonic Wars, follows the reclusive scholar Mr. Norrell and his daring pupil Jonathan Strange as they revive practical magic. As their rivalry and different approaches to magic grow, their dealings with dangerous faeries threaten to overwhelm the nation's war efforts. Jonathan Strange & Mr. Norrell is praised as a great in the genre for its blend of 19th-century literary style with dark, grounded magic. Winner of the 2005 Hugo Award for Best Novel and longlisted for the 2004 Booker Prize, Jonathan Strange & Mr. Norrell is a readable wonder.

In a rich pastiche of Jane Austen and Charles Dickens, the book reads like a lost Regency classic rather than standard modern fantasy. By playing in that realm, Clarke builds a deep fictional academic history of English magic and the Raven King, making the world feel lived-in and authentic. Her characters are brilliant and vibrant. The cautious, book-hoarding Mr. Norrell and the daring, charismatic Jonathan Strange create a compelling mentor-student rivalry through their contrasting magical views. Magic here is wild, dangerous, and tied to ancient faerie pacts rather than neat spell systems. A perfectly adaptable story, Jonathan Strange & Mr. Norrell became a BBC miniseries in 2015 starring Bertie Carvel as Jonathan Strange and Eddie Marsan as Gilbert Norrell.

One of the most beloved book series of all time is His Dark Materials by Philip Pullman. Comprising The Golden Compass, The Subtle Knife, and The Amber Spyglass, His Dark Materials follows Lyra Belacqua and Will Parry as they travel across parallel universes filled with witches, armored polar bears, and a mysterious particle called Dust. Blending coming-of-age adventure with ideas from physics, philosophy, and theology, the story challenges tyrannical religious authority while reimagining themes from John Milton's Paradise Lost. His Dark Material blends fast-paced adventure with deep literary, philosophical, and scientific ideas.

Though it’s geared for a younger audience, His Dark Materials presents adult themes without ever talking down. Pullman weaves ideas from theology, philosophy, and physics into a story led by children. Rather than treating growing up or questioning authority as a fall from grace, the trilogy treats consciousness, free will, and human connection as vital virtues. It’s a bold moral perspective. Pullman’s greatest achievement is his memorable works building. Every human in Lyra’s world has a "dæmon"—an external animal companion that reflects their inner self, shifting shapes in childhood and settling into a permanent form at adulthood. Alongside armored polar bears, witches, and parallel worlds, this idea gives emotional weight to the story's central conflicts. The novels have won several awards, including the Carnegie Medal in 1995 for Northern Lights and the 2001 Whitbread Book of the Year for The Amber Spyglass. Beyond the page, the stories have been transformed for radio plays, theater productions, and a prominent film, The Golden Compass, starring Daniel Craig and Nicole Kidman, as well as an epic three-part series featuring Dafne Keen, Ruth Wilson, James McAvoy, and Lin-Manuel Miranda.

He may be the King of Horror, but Stephen King sure knows how to write masterpieces in other genres! Such is the case for The Dark Tower. In his genre-blending epic, King tells the story of Roland Deschain, the last surviving gunslinger in a decaying, post-apocalyptic landscape known as Mid-World. Alongside a band of companions drawn from alternate realities, Roland journeys across multiple dimensions to reach and protect the titular Dark Tower, the central nexus holding all of existence together, before evil forces can destroy it and collapse the multiverse. With many fans pointing to the novels serving as the author’s magnum opus, The Dark Tower’s ambitious world-building, evolving characters, and bold meta-storytelling set it apart from all the rest.

Comprising nine books released over a span of 30 years, The Dark Tower mixes dark fantasy, Westerns, science fiction, horror, and Arthurian myth rather than sticking to traditional sword-and-sorcery tropes. Each installment takes on a distinct structure and mood—from stark wasteland survival to flashbacks and meta-fiction—which keeps the long arc unpredictable. The Dark Tower serves as the linchpin connecting many of King's other stories across shifting worlds and realities. The novel series has a passionate fandom that was none too pleased with the execution of the 2017 film starring Idris Elba and Matthew McConaughey. Fingers crossed that Mike Flanagan does it justice as a TV series!

An exemplary high-fantasy series, The Wheel of Time spans 14 books and has two credited authors: Robert Jordan and Brandon Sanderson. The epic fantasy series in a cyclical world where a prophesied hero, the Dragon Reborn, must unite nations and master dangerous magic to fight the Dark One in the Last Battle. Begun by Jordan and finished posthumously by Sanderson using Jordan's notes, the story follows young companions swept into ancient prophecies, political upheaval, and a race against madness and darkness. Praised for its massive scale, intricate world building, unique magic system, and long-term character arcs, The Wheel of Time delivers an expansive fantasy journey that has captured the imaginations of its devoted readers.

With distinct magic and themes, “channeling” is divided by gender, with male channelers facing madness from a taint on their half of the power. The story blends classic good-versus-evil stakes with cyclical time and reincarnation. Rather than infallible heroes, characters make costly mistakes, face moral ambiguity, and grow. Spanning over 4.4 million words, the series maps out diverse nations, political factions, histories, and prophecies in an intricate, distinct manner. The depth with which the novels are written proves the true dedication to storytelling. The Wheel of Time has found itself adapted into various forms of media, including the brilliant three-season wonder starring Rosamund Pike. The Wheel of Time reshaped modern epic fantasy forever, bridging fantasy past and present. ​​​​​​​

Perhaps the underdog among these titans of literature, modern fantasy wouldn’t be what it is today without A Wizard of Earthsea by Ursula K. Le Guin. The 1968 novel set in the fictional archipelago of Earthsea, a proud young wizard named Ged accidentally unleashes a dangerous shadow creature after trying to prove his power. To restore balance and save himself, he must journey across the world to confront what he has released and learn true self-mastery. A Wizard of Earthsea is praised for its lyrical prose, thoughtful coming-of-age arc, and a magic system rooted in language and balance. Part of the larger The Earthsea Cycle, the novel recalibrated the genre by shifting the genre away from epic battles and good-versus-evil clichés toward inner growth, ecological and cosmic balance, and a non-white protagonist.

Rather than focus on bloated lore, it centers on a gifted, prideful young mage who releases a shadow born of his own arrogance and must learn humility, balance, and self-mastery. Ged’s main adversary is internal, making his growth feel three-dimensional and earned. Magic is rooted in language and balance, working through true names and the language of dragons, where every spell affects the world's equilibrium and carries real consequences. Le Guin writes with a sparse, fairy-tale clarity that reads like an ancient legend. Coming long before many of the greats on this list, A Wizard of Earthsea helped establish the magic-school trope while offering a thoughtful, post-Tolkien alternative focused on wisdom over conquest. The book has been adapted multiple times in various media, namely a Sci-Fi series starring Shawn Ashmore, Kristen Kreuk, and Danny Glover, as well as a Studio Ghibli animated epic called Tales from Earthsea. ​​​​​​​

Pop culture forever changed when Harry Potter entered the literary lexicon. In the seven-book series, orphaned young wizard Harry Potter discovers his magical heritage and attends Hogwarts School of Witchcraft and Wizardry, where he befriends Ron Weasley and Hermione Granger. Across seven novels, they battle the dark wizard Lord Voldemort to stop him from controlling the wizarding world. Hailed by many as the greatest fantasy series of all time, regardless of controversy, Harry Potter blends a cozy boarding-school fantasy where magic dominates the world with mystery plots and a coming-of-age arc that matures alongside its characters from ages 11 to 17. The novels brilliantly invite readers into an immersive world that weaves clues and twists into a school-year structure, keeping readers guessing and rewarding close attention.

Sparking a global phenomenon, Harry Potter is something special. The author builds a vivid magical society complete with Hogwarts, Quidditch, enchanted objects, and distinct systems of government and education that feel lived-in and timeless. The book’s ability to make the heroes feel like your friends is a testament to its rich character development. Beyond the primary trio, the books present a plethora of memorable characters across the spectrum of good and evil, even reminding readers that there is no binary when it comes to morality. Harry Potter revived youth reading worldwide, reshaped young adult fantasy publishing, and launched a massive cross-media entertainment franchise. The books were adapted into a monumental film franchise starring Daniel Radcliffe. The stories are being revived for the small screen on HBO with a brand-new ensemble, and will be more book-focused and faithful. ​​​​​​​

Let’s just state it now: this is entry number one of two for J.R.R. Tolkien. In the grand scope of fantasy, The Hobbit is the novel that set the tone for the century and beyond. As an early entry point for young readers, The Hobbit follows Bilbo Baggins, a comfort-loving hobbit recruited by the wizard Gandalf and thirteen dwarves to serve as their burglar on a quest to reclaim their ancestral Lonely Mountain and its treasure from the dragon Smaug. Along the perilous journey, Bilbo discovers a magical ring of invisibility and grows from a timid homebody into a courageous, resourceful hero. The Hobbit works so well because it pairs a brisk, accessible adventure with rich world-building, relatable growth, and timeless themes of courage and greed.

At around 300 pages, it reads like a lively, warm quest with a lighter, more self-contained arc compared to its sibling story. Tolkien mixes playful humor, songs, and riddles with real suspense and darker moments. Unlike sprawling epics that can bog down in exposition, The Hobbit moves in a masterful manner from set piece to set piece while letting characters drive the story. Bilbo Baggins starts out loving comfort and quiet routines rather than grand heroism, making his growth feel grounded and human. For many readers, he’s one of the greatest literary heroes ever written. With a whimsical charm and dangerous stakes woven into the narrative, The Hobbit taps into timeless archetypes of self-discovery. With a plentiful number of adaptations since its release, The Hobbit is most certainly remembered for its 1977 animated television special. And then there was that trilogy between 2012 and 2013 starring Martin Freeman, which we appreciate was made, though a redo would be nice. It’s no match for Peter Jackson’s other Tolkien trilogy!​​​​​​​

C.S. Lewis is truly a magician with words, and The Chronicles of Narnia is proof. Across seven books, The Chronicles of Narnia follows children from the real world who are magically transported to Narnia, a fantastical realm of talking animals and mythical creatures. Guided by the noble lion Aslan, these young heroes must fulfill ancient prophecies and battle evil forces to restore peace across Narnia's entire history, from its creation to its ultimate end. The novels are universally praised for combining accessible portal fantasy with rich moral themes, vivid imagery, and stories that span an entire world's history from creation to its end. Lewis delivers immediate wonder, bold storytelling, and deep moral weight without heavy exposition.

Lewis was an early leader in the genre, helping elevate fantasy and children's literature by treating big ideas, danger, and loss honestly rather than talking down to readers. Rather than rigid world-building, C.S. Lewis blends Roman fauns, talking animals, mythical creatures, and Father Christmas into a lively, fairy-tale atmosphere. Ordinary children step through a wardrobe—or into other portals—into Narnia with almost no setup or dense lore dump. And yet, these worlds become brilliantly immersive, allowing the celebration of imagination to roam free. The series shifts perspectives across installments, but Lewis maintains the narrative DNA as it expands the world throughout the timeline. Through the Christ-like lion Aslan and broader narrative arcs from creation to an end-times, Lewis communicated Christian ideas through story rather than direct lecturing. No matter your ideology, the books still resonate. The volumes of books have been adapted three times on TV and, with Netflix releasing Narnia: The Magician’s Nephew, twice for film, with the Walden Media franchise presenting a beautiful trilogy. ​​​​​​​

Four worlds and four completely different ideas of what a good life looks like. This is where you belong, what it would ask of you, and what you would get back in return.

You belong somewhere old enough that the mountains have names and somebody can still tell you who gave them. Middle-earth is for people who think the right thing is worth doing even when it will not be finished in their lifetime.

You want the sea, a crew, and absolutely nobody telling you where you are allowed to go. The Grand Line is the world for people whose main ambition in life is finding out what is over there.

You would rather be feared and useful than liked and useless. The Continent is a world where the monsters are the straightforward part of the job and the people are where it all gets complicated.

You want the myths to be real and loose in the actual world, and you want to find out at about thirteen that you were never anywhere near as ordinary as everybody kept telling you.

Though it existed long before the hit HBO series, A Song of Ice and Fire is high fantasy at its finest. Written by George R.R. Martin, consisting of five existing books at the moment, A Song of Ice and Fire is set in the medieval continent of Westeros, where rival noble families engage in a brutal political civil war for control of the Seven Kingdoms. As these human power struggles unfold, ancient magic slowly returns in the form of Daenerys Targaryen's reborn dragons in the east and an existential threat of undead creatures gathering beyond a massive ice wall in the far north. Praised for its morally complex characters, ruthless political intrigue, and stories where choices carry real consequences, A Song of Ice and Fire grounds the fantasy in human motives while subverting classic genre tropes.

What A Song of Ice and Fire does brilliantly is its unpredictability. No one is safe to Martin's pen. Major figures can be killed or maimed, rejecting traditional fantasy comfort for a high-tension thrill ride. The morally gray characters operate in a middle ground where their complex motives override the clear heroic or villainous traits. Martin paints Westeros as a brilliantly rich land where power struggles, alliances, and the harsh realities of survival drive the action. Shifting third-person limited viewpoints allow readers to experience deep personal perspectives across a vast, detailed world. A Song of Ice and Fire had a monumental impact that reshaped modern fantasy. Though the question remains: will it ever get finished!? Whether you've read the collection or not, Martin's vision came to life in the eight-season series Game of Thrones. There was a definitive ending, though based on fans' reactions, the final two planned novels may veer in a vastly different direction! Fingers crossed, at least!

We greatly revere J.R.R. Tolkien because he is such a prolific writer. The Lord of the Rings is all the proof you need. The epic high fantasy novel, broken up into three parts, is set in Middle-earth wher hobbits, wizards, elves, and men unite against the Dark Lord Sauron. It's central quest centers on the journey to destroy the One Ring in Mount Doom to thwart Sauron's campaign to conquer the realm. Through deep world-building, timeless themes of hope and friendship, and its foundational influence on modern fantasy, The Lord of the Rings has had a lasting cultural reach beyond its initial release.

Tolkien built Middle-earth over decades, grounding the story in deep lore, past ages, and fallen kingdoms. His vast vision became a benchmark for scale and depth for fantasy storytelling. He crafted distinct languages, scripts, and histories for peoples, making Middle-earth feel lived-in rather than a simple backdrop. Shaped by the author's experience in World War I and his views of industrialism, the story examines the corrupting pull of power, the cost of war, and the value of everyday courage and compassion. Humble hobbits carry the weight of stopping a massive evil, showing how quiet courage and persistence can change the world. Tolkien moves the narrative from cozy beginnings to high-stakes battles and tragic moments, offering readers a wide emotional range. With over 150 million copies sold, it has inspired countless media adaptations, most notably Peter Jackson's Oscar-winning trilogy. Whether it's your favorite fantasy story or not, its lasting impact on the genre, pop culture, and entertainment is undeniable.

Chivu: ‘Inter had right attitude, cannot dominate start to finish in Champions League’

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Cristian Chivu insists Inter had the right attitude in their 2-0 win over VfB Stuttgart, warning ‘in the Champions League you cannot dominate from start to finish.’

The Nerazzurri were feeling the pressure after fumbling a 4-2 lead to draw 4-4 with Atalanta in Serie A at the weekend, and knew this was a must-win game to leapfrog VfB Stuttgart in the Champions League table.

It was a tough match, decided only by the Petar Sucic penalty for handball, and Lautaro Martinez coming off the bench to lock down the result for 2-0.

“We relied on mentality, organisation, intensity, accepting that Champions League games are not easy, that you cannot dominate them from start to finish,” Chivu told Sky Sport Italia.

“At times you need to stay in the match, try to make the most of opposition errors, but also try to be proactive when possible. The moments of a match are important and you need to read them.

“We did well not to get swayed by their intensity in the first 20 minutes, so we accepted that we had to defend a bit deeper, but still with energy and pressing them on the ball. We did well to defend the crosses, as we knew they would be dangerous down the wings.

“It is also true that we created chances in the first half and with a little more sharpness or determination, we could’ve taken the lead before going into the break. We stayed in there, and were eventually rewarded with the victory.”

This result means Inter are feeling more solid in the Champions League after three consecutive victories, having started the campaign with back-to-back defeats against Real Madrid and Club Brugge.

There are still concerns that the Nerazzurri lose intensity at times and need to be woken up before reacting.

“The job of a coach is to transmit the right motivation in every match. It’s not always easy to maintain the same level, sometimes we do it better than others, but the important thing is that the team remains united and nobody starts pointing the finger at anyone else,” added Chivu.

“We must always try to do better in every competition. We cannot always be perfect, but what we must never lack is the right attitude and mentality. We saw that tonight, and it is no coincidence that we brought home the victory.”

Did Chivu have to give the squad an intervention after they collapsed from 4-2 to draw 4-4 with Atalanta on Sunday?

“There always need to be interventions, when things go well or badly. We made a clear analysis of that match, because for around 75-80 minutes we played well, we lacked something in the final 10 minutes, but that doesn’t mean throwing away all the good things that were done that night.

“We are trying to keep a clear head in analysing the situation and whittling down those errors that can be tactical, technical or mental,” replied the Inter coach.

There was bad news too, as Alessandro Bastoni limped off with a thigh injury after 40 minutes, replaced by Federico Dimarco, moving Carlos Augusto to central defence.

How Does Gabe Vincent Signing Impact the Kings?

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

On Sunday, Nov. 22, the Sacramento Kings signed veteran point guard Gabe Vincent to a one-year, non-guaranteed deal. The move comes after it became increasingly apparent that Malik Monk was not cutting it as a backup point guard. The Kings have played Monk less and less as the season has progressed because their offense is poor with him on the floor.

Vincent slotting into the Kings' lineup as backup point guard shifts Monk to a secondary ball-handling role, where he has been more comfortable in the past. He also provides continuity behind rookie Darius Acuff Jr., a player the organization sees as their superstar of the future.

Acuff is third in the NBA in minutes per game, averaging 36.6. It is unprecedented for a rookie to lead that category, but the coaching staff has deemed it necessary to play him so much. With Acuff off the floor, turnovers increase, offensive possessions stall, and players take poor shots. Vincent represents stability; he makes wise decisions with the ball.

His numbers don't jump off the stat sheet — Vincent averaged just 3.9 points, 1.6 assists, and 1.1 rebounds in 13.3 minutes per game. What does stand out, however, is his minuscule 0.6 turnovers per game. The 1.6-to-0.6 assist-to-turnover ratio means he averages 2.67 assists for every singular turnover. Monk's ratio is 3.6-to-1.69, meaning he averages 2.11 assists before turning the ball over.

Vincent doesn't play himself off the floor; he does what the coaching staff asks of him in his minutes. One could categorize his play style as boring, but Kings fans would much prefer boring over unpredictable.

Looking beyond his offensive impact, Vincent is not a liability on defense. When engaged, he's a solid point-of-attack defender who plays with hustle and physicality. Monk is lack-luster defensively, so Vincent has a leg up on him there. Even if Vincent's shot isn't falling on a given night, he can ensure the second unit doesn't completely lose the game.

Vincent's journey to the NBA was anything but easy. After completing his college career at UC Santa Barbara, Vincent entered the NBA Draft, but went undrafted. He wound up playing for his hometown Stockton Kings in the NBA G League.

Vincent then worked his way to the NBA, receiving a two-way contract from the Miami Heat in 2020. He worked his way into the rotation and was a key piece for their 2023 NBA Finals run.

Whether Vincent is an All-Star or plays ten minutes per night, the advice he can give Acuff is invaluable. Acuff said it himself in a postgame press conference; he is not a "loser." He's not used to the trials and tribulations that come with leading a, frankly, terrible team. Vincent has experienced his fair share of bumps in his career, and he can help the young rookie navigate what this season has to offer.

Being a veteran presence in the locker room alone is important for the rookie. He hasn't played extended minutes yet, as he is easing into Doug Christie's system and getting back into game shape. He has the skill set to make an impact once the dust settles.

Adding Vincent leads to a level of competition to the team's backcourt. Monk may try to fight for his spot as the primary ball-handler in the second unit. He has already stepped up his game since Vincent's arrival.

Signing Vincent isn't necessarily a needle moving acquisition for a 2-15 Sacramento Kings team. It does, however, provide backcourt reassurance and takes a good deal of initiating off Acuff's plate. Acuff is tied for the fifth-highest usage rate in the league (31.6%). Passing off that burden to a veteran will go a long way to keeping the rookie healthy and confident.

Over a long, grueling 82-game season, a break from having your foot on the gas pedal is often needed. Signing Vincent is not just a solid move because of what he brings to the floor, but in what he symbolizes. He exemplifies continuity, sturdiness, and peace of mind.

Marinozzi ottimista: "Milan squadra forte nel presente, ma in evoluzione per il futuro"

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Andrea Marinozzi, giornalista ed opinionista sportivo ha parlato così del Milan e dell'ottimo rendimento che la squadra di Amorim sta avendo in campionato. Questo un estratto delle sue considerazioni anche in vista della partita di domani sera contro l'Olympiacos:

"Quella contro il Frosinone è stata una partita di transizioni, una partita di velocità e di spazio, una partita pazza che succede spesso quando c'è in campo il Frosinone. Ci sono stati tanti cambi di possesso, con Modric che ha rotto una marea di linee avversarie e ha superato la pressione con palloni illuminanti giocati benissimo per le frecce che poi hanno attaccato lo spazio, Rabiot e Moreira su tutti. Il Milan a me piace, lo dico da tempo: mi piace il percorso di questa squadra, mi piace come la stanno costruendo. È una squadra forte nel presente, ma in evoluzione per il futuro, con tanti giocatori che non sono ancora all'apice della loro carriera, ma possono arrivarci e partono da una base molto alta".

"Se confrontiamo, per esempio, ora le competizioni, per noi come squadra e anche per ogni giocatore come me, è importante vincere le partite e finora l'abbiamo fatto molto bene in Serie A. Ora è una competizione diversa che iniziamo, questa è la verità. E domani è una grande, grande opportunità, ma anche, diciamo, c'è una sorta di pressione per tutti noi. E penso che la partita contro il Frosinone sia la Serie A, è una competizione diversa, ma comunque per tutti i giocatori, ha portato buon umore, una buona atmosfera e domani dobbiamo essere pronti a a competere e a vincere la partita".

Excuse me if I’m reading between the lines too much, but it seems like you’re trying to weigh the benefits of running out the clock by continuing to work vs. having a concrete plan that you can track your real retirement against. Most people I know definitely choose the former option, but I don’t think you need to overcomplicate the latter.

TPAW planner is a fairly good tool if you’re trying to figure out whether you can support temporary periods of higher spend. It gives you a month-by-month figure out what you could spend in the 5th, 50th, and 95th percentile of return outcomes. You could look at the range and see if it seems realistic to you. You will be projecting assumptions just like all the other tools, but I think it is clearer mental model to consider “could I live on $x” than it is to see it as a Y% of success.
Thanks for the TPAW rec! I've glanced it a few times but never concluded that it offered something more useful than I was getting from Pralana Online. To be totally transparent, I'm just iterating through possible scenarios in Pralana, but none of them rely on coming to any conclusion based on any year's withdrawal rate percentage. I mean, I do have numbers in the 4-5% range in mind when I look at what Pralana's deterministic projections show me needing to withdraw based on what I've told it about in expense assumptions but that's only casually useful as a sort of qualitative observation that most years are nicely below that 4-5% range. But I don't conclude anything from that. I much more heavily rely on Monte Carlo runs and testing sensitivity to things like my asset class return assumptions over time, AA, retirement date, LTC costs, how big a backstop the paid-off house would be, etc.

I think I was just wanting to validate that I'm not fundamentally missing some aspect of SWRs that people are leaning into. Your last sentence kind of sums up what's seemed off to me about SWR-based analyses:
“could I live on $x”
It's just too simple a question to try to answer in that form (unless you try to compensate like delamer suggested by modeling lifetime lumpy expenses as a fixed annual expense).
