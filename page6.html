US court blocks regulator's early review of ABC's broadcast licenses

📅 Nov 26, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The FCC chairman had ordered an early review of ABC's licenses a day after President Donald Trump called for the broadcaster to fire Jimmy Kimmel earlier this year. The licenses were not set to expire until 2028.

A US judge on Wednesday barred the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) ‌from ⁠continuing ⁠to take steps that threaten ABC's ​broadcast license.

In April, FCC Chairman Brendan Carr ordered an early license review for eight ABC stations, even though their licenses were not set to expire until 2028.

ABC sued, arguing that the move was retaliation for broadcasting content that angered President Donald Trump.

On Wednesday, US District Judge Loren AliKhan said the FCC ​chair appeared to have taken "marching orders" from Trump's rhetoric.

"This is not a close case," she wrote in her ruling. "No one doubts that the commission may enforce the law in the manner it sees fit. But in doing so, defendants may not retaliate against or punish plaintiffs and other broadcast license holders for disfavored speech."

Carr's order to review ABC's licenses had come a day after Trump called for ABC to fire late-night host Jimmy Kimmel over a joke he made. His show was taken off the air for six days amid pressure from the White House.

The broadcaster, which is owned by the Walt Disney Company, welcomed the ruling on Wednesday.

"Today's decision is an important victory for the First Amendment," an ABC News spokesperson said in a statement.

Meanwhile, a spokesperson for the FCC said the ruling was "wrong on the facts and wrong on the law — as controlling Supreme Court precedent makes clear."

"We will ​continue to use our lawful authority to hold broadcasters accountable to their public interest obligations," the spokesperson added.

But one dissenting opinion within the FCC came from its sole Democratic commissioner, Anna Gomez.

She said the ruling brought "badly needed accountability to a commission and an administration that have politicized the machinery of government in a sustained campaign of censorship and control."

PLAINVILLE - On Thanksgiving eve 2026, Plainville, Connecticut was the little town that didn't sleep.

The lights of Dinty Stadium were blazing and unlikely to be shut down any time soon as the Plainville Blue Devils and their fans were madly celebrating the first 10-0 season in the 101 years this town has been playing high school football after a dramatic 33-28 victory over Farmington in the 15th playing of the Olde Canal Cup.

Down by two, just stopped on fourth down at the 1-yard line with time running out, the Blue Devils were facing their first loss of the year. Instead, improbably, miraculously, Farmington fumbled.

Plainville junior defensive lineman Nick Serrano recovered and, two plays later, senior quarterback Anthony Abarno lofted an 8-yard touchdown pass to sophomore wide receiver Christian Hairston in the middle of the end zone with 34 seconds to play.

When the Plainville defense thwarted a hook-and-ladder play and Farmington couldn't get off one last play, the Blue Devils could, indeed, start spreading the news. They had gone unbeaten in 1946, but that was 8-0. This was 10-0, with a home quarterfinal as the No. 2 seed in Class SS dead ahead next Tuesday. It's Plainville's first trip to the CIAC football playoffs since 2019.

Farmington, in need of a win to qualify in Class L for the first time since 2013, fell to 6-4 and can only hope that all the 6-3 teams playing Thanksgiving Day go down to defeat to have any hope of sneaking in. If not, the River Hawks will have to be content with a strong season that was their first over .500 since going 6-4 in 2018.

With just over two minutes to play, it looked like Farmington's season would extend and Plainville's perfection would end. Famrington defended an inside run on the fourth down at the 1. Needing to just run out the clock, the River Hawks fumbled on third down.

Plainville took over at the 2 with 1:14 to go, lost six yards on the first snap. On second down at the 8, Abarno took the shotgun snap, rolled a few steps to his right and put a pass into the end zone that Hairston brought down between two defenders.

It was Abarno's fifth touchdown pass of the night and his second to Hairston. The senior QB, among the state's top passers this season, also threw two scoring strikes to Billy Putnam and one to Chase Bounchard.

The second to Putnam, a 2-yarder that Putnam cashed in reaching the ball across the goal line, came with 4:46 left to play and pulled the Blue Devils to within 28-26. Farmington denied the two-point pass attempt to stay in the lead.

Down 20-14 at halftime, Farmington grabbed the upper hand during a dominant third quarter. The River Hawks opened the second half with a 14-play, 61-yard drive capped by a 12-yard pass from junior QB Mason Kiniry to senior receiver Grant Kintner. Tyler McMahon's PAT put Farmington ahead 21-20.

After initially failing to extend that lead on a missed 22-yard field goal, Farmington got the ball back on a fumble recovery by senior linebacker Bretton Phillips and this time cashed in on Kiniry's fourth-and-14 pass from the 24 to Chris Huelin just over two minutes into the fourth quarter.

Junior running back Tommy Zadrozny had Farmington's two touchdowns in the first half on runs of 11 and 12 yards. The first score came after Farmington pooched the opening kickoff and recovered at the Plainville 37.

The Blue Devils countered with Abarno TD passes to Bouchard, Putnam and Hairston. Sophomore defensive lineman Aaron Madore recovered a fumble for Plainville and also chalked up a handful of hurries on Kiniry.

Farmington, which had won the three previous Olde Canal Cup games, still leads the series 10-5.
