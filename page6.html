One goal is enough: Stuttgart overcome Bremen to climb the Bundesliga table

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

VfB Stuttgart secured a narrow 1-0 victory over Werder Bremen in Saturday's Bundesliga Topspiel. An early goal from Maximilian Mittelstädt proved decisive, as the hosts held off a much-improved Bremen side after the break to claim all three points.

VfB Stuttgart made their intentions clear from the opening whistle, putting Werder Bremen under considerable pressure during the first 20 minutes. The hosts dominated proceedings and were rewarded for their aggressive start in the seventh minute.

A low cross from Grischa Prömel found Maximilian Mittelstädt arriving perfectly inside the penalty area. The left-back timed his run to perfection and fired the ball into the net to give Stuttgart an early 1-0 lead.

Much of the attention before kick-off had been on Niclas Füllkrug following his latest Germany call-up. However, the Bremen striker struggled to make an impact against Stuttgart defender Jeff Chabot. The two were involved in several physical duels, with Chabot largely keeping his opponent at bay.

Bremen had little to celebrate during the opening 45 minutes, although their recent record offered some encouragement. The visitors had already collected six points this season after falling behind, while also enjoying more possession than Stuttgart in this match. Can they turn it around once more?

The second half began with another dangerous Stuttgart opportunity. Following a threatening corner, Mittelstädt found himself with space on the edge of the penalty area and unleashed a powerful shot. However, Bremen goalkeeper Karl Hein managed to get his fingertips to the ball, diverting it onto the post and preventing Stuttgart from doubling their advantage.

The save proved important as Bremen gradually grew into the game. The visitors looked considerably more confident after the interval, won more individual duels and managed to create several promising attacking situations.

Stuttgart, meanwhile, struggled to maintain the intensity of their opening-half performance. Sebastian Hoeneß's side increasingly found themselves on the back foot as Bremen searched for an equaliser.

One positive for the Stuttgart coach was the performance of 19-year-old Yanik Spalt. The youngster is gradually establishing himself in the starting eleven and once again demonstrated his importance to the team. After featuring prominently during the midweek trip to Istanbul, he delivered another encouraging performance against Bremen. His playing style offers Hoeneß a valuable alternative in midfield, particularly when Angelo Stiller, like currently, is unavailable.

Despite their improved display, Bremen struggled to turn their attacking pressure into clear-cut finishing opportunities. Füllkrug, in particular, was unable to produce his best performance against a well-organised Stuttgart defence.

Ultimately, Mittelstädt's early strike proved enough to separate the sides. Stuttgart secured an important three points and climbed to eighth place in the Bundesliga table, while Werder Bremen remain fifth for the time being, despite their defeat.

Zcash’s new security fund is 99.7% old money

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

A November 7 mainnet snapshot puts the historical seed at 99.7% of the reserve, with 1.12 ZEC accumulated under NU7’s fee rules.

Zcash’s new reserve for future block rewards is still overwhelmingly inherited value. About 99.7% of its balance in an early post-NU7 mainnet observation on November 7, 2026, came from previously unclaimed subsidy and fees, leaving just 1.12 ZEC accumulated under the new rules.

At 05:08:38 UTC, the mainnet API for Zakura’s NU7 monitoring dashboard reported a Network Sustainability Mechanism, or NSM, balance of 369.7 ZEC at block 3,547,068. Its status data showed NU7 active.

ZIP 237, the specification governing the reserve, sets the historical seed at 368.58445520 ZEC. Subtracting that seed leaves 1.11895065 ZEC of subsequent accumulation, or about 0.3% of the observed total.

An early mainnet snapshot separates the historical seed from new fee accumulation, then traces the fee path into future block subsidies.

The historical seed consists of subsidy and transaction fees that miners left unclaimed in coinbase transactions before NU6. ZIP 237 brings that fixed amount into NSM accounting. It therefore gives the mechanism a positive starting balance before any new fee contribution arrives.

For the rules deployed at NU7, ZIP 237 registers transaction fees as the reserve’s only contribution mechanism. ZIP 259, the deployment specification, excludes ZIP 233’s separate removal mechanism and ZIP 234’s alternative issuance design.

There is also no reserve reissuance at the observed height. Taken together, those rules mean the excess over the historical seed represents accumulated fee contributions under the specified accounting. The 1.12 ZEC figure follows from the reported reserve balance and those accounting rules.

The NSM balance itself is public consensus accounting for value outside issued supply. Distribution follows the protocol’s future subsidy rules.

ZIP 235 directs 60% of each block’s aggregate transaction fees into the NSM. The contribution is rounded down once per block to whole zatoshi, Zcash’s smallest unit; miners receive the remainder, including the rounding difference.

Scheduled subsidy remains separate from this fee allocation. The mechanism temporarily removes fee value from circulation and returns it through future block subsidies.

The delay is substantial in block terms. ZIP 259 assigns mainnet activation to block 3,543,405, while reserve reissuance starts at block 8,939,664. ZIP 237 sets the additional NSM subsidy to zero before that later height. Reaching that block threshold determines when payouts begin.

Once reissuance starts, each block adds a small fraction of the preceding reserve balance to the scheduled subsidy, rounded up to a zatoshi. The existing halving schedule remains in place.

ZIP 237 illustrates the scale with 100,000 ZEC removed from circulation: that amount would initially contribute 0.01375 ZEC per block under the post-NU7 formula. This hypothetical illustrates the payout rule; the actual starting payout will depend on the reserve accumulated by then.

Funding streams also receive a share of future reissuance. ZIP 237 applies funding streams’ prescribed percentages to the combined scheduled and additional subsidy. The reserve mechanism and discretionary developer grants are different arrangements.

Before payouts begin, the fee-funded portion grows with cumulative fee spending. More time permits more contributions if fee-paying activity continues; elapsed time alone supplies no funding.

That makes the useful follow-up measure the amount added beyond the fixed historical seed over clearly dated intervals. A growing total can otherwise obscure how much of the reserve still comes from inherited value.

The opening snapshot cannot establish a sustainable annual funding rate, predict future usage or show whether eventual support will be sufficient for network security. Those questions depend on fee demand over time and the balance available when gradual reissuance begins.

Florida Man Finishes Four Corners Walk Across America

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

After over three years of trekking 20,000km back and forth across the continental United States, endurance athlete Kyndal Ray Edwards has completed his ambitious Four Corners Walk. His route took him from coast to coast, then corner to corner, starting in Florida and ending in Maine, where a crowd of supporters met him last week.

Kyndal Ray Edwards, 33, is originally from Orange Park, Florida. After struggling with drug addiction, mental illness, and the justice system during his early adulthood, he decided to become an endurance athlete and mental health advocate. On January 1, 2022, he began his walking project from the beach in Jacksonville.

Over the next 15 months, Edwards walked to Long Beach, Washington, traveling 5,298km from the Atlantic to the Pacific. His plans expanded, and he undertook the challenge of walking to all four corners of the contiguous United States. From Long Beach, he headed to the northwesternmost point, Cape Flattery, also in Washington. After trekking south to San Diego, the next corner he headed for was all the way back in Florida, the southeast extremity.

Along the way, Edwards posted frequently on social media about his journey and his recovery from mental illness.

On October 25, Edwards reached West Quoddy Head Lighthouse in Lubec, Maine. This is the northeast corner of the U.S., and the finish line for Edwards. He walked a total of 19,544 kilometers in 1,022 days, averaging nearly 20km per day.

Edwards and supporters at the finish line. Photo: Kyndal Ray Edwards

A cross-country walking journey, Edwards says, has been his dream since 2013. But with the end of his Walking Across America project, Edwards is already ruminating on future challenges. Online, he has proposed going on an adventure with a stranger in all 50 states, running more ultra marathons, and undertaking endurance challenges on every continent. However, he has yet to commit to one.

The end of Kyndal Ray Edwards’s journey came only days before Karl Bushby, another long-distance trekker, completed his 28-year Goliath Expedition. The pair also happen, Edwards pointed out, to share a birthday.

Introduction: An Orient Express Adventure Via Africa

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Welcome to my next trip report series, covering a trip that I recently wrapped up, which will include reviews of flights and a cruise. This will be a relatively short series, since the trip didn’t last that long, and had a specific purpose. However, as usual, I tried to take an unconventional routing to get to and from the destination, so I could review new experiences.

You can expect reviews of Air Algerie and Royal Air Maroc business class, as well as a review of Orient Express Sailing Yachts, which is Orient Express’ new cruising venture. In this post I’d like to share a basic outline of the trip, and then I’ll have individual review installments in the near future.

The primary reason for this trip was simple, and it was to take a cruise with Orient Express Sailing Yachts. It’s a fascinating time for the luxury cruising industry, and in particular, the increasing number of new lines associated with existing luxury hospitality brands.

Prior to this year, I hadn’t taken a cruise with any cruise line since I was a child, so I’ve been curious to check this all out. At the beginning of the year, I reviewed Ritz-Carlton Yacht Collection, which was surprisingly lovely, and a very nice reintroduction to cruising.

Then over the summer, I reviewed Four Seasons Yachts, which was definitely upmarket, but had some growing pains.

The newest entrant into this market is Orient Express Sailing Yachts, and arguably it takes luxury cruising to a new level, so you can expect a full review of the experience. Full disclosure — Ford was invited on the sailing (so traveled as a guest of Orient Express) and I tagged along. I’ll still review the experience objectively, of course, but I’ll say upfront that Orient Express simply nailed it, and this matched the feedback Ford has received from clients who have sailed with the company.

Anyway, we sailed with Orient Express Sailing Yachts on a four night journey that started in Marseille, France, and ended in Malaga, Spain, so we needed to position to and from there.

I’m of course always looking for an interesting airline angle when I travel long haul, since my goal is always to try as many new airline products as possible. So rather than flying to Marseille and from Malaga on the “typical” airlines (Air France, British Airways, Lufthansa, etc.), I tried to see if I could instead check some airlines off my list that I’ve been wanting to try. I accomplished that, and below is what my routing looked like.

While I sent Ford direct, I booked a four segment routing for myself, spread across three different tickets.

I had heard a lot about Air Algerie over the years (and not in a good way!), so I decided I finally had to give the carrier a try. Air Algerie typically has very high business class fares, despite the carrier’s lackluster product.

However, I found one decent fare on the carrier’s only route from North America, from Montreal (YUL) to Algiers (ALG) to Tunis (TUN). Okay, that doesn’t start where I’d like to start, and it doesn’t end where I’d like to end, but that has never stopped me if the price is right. 😉

This included a flight in Air Algerie’s Airbus A330-900neo business class across the Atlantic, and then a connecting flight in Avion Express’ Airbus A320neo business class for the short hop in Africa (this was a wet leased flight operated on behalf of Air Algerie). So I booked the following in business class for $1,171 in cash:

10/03 AH2701 Montreal to Algiers departing 4:10PM arriving 5:00AM (+1 day) 10/04 AH4000 Algiers to Tunis departing 7:10AM arriving 8:30AM

From there, I of course had to get from Tunis to Marseille (MRS), so I had booked Tunisair’s A320 business class for $270, with the following schedule:

10/04 TU902 Tunis to Marseille departing 1:05PM arriving 3:40PM

Unfortunately I ended up being downgraded shortly before departure due to an aircraft swap. That’s because the flight was swapped to a wet leased Fly2Sky Airbus A320, and I guess they don’t offer business class on those planes. I’m still waiting on any sort of a refund.

Suffice it to say that the outbound wasn’t that smooth, between Air Algerie just royally sucking, and Tunisair downgrading me. I’m happy I had the experience, though!

I should also mention that I had to position to Montreal, so I did that in Air Canada Rouge economy, and flew the following for 11,500 avianca lifemiles plus $54 in taxes & fees:

10/04 AC1651 Miami to Montreal departing 7:55AM arriving 11:28AM

The return portion of our trip was a little more straightforward. Our priority was to get home the same day as our cruise arrived, since we wanted to minimize time away from our kids, and had a family event the next day. So we booked the one routing that would best get us home the same day, which was on Royal Air Maroc, from Malaga (AGP) to Casablanca (CMN) to Miami (MIA), with the first flight in Royal Air Maroc’s Boeing 737-800 business class, and the second flight in Royal Air Maroc’s Boeing 787-8 business class.

I booked one ticket using 57,500 American AAdvantage miles plus $85.72 in taxes & fees, while the other ticket was booked with cash, at the cost of $1,620, with the following itinerary:

10/09 AT989 Malaga to Casablanca departing 2:15PM arriving 1:30PM 10/09 AT204 Casablanca to Miami departing 3:00PM arriving 7:45PM

Obviously paying cash for a ticket wasn’t ideal, but that still ended up being less out of pocket than flying British Airways business class through London on awards, factoring in the carrier surcharges.

On this trip, we just had a single hotel night ahead of our cruise in Marseille (in order to build in a buffer), and we decided to stay at the Hôtel Dieu Intercontinental Marseille. I booked this using an IHG free night award, and it cost 60,000 points before applying the award, so I just had to redeem the difference in points.

I had intended to review the hotel, but I have to apologize, it just didn’t end up working out (since I pride myself in thoroughly reviewing hotels). To be honest, I was dead tired from the journey over, and then we had some drama to deal with back at home most of the afternoon. Then the next morning I prioritized sleeping in, as I was just getting over a cold, rather than getting up at 5AM so I could photograph all parts of the hotel.

So I’m sorry about that. My conclusion is that the hotel has a central location, and from the outside you’d think it’s super charming, but the interior feels mismatched and rather uninspiring and generic.

We had an absolutely amazing time cruising with Orient Express Sailing Yachts, and I was blown away by the experience. What I was less impressed by was my flights to and from Europe, which I routed via Africa in both directions. Air Algerie was downright terrible, while Royal Air Maroc was just unnecessarily bad.
