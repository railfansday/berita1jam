Janus Electric secures battery supply deal for its battery-swap semi truck

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Australian conversion specialists Janus Electric have been celebrating a growing slate of orders for their Class 8 electric truck conversions, which ditch diesel in favor of advanced battery-swap electric power – but exactly how they’d deliver all those trucks was still a question. Now, we have an answer: Electrovaya will supply the batteries.

The concept of dropping an electric power unit into a Class 8 semi makes perfect sense, as most of these trucks are built as gliders. That is, rolling body and chassis without an engine and transmission that’s ready to receive power from Cummins, Detroit, International, or Volvo Penta. That’s the role that Janus Electric sees itself occupying, and it seems to be working.

Janus’ reservation book crept past 70 units this past summer, helped along in part by California’s HVIP Program and the US EPA’s Diesel Emission Reduction Act (DERA) Grants. When the HVIP voucher is combined with the Port of Los Angeles’ “Plus” support, customers can received up to $166,000 per truck conversion. Factor in the massive fuel savings of electric and the classic good looks of square-nose Western Star truck body, and it’s easy to see why they’d be a popular choice.

As Jerry Seinfeld taught us however, it’s one thing to take a reservation – it’s another thing to deliver. To help make it happen, Janus has inked a battery supply deal with Electrovaya that will see a $26 million initial purchase orders for Electrovaya battery packs to demand for Janus’ products in the US and Canada, along with the battery company purchasing approximately $1.4 million in Janus stock with (what seems to be) actual money.

“Electrovaya is an important partner to Janus Electric, and we’re particularly pleased it has decided to invest directly in our business,” explains Ben Hutt, Managing Director and CEO of Janus Electric. “The investment is a strong vote of confidence in our technology and approach to electrifying heavy transport.”

The Janus Electric-repowered trucks carry a stout, 480 hp electric motor drawing power from a standard 310 kWh battery pack, with up to two battery packs (one on each side) of the truck. In practice, that gives the Janus trucks up to 370 miles of loaded range. After that, the batteries can be DC fast charged in under 90 minutes, or swapped for fresh ones in about four minutes without too much in the way of specialized equipment. That’s not just Janus’ killer app, it means that a battery supply deal like this one with a strategic partner who’s invested in the fledgling company’s success is such a huge deal.

“Reliable battery supply is critical to our growth,” adds Hutt, “and Electrovaya’s expanding North American manufacturing capabilities will be important as we scale. Both companies see considerable opportunity in this market, and we look forward to building on our partnership.”

For their part, Electrovoya executives seem to know their role here, and seem excited to be a part of what Janus is doing. “Class 8 trucking is one of the most exciting and demanding frontiers in electrification, and Janus has demonstrated real commercial traction with fleets in the US, Canada and Australia,” said Dr. Raj Das Gupta, CEO of Electrovaya. “Janus has developed a practical solution to one of the major challenges facing heavy transport, enabling diesel-powered trucks to be converted to electric without compromising their commercial operations.”

Electrovaya will be expanding its American manufacturing capacity with lithium-ion cell, module, and battery pack production at its new, potentially BABA-compliant facility in Jamestown, New York, which is expected to come online in 2027.

Salon, Digital News Trailblazer, Folds After 31 Years

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The website said its "publishing model is no longer sustainable" given the "business realities of independent digital journalism."

Salon, a left-leaning publication known for its sharp political and cultural commentary that showed what online journalism could look like before most mainstream outlets entered the space, is closing up shop after 31 years.

In an unsigned farewell announcement on Thursday, Salon pointed to the profound changes in the economics of independent digital journalism and said its publishing model is no longer sustainable.

“Salon was never just a website,” the announcement said. “It was an idea about what journalism could be, born at a moment when the internet was still a largely unexplored frontier.”

The shuttering highlights the rapidly-shifting economics of digital media, where even established publications are contending with massive declines in traffic. Search and social media have upended how outlets attract readers. Artificial intelligence search overviews, which allow audiences to bypass clicking links, are now doing the same. In this landscape, longevity and editorial influence no longer guarantee financial survival.

Launched in 1995 by then-San Francisco Examiner editor David Talbot, Salon was an early experiment in digital publishing without a print offering. It went public four years later and went on to cultivate a sizable readership. Prominent journalists who got a foothold in media through the website include CNN anchor Jake Tapper, NBC News’ Steve Kornacki, The Intercept founder Glenn Greenwald, The Nation national affairs correspondent Joan Walsh, who served as editor-in-chief of Salon for five years.

The site also played a starring role in Nora Ephron’s 2009 feature Julie & Julia as the home blog of Amy Adams’ Julie, who pledged to cook all of Julia Child’s cookbook in a year.

Like many other media outlets, the site saw a massive decline in traffic in 2016 after then-Facebook changed the social media platform’s news feed algorithm to prioritize posts from family and friends over publishers. In 2023, Find.co acquired the website from its previous owners, Drew Schoentrup and Chris Richmond. The privately held media company bills itself as a marketing operation revolving around crypto brands. Its portfolio includes CCN, Webopedia and CryptoManiaks.

Salon’s Thursday post didn’t mention what will happen to its immense archive. On Bluesky, Salon chief content officer Erin Keane said it will “stay live for now.”

Melania Trump touts rare agreement she brokered between Russia and Ukraine

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

WASHINGTON, DC - NOVEMBER 05: First lady Melania Trump gives hand written letters to Russian special envoy and head of the Russian Direct Investment Fund Kirill Dmitrievon (L) and Ukrainian human rights Ombudsman Dmytro Lubinets (C) to deliver to their presidents during an event on a temporary Russia-Ukraine ceasefire at the White House on November 5, 2026 in Washington, DC. Samuel Corum/Getty Images hide caption

First lady Melania Trump hosted a rare meeting between Russian and Ukrainian officials at the White House to recognize a temporary ceasefire in southeastern Ukraine.

The six-day pause from the Russian-occupied city of Oleshky comes as the administration ramps up efforts to secure an agreement to end the war in Ukraine.

"We have chosen humanity over war," the first lady said. "Undeniably, the lives of innocent people must transcend the divisions of war. I hope Oleshky inspires greater peace."

Trump has accomplished something that has so far eluded her husband - helping broker a temporary ceasefire and bringing together representatives of the two sides in acknowledgement of the human costs of the war.

The White House sees it as a diplomatic breakthrough, but some European analysts worry the efforts can backfire.

The temporary reprieve, which expired on Sunday, enabled humanitarian aid to reach the city while allowing hundreds of residents to be evacuated.

Kirill Dmitriev, Russian President Vladimir Putin's special envoy, thanked the first lady. He said Putin personally gave the instructions to ensure the successful implementation of the ceasefire.

"On behalf of President Putin, on behalf of the people of the Russian Federation, we would like to thank first lady Melania Trump for her distinguished humanitarian role in resolving a key situation and resolving this humanitarian issue," he said.

Ukrainian human rights ombudsman Dmytro Lubinets presented Melania Trump with an award for her humanitarian work. The Order of Princess Olha, 1st Class, is Ukraine's highest award for women.

"I have only one request from all Ukrainian nation," Lubinets said, "please continue doing your wonderful work and support people."

The event comes just days before the U.S. leads a new round of peace talks between Russia and Ukraine in Abu Dhabi next week.

President Trump also met with Dmitriev at the White House.

Trump told reporters that the sides were close to reaching a deal.

"It's going to be an agreement. It's going to be a big agreement, ‌and ⁠it's going to involve more than Ukraine," he said. "I could even say it might involve all of Europe. It's ⁠going to be a big, beautiful agreement, and everyone's going to be thrilled."

Heather Conley, who worked on European issues in President George W. Bush's State Department, supports all efforts to help suffering Ukrainians, but she also said the U.S. must be careful not to normalize Russia's bad behavior.

"In this White House ceremony, we were honoring the Russians that were the cause of both the occupation of Oleshky, the starvation of Oleshky, and now we're honoring them for allowing aid to come into Oleshky," said Conley, who is now a senior fellow at the American Enterprise Institute. "The more we create this equivalency where the Russians and the Ukrainians are equal in this crisis, it is putting this thing upside down."

Letterboxd Finalizing $330 Million Sale to The New York Times

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Letterboxd, the social networking platform for cinephiles, will soon have new owners. The website is finalizing a sale to The New York Times Company in a deal valuing the website at $330 million, according to a report from the NYT on Thursday.

An agreement has not yet been reached and could still fall apart, but the companies have entered into exclusive negotiations. The $330 million price tag is more than 20 times Letterboxd’s projected earnings in 2026 of about $15 million, the Times reported.

The New York Times would acquire the controlling stake in Letterboxd from Tiny, a Canadian-based public company, which originally picked up a 60 percent stake in 2023 when the website was valued at just $50 million. Tiny has been exploring a sale since April; a deal has finally emerged after film studios, tech executives, and private equity companies all kicked the tires. IndieWire understands that A24 was also in late-stage talks to acquire Letterboxd, with the studio promising Letterboxd’s founders to maintain its independence and its business model unchanged. The Times reported that it has discussed keeping founders Matthew Buchanan and Karl von Randow in place, with each reporting to a Times executive.

Letterboxd has grown significantly since being acquired by Tiny, now reaching 26 million users (though the company does not disclose how many of them are on paid subscriptions.) Its Four Favorites social media segments have proven reliable viral hits. It has attracted young celebrities who have become semi-active users. It also introduced a Letterboxd Video Store that offers digital rental of under-the-radar films or recent festival releases. OpenAI makes new data center commitments, AI policy recommendations

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

OpenAI Group PBC today made a set of commitments designed to ensure that its data centers benefit the communities where they’re built.

It also proposed that lawmakers make some of the commitments mandatory for artificial intelligence companies.

The move comes two days after Democrats won both the House and the Senate on a campaign backing data center construction curbs. Additionally, voters approved dozens of ballot measures designed to scale back or limit AI infrastructure projects.

OpenAI today reiterated a commitment it made in January to fully pay for the electricity and grid upgrades that its data centers require. That covers items such as the construction of new overhead power lines. Additionally, the company pledged to provide “financial assurances” that will protect other grid users from potential project cancellations and delays.

OpenAI will also take steps to reduce grid strain. In particular, it will scale back its data centers’ power draw at times when energy demand is particularly high. That approach, which is known as a flexible data center model, has also drawn the interest of other industry players as Nvidia Corp. and Google LLC.

Some data center builders sign non-disclosure agreements with local officials to keep construction details under wraps. OpenAI today pledged not to use NDAs. It will instead only seek “narrow protections covering legitimate security information, trade secrets, or proprietary or competitively sensitive information.”

OpenAI will publicly share construction plans and noise projections before construction of a new facility begins. Additionally, it will provide information about the jobs that each data center is expected to create. The goal is to help local unions and training providers prepare residents for the positions.

OpenAI plans to tailor its commitments to each data center. It will publish the customized project terms in a document it calls a local compact. Once construction is underway, the company will provide annual updates and designate a local contact to field residents’ questions.

OpenAI hopes to codify some of the best practices that it published today into law. The company released a policy paper that calls on lawmakers to limit data center developers’ use of NDAs and require them to cover their electricity costs. Furthermore, OpenAI argues that AI companies should be offered incentives to lower their power usage at times of peak grid stress.

Some of the best practices that the ChatGPT developer is prompting are already being adopted by other industry players. Google, for example, today pledged to fully cover the electricity costs of a new $17 billion data center that it plans to build in Wyoming. It will also finance related grid upgrades. Anthropic PBC made a similar commitment in February.

Google, Meta, TikTok sue over social media law

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Three of the biggest social media companies want to block a California law designed to reduce youth social media addiction from going into law.

On Wednesday, Google, Meta, and TikTok each filed lawsuits against California's attorney general, Rob Bonta.

Beginning on Jan. 1, 2027, the bill (AB 1709) would prohibit platforms including Google, Meta, and TikTok from providing "an addictive feature" to users under 16. The law describes such features as "psychologically exploitative" and intended to maximize engagement in a way that leads to compulsive use. This includes infinite scroll, autoplay, and an "addictive" feed.

The law also requires platforms to "implement reasonable measures" so that young users aren't offered such features.

The companies argue that the law infringes on their free speech rights, as well as Section 230, a provision of a 1996 federal law designed to shield online platforms from liability related to user content. Critics of Section 230 say it wrongly provides online platforms with broad immunity.

Google claimed in its lawsuit that its subsidiary, YouTube, personalizes recommendations to ensure that young users receive content relevant to their interests and appropriate for their age and developmental needs. The company defended autoplay as helping users enjoy content uninterrupted and sequentially, like a playlist for educational videos.

"AB 1709's bans on personalized recommendations and autoplay would therefore frustrate YouTube's efforts to provide under-16 users with safe, responsible, and enriching experiences on the platform," the lawsuit said.
