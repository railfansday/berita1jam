‘Gilmore Girls‘ Star Kelly Bishop to Release First Novel

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

'Broad Appeal,‘ releasing next year, will blend "the friendship and humor of 'The Golden Girls' with the banter of 'Sex and the City.'"

Gilmore Girls star Kelly Bishop is making her literary debut.

The actress will make her fiction debut to be published by William Morrow in Fall 2027 written with New York Times bestselling author Ali Rosen, it was announced Thursday.

The actress, also known for her acclaimed roles in Dirty Dancing, The Marvelous Mrs. Maisel and originating the role of Sheila in A Chorus Line (for which she won a Tony), will write a novel that “blends the friendship and humor of The Golden Girls with the banter of Sex and the City.“ Related Stories Book Excerpt The Origins of 'Elf': How Will Ferrell Came to Replace Chris Farley in the Christmas Classic Beyond the Book Book to Scream: How Literature Has Fueled 35 Years of Universal Orlando's Halloween Horror Nights

The novel, titled Broad Appeal, will center on four women in their 70s who unexpectedly become roommates in a Manhattan apartment and discover that life’s greatest adventures can happen long after retirement.

At the center of the novel is “Roz, a 78-year-old retired divorcée whose comfortable Upper West Side life is upended when rising housing costs threaten her ability to keep the apartment she loves. Forced to take on roommates, she finds herself sharing her home with an eccentric entomologist sister-in-law, a former trophy wife rebuilding after financial disaster and a spirited dance teacher she met while protesting gentrification in Spanish Harlem. What begins as a practical arrangement soon becomes an unexpected family, until long-buried secrets, financial pressures and unresolved heartbreaks test the bonds they’ve built.“

“Filled with sharp wit, unforgettable characters and keen insights into the complexities of human relationships, the novel marks an exciting new chapter in Bishop’s celebrated storytelling career,“ William Morrow said of Bishop‘s novel.

“I’m so excited to share Broad Appeal with readers,” Bishop said in a statement. “This story is about friendship, resilience, second chances and the surprising ways our lives can change when we least expect it. I’ve always believed that growing older doesn’t mean growing smaller. If anything, it means there are still stories worth telling and lives worth reinventing. I’ve had so much fun with Ali Rosen bringing this novel to life.”

“With this novel, Kelly Bishop has created a cast of characters readers will immediately invest in, delivering a story that is both entertaining and deeply resonant,” Jennifer Hart, senior vp and deputy publisher, William Morrow, said in a statement. “As a devoted Gilmore Girls fan, I know firsthand the power of Kelly’s storytelling, and this novel showcases that gift in an entirely new way.”

William Morrow vice president and executive editor Liz Stein added, “Kelly has charmed audiences for decades with her intelligence, warmth and unmistakable voice. Those qualities shine brilliantly on the page in Broad Appeal. It’s funny, wise and deeply moving, with unforgettable characters readers will want to spend time with long after the final page.”

Bishop released her New York Times bestselling memoir, The Third Gilmore Girl, in 2024.

Bangladesh Labor Protests Turn Violent

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Click to expand Image Garment workers demanding fair wage increases, payment of salaries, improved workplace facilities, and other benefits at a demonstration in Dhaka, Bangladesh, November 4, 2026. © 2026 Sipa via AP Images

Bangladesh police have used batons and tear gas to disperse thousands of garment workers protesting for higher wages. Violence by thugs allegedly linked to factory owners and political parties, arson, factory vandalism, and attacks on police have all been reported. 

Consistent with international standards, Bangladesh authorities should ensure that police use force only when necessary and proportionate, and that those responsible for violence are held accountable.

A garment worker, Toufiq Hossain, died on November 3. A co-worker who had been with him told Human Rights Watch: “We were returning to the factory [after lunch] when Toufiq got caught between protesting workers and men hired to tackle the protest by another garment factory. When I looked back, I saw Toufiq lying on the ground. The hired men had beaten him brutally.”

The striking workers are seeking a monthly wage of 30,000 taka (US$241), up from the 12,500 taka set in 2023. The government formed a wage board on November 1, which announced that a new pay structure, once determined, will apply from December.

“My rent is 6,000 taka ($48),” a 31-year-old sewing machine operator said. “Cooking gas now costs 2,300 ($18.50). I earn 13,500 ($108) [a month]. This year I can’t afford my children’s schooling. Tell me, what’s left for food?”

Instead of addressing legitimate demands for living wages, Bangladeshi authorities are blaming the political opposition for instigating protests. On November 3, Dhaka police said they had arrested 33 supporters of the former ruling party, the Awami League, for sabotage. Others alleged that members of the ruling Bangladesh Nationalist Party (BNP) youth wing had marched against protesting workers.

Three labor leaders were reportedly detained in Ashulia on November 3, accused of incitement. Police have also filed cases under Anti-Terrorism Act against thousands of unnamed people.

“They took my husband away,” said a 28-year-old woman whose husband was arrested. “He had joined the protest for a short time. He wasn’t involved in any violence.”

The BNP government promised inflation-linked wages before its election in February. An amendment to the Labour Act, adopted in April, provides for wage reviews every three years and prohibits employers from blacklisting trade union organizers. 

Bangladesh authorities should end the cycle of violence and impunity around wage negotiations. Global brands also have a role to play; they should always ensure that their contracts with suppliers don’t incentivize workplace abuses or make living wages impracticable.

How Jonathan Gannon built a Top-5 Packers defense without Micah Parsons

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

If you're reading this, you obviously know that on Aug. 28, 2025, the Green Bay Packers traded defensive tackle Kenny Clark, and first-round picks in 2026 and 2027, to the Dallas Cowboys for the services of transcendent edge-rusher Micah Parsons. And when the 2025 season began, it appeared to foster a major improvement in Jeff Hafley's defense. In the first half of the 2025 season, Green Bay's defense ranked 10th in DVOA and Parsons was on a tear. Through Week 15 of the 2025 season, he totaled 14 sacks and 79 pressures, looking every bit the force he had been with Dallas.

Then, in Week 15 against the Denver Broncos, Parsons suffered a torn ACL, and things quickly fell apart for the Pack on Parsons' side of the ball — and overall. The previously 9-3-1 Pack lost every game from then on, and their season ended abruptly with a 31-27 Wild Card playoff loss to the Chicago Bears.

There wasn't enough juice without their defensive epicenter to get things done. The Packers fell to 25th in Defensive DVOA in the second half of the season, and a primary reason was pretty clear.

EPA/play allowed: -0.048 (favorable for the defense)

Pass defense: 367 attempts, 6.29 yards per attempt allowed

Run defense: 273 attempts, 4.13 yards per carry allowed

Without Parsons on the field (469 snaps):

EPA/play allowed: +0.09 (unfavorable for the defense)

Pass defense: 230 attempts, 6.82 yards per attempt allowed

Run defense: 231 attempts, 4.18 yards per carry allowed

"We just need to get healthy," Parsons told me on September 16, three days after the Packers lost their season opener to the Minnesota Vikings, 39-22. "I think once we get healthy, and I'm back on that grass or that turf, we're going to take it to another level."

Thing is, new defensive coordinator Jonathan Gannon couldn't wait around for Parsons to get healthy. The hope was that he'd be ready by Green Bay's Week 6 game against the Cowboys, but that didn't happen. Gannon had to do the best he could with what he had. And the extent to which he's done that is one of the NFL's most impressive stories in the 2026 season.

The Packers currently rank fourth in Defensive DVOA, and Gannon's concepts have been profoundly unsettling to their opponents.

So, how is Gannon doing so well in a new environment, with players he didn't know last season? Everything is working at all three levels.

As you would expect, Gannon is blitzing at a pretty high rate (31.9% this season, 12th-highest in the NFL) to maximize pressure, but Green Bay's pressure rate of 38.7% is only 18th-highest, per SumerSports. Lukas Van Ness leads the team with five sacks and 28 total pressures. Rookie Dani Dennis-Sutton is second with four sacks, and fellow edge-rusher Brenton Cox Jr. is second with 22 pressures. This isn't a Murderer's Row in that regard, but the ways in which Gannon has used his guys up front give those guys an edge.

It's also one reason the Packers have been so effective when they don't blitz.

So, how do you generate effective pressure when your pressure rate isn't that high? In Gannon's case, he's been marvelous with his simulated pressure designs. Showing a blitz look and then having supposed blitzers drop into coverage can create mental pressure for a quarterback when you do it right, and the Packers have done it right more often than not.

Now, imagine Parsons as part of all this skullduggery.

Gannon thought that he'd have linebackers Edgerrin Cooper (season-ending ruptured Achilles tendon) and Zaire Franklin (hairline shin fracture) full-go, but things happen. Now, Isaiah McDuffie and Ty'Ron Hopper are the primary 'backers, and Hopper has been a revelation. He made NFC Secret Superstars for Week 8 after a 27-19 win over the Carolina Panthers in which he totaled one sack, four pressures, six solo tackles, three pass breakups, and an interception. Hopper became the first Packers player since Hall of Famer Charles Woodson in 2009 to record three pass breakups, a sack, and an interception in the same game.

Anytime you can put yourself in the same conversation as Charles Woodson, that's obviously a good thing.

When Gannon runs all of those sim pressures, he needs linebackers who can both threaten pressure and drop into credible coverage. Hopper certainly has it on the ball, and McDuffie has been an effective starter of late.

"Ty'Ron Hopper. I can't say enough great things about him," Matt LaFleur said after the Panthers game. "The night he had. I think, what? Sack, PBU, interception? What a great performance. I think that's something that we did see coming more so in the preseason. I can't say enough about the job that obviously [linebackers coach] Sam Siefkes does with our inside linebackers to get them ready, and I just see a lot of growth from that group. They're playing really well."

They are, and they jibe with the secondary very well.

Safety Evan Williams made Secret Superstars following Green Bay's 23-13 Week 6 win over the Cowboys — the one Micah Parsons wasn't ready for, unfortunately — and that was a game in which Williams, the 2024 fourth-rounder out of Oregon, really put his name out there nationally as one of the NFL's better players at his position(s). This season, Williams has a sack, nine pressures, 49 solo tackles, 13 stops, and two tackles for loss. In coverage, he's allowed 11 catches on 20 targets for 79 yards, one touchdown, two interceptions, four pass breakups, and an opponent passer rating of 41.5.

That's all great, but where Williams really shows up in Gannon's defense is how well he plays to all three levels of the defense. Whether he's starting at the line of scrimmage and backing up to the deep half in coverage, or running the opposite by coming down for a pressure or a run fit, or maintaining the LOS integrity from the box, there isn't much Williams can't do right now.

And when you have one of those players, it allows you to be as multiple as you'd like without the breakdowns you see on defenses where the personnel doesn't quite measure up.

"He's intelligent… I love how Matt says it, 'if you wanna play like a G, you have to prepare like a G', and he studies and when you study the way he does, he knows what's going on," Gannon said of Williams in late October. "Nothing is catching him by surprise… He's playing very well right now."

And now, with all that said… here comes No. 1.

We all know how potentially explosive LaFleur's offense can be, but it's past time to recognize that as the 5-3 Packers progress through the rest of the season, it's the defense that has an equivalent (at least) amount to say regarding how far the team will go.

That's very good news, because Jonathan Gannon has proven to be quite the "G" himself.

Binance, Deribit revoke SignalPlus API keys after fake Teams invite attack

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

SignalPlus said attackers compromised an engineer’s computer in a breach that resulted in losses of less than $82,000, which the crypto trading platform pledged to reimburse in full.

Exchanges Binance and Deribit said Thursday they had revoked API keys connected to crypto derivatives trading platform SignalPlus as a precaution following a security breach. Both exchanges said their own systems were unaffected, while Deribit warned that trading, monitoring and automation through SignalPlus had stopped working for its customers.

Binance co-founder Yi He also urged third-party platforms integrated with the exchange to review their security controls and monitor for suspicious activity, warning that the threat extended beyond any single company.

SignalPlus provides trading infrastructure for crypto derivatives, particularly options, allowing professional and institutional traders to manage positions across multiple exchanges through a single platform.

According to the company on X, the attack originated from a compromised Telegram account belonging to a contact at a third-party technology vendor. The attacker impersonated the contact and sent a fake Microsoft Teams meeting invitation to a SignalPlus engineer, compromising the employee’s computer.

The attacker remained dormant on the compromised computer before using the engineer’s access privileges to target SignalPlus’s internal network early Wednesday, the company said.

SignalPlus said it detected and contained the intrusion, with total losses of less than $82,000, and had begun fully reimbursing affected users. The company temporarily suspended its services and expects to restore operations over the next few days after completing security reviews and applying patches.

SignalPlus also acknowledged that it had not required IP whitelisting for exchange API access. “We take responsibility for this oversight,” the company said, adding that it would tighten API access requirements and strengthen internal security controls. IP whitelisting is a security measure that restricts API access to a predefined list of approved internet addresses.

The SignalPlus incident comes days after Binance disclosed details of a $6.3 billion hack on Oct. 29, which also involved compromised employee computers.

According to Binance’s postmortem, attackers posing as recruiters tricked an engineer into downloading a malicious file disguised as a coding assignment. The attackers subsequently used the compromised computer to gain access to additional systems and eventually breach the exchange’s wallet infrastructure.

Binance co-founder Yi He said Tuesday that the exchange had detected and prevented 57 suspected North Korea-linked cyber threats in 2026, including fraudulent job interviews, malicious software packages and attempts to steal employee credentials.

‘Primetime’ Star Skyler Gisondo On Pushing Beyond His Comfort Zone & Teasing What’s Next For Jimmy Olsen

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

You’d think in the last year, especially given that he’s coming off a successful outing as Jimmy Olsen in James Gunn’s Superman, that Skyler Gisondo would imagine himself as pretty cool. Though, jokingly, by his own account, nothing quite topped appearing in Phoebe Bridgers’ “Lost Boys” music video. “I never felt cooler in my life than being in a Phoebe Bridgers music video,” he tells Deadline, recalling how the appearance prompted texts from people he hadn’t heard from in 15 years, saying, “Dude, I had no idea you were so cool.”

On the day of our conversation, Gisondo is in Florida, perched at a nearby playground beneath a vibrant blue sky. Bright-eyed, handsomely freckle-faced and dressed in colorful attire, he is all smiles and eager to chat. Ironically, it’s a decidedly sunny setting for a conversation about Primetime, Lance Oppenheim’s dark, satirical thriller. There is a familiar eagerness in Dan, the aspiring actor Gisondo portrays, but the similarities largely end there. When Dan takes a job as a decoy for Chris Hansen (Robert Pattinson) on To Catch a Predator, his excitement about the opportunity gives way to an increasingly disturbing reality. Dubbed “Decoy Dan,” he finds himself in the middle of a glossy, exploitative operation that is a far cry from the acting career he imagined for himself. For Gisondo, the role presented its own challenge. He admits that reading the script initially left him questioning whether he could pull it off. “It was the most challenging thing I’ve ever done, and ultimately the most gratifying by far,” he says. Watch on Deadline

A day after our conversation, Gisondo received a Gotham Award nomination for Outstanding Supporting Performance, becoming Primetime’s sole acting nominee. Below, he discusses stepping outside his comfort zone, returning to comedy with Focker-In-Law, and what lies ahead for Jimmy Olsen.

DEADLINE: Help me unravel the timeline. You were in Phoebe Bridgers’ “Lost Boys” music video, directed by Lance Oppenheim. But you had already been in Primetime by that point?

SKYLER GISONDO: Right. The music video was maybe six months after or perhaps even longer. But Primetime happened because Lance had seen some of my work and wanted to talk. I was elated because I had watched Some Kind of Heaven and thought, “This guy is clearly an unbelievably gifted filmmaker.” For Lance to have already achieved such a high level of mastery at such a young age is incredible. It usually takes directors a long time to get there. I had never worked with a director my age. So, we immediately developed a real friendship, and I was in disbelief that he was interested in me for the part. I remember asking him in our first meeting, “Dude, what have you seen of mine that convinced you this would be a good idea? I don’t want to talk myself out of this incredible opportunity, but at the same time…”

Phoebe, though, to my understanding, had never really acted before, but she knew Lance organically and went quite far back. She was amazing, especially given that she had never acted before. In addition to being a generationally talented artist, she’s just the best and such a great hang. We all became friends. When her music video came together, I thought I had never felt cooler in my life than being in a Phoebe Bridgers music video. I had people texting me whom I hadn’t heard from in 15 years, saying, “Dude, I had no idea you were so cool.” It gave me an incredible amount of cred, and it was so much fun.

Thankfully, I hadn’t been overly exposed to her music before the movie because I would’ve been freaking out the entire time. I would’ve been fangirling and wouldn’t have been able to keep it together. Before the music video, I went through her whole discography and, like everyone else, was in awe of her. It was such a fun experience. Lance and our other friend, Pablo [Rochat], directed the video together. I’m still bumping that album [Lost Weekend] nonstop. It’s so good.

I vacillated wildly between thoughts of major imposter syndrome. I’d never done anything like this. Am I capable of it? Do I have this thing in me that he thinks I do? I’m not convinced that I do. But ultimately, those thoughts are a distraction, and you try to shut them out.

DEADLINE: You’ve played a lot of characters who are funny, charming, and usually serve as conduits for the audience. Dan has some of that earnestness before he goes into darker territory. Did you see Primetime as an opportunity to challenge how people perceive you as an actor, particularly after a year that also includes Jimmy Olsen in Superman?

GISONDO: I was really excited by the opportunity to do something I had never done before. I was initially unsure I could do it after reading the script. I’d heard actors I love talk about being drawn to challenging projects, and I almost never understood that. I thought, “Why would you want to show up scared to work every day?”

For the first time, I understood it. A lot of that was Lance instilling an incredible amount of confidence in me from the beginning. He’s the kind of filmmaker you trust. A lot is out of your control as an actor, so it doesn’t really behoove you to overly concern yourself with where everything is going to land or what the perception might be. All you can do is put one foot in front of the other, be present, and look at everything through the lens of your character as you’re reading the script. That’s really all there was. I vacillated wildly between thoughts of major imposter syndrome. I’d never done anything like this. Am I capable of it? Do I have this thing in me that he thinks I do? I’m not convinced that I do. But ultimately, those thoughts are a distraction, and you try to shut them out.

In this case, with Dan specifically, those thoughts were almost helpful in a meta way because there was so much from my own life to draw from. Having started acting when I was so young, I understood that quiet desperation and eagerness Dan has when he first comes into the picture. When you start thinking about the big picture, it’s easy to get overwhelmed. It really is one scene at a time. It’s, “What are we doing today? What is this scene? What is this moment?” That’s why, as an actor, working with the right filmmaker is everything. What Lance and his editor, Dan Garber, ultimately did was take this very small piece that you contribute as an actor and make it part of a much larger puzzle. When it all works, it’s incredible. The whole becomes so much greater than the sum of its parts.

I was surprised, too. Lance has such a distinct visual style, as does his cinematographer, David Bolen. Watching the movie for the first time, I had never seen myself that way. It had never occurred to me that I could exist in that kind of movie or world. It was an immensely formative experience because I found that it was the most challenging thing I’ve ever done, and ultimately the most gratifying by far. When you see a movie work, it really is a magic trick. Everybody has an idea of what they’re doing, but you can’t possibly understand how you fit into the whole until you see it come together. Everyone brings their own thing to it, and then you have a filmmaker like Lance who creates something so exciting.

It really lit a fire under me moving forward. I don’t know that it would’ve been lit otherwise. I know I want to pursue that feeling of uncertainty and fear in the work.

DEADLINE: OK, we have to talk about this friendship dynamic between Troy and Dan. I don’t think Dan needed to apologize to Troy at the end. Troy was helpful in the beginning, but I think as the movie goes on, Troy becomes passive-aggressive or slightly envious. What’s your take on their relationship?

GISONDO: Hm. You know, I think they have a very odd, special relationship. Eric [Rahill] was so unbelievably funny and is just a singular kind of actor. He brought so much to that character; ultimately, a good amount made it into the movie, but a lot of it also didn’t because it was so funny. I don’t think anyone knew where to put it, almost because he could take a scene and go somewhere completely new every time. Should Dan have apologized though? You know what… I think it’s wrong of Dan to come at him. I do think Troy wants what’s best for Dan, and that’s why he’s not as immediately excited about the opportunity as Dan is. He’s trying to make sense of what it is, while Dan is still in a place where he’s ready to compartmentalize and get excited about whatever it is. He has this job; he’s going to be on TV.

His buddy is being a good buddy and saying, “It doesn’t really sound like acting, man. It doesn’t really sound like what I know you want to do.” So I think Dan is in the wrong. Honestly, I hadn’t thought about it, but I think Dan’s in the wrong there.

Parker will have the international break to properly implement his methods, but first he must use the lessons from Cardiff to select a side capable of taking three points into it.

I have already covered the Cardiff match extensively, so there is little value in going over every detail again. What matters for this preview is the enormous contrast between West Ham’s two performances.

I very much detach the three goals conceded in the first half from what happened after the break. The opening 45 minutes looked like a continuation of everything we had witnessed under Nuno Espírito Santo, while the second offered the first glimpse of Parker’s influence.

Parker introduced Ezra Mayers, Mohamadou Kanté and Joël Piroe at half-time before later replacing Manor Solomon with Edvin Austbø. Those decisions gave West Ham greater energy, aggression and bite.. in short, the changes worked an absolute treat.

The substitutions worked, but so did the tactical changes. West Ham pressed higher, attacked space behind Cardiff’s defence and played with an urgency which had been completely absent before the break. As I discussed in my reaction to Parker’s first post-match interview, the new manager was particularly enthusiastic about the more aggressive, front-footed approach.

That should influence Parker’s selection at Blackburn far more than anything he can achieve during two brief training sessions.

Engels & Piroe can both expect to start against Blackburn

If Parker selects the players who drove that comeback and retains the more aggressive approach, I believe West Ham will have too much quality for Blackburn. There may still be another defensive mistake in this team, but I expect the Hammers to win convincingly enough.
