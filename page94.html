Inside Well Beyond Breast Cancer’s Fundraiser to Give Survivors Access to Rehabilitation

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Many don’t realize that breast cancer care doesn’t end with treatment.

Under violet lights, hanging florals, and rattan lanterns, guests at Well Beyond Breast Cancer’s third annual fundraising event mingled, cried happy tears, and came together in New York City to support survivors. Well Beyond Breast Cancer is a nonprofit organization that helps survivors access rehabilitation services after breast cancer treatment. Founder Amanda Grilli, an oncology physical therapist, decided to start the organization just three years ago after moving to the United States from Canada and recognizing a major gap in care.

“I was shocked by the differences in the health care system between the two countries,” Grilli told ELLE. “I quickly realized that the majority of individuals who are affected by breast cancer don’t have access to a therapist like myself because they’re experiencing so many barriers, whether that’s financial toxicity, insurance gaps, or a fragmented health care system. [Many] people aren’t able to access this care on their own.”

For breast cancer survivors, care continues even after surgery, chemotherapy, or radiation end. But rehabilitation is a major piece of breast cancer recovery that often isn’t considered. Grilli says, “So many people experience lingering pain, or they have difficulty lifting their arms. What these therapists are doing is helping people overcome these side effects so that they can get back to living well.”

Guests had the opportunity to hear from one of the organization’s beneficiaries, Allison Berg, an independent documentary filmmaker who was diagnosed with breast cancer in 2025. Even after treatment, her recovery continued. Berg described shooting pain through her arms and fingertips, tightness every time she reached for anything, and tender scars. “My body is part of my equipment,” she shared. “I was having trouble just sitting at a laptop and typing. How was I ever going to carry gear and hold a camera again?” Well Beyond Breast Cancer covered 12 one-hour rehabilitation sessions for Berg, and in September, she was able to shoot for a new film.

Prior to the event yesterday, Well Beyond Breast Cancer had funded 250 breast cancer rehab appointments and allocated more than $50,000 to direct patient care. Last night, at ILA Penthouse in Manhattan, guests were able to participate in a live auction, which is still ongoing and has already raised thousands of dollars. Before they left, guests were also invited to share a wish with the “Keeper of Wishes,” a figurehead with “hopes” pinned to her dress, creating a growing display and fountain of hope that continued throughout the night.

Trump admin orders Kalshi to halt wagers on what words Trump will say

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

CFTC: Kalshi bets may violate US ban on contracts that can be easily manipulated.

The Trump administration ordered prediction market Kalshi to stop offering event contracts that pay out based on whether President Trump will say a specific word before a particular date. Kalshi halted trading in the contracts while the US Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) conducts a review into whether the Trump mention contracts violate federal law.

CNBC reported yesterday that “Kalshi quietly paused trading in a handful of ‘mention’ contracts tied to President Donald Trump at the end of last week at the request of the CFTC.” Kalshi told media outlets that it is complying with the CFTC order.

The markets taken down by Kalshi allowed users to wager on whether Trump would say certain words, including nicknames and the names of countries and companies. A similar expired event contract that can still be viewed on Kalshi’s website asked whether Trump would use the nicknames “Marjorie Traitor Greene,” “Sleepy Joe,” and “Whack Job” or “Wack Job” before April 2026.

The CFTC order issued to Kalshi last week applies broadly to “the TRUMPSAY contract series.” The event contracts in this series include trades in the format, “Will [word] be said by Donald Trump after Issuance and before [date]?” The CFTC order also applied to a specific event contract that asks, “What nicknames will Trump say before 2027?”

The CFTC order came several months after the federal agency settled charges against a Trump teleprompter aide who made over $100,000 in Kalshi wagers on what Trump would say in prepared remarks. On August 28, the CFTC required the man, Gabriel Perez, to disgorge profits of $107,539.02 and pay a civil penalty of $65,000.

“In his position, Perez had access to presidential speeches prior to those speeches being delivered and Perez misappropriated that information—in breach of his duty of trust and confidence—to trade presidential mention market contracts, generating over $107,500 in profits,” the CFTC said at the time.

Senate Democrats recently said the White House hasn’t taken meaningful action against insider trading, “all while the president and his family stand to personally profit from their participation in prediction markets.” Prediction markets have flourished under the Trump administration’s permissive regulatory approach. The CFTC has allowed prediction markets to offer sports gambling nationwide and filed lawsuits against states that attempt to regulate Kalshi under their gambling laws.

Kalshi recently dropped Donald Trump Jr. as an advisor, saying it made the decision because Trump Jr.’s venture capital firm increased its investment in its competitor, Polymarket. Kalshi CEO Tarek Mansour also acknowledged that the “optics” of Trump Jr.’s advisory role weren’t good for Kalshi. Trump Jr. still stands to benefit from Kalshi’s success, because he was given equity shares in the company.

The CFTC has said that mention markets are easily manipulated but reviews them on a case-by-case basis instead of prohibiting them. Prediction markets can “self-certify” that any new event contract complies with US law and start offering it without waiting for the CFTC’s permission, but the CFTC can halt trading afterward based on reviews of specific contracts.

The Commodity Exchange Act (CEA) prohibits the listing of contracts that are readily susceptible to manipulation. CFTC staff issued a September advisory about the legality of markets on what people will say, and whether they will attend an event or interact with another person. The advisory said these contracts may be viewed as “presumptively readily susceptible to manipulation,” because they “may be controlled by a single individual, a small group of individuals, or persons with access to or influence over the individual whose words, attendance, or interaction determines settlement.”

The CFTC said this type of contract requires measures “to prevent manipulation and misappropriation of material non-public information,” and that Kalshi failed to comply with the CFTC staff advisory. About “a week after the Staff Advisory was released, Kalshi listed the Contract [on nicknames] for trading without amending the [TRUMPSAY] Contract Series’ self-certification (or submitting a new self-certification) to make the ‘heightened showing’ of non-manipulability required by the Staff Advisory,” the order said.

The CFTC said it will conduct “a proceeding to determine whether the certification of the Contracts was false—that is, whether the Contracts comply with the Act, including the Core Principles, and Commission regulations thereunder as Kalshi has certified.” So far, “Kalshi has not adequately demonstrated that the Contracts are not readily susceptible to manipulation” as required by law, the order said.

“Given the available supporting evidence, including that Kalshi has not attempted to establish that the Contracts are non-manipulable with respect to the Staff Advisory’s guidance, the Commission presumptively believes the certification is false,” the order said.

The CFTC order is not a determination on whether the Trump mention markets can meet the standard of not being readily susceptible to manipulation. Rather, it is a determination that Kalshi has so far failed to show that the contracts are not easily manipulated.

The order “resolves only that, based on the current record, Kalshi has not demonstrated that the Contracts as certified comply with the Act and that the Contracts should not trade while the Commission completes its review,” the CFTC said.

We contacted Kalshi and will update this article if it provides a response.

Mansour defended the Trump mention contracts at an Axios event on Tuesday, according to Bloomberg. “What the president does say moves markets,” Mansour said. “So predicting what he’s about to say is actually pretty meaningful.”

The Bloomberg article noted that “Kalshi’s main rival Polymarket still has live mention markets tied to Trump’s speeches on its international website, which is not regulated by the CFTC.” Polymarket offers a separate US-regulated version of its prediction market.

Bitcoin Miners Are Selling Their Stacks to Fund the AI Boom

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Bitcoin Miners Are Selling Their Stacks to Fund the AI Boom

Two of the biggest U.S. Bitcoin miners are turning their Bitcoin into AI infrastructure.

MARA sold 13,597 BTC after the end of September at an average price of about $81,039, raising roughly $1.1 billion, according to its third-quarter shareholder letter filed with the SEC.

That’s about 40% of the 34,012 BTC it held at the end of September. Combined with the 26,984 BTC it sold in the first nine months of the year, MARA has sold more than 40,000 BTC in 2026, worth roughly $2.5 billion at today’s prices.

MARA used $750 million of the proceeds to fully repay Bitcoin-backed loans it took out to fund its acquisition of the Long Ridge power plant.

The company said it intends to keep using Bitcoin to raise cash for AI infrastructure, convertible note puts that become exercisable next year and potential share buybacks. Its board has authorized a repurchase of up to $500 million.

MARA insisted the shift is “not a pivot away from bitcoin,” describing mining as its operating foundation while it builds out AI and high-performance computing infrastructure.

Meanwhile, Riot Platforms sold 20,264 BTC in the first nine months of the year for about $1.5 billion, primarily to fund data center development, and ended September with just 2,418 BTC, according to its Nov. 4 results.

The miner also signed a 15-year lease for its entire Corsicana, Texas, campus, covering 910 megawatts of capacity and worth about $28 billion over the initial term. CEO Jason Les called it “the largest lease in Riot’s history.” Riot’s quarterly filing identifies the tenant as Anthropic.

Mining economics help explain the shift. Riot’s Bitcoin mining revenue fell to $116 million in the third quarter from $160.8 million a year earlier, as the average value of each Bitcoin it mined dropped to $70,855 from $114,361.

Including equipment depreciation, Riot said it cost $78,805 to mine each Bitcoin, more than the coin’s average value over the quarter. MARA’s revenue fell 35% to $164.2 million.

The sales add supply to an already fragile market. Onchain analytics firm Glassnode said Tuesday that the two miners drove the recent selling by Bitcoin treasury companies, and that treasury firms outside those two were net buyers in November.

Strategy, the largest corporate holder, kept buying, adding 485 BTC last week to bring its holdings to 850,849 BTC, worth around $54 billion.

The breakthrough came during negotiations for West Ham’s backing for the 2029 World Athletics Championships, which London Stadium bid to host.

The bid required West Ham’s backing, which was secured in August this year, but ultimately the bid failed in September, having lost out to Nairobi.

Ultraprocessed Foods Alter Food-Reward Brain Activity

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

A controlled feeding trial reveals that just two weeks on a diet high in ultraprocessed foods alters brain reward responses in emerging adults ages 18 to 21, while leaving adults ages 22 to 25 unaffected. The fMRI study shows blunted activation in the developing orbitofrontal cortex, a key region for food valuation and self-control, even when diets are strictly matched for calories, sugar, fat, and nutrients.

Ultraprocessed foods, products formulated with industrial processing methods and ingredients rarely found in a home kitchen, account for more than half of the calories consumed by young adults in the United States. While extensive research links these foods to obesity, metabolic syndrome, and cardiovascular disease, neuroscientists are increasingly uncovering how industrial processing directly interacts with the developing human brain.

In a controlled feeding study published this month in the journal Obesity, researchers at Virginia Tech’s Fralin Biomedical Research Institute at VTC and Department of Human Nutrition, Foods, and Exercise found that emerging adults ages 18 to 21 exhibit distinct neural changes in food-reward circuitry after consuming an ultraprocessed diet for just two weeks.

Led by first author Dr. Emma Leslie alongside neuroscientist Dr. Alex DiFeliceantonio and registered dietitian Dr. Brenda Davy, the findings complement earlier results from the same trial showing that 18- to 21-year-olds consumed excess calories at a buffet meal after eating ultraprocessed foods, even when they were no longer hungry.

To separate the effects of industrial food processing from basic nutritional content, the researchers recruited 22 healthy adults ages 18 to 25 (including seven participants ages 21 or younger) for a randomized crossover feeding trial. Each participant followed two strictly controlled diets for two weeks apiece, separated by a four-week washout period:

Critically, both diets were engineered to have identical macronutrient profiles (50% carbohydrates, 35% fat, 15% protein), matching levels of fiber, added sugars, saturated fat, sodium, energy density, and overall nutritional quality, calibrated to keep each participant’s body weight stable.

Before and after each two-week dietary phase, participants underwent functional MRI (fMRI) brain scans while receiving small sips of an ultraprocessed milkshake or a tasteless control solution through a specialized delivery tube.

When the researchers analyzed the neuroimaging data, they discovered a striking developmental divide:

“Among the younger participants, consuming a diet high in ultraprocessed foods for just two weeks was associated with decreased brain response to a rewarding food — a milkshake — in the orbitofrontal cortex,” explained Leslie, who conducted the work as a doctoral student at Virginia Tech and is now a postdoctoral associate at the University of Michigan.

Located just behind the eyes, the orbitofrontal cortex is one of the final regions of the human brain to finish maturing, continuing to remodel into the mid- to late 20s. It plays a central role in evaluating food rewards, regulating cravings, and exerting executive self-control to stop eating.

Because the two diets were matched across nutritional metrics, the findings point toward non-nutritive properties of ultraprocessed foods, such as chemical additives, emulsifiers, texture alterations, or compounds generated during industrial processing, as potential drivers of altered brain signaling.

“This study is important because you have this change in an area of the brain known to govern self-control and potentially put a stop to eating,” said DiFeliceantonio, associate professor at the Fralin Biomedical Research Institute and interim co-director of its Center for Health Behaviors Research. “This is giving us insight into how ultraprocessed foods are affecting the bodies and brains of younger people, who might be especially vulnerable to their effects. And that’s important to know for things like dietary guidelines, doctor recommendations, and policy decisions.”

While the researchers note that the sample size was small and the age-based findings were exploratory, and did not directly correlate individual fMRI shifts with buffet calorie intake, the results highlight adolescence and early adulthood as critical windows of neurodevelopmental sensitivity.

Next, DiFeliceantonio and Davy plan to investigate whether adolescents under 18, who consume the highest proportion of ultraprocessed foods of any U.S. demographic, show even stronger neural sensitivity, and whether brain responses recover once individuals return to whole-food diets.

How do ultraprocessed foods affect the brains of young adults?

A Virginia Tech study found that after eating a diet high in ultraprocessed foods for just two weeks, young adults ages 18 to 21 showed decreased brain activity in the orbitofrontal cortex, a region involved in food reward, valuation, and self-control, when tasting a milkshake. Adults ages 22 to 25 did not show these brain changes.

Was the brain effect simply caused by extra sugar, fat, or calories?

No. Both the ultraprocessed diet and the unprocessed comparison diet contained the exact same amounts of calories, carbohydrates, fat, protein, added sugars, saturated fat, fiber, and sodium. This suggests that industrial food processing or additives themselves, rather than basic nutrients, drove the brain changes.

Why are 18- to 21-year-olds more sensitive to ultraprocessed foods than slightly older adults?

The orbitofrontal cortex is one of the last brain regions to finish developing, continuing to mature into the mid- to late 20s. Because this self-control and reward-evaluation circuit is still actively wiring in 18- to 21-year-olds, it appears more sensitive to dietary shifts.

We investigated if a high-UPF diet alters brain response to milkshake compared with a diet free from UPF in emerging adults, who are in a critical brain development period and typically consume high amounts of UPF.

In a randomized controlled crossover trial participants aged 18–25 years completed two 2-week controlled feeding periods including a UPF (81% UPF) and non-UPF (0% UPF) diet. Before and after each diet intervention, participants consumed milkshake concomitant with functional magnetic resonance imaging.

In the entire cohort, there were no differences between diet conditions in brain response. An exploratory analysis revealed orbitofrontal cortex response to milkshake decreased after the UPF diet and increased following the non-UPF diet in late adolescents (18–21 years) but not young adults (22–25 years). In all participants, habitual UPF intake (g) was positively associated with orbitofrontal cortex response to milkshake, independent of diet condition.

An acute UPF dietary intervention may only alter brain response in late adolescents. Further work is needed to determine potential vulnerability of late adolescents to changes in dietary UPF on brain response to rewards.

A judge in Michigan was found guilty Monday of drunk driving after he rear-ended a public transit bus, but a district court judge ruled that prosecutors failed to prove beyond a reasonable doubt that his blood alcohol level met the state’s threshold for intoxication. (Image from Shutterstock)

A judge in Michigan was found guilty Monday of drunk driving after he rear-ended a public transit bus, but a district court judge ruled that prosecutors failed to prove beyond a reasonable doubt that his blood alcohol level met the state’s threshold for intoxication.

Warren 37th District Court Chief Judge John M. Chmura, 63, was found guilty of intoxication while driving his Ford Fusion into a parked bus that was displaying hazard lights March 25. The court rejected the charge of Chmura driving with an elevated high blood alcohol content of 0.17 or greater, according to coverage on Law360.

Novi 52nd District Court Judge David Law noted that Chmura’s judgment had been affected, but he questioned the blood alcohol concentration at the time of the crash.

“This court may think it knows the answer to whether or not this was a high BAC case,” Law said. “But thinking it knows is not enough. It needs to be proven beyond a reasonable doubt.”

A bus driver sitting inside the bus waiting to resume his route was uninjured in the crash. Sterling Heights, Michigan, police officer Jonathan Journeau testified that Chmura smelled of alcohol, slurred his speech and couldn’t maintain his balance.

Chmura’s attorney, David J. Kramer of the David J. Kramer Law Firm, argued that prosecutors could not establish the higher offense because Journeau failed to determine when the judge’s final alcoholic beverage was consumed.

Would Ukraine trade land for NATO membership or protection?

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

The US is exploring whether Ukraine would cede parts of Donetsk province still under its control in return for NATO membership or protection. Experts warn the idea is risky.

The US has asked Ukraine whether it would be willing to cede Donetsk province to Russia in return for NATO membership. Together, Donetsk and Luhansk constitute the so-called Donbas region. Russia currently controls most of Luhansk province, and large swathes of Donetsk, though Ukraine continues holding a number of major cities there, including Kramatorsk and Sloviansk.

So far, no concrete proposal had been put forward, according to Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy. He said he would publicly discuss the matter once the US and NATO member states had agreed on the plan, stressing that he could not make any such decision "on his own." Zelenskyy also said any potential agreement would go ahead "without recognizing Russia's occupation of these territories."

The Ukrainian leader said the US was talking about guarantees similar to those enshrined in Articles 4 and 5 of the North Atlantic Treaty, though did not envision US troops on Ukrainian soil. Article 4 provides for consultations among member states in the event of imminent threats, while Article 5 stipulates that an attack on one state is considered an attack on all. The US, however, has said it pictures bilateral guarantees for Ukraine similar to those established with Israel and certain Middle Eastern countries.

Russia has yet to comment on these proposals, with President Putin's spokesperson Dmitry Peskov stating that "we maintain our position that, in the interest of the peace process, it is not appropriate to disclose details of the talks between us and the Americans."

Experts interviewed by DW are skeptical of the land-for-security-guarantees idea, objecting that the US cannot uphold such an arrangement on its own.

"It seems to me that this is what happens to normal diplomacy when it's been run by real estate agents who are used to selling people something that doesn't really exist," Oleksandr Kraiev of the North America-Israel Research Program at Ukraine's Ukrainian Prism think tank tells DW. He says that at least half of NATO's member states have reservations about Ukraine's accession.

"We'll just end up with a second Budapest Memorandum, it'll just carry a different name," Kraiev told DW. The original 1994 Budapest Memorandum stipulated that Russia, the US and UK respect Ukraine's independence and sovereignty in its existing borders and refrain from the threat or use of force to change its territorial integrity. Ukraine in return committed to giving up all nuclear weapons.

The Budapest Memorandum, however, did not contain any commitment by the UK or US to defend Ukraine in the event of an attack.

Most experts interviewed by DW are critical of the idea of holding a referendum to let ordinary Ukrainians decide whether their country should hand over full control of Donetsk province in exchange for NATO membership.

This is because ceding territory would not only violate Ukraine's constitution and harm its national interests, but would also undermine a world order "in which borders cannot be redrawn by force," Oleksandr Khara of Ukraine's Centre for Defence Strategies told DW. "It doesn't matter whether the citizens of Ukraine vote for or against such a proposal," Khara said, adding that any such referendum would set "a very dangerous precedent."

A survey by the Kyiv International Institute of Sociology (KISS) conducted between October 8 and 16 found that 61% of respondents categorically reject withdrawing troops from Ukrainian-controlled parts of the Donbas, such as cities like Kramatorsk and Sloviansk, even in exchange for US and European security guarantees. 35% of respondents said they would begrudgingly accept a land-for-security deal.

Defense analyst Khara doubts a bilateral agreement between Ukraine and the US, akin to those established between the US and Israel, would guarantee Kyiv any more support than it presently receives.

"This concerns financial support, joint weapons production and development, and the training of Ukrainian troops — everything that falls below the threshold of collective security," Khara told DW. "It is good that these commitments — security agreements, political declarations, and the allocation of funds in the budgets of the countries supporting us — are set out in writing, at least for the next five years." Yet to prevent another war, Khara believes that ideally, Ukraine needs "a nuclear shield," specifically, a "strategic one from the US" and a "tactical one from Europe."

Ukrainian experts already floated to the idea of an alternative security arrangement to NATO members in December 2023, says Sergiy Solodkyy of Ukraine's New Europe research center. The arrangement would see Ukraine join the alliance within its internationally recognized borders, though specific protocols would stipulate that only territories under Kyiv's control would be defended.

This arrangement could be modeled on the German case, where West Germany became a member of NATO first, while East Germany joined the alliance only after German reunification.

West Germany never gave up on reunification, Solodkyy told DW. "That means we, too, should not give up on our territory or recognize the border." That is why he says the land-for-security deal proposed to Ukraine is unacceptable.

"This is a trap that Russia and the US negotiators are trying to lure the Ukrainian side into," Solodkyy told DW.
