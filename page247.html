Servono risposte. Tresoldi, Endrick, Inacio: bene, ma il terzino sinistro? Chi farà il mercato di gennaio?

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

È già tempo di mercato. Un po' perché gennaio è alle porte, un po' perché Cardinale ha promesso interventi, un po' (e soprattutto) perché qualche aggiustamento è indispensabile. Nella sessione invernale, di norma, si migliorano le rose, più difficilmente la prima squadra, ma qualche volta le eccezioni cambiano la storia: si tratta di capire bene cosa serve e muoversi di conseguenza. Questo è il momento di mettere pezze salde, di avere qualche freccia in più all'arco, ma ci sono latenze ataviche da colmare. Quella del terzino sinistro è quella più datata: il dopo Theo è un calvario, il Milan è arrivato alla vigilia della seconda sosta schierando Musah in quel ruolo. È evidente che ormai Amorim consideri Bartesaghi un esterno d'attacco e piano piano (difficile capire come mai non più rapidamente...) stia prendendo atto che Estupinian non è all'altezza.

Dunque, nessuna obiezione per gli sbandierati obiettivi come Tresoldi, Endrick o Inacio se sono funzionali a sistemare i buchi, se è l'allenatore che li chiede, se possono dare una mano. Bene anche un esterno destro, come si legge da qualche parte, posto che Athekame sta facendo bene a Lione e non sarà facile riportarselo a casa dopo l'Epifania. Queste settimane che separano dal mercato devono schiarire le idee al tecnico anche su Terracciano, Comotto, Camarda: il primo sembra godere di maggior considerazione rispetto agli altri due, con nostra grande sorpresa. Chiaro comunque che anche a centrocampo, stante le prolungate assenze di Loftus-Cheek, Chukwueze e Saelemaekers, va operato qualche innesto.

La questione più urgente è però chi farà il mercato. Il nuovo responsabile dell'area sportiva sarà dunque Marcus Krosche, il quale avrebbe intenzione di arrivare accompagnato dal direttore sportivo Timmo Hardung, la cui posizione è però letteralmente blindata dall'Eintracht Francoforte. Hardung - come lo stesso Krosche del resto - ha mansioni e ruoli più circolari rispetto alla qualifica tradizionale: nel club tedesco è anche responsabile del comparto medico e del settore giovanile, ed è considerato una figura centrale cui è difficile rinunciare. Krosche queste cose le sa perfettamente, avrà in serbo un piano alternativo. Ben sapendo che bisogna cominciare a muoversi adesso per arrivare dopo le Feste preparati e pronti ad agire. Lo scorso mese di gennaio fu deleterio per il Milan: da una parte Furlani e Moncada, dall'altra Tare a inseguire - ognuno per conto proprio - il centravanti, per arrivare a Fullkrug che a Brema sta dimostrando come tanto male non sia, ma si ruppe un dito del piede dopo pochi giorni a Milanello e buonanotte.

Al di là dei proclami e degli slogan, l'obiettivo è restare agganciati alla locomotiva in campionato e, per quanto possibile, provare a recuperare in Europa League: per il primo traguardo c'è la possibilità di lavorare per migliorare, per il secondo il ritardo è già grave. Bisogna fare bene e alla svelta, tutti. E su tutti i fronti.

Un pensiero finale per Toni Capuozzo, grande milanista, splendido giornalista, meravigliosa persona. Raccontaci da lassù.

A coffee shop latte costs about $5, and buying one most workdays adds up to more than $1,000 a year. At home, the same drink costs about 70 cents. Make most cups yourself with a setup that pays for itself within two months, and keep a café visit or two a week as the treat.

That $5 is the typical price of a 12-ounce latte at independent coffee shops, according to the fall 2026 Coffee Price Index by Fresh Cup. Buy one five workdays a week for 50 weeks and that’s $1,250 a year, before tax and tip.

Making the latte yourself costs a fraction of that. Ground coffee averaged $9.25 a pound in October 2026, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics. At the weakest strength the National Coffee Association suggests for a French press, a 12-ounce mug takes about 22 grams of coffee, roughly 45 cents’ worth. Brew those 22 grams with less water for a strong base, add 8 ounces of whole milk at the bureau’s average of $4.10 a gallon, and a latte costs about 70 cents.

Pricier beans don’t close the gap. At $15 a pound, that latte costs about $1, still about $4 less than the café’s $5. Plain drip has a gap too, $3.25 for 12 ounces in those shops against about 45 cents at home.

The switch lasts only if you like the home cup, so spend on beans you enjoy before you spend on gear. A drip coffee maker or French press handles plain coffee. For lattes, pair a handheld milk frother with a moka pot, a stovetop brewer that makes strong, espresso-style coffee. Add a travel mug for the commute, plus a grinder if you buy whole beans, and keep the total near $100. At about $4.30 saved per homemade latte, that setup pays for itself in about 23 cups, six to eight weeks at three or four homemade lattes a week.

Judge any upgrade the same way. A $500 espresso machine takes about 117 homemade lattes to break even.

Keep the café for the visits you’d miss most. Brew at home three or four days a week, buy a latte on the others, and the year’s coffee costs about $390 to $610 instead of $1,250.

TL;DR: Bottomline has completed live USDC transfers inside its enterprise payments platform. The initial transfer was between company entities.

Bottomline disclosed the cross-border transactions in a November 10 announcement, using its Global Payments Hub and Cash Management platform.

The practical point is where the payment fits. A finance team may be more interested in approving, tracking and reconciling a transfer within its existing systems than in opening a separate crypto application to make it happen.

Bottomline describes the milestone as an intra-company transfer. That establishes that the company used the integration for a live movement of funds; it does not establish that unrelated businesses are already using it at scale.

The release says the aim is to let treasury teams handle stablecoin payments alongside traditional methods, retaining familiar approvals, controls and audit processes. It presents near-real-time settlement for selected scenarios, especially cross-border payments, as part of the attraction.

The company has not supplied a transaction-by-transaction performance dataset in this announcement. Readers therefore cannot use it to calculate an average fee saving or a reliable improvement in end-to-end delivery time.

Bottomline’s cash-management product description emphasises connecting treasury, accounting, compliance, banking and enterprise resource planning data. It describes tools for cash visibility, reconciliation and reporting, helping place the USDC announcement within the wider product.

Consider a group with businesses in two countries. Moving value between them is only one part of the job. Staff may also need to identify the correct entities, record the purpose of the transfer, obtain approval and match the completed payment to the accounts.

If a stablecoin route requires those steps to be reconstructed manually outside the normal workflow, a fast blockchain transfer can still leave an expensive administrative task. Integration is useful when it keeps the financial record attached to the movement of money.

NewsBTC explored a related implementation issue in Circle and ACI’s Latin American settlement collaboration, which examined how a digital-dollar option fits within existing payment operations.

Circle describes USDC as a dollar-redeemable token backed by liquid reserves. That explains the asset being transferred, but a corporate payment can involve more than the onchain movement itself.

A business funding a transfer from a bank account needs an entry route. A recipient that pays suppliers in local currency may need conversion and a bank payout. Each stage can affect the amount received and the time before it can be spent.

For that reason, the useful comparison is between two complete payment routes serving the same need. A low network charge does not settle the comparison if conversion costs are higher, or if an exception requires substantial staff time to resolve.

The next useful disclosures would identify supported use cases, the businesses choosing them and performance over repeated transfers. Clear handling of failures, rejected payments and reconciliation exceptions would be particularly informative.

There is also a difference between a first transaction and a mature operating metric, as NewsBTC’s analysis of Bitso Business payment volumes explains. Neither measure should be mistaken for fee revenue or universal customer availability.

Bottomline has demonstrated a concrete integration step. Its commercial value will come from making a recurring treasury task easier to complete and account for, with enough transparency for finance teams to compare the new route with the options they already trust.

In reversal, American Cancer Society urges prostate cancer tests for all men

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Men aged 50–69 and at average risk should get PSA screening every two years.

In a significant update to its guidelines for prostate cancer screening, the American Cancer Society is now recommending that all men get routinely screened for prostate cancer with a PSA test.

Men at average risk should get a PSA test every two years from age 50 to 69, according to the new guidelines released Thursday.

Men considered at high risk due to race and family history should start testing earlier, at 45, and most are recommended to get tested every year. Specifically, yearly testing starting at age 45 is recommended for all Black men, who have higher rates of prostate cancer. Non-Black men should also get yearly testing starting at 45 if a close relative was diagnosed with prostate cancer before age 65, or if more than one of their relatives has had prostate cancer. On the other hand, if only one close relative was diagnosed with prostate cancer and it was diagnosed at age 65 or older, non-Black men in this case should start PSA screening at 45, but are recommended to get testing only every two years.

Men ages 70 to 74 should consult their doctor on continuing screening, while those 75 and up are not recommended to get screened.

The new guidance departs from ACS’s previous iteration, published in 2010, which made no universal recommendations for routine screening. Men at age 50 were recommended to use shared-decision making with their doctors on whether or not to get tested. The recommendation was similar to that from the US Preventive Services Task Force, a federal panel that regularly evaluates evidence and makes recommendations on such preventive medical care. (The USPSTF is currently working on an update to its prostate cancer screening recommendation, though it should be noted that the panel has recently been overhauled by anti-vaccine Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. and new members are reportedly rife with conflicts of interest.)

In announcing the guidelines today, the ACS says its updates are based on new evidence since 2010 that shows screening saves lives, and that advances in case management can avoid the concerning harms from overdiagnosis and overtreatment. For many men with prostate cancer, their cancer will never grow or cause harm in their lifetime, while treatment can run the risk of erectile dysfunction and urinary incontinence.

In the new guidance, the ACS adds guardrails, firstly advising all men to be informed about the risks and benefits. If a PSA test comes back high—defined as 3 ng/mL or higher for men under 70 and 6.5 ng/mL or higher for those 70 and up—the ACS recommends repeating the test before taking any next steps.

If repeat testing is still high, then men should get either a prostate MRI or a validated biomarker test to assess risk. Only if there is a high suspicion of cancer—such as having a very high PSA test result—should men jump to a prostate biopsy.

For men diagnosed with prostate cancer, active surveillance should be the initial management strategy for men whose cancer appears to be at low risk of growing or spreading.

“By catching aggressive cancers with a simple PSA blood test, you open the door to highly effective treatments that protect your future,” Dr. Shanthi Sivendran, interim chief patient officer for the ACS, said in a statement. “Meanwhile, lower-risk findings can be safely managed through careful, active surveillance.”

Prostate cancer is one of the most common cancers in men and the second leading cause of cancer death in US men, the ACS notes. Nearly 334,00 men are estimated to be diagnosed with prostate cancer in 2026, and it’s estimated that over 36,320 will die from the disease.
