Why database touch plans fail real estate agents

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Despite 91% of buyers saying they would reuse or recommend their agent, NAR data shows most repeat clients hire differently, with only 18% returning to a prior agent. The article proposes replacing high volume touches with intentional conversations across a 150 person network.  AI Summary

Agents are consistently trained to do their touches. “Have you done all your touches?” “Have you done your outreach?” “Are you touching people?” “Are you sending things of value?” That antiquated philosophy needs to stop and almost nobody in this industry cares to address it or examine why it fails. It is wildly ineffective, and I would argue that most of the time it hurts the agent’s brand rather than helping it.

If I walked up to your front door 32 times a year, rang the bell, and said, “Just a reminder that I’m in real estate,” how long would it take you to look into a restraining order? Nobody would call that a relationship. Yet the same schedule applies when it arrives as an email one week and a postcard the next.

Agents are constantly told they need to remind people that they are in real estate. That is an interaction about what the agent cares about, not what the other person cares about. Trust isn’t built by constantly telling someone how amazing I am, why they need me, or why I’m relevant or important. That is blatant narcissism, and yet somehow we call it great top-of-mind marketing.

The National Association of Realtors’ 2025 Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers shows the problem. Ninety-one percent of buyers said they would use their agent again or recommend them. But among repeat buyers, only 18% went back to an agent they had worked with before, and among sellers the figure was 29%. The touches are supposed to close that gap.

The industry explains that clients forget, so its answer has always been more reminders—the rest of the survey points somewhere else. 80% of sellers contacted only one agent before hiring, and 66% hired someone referred to them or someone they had used before. Sellers are not comparing the agents who emailed or mailed them the most. They call the one person they trust. Seeing your name in their inbox every month does not make you that person.

One of the agents I coach has phenomenal email automation, with reminders and alerts set up for everyone they have ever worked with. By their own count, 90% of their business comes from less than 5% of the people in their database. If 90% of your business comes from less than 5% of those people, why are you digitally harassing the other 95%?

In 2003, researchers Russell Hill and Robin Dunbar measured how many people we keep up with by using the most ordinary list there is, the one for Christmas cards. Participants logged every card they sent and everyone in the household it went to. Networks averaged about 150 people, and participants stayed in touch weekly with the people they felt closest to.

A later study by Sam Roberts and Dunbar followed people for 18 months. Friendships lost closeness when contact dropped off, while family relationships were far less affected. Contact matters, and the touch plans are right about that much. They go wrong by treating a Christmas card to the whole database as the same act as staying close to 150. A mailing treats everyone alike, so the people who matter most get the same thing as the strangers, and the relationships that produce business fade.

Pick your 150. Write down the people you would be glad to call and stop when you reach that number. They will be past clients and friends whose lives you know something about. Dunbar’s later research found an inner circle of about 15 inside the 150. Give those 15 their own rhythm, a conversation with each of them every month, because in my experience that inner circle is where most referrals come from. A database you can’t keep up with starts to feel like a weight. A list of 150 is small enough that you notice when one of them is about to move.

Reconnect at four months. A conversation creates what I call mind share: the trust that comes from talking with someone. Name recognition never builds it, and without the next conversation it fades. In my coaching, I see it run low about three to four months after the last conversation. Once a week, scroll back about four months in your text messages, pick three people, and send something like “Hey, was just thinking about you. Hope all is well; would love to catch up.” Leave real estate out of it. Anyone in your text messages already has a relationship with you that a mailing list never will, so the message reaches someone who trusts you at the point where that trust is lowest. Keep it to three. Send 30, have 10 people answer and your afternoon is gone, which is the fastest way to make sure you never do it again.

If you lead agents, ask how many conversations they had with their 150 this month, not how many touches they made. That number tells you whose relationships are fading before the referrals stop. We keep overcomplicating this business by pushing agents to do more and more, which only creates overwhelm and makes them more likely to do less. We owe them better.

Warren Buffett donates $5.5 billion, says kindness 'compounds' in Thanksgiving letter

📅 2026-11-16  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Billionaire Warren Buffett donated Berkshire Hathaway shares worth about $5.5 billion to four philanthropic foundations, stepping up his annual charitable giving from the prior year.

Buffett, 96, converted 6,150 Class A Berkshire shares into 9,225,000 Class B shares and gave 3,690,000 of those shares to The Susan Thompson Buffett Foundation, and 1,845,000 shares each to The Sherwood Foundation, The Howard G. Buffett Foundation and NoVo Foundation — the foundations run by his three children, Susie, Howard and Peter Buffett.

Following the donations, the chairman emeritus of Berkshire still owns 182,140 Class A shares — roughly $162 billion.

"Last Thanksgiving I wrote that kindness is costless but also priceless. A year later, I would only add that it compounds, and at a better rate than anything Charlie and I ever bought," wrote Buffett, who was succeeded as chairman in September by his son Howard, in his annual Thanksgiving message.

The letter was much shorter than his message last Thanksgiving. Charlie Munger, former vice chairman of Berkshire, passed away in November 2023.

Berkshire shares have been on a tear recently, up more than 19% since the start of the fourth quarter and ending last week with four consecutive record high closes. That compares with the S&P 500's decline of 1.2% so far for the fourth quarter. Berkshire has been seen as a safe haven as investors have worried about the artificial intelligence buildout, corporate drama and geopolitical risk.

The shares are now outperforming the S&P 500 for the year by about 7.5 percentage points.

In his letter, Buffett reiterated that all of his Berkshire shares eventually will be donated to the four foundations, with the goal being to increase the size of the grants each year.

Buffett wrote that the gifts were a signal of his confidence not only in his three children, but also in Berkshire CEO Greg Abel:

"My children have given thoughtfully to worthy causes for decades, and I trust 100% both their abilities and their fidelity. ... You heard plenty from me in September about my confidence in Berkshire. That confidence is as strong as ever," he said.

Referring to Munger and himself, Buffett added, "Both of us received beyond measurement costless benefits from a few teachers such as Ben Graham, Joe Rosenfield and Tom Murphy. Because of this we not only prospered mightily but were able to pass along many of the benefits we received."

"Warren continues to share his wisdom with us all during this holiday season. His example reflects the kindness he encourages in all of us," said CEO Abel of the letter.

Buffett closes his message with this advice: "This Thanksgiving, thank one of those selfless people in your own life. Better yet, be a benefactor for someone else."

NVIDIA, IBM and Fermilab Plan Modular Quantum Computing Platform

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

NVIDIA said in a November 16, 2026 announcement that it plans to develop a modular quantum-classical co-design platform with IBM and Fermi National Accelerator Laboratory, and that its newly released GPU-accelerated error-correction decoder achieved up to a 13,000-times speedup in a specific benchmark comparison.

Fermilab describes the initiative as a proposal rather than an operating facility. Its initial design would physically co-locate IBM quantum computers with NVIDIA DGX B200 accelerated-computing systems, including computers powered by IBM Quantum Nighthawk. Researchers would develop and evaluate error-correction codes, decoders and quantum-classical applications while studying how classical systems scale alongside multiple linked quantum processors.

IBM describes Nighthawk as its most powerful quantum processor, with support for circuits of up to 7,500 two-qubit operations on 120 qubits and speeds of up to 100,000 samples per second.

The scientific program would concentrate on high-energy and nuclear physics. Fermilab would lead engagement with researchers and coordinate software workflows and access through HEPCloud, while the partners develop algorithms, shared software and benchmarks for lattice gauge theories, neutrino interactions and nuclear simulations. Those problems would be used to translate scientific needs into requirements for future fault-tolerant quantum systems.

Modularity is intended to connect processors for calculations too demanding for a single device. Researchers would first model and emulate communication between modules, studying link errors, latency and feedback to guide work by IBM and Fermilab’s Superconducting Quantum Materials and Systems (SQMS) Center on physical interconnects. In a later phase, SQMS intends to integrate ultracoherent superconducting cavities and investigate their use as quantum memories, entanglement buffers and resources for bosonic error correction.

Fermilab chief technology officer and SQMS director Anna Grassellino said the initiative would help establish the capabilities and research community needed for a future Department of Energy quantum computing user facility.

Available separately through CUDA-Q QEC 0.9.0, TCBP-JOBS addresses the classical side of quantum error correction. Error correction encodes quantum information across multiple physical qubits; a decoder interprets measurement data to identify likely errors so they can be corrected. NVIDIA says the algorithm reduced one research computation from 12 weeks to 45 minutes, accelerating the evaluation of candidate codes and decoder configurations.

The NVIDIA Research abstract specifies that the 13,000-times result was measured against unsparsified long-beam Tesseract on bivariate-bicycle codes, at logical-error-rate ratios of 0.97 to 1.57. Against Tesseract’s sparsified presets, reported serial speedups ranged from 10 to 7,400 times. Tests covered surface, color and bivariate-bicycle code memory and surgery circuits; the results do not establish a uniform speedup across decoders or workloads.

The method combines trajectory-calibrated belief propagation, which learns calibrated fault probabilities from belief-propagation trajectories, with jittered OSD beam search, which uses those probabilities to guide parallel GPU searches. NVIDIA Research reports logical error rates comparable to Tesseract and up to 4.5 times lower on the largest codes, batched execution for offline simulation, and millisecond-scale latency in serial memory and logical-surgery experiments for QPUs with longer logical clock cycles.

A related integration changes how supercomputing centers allocate quantum hardware. Slurm previously had no native concept of a quantum processing unit (QPU), so sites attached QPUs through plugins outside the scheduler’s core resource model. IBM, NVIDIA and Pasqal worked with the open-source community on the Quantum Resource Management Interface (QRMI), a vendor-neutral layer that connects to Slurm Hierarchical Resources.

Unlike GPUs, which operate within individual computing nodes, QPUs can be shared across multiple nodes. Hierarchical Resources lets administrators manage that shared resource, allocate and reserve QPU time, and account for it under familiar high-performance-computing policies. A hybrid workload can therefore run as a normal Slurm job without a separate queue or login.

NVIDIA’s Slurm announcement says support for the QRMI and QDMI quantum-HPC interfaces will let QPU builders reuse one integration across deployments rather than create bespoke software for every QPU-center combination. The adopters listed for the expansion are Alice & Bob, IBM, IQM, Pasqal, Quantinuum and MQSC, and Slurm 26.11 is targeted for availability later in November 2026.

Together, the proposed platform and software releases address three distinct constraints: evaluating error correction, scheduling shared hardware and connecting processors. The first interconnect studies are the central hardware test, because their measurements of link errors, latency and feedback are intended to establish what later fault-tolerant, multi-processor systems must handle before Fermilab adds its cavity hardware.

New Guidelines Recommend Routine Prostate Cancer Screening for Men Over 50

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Follow Healthline on our socials for all things health.

As rates of advanced prostate cancer continue to rise, the American Cancer Society (ACS) has issued new guidelines recommending routine prostate cancer screening for many men starting at age 50.

The updated recommendations, published November 12 in CA: A Cancer Journal for Clinicians, mark a shift from the organization’s previous emphasis on shared decision making about whether to undergo screening.

Under the new guidelines, the ACS recommends regular prostate-specific antigen (PSA) testing beginning at age 50 for men at average risk of prostate cancer and age 45 for those at higher risk.

Advances in MRI technology and biomarker testing may also help doctors determine which men need further testing, potentially reducing unnecessary biopsies and overtreatment.

The ACS last updated its prostate cancer screening guidelines in 2010, when conflicting research findings raised questions about whether the benefits of PSA screening outweighed the potential harms.

At the time, the organization emphasized informed, shared decision making between patients and healthcare professionals.

However, longer-term follow-up studies have since provided stronger evidence of the benefits of early detection, prompting the ACS to conduct a systematic review and update its recommendations.

The authors of the report noted, “In making these recommendations, we recognize that PSA testing is only the first step in a screening cascade that includes secondary testing of men with an elevated PSA level and treatment decisions for men diagnosed with prostate cancer.”

The new guidelines recommend a PSA test every 2 years for men ages 50–69 with an average risk of prostate cancer.

The recommendation for those at high risk is a PSA test every year starting at age 45.

For men ages 70–74, the ACS recommends individualized decision making. If an individual chooses to be screened, the ACS recommends a PSA test every 2 years until the age of 74.

Ramkishen Narayanan, MD, Director of the Center for Urologic Health at The Roy and Patricia Disney Family Cancer Center at Providence Saint Joseph Medical Center in Burbank, CA, who wasn’t involved in the report, told Healthline that screening shouldn’t automatically be ruled out for men over 75.

“[A] point worth noting is that for men over 75, their PSA history as well as their individual clinical scenarios should be evaluated, and that PSA should not be discouraged in this group of men if their life expectancy and clinical history warrant a PSA check.”

According to the ACS, approximately 1 in 8 men will be diagnosed with prostate cancer during their lifetime.

Prostate cancer diagnoses in the United States increased by around 3% per year between 2014 and 2022, according to the ACS.

The rise in diagnoses, particularly advanced-stage prostate cancer, has renewed attention on the importance of regular screening.

“[The] current state of prostate cancer screening guidelines is vague and generalized. This has led to ambiguity, disagreement, and variation of opinion among healthcare providers and institutions in their approach to men’s health,” said S. Adam Ramin, MD, board certified urologist, urologic oncologist, and medical director of Urology Cancer Specialists in Los Angeles, CA, who wasn’t involved in the report.

The updated ACS guidelines drew partly on findings from a 2025 follow-up of the European Randomized Study of Screening for Prostate Cancer (ERSPC), which found that regular PSA screening was associated with a 13% reduction in prostate cancer deaths over 23 years.

A 12-year ERSPC follow-up in 2012 also suggested that regular screening was associated with a decrease in metastatic prostate cancer diagnosis, which is another finding that influenced the updated guidance.

“Prostate cancer can take many years, even decades, to become clinically significant, so it takes a long time for us to see the consequences of changes in screening practices,” Taylor Goodstein, MD, assistant professor of urology in the College of Medicine at the University of Cincinnati, who wasn’t involved in the report, told Healthline.

“With longer follow-up from randomized trials, we now have more convincing evidence that PSA screening reduces the risk of dying from prostate cancer,” she said.

“The ACS guidelines are important now because they drive home the most important point that prostate cancer screening saves lives,” said Narayanan.

Healthcare professionals use PSA levels to help determine whether additional testing for prostate cancer may be necessary.

The ACS recommends further testing for men under 70 with PSA levels of 3 ng/mL or higher.

For those ages 70 and older, the ACS recommends further testing if their PSA is 6.5 ng/mL or higher. This is because PSA levels tend to be naturally higher in older men.

You can speak with your healthcare professional to find out more about PSA cutoff levels.

Goodstein told Healthline that the ways this new guidance can change the outlook for prostate cancer are simple.

“If we screen men, we can catch troublesome cancers early, before they have a chance to spread,” she said.

Goodstein also emphasized how much the evaluation process has changed. In the past, an elevated PSA level often led directly to a biopsy.

Today, doctors generally repeat the PSA test within two weeks to three months to confirm the result. If PSA levels remain elevated, additional testing, including a prostate MRI or validated biomarker test, can help determine whether a biopsy is necessary.

Narayanan told Healthline, “The new guidelines will hopefully remove any confusion about the value of PSA screening and encourage prostate cancer screening as an integral part of a man’s ‘checkup’.”

He added that it is worth noting that the DRE (digital rectal exam) adds little value to the initial prostate cancer screening and is no longer something that men should fear.

“Adoption of this set of specific guidelines standardizes the approach to screening for prostate cancer. It provides a unified effort among all levels of healthcare. It most likely will increase the chances of finding prostate cancer in earlier stages and lead to improved cancer treatment outcomes,” said Ramin.

U.S. to Lose Measles Elimination Status After 26 Years. Here’s Why

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Follow Healthline on our socials for all things health.

The United States will likely lose its measles elimination status, a major public health achievement that was secured 26 years ago.

The Pan American Health Organization (PAHO), the World Health Organization’s (WHO) regional office for the Americas, determined that measles has spread continuously in the U.S. for more than 12 months. This marks the end of the country’s measles elimination status, which it has held since 2000.

Researchers familiar with the discussions told KFF Health News that the Pan American Health Organization (PAHO) determined this week that measles is once again spreading through sustained transmission within the country. PAHO is expected to make an official announcement on November 17.

PAHO’s review examined measles spread starting January 20, 2025, when the first person became ill in a large West Texas outbreak, according to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC).

CDC data show 5,412 confirmed measles cases this year as of November 12. That’s the highest annual total since 1991 and more than double the 2025 count of 2,289 cases.

Andi Shane, MD, chief of pediatric infectious diseases at Children’s Healthcare of Atlanta, called the U.S. losing measles elimination status “a public health alarm that we are not being effective and we’re not controlling measles as well.”

The measles virus is highly contagious. It spreads through the air when an infected person coughs or sneezes. People can catch it in a room up to two hours after an infected person has left, according to the CDC.

“There’s really been very little measles in the United States over the past 25 years until January 2025,” William Moss, MD, a professor of epidemiology at the Johns Hopkins Bloomberg School of Public Health, told Healthline.

Losing the status itself doesn’t change much about daily life for most Americans, Moss said. The concern is the measles outbreaks, hospitalizations and deaths behind the loss, he said.

He called the loss “a big red flag” after “enormous efforts over the past 26 years” to keep the status.

A country loses its elimination status when there is “uninterrupted circulation of the same virus genotype and lineage for 12 months or more,” according to PAHO. Shane explained it this way: “For the past 12 months in the United States, we’ve had ongoing circulation of the measles virus and person-to-person transmission is occurring.”

Canada lost its elimination status in November 2025, and the U.K. and five European countries lost theirs earlier in 2026.

The U.S. can regain the status. “Other countries have done that,” Moss said. Brazil, which lost its status in 2019, regained it in November 2024. Venezuela, which lost its status in 2018, regained it in 2023, according to PAHO.

Before the measles vaccine, measles infected an estimated 3 million to 4 million Americans each year, according to the CDC. Among reported cases, about 400 to 500 people died each year, 48,000 needed hospital care, and 1,000 developed encephalitis, or brain swelling.

Moss said elimination efforts “really intensified” after a large outbreak from 1989 to 1992. Part of that push was “really promoting school entry vaccination checks,” he said.

“That was one of the key policy changes that I think allowed for measles elimination in the United States.”

Every state and Washington, D.C., require children to be vaccinated against measles to attend school, though exemptions vary by state, according to the nonprofit health policy organization KFF.

Fewer kindergartners are getting the MMR vaccine, according to the CDC.

Kindergarten MMR coverage fell from 95.2% in the 2019–2020 school year to 92.4% in 2025–2026. About 280,000 kindergartners started the 2025–2026 school year without records showing they had completed the MMR series, the CDC reported.

Shane said “95 out of 100 people need to be protected against measles” for a community to get herd protection.

“So since we’ve been below that for many, many months, that is also contributing to the loss of our elimination status,” she said.

“It takes two things to have a measles outbreak,” Moss said. A community needs low vaccine coverage, and someone with measles has to enter it and “start the chain of transmission,” he said.

About 96% of this year’s cases were among people who were unvaccinated or had unknown vaccination status, up from 93% in 2025, according to the CDC.

Moss said many of those cases are adults.

“This is particularly true in Pennsylvania, where about two-thirds of the cases are in adults,” he said. Many never got vaccinated as kids and grew up while measles was rare, so they never built natural immunity, he said.

He noted that having so many unvaccinated adults puts pregnant people and adults with chronic conditions like lung disease at risk of serious illness. The U.S. has already seen “dozens of pregnant women actually getting infected during pregnancy,” he said.Infected parents can also spread measles to young children and grandparents, Shane said. Shane traced much of the hesitancy to the pandemic. “With COVID there’s been an overall distrust in vaccines and vaccination that initially started with COVID but has now spread to other vaccines,” she said.

Moss pointed to the federal government. “We know what it takes to achieve and sustain elimination. We have the expertise. We have the resources,” he said.

He cited staff cuts at the CDC and said messaging from Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. and President Donald Trump has undermined vaccine confidence. He called federal messaging on vaccines “really mixed and weak.”

Moss also criticized the President’s August 10 executive order calling for the MMR vaccine to be split into separate shots for each disease.

“There’s no justification for that, and those vaccines don’t even exist in the United States,” he said.

Measles may cause ear infections, diarrhea, pneumonia, encephalitis and, in some cases, death, according to the CDC.
