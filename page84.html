Doc Rivers shares criticisms of Giannis Antetokounmpo's game − and how he can improve

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

If you ask Doc Rivers, Giannis Antetokounmpo is in the perfect place to take the next step in his career.

In the most recent episode of "The Bill Simmons Podcast," Rivers dished on his time with the Milwaukee Bucks, how he navigated the ill-fated Damian Lillard trade and the highs and lows that came with coaching the Greek Freak.

"I liked Giannis a lot. I liked him more as a dude," Rivers told Simmons. "He's interesting. He's not easy. He plays at this crazy downhill pace. He's really a difficult guy because he has this great passing ability, but he doesn't necessarily have super IQ at the same time. It's really a strange thing, and he really has one way. Downhill. Force, force. Every play, he wants to force it."

The number one thing Rivers emphasized was execution. Preparing the right way and setting his teammates up for success is the area the Hall of Fame coach believes Antetokounmpo fell short in during their time together. But now, with Antetokounmpo on the Miami Heat under Erik Spoelstra, Rivers is hopeful that will come to fruition.

"I wish he learned how to execute better," Rivers said. "He's one of those guys − you get a lot of them from the U.S., AAU guys − that have never needed to execute because they can do it on their own. But that doesn't help the team. And I told Giannis this, the thing he needs to still learn is the value of execution. How important it is when you're in practice and shootarounds to lock in on execution. And now he has a coach who is as good as there is in execution."

Antetokounmpo landed in South Beach this summer after a blockbuster trade that put an end to the long-running standoff between the 10-time All-Star and the Bucks front office, but it's been anything but greener pastures early on. The Heat are 3-6 in their first nine games despite him averaging 28.3 points and 12.5 rebounds a night.

The Golden State Warriors pulled away from a tie game against Miami at halftime to earn a comfortable 120-100 win Thursday, thanks in no small part to the ability of Draymond Green, Al Horford and Charles Bassey to contain Antetokounmpo to just one point in seven fourth quarter minutes down the stretch.

Martine Rose is the New Creative Director of Balenciaga

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Martine Rose is getting her flowers. The London-based designer, known for her beloved eponymous menswear label, is the new creative director of Balenciaga, the company announced on Friday.

She was long tipped to take the helm at the French maison, having helped shape the brand’s Demna era as a menswear consultant from 2015 to 2018, just as he conceived its signature oversized clothes. Now, she is returning with creative control over the whole house, succeeding outgoing creative director Pierpaolo Piccioli, who departed on Thursday after just three seasons in the role. Rose will take on the role, based in Paris, effective November 16. Rose will be the first female creative director at Balenciaga in its 109-year history.

“Balenciaga has always been a house of radical creativity, challenging conventions and redefining the boundaries of fashion. Martine Rose embodies this spirit with a fresh, fearless and deeply contemporary vision,” Kering CEO Luca de Meo said in a statement. “Her connection to the new generation and her instinctive understanding of culture bring a new dimension to the house. Her appointment reflects our ambition to keep Balenciaga at the forefront of innovation, creativity and culture.”

“Martine Rose is an extraordinary creative talent with a bold and highly distinctive vision,” Balenciaga president and CEO Gianfranco Gianangeli said. “Her ability to challenge conventions, reinvent silhouettes and bring an unexpected perspective to fashion resonates deeply with the spirit of innovation that defines Balenciaga. We are delighted to welcome her as creative director and look forward to this exciting new chapter for the house.”

Kendrick Lamar wearing a custom Martine Rose jacket at his Super Bowl performance in 2025.

Rose carries a lot of industry goodwill. Since rumors began swirling on social media about her appointment, scores of editors, stylists, influencers and celebrities rejoiced online. Her offbeat menswear, inspired by British subcultures from football casuals to rave kids, skinheads to reggae heads, has won the designer many fans since she launched her eponymous label in 2007. Not least Kendrick Lamar, who posted about Rose after she made looks for his Big Steppers tour in 2022: “Bucket list to work with Martine Rose…” he posted to his then-12 million Instagram followers, alongside a photo of Rose peace-signing into the camera. Other celebrity fans include Rihanna, A$AP Rocky and Timothée Chalamet, all of whom have been spotted wearing her oversized, sports-infused fits in recent years.

The Balenciaga appointment has come at a good time for the designer. She has faced major challenges at her eponymous label in recent years, since selling a 60% stake in the business to incubator Tomorrow Ltd in 2023. Rose was in a dispute with Tomorrow Ltd over the handling of her brand, which became apparent when she announced she would not produce her FW26 collection in March. Tomorrow released a counter statement, and ultimately, as major shareholders of the brand, they were able to produce the collection, despite Rose’s announcement.

Shortly after, in March of this year, Tomorrow sold Rose’s brand to Italian company The Level Group (Paris label Coperni was also bought by The Level Group, and the founders announced this week that they will shutter the brand). Rose has not staged a fashion show since SS26 last June.

Martine Rose’s last fashion show for SS26.

Another designer in the Tomorrow/The Level Group’s stable, Charles Jeffrey, posted a video online on October 26, explaining how thrilled he would be if the Martine x Balenciaga rumors were true. “She would be so incredible in the role because she speaks to a community that doesn’t see themselves in the [luxury] space,” he says, “and she does it with such warmth.”

In his ReconKering turnaround plan, Kering CEO Luca de Meo explained that he sees Balenciaga as a house for Gen Z. “Balenciaga is one of the brands that better understands the vibes of the new generation,” he said during the group’s Capital Markets Day in April. Rose also understands those vibes. 8 Episodes Of Netflix’s Greatest Sci-Fi Hit Deliver Peak Lovecraftian Horror

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Reading Lovecraftian horror feels like reading an ancient forbidden text. There is a timelessness to H.P. Lovecraft's display of the "fear of the unknown" that transcends the page and immerses one into complete existential dread. Yet, despite feeling ancient and timeless, Lovecraftian horror rarely translates well to the screen.

There are not even a handful of worthy direct adaptations of H.P. Lovecraft's stories. Quite a few cosmic horror movies and shows, however, have succeeded at loosely drawing from his works. One of them is Netflix's greatest sci-fi hit, Stranger Things. To clarify, Stranger Things, as a whole, is not a Lovecraftian horror show. It is arguably not even horror.

However, in its first 8 episodes, Stranger Things draws heavily from Lovecraft's chilling Eldritch lore and his ambiguous descriptions of monsters and ancient gods. Lovecraftian horror is certainly one of the many literary influences that ultimately shaped Stranger Things. However, the Netflix sci-fi show's heavy inclination towards cosmic horror in season 1 just cannot be ignored.

The origins of cosmic terrors in every Lovecraftian story stem from humanity's hubris. For instance, in H.P. Lovecraft's seminal short story From Beyond, an ambitious scientist makes an electronic device that allows humans to perceive what lies beyond our scope of accepted reality. With this, he unknowingly allows interdimensional creatures from alternate dimensions to perceive and manipulate humans.

Something very similar unfolds in Stranger Things season 1.

Dr. Martin Brenner and Hawkins National Laboratory open "The Gate" in an attempt to "expand" human consciousness and weaponize it to spy on the Soviets. Little do they realize that they have exposed humanity to an unknown dimension. Just like the terrifying co-existing but hidden dimensions in Lovecraftian horror, Stranger Things' "Upside Down" is not merely hell ruled by the devil.

Instead, it is more of an ancient and toxic "non-Euclidean" plane that mirrors Hawkins. Just like the sunken city of R'lyeh from The Call of Cthulhu, even the "Upside Down" is cold, dark, endless, and incomprehensible.

Almost throughout its run, Stranger Things season 1 also avoids revealing the true nature or form of the Demogorgon. It is packed with horrifying ambiguous imagery where Will attempts to communicate with his family from the "other side" while struggling to escape the monster's grasp.

Similar to Lovecraftian protagonists, even Stranger Things characters like Joyce feel so helpless against the cosmic indifference of their world that they start losing their minds.

In its final arc, Stranger Things season 1 also gives viewers and its characters an illusion of control. It establishes that the power of friendship seemingly saves the day and Will and his family live happily ever after. But just before Stranger Things season 1 ends, Will realizes that control and autonomy were mere illusions. With this, Stranger Things subtly mocks human authority just like Lovecraftian stories.

The Duffer Brothers have previously cited H.P. Lovecraft's stories as an influence on Stranger Things (via EW).

Going into season 2, Stranger Things manages to retain some of its cosmic horror aspects. However, at the same time, it makes its terrors and scares a little too "tangible." This marks the show's departure from cosmic horror. As it progresses, Stranger Things bends genres and even covers coming-of-age themes in latter seasons.

Towards the end, the Netflix sci-fi show feels more like a conventional supernatural thriller than a groundbreaking take on Lovecraftian horror tropes. Perhaps, this explains why, in hindsight, most viewers are kinder towards Stranger Things' earlier chapters and more harsh when it comes to its divisive season 5.
