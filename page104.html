Mancini explains why he left Sebastiano Esposito out of Italy squad to face Belgium

📅 Nov 15, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Roberto Mancini gave a brief interview to Rai ahead of Italy’s Nations League clash against Belgium on Sunday, paying his respects to the victims of the Heysel disaster and explaining why he decided to leave Sebastiano Esposito out of his matchday squad.

Italy are in Brussels for their final fixture of the Nations League group stage on Sunday evening. The Azzurri top the group on 10 points ahead of kick-off, ahead of France on 10, Belgium on nine and Turkey on zero.

Italy require a draw to qualify for the Nations League quarter-finals and in all likelihood a victory to secure top spot. If Italy lose, they will require Turkey to beat Belgium in order to still qualify for the quarter-finals.

Head coach Mancini spoke briefly to Rai a few hours before kick-off. He says that his players ‘deserve’ qualification for the next round of the Nations League given the work they have put in, but does not need reminding that the job is far from over.

“The boys deserve to get through to the last eight of the Nations League because they’re doing an extraordinary job, but there’s still a lot to do,” said Mancini.

“France are among the top three sides in the world and Belgium are not far behind them,” he added.

When asked how to stop Belgium’s creator-in-chief Kevin De Bruyne, Mancini simply replied: “By not allowing him to play.”

As is customary for Nations League group stage fixtures, Mancini has whittled his list of players down to a matchday squad of 23. Davide Frattesi and Nicolo Cambiaghi have not recovered from the France game on Thursday night and have been left out of the squad.

The surprising omission is Sebastiano Esposito, who missed a penalty during Italy’s 3-1 victory over France on Thursday night. Samuele Ricci, Federico Gatti and Matteo Palma have also been left out of the matchday squad to face Belgium.

Asked if Seba Esposito had been left out as a ‘punishment’ for missing the penalty against France, Mancini replied: “Don’t make me laugh. It’s just a technical decision, he’s played in practically every game.

“It’s a shame that we won’t have Frattesi or Cambiaghi, who were decisive in the game against France. Dimarco can also play as an attacking winger.”

Lastly, Mancini was asked about his memories of the Heysel disaster in Brussels back in 1985.

“We will never forget a tragedy as immense as this,” said Mancini. “I remember everything from that day of May 29, 1985. I played for Sampdoria at the time, but I am a Juventus supporter.”

The Nvidia scandal has sent shockwaves through Silicon Valley as well as the political establishment

Alice Huang, a suspected China spy who has been linked to Nvidia's former boss Jensen Huang, left her executive role at Megaspeed in August last year, the company says.

Jensen Huang resigned as chief executive of the AI chip-making giant Nvidia after it emerged he had allegedly had affairs with Alice Huang as well as with one of his employees.

Megaspeed is the biggest buyer of Nvidia chips in south-east Asia and the scandal at Nvidia has raised questions about the possibility that high-end AI chips may have ended up in China despite US export restrictions.

The Singapore-based company said on Sunday it had launched an internal investigation into its business.

It said it was aware of reports "containing assertions about our business, supply chain and global operations that are not supported by verifiable evidence".

It added that it "conducts legitimate and lawful business" and has "retained external legal counsel to conduct a thorough internal investigation".

On Friday, US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent blamed fellow Trump cabinet colleague Howard Lutnick's commerce department for failing to properly oversee the export of high-end chips.

Bessent told CNBC that several agencies "were in favour of putting Megaspeed on the entity list for months... but Commerce has the pen... and you will have to ask them about their internal process".

The so-called entity list restricts US trade with overseas firms and individuals.

Lutnick has been called to testify before the Senate Banking Committee next month over his department's role in allowing Nvidia to export its advanced chips.

Other congressional committees have asked both Lutnick and his department to retain all records relating to Nvidia and Jensen Huang.

Megaspeed is suspected of giving backdoor access to Nvidia chips to Chinese companies via its data centres in Malaysia, Indonesia and Thailand.

Robert Christgau and Carola Dibbell Talk New Documentary ‘The Last Critic’ and Staying Crazy in Love

📅 Nov 15, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The Last Critic is one of the year’s most fascinating rock documentaries — even though it’s not about a musician. It’s a portrait of the legendary writer Robert Christgau, the self-proclaimed “Dean of American Rock Critics.” He was one of the pioneers who invented pop-music criticism in the Sixties — but six decades later, at 83, he’s still at the top of the game. He began his Consumer Guide column in the Village Voice in 1969, and has kept cranking it out ever since, now on Substack. For generations of music fanatics, Christgau’s voice hits like Bob Dylan or Neil Young — two artists his age who are still doing it with the same fiercely independent spirit he brings to his writing.

The debut feature from director Matty Wishnow, The Last Critic won the jury prize at the South by Southwest and Oak Cliff Film festivals, as well as the 2026 Screen Dallas grand prize. It gets its New York premiere tomorrow. The film, which is co-produced by longtime Rolling Stone contributor and editor Joe Levy, goes beyond music, because it’s also the portrait of a marriage. Christgau and his wife, novelist Carola Dibbell, have been together more than 50 years. Over the decades, they’ve shared an intellectual life, raised their daughter, collected way too much vinyl, and — the weirdest part — remained crazy in love.

Christgau has a fearsome rep for never pulling his punches, with his own savage wit. Lou Reed spent a chunk of his live album Take No Prisoners complaining about getting a B+ grade for Street Hassle; when U2 won the Grammy Award for Album of the Year in 1988 for The Joshua Tree, Bono jokily groused in his speech about his band’s reviews in the Village Voice. Christgau’s inspired more songs attacking him than any other journalist, from Sonic Youth (“Kill Yr Idols”) to Public Enemy (“Bring the Noise”).

But there’s something obsessive about his lifelong quest to chronicle the music, still going strong after 60 years — he’s heard more albums than anyone on the planet. When he reviewed Prince’s Dirty Mind in 1980, he dropped one of the most-quoted one-liners in critical history: “Mick Jagger can fold up his penis and go home.” Editor’s picks The 250 Greatest Albums of the 21st Century So Far The 100 Best TV Episodes of All Time The 500 Greatest Albums of All Time 100 Best Movies of the 21st Century

The Last Critic features artists Christgau has reviewed, as well as writers he’s inspired, from Sonic Youth’s Thurston Moore to Pulitzer-winning novelist Colson Whitehead. (I make an appearance as well.) There’s a touching interlude with his lifelong friend Greil Marcus, the only other OG rock critic who’s kept doing it this long (or this well). In one of the most surprising moments, the legendary songwriter Randy Newman recalls their friendship, and admits he wrote a song about Christgau and Dibbell, “Lost Without You,” inspired by the closeness of their marriage. It’s the kind of movie where you come for the rock, stay for the romance.

Rolling Stone met up with the couple at the same East Village pad they’ve called home for 50 years. The place is packed with albums, books, art — the first thing that greets you at the door is a painting of the Shirelles. They talked about the doc, seeing their marriage up on the big screen, the art of arguing, playing hoops with Randy Newman, and the strange college semester when Christgau was Gene Simmons’ professor.

However, he came to feel that it was the marriage that made the movie, which is what I believe.

It is the marriage that makes the movie.Carola Dibbell: But it’s still about you. I’m a co-star. Crucially, the agreement would also include a buy-back clause worth around €80m (£70m), giving Real Madrid the opportunity to bring Endrick back to the Santiago Bernabeu if he develops into the player they believe he can become.

The proposed structure resembles Madrid's arrangement with Como for Nico Paz and could provide a potential solution to the disagreements that have previously prevented Endrick from leaving.

Milan's interest in Endrick is not new, but their latest reported strategy represents a change from the short-term loan previously considered.

Rather than simply borrowing the striker for six months, the Serie A club would be prepared to make a substantial financial commitment while offering Madrid assurances over the player's future.

An eventual €60m purchase would represent a major investment for Milan, who are expected to have around €70m available for the January transfer window.

The proposed €80m buy-back clause would give Madrid the possibility of reversing a permanent departure, although there is currently no indication that the Spanish club have accepted the arrangement.

There is also uncertainty over whether Milan would seek an obligation to buy or merely an option, with Italian reports discussing both possibilities.

Either structure would require an agreement over the conditions attached to the purchase and Madrid's subsequent right to re-sign the player.

Real have so far been reluctant to sanction Endrick's exit despite his limited opportunities.

Mourinho has publicly expressed his desire to retain the Brazilian, acknowledging that he has found it difficult to provide him with the playing time his development requires.

Endrick has made only limited appearances this season, contributing one goal and one assist in 141 minutes of football.

His situation follows a productive loan spell at Lyon during the 2025-26 campaign, when he registered eight goals and seven assists in 21 appearances.

Those performances demonstrated his ability to make an impact with regular minutes, strengthening interest from clubs seeking attacking reinforcements.

Real Madrid's previous reluctance to approve a permanent move for Endrick has complicated Milan's efforts, but the inclusion of a buy-back clause could offer a different basis for negotiations.

Milan may also have an advantage through their connections with Endrick's representatives.

Club owner Gerry Cardinale reportedly maintains a positive relationship with Roc Nation, the agency responsible for the Brazilian's career.

Cardinale had already expressed an interest in bringing Endrick to San Siro during the summer and is said to have requested updates whenever Madrid make a definitive decision about the youngster's future.

Former Milan and Real Madrid midfielder Kaka could also become involved in attempts to persuade Endrick to consider the move.

According to La Gazzetta dello Sport, the Brazilian legend is viewed as a potentially influential figure because of his connections with both clubs and his standing in Brazilian football.

There is no confirmation that Kaka has formally entered negotiations or spoken directly to Endrick about joining Milan.

Nevertheless, his possible involvement adds another dimension to the club's pursuit.

Another former Milan favourite, Ronaldinho, has also offered an encouraging assessment of Endrick's ability to succeed in Italian football.

Speaking in an interview with La Gazzetta dello Sport, the 2005 Ballon d'Or winner insisted that his compatriot possesses the qualities required to adapt to Serie A.

"He is a player who can play anywhere in the world," Ronaldinho said.

"He has played in different leagues and done well, in France, Brazil and Spain. He is very young and can play well, yes."

Ronaldinho's comments were an assessment of Endrick's qualities rather than an indication that he is involved in Milan's transfer plans.

The Rossoneri face competition from Roma, where Gian Piero Gasperini is also interested in strengthening his attacking options.

The capital club have been linked with a January loan, while interest from England and France has also been reported.

However, a recent report from Corriere dello Sport suggested that Endrick was not particularly enthusiastic about moving to Milan, potentially presenting another obstacle even if the clubs can agree financial terms.

Milan are therefore maintaining alternative options, with Club Brugge striker Nicolo Tresoldi also under consideration.

The 22-year-old has scored 14 goals in 17 appearances this season and is reportedly valued at approximately €60m (£53m).

Brugge would prefer to retain Tresoldi until the summer, meaning Milan could explore signing one of their attacking targets in January before returning for the other at the end of the campaign.

For Endrick, the decisive factor remains Real Madrid's willingness to reconsider their position. Milan's proposed buy-back arrangement may offer a compromise, but no formal agreement has been reached and Mourinho has not changed his publicly stated desire to keep the striker.
