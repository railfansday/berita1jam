What we've been playing - "Sniffing out the warehouses on Prawn Island"

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

A few of the things that have us hooked this week.

Well, hello! I am once again wearing the Bertie hat in your usual host's absence, so joyous welcomes to our regular feature in which we talk about what we've been playing in recent times. This week, Marie has been revisiting Zelda - although perhaps not the one you might expect - while Dom is rediscovering Vice City of the 80s. Kelsey is playing some very weird cards, Connor is back in WoW - possibly forever - and I'm equally smitten with a different RPG.

But enough about us, what about you? What have you been playing this week? Do tell all! And don't forget we have an entire archive of previous What We've Been Playings for your leisurely perusal, just in case you forgot what you were playing a year or a month or a week ago. Bye!

Once again, I am finally playing a game from my backlog that I should've played years ago, and that's the delightful albeit dark card game Inscryption. There's so much more to this than you'd first expect, and I'm surprised the community has been able to keep the game's best bits under wraps for so long. Ultimately, nothing could've prepared me for Act 2, and my only real qualm with the whole experience is that I wish Act 2 had its own endless roguelike mode; you'd never be able to pull me away from it.

The game falters a little at the start of Act 3, I'll admit, and I've found myself very frustrated by what is an otherwise relatively easy time. This is down to me missing some rather useful earlier cards that I've since learnt would've made this final stretch much breezier, but oh well. I will still take down the Uberbots one way or another for the love of the game!

I've sunk every free hour I have into World of Warcraft: Forever since its release on Wednesday night. As of writing I am Level 19, waiting for an escort quest to come up in Westfall. So far things seem largely the same as in the original MMO, though the little changes and improvements I've seen have firmly planted a smile on my face.

This includes the new weather system. Like many others I was caught in a snow storm in Dun Morogh this week, a seemingly random environmental transformation that drastically limits view distance and blankets your screen with the cold stuff. It adds a lot, I think, to the fantasy of the game. I'll be playing this all weekend and for the foreseeable future.

I'll let you guess why I'm playing this right now of all times. Ahead of Grand Theft Auto 6's launch, I've made it my unofficial job to internalise the map of Vice City. I'm spending time driving around the strip, familiarising myself with the docklands, going for a few rounds of golf up in Leaf Links, and sniffing out the warehouses on Prawn Island. I'm immensely curious how similar the map is in GTA 6, and can't wait to see what's changed in the interceding 40 years in-universe. It's even better having V-Rock blast Iron Maiden and Twisted Sister at me; over the past few evenings, I've been playing this extra loud so it drowns out the sound of local fireworks for the sake of my poor, terrified dog. I don't think he's a fan of Bruce 'the air raid siren' Dickinson's voice - but it's better than all the apocalyptic banging.

The cold winter nights are drawing in, which calls for blankets, a warm drink, and a nice peaceful game before bed. I don't have an exact reason for going back to Tears of the Kingdom. Perhaps it's me diving into Ocarina of Time that fuelled my curiosity about returning, but what's surprised me the most is there's still so much I didn't see or do in my first 190 hours with it.

I started again, cleared my save file, and it turns out I've completely forgotten most things about the game - which is by no means a complaint! I'm rather enjoying making the little discoveries that are either brand-new to me, or ones that make me go 'AH-HA, I remember you' (in the case of Gloom Hands, the reaction was 'NOPE, NOPE, NOPE, NOPE.')

I've also had another surprise. I play in handheld mode mostly, and ToTK was all looking a bit blurry on the Switch 2's screen. So I got the upgrade pack and suddenly everything became beautiful again - Link was in focus, the individual blades of grass in Hyrule Field were clearly whipping in the wind, and the crispy snow around Rito village almost sparkled. I don't often get upgrade packs for games I already own, but this one was worth it.

I suspect this won't be the last time you see me yapping about Queen's Domain here. I've already enthused about it elsewhere on the site this week, but I've managed to squeeze in a few more hours since then, and it continues to enthrall. Here's a small moment from my most recent go: I'd been fumbling about the Tower of Knowledge for a while at this point, dying slightly more than I'd like to those jabby gargoyle gits and lumbering chimera things, so I thought I'd have a little wander away from the violence and have an explore. That was when I noticed the gap on the bookshelf, wide enough to slot in the mysterious Ratty Bug Compendium I'd been wondering about ever since I picked it up elswhere some five hours early.

Moments later a secret passage had opened, leading out to a balcony overlooking the ocean. Treasure! Exciting! But just as I'm about to go back inside, I notice a little outcrop on the exterior of the tower, seemingly reachable with my sword's magic dashing power. So over I go, discovering an otherwise inaccessible route into an inner chamber. There, I find a lever, which I excitedly pull, only to be rewarded with... nothing at all. Until five minutes or so later, when I discover a secret panel has now opened up on the other side of the tower, granting me access to the treats within. In isolation, it's not a remarkable gaming anecdote, but Queen's Domain is just crammed with these little incremental secrets and surprises, full of clever design that makes exploration feel so rewarding. I've already spotted more enigmas waiting to be cracked, and if Queen's Domain keeps distracting me like this, I might never escape its pull.

Report - Disaster: Agents of Liverpool star now in talks to leave, Reds desperate to keep him

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Liverpool could lose yet another star. His agents are now in talks with a UEFA Champions League side as a potential exit is on the cards.

Liverpool have lost a lot of truly great talent over the last few years. Not all of it has been choice, either, though some of it absolutely was. Mohamed Salah leaving this year, for instance, was down to the club as much as the player.

There have been plenty of sales, too. Curtis Jones departed for Inter in the summer while homegrown talent like Caoimhin Kelleher and Jarell Quansah were both sold to starting positions elsewhere.

But then there are the ones that aren't by choice. Trent Alexander-Arnold was offered a new contract but rejected it to join Real Madrid. One year later, Ibrahima Konaté did literally the exact same thing.

And it likely won't stop there. That doesn't mean players will leave next year to join Real Madrid (but don't rule that out) but there are stars who could get their heads turned.

Bayern Munich were surprisingly linked with a move for Rio Ngumoha in the summer. They were supposedly willing to put up quite a bit of cash to make it happen, though Liverpool never entertained the idea.

But Christian Falk reports that they haven’t actually stopped looking. He claims that Bayern remain in talks with representatives of Ngumoha, though not directly the player himself.

They’re eager to find the player who could take over from Luis Diaz on the left wing. Ngumoha is certainly a fit for that - even if a successor to the Colombian is the exact same role that Liverpool want him for.

Now, the reason these talks are important is that Ngumoha’s contract expires in 2028 and Liverpool are in their own talks to get a new one signed. If they fail, there will be immense pressure to sell next summer while they can still get a fee for him.

That then becomes even more complicated because Chelsea get 20 per cent of any sale. Essentially, there’s no good outcome here other than Ngumoha re-signing with Liverpool.

Bayern are going to do all they can to make sure that doesn’t happen, though. We’ll probably refrain from predicting how this one goes, given the exits over the last couple of years, but we know Liverpool are pushing hard.

Her extraordinary wisdom, mysterious past, and close friendship with Captain Jean-Luc Picard made her a true standout.

However, Guinan was never just an ordinary bartender. Having lived for centuries, she possessed remarkable knowledge and unusual abilities, including a heightened awareness of changes to the timeline. She's even one of the few characters capable of standing up to the omnipotent Q.

Why Footballers Wear Tiny Shin Pads

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Watch any professional football match closely, and you may notice something odd about some players' legs: socks rolled down to the calf, with holes cut out of the back and, tucked inside them, a shin-pad barely bigger than a credit card. Jack Grealish made the look famous, but the trend has since spread from the elite game down to Sunday league and junior pitches.

Shin-pads are compulsory under football's laws, so how are players getting away with protecting so little of their shins? And why would they want to flout a rule made to protect them and their most prized asset?

Shin-pads became mandatory in 1990 due to the increasing physical brutality and injury rates seen in Europe's elite leagues.

According to Law 4 of IFAB's (International Football Association Board) Laws of the Game, shin pads must be made of a 'suitable material' and must be 'of an appropriate size to provide reasonable protection'. However, it also states that players are responsible for the 'size and suitability' of their shin-pads, meaning that, ultimately, the decision is up to the individual.

IFAB told BBC Sport that referees are only required to check that some form of shin pads are being worn, meaning that even match officials are not able to force a player to change into bigger, more protective equipment.

An explanation has emerged after players have been spotted with cut holes in the back of their football boots

The majority of professionals who have been quizzed on their shin-guards cite comfort and freedom of movement as the main factor in opting for micro-pads. Given the speed and intensity that the modern game is played at, every gram less that their equipment weighs gives a minute boost to their range of motion and explosiveness.

Another reason is the recent trend of low socks that sit around the calf. According to Law 4, the shin pads must be covered by the players' socks. Therefore, if they were to use the more traditional, bigger shin-pads, players would have to wear longer socks.

The reason Jack Grealish wears his socks rolled down to his ankles and the smallest shin pads you're ever likely to see

Whilst there has not yet been an in-depth study into the effectiveness of mini-shin-pads compared to traditional ones, guidance written by the FA, and the actions of various amateur and semi-professional clubs, offer a damning verdict.

FA guidance written in 2024 states that "any part of the shin that is not covered is not as well protected", before adding that micro shin pads may "increase the risk of injury due to the smaller amount of shin covered." The statement continues by advising that "safety is prioritised by ensuring a good proportion of the shin area is covered and therefore protected."

Various amateur and non-league clubs have been keen to ban mini-shin-pads, including Ilfracombe Town, who prohibited the use of such equipment across all age groups and squads after forward Riley Malin suffered a double-leg break (tibia and fibula) in a 50/50 challenge.

According to the BBC, in August 2024, Penistone Church took similar action after teenager Alfie Collins also suffered a double-leg break, having switched his bigger shin-pads at halftime for a pair that were "no bigger than a credit card."

Peeling paint, bubbling wallpaper, mold behind wardrobes, and water-stained ceilings — I’ve seen all the classic signs of excess moisture in my own home.

But while window condensation is an obvious telltale sign of too much moisture inside my house, the root cause of the other issues isn’t so clear.

But before calling in a specialist, there’s a simple test you can try to determine whether the moisture is coming from inside or out.

Beyond covering your Thanksgiving turkey, aluminum foil can help with a stack of hacks around your home, including identifying which sides of your walls are holding excess moisture.

All you need to do is take a piece of aluminum foil and tape it to your wall. Within 48 hours, it should give you an indication of where the moisture is coming from.

But how does this aluminum foil hack work, and is it reliable? Michael Rubino, mold and air quality expert at HomeCleanse, explains: “Aluminum foil doesn’t allow water vapor to pass through it easily, because it’s a non-porous surface. When you tape it against a wall, you’re essentially blocking moisture from moving between that section of the wall and the air in the room.”

And he adds, “If the air inside your home is humid and the foil gets cool enough, that moisture in the air can condense into water droplets on the side facing the room.”

The same process happens when you spot condensation on the inside of your windows.

However, what if the moisture is coming from the wall? Rubino explains, “If there is already moisture in the wall, that moisture would normally have some opportunity to evaporate into the room as the material dries. Covering it with foil, prevents that from happening in that particular spot. In this case, moisture collects behind the foil instead.”

Rubino explains that checking which side of the aluminum foil is wet can tell you something about how moisture is behaving in that exact area. But it can’t tell you exactly where that water came from.

If you remove the foil and find moisture against the wall, you might assume rainwater is getting in from outside, but he says that’s only one possibility. “A pipe could be leaking inside the wall, or water could be entering through the foundation.”

He explains that other possibilities include water entering through the foundation or condensation forming inside the wall cavity due to high humidity or poor insulation.

It's also possible you have more than one moisture issue at once. Rubino explains, “If you find water droplets on the side facing the room, that doesn’t mean you’ve ruled out a leak behind the wall, either.”

Dr. Alex Le Beau, a toxicologist and environmental scientist at Exposure Consulting, says a visual assessment is a good way to start checking whether moisture has entered your home, and professionals will do this too.

He explains that wet building material, such as a drywall, within the built environment is not normal, and that the location of the moisture should be the first thing to note — is it on an exterior or interior wall or the ceiling?

He also advises looking at the pattern it is making. Depending on the material, he says, "It will take a good amount of water to soak through the back of something. Soaking like this may make the material brittle to the touch or could cause surface deformations."

Dr. Le Beau says to look out for paint bubbling or staining coming through from the back of the material.

Internal condensation may also look different. He says, “There may be a pattern to the moisture from internal humidity, or it may form at the same spot and then evaporate when internal conditioning (HVAC) begins to cycle."

While a dry sheet of aluminum foil on your wall may seem like a good sign, Rubino warns against using a single piece of information to judge your home’s condition. "There’s too much it can’t account for, and a dry result doesn’t mean you’re in the clear,” he says.

Another of Rubino’s concerns about this hack is that it can’t be used to detect mold. "You could find moisture behind the foil and have no idea whether mold has started growing inside the wall,” he says.

It also won’t determine how much of the surrounding area is affected or whether contamination has spread beyond that area.

He’s also found that the opposite can be true. "I’ve seen plenty of situations where the original water problem happened months or even years earlier,” he says, “The material may have dried out since then, but that doesn't mean any mold growth that developed during that time has disappeared as well."

From Rihanna to Rosalía, These Are Martine Rose’s Biggest Celebrity Superfans

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

At last, the news has officially broken: Martine Rose has been appointed to the helm of Balenciaga. While expectations of the announcement have been brewing for the past few months, it’s one that has been met with pretty much ubiquitous celebration.

That’s not to dismiss the achievements of her predecessor, Pierpaolo Piccioli, whose tenure was marked by a sculptural, romantic flair and a rich sense for color and textural play. Still, the warmth expressed for the British designer’s appointment is uniquely multifaceted. There’s its representational significance, as she joins a growing coterie of women of color at the helm of fashion’s most influential houses, but beyond that—and arguably more importantly—is her skill as a designer, and its capacity to shape the fashion conversation at large.

That’s something she’s more than proved at her namesake label, where her tweaked-and-twisted, often quietly kinky riffs on the everyday dress codes she observes in her native London have been key in bridging the gap between the realms of streetwear and high fashion over the past decade. There is also, as anyone versed in their menswear lore will know, the fact that Rose’s big break arguably came at Balenciaga, where she played an instrumental role in shaping the aesthetic of the house, working alongside Demna during the early, agenda-setting years of his tenure. And then, as we’ve come to know over the years, there’s Martine Rose herself: one of the sharpest and funniest voices the industry has in its midst.

You don’t have to look far for proof of just how widely her influence has spread. Though often framed as something of a fashion insider’s favorite, Rose has an equally devoted A-list following. Rihanna is regularly spotted in her designs, while Kendrick Lamar has all but appointed her his de facto dresser for some of the biggest moments of his career, wearing custom Rose looks to collect his Best Rap Album Grammy in 2023 and for his critically acclaimed 2025 Super Bowl half-time performance.

Below, revisit the moments your favorite celebrities repped one of fashion’s favorite designers.

In October 2025, Justin Bieber was spotted in LA wearing a graphic, color-blocked, logo-emblazoned sweater by Martine Rose.

Rihanna is a Martine Rose superfan. In May 2025, she was seen sporting one of the designer’s signature striped polos and baggy-fit jeans on the streets of New York, shortly after revealing her baby bump at that year’s Met Gala.

We’re naturally biased, but one of our favorite Martine Rose moments is without doubt when Billie Eilish graced the cover of our British Vogue’s May 2025 issue, sporting a hot-pink track jacket and headscarf in an updated riff on Balmoral chic.

At the 2025 Super Bowl, Kendrick Lamar—another of Martine Rose’s highest profile superfans—wore a full custom look by the designer for his critically-lauded half-time show, single-handedly reinvigorating the market for denim flares.

In 2025, Timothée Chalamet opted for Martine Rose tailoring for the London premiere of A Complete Unknown, shortly after being spotted in LA wearing one of the brand’s distressed caps.

Kendrick Lamar’s penchant for wearing the brand goes back further than his Super Bowl performance. In 2023, he wore a full look—including a customized track jacket and a then-unreleased colorway of the designer’s viral Nike Shox MR4 mules—to pick up the Grammy for that year’s best rap album.

For further proof of the Biebers’s appreciation for the sportier side of Martine Rose’s offering, look no further than a 2023 Hailey Bieber.

Rosalía’s appetite for an IYKYK style staple is renowned—as proved by her status as an early adopter of the Nike Shox MR4 in 2022, one of the most hotly coveted products from Rose’s longstanding collaboration with the sports giant.
