In the Wake of the 2026 Midterms, Can We Reframe the Patriotic Song?

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

When Brandy Clark arrived on set to film the lyric video for her new single “American Roots,” her makeup artist had an idea. He wanted to paint her face and hands red, white, and blue, to riff on the song’s ideas of envisioning a loving, inclusive America where everyone, including immigrants, belong.

Clark was a little nervous at first, but it didn’t stop her from moving forward. “I’m so associated with the rainbow flag,” Clark, whose excellent new album The Bird is out in January, tells Rolling Stone. “But we deserve this. My fans deserve to see this.  And a lot of people in my family, my dad being one of them, served in the military for that flag. That’s all of our flag. MAGA does not own the flag.”

It’s a sentiment that a lot of Americans have been wrestling with as the Trump administration has worked to turn the flag into a symbol not of pride, but exclusion — and for country fans, one that was co-opted in a post 9/11 landscape where jingoistic songs passing as patriotism became radio gold. In the wake of the November midterms, however, when a blue wave saw Democratic gains in states once dismissed as lost causes, can artists change their relationship to patriotic songs?

Long before Tuesday’s elections, it’s something that Clark was thinking about with “American Roots,” along with others like Mickey Guyton, the War and Treaty, and many more country and roots artists interested in reframing exactly who gets to own the idea of patriotism. In a clip from Newport Folk Festival making the viral rounds this week, Joan Baez, alongside Mavis Staples, Nathaniel Rateliff, Brandi Carlile, and others, close the festival with their take on “America the Beautiful,” introduced with some potent words. “This song is something that we are taking back,” Baez said.

Part of this process, according to the War and Treaty’s Michael Trotter Jr., is divorcing patriotism from partisan (and often hateful, exclusionary) politics. Zac Brown tried to claim his performance of the National Anthem at President Trump’s UFC match on the White House lawn last summer was just “patriotism, not politics.” That was hard to square, however, when the country singer stood on the same stage where heavyweight fighter Josh Hokit later celebrated his win by shouting, “Michelle Obama is a man.” Editor’s picks The 250 Greatest Albums of the 21st Century So Far The 100 Best TV Episodes of All Time The 500 Greatest Albums of All Time 100 Best Movies of the 21st Century

“I take great pride in being able to say that I served this country and served it well,” Trotter, a combat veteran of the Iraq war, told Rolling Stone. “So, I am ashamed that people would hide behind the term ‘patriotism.’” Sheryl Crow, in a visit to Nashville Now, agrees.

“There are words we use against each other to create sides: ‘fake,’ and ‘woke,’ and ‘patriot,’” Crow said. “The word ‘patriotic’ and ‘patriot’ really is offensive to me. Because I feel very patriotic about my country, but I’m not a conservative. I’m a middle-of-the-road pragmatist. But [patriotic] is used for one category of people and it’s used against others and I take offense with that.”

That approach has been a part of the country music playbook for decades now, and was on Clark’s mind when she started writing “American Roots,” a song that is gentle in its messaging: we’re all Americans, with equal rights to walk on our hallowed land, wherever you may come from originally. It hinges on a lyric in the bridge. “When you set foot onto that shore,” she sings, “it’s no more mine than it is yours.”

“To me that song is a conversation between two people, and two people who might not agree,” Clark says. “Like, tell me about your family tree, when we start digging where’s it going to lead? It’s about finding commonality throughout the whole song. And then when you get to the bridge, it’s like, okay, here it is. Whether it leads to more artists feeling emboldened to reclaim their version of a patriotic anthem is still yet to be seen, but with songs like “American Roots,” the tide seems to be turning. Guyton, back in 2021, was already pushing the concept on “All American” from her debut LP Remember Her Name, an album all about claiming and reclaiming space in both country music and the country itself. “We got the same stars, the same stripes, just wanna live that good life,” she sang. “Ain’t we all? Ain’t we all all-American?” A version of that sung by a white conservative man would have been a radio hit, but Guyton’s song has more impact than a fleeting Number One: it changes the definition of who gets to own a patriotic song.

LIV Golf Shutting Down After Last-Ditch Financing Plan Fails

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Five years after it began its quest to upend a genteel sport with billions in Saudi Arabian money and brash style, LIV Golf is liquidating its operations.

The would-be rival to the PGA Tour, which was championed by major winner Phil Mickelson and run by another former player of note, Greg Norman, had financing in place to attempt a reboot after Saudi Arabia pulled its investment. But BC Partners Credit, which had promised $300 million for what was dubbed “LIV 2.0,” reversed course after seeing the difficulty the golf league was having recruiting players.

Executives who were leading the effort to shepherd LIV through Chapter 11 bankruptcy told a U.S. Bankruptcy Court judge in New Jersey on Thursday that they would pivot toward a full liquidation of the league. The Saudi Public Investment Fund‘s decision to back away from LIV, which had been a major component in the Mideast nation’s effort to promote itself on the global stage, proved to be an insurmountable hurdle. The league regularly drew controversy due to its Saudi backing, drawing protests at a Donald Trump-owned tournament site in New Jersey from families of 9/11 victims outraged by what they saw as the legitimizing of a state sponsor of the terrorist attacks.

Jon Rahm, one of the PGA veterans who accepted large paydays in order to go exclusive with LIV, has recently expressed a willingness to face penalties in order to return. Bryson DeChambeau, perhaps the most ardent promoter of LIV among current major championship contenders, faces an uncertain future after the dissolution of the league. The reluctance of Rahm, DeChambeau and others to sign onto LIV 2.0 sealed its fate.

LIV got its name from the Roman numerals for 54, which is the number of holes its three-day tournaments encompassed. Traditional PGA and USGA events span four days and 72 holes. Along with that departure from golf custom, LIV also allowed players to wear shorts and organized them into Formula 1-style teams with names like the Crushers. On the courses, loud music from DJ tents blared as players teed off, part of an intentional bid to disrupt the country-club feel and hushed tones of the PGA.

While the effort didn’t ultimately succeed, it did force the PGA to update its strategy and philosophy with regard to prize money and other aspects of its relationship with players.

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Max Justicz, the chief technology officer and cofounder of the startup Charge Robotics, used an OpenAI model to find a severe vulnerability that — if exploited — could allow hackers to take control of servers running Ubuntu, a widely-used open-source Linux based operating system.

Justicz, who has a hobbyist’s interest in cybersecurity and previously found a vulnerability in the same codebase, told TechCrunch that he found this issue using GPT 5.6 Sol, released in July, through the AI maker’s Daybreak Blue, a program that gives cybersecurity defenders access to powerful frontier models.

“I literally just aimed ChatGPT at the same body of code and said ‘I found this bug previously that was fixed several years ago, please find bugs as bad or worse,’ and it came back in 2 hours with this,” Justicz said. “It’s extremely convoluted and hard for a human to find, but it works!”

He reported the issue to the Debian Project, which maintains the software in question and issued a patch to fix the vulnerability. There has been no known evidence of exploitation. Still, the ease with which he found it suggests that we’ve only seen the tip of the iceberg when it comes to potential exploits that even novice hackers can utilize with the help of AI models.

“I can only imagine what one could do with more time and access to less neutered models. We should expect some extremely crazy bugs to be found and exploited over the next few months,” Justicz wrote in a social media thread discussing his finding. “This might be obvious to most folks here, but there is currently huge alpha in taking some code or binary someone has not audited yet and just aiming a powerful AI model at it.”

OpenAI appears to have noticed. Today, the AI giant announced a new model focused on cybersecurity, ChatGPT Cyber, would be made available to the highest-tier of users in the cybersecurity program, Daybreak Red, which allows offensive cybersecurity researchers tools for vulnerability hunting and development. Yesterday, OpenAI said that the same model had been used to scan and prepare patches for vulnerabilities in the Linux kernel, finding 948 potential issues. OpenAI also found 81 “novel” vulnerabilities in Firefox, the web browser.

“Our goal is to dramatically expand Daybreak, bringing advanced security research to organizations of every size, independent researchers, and open-source maintainers,” the frontier lab said in a statement.

OpenAI has insisted that there is a narrow window of time for the people who safeguard digital infrastructure to take action before AI models become even better at hacking and easier to access. For cyber defenders, however, it can feel like the frontier labs have unleashed a mountain of new work that outstrips their resources. Justicz said that he personally spent about $5,000 on subscriptions and tokens to find several exploits.

Besides Daybreak’s model access, OpenAI has also announced a $1 billion initiative to support cybersecurity professionals in the program who work on critical infrastructure like the electrical grid or water systems.

The Daybreak program has its own critiques. Justicz notes that the frontier lab appears to have recently “nerfed” Daybreak Blue to make it less useful at finding vulnerabilities. One one hand, it may be good to limit access from potential bad actors — Justicz said he just uploaded a photo of his drivers’ license to join the program. On the other, cybersecurity professionals say they have one hand tied if they can’t use frontier technology while bad actors can deploy open-weight models with abliterated guardrails.

Still, OpenAI says just “thousands” of cybersecurity professionals are participating in the Daybreak program, versus a far larger number of potential hackers.

It’s unclear if frontier labs are devoting enough of their resources to this issue. Eric Wallace, a researcher on OpenAI’s technical staff, said that “we are now allocating a significant amount of our frontier compute towards discovering and patching vulnerabilities in open source codebases, fully pro bono.”

But the Linux bug hunt cost $1.2 million, which is significantly less than the approximate $6 million token spend the company invested in finding a potential solution to a famous problem of fluid dynamics.

If there is a narrow window of time to patch the digital infrastructure the world depends on, more urgency may be required.

Liam Neeson's 2022 Flop Gets A Shot At Redemption With Fox's Dark Thriller

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Although one Liam Neeson action thriller from 2022 never came together quite right, Memory has gotten a shot at redemption with a dark new show from Fox that converts its story into a long-form format. Sometimes, even great adaptations can withstand a second go-around. When 1994’s Anne Rice adaptation Interview with the Vampire assembled an astounding cast including Tom Cruise, Brad Pitt, Antonio Banderas, and Stephen Rea, it seemed like the beguiling movie might never be outdone.

However, after a few decades of killer vampire shows like From Dusk Til Dawn: The Series and Buffy the Vampire Slayer, AMC took another shot at Interview with the Vampire with a longer series based on the same book that debuted in 2022. The result was a genuine masterpiece that made major stars of its leading men, Sam Reid and Jacob Anderson, as well as reintroducing Rice’s classic Gothic tragedy to a new generation of viewers.

Since the original movie was already perfectly good and its TV remake was even better, it stands to reason that a TV show remaking an already-flawed movie is even more understandable. Released in 2022, the Liam Neeson vehicle Memory was an action thriller from Just Play Dead director Martin Campbell. Based on the Jef Geeraerts novel De Zaak Alzheimer, the movie had a killer pitch, but squandered it with forgettable action sequences and a lack of depth. Fortunately, Fox’s Memory Of A Killer can now do justice to the novel.

Created by Ed Whitmore, Fox’s Memory Of A Killer stars Patrick Dempsey as the hitman Angelo Doyle. While Angelo’s brother has already lost his memory to Alzheimer’s disease, Angelo is only just starting to see the effects of the disease materialize in his own faltering memory. Angelo has managed to meticulously hide his secret criminal career from his family for years, but when a hit is put out on his pregnant daughter, his fading memory starts to blur his worlds together.

While Memory Of A Killer has a bigger cast of characters than 2022’s Memory, that is not the only difference between the two movies. In Memory, Neeson’s aging hitman lives in Mexico and is sent on one last job in El Paso, whereas in Memory Of A Killer, Dempsey’s Angelo is based in New York City. Angelo maintains an elaborate second life posing as a photocopier salesman, while Neeson’s lonely protagonist is a full-time contract killer.

Both Dempsey and Neeson's projects are based on 2003’s Belgian thriller The Alzheimer Case, directed by The Loft’s Erik Van Looy. However, while Memory is almost too straightforwardly similar to The Alzheimer Case, Memory Of A Killer changes a lot of plot details. These range from changing the setting to introducing the double life story, to adding Angelo’s backstory as a widower.

The end of Memory Of A Killer season 1 threw Angelo a curveball when one villain’s death only opened up another can of proverbial worms with the revelation of a mysterious document. Meanwhile, Angelo learns that his dementia is getting worse near the end of season 1, meaning it seems increasingly unlikely that Memory Of A Killer’s protagonist will be able to keep his work and personal lives separate as the show continues.

While Memory Of A Killer season 2’s release date has not yet been announced, the Fox show is expected to return early in 2027 as part of the network’s mid-season schedule. Deadline reports that season 2’s cast will introduce Soul Food’s Boris Kodjoe as Tre, the Fourth Musketeer of Angelo’s friend group, while Meta Golding will play Angelo’s childhood love Mel when Memory Of A Killer season 2 arrives next year.

Following the release of macOS 27.3 developer beta 1, Apple is now rolling out the first test build for users enrolled in the public beta. Here’s what’s coming

Contrary to macOS 27.2, it looks like Apple had quite a bit in store for macOS 27.3 and its counterparts.

In addition to expanding Siri AI to four more languages, including Traditional Chinese, Norwegian, Turkish, and Vietnamese, the system also introduces several new Safari features, including Vertical Tabs, Adaptive Dark Mode for websites, and the ability to organize and manage tabs using Siri AI.

Earlier this week, Apple also confirmed that macOS 27.3 will introduce new checks for potentially harmful content shared through public iCloud links. Starting with the update, files and photos shared using the “Anyone with the link” option will be checked by Apple, which may remove flagged content or take action against accounts that violate its policies.

The release build for macOS 27.3 public beta 1 is 26C5035r, which is the same as the developer build.

Are Young Men the New Kings of the Swing Vote?

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Two years ago, it seemed that the young men of Gen Z were shaping up to be hardcore conservatives. More than half of them voted for Donald Trump’s second presidency, refuting the idea that young people tend to lean progressive. Liberal media panicked about finding “a Joe Rogan of the left” who could reach them, and conservatives took a victory lap. “Gen Z Has Turned Right!” Fox News’s Greg Gutfeld announced, citing a report from the Harvard Kennedy School that highlighted, among other insights, the ways social media has led to an increase in conservatism among young folks. A Turning Point USA Poll from around the same time declared Generation Z “the most conservative generation in decades.”

On Tuesday night, though, as a blue tidal wave flipped states from Iowa to Texas, it was young men who comprised a critical segment of the new Democratic coalition. As reported by CIRCLE, a non-partisan research organization at Tufts University, white men between the ages of 18 to 29 voted blue by a 10-point margin; in 2024, exit polling indicated that 56% of all young men voted for Donald Trump. Even more remarkably, young people across the board voted for Democratic congressional candidates by a 40% margin, according to data from NBC.

“In the wake of 2024, there was this whole catastrophizing panic,” says Aidan Kohn-Murphy, the founder of Gen Z for Change (formerly known as TikTok for Biden) and a prominent political influencer. “Oh my God, everything we thought about Gen Z is wrong. It's actually a conservative generation. And no, that's not what was happening. I mean, a huge part of Gen Z just didn't show up to vote because they didn't feel like they had a reason.”

For Democratic strategist Eric Stern, connecting with men on an emotional level was critical in getting them out to vote. Stern has worked on Ruben Gallego and John Fetterman’s campaigns; his political ad agency, FIGHT, has produced spots for Mayor Zohran Mamdani, Senator-Elect Abdul El-Sayed, and Governor-Elect Gina Hinojosa, to name a few. This election cycle, Stern sought to change voters’ perception of what a Democrat can look like.

“Our most notable ad of the general election was Bob [Brooks] standing in front of a literal dumpster fire and then putting it out because he's a firefighter,” he said. Brooks, now the Representative-elect in the Lehigh Valley, flipped his seat on Tuesday. “A ton of Latino men are really into boxing and MMA fighting, and so Ruben [Gallego] held events around boxing,” Stern told me. For Texas’ Hinojosa, who'll become the state’s first Democratic governor in 35 years when she's sworn in in January, that meant running an ad during Mexican boxing champion Canelo Álvarez's match against Christian Mbilli, an event with a massive Latino viewing audience.

Still, Kohn-Murphy and Stern both argued that the main issue on the ballot this cycle was affordability. “If you look at polling, far and away the top issue for voters is cost of living,” Stern said. “And so you can run a million ads saying that ‘Jane Doe is for they/them,’ but it's not going to resonate with people in the same way if they can't afford to keep their lights on.”

When I asked Republican political consultant Kirsten Kukowski about young men voting blue, she brought up affordability too. After all, nearly nine months into the Iran War, a barrel of oil still costs $100. “Men and young men voted with their bank accounts,” she offered. “And for the last several years, that's how they've been voting. And I think that's how Donald Trump won that group two years ago, because he was talking about affordability and inflation.”

Kukowski, the former Communications Director for the Republican National Committee, now runs her own firm, K2 and Company, out of Minneapolis. “At some point here, people expect results,” she continued. “People expected results from Donald Trump, and they didn't see that. So now we're on the clock again for another two years.”

Met Gala 2027: Costume Institute Announces New Theme After John Galliano Controversy

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Lifestyle  |  Read Source →

The 2027 Met Gala has a new focus. On November 5, Vogue, alongside the Metropolitan Museum of Art Costume Institute, announced its new spring 2027 exhibition and Met Gala theme. And its new focus is on the “extraORDINARY: Fashion and the Poetics of the Everyday,” which will be curated by Andrew Bolton alongside Stephanie Kramer and Emily Mushaben. This pivot comes after designer John Galliano pulled out of the 2027 Met Gala months after political and civic leaders raised concerns over his previous racist and antisemitic comments that led to his termination as Dior’s creative director in 2011.

The exhibition will be “devoted to the undersung beauty and allure of quotidian garments—think the aforementioned t-shirts and jeans, plus raincoats, sweatshirts, puffer jackets, and more. It will ask visitors ‘to see the everyday anew, and come at its subject from two perspectives: First, exploring how designers have reinvented and elevated archetypal garments to runway-worthy creations and then, secondly, highlight how humble, even ‘disposable’ materials—like paper, aluminum, and plastic—are transformed into wearable art.”

Google’s first Wyoming data centre will run on gas with carbon capture

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The $17B Cheyenne campus leans on a 702 MW fuel cell plant, with emissions stored underground.

The entrance to Google’s office at Pancras Square in London.

Google will build its first Wyoming data centre, a $17B investment near Cheyenne. Most of its power will come from natural gas, including a 702 MW fuel cell plant from AEP and Bloom Energy, with carbon capture meant to store most emissions underground.

Google will build its first data centre in Wyoming, a long-term $17B investment in Laramie County, the company said on Thursday. Much of its power will come from natural gas, with carbon capture meant to keep most of the emissions out of the air. It follows a $15B plan for Malaysia that Google announced on Wednesday.

The main source is a 702 MW fuel cell plant from American Electric Power, which turns natural gas into electricity without burning it. AEP said in October it would spend about $4B on the Bloom Energy plant near Cheyenne. Google will buy its power under a 20-year deal, POWER Magazine reported, and the plant should be fully running by the end of 2028.

Carbon from the fuel cells will be captured and stored underground at Tallgrass Energy’s nearby Eastern Wyoming Sequestration Hub. Google says this will remove “a large majority” of the plant’s emissions, but has not given a figure. It calls the plant the largest of its kind planned so far.

Google will also buy power from gas plants run by Tallgrass and Black Hills Energy, and is studying whether carbon capture can be fitted to Tallgrass’s turbines. With Wyoming, its contracted power from plants with carbon capture reaches nearly 1 GW. Its first such deal, for a gas plant in Illinois, came in October 2025.

Google says it will pay for all the power it uses and the grid upgrades its growth requires, something US lawmakers want Big Tech to do. The data centre will be air-cooled to save water.

Water is a sore point in Cheyenne. In July, the city halted some data centre water discharges after a Meta contractor released a rare bacterium into its wastewater reuse system.

Google expects the project to bring in hundreds of millions of dollars in property taxes for the county and its schools. It and its partners are also setting up a community council, after more than 200 residents raised worries about how data centre growth is changing Cheyenne. Google announced the project two days after US voters rejected data centres in 40 of 41 local ballot measures.

The Colombian superstar's massive party featured guests including Greg González, John Summit and more.

Karol G celebrated her album No Me Arrepiento de Sentir Tanto with a massive party in Mexico City, an exclusive event streamed on TikTok on Wednesday night (Nov. 4), where millions of fans tuned in simultaneously. The Colombian superstar performed a variety of songs during the show and featured special guests such as Greg González, the group La Obsesión and DJ John Summit, who announced the remix of her hit “BbY WOW.”

“La Bichota” achieved a historic milestone as the artist with the highest number of simultaneous viewers on a TikTok Live, reaching 4.8 million stream entries on the platform and recording 3.6 million unique viewers, according to a press release.

Held at Quarry Studios with about 2,000 fans in the venue, the performance served as a prelude to the Latin American leg of her current Viajando Por El Mundo Tropitour, which kicks off with six shows in two of Mexico’s biggest stadiums: Nov. 6, 7 and 8 at Monterrey’s Estadio BBVA, and Nov. 13, 14 and 15 at Mexico City’s Estadio GNP Seguros.

The nearly hour-long celebration began at 8 p.m. local time with the songs “Maybe” and “Bebiendo lágrimas.” It followed with “Eclipse,” a piano rendition of “Final Feliz” and “Matadora.” Joined by Greg González, leader of the rock band Cigarettes After Sex on guitar, she performed “Después de Ti.”

“This is my favorite song from the entire album, so I hope you sing it really loud,” Karol G said as she introduced “Con Quién Andará?” while dancing with her fans.

Released Aug. 7 via Bichota/Interscope/Capitol, No Me Arrepiento de Sentir Tanto debuted at No. 1 on Billboard’s Top Latin Albums chart and spent 11 weeks at the summit. It also entered the Billboard 200 top 10, peaking at No. 8.

The original track, recorded with Judeline and rusowsky, has spent three weeks at No. 1 on the Latin Airplay chart and six weeks atop the Billboard Global 200 and Billboard Global Excl. U.S. charts. The setlist also included a remix of “AltaFarra,” featuring Mexican band La Obsesión and DJ Maracuya.

Following her North American trek, the Viajando Por El Mundo Tropitour now begins its journey in Latin America, with Mexico as one of its key stops before continuing to Europe.

Karol’s arrival in the country comes after a year of significant milestones in her career. At the 2026 Billboard Latin Music Awards, she took home four honors: Hot Latin Songs Artist of the Year, Female; Top Latin Albums Artist of the Year, Female; Latin Pop Song of the Year for “Coleccionando heridas,” with Marco Antonio Solís; and Fan Favorite Artist, a new category based on public voting.

These achievements add to her seven nominations at the 27th Annual Latin Grammy Awards, including in general categories such as album of the year for Tropicoqueta, as well as record of the year and song of the year for “Coleccionando Heridas.”
