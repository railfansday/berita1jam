Contenders Hall Of Fame Honoree Peter Farrelly Talks ‘I Play Rocky’, ‘Dumb & Dumber’, The Hair Gel Bit In ‘There’s Something About Mary’ & More

📅 Nov 14, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Prolific director, screenwriter and producer Peter Farrelly reflected on some of his most famous films Saturday at Deadline’s Contenders Film Los Angeles event.

Contenders Hall of Fame honoree Farrelly talked with Deadline’s Mike Fleming Jr about his most recent film I Play Rocky and the process of bringing it to the screen. Earlier, Farrelly had appeared on the film’s panel with star Anthony Ippolito, who plays Sylvester Stallone in the film, and talked about the uphill battle to making the crowd-pleasing underdog tale.

Farrelly revealed that it was his daughter’s ex-boyfriend who first read the script and told Farrelly it “was the best script he had ever read.” Farrelly agreed and pushed forward with the film, noting it was imperative that he had Stallone’s approval on the script, which came quickly.

He also shared his own “Rocky” moment: “The bravest thing I ever did in my life was decide to write when I was in my mid-20s,” he said.

RELATED: ‘I Play Rocky’s Peter Farrelly Talks Why The Making Of ‘Rocky’ Was The Next Real-Life Story He Wanted To Take A Swing At – Toronto Studio

Farrelly also talked about his years and years of getting turned down for scripts and having to push through the rejection and not give up. As for 1994’s Dumb & Dumber, “We wrote that script five years before it got made, and it was the exact same script,” he shared. “Every studio passed, every actor passed.” He acknowledged that he changed the title to A Power Tool Is Not a Toy and “everyone started reading it.” It was the eventual film’s star Jim Carrey who told him Dumb & Dumber is the title, “so we went back to that,” he said.

“You’ve got to believe,, that they’re wrong and you’re right,” he said regarding the rejections. “You know, year after year getting turned down, it’s hard. You just gotta stick out and hope that you’re right.”

One of the most memorable and hilarious scenes in Farrelly’s films was in There’s Something About Mary and Cameron Diaz’s “hair gel.” “I realized we were going to have to go to R [in that film] because we were right on the PG-13 line.

“There’s two things that the studio didn’t want was the zipper joke in the beginning and the hair gel thing of course, and we understood that, especially the hair gel.” But of course, the hair gel stayed in and the rest is history.

Netflix's Magic: The Gathering series was initially announced in 2019 with the MCU's Russo Brothers at the helm, though they left a couple of years later thanks to the all-purpose "creative differences". That was all we heard until 2024 until Brandon Routh, who had been cast as planeswalker Gideon Jura, casually mentioned in an unrelated interview that the project had been binned.

Netflix announced soon after that actually the animated series was a go, now with Terry Matalas of Star Trek: Picard and the 12 Monkeys TV series as its head showrunner, with a teaser image showing pyromancer Chandra and leonin Ajani Goldmane silhouetted against a red sky.

Now, a mere seven years after the first reveal, there's a "sneak peek" trailer with a bunch of excited Wizards of the Coast staff, art labeled "early development", and a few seconds of Ajani and Chandra doing their stuff.

That's a good idea. An animated series with different art styles for each plane the characters travel to would be a great way of representing Magic, a card game where every artist gets to put their own spin on how things look. Young talent will be trusted, Bruno Fernandes can thrive and touchline presence is vital - what Man United fans can learn as incoming boss Mauricio Pochettino masterminds 6-0 win for USA

📅 2026-11-14  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Published: 18:47 EST, 14 November 2026 | Updated: 19:39 EST, 14 November 2026

Mauricio Pochettino enjoyed a stellar penultimate game in charge of the United States, thrashing Haiti 6-0.

The newly appointed Manchester United manager has just one game left in charge of the USA before getting to work at Old Trafford.

He will link up with United on Wednesday, but first must navigate the second leg of their Concacaf Nations League quarter-final tie with Haiti on Tuesday.

Here, Pochettino cut a fairly reserved figure on the touchline as he was rewarded for trusting youth - and even saw his team score from a corner, something United don't do much of these days.

Without further ado, here are four things Manchester United fans learned from the win.

Mauricio Pochettino, dressed in a scarf, was fairly reserved as the United States beat Haiti 6-0

Pochettino’s arrival spells good news for United’s proud tradition of promoting homegrown youth - it is glad tidings for the likes of Shea Lacey and Kobbie Mainoo. You wonder if, for a while, JJ Gabriel will look on forlornly from Real Madrid’s reserves.

The Argentinian handed out 13 debuts in the September-October international break, including to eight teenagers in one 4-1 victory over Peru.

His strategy was no different here as he started 18-year-old Julian Hall up front and Diego Kochen, 20, in goal - both players he has given debuts to this year. He was rewarded, with Hall scoring his fourth goal in just five games for the United States, while Kochen kept a clean sheet.

Despite his affinity for the stars of tomorrow, he is a hard schoolmaster. In a column for The Athletic in 2022, he revealed that he sat a young Harry Kane down for a brutal meeting where he showed him stats and clips to justify why he wasn’t playing him more.

Pochettino had known Kane’s potential from their first training session but wanted the ‘tough conversation’ to get the best out of him. Kane went on to score 169 goals under him for Tottenham, and now he’s the best player in the world.

His trust in fresh faces could inject some much-needed enthusiasm around the club, strengthen the squad's connection with the fanbase, and increase the energy levels in one of the Premier League's oldest, most lethargic teams.

The likes of Shea Lacey will be delighted at how Pochettino puts trust in younger players

Julian Hall, 18, struck his fourth goal in five games for the United States under Pochettino

Pochettino has a history of getting the most out of attacking midfielders such as Dele Alli, Christian Eriksen, and Adam Lallana. You can add Cole Palmer to that list too, though he was primarily a right winger under him at Chelsea.

Manchester United desperately need him to get a tune out of Bruno Fernandes, who has cut a frustrated figure this season and whose creative efficiency has declined - though he still has nine goal involvements.

If Christian Pulisic’s performance against Haiti is anything to go by, Fernandes will be happy. The attacking midfielder scored twice, playing a free role and being given penalty duty.

The 28-year-old was also asked to muck in defensively, and that’s something Fernandes is willing to do. Across the seasons, he has often covered more ground than any of his team-mates.

Pochettino and Pulisic have seemingly had a distant relationship, despite the latter’s insistence to the contrary. The manager branded it ‘b*******’ when the AC Milan star opted out of the Gold Cup last year, and left him out of his selection a couple of months ago, bringing in 16-year-old Cavan Sullivan.

Asked about Pochettino's upcoming final game, Pulisic said at full-time: 'We're treating it like any other game.' No emotion on display.

Depending on how you interpret Fernandes - either a die-for-the-badge club servant or a primadonna - Pochettino could draw the best or worst out of the Portuguese.

Pochettino often gets the best out of attacking midfielders - will he work well with Bruno Fernandes?

He has had a distant relationship with Christian Pulisic, but the AC Milan star scored twice here

Pochettino tends to push his defensive lines high up the pitch and his strategy with the United States has been no different.

They were almost caught out for trying to play the offside trap early on as a simple ball from midfield sent Danley Jean Jacques through one-on-one, forcing Kochen into a good save.

Meanwhile, full-backs Antonee Robinson and Alex Freeman were constantly pushing high and overlapping in his 4-2-3-1 system - although you’d obviously expect a strong level of intent when they’re favourites against Haiti.

Given his high-line tradition, Pochettino may well favour more mobile defenders such as Leny Yoro and Patrick Dorgu.

The battle for Harry Maguire will be to convince his new boss that his other qualities compensate for his lack of pace.

Pochettino takes inspiration from the frenetic football of Marcelo Bielsa, who managed him at Newell’s Old Boys in Argentina.

But he'll have to balance his philosophy with the reality of United's woeful defensive record this campaign. Just the 15 goals conceded in the last five games...

Pochettino showed an eagerness to intervene early in the game - a possible boost after Michael Carrick suffered from slow starts

Pochettino is typically seen as an animated character on the touchline, in contrast to Carrick's statesmanlike behaviour, but he spent lots of time in his seat here.

He has calmed down somewhat these days - though he did kick a crate of bottles in frustration at the World Cup - and didn’t even react much when his team scored.

A major criticism of Carrick is that he takes too long to change things in-game, often going late with substitutions. Pochettino has traditionally leaned that way, too, but he was eager to influence proceedings here.

Even three minutes in, at a break while Antonee Robinson was being treated, he called Pulisic over for what looked like an intense tactical chat.

United have been plagued by slow starts this season, so it was encouraging to see Pochettino so alert and full of ideas early into the game. He could have coasted, really, but wanted to eke out every advantage.

Common reasons your Roku TV can turn on by itself and how to fix it

📅 Nov 14, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Some people have been experiencing random power-ons recently.

You're sound asleep in the middle of the night when, all of a sudden, your TV turns on. You know you turned off the TV before you went to bed, no one else is awake, and there's nothing near your remote to accidentally push the power button. What — or who — could have turned on the TV? A bored spirit haunting your home? A burglar looking to take a quick break?

Those scenarios are highly unlikely. But, unfortunately, some Roku TVs and Roku streaming device owners have been jolted out of their slumber by those products inexplicably powering on randomly over the past few months. And no one really knows why it's happening. There may be a few explanations, however, and a couple of potential fixes to try if your Roku device has gone rogue during the wee hours.

When Roku OS version 15.3 went live in August 2026, owners of Roku TVs and Roku streaming devices began reporting these middle-of-the-night disturbances. Not every Roku TV or streaming player has been impacted by this supposed 15.3 bug, but the timing of the reports with the software update seems more than coincidental.

What's odd about that, though, is that there was nothing in the 15.3 update that involved autoplay features or even power management. So, in theory, the changes that were made to the Roku OS shouldn't have spurred these instances of people's Roku TVs "waking up" without being powered on by anyone (or anything) and playing a TV show or movie. But we all know that software updates often introduce bugs with no real rhyme or reason, and that feels like the most logical explanation for these strange occurrences.

Having your Roku TV's HDMI-CEC feature enabled is another very plausible reason the TV powers on randomly. If you're not familiar with HDMI-CEC, it's basically a chain that links all your A/V components with built-in HDMI switching (like soundbars, gaming consoles, receivers and speakers) since they're all connected via HDMI cables. And this allows all of those devices to be controlled with one remote.

So, when you power on your Roku TV, for example, HDMI-CEC essentially notifies all the other connected components to turn on. All it takes is one glitchy signal to confuse the whole chain, which could lead to a random power-up. And there is a history of HDMI-CEC causing strange switching between inputs and components, as well as phantom power-ons. Samsung Anynet+, Sony BRAVIA Sync, LG SimpLink and Roku 1-Touch Play are all just different names for the same HDMI-CEC standard, but each has its own custom software added to it to create the proprietary HDMI-CEC feature for each brand of smart TV.

Unfortunately, compatibility sometimes becomes an issue unless all of your connected devices are made by the same company (though even that doesn't guarantee incident-free performance). This can cause signal miscommunication that results in the random behavior of your Roku TV or streaming device.

There are other possible explanations for your Roku TV turning on by itself. For example, the Fast TV Start feature is a proprietary power-management setting that keeps your Roku TV's OS on in the background even when you shut off the TV.

This allows the TV to turn on instantly without any boot time, and it keeps the TV on alert for voice commands to turn the TV back on. Fast TV Start has also seemingly caused phantom power-on problems in conjunction with the Roku OS 15.3 update, as the always-on network connection kept open the door for overnight glitches.

You may try all of these suggestions and still not have much luck because the root of the issue has not been "officially" identified by Roku, or anyone else, for that matter. And without the root cause, there remains no guaranteed fix. But these possible solutions are certainly worth trying and just might do the trick.

The first thing to try, if you haven't already, is updating your Roku TV's OS to version 16, which was released in October. Fixing the 15.3 bug wasn't part of the update, and no reports have surfaced that OS 16 patched the automatic power-on problem, but it's definitely worth trying. The worst-case scenario is that your Roku TV's OS is fully updated.

Next, disable 1-Touch Play (HDMI-CEC) not just on your Roku TV but on all the other components connected to the TV, regardless of brand. This includes your A/V equipment and gaming consoles. To do so on your Roku TV, go to Settings > System > Control other devices and uncheck 1-Touch Play. Also disable Fast TV Start, which you can find under Settings > System > Power > Fast TV Start, then either clear the Enable Fast TV Start option or check Off to disable it.

If these don't work, try unplugging the TV when you're done watching it each night (no, not ideal), using a smart plug to automatically cut the power for you or consulting Roku's tech support portal at support.roku.com for further assistance.

Two different shocks, a historical Binance breach, and an AI token sell-off. Though they seem unrelated on the surface, this time they were actually the catalyst for the same event. What Bitcoin saw was a surge in speculative positioning, but what the king coin did not see was a buyer.

The market’s first take on the Binance hack was that crypto had passed a test. Bitcoin fell by about 6% from its 28th October close — a fraction of the 15% to 39% drawdowns after the controversies surrounding Celsius, FTX, BlockFi, and Bybit.

Matt Mena, Crypto Research Strategist at 21Shares, told AMBCrypto,

What we find most encouraging is how the market handled the worst of it. The largest hack in crypto’s history caused a far smaller drawdown than past crises; the protocols kept running, and none of the structural drivers broke.

Unfortunately, institutional resilience is not retail demand. The market simply treated a smaller slump as evidence of Bitcoin’s underlying strength. However, whether anyone wanted to buy Bitcoin [BTC] was a separate question, one the hack never answered.

Still, institutional strength hasn’t eased the feeling of dread felt by many retail investors. And, a major factor can explain why.

The most important signal is what happened after the liquidations: Nothing. Bitget Wallet’s analysts put it plainly that the leverage reset “has cleared some speculative excess, but is yet to address the underlying demand problem.”

Looking at the data, one can see why. Most importantly, the ETF flows changed drastically. There were $2.6 billion worth of inflows in September 2026, which turned into $290 million of net outflows in October. Currently, U.S ETF holders sit underwater below their cost basis of $82K.

How ETF holders behave below breakeven remains the key tell for November.

Also, stablecoin supply has not been moving outside the $305 billion zone, a sure sign that liquidity is sitting still.

Finally, one cannot forget the fallout caused by the Binance hack.

When the exchange reopened withdrawals, the queue moved fast. The exchange processed $4.65 billion against $1.01 billion of deposits in under ten hours; net outflows reached about $5.8 billion over 24 hours!

Each of these factors may not matter when seen separately, but when read together, they describe the same thing — capital leaving or sitting still. The leverage is gone, and the bid is not there to replace it.

For 21Shares, $73,000 is the 200-day moving average, and “the line that would reopen the bear-market debate.”

Bitget analysts told AMBCrypto that Bitcoin must hold the $73K–$74K range.

The 73,000-74,000 range is now the zone Bitcoin needs to hold. Stabilization would require more balanced derivatives positioning and sustained positive ETF flows. Reclaiming and holding $80,000 would signal stronger demand, while a decisive break below $73,000 could expose $68,000. If $68,000 also fails to hold, the selloff would suggest more than a temporary leverage flush.

When the same number pops up again and again, it stops being a coincidence and starts pointing to a pattern — that Bitcoin is either in a temporary flush or at the start of something deeper.

If buyers do return, Bitcoin can comfortably start holding $80,000, and stronger demand would emerge as a result. Getting back above $81,000 to $82,000 would finally put the largest holder cohorts back in profit.

Both reports are similarly aligned on what won’t fix Bitcoin’s buyer problem: Macro relief without demand. In fact, Bitget’s analysts asserted,

A durable Bitcoin recovery will depend on improving liquidity and Spot demand, not macro relief alone.

Matt Mena framed the same requirement as a four-part checklist.

We are watching four things into November: Bitcoin holding $76,000 to $78,000 and reclaiming $80,000 to $82,000, Binance completing withdrawals and proving its reserves, credit spreads, oil and long-end yields, and whether ETF flows return. If those turn, the setup for the final weeks of the year remains constructive.

Notice what’s on both lists: ETF flows, liquidity, Spot demand, and positioning. All of them are things buyers have to do.

There is naturally no doubt that macro factors can move Bitcoin’s price. If everything aligns, a rally can happen tomorrow. However, seasoned investors know that this will never be the same as rebuilding buyer interest.

Ultimately, a macro relief rally would leave Bitcoin’s demand problem exactly where it was.

The calendar offers a boost for Bitcoin regardless of which way the ball drops, as December’s Fed expectations hinge on U.S CPI and PPI readings. For the uninitiated, hotter readings will reinforce rate-hike expectations, and a softer core inflation will trigger a relief rally.

However, Bitget’s analysts draw a line between the two outcomes, noting,

This week’s inflation data adds another test. Hotter readings could reinforce December rate-hike expectations, while softer core inflation could trigger a relief rally. But a durable Bitcoin recovery will depend on improving liquidity and spot demand, not macro relief alone.

Basically, a relief rally can be classed as a macro event, not a demand event. The inflation print can only deliver the former, and the latter can only come about by real flows.

Here, it’s also worth being precise about what the 5th November AI crash tells us. 21Shares calls it,

A renewed wobble in artificial intelligence (AI) equities pulled the Nasdaq-100 down almost 4%.

That’s the mechanism in a nutshell. Even this time, crypto’s fall was the result of trading alongside a risk asset.

Still, if Bitcoin’s move below $74,000 was driven by the AI wipeout, then the absence of a bid is more telling. Eventually, we come back to the same problem of missing buyers.

Leverage has been reset twice in a week — Once by Binance’s $6.3 billion hack, once by an AI shocker. And the buyer bid has hardly appeared either time.

That’s what makes $73,000 a critical level to watch. If buyers return and hold this level, they could help Bitcoin reclaim $80,000. This would also imply that BTC’s demand problem is cyclical, not permanent.

However, a break below $73,000 will hint at — and a failure at $68,000 will confirm — that Bitcoin’s breakdown is structural. Bitcoin’s recovery hinges on a single level, and the lack of buyers has never been felt stronger.
