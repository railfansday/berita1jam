U.S. export controls and Nvidia’s leadership transition remain key watchpoints

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Nvidia’s solid third-quarter results will no doubt reassure Gulf investors that the U.S. chipmaking giant is on course to maintain a healthy growth trajectory despite the resignation of Jensen Huang.

Nvidia reported revenue of $116 billion for the three months to October, up 103% from a year ago, and above Wall Street estimates of $109 billion. It has beaten estimates in each of the last five quarters.

For the Gulf states, which are betting big on AI to help move their economies away from oil and rely on Nvidia’s advanced chips to achieve that, the company’s future success is critical.

Huang’s affair with a woman suspected of being a Chinese spy has heightened political pressure to tighten export controls amid long-running concerns in the U.S. that China’s access to advanced American AI chips could undermine national security.

It is also likely to sharpen scrutiny of Nvidia’s future dealings with the Gulf as part of a broader effort to prevent China from accessing banned American technology via intermediaries.

Such concerns have previously made securing Nvidia’s advanced chips an uphill battle for regional AI champions such as PIF-owned HUMAIN and Abu Dhabi-backed G42.

In July, the U.S. Department of Commerce loosened export restrictions on the UAE so that approved government agencies and entities—including its tech champion G42—can receive advanced computing hardware, including from Nvidia, under broader license exceptions. The new rule set no caps on how many chips or other sophisticated goods can be sold to those entities.

Democratic China hawks have frequently raised the alarm over the large-scale sales of AI chips to the Gulf region, arguing they could give China backdoor access to U.S. technology. Last May, Democratic senators demanded that AI chip deals with Saudi Arabia and the UAE contain adequate guardrails to prevent the leakage of sensitive technology to China.

Both Gulf countries maintain strong commercial ties to Chinese tech entities.

In September, Chinese tech giant Alibaba Cloud announced plans to ramp up its data center capacity and expand localized AI services across the UAE. Meanwhile, in Riyadh, Lenovo unveiled its first laptop made in Saudi Arabia at its sprawling 200,000-square-meter manufacturing facility, developed as part of a $2 billion partnership with Alat, a subsidiary of Saudi Arabia’s Public Investment Fund (PIF).

In its first policy response to the Nvidia scandal, on Monday the U.S. Commerce Department introduced an interim rule requiring licences for global transfers of AI chips and servers to companies in 22 embargoed countries, including China. The move aims to close a loophole that has allowed Chinese companies to lease Nvidia’s computing power from locations in other countries.

With Democrats holding majorities in both chambers of Congress from January next year, increased congressional scrutiny of Gulf AI deals is likely.

More broadly, the Gulf’s exposure to the volatility in AI stock prices has been further rattled by Jensen’s resignation amid a broader sell-off in AI tech stocks over the past few months, on the back of valuation and investment bubble concerns.

While not all Gulf funds disclose their individual investments, publicly available filings suggest that Gulf sovereign wealth funds, such as Mubadala, have relatively limited direct exposure to Nvidia shares compared to the scale of their investments in other tech firms.

According to the consultancy, Global SWF, Mubadala ranks among the top institutional backers of AI worldwide.

MGX, an AI and advanced tech investment firm backed by Mubadala, holds stakes and has participated in funding rounds for OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI, Databricks, GlobalFoundries, Isomorphic Labs, and SpaceX.

Saudi’s PIF-backed AI venture HUMAIN invested $3 billion in xAI’s $20 billion Series E round in February, shortly before it merged with SpaceX.

Global SWF estimates that Mubadala’s portfolio may have declined by $17.5 billion since the onset of the AI selloff at the end of June, largely driven by a more than 50% drop in chipmaker GlobalFoundries’ share price. PIF, meanwhile, is estimated to have suffered a $5.6 billion decline in the value of its AI-related holdings, largely attributable to its stake in SpaceX.

“Indirectly and strategically, Gulf wealth funds are exposed to the current market turbulence,” Sam Winter-Levy, a senior fellow in the Technology and International Affairs program at the Carnegie Endowment for International Peace, told me.

“MGX and other Gulf vehicles hold large stakes in AI labs, data center platforms, and other companies whose valuations depend on the AI boom—and Nvidia, of course, is central to that boom. The Gulf’s national AI strategies are built on Nvidia hardware. I think a prolonged loss of confidence in Nvidia would hit the Gulf relatively hard.”

In a research note published on Monday, London-headquartered Capital Economics warned that the ongoing shakeout in the AI sector is setting the stage for a larger downturn in the U.S. equity market over the next year.

With Nvidia considered a bellwether for the AI industry, its results should help to calm the markets and buoy investor sentiment ahead of Anthropic’s planned listing on Thursday.

Abu Dhabi-backed Masdar has completed its $950 million Lockhart Solar and Storage complex in California, marking another major step in the clean energy giant’s growing U.S. renewables push.

With 365-MW of solar generation and 173.7MW of battery storage, the project can produce electricity to meet the annual needs of about 350,000 homes and businesses.

Commercially, the project is backed by a hybrid of long-term power purchase agreements with California utilities and corporates.

The project was developed by Terra-Gen, which is owned jointly by Masdar and U.K.-headquartered global infrastructure manager, Igneo Infrastructure Partners.

Masdar operates a major part of its U.S. footprint through Terra-Gen, an independent U.S. renewables developer and operator, in which Masdar acquired a 50% stake in 2024 alongside Igneo.

Masdar has already invested around $3 billion in renewable energy projects across the U.S., with a portfolio reaching 6.3 GW across onshore wind, solar, and battery energy storage systems as of Sep. 2026.

It is targeting an expansion of its portfolio by as much as 25-GW by 2030, capitalising on rising power demand from data centers, industrial electrification, and expanding domestic manufacturing.

Globally, Masdar is also pressing ahead with its ambition to reach 100-GW of portfolio capacity by the end of the decade.

Its U.S. expansion comes despite the federal rollback of clean energy incentives under the Trump administration. A strong recovery in global oil supplies helped the market return to a surplus in October, the first time since the outbreak of the U.S.-Iran war.

The higher export rates prompted the Paris-based agency to "significantly" raise its expectations for global oil supply for the remainder of 2026, with fourth-quarter estimates now 3.3 million bpd higher.

The IEA forecasts fourth-quarter supply will average 105 million bpd, exceeding projected demand by 1.7 million bpd.

The oil market is "straightening out" and "showing tentative signs of rebalancing,” it said.

It follows seven months of disruption to global oil flows, as the ongoing war choked off shipments through the Strait of Hormuz, a critical shipping route that normally carries around a fifth of the world’s oil and gas supplies.

“But there is an obvious risk that things either fail to improve, or even get worse,” it added.

The Perfect 'Deadwood' Cast if the Show Were Made in 2026

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

It's been more than 20 years, and Deadwood still remains one of the greatest shows in HBO history. Created by David Milch, the three-season series is set in 1876 Deadwood, South Dakota, and follows the titular camp as it operates as a lawless sinkhole of crime, gambling, and rough-and-tumble survival. The town explodes from a muddy, chaotic tent camp into a bustling settlement driven by a massive gold strike that draws prospectors, outlaws, entrepreneurs, and working women.

Filled to the brim with dramatized figures from history, Deadwood is a masterful mix of Western idealism and the true grit of surviving in a changing moment in history. At its core, the series explores how people piece together rules, commerce, and community from pure chaos when formal government and laws are absent. The heart of the show centers on incredible characters, brought to life by an iconic ensemble. So, what would happen if Deadwood were made in 2026? Though the show is untouchable for devoted fans, this fantasy casting honors the project's heartbeat with a new ensemble of actors who capture the characters' essence.

The true heartbeat of Deadwood came from the heroic man with the badge, Seth Bullock. He arrives in the lawless mining camp as a former Montana marshal looking to start a new life. Alongside his friend and business partner, Sol Star (John Hawkes), he sets up a hardware and general store before becoming Deadwood's first sheriff. Loosely based on the real-life historical figure, much of Seth's story revolves around keeping Deadwood morally afloat. Timothy Olyphant brought him to life perfectly, launching him into an archetype that made him famous. Offering a soft-spoken, gentle demeanor, Olyphant subverted the traditional Western hero. With a physical presence and fixed glare, Olyphant's restraint made Seth's moments of explosion feel earned. He masked a deep internal rage, but he was still quite stubborn.

As one half of the dominating duo on Deadwood, selecting an actor who has the same essence as Olyphant is required. A brilliant selection would be Logan Lerman. His career evolution aligns perfectly with Seth's core tension. He went from boyish charm with a quiet side hiding impulses within. In projects like Hunters and Indignation, Lerman mastered the art of playing characters who look unassuming but harbor boiling, dark intensity beneath the surface. He carries a classic, honorable leading man quality that evokes a trustworthiness that would allow him to lead a camp of chaotic and unpredictable characters. Even early in his career, Lerman excelled at characters with heavy dramatic undertones, namely The Perks of Being a Wallflower, but as he has matured, he has built the gravitas to take on a hardened lawman.

There is no doubt that Al Swearengen is one of the greatest television characters of all time. Some might have felt he was a caricature compared to more grounded characters, but Swearengen's quirks and eccentricities made him genuinely Western. Swearengen is a criminal operator, pimp, and master manipulator who runs the most successful bar and brothel in Deadwood. He is foul-mouthed, violent, and cold-blooded, yet he has a complex code of pragmatism, fierce loyalty to his inner circle, and a driving urge to guide Deadwood toward survival. He's a brilliant antihero who begins the series as the established villain, only to be outdone as new faces arrive. Swearengen is a masterclass in character, thanks to Ian McShane's pitch-perfect performance. Though based on the real-life figure, McShane made him larger than life. He blended a terrifying ruthlessness with theatrical, Shakespearean depth. McShane delivered Milch's dense, rhythmic monologues with a commanding cadence.

With a Machiavellian presence, McShane could have Swearengen command a room atop a balcony, offering a cold calculation like a mob boss. To play Swearengen, an actor with the same power and mastery of the text is required. An actor who loves a good antihero and could reinvent brutal, eloquent, fierce loyalty is Rufus Sewell. He has built a career playing characters who operate in moral grey areas. From his chillingly calculated performance as John Smith in The Man in the High Castle to the charismatic yet deeply flawed Hal Wyler in The Diplomat, he brings a dark magnetism to the screen. Sewell has an imposing, intense gaze and powerful vocal delivery that can pivot from quiet threat to an explosive outburst. As an accomplished stage actor, Sewell has the linguistic dexterity to tackle Swearengen's text. Sewell could easily pull back the layers of the monstrous man to reveal the broken humanity underneath.

One of the more tragic and human characters in the series was Alma Garret. She arrived in the South Dakota gold-mining camp with her husband, Brom Garret (Timothy Omundson), to seek their fortune. After her husband's suspicious death, which was orchestrated indirectly through camp politics, she inherited a rich gold claim, establishing her own bank and independence, as she was not going to be pushed out by men. She struggled with and overcame an addiction to laudanum, had a complicated romance with Seth Bullock, but ultimately formed a unique family when she married prospector Whitney Ellsworth (Jim Beaver) and adopted a young orphan girl named Sofia Metz (Bree Seanna Wall). Bringing a quiet, nuanced strength to a complex frontier world, Molly Parker was anything but fragile. She shone by showing Alma's resilience, then shifting into an emotional palette comprised of fear and grief.

At her core, Alma was a brilliant character because she celebrated her determination, savviness, and sharpness. She had to mature into an independent woman quickly, yet still had an inherent youthful refinement. An actress who could capture that same emotional intelligence is Hailee Steinfeld. And wouldn't it be perfect since her rise to stardom came in a heavy Western, True Grit? Even at a young age, Steinfeld proved she could handle the demanding, rhythmically complex dialogue of high-end Westerns. Alma needs a duality of high-society and internal gruffness. Steinfeld has shown those traits in projects like Dickinson and The Edge of Seventeen, playing women constrained by societal environments but with a rebellious edge. Having previously played in male-heavy, ensemble-driven environments like the Marvel Cinematic Universe and Bumblebee, Steinfeld could capture Alma's core arc of navigating a camp run by aggressive, dangerous men.

There were certainly plenty of bad guys in Deadwood, but there was one man known for being beloved and ethical: Whitney Ellsworth. The outspoken prospector began his journey as a loquacious man; then, when he witnessed Dan Dority (W. Earl Brown) murder Brom Garret, he swooped in to help Alma Garret with her gold claim, ultimately finding a marriage arrangement that changed their relationship and formed a surrogate family in a camp of individuality. His fate was sadly sealed when he went toe-to-toe with George Hearst (Gerald McRaney), standing up for what was right. But in the Wild West, adversity can lead to death. Ellsworth's death remains one of the most devastating events in the entire story. Jim Beaver transformed the minor, unkempt prospector into an emotionally resonant hero. Beaver found a tenderness in Ellsworth that matched the working-class dignity that gave the camp warmth.

Ellsworth was created with great charisma and unpretentious decency. Whoever plays the part must be able to transform from heavy-drinking gold miner to beloved father figure. Shea Whigham would be a perfect fit, as he possesses the exact blend of working-class grit, underlying nobility, and tragic weariness required to build Ellsworth. Ellsworth is a tragicomic character, and Whigham has created a career playing deeply flawed men wrestling with guilt and searching for redemption. His performances in Boardwalk Empire and Lake George prove he can tackle an individual beaten down by life but still capable of good. Whigham's masterful resume in ensemble pieces is further proof that his generosity would be welcome in this profoundly brilliant series.

The camp was filled with a lot of eccentric personalities, often defined by their trade. As one of the most needed individuals in Deadwood, Dr. Amos "Doc" Cochran was the sole physician in the mining camp. A former Civil War battlefield doctor, Doc is deeply scarred by violence, suffering, and amputations witnessed during the war. He is honest, compassionate, and moral, treating anyone who needs help, regardless of their status, creed, or background. Doc had some wonderful moments during his time, including inventing a specialized leg brace attached to a boot for the character Jewel (Geri Jewell), but that was countered by a bout with tuberculosis and an affinity for grave robbing. Played by Brad Dourif, he balanced deep, raw empathy with a weary, nervous intensity that allowed Doc to feel richly human. Dourif brought a layered performance to the series, finding nuances with each character he shared a scene with. Whether being berated by Swearengen or having a delightfully playful bond with Jewel, Doc's presence was always spelled fun.

To take on Doc in a new iteration, you need an actor with the same decency. He must have instinctual intelligence, yet emotional honesty. A great option would be Ben Whishaw. He has the precise blend of raw vulnerability, sharp intellect, and world-weary cynicism to step into his iconic boots. As the sole medical authority in camp, Doc needs to be dependable, yet never let that go to his head. Whishaw showed that same energy in This Is Going to Hurt. His portrayal of a brilliant, utterly depleted, and stressed doctor managing a broken system perfectly mirrors Doc's overwhelmed, yet deeply dedicated bedside manner. Whishaw has played men with fractured psyches, including The Hollow Crown, which would allow him to tap into the trauma that Doc carries. While Dourif brought a wild-eyed, older eccentricity to the character, Whishaw would have a bit more youthful energy. Arriving alongside Seth Bullock, he was a natural entrepreneur, and unlike many opportunistic figures, Sol served as a steady, practical, and compassionate moral conscience. Based on the real-life Jewish immigrant, John Hawkes brought a quiet, genuine, gentlemanly quality to the story. He navigated the subtle camp prejudices authentically. He may not have been the biggest personality on the series, but when he was on the screen, he brought brilliant intrigue. Hawkes played the perfect narrative foil throughout the show's run.

What Deadwood did particularly well was never skimping on reality. If that meant violence, they showed it. If it meant showcasing "sinful" lives to survive, it was presented. In Deadwood at the time, women were relegated to some very unfortunate positions to survive, which included sex work. Perhaps the most prominent figure in that world was Trixie. She was a sharp-tongued prostitute at Swearengen's Gem Saloon. As his favorite girl, she shared a complex, volatile, and deeply codependent dynamic with him, marked by both abuse and unspoken affection. Over the course of the series, Trixie evolved, found her independence, and found love with Sol Star. Paula Malcomson made her a standout character with an impeccable performance. She smartly avoided the "hooker with a heart of gold" cliché by making her sharp, intelligent, and fiercely resilient. Her fight for her agency was what made her a profound character, and one to truly champion.

Growing from a traumatized young woman into a fervently protective moral compass gave her one of the most natural arcs of any character. Yet, no matter where she was in life, Trixie never lost her edge. To bring a similar ferocity and the same exact emotional depth, Dominique Fishback would be perfection. Fishback's greatest strength as an actress is mastering vulnerability wrapped in armor. Trixie's defining trait is her extreme defense mechanisms. When she launches into contextless, explosive tirades, it's meant to mask deep trauma. In The Deuce, Fishback played a part that mirrored much of this attitude. As Darlene, a 1970s sex worker, she navigated an incredibly harsh, predatory environment, yet refused to let others crush her. Able to take on darker parts, her Emmy-nominated performance in Swarm highlighted how she can lean into uncomfortable, deeply intense, psychologically rich moments. She can play tender, like in Judas and the Black Messiah, and gritty, like in Project Power, but her ability to tackle Milch's text was proven in her one-woman show Subverted. Kindhearted and empathetic, Joanie carries deep emotional trauma from a severely abusive childhood when she was sold into servitude. During her time, she established a tender romantic and emotional bond with Calamity Jane (Robin Weigert). Brought to life by Kim Dickens, she ensured the hardened woman had a soul inside. She mastered the dense text, making it feel colloquial. Dickens' greatest strength was finding subtle humor, giving Joanie a bit of dry wit amidst the weight of the world.

Whether playing the grieving Stacey Ehrmantraut in Better Call Saul or capturing the ticking-clock internal panic of Kay Summersby in Pressure, Condon excels at conveying a character's internal, unspoken pain. Joanie has a duality: an empathetic woman and a business powerhouse. That was on full display as Siobhan Suilleabhain in The Banshees of Inisherin, capturing the sharp-tongued, no-nonsense exhaustion while remaining morally centered. Having extensive experience of performing Shakespeare, she would ease into tackling the Western Shakespeare of Milch's text. Period pieces are also Condon's forte. From Octavia in the lavishly decadent historical drama Rome to the melancholic wilderness drama Train Dreams, Condon's ability to slip into Deadwood's period setting would make her an absolute MVP.

Part of the dynamics that made Deadwood a deliciously delectable story was watching the variety of individuals who called the camp home. The show began with a few new factions of characters arriving, shaking up Deadwood's natural order. One prominent trio was Charlie Utter, Calamity Jane, and the infamous Wild Bill Hickok (Keith Carradine). Honoring the history of the West, the show killed off Wild Bill quite early on, which came as a shock. In turn, it allowed Charlie to grow into a brilliant individual. He is the loyal, longtime friend and traveling companion who tries to manage his heavy drinking and gambling while staying solvent. He is deeply honest, morally uptight, and famously blunt, tough, and fearless in the face of danger. If there are scoundrels in camp, Charlie will sniff them out. Dayton Callie brought an impressive, gritty, world-weary loyalty that grounded the moral core. Callie kept Charlie as the ethical compass. Callie infused the character with a weathered physicality, bringing a slightly hunched posture, observant eyes, and the quiet dignity of a man who has survived hard miles.

Grand Theft Auto VI is finally less than 24 hours away, and it's difficult to overstate just how much impact the game is set to have on the medium as a whole. For example, a major GTA Online update last year drew hundreds of thousands of players away from other major live service titles, and the effect of the arrival of the new game could magnify that effect significantly.

However, it turns out, console players are the only ones who will be able to find out for themselves for the time being, with a PC release rumored to be coming relatively soon (in the first portion of next year). If you are getting in on the day one action as a PlayStation player, there's something that you'll absolutely need to make sure you get done to get the game working in the first place.

As first reported by Push Square, PlayStation themselves have confirmed that they are releasing an important firmware update for their flagship console, and it's a mandatory download if you want GTA VI to work at launch. They confirmed the revelation via a post on Twitter/X, which reads:

"Sony Interactive has issued a PS5 system software update that is required before playing Grand Theft Auto VI. Players should download this update prior to midnight on November 19 so that they’re ready to play on launch day, November 19."

All of this comes at a time when those working at Sony are doing everything they can to improve the security of their flagship console. Hackers are finding new and more ingenious ways to jailbreak the PlayStation 5, largely thanks to AI tools which allow them to analyse and find vulnerabilities much more quickly than normal.

But even as Huang was fighting to have the trade ban on the Nvidia H20 chip lifted, he continued praising Trump’s tariff plan, calling it “utterly visionary.” The president eventually reversed the ban three months later, and by December 2025, he had approved Nvidia H200 exports to China, but only if Nvidia pays a 25% fee. The newer Blackwell and Vera Rubin chips remain out of reach for Chinese companies, though — at least on paper.

Allegations that Chinese companies have been breaking Washington’s trade ban and smuggling Nvidia AI GPUs into China have been circulating since June 2023, after Reuters revealed that vendors in Shenzhen could secure A100 chips when asked discreetly. They reportedly bought these chips from the excess stocks sold in the open market or through intermediaries in India, Taiwan, and Singapore. Another story published in January 2024 asserted that the Chinese military and related institutions, state-run data centers, and even universities continue buying banned A100 and H100 chips. Six months after that, accounts of underground networks dedicated to smuggling AI GPUs — even entire servers — through other countries started emerging.

In 2025, an investigative report showed that at least a billion dollars’ worth of Nvidia AI chips were allegedly funneled into China. Some sellers likened the illicit AI GPU trade to a seafood market, with brokers and retailers openly advertising the most advanced Nvidia AI chips online, from RTX 5090 GPUs to complete B200 servers.

Even with all these reports, Huang continued denying that Nvidia’s chips were being smuggled into China. “There’s no evidence of any AI chip diversion,” the then-CEO told reporters at Computex 2025. He also added, “The Grace Blackwell system is nearly two tons, and so you’re not going to be shipping — you’re not going to be putting that in your pocket or your backpack anytime soon. And so, these systems are fairly easy to keep track of... but the important thing is that the countries and the companies that we sell to recognize that diversion is not allowed, and everybody would like to continue to buy Nvidia technology, and so they very well monitor themselves very carefully and they’re quite careful about that.”

In the midst of all these AI chip smuggling rumors, the Bureau of Industry and Security, the agency in charge of enforcing U.S. trade sanctions, was found to be “inadequate” and underfunded, and it has been relying on outdated manual processes to enforce restrictions on Chinese tech companies. Despite that, companies and individuals across the world are being investigated and arrested for bypassing Washington’s export controls.

Singaporean authorities broke up a smuggling ring in early 2025 that smuggled Nvidia GPUs for Chinese AI lab DeepSeek, resulting in a raid of 22 different locations and the arrest of nine people. In October of the same year, a Singapore company called Megaspeed, which was led by Alice Huang, allegedly diverted $2 billion worth of Nvidia chips. Four Americans were then arrested the following month for shipping 400 Nvidia A100s and attempting to send 50 more Nvidia H200 GPUs to China.

An even bigger smuggling scandal unfolded some four months after the arrests, after three Super Micro employees were arrested for smuggling $2.5 billion of Nvidia hardware into China. The suspects included company co-founder Yih-Shyan “Wally” Liaw, although Super Micro itself was not implicated. The Super Micro case spanned the globe, with Taiwanese authorities raiding 12 locations related to the case in May 2026, with another nine sites and six people investigated in June. Jensen Huang urged the company to fix its export control compliance after the arrests and raids, telling the media, “We insist our partners are compliant. We hope that they will enhance and improve their regulatory compliance and prevent that from happening in the future.”

But just five months after he made this statement, one stunning accusation unraveled it all.

On October 29, 2026, The New York Times published a report that Jensen Huang may have been the target of a Chinese spy plot through the executive of a firm who was “close” to him — that executive is Alice Huang. Nvidia denied the accusation. “Megaspeed has been no different than many other regional cloud customers. Jensen’s interactions with Ms. Huang are not unusual or unexpected. Meetings with customers are part of a CEO's job; Jensen talks with thousands of customer executives every year,” a company spokesperson told Tom's Hardware. “Our regional cloud customers receive ordinary business information — not sensitive NVIDIA trade secrets — and zero information relevant to national security."

But a little over a week later, U.S. intelligence sources claimed that the former Nvidia chief had an affair with the Megaspeed executive. This is the same Megaspeed that has been accused of smuggling Nvidia chips to the tune of $2 billion. By the evening of Sunday, November 8, Jensen admitted to the affair, although he did not name the third party in his statement.

Many government officials would usually turn a blind eye towards a tech executive’s personal life, but the fact that he is the leader of the U.S.’s premier AI chip maker and that he is also close to the president has caught the attention of authorities. This has led to the U.S. Congress asking him to a closed-door meeting to discuss Nvidia’s “security protocols against foreign adversary espionage” and the FBI investigating his relationship with the alleged Chinese spy.

The admission and allegations led to people digging deeper into Alice Huang. An investigation by The Information revealed that she was connected to a “labyrinth” of companies spanning Singapore, China, and the British Virgin Islands, and that she allegedly helped move around $7.5 billion worth of AI GPU imports. $2 billion was routed from Megaspeed to Speedmatrix, its Malaysia subsidiary, while another $4.6 billion moved directly from the Singaporean company into Shanghai Century Litong, a China-based cloud services company. The new Marvel Comics app offers over 35,000 comics from the company's extensive catalog of Marvel, Star Wars, and 20th Century Studios. The Base subscription costs $9.99 a month or $69.99 a year, while the Ultimate tier costs $12.49 a month or $99.99 a year.

You can follow your favorites by clicking the follow button so you don’t miss new books or revisit older titles. The Pull List is especially useful because it surfaces the latest issues from followed series, characters, or creators. The Marvel Comics app offers two subscription tiers. The Base tier costs $9.99 a month or $69.99 a year (both plus tax). This gives you access to the full Marvel Comics digital library, with new issues arriving as soon as three months after their print release. The Ultimate tier gives you access to new comics as soon as 30 days after their print release, on top of the full library. This tier costs $12.49 per month or $99.99 a year (plus tax for both).

At first glance, the new Marvel Comics app doesn’t appear all that different from the old Marvel Unlimited app. As before, the interface has colorful comic book covers on a black background. However, the differences become apparent when you take a closer look at the dedicated sections on the Home page.
