Andrew Scott on Starring in ‘Elsinore’ and Feeling ‘Very Grateful’ to Be an Out Gay Actor

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Andrew Scott remembers first learning about HIV and AIDS when he was growing up in Dublin, Ireland.

“I would have been incredibly young, but I remember Oprah Winfrey talking about it,” he tells me. “She did quite a lot. She had Magic Johnson. That was a little bit later, but I remember she had Magic Johnson on her show and she spoke about the crisis. She had people who were living with HIV. It seemed kind of far away at the time.” Related Stories ‘PAW Patrol’ Spinoff Series ‘Skye’s Pup Squad’ Set for 2027 Release

Scott is now at the center of the conversation because of his starring role in “Elsinore.” He plays Ian Charleson, a star of the 1981 Oscar-winning “Chariots of Fire,” who starred in a 1989 production of “Hamlet” at the National Theatre in London as he was dying of AIDS.

Charleson never publicly revealed his sexuality or his illness before passing away just weeks after completing his run in “Hamlet.”

Scott came out in 2013 in an interview with “The Independent.”

“I relate to it deeply, and I relate to it as a professional who wants to just do my job and as somebody who feels very grateful to get those opportunities that Ian and his counterparts didn’t,” says Scott, who is considered a front-runner in the Oscar race for best actor. “I feel celebratory that I can look around and that there are out gay actors who are growing in number and not placing us in this insidious heaviness burden about separating actors in that way. It feels really wonderful that we have a lot of out gay actors in the movie in a movie about there not being a lot of out gay actors.”

Director Simon Stone says casting an openly gay actor as Charleson was “hugely important.”

“I think what message that sends to the world is that times have changed, and that we’re not going to apologize for the story that we’re telling here,” he says. “Andrew has had the huge benefit of the pioneering work of so many actors before him, that his career was not disadvantaged at all by being openly gay. He’s probably the first generation of actors who can say that.”

“Elsinore” also stars Billie Piper as Patsy, Charleson’s best friend who took care of him through his illness. Piper remembers Stone being mindful of how heavy that subject matter was. “It was a very sensitive set. Simon was very good at setting a very peaceful environment,” Piper says. “People were in their socks, sort of padding around. It felt like a womb. It felt like a nest. People could be extremely emotional. I think a lot of the crew were very upset watching takes. I think for the actors sometimes you’re so adrenalized that you’re sort of just in it. Reflection is something that sometimes comes later.”

The “Elsinore” cast also includes Olivia Colman as Charleson’s physician, Dr. Margaret Johnson, David Dawson as filmmaker Derek Jarman and Joe Locke as a fellow AIDS patient.

Screenwriter Stephen Beresford’s research included talking to the real “fantastically generous” Patsy as well as many other people who knew Charleson. “Some of the people I spoke to are not in great health now, who are older,” Beresford says. “But they made the effort to come and talk to me because they wanted to honor Ian, and that’s very special.”

As Charleson’s health deteriorates in the film, he develops Kaposi sarcoma lesions on his skin. Scott says he tried not to pay attention to the transfer tattoos that makeup department head Nadia Stacey applied to his skin to duplicate the dark cancerous growths.

“In some ways, a little bit like Ian, I had to try and think, ‘Well, they aren’t there.’ He did what he had to do, but he was determined with getting on with the business of living,” Scott says. “He threw himself into ‘Hamlet,’ and I threw myself into ‘Elsinore.’ It was very important to me to not fetishize Ian’s death, but to really think about his living. If everybody else was playing the tragedy around him, he was certainly looking toward the light and in some ways I tried to mirror that.”

Meanwhile, you can also watch Scott talking about Charleson and the making of “Elsinore” with Oprah Winfrey on the latest episode of “The Oprah Podcast.”

Bitcoin Losses Are the Worst Since FTX. Is the Bottom Near?

Bitcoin investors are locking in their heaviest losses since the collapse of FTX in November 2022, according to a report published Tuesday by blockchain analytics firm Glassnode.

The world’s largest cryptocurrency slipped below $60,000, trading at $59,992, more than 50% from its October 2025 peak.

“Bitcoin losses are the heaviest since FTX,” Glassnode wrote on X. “Set against the money that flowed into Bitcoin over the past year, they have almost doubled in a week.”

Glassnode tracks realized losses by looking at coins that move on-chain at prices lower than when they previously moved. Measuring those losses against the capital that entered Bitcoin over the preceding year gives analysts a way to compare sell-offs across market cycles, even as the cryptocurrency grows in value.

The last time the indicator reached these levels, Sam Bankman-Fried’s crypto empire was collapsing and Bitcoin was trading below $16,000. This time, the selling pressure has coincided with a much broader retreat from risky assets, and even good news for the tech industry hasn’t been enough to keep Bitcoin afloat.

Bitcoin rallied 5% in about half an hour on Monday after Nvidia announced that Jensen Huang was returning as CEO. Less than six hours later, Bitcoin had given back the entire rally, while the Nasdaq 100 still held roughly three-quarters of its gains. In trading, this is known as a bull trap, and happens on strong bearish trends.

Bitcoin’s correlation with the Nasdaq 100 is now at its highest since September 2022, Glassnode said. Over the past 60 trading days, Bitcoin has tended to gain less when tech stocks rise and lose more when they fall.

U.S. investors have also been pulling money out of the market. Spot Bitcoin ETFs have lost $4.3 billion on net over the past 20 trading days, while Coinbase traders have been heavy net sellers over the past month. Buying and selling on Binance, meanwhile, have been roughly balanced.

“Spikes like this often came close to past lows, but some came early,” Glassnode said. The firm pointed to the sell-offs of early 2019, March 2020, and November 2022, when losses surged near market bottoms, but also to earlier episodes in 2018 and 2022 when prices had further to fall.

Long-term holders—investors who have held their coins for roughly five months or longer—accounted for most realized losses from mid-April to mid-November. Nearly 90% of the long futures positions vulnerable to liquidation between last week’s Bitcoin price and $53,200 are also gone, reducing the potential for another cascade of forced selling within that range.

Glassnode is watching Bitcoin’s realized price, the average price at which circulating coins last moved, which currently stands at $53,200. Bitcoin traded below that metric before bottoming in each of the previous three major bear markets.

Applying the discounts seen at the lows of 2022 and 2018 to today’s realized price would put Bitcoin at roughly $41,000 and $37,000, respectively.

For now, the more immediate concern is whether Bitcoin can close above its June closing low of $58,500. Glassnode said a recovery above $60,000 accompanied by renewed ETF inflows would help establish whether the summer lows are holding.

Packers activate Micah Parsons to 53-man roster

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

The Packers have made it official — Micah Parsons is back on the roster.

Green Bay announced on Tuesday that Parsons has been activated to the 53-man roster in advance of Wednesday’s game against the Rams.

Parsons has been medically cleared to play. While he’s officially listed as questionable, all signs point to the edge rusher returning from his torn ACL this week in Los Angeles.

In 14 games for the Packers last year, Parsons tallied 12.5 sacks with 12 tackles for loss and 27 quarterback hits. He also had one pass defensed and two forced fumbles.

Though he wasn’t able to finish the 2025 season, he was still an AP first-team All-Pro for the third time in his career.

Green Bay had an open spot on its 53-man roster, so the club did not need to make a corresponding move.

German Women Climb New Route on Sharphu VI

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Last month, five women with the German Alpine Club established a new route on the northwest ridge of Sharphu VI in eastern Nepal. It was only the second overall ascent of the 6,076m peak near Kangchenjunga.

In October, Steffi Feistl, Anna Gomeringer, Kristin Hinkelmann, Kathi Huber, and Fenja Kochl set up their base camp at 4,000m. A huge amount of snow had fallen earlier that season, guaranteeing arduous slogging in some areas.

Their preliminary plan had been to attempt either Sharphu II or Sharphu VI, but Sharphu II’s condition looked too dangerous due to unstable rock, threatening seracs, and hazardous snow conditions.

Sharphu II and VI. Photo: Xtreme Climbers/labels by Gunter Seyfferth

On the other hand, the northwest ridge of Sharphu VI seemed feasible. The team acclimatized over several reconnaissance trips, overnighting up to 4,800m. They then set up their advanced base camp on a moraine ridge at that altitude. Then Kochl, Hinkelmann, and Huber set off over initially easy but deep snow-covered terrain.

They eventually reached a steep section at around 5,300m where powder snow overlying bare rock made placing protection difficult. “The mixed pitches had looked easy, but were steeper than expected,” recalled Huber. “Nasty, granular snow, and no hold on the rock underneath.”

Their work complete, the trio rappelled down, and the following day, Feistl and Gomeringer took their turn. They inched up a difficult mixed pitch followed by 200 vertical meters of burrowing through waist-deep powder.

The route up the northwest ridge of Sharphu VI. Photo: German Alpine Club

After a rest day, the five women set off for the summit. They reached a glacier plateau via a 5,800m, previously unclimbed sub-peak. Here, they bivvied. On October 21, they began the final two kilometers to the main summit. The recent sun had hardened the snow, so they sank at most knee-deep. Eventually, they reached the summit ridge. They had to take turns standing on the summit, because that final ridge was so narrow.

Gomeringer and Kochl on the summit of Sharphu VI. Photo: German Alpine Club

Sharphu VI was first climbed on March 25, 2025, by a Chilean-Nepalese team who thought they’d climbed a different mountain. They went up via the east flank. Before that, Sharphu VI had been attempted only once, in 2023.

The German women’s route featured 1,300m of elevation gain, graded M5, 55°. The expedition was the culmination of the German Alpine Club’s three-year women’s training program.

‘Hexed’ Creatives on Telling a Modern-Day Witchcraft Story, Casting Hailee Steinfeld, and Their Favorite Easter Eggs

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

SPOILER ALERT: This story discusses mild plot points for “Hexed,” opening in theaters on Wednesday.

Walt Disney Animation Studios’ 65th film “Hexed” tells the story of the hidden world of witches and how, throughout history, they have been misunderstood. At its heart, however, is a story of motherhood and the powerful bond between a mother and daughter.

Disney animated films often kill off a parent to propel a story forward. But the creatives behind “Hexed” embraced that important relationship. Related Stories ‘PAW Patrol’ Spinoff Series ‘Skye’s Pup Squad’ Set for 2027 Release

Directed by Fawn Veerasunthorn and Jason Hand, and co-directed by Josie Trinidad, the film follows Billie (Hailee Steinfeld), a rebellious teen who escapes into the secret world of Hexe, a realm of witches and wizards who live in harmony with nature.

Veerasunthorn explained that the story idea was originally pitched by Trinidad. “She wanted to tell a story about the hidden world of witches. What if there’s a place where they can thrive on their own? The place built by witches for witches.” More importantly, Veerasunthorn notes, Trinidad wanted to tell a story of motherhood and the true heart of the story lies in the complicated, relatable relationship between Billie and her mother, Alice (Rashida Jones).

In developing Billie, she was portrayed as a modern teenager who wears combat boots and has an edgy style. Producer Yvett Merino explains that Billie oozes confidence: “She doesn’t quite fit in. There’s something that feels different about her.”

The key was making the film’s main protagonist relatable. “We all go through that at some point in our lives, especially in those teenage years where you’re just trying to figure out who you are,” Merino says. “She takes her mom’s car. She borrows her mom’s car to go and free the school mascot. She’s imperfect, impulsive and maybe doesn’t always make the best decisions.” Merino goes on to say, “When I was a teenager, I didn’t always make the best decisions. But you know what? I got away with it. We’ll just learn from that lesson.”

Imperfect relationships were key when it came to Alice as well. Alice is protective of Billie and wants to keep her daughter safe. Hand points out, “We’re all imperfect. We’re all trying our hardest, but we’re still figuring it out and that was really important to us.”

Visually, the world of “Hexed” is vibrant and it took over 700 artists to bring it to life, sometimes with up to 40 people working on a sequence. “The animation style pushed boundaries as far as they could by creating a world audiences had not seen before, filled with characters they’d never seen,” Veerasunthorn says.

The idea was that it would feel like a crisp autumn day after it had just rained and the air felt a bit chilly. “It’s a perfect place where magic can thrive,” she adds.

Producer Roy Conli credits the film’s production designer, Lorelay Bové, for her creativity. “When people see this film, they will see a world they’ve never seen; they’ll see characters they’ve never seen before in a Disney film.”

If the textures of Hexe look heightened, that was a deliberate choice. “We turn the dial up so it feels more like a caricature drawing could feel,” Hand explains. “We wanted this world to be so enticing to Billie. All of our decisions within the world of Hexe go back to Billie. When she goes out into that city for the first time, what’s she going to want to see? Why is this world built for her? What is it speaking to her inside?” Hand says. All those factors were important from both a production design standpoint, but also from a character standpoint. “And then for that journey to bring her mom back into it, throws her for a loop. That’s where the story starts to get its juice and interesting.”

Voice casting was important too. Hand recalls seeing Steinfeld in “True Grit” and later in “The Edge of Seventeen.” In that film, Steinfeld plays a troubled teen who had a contentious relationship with their mom. He says, “I always loved her in that role. I still loved her, even though she was doing the worst things and saying terrible stuff and that’s a really great quality.” But it was her voice quality that made her perfect to lead “Hexed.” “Our animators even picked up on particular mannerisms of the way she holds her mouth, little micro expressions that she does.”

Jones was perfect for Alice. Verasunthorn says, “Rashida brings this sense of comedy in a very uptight mom. Those two words don’t go well together, but somehow she makes it work. The depth of vulnerability that we get to see in Alice is quite beautiful.”

“Hexed” wouldn’t be a Disney movie without a villain to stir up a little trouble. Enter Jodie Foster, who voices Queen Celeste, a sinister ruler who stops at nothing to maintain her power.

Was it easy to get her to say yes? Conli says, “We asked her and she did it. She’d never played a villain before. She’s done a phenomenal job at this incredible narcissistic witch that is a little over the top.”

Joining Steinfeld, Jones and Foster are Walton Goggins as Beef Roger Krumchuk, a three-eyed cat.

“He brought so much comedy and so much personality, way beyond what we ever imagined,” Merino says.

As for the film’s Easter eggs, there are plenty to look out for. Among them are Aladdin’s magic carpet in Billie’s bedroom, a floating head fortuneteller that nods to “The Haunted Mansion.” Mother Gothel from “Tangled” appears in a blink-and-you’ll-miss-it moment.

Hand’s favorite one is Cursula, a nod to “The Little Mermaid’s” Ursula. “It’s voiced by Jodi Benson — who provided the voice of Ariel in the original animated film. I love ‘The Little Mermaid’ so much, and so to have Jodi Benson come in and do a complete twist on it and have her play Cursula, she was so amazing. She gave us such energy.”

Merino and Conli concur that the scene with dancing brooms is one that is special for both of them. “It reaches back to our history and goes back to Walt Disney’s “Fantasia,” and the Sorcerer’s Apprentice scene,” Merino says.

“The brooms, that’s 80 years in the past. That’s ‘Fantasia’; it touches on this amazing legacy that we all live with, and we all celebrate.”

But as much as I know I need to break through and see the rest of what GTA 6 has to offer, it's hard to resist the call to just live out Jason Duval's mundane daily routine for eternity.

Warning: There are some light spoilers for early parts of GTA 6's story in the following section.

For those who haven't played GTA 6 yet or are still in the early stages like me, chapter 1 follows Jason's meager existence as a sort of paid goon for Brian, a local boatyard owner who dabbles in drug trafficking on the side. The other protagonist, Lucia, gets out of prison partway through the chapter, but for most of it, she's not playable yet. Most of the story and side missions in chapter 1 focus on Jason doing little odd jobs that occasionally spiral into chaotic violence. He spends time with his girlfriend or buddies and otherwise just kind of chills when he's not doing all the crime stuff.

GTA 6 chapter 1 immediately establishes an endearingly ordinary routine for Jason, at least by action video game protagonist standards. Every in-game day I wake up, feed the pet snake, drink coffee, work out, drink a protein shake, and take a shower before heading out to do whatever it takes to get enough money to set up a nest egg for Jason and Lucia.

While there are some minor gameplay incentives to doing all of that, GTA 6 doesn't outright demand any of it from the player. I'm just doing it because it's a legitimately interesting loop, especially by the standards of big-budget mainstream games. I'm not just endlessly traversing between icons on a map and shooting some bad guys when I get to each one. I feel like an active participant in the everyday lives of two adults, which at the very least is novel for this type of game. It also helps that Brian is voiced by Stephen Root, so you spend most of chapter 1 being yelled at by Buck Strickland, and that's lovely.

I mean, most of my favorite missions so far have barely involved crime at all. The one where Jason and Lucia go to a bar after she gets out of prison is a remarkable virtual rendition of a romantic night out, while I've already lavished the Carly Rae Jepsen mission with praise on this website.

All of that is why I'm having such a hard time moving forward with the main story. I know in my heart that chapter 2 will likely introduce a new setting with new stakes, new mechanics, and an increased focus on crime. And all of that will probably be fantastic in its own right, but it won't be the same as the game I've loved up until now.

And if that's the feeling Rockstar intends to elicit in players when chapter 1 draws to a close, I would say they pulled it off. Maybe even a little too well.
