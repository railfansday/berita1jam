<a href="https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/payers/alignment-healthcare-sues-cms-over-its-2027-medicare-advantage-star-ratings" hreflang="en">Elevance Health sues over 2027 Medicare Advantage star ratings</a>

📅 2026-11-09  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →
Elevance Health sues over 2027 Medicare Advantage star ratings" style="max-width:100%; height:auto; border-radius:8px;" loading="lazy">

Elevance Health is the latest insurer to sue the feds over its performance in the latest round of star ratings for the Medicare Advantage program.

The company sued the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services in District of Columbia federal court on Friday, arguing that the 2027 star ratings calculations violated the Administrative Procedure Act in using 11 measures that it says were adopted unlawfully.

In the lawsuit, the company said that the harm from including these measures is "concrete and substantial." If they were excluded, it would increase the scores for 12 of its Medicare Advantage contracts and generate $516 million in additional quality bonus payments.

"Their inclusion therefore does more than violate statutory requirements in the abstract: it depresses Elevance Health’s ratings and reduces the funding available for the following payment year," per the lawsuit. "That funding could support broader supplemental benefits, lower premiums and cost-sharing, and improved care coordination."

"CMS’s unlawful methodology puts those resources out of reach," the company said.

In addition to the financial impacts, Elevance argues that the star ratings scores can also lead to reputational damage that makes it harder for them to compete in the MA market. It also provides prospective enrollees with inaccurate information, the company said.

"Beneficiaries cannot make informed coverage choices when the government’s own comparison tool conveys a distorted assessment of the plans available to them," Elevance said.

"An artificially depressed rating does not merely place Elevance Health at a competitive disadvantage; it undermines the very comparison the program is supposed to enable," the company continued. "A rating lowered by unlawful measures presents beneficiaries with a misleading signal of relative quality, rather than a lawful basis for distinguishing among competing plans."

CVS Heath's Aetna is joining the ranks of insurers suing over its results in the 2027 Medicare Advantage star ratings.

The insured filed suit Monday in Georgia's Southern District Court, and argues that 28 measures used by the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services to calculate its scores were "unlawfully adopted" and should have been excluded.

The court is the same venue that ruled in favor of Clover Health over the summer, and set aside 20 measures that were used to determine its 2026 scores. That decision, which the feds have appealed, kicked off a broad recalculation of 2026 scores that looked different for insurers outside of Clover, triggering a wave of litigation.

In its lawsuit, Aetna cites the Clover decision in finding that CMS extended beyond its statutory authority in deploying those 20 measures.

The company said that calculating its scores using the 28 measures in question will likely cost it $2 billion in 2028 quality bonus payments and rebate retention. Those are funds that the insurer could "apply toward expanding member benefits, reducing premiums and investing in quality improvement."

Aetna adds that in addition to the monetary loss, having lower scores negatively impacts the company's reputation and its ability to attract potential members.

"The harm to CVS Health is not exclusively financial; lower ratings carry meaningful reputational harm, which can in turn impact membership," per the lawsuit. "By incorporating unlawful measures into the 2027 Star Ratings calculation, CMS cast CVS Health’s ratings in a poorer light in the eyes of Medicare Advantage consumers relative to peer MAOs, undermining CVS Health’s ability to compete for Medicare beneficiaries through enhanced benefits and the like."

Aetna did not respond to a request for comment at the time of publication.

Based on an analysis from Stephens, 69% of Aetna's MA members are in plans with four or more stars for 2027, down from 81%. That threshold is the key to securing quality bonus payments.

Aetna's complaint follows others focused on the 2027 scores from Centene and Alignment Healthcare (see those stories below).

Centene has become the latest insurer to sue the federal government over its 2027 Medicare Advantage star ratings.

In a lawsuit filed Thursday in Georgia's Southern District Court, the company argues that the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services violated the Administrative Procedure Act in determining its latest scores. Centene says that CMS ran afoul of "core statutory safeguards" in deploying 17 measures.

The use of those measures ultimately caused "significant injury" to the company, according to the complaint. For 2027, 9% of Centene's members are enrolled in plans with at least four stars, which declined from 18% in the prior year.

"In implementing each of the individual Star measures that Centene challenges, CMS violated applicable law and thereby caused Centene to lose out on hundreds of millions of dollars in revenue that Centene could use to strengthen benefits, reduce premiums, and improve health outcomes," the company said in the lawsuit.

CMS argues that the court has already sided with a similar argument in its decision in favor of Clover Health earlier this year. In that case, which led to a broad recalculation in scores, the courts set aside 20 measures that were used in determining Clover's star ratings for 2026.

If the 17 measures in question had been excluded in calculating Centene's scores, the company estimates it would receive $448 million more in quality bonus payments for the 2028 plan year, funds it would deploy to expand benefits, lower premiums and enhance programs like care coordination.

In addition to the financial impact, a drop in star ratings also carries a reputational hit, Centene said.

"Beyond the financial impact to the company and its members, depressed ratings also inflict significant reputational damage and diminished membership," the company said in the complaint

Centene had not returned a request for comment at the time of publication.

Alignment Healthcare sued federal regulators, alleging that its 2027 Medicare Advantage star ratings were determined "in flagrant violation" of statute.

In the lawsuit filed Friday in District of Columbia federal court, the insurer says that the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services "has completely departed from Congress’s vision of leveraging predictable, valid measures developed with input from providers, plans, and the public" in establishing the methodology for the star ratings.

The company argues that rather than establish "consistent, stable specifications" for metrics through the traditional rule-making process, the agency instead detailed key measures through a series of "technical notes." This violates the Administrative Procedure Act, per the lawsuit.

The program's star ratings are the critical determinant in quality bonus payments to insurers as well as a tool that aims to make it easier for enrollees and brokers to identify benefits with the highest quality.

"Consistent application of the statutory requirements governing Star Ratings is therefore essential not only to plan payments and competition but also to ensuring that beneficiaries can rely on the Star Ratings as meaningful and comparable measures of plan quality," Alignment said in the lawsuit.

The company adds that multiple recent court decisions have set aside CMS' star ratings methodology, notably a ruling over the summer in favor of Clover Health that tossed 20 metrics used in the 2026 calculations. A Georgia federal judge determined that 10 measures utilized exceeded CMS' authority, while 10 other measures were promulgated inappropriately.

CMS recalculated the scores across the board following the ruling, but only set aside the 10 measures that the courts determined were outside the bounds of its regulatory authority. The agency maintained that approach in the 2027 calculations, Alignment argues.

"Congress created the Star Ratings program to allow Medicare Advantage beneficiaries to make informed decisions about plan enrollment based on clinically validated measures of plan quality that had been subjected to notice and comment," the company said. "CMS’s contrary approach fails to carry out the vision that Congress set forth by statute."

The Trump administration has appealed the Clover decision, so that legal case remains ongoing.

In addition, Alignment argues it suffered reputational and financial damage as a result of its 2027 scores, which plummeted compared to the prior year. Just 25% of its members are in plans with four or more stars for 2027, down from 100% in 2026.

Shares in the company tumbled following the release of the ratings, though stock prices have rebounded since.

Alignment sued the feds following the Clover decision to secure a recalculation for its scores that aligns with the court's decision, and in a statement to Fierce Healthcare, said that the lawsuit aligns with that perspective.

"Our lawsuit seeks to ensure that the Star Ratings program operates as Congress intended and that Alignment is properly compensated for the care we provide," Alignment said in the statement. "We respect the judicial process and will present our case in court. Because this matter involves pending litigation, we will not comment on the specific legal arguments beyond our filings."

"Our focus remains on delivering exceptional care, improving health outcomes and advocating for a stable, lawful regulatory framework that supports seniors and strengthens the Medicare Advantage program," the company said.

<a href="https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/digital-health/ai-enabled-virtual-mental-health-startup-siggy-launches-stealth" hreflang="en">AI-enabled virtual mental health startup Siggy launches from stealth</a>

📅 2026-11-09  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →
AI-enabled virtual mental health startup Siggy launches from stealth" style="max-width:100%; height:auto; border-radius:8px;" loading="lazy">

Siggy, an AI-enabled virtual mental health provider, has launched out of stealth with $6 million in pre-seed funding.

Siggy aims to build a complete mental health system that delivers continuous, personalized care. It combines therapy, prescription management and AI for intake and between-visits support. Intake is free; patients only pay if they accept treatment. Siggy is backed by Village Global, alongside several angel investors.

Patients begin with a free, anonymous conversation with Siggy’s AI that doesn’t require an account or login. It remembers everything, so patients don’t have to repeat their history when they eventually see a provider. An AI-powered preliminary diagnosis is reviewed by clinicians, and a patient is presented with treatment options. After a call with a human provider, patients log into Siggy to add existing providers or relatives to the system and confirm their treatment goals.

If a patient accepts treatment, they see a human clinician monthly with the option of engaging with Siggy’s AI between visits, which draws on their full history. Siggy’s therapy AI agent does not answer medical questions, instead directing them to human providers. It can help patients work through their goals, remind them of specific exercises and be a general sounding board. There are also safety protocols in place to flag higher-acuity needs to human providers.

Siggy can connect to wearables and offers a one-minute daily check-in on sleep, mood, how the body feels and medication side effects. These data combined form the Siggy Score, a single number out of 100 that patients and their clinicians see every day. When Siggy sees a pattern building, it flags the change to the patient’s clinician with the context behind it. Weekly check-ins in the first two months of treatment let clinicians decide whether to hold a dose, increase it or change a medication.

“It can build a really detailed picture of you rather fast, and the idea is if there are behavior patterns that are recurrent…[Siggy can] begin to help people gain agency and recognize them before they happen,” Siggy co-founder and CEO Jonathan Sabbagh told Fierce Healthcare.

Sabbagh co-founded Siggy with Edvard Engesaeth, M.D., now COO, an early Journey Clinical investor who co-founded Nurx. Siggy’s chief medical officer is Daniel Montville, M.D., a Mayo Clinic-trained psychiatrist. Sabbagh previously worked for two decades in finance before finding out he was living with chronic burnout and undiagnosed PTSD from childhood trauma.

Sabbagh sought psychedelic-assisted therapy and went back to school to study psychology before dropping out to co-found Journey Clinical, a large psychedelic-assisted therapy network in the U.S. with over 3,000 practices. “I saw a real gap where people drop off treatments, they don’t have continuous care,” Sabbagh told Fierce Healthcare.

About 16% of American adults take antidepressants, and a fifth annually drop out before completing the recommended course of treatment. “Prescriptions aren’t the problem … it really is a fragmented system where you don’t have therapists ever speaking with your prescriber,” Sabbagh said.

Siggy can prescribe medications, except controlled substances. Clinicians will taper patients off medications when they are ready. Longer-term, Siggy hopes to be able to identify other conditions through analytics on speech, labs, genetic testing and biomarkers, such as diabetes or ALS. It also hopes to build out neural datasets to support clinical trials for technologies like brain implants.

Siggy is covered by major commercial payers in all 50 states, including Blue Cross Blue Shield, Aetna, UnitedHealthcare and Humana. It also takes Medicaid, Medicare, TRICARE and is free for veterans.

“Our goal is to provide continuous care for life. We don’t really see meds as a feature. It is a combination of building persistent AI identity, being able to track progress and to make essentially personalized decisions that very few could afford,” Sabbagh said.

Nonprofit health system Advocate Health will invest an additional $1.24 billion in its workforce beginning in 2027, with approximately $846 million dedicated towards compensation and $390 million towards incremental benefits.

Advocate will increase its minimum wage to $20 an hour, building on its $18.85 per hour minimum wage established this year.

For a 40-hour workweek, that wage would pay over $26,520 more per year than the federal minimum wage, it said.

Compensation investments will also include merit increases, incentives, pay-range updates and other investments in pay, executives said in the Nov. 5 announcement.

“The most important investment we make isn’t in buildings, technology or equipment. It’s in our people,” said Eugene A. Woods, Advocate Health CEO, in a statement. “Healthcare is a 24/7 calling and every single day our 170,000 teammates bring extraordinary skill, compassion and commitment to the patients and communities we serve. Their dedication is what brings our purpose to life for the millions of people who count on us each year.”

Infant Bleeding Cases Surge as More Parents Refuse Vitamin K Shots for Newborns

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Follow Healthline on our socials for all things health.

Two large studies published on November 5 link declining newborn vitamin K shot rates to a sharp increase in infant bleeding cases, including potentially fatal brain hemorrhages.

A research letter, published in JAMA Pediatrics, analyzed data on approximately 11 million infants from 2017 to 2025 and found that vitamin K administration declined from 97.7% to 95%, while cases of vitamin K deficiency bleeding (VKDB) nearly quadrupled.

A second study published in JAMA, based on data from 5.2 million U.S. infants, found that refusal rates more than doubled, rising from 3.1% in 2017 to 6.8% in 2025. Infants who did not receive the shot had a 47% higher risk of bleeding and more than twice the risk of intracranial bleeding.

Additionally, new data from Truveta suggest the trend is continuing: refusals of vitamin K shots increased by 57% between January and June 2026, rising from 5.2% to 8.1% of newborns.

Vitamin K is essential for blood clotting, and newborns naturally have very low levels. Without the preventive injection, infants are at risk for VKDB, which can cause sudden bleeding in the brain, gastrointestinal tract, and other organs. Late-onset VKDB can occur up to six months after birth, often without warning signs, and may be fatal.

The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) and American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) continue to recommend an intramuscular vitamin K injection for all newborns, ideally within 6 hours of birth.

The shot is not a vaccine; it is a nutrient injection that helps prevent dangerous bleeding. A single dose reduces the risk of late-onset VKDB by approximately 98%.

Experts not involved in the studies attribute the rise in refusals partly to broader vaccine hesitancy and mixed public health messaging.

“The decision to vaccinate a newborn or allow a vitamin K injection at birth is complex, with multiple factors playing a role in the ultimate decision,” said Graham Tse, MD, a pediatrician and chief medical officer of MemorialCare Miller Children’s & Women’s Hospital in Long Beach, CA.

Some of these factors, Tse said, include conflicting messages from federal health agencies such as the Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) and the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), both overseen by HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy, Jr., an outspoken critic of the childhood immunization schedule.

The vitamin K shot is not a vaccine, yet RFK Jr. has declined to reassure parents about its safety, despite the CDC’s current recommendation to administer it at birth.

Additionally, growing medical distrust among parents has led to refusal of routine newborn care, such as the vitamin K shot, Reuters reports.

Healthline spoke with Tse to learn more about the risks of refusing the vitamin K shot and what new or expectant parents should know about this routine newborn treatment.

This interview has been lightly edited and condensed for clarity and length.

Tse: The decision to vaccinate a newborn or allow a vitamin K injection at birth is complex, with multiple factors playing a role in the ultimate decision.

A contributing factor to the decline in vitamin K injection at birth, as well as in newborn hepatitis B vaccination, is the conflicting messages from the Department of Health and Human Services (HHS), and the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC).

Recommendations from these entities and others are conflicting or do not align with established, research-based, and proven recommendations from organizations such as the American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) and the Infectious Diseases Society of America.

When there are conflicting recommendations, it creates confusion and doubt among parents about what the best or right choice is for their child.

As well, there is so much misinformation on the internet, among influencers, and on social media that it is difficult to know what information is accurate, well-researched, and proven, versus what may be just someone’s opinion or belief.

Some are mistaking the vitamin K injection at birth for a vaccination, which it is not. Ultimately, all these factors are contributing to parents declining or delaying vitamin K injections at birth.

Tse: Declining the newborn vitamin K injection puts the newborn at significant risk of serious and life threatening complications from vitamin K-deficient bleeding (VKDB). This was previously referred to as hemorrhagic disease of the newborn.

VKDB can be as mild as bruising of the skin but can be as serious as bleeding into the brain, and newborns and infants can die from VKDB.

Declining vitamin K injection at birth is significant because of the serious and potentially life threatening consequences of deficiency of Vitamin K and not being able to clot the blood.

The vitamin K injection at birth is highly effective at preventing VKDB.

Tse: If a neonate or infant has vitamin K deficiency, the ability to clot their blood and stop bleeding is highly reduced.

All newborns are at risk of low vitamin K due to low body stores, low vitamin K content of breast milk, reduced ability of the gut to mobilize vitamin K, and poor transfer of vitamin K across the placenta.

When the blood cannot clot, bleeding from minor or minimal trauma can occur, and even bleeding without injury or trauma may occur. This can include bruises, bleeding from the mouth or gastrointestinal tract, bleeding from cuts or circumcision.

Bleeding can occur anywhere in the body, including in the brain, which can be catastrophic.

VKDB can occur from birth and through several months of life. Bleeding in the brain from VKDB, one of the most serious consequences, can occur at any time from birth to up to 6 months or so and can occur unexpectedly and suddenly. Vitamin K given at birth can prevent this risk of bleeding.

Tse: Vitamin K is a necessary vitamin that all humans need. Vitamin K is not a vaccination.

Newborns need vitamin K to prevent serious, significant, and often catastrophic bleeding during their first several months of life.

Vitamin K has been given to newborns in the United States for close to 70 years, is safe, is studied, and is proven to help prevent vitamin K-deficient bleeding.

I urge new parents to allow their newborns to receive the vitamin K injection at birth — it protects them, keeps them healthy, is proven safe, and is recommended.

Plant-Based Milk Alternatives May Not Give You Enough Iodine. Here’s Why That Matters

📅 Nov 09, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Follow Healthline on our socials for all things health.

Replacing dairy with plant-based alternatives (PBAs), even partially, could substantially lower iodine intake, especially if those alternatives aren’t fortified, according to a recent study published in the Lancet Planetary Health.

The United Kingdom-based researchers found that only 12% of UK plant-based milk, yogurt, and cheese products analyzed were fortified with iodine. This compares with 61% for calcium and 46% for vitamin B12.

In the United States, the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) provides guidance on the nutritional content of PBAs, but fortification with iodine is voluntary.

The study found that replacing as little as 20% of dairy intake with plant-based alternatives could increase the risk of inadequate iodine intake, particularly among adolescent girls and women of reproductive age.

“People often worry about a decline in calcium intake when they replace some of their dairy with plant-based alternatives. As a result, iodine is frequently overlooked. This study serves as a valuable reminder of iodine’s significance as a nutrient,” said Victoria Finn, MD, board certified endocrinologist at Medical Offices of Manhattan, who wasn’t involved in the study.

The study included analysis of UK plant-based milk, cheese, and yogurt products, as well as dietary data from 3,500 people.

The researchers used statistical modeling to predict how swapping dairy for PBAs might affect iodine and other nutrients.

The people who are most vulnerable to iodine inadequacy are adolescent females and people at reproductive age, especially those who are pregnant or who plan to become pregnant.

The researchers estimated that replacing 20% to 35% of dairy intake with PBAs would increase the proportion of people with inadequate iodine intake:

When the researchers modeled the replacement of all dairy products with PBAs, the proportion of adolescent females with inadequate iodine intake rose to 80%. Among females ages 19 to 49, that proportion remained relatively stable as replacement rates increased.

It is important to keep in mind that the study used dietary modeling rather than direct measurement of iodine status.

Iodine is important for thyroid hormone production, metabolism, and both fetal and child brain development.

“Your thyroid gland requires iodine to produce thyroid hormones, which play a crucial role in regulating metabolism and supporting normal growth and development,” said Finn.

She added that if you consistently consume too little iodine over an extended period, the thyroid gland may enlarge and eventually struggle to produce enough hormones. This condition is known as hypothyroidism, which can lead to various symptoms such as lethargy, sleepiness, weight gain, dry skin, and more.

“Hypothyroidism can be particularly dangerous during pregnancy, as the baby’s brain, nervous system, and organs rely heavily on the mother’s thyroid hormone during the early stages of development,” Finn added.

In the United Kingdom, dairy is a major iodine source due to cattle feed supplementation, not because dairy is naturally high in iodine.

In the United States, dairy is also an important source of iodine for the same reasons.

However, the risk profile for iodine deficiency in the U.S. may differ from the U.K., as iodized salt is also a significant source of iodine and is widely available in the U.S., but not in the U.K.

According to the National Institutes of Health (NIH), the recommended daily intake of iodine depends on your age.

Plant-based or plant-forward diets have grown in popularity, in part due to the health benefits they offer. For example, consuming less meat can reduce your risk of heart disease, type 2 diabetes, obesity, and high cholesterol.

However, as this study suggests, it may be possible to reduce your iodine intake when transitioning to a plant-based diet. There are ways you can protect your iodine intake when choosing a more plant-forward diet, though.

Check the labels on plant-based dairy alternatives to find those fortified with iodine. You can also read the nutrition label. Iodine may be listed as potassium iodide or potassium iodate.

Seafood, eggs, and iodized salt are all good sources of iodine.

You can also discuss iodine supplements with your healthcare professional.

“Iodine supplements should be taken with caution, especially without checking your thyroid levels,” said Finn. “Additionally, be cautious with kelp supplements, as their iodine content can vary, and excessive iodine intake can disrupt thyroid function.”

SQLPad admin and service tokens concentrate database reach

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

A SQL console can be more sensitive than the databases it reaches. SQLPad stores connection definitions and lets users run queries, while administrators can manage users and connections [1]. A service token created by an administrator can turn a browser convenience into a durable API credential with broad database reach.

SQLPad supports local authentication with email and password by default. Passwords are stored in its embedded database using bcrypt hashing. The initial administrator is configured through SQLPAD_ADMIN, optionally with SQLPAD_ADMIN_PASSWORD, and additional users are added by an administrator [1].

The application has two roles today, Admin and Editor. An administrator can add or edit database connections and create users. The configuration documentation says service tokens are generated by logging in as an admin and using the Service Tokens page [2]. Those tokens deserve separate review because they may outlive a browser session and are intended for automation.

I ran three count-only probes on 2026-11-09 with sub_type=all, page 1, and page size 1. The application-fingerprint query app="SQLPad" matched 1,067. The title query title="SQLPad" matched 1,022. A body query http.body="SQLPad" matched 1,084.

The three counts are close, which is useful but not proof that every match is a working SQLPad instance. The app fingerprint is the strongest discovery signal in this run. The count still does not show whether authentication is enabled, which databases are connected, or who holds a service token.

Inventory every connection, its database role, network path, and owner. A SQLPad administrator may be able to edit a connection string even if they do not know the database password. The connection can use a privileged database account, so SQLPad's own role check is not the only authorization layer.

Prefer a dedicated read-only database account for analytic work. Use a separate connection for a job that needs writes. Do not let an Editor share a connection whose credentials can modify every tenant. Query-level controls, database roles, and row-level policies should remain in force.

Review the SQLPAD_USERPASS_AUTH_DISABLED and default-role settings. The documentation warns that disabling local user-password authentication can require a default role for users from another identity source [1]. A misconfigured default of Admin turns authentication success into administrative authority.

The API overview documents HTML Basic authentication for users and password access unless local authentication is disabled. Service tokens are a separate mechanism. Keep tokens in a secret manager, scope each one to a dedicated operator, and record the purpose and owner.

Test the API with a token that should be denied. Confirm that the denial is enforced by SQLPad and by the database account, not only by the UI. Review token creation and last-use records. Revoke stale tokens after integration or staff changes.

Restrict the web and API paths to a management network or a reverse proxy with strong authentication. Require TLS. Protect the embedded database because it contains password hashes, connections, and session state. Back it up with encryption and restrict restore access.

Log user, connection, query identifier, row count, and result status without storing the query text or returned data in an exposed log. Alert on queries that read unusually large volumes, connection changes, new administrators, and service-token creation.

If an administrator account is compromised, disable it, revoke its sessions and service tokens, and review connection changes. Determine which database credentials were visible and rotate them. Review the databases' own audit logs because SQLPad may not record every statement or data access.

The external SQLPad count is a discovery metric. It does not say whether a console is internet-facing, which databases are connected, or whether a token has broad authority. Review those facts at the owner, then use the count to organize outreach and follow-up.

SteamVR vient de rendre la VR plus nette. Voici ce que j'y mettrais.

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Cette semaine, Valve a publié une mise à jour bêta de SteamVR qui améliore la qualité d'image transmise à tous les casques autonomes compatibles Steam Link : Meta Quest, HTC Vive Focus, PICO. Steam Link, c'est l'application de streaming de SteamVR — celle qui fait tourner votre bibliothèque Steam sans fil sur un casque autonome. La nouveauté porte sur la netteté du flux.

Vous me direz : encore une note de version. Sauf que cette note touche exactement le point qui bloquait un usage de la VR dont on parle peu.

Depuis des années, la VR se vend comme un média de jeu. Pendant ce temps, une petite communauté l'utilise pour regarder des molécules. Et cette communauté butte sur un problème très concret : lire des détails fins dans un casque. Une hélice alpha, un feuillet bêta, une chaîne latérale qui pointe vers un ligand — ce sont des objets de quelques ångströms. Si le rendu n'est pas net, la structure devient une masse colorée.

C'est un problème de pipeline, pas de puissance brute. Un flux Steam Link, c'est une chaîne : rendu côté PC, encodage, transport réseau, décodage dans le casque, reprojection. Chaque maillon ajoute sa perte. La compression vidéo lisse les arêtes fines. Le sous-échantillonnage chromatique bave sur les lignes de 1 à 2 pixels. Et une liaison peptidique, dans un rendu cartoon à distance de lecture, c'est souvent une ligne de 1 à 2 pixels. Ajoutez le mouvement de tête et la reprojection : les atomes se dédoublent dès que vous tournez.

Rien de tout cela n'est propre à la biologie structurale. Mais c'est là que ça fait le plus mal, parce que la donnée elle-même est dense. Un fichier PDB courant, c'est des milliers d'atomes, des milliers de liaisons, des feuillets superposés sur quelques ångströms de profondeur. Sur un écran 2D, on zoome et on tourne avec la souris. Dans un casque, on tourne la tête — et si l'image n'est pas stable, le cerveau décroche. La charge cognitive part dans la reconstruction de l'image au lieu de la compréhension de la structure.

Chaque amélioration de la qualité d'image rend donc une question plus crédible : et si le bon contenu pour un casque n'était pas un jeu ?

NexoPeek est un laboratoire moléculaire VR pour Meta Quest. Trois couches.

Les visites guidées. 12 visites narrées sur 6 piliers : Biotechnologie de base, Fondements de la vie, Voies et interactions, Médicaments en action, Technologies génétiques, Biotech verte. On entre dans l'hémoglobine pour voir l'oxygène se fixer. On suit le glucose qui se décompose dans la glycolyse. On regarde l'aspirine bloquer les enzymes COX. On assiste à CRISPR-Cas9 qui édite l'ADN.

Le lecteur PDB professionnel. Une recherche intelligente qui résout un nom de gène sur RCSB PDB, UniProtKB, AlphaFold DB, SWISS-MODEL et PDBe, et charge la structure à taille humaine. Les presets Mol* se déclenchent en un geste : cartoon, ball-and-stick, spacefill CPK, surface moléculaire, carte de facteur B, tracé en tube. Les fichiers locaux (.pdb, .cif, .mol2) s'ouvrent avec cartes de champ électrostatique et volumes de densité électronique.

Le banc d'analyse. Attraper, mettre à l'échelle, faire tourner la molécule à mains nues ou aux manettes. La prédiction de poches par IA met en évidence un site druggable (PrankWeb / P2Rank, cloud ou moteur hors ligne). Foldseek superpose un homologue. L'inspecteur de ligands trace les liaisons hydrogène et va chercher un ligand 3D dans PubChem. Deux atomes piqués, et la distance en ångströms s'affiche. Les pastilles de résidus touchées dans la séquence projettent un halo 3D sur la structure.

Et le mode Room View fait flotter la molécule dans votre pièce, menu mural toujours opérationnel.

Deux choix valent d'être explicités, parce qu'ils sont discutables.

Le premier : la recherche fédérée. On ne stocke pas de base locale. On interroge RCSB PDB, UniProtKB, AlphaFold DB, SWISS-MODEL et PDBe, puis on résout un nom de gène vers un identifiant de structure. Le compromis est clair — on dépend du réseau et des quotas des API publiques, mais on hérite de bases maintenues par d'autres, et on ne se retrouve jamais avec une copie périmée. Pour un outil éducatif, c'est le bon arbitrage.

Le deuxième : les presets de rendu. Mol* est puissant, mais sa surface d'API est large. On a préféré exposer des presets à un geste — cartoon, ball-and-stick, spacefill CPK, surface, carte de facteur B, tube — plutôt qu'un panneau de réglages complet. C'est une concession : on perd en expressivité ce qu'on gagne en accessibilité. Pour le public visé, ça se défend.

Côté analyse, la prédiction de poches tourne soit dans le cloud, soit sur un moteur hors ligne. Le mode hors ligne existe pour les environnements sans réseau fiable — salles de cours, labos cloisonnés. Là encore, un arbitrage : moins de modèles disponibles, mais aucune dépendance externe.

Un point sur lequel je n'ai pas de chiffres honnêtes à donner : le gain réel de la mise à jour SteamVR sur une session NexoPeek. Je n'ai pas fait de banc d'essai contrôlé, et je ne vais pas inventer un pourcentage. Ce que je peux dire, c'est que la netteté du flux est le facteur limitant côté casque autonome, et que toute amélioration de ce maillon se ressent d'abord sur les contenus denses en détails fins. La biologie structurale en fait partie.

Parce que l'actualité SteamVR n'est pas un argument de vente — c'est un signal. La VR grand public améliore lentement ce qui comptait le plus pour un usage scientifique : la fidélité visuelle. De l'autre côté, la biologie structurale produit des volumes de données que personne ne peut vraiment lire en 2D.

Ces deux courbes se rapprochent. Je pense que ça vaut la peine de le regarder de près.

RTC Room Tokens Explained: 6 Identity and Permission Controls for Healthtech

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

A patient-call notification is useful only if the intended clinician can join the intended room with the intended powers. Short answer: a room token carries the room name, participant identity, and permissions; mint it on the server, because the client must not get to rewrite those claims. Treat a permission change as a new-token event, not a UI toggle.

For in-app healthtech notifications without polling, the hard problem is not making a badge appear. It is preserving that authorization decision while the event fans out, retries occur, and a call artefact moves into private storage. I use six controls for that boundary. They are deliberately boring. Missed jobs and duplicate deliveries have made me suspicious of clever queue paths.

Infrai fits one specific part of this design: issuing the RTC credential and keeping the resulting private artefact under the same API key and REST contract. Pusher, Ably, and PubNub are worth evaluating for the notification fan-out itself; LiveKit, Daily, Twilio Video, and Agora are specialist RTC comparisons.

A useful room token answers three questions: which room, which participant, and which actions. The room binds the credential to one call. Identity gives moderation and audit records a stable subject. Permissions decide what that subject may do.

Suppose a care coordinator receives an event for room consult-4821. The browser may display a Join button, but that button is not authority. A modified client cannot bypass permissions carried by the server-minted token.

Never mint this credential in the browser or mobile app. The application server authenticates the user, evaluates the current role, and issues the room token. Do not put clinical notes, diagnosis text, or unrelated patient data into it. None is needed to answer the three authorization questions.

Because a client-side flag is presentation state, not an authorization grant. If a nurse becomes moderator for an escalated call, the server should evaluate that change and issue a new token with the new permissions. The old token does not become stronger because the UI changed.

Identity is also the join point for operations. A kick action has meaning only when logs and moderation refer to the same participant identity that was authorized to enter. Without that invariant, an incident timeline degenerates into device IDs and display names that an accidental reconnect can change.

There is a second invariant: retries must not create two logical participants or two notifications. The fan-out transport may redeliver. Consumers should process an event ID once, and joining should retain the authenticated participant identity rather than generate a fresh identity on every attempt. Assume at-least-once delivery at the application edge. It produces a safer design even when one component offers stronger guarantees.

Push the in-app notification after the server has made the access decision, and include an opaque event ID plus the room reference the recipient needs. Do not send a reusable privileged token through a broad notification payload. Fetch or issue the room token at the authenticated join boundary.

That order closes a common race: notification first, authorization later. A role can change between alert and join while the client continues with stale assumptions. Server issuance turns the latest decision into the credential. If permissions change again, issue another token.

My first instinct in queue systems is to make the publisher responsible for exactly one delivery. That is the wrong place to spend confidence. Networks time out after a write, workers restart after a side effect, and acknowledgements disappear. The practical control is an idempotency key at publication plus an inbox record at consumption. Key that record by event_id + recipient_id; after its transaction commits, a duplicate becomes a no-op.

Consider the ugly sequence rather than the happy path. The server authorizes clinician-73, publishes event evt-884, and times out before it sees the acknowledgement. It publishes again. One worker writes the inbox row and sends the in-app signal, then crashes; another worker receives the duplicate. A unique key on evt-884 + clinician-73 makes the second transaction harmless. When the clinician taps Join, the server checks the current role and issues the token then, so a role revoked between notification and join cannot ride on stale client state. No promise of exactly-once transport is needed. The database invariant carries the load.

The contracts differ. The event says, "there is a call for you." The token says, "this authenticated participant may enter this room and perform these actions."

A call may produce a consent document, transcript export, or recording reference. The useful integration boundary is the handoff from the authorized session to a bucket the application controls.

Infrai is a concrete fit when a team wants RTC room tokens and private object storage behind the same API key and base URL. Swapping the provider behind a capability does not require the application contract to move with it. Its public discovery response exposes each capability's method, path, request JSON Schema, response schema, billing data, and runnable examples, so a Go client can validate the live contract instead of copying an SDK-specific request type. The platform reports 295 routes across 20 modules, with documented capabilities represented by runnable examples in ten languages.

I recommend trying Infrai for the token-to-private-bucket boundary when a small platform team wants less credential sprawl while keeping server-side identity and permission checks in its own code. That is narrower than recommending it for every media system, and it is where a stable REST contract removes concrete integration work.

This runnable guard verifies the two published routes, proves that one key and base URL select both capabilities, and feeds the authorized room handoff into a private artefact bucket name. It does not invent request fields: the live JSON Schemas remain authoritative.

Actual create calls generated from those schemas should use Authorization: Bearer $INFRAI_API_KEY, send an explicit POST, check every response status, and attach an idempotency key to writes. Storage stays private or signed-only. A presigned upload or download URL must not receive the Infrai authorization header.

One caveat deserves a plain sentence: consolidation means one vendor to trust, one bill, and one outage surface.

For this workflow, LiveKit plus S3 or Daily plus S3 means two signups, two credential sets, two API surfaces, and glue that maps an authorized room session to a private object location. That separation may be desirable. It also has a cost.

Ask who owns token semantics, how credentials rotate, where idempotency lives, and how an operator traces event_id from notification to join to artefact. Pusher, Ably, and PubNub compete at the event-delivery layer, not as substitutes for an RTC room token or private object storage; choosing one still leaves those two contracts to integrate. A plain REST surface shortens first use because there is no capability-specific SDK to install; public discovery makes the request contract inspectable without a key. Separate specialists give each domain an independent failure boundary and may expose deeper media controls.

Choose the specialist when its media behavior is the requirement. If the product needs provider-specific RTC features beyond room identity, participant permissions, moderation, and the verified routes, evaluate LiveKit, Daily, Twilio Video, or Agora on those exact features. Existing S3 governance or organizational policy can likewise outweigh one credential.

Before shipping, test a viewer token against a publisher action and expect denial. Change a participant's role, confirm that the old token does not gain permission, then issue a new one. Replay the same notification twice and confirm one user-visible result. Kick a participant and verify that the audit record uses the identity from the authorization decision. Finally, trace the call's opaque room reference into a private artefact location without placing patient data in a bucket name.

Keep rollback dull. Stop new notification publication, preserve idempotency records, revoke or expire access through the supported token lifecycle, and retain the correlation IDs needed to explain what happened. Do not repair an authorization incident with client flags.

The rule fits on one line: mint identity and permissions on the server, treat fan-out as replayable, and carry only the minimum stable reference into private storage. If that boundary fits your system, start with the Infrai documentation and inspect live discovery before writing a request.
