Weather Guard’s Pack Rat Turned My Nephew's Pickup Into a Rolling Workshop

Instead of digging through tool bags and loose hardware at every stop, the Pack Rat Drawer Unit kept everything organized, secure, and within easy reach—making every job site a little more efficient.

Let’s start with a bit of transparency here: When Weather Guard sent me its Pack Rat truck toolbox, I thought it was destined for my new Honda Ridgeline. Then reality set in. While the Ridgeline is a fantastic daily driver and weekend project truck, its composite bed and in-bed trunk make it a poor match for a permanently mounted contractor toolbox. That trunk alone is a vehicle highlight, so burying it beneath a steel monolith seemed almost criminal. So instead of writing a review based on specs alone, I handed the Pack Rat over to someone who actually needed it—my nephew, a newly minted electrician who spends his days hauling electrical tools, fittings, wire, and hardware from one job site to the next.

A few hours after we installed it into his pickup and began organizing his gear, it became obvious this wasn’t just another truck accessory. It fundamentally changed how he uses his truck.

Picture a broad, low steel box bolted to the pickup bed’s floor, with a single wide drawer that pulls out over the lowered tailgate. The model we tested measures 60 inches long by 49½ inches wide, and stands 9½ inches tall. At that expanse, the top of the box system essentially becomes the new carry bed of the truck, sitting just about level with the wheel wells.

That measurement is something to be mindful of if you carry a lot of taller cargo. Trucks vary in cargo bed depth, so you’ll have to subtract 9½ inches from the overall depth of your bed to know the remaining height to the top of your bed rails. The top of the bed rails on my nephew’s Chevy Silverado 1500 sits at around 21 inches, so he still has a substantial 11½ inches to carry cargo.

To that end, for the model we tested—the 369-3-04—Weather Guard rates the top for up to 2,000 pounds evenly distributed. The drawer itself has a separate 425-pound capacity. That’s a lot of weight. It should be noted that your truck’s payload limit still applies to the combined weight of the box—about 300 pounds—its contents, and any cargo on top.

The Pack Rat is less a toolbox and more like a mobile organization system. Beneath the steel top, a massive full-width drawer rides on a set of internal rollers that allow it to glide smoothly in spite of the load within. Fully loaded with cordless tools and hardware, the drawer never sagged or bounced once. To that end, we thought pushing it shut would be a chore, but the drawer rolled back in with little effort, closing with an authoritative thunk that spoke to the solid overall build quality. It’s the kind of rolling momentum that reminds you to keep your fingers clear–not because it’s flawed in any way, but because it’s built with serious heft.

Organization is where the Pack Rat really makes its mark. Four walls fixed across the drawer space are separated by 24 adjustable divider panels. This allows you to tailor the drawer layout to fit your needs instead of forcing your tools into compartments of predetermined size. My nephew dedicated one side almost entirely to his Milwaukee cordless tool collection–drills, impact drivers, oscillating tool, batteries, and chargers–while configuring the remaining space to hold electrical supplies that change from job to job. Connectors, wire nuts, fittings, fasteners, electrical tape, and specialty tools all have designated homes, making it immediately obvious when he is in low supply on any item.

Weather Guard also paid attention to the details outside the drawer. The top surface is lined with aggressive grip tape, providing secure footing when climbing into the truck bed on damp mornings or during inclement weather. It seems like a small touch until you realize you’ll be stepping on this box as a platform throughout the day.

Before installing the Pack Rat, my nephew would often toss fittings, connectors, and hand tools into half a dozen different bags and bins. Now everything lives in one place, where it stays dry as a bone. He spends less time digging through piles of tools, knows exactly where everything belongs, and can get back to work faster. For professionals whose trucks serve as rolling workshops, those small gains in efficiency translate into real money saved over the course of months and years.

The Pack Rat is unapologetically designed for tradespeople–or at least serious enthusiasts whose vehicles are dedicated to their everyday work life. It’s a substantial piece of equipment, and that comes with trade-offs. The heavy-gauge steel construction adds noticeable weight to your vehicle, which will inevitably have some impact on your vehicle’s fuel economy. Installation is more involved than dropping, say, a Tmat organizing system or cross-over box into the bed since it’s meant to stay put. Furthermore, once installed the box consumes roughly 7 to 8 inches of vertical bed space. The tradeoff, of course, is that you retain the entire footprint of the bed above it. But if you frequently haul taller gear or equipment, that’s something to consider.

For the right buyer, though, those compromises will likely fade into the background. For years, my own truck was my office, my warehouse, my livelihood—even my diner most days. So it would have been a joy—and a value add—to have the Pack Rat at that stage of my work life. At the end of the day, having every tool, fastener, and accessory protected, organized, and within arm’s reach isn’t just convenient—it’s a real productivity upgrade.

Does the Oleshky ceasefire signal progress to come in Ukraine?

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Food  |  Read Source →

Hundreds of civilians were evacuated from the city of Oleshky after Russia and Ukraine agreed to a US-brokered ceasefire. Some observers have expressed optimism that more such humanitarian missions might follow.

Nearly 800 residents have been evacuated from the Russia-occupied city of Oleshky in southern Ukraine. This was announced by US first lady Melania Trump, who was credited with helping to mediate the ceasefire. The mission was coordinated by Ukrainian Human Rights Commissioner Dmytro Lubinets and his Russian counterpart, Yana Lantratova.

Most evacuees chose to be sent to Ukrainian-controlled territory, although some opted for transfer to Russia. Currently, more than 1,000 people remain in Oleshky, according to Kherson's regional administration. They were supplied with food, water and medicine.

Melania Trump hailed the mission as a success, taking to social media to thank Kyiv and Moscow for their "leadership and cooperation." She said the Oleshky ceasefire could go on to inspire a "lasting peace between the two peoples."

Dmytro Lubinez similarly wrote on the Telegram messaging platform that it is "important to maintain this cooperation mechanism," adding that "we are currently exploring options with Yana Lantratova to continue similar humanitarian efforts."

The Kremlin, however, was quick to dampen any such expectations, making clear that no further plans for humanitarian missions exist at this stage.

One day after the ceasefire ended, Ukraine's general staff stated that Russia had "used the humanitarian pause to fortify its positions." It also said Russia had carried out troop rotations, expanded its anti-drone defenses, and brought in ammunition.

British broadcaster Sky News aired drone footage, produced with the support of the Ukrainian army, showing the Russian army transporting heavy equipment, weapons, personnel and food to Oleshky and neighboring villages.

"It was clear to the Ukrainian government and international mediators that Russia could exploit the pause in this way. But it was the right thing to do to allow people to leave the country," Volodymyr Vyatrovych of the Ukrainian European Solidarity opposition party wrote on Facebook.

He believes Russia would do the exact same thing if Kyiv were to withdraw its troops from the Donetsk region, as Russian President Vladimir Putin is demanding.

"Russia would use this to prepare for its next offensive, to further destroy Ukraine," Vyatrovych wrote. That said, humanitarian missions have their place, says Ihor Chalenko of the Ukrainian Center for Analysis and Strategies. "We shouldn't speak ill of the Oleshky mission, even though it's clear that the Russians have used the ceasefire in the occupied territories to their advantage," Chalenko told DW. That's why he says Kyiv should insist on banning such behavior in future ceasefires.

The Oleshky mission took place just shortly before US voters headed to the polls in the midterm elections. Dmytro Levus of the United Ukraine research center believes the timing was no coincidence. "The Russian side played right into the hands of US President Donald Trump, whose Republican Party was expected to face a crushing electoral defeat," Levus said.

Involving Melania in the mediation was a "subtle public relations move" for Trump, through which he hoped to score points ahead of the election, Levus explained. First Lady Melania Trump has long been an advocate for humanitarian causes.

Chalenko says the Oleshky ceasefire was in Washington's interest because it allowed Donald Trump to cast himself as a "peacemaker."

Moscow also hoped it could improve its image through the humanitarian mission, especially as it caused Oleshky's humanitarian catastrophe in the first place, Chalenko said. Rescuing civilians is intended to serve as proof that "not everything is bad in Russia."

Cooperation between the two human rights ombudspersons had reached "a new level," Chalenko said, adding that the Oleshky case was just one of several noteworthy examples that also included prisoner swaps and foreign representatives gaining access to Ukrainian political prisoners held in Russia.

He does not, however, believe that Russian Human Rights Commissioner Yana Lantratova makes her own decisions. "We are seeing a certain top-down realignment of Russian policy," Chalenko told DW. He also said it was important to further develop this communication channel. The lessons learned from the evacuation of Oleshky could be applied to other towns along the front, Chalenko believes.

Dmytro Levus says the Oleshky mission has proven that, if Moscow were so inclined, a ceasefire could be established along the entire front line. Yet Russia has so far shown no willingness in doing so, partly because Ukraine has not agreed to Putin's terms, Levus explains.

Preparations are underway for a new round of negotiations between Ukraine, the US and Russia on November 10 and 11 in Abu Dhabi. Dmytro Levus believes the Oleshky ceasefire could provide substance to talks on humanitarian issues. He does not, however, think there will be much progress on a political level, even though he does see scope for a ceasefire at sea, for example.

Levus says that the US will be particularly keen to present further humanitarian successes. Ukraine may use the talks to negotiate a new prisoner exchange, though Levus says Russia will likely only budge under pressure.

Ihor Chalenko says one should not expect too much of the upcoming talks. He believes that Russia is looking for a pretext to withdraw from the negotiations, preferring instead to expand its cooperation with the US, without wanting to make any concessions to end the Ukraine war.

Samuel Greene of King's College London is similarly skeptical. "I assume that at some point, the Kremlin will come to realize that there is nothing left to gain on the battlefield and that they will have to reach an agreement."

"That moment hasn't yet come though," Greene told DW, adding that Russia was still trying to buy time by feigning a willingness to engage in dialogue with the US.

Te Araroa: the boogeyman got me

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Food  |  Read Source →

Every trail has its boogeymen and its icons. On Te Araroa you run into your first of each essentially at the same time. Kilometre 135.5 marks the base of the Raetea Forest. Notorious for its mud and for being one of the first places on the trail where people end up getting heli-lifted out. At the base of the hill sits the Krishna Sanctuary, where for a donation you can camp and get fed a massive curry meal by Abhay and Jaya, the owners of the sanctuary.

I hit this on day 5, after one and a half rest days and a resupply in which I bought way too much food and also deodorant since it seems like this trail actually has showers every couple of days.

On day 5 I woke up feeling like a nervous wreck and unsure what to do with my day. I knew rain was coming the next day and was worried about the already muddy forest. I decided to just go loosely with my original plan – to get to the Krishna Sanctuary and then make a call about whether I’d climb halfway up to the Raetea summit to camp with some of my friends. That led to my first time hitching, a mere two minutes into walking, a woman pulled up in her ute with her very cute dog and loaded us into the car and drove us all the way to the Krishna sanctuary. Meeting her and being at the sanctuary was like a full reset for my nervous system. I stayed the night there on the floor of a cabin, massaged my tight calves and nursed my remaining blisters from the beach.

By the time my alarm went off at 5:15am I was feeling good. A little nervous for this boogeyman of a hill but physically strong and ready for a big push. I was so excited to be off the beach.

The day was hard, but getting up the hill went quickly, I walked with two couples I had met the night before and they were fast up and down the hills. We laughed a lot as people slipped in mud and the boys sang and did gollum impressions. By the time we reached the summit I was feeling triumphant, this hill was not so bad!! But then the rain really set in and the descent began. Steep, deep mud for 8km. Eventually I lost the couples, preferring to not rush and navigate the mud alone. I got stuck up to my mid-shins and had to dig myself out with my poles. But overall I was feeling great, my usually-a-problem knee was holding up fantastically and I had only fallen once.

Then, about 5km from the end I slipped and my left leg folded under me and bashed into some sharp roots. It was one of those falls where your whole brain sets off an alarm and you have to count to ten before you can start assessing if you’re really hurt. The sharp pain from the sicks had me wondering if my sock had ripped (luckily no) and I stood and tested my ankle – it seemed ok so I kept walking. I didn’t have much choice.

By the time I made it out of the forest, the rain was getting lighter and I meandered through a misty field trying to find the trail markers.

I was so close to the gravel road to camp when I had my third fall of the day, walking down what I thought was regular dirt but was actually extremely slippery mud, toward a gate that would let me onto the road. I fell in slow motion, swearing at the universe. This fall didn’t hurt my body so much as my ego. I threw my pack over the gate and climbed over the stile before hobbling my muddy butt up the road to the campsite.

I was so incredibly happy to see my friends and the sun peaking through the clouds. After nine and a bit hours I had made it! There was a stream for washing and collecting water and I did both, using my muddy shorts to wash both themselves and my legs. I got my tent up and airing out, and someone gave me pre-boiled water for a hot chocolate. Everything was looking up!

But what goes up must come down I guess, and my left ankle started to swell. Turns out the fall and bashing it on those sticks did hurt. I massaged it and did my best to keep off it and by morning it felt walkable, so off we set.

This day was one of emotional ups and downs, it was supposed to be the chillest, easiest day of the section. Mostly gravel roads for 23km and a dairy (convenience store) 6km in. Unfortunately, my ankle was GRUMPY.

There was a mobile voting place at the dairy so I could vote right on trail and have an ice cream at the same time! The ladies at the voting place were so kind and I told them it was the first time I was able to vote in NZ so this was a big deal for me. I cast my vote then went to the dairy and bought myself an icecream and a canned iced coffee. Glorious.

The next 10km or so up a forestry road flew by. Then my ankle decided it was once again in a bad mood. I hobbled down the forestry road to reach a stream about 1.5km out from camp and stood in its very cold waters for ages to get the swelling down. It was gorgeous and a relief.

Camp this night was much more established, with a water tank, toilet and a shelter. I decided that if my ankle was still sore in the morning I’d stay, I had plenty of food, battery charge, and I knew more people would turn up the next day.

I felt a bit sad at this prospect but I knew I’d feel stupid if I pushed it and had to get off trail. The magic of thru hiking struck this night, and we had a lovely time chatting in the shelter and eating the whole block of Whittaker’s chocolate my friend brought. I got to see a ruru (a native NZ owl) for the first time ever. We all had a good laugh when someone’s tent got raided by a possum. It’s all ups and downs.

My ankle was, indeed, still sore this morning. One of my day-one friends decided to stay with me for the day as she also had tons of food and wanted to take some time to sort out her gear and the blisters on her toes. I managed to get some messages out to Fraser and the trail angel who was going to pick me up to let them know that I was okay but delayed.

We spent the morning slowly drinking our tea and coffee, and eating breakfast. I washed some of my clothes, read with my feet up in the air to help with swelling and munched on our over-abundance of snacks. To be honest, it was exactly what I needed.

Another group of hikers turned up in the afternoon and one was a physio (the trail provides!) she assessed my ankle and did a lymphatic drainage massage on it. She was a life saver. In the morning I felt way better but decided it wasn’t smart to continue on the rougher track ahead and backtracked to the road where I was picked up by a trail angel. This is NOT how I wanted this section to go, I really was looking forward to the river sections, but I needed to be smart and recognize that even with some improvement on my ankle, it was likely an overuse injury and needed real rest and to be checked by a physio in town.

To say I was disappointed is an understatement, I have never had an issue with my ankle like that so it really blindsided me. But taking a break is not quitting. And boy will it make for a good comeback story when I’m recovered and back on trail. For now, it’s several rest days and hoping I don’t get too stir crazy.

The whole album Grace by Jeff Buckley. I had never listened to it and Fraser told me it was essential listening. I listened to it while sitting in the sun in the Krishna Sanctuary garden, I loved it.

Honourable mention: that “just keep swimming” song Dory sings in finding nemo which got me through the last 3km of the Raetea forest.

Pickle Juice Vs Buttermilk: Which Marinade Is Best For Fried Chicken?

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Food  |  Read Source →

When it comes to down-home Southern cooking, buttermilk and pickles both have a prominent place thanks to their flavors and how they affect the chemistry of cooking. Todd Johnson, Proprietor of Strips Chicken & Brewing, insists that both have a place in fried chicken — although he asserts that buttermilk is the better all-around choice.

"If you're going to marinate your chicken, buttermilk wins," Johnson told Food Republic. "It's gently acidic, so it softens the surface of the chicken without turning it mushy. It's also thick enough to leave a tacky layer that flour clings to. That's the foundation of a craggy, crunchy crust." Just about any marinade can infuse the muscle fibers with moisture and keep it more tender, but it's that combination of subtle acid, sugars, and milk solids that gives it a more well-rounded, heartier flavor and makes it such an essential ingredient in fried chicken. Since it lacks the potency of a pickle brine, it also tends to absorb other seasonings quite well, so you don't have to rely solely on your breading.

While Johnson admits to being "not a fan of pickle juice as a marinade," he noted that it can have its place. "It's already salty and acidic, so it seasons and moistens the meat quickly," he explained. "You'll notice results in a couple of hours instead of overnight." Still, its high acidity means you have a narrower window between tender and mushy chicken, so it's not an ideal choice for beginners. Brine is a pretty potent ingredient, so while it's effective, it also calls for an experienced hand unless you're willing to experiment.

Good fried chicken has three components: meat, breading, and oil. Buttermilk is such a mainstay in recipes because it has a delicious effect on all three of these, promoting flavor, browning, and texture.

One of many major mistakes everyone makes with fried chicken is creating a breading that doesn't stick, and dry ingredients aren't the only contributor to this. "Oil and fat act like a release agent between meat and crust," Todd Johnson explained. "That's why we use fat-free buttermilk." For this aspect, it may seem like pickle brine is the right choice, but Johnson said that its lack of fat comes at the cost of a thinner consistency. Fat-free buttermilk is far more adhesive, giving your dry ingredients a prime surface to cling to, so they won't fall off the meat once they hit the oil.

Johnson conceded that buttermilk has a tendency to "over-brown" if your oil gets too hot, burning the milk sugars and solids, fouling the flavor. This is a pretty hefty disadvantage, but one that can be turned to an advantage with a careful eye. If you maintain Johnson's recommended oil temperature of 350 degrees Fahrenheit, you can toast, rather than burn, those solids, delivering a robust flavor profile focused on a hearty, umami-rich breading. Buttermilk is also a more convenient option, as you can just remove the excess drippings and drop your meat directly into your breading.

When you're looking to make fried chicken on the fly or don't have a surplus of seasoning, there are few options better than pickle brine. Todd Johnson said it's indispensable for achieving that "sandwich shop flavor" that some folks prefer, even if it has its quirks.

While you can always add some salt to your buttermilk, it won't have quite the same effect as using a jar of leftover pickle brine. "It's already salty and acidic, so it seasons and moistens the meat quickly," Todd Johnson clarified. Its extremely high and well-dissolved sodium content loosens up muscle fibers, making them far better at retaining moisture. While it lacks the rich solids and sugars of buttermilk, it brings far more tang, so it'll cut through even the heartiest, most marbled pieces of fried meat quite easily.

The biggest downside to pickle juice in your marinade is inconsistency and lack of other seasoning options. Not all brines are made the same, so unless you work with the exact same brand and product every time, there's always some amount of guess work involved. While pickle juice is one of the most powerful vehicles for flavor, it also leaves very little room for anything more subtle or nuanced. After marinating your meat, you'll also need to carefully pat dry each piece before tossing it in a batter or dry ingredients. Excess surface moisture creates a steam barrier between your chicken and breading, so even a bit left on the surface can ruin your food.
