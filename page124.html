Tuchel and Bellingham Snr have not always seen eye to eye during the German's time as England boss.

'It was a bit of an up and down [relationship] of course but we are in a good place,' said Tuchel.

'Yeah Jude and I have spoken about his brother because I was aware it was always his dream to play with Jobe for England,' explained Tuchel.

'Jude was always saying: "Watch him, watch him he's good, he can make it". 'I would say: "Yeah we are watching him".

A Costa Rican Beach Resort with a World-Class Coffee Program

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

In Hotels With Great Taste, we’re pulling back the curtain for a peek at the “special sauce” that hotels use to create memorable, meaningful culinary experiences for their guests.

Check-in at Nekajui on Costa Rica’s verdant Papagayo Peninsula happens inside a sparsely furnished bungalow the staff call La Casita. But walk across the drive, past a massive ceiba tree hung heavy with lanterns, and all humility vanishes. The whole property sweeps out before you: an endless green lawn, pools cascading down two levels, and, finally, the serenely sparkling bay beyond.

Nekajui is Ritz Carlton’s first Reserve resort in Central or South America. The Reserve’s are Ritz Carlton’s nod to the idea of complete escape, often in the far flung retreats—the Chinese Himalayas, or the Saudi Arabian desert.

Now, the recently opened Nekajui allows you to unwind on the lush Costa Rican coast teeming with wildlife like capuchins, coatis, and iguanas. Hidden away at the remote property, you’ll also find a local coffee program that’s on par with the best big city cafes in the world.

Sprawling over 1400 acres, Nekajui is a dreamy jungle beach resort that opened its doors on the secluded peninsula in February 2025.

Each of the 107 rooms and suites peeks out onto treetop and ocean sunset views. Inside, the decor features outdoor access and carved wood motifs typical of the Guanacaste region.

Many rooms also have access to patios large enough for morning yoga sessions and private plunge pools (take note: if a guest above you has one, you may see water splashing down your way). For larger parties, treetop tents and villas on the property afford even more space than standard rooms.

The main campus includes rooms, restaurants, and a spa, but the property’s grounds extend: a funicular (like a tiny cable car) runs dramatically down the cliffs, past howler monkeys, to the beach with water warm as a pleasant bath and a cave to explore.

Peruvian chef Diego Muñoz, whose career includes stints at world-renowned Spanish restaurants El Bulli and Mugaritz, leads the culinary program at the property’s main restaurants Puna and the tiny, six-seat chef’s counter within, Paruro.

At Puna and Parura, Muñoz’s menu is filled with dishes with the layered flavors and artistic compositions that helped push Peruvian cuisine into the spotlight on a global stage. Puna has a fusion feel , sliding flavors and spices from Munoz’s international travels into Peruvian classics like a robust sudado, and zippy ceviche. Paruro, the chefs counter located inside Puna, is an East Asian experience with bites like a nori-cured tenderloin and a seabass curry. You’ll find one of Munoz’s best creations, a deboned, prawn-stuffed chicken wing, on both menus.

It’s important to Muñoz that Puna uses local ingredients that are uncommon on Costa Rican menus. Fresh seafood, for example, can be a particular challenge to source, even as the overwhelming majority of Costa Rican territory is marine.

Aspirations are high for the beverage program as well. Cooper’s is a moody, mysterious cocktail space with a menu full of unexpected Guanacaste ingredients. It leans into the speakeasy style, and as such, is invite-only, but don’t be intimidated—schmooze your server a bit, or ask your personal concierge to score you an invite.

Inside, the menu is ambitious, dotted with some adventurous local distillates—goat cheese, cascara, mango leaf—which find their way into thrilling riffs on classics like the martini-esque Turrialba using fino sherry and the aforementioned goat cheese distillate.

Elsewhere on the property, you can dine at Niri Beach Club, with an Iberian-focused menu, and the all-day Cafe Rincon which has sandwiches, bowls, and pastries.

Nekajui’s commitment to on-the-ground ingredients shines brightest in its coffee program atCafé Rincon. Fabi Rojas operates the micro-roastery at Nekajui, which features always updating selections from each of Costa Rica’s seven coffee growing regions.

For a country about the size of West Virginia, Costa Rica produces an impressive diversity of coffee: easy-drinking crowd pleasers in the lower elevations of the coastal west, bright acidic pops from Valle Occidental in the north, fruity, prize-winning geishas—one of which fetched over $300 per pound at auction—in the mountainous Los Santos region of the south central. And Fabi has nurtured personal relationships with farmers all over the country to ensure she has access to all of it.

And while she’ll make your coffee however you want it, the move is to have it brewed in a vandola. The one piece clay carafe and pour-over cone looks like something that might date back hundreds of years, but the design actually came from Costa Rican coffee roaster and barista Minor Alfaro in 2015. Brewing in clay helps maintain aroma longer, and the valve into the base of the dripper allows more gentle oxygenation according to Alfaro. Oxygen is usually the enemy of coffee, but allowing a small amount in through the valve can sweeten the flavor.

Besides brewing coffee better than my Chemex, each vandola is a handmade work of ceramic art—an apt vessel to showcase coffees grown, roasted, and brewed with exquisite care.

Trump says Stephen Miller to replace Susie Wiles as White House chief of staff

📅 2026-11-06  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

President Donald Trump on Friday announced White House deputy chief of staff Stephen Miller will be promoted to chief of staff, replacing Susie Wiles.

Trump revealed the stunning shake-up just three days after Democrats won a landslide victory in the midterm elections, gaining majority control in the House and Senate and scrambling the trajectory of Trump's last two years in office.

Elevating Miller, an architect of Trump's controversial mass-deportation efforts, is bound to raise the hackles of the incoming class of congressional Democrats, who have already vowed to investigate the government's immigration policies.

Miller will take over starting Monday, Trump said in a Truth Social post, praising his longtime aide as "one of my most trusted advisors."

Wiles, "who has done an outstanding job, will be moving over to the Trump Political Operation," the president wrote.

Asked about the move later Friday afternoon, Trump said, "I wanted to make a change."

"I think Susie's done a very good job, great job. But I just wanted to make it," Trump told reporters as he left the White House for an event in Cape Canaveral, Florida.

"Stephen's been great," Trump said, adding, "Sometimes in life you have to make moves."

Wiles resigned Thursday night, a senior White House source told MS NOW. Wiles told Trump, who was said to be angry with her following the GOP's huge election losses, "let me make this easy on you — I am going to resign," MS NOW reported.

"Today is my last day as White House Chief of Staff," Wiles said in an X post Friday afternoon. "Serving President Trump in the White House has been the honor of a lifetime."

"While my role may be changing, my commitment to the President and the mission never will," she wrote.

Senior administration officials were quick to congratulate Miller.

"Patriots in control," read an X post from Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, whose job security has come into question after some Republicans, including Vice President JD Vance, blamed the midterm results on the Iran war.

Vance also praised Miller on X, saying he "will do great things."

The official X account for Trump's political operation celebrated the announcement by posting a black-and-white photo of Miller under the caption, "It's Miller time."

Democrats and other critics, meanwhile, filed in to denounce the pick.

House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries, D-N.Y., who could be elected House speaker next year, called Miller "a dangerous xenophobe and reckless extremist who has American blood on his hands."

"His presence in government should be eradicated like a malignant tumor. Fire him today," Jeffries wrote on X.

Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer, D-N.Y., wrote that Trump "learned nothing from his historic defeat."

"Stephen Miller is the architect of the most barbaric, inhumane policies of this administration. Trump couldn't have made a worse choice. Our Democratic majorities will fight him every step of the way," Schumer wrote.

Others took Miller's promotion as a sign that Trump, who seemed to express some willingness to negotiate with a blue Congress, will instead remain combative.

"For those who hoped the @POTUS might pivot to work with the incoming Congress, the appointment of Miller is a bucket of ICE cold water," wrote David Axelrod, a former senior advisor to President Barack Obama.

The White House reshuffle follows multiple resignations of senior staff in the Department of Justice since Election Day. They include Civil Harmeet Dhillon, a leader in the DOJ's civil rights division, John Eisenberg, who headed the national security division, and Jason Reding Quiñones, who had been in charge of conspiracy investigations related to Trump's foes.

Wiles oversaw Trump's winning 2024 presidential campaign and has wielded major influence in his second administration, while largely staying out of the spotlight. She drew focus in late 2025, when Vanity Fair reported that Wiles admitted that Trump had pursued "retribution" as president and that he had an "alcoholic's personality."

Trump, and many other top administration officials, defended Wiles at the time, underscoring her status in the White House.

While Wiles is not fully leaving Trump's orbit, her resignation follows a pattern of other top female officials who have left or been fired from Trump's second administration, and then replaced by men.

Ralph Lauren Pre-Fall 2027

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Ralph Lauren picked up the Menswear Designer of the Year Award at the CFDAs earlier this week. He also took home the organization’s first men’s prize in 1981. That means—if I have my dates right—he’s been at the top of American fashion since before this year’s other winners were born. In an industry built on change, renewing and maintaining a legacy can be as difficult as building one, but even as he approaches the brand’s 60th anniversary, Lauren still has fashion’s attention. PoloFest, the vintage marketplace the company organized in Tokyo last month, with its 5,000 specially selected archival Polo pieces and 10,000 visitors, is bound to spawn imitators across the business.

This pre-fall collection wasn’t designed to make that kind of noise, but it’s as rooted in the brand’s iconography as anything else it does. Take the glen plaid pantsuit in look 3—it’s based on a suit that Lauren himself frequently wears and made by tailors in Italy with the specialized techniques that give men’s suiting its sharp lines and structure. The track pants in look 1 are another Ralph tic; he’s always liked mixing casual and formal, low and high. Purple is the accent color here this season, as it is across so much of fashion, alongside minty green, though we can’t blame the prevailing currents. Lauren pays no heed to trends. Case in point: The silvery plunge-front halter top is a lift from his own 1990s archives.

Hims & Hers struck a deal with Eli Lilly to offer the drugmaker’s branded obesity treatments through its online health and wellness platform.

Under the agreement, Eli Lilly will give Hims & Hers pharmacies direct access to its U.S. Food and Drug Administration-approved GLP-1 medications at self-pay prices. Eligible patients on the Hims & Hers platform can be prescribed and order Eli Lilly's injectable Zepbound (tirzepatide) and daily oral medication Foundayo (orforglipron) without leaving the company's platform.

Prior to this agreement, Lilly drugs were available to patients on Hims & Hers only if they left the site and went to Lilly's direct-to-consumer platform, LillyDirect.

Shares of Hims & Hers jumped 8% Friday following news of the Eli Lilly partnership.

The agreement brings together Lilly’s obesity medicines and Hims & Hers’ consumer platform to expand consumers' access to FDA-approved weight loss drugs, the companies said.

"Patients can complete a consultation, receive a prescription if their provider determines it is appropriate and have an FDA-approved Lilly medicine shipped directly to their home, all in one seamless experience," the companies said in a press release.

Self-pay pricing starts at $149 per month for Foundayo and $299 per month for Zepbound.

"At Lilly, we are always looking for new ways to help more people get the medicines they need, and that means meeting patients where they already are," said Ilya Yuffa, executive vice president and president, Lilly USA and global customer capabilities, Eli Lilly and Company, in a statement. "Millions of people turn to Hims & Hers for their care, and through this agreement, those patients now have a simple, more direct way to get FDA-approved Lilly medicine at an affordable price if prescribed."

Hims & Hers' technology platform supports provider outreach, follow-up care and treatment planning. Weight loss patients on Hims & Hers receive a membership experience that includes 24/7 access to a care team, nutrition guidance, regular clinical check-ins and access to the Hims & Hers Weight Loss community, according to the company.

The agreement with Eli Lilly mirrors Hims & Hers' partnership with Novo Nordisk, which has been selling its branded weight loss drugs Wegovy and Ozempic through Hims & Hers since March. Novo had previously sued the telehealth company for offering compounded versions of its medications. Hims & Hers' agreement with Novo marked a shift in its obesity strategy toward FDA-approved branded GLP-1 treatments and away from cheaper compounded GLP-1 drugs.

Earlier this year, Hims & Hers took heat from the pharmaceutical manufacturer and federal regulators after announcing plans to launch a knockoff of Novo's oral pill. It backed down from those plans under pressure.

Hims & Hers stated that it never offered access to compounded copies of Lilly’s GLP-1 drugs and has committed not to do so in the future.

As part of the agreement, the companies have committed to protecting independent clinical decision-making, so that providers on the Hims & Hers platform continue to prescribe the treatment they determine is right for each patient.

“Lilly is one of the most innovative pharmaceutical companies in history, and I’m incredibly proud that Hims & Hers is a part of making sure more people can access the medicines they’ve created,” said Andrew Dudum, co-founder and CEO of Hims & Hers, in a statement. “By working together, we’re not just reaching more people with obesity with seamless, personal access to FDA-approved Lilly medicine. We’re showing the industry what innovators can achieve for customers everywhere when we work together to make the world well.”

Hims & Hers has faced financial pressure as it shifts from high-margin compounded GLP-1s to lower-margin branded weight-loss drugs. The company posted a $92 million loss in Q1, including $33 million in restructuring costs, largely from write-downs of its compounded GLP-1 supply chain, which executives said faced “risk of obsolescence.”

In Q2, Hims & Hers reported an $86 million loss, compared with a $43 million profit a year earlier. The quarter included $81 million in one-time costs, including expenses tied to its exit from compounded GLP-1s.

<a href="https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/pharma/cms-makes-it-official-codifying-medicare-drug-price-negotiations-rolled-out-under-ira" hreflang="en">CMS makes it official, codifying Medicare drug price negotiations established under IRA</a>

📅 2026-11-06  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →
CMS makes it official, codifying Medicare drug price negotiations established under IRA" style="max-width:100%; height:auto; border-radius:8px;" loading="lazy">

Against a backdrop of repeated industry challenges in court, the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services (CMS) is locking in the drug price negotiation framework established under the Biden-era Inflation Reduction Act (IRA).

In turn, the process under which CMS selects certain high-spend Medicare Part B or Part D drugs and negotiates lower prices with manufacturers will carry on into 2029 and beyond. The rule codifies and expands policies previously established through guidance for the initial 2026-28 period.

The final rule is slated to go into effect Dec. 5, CMS said in a fact sheet accompanying Thursday’s announcement. By Feb. 1 of next year, CMS will select up to 20 drugs covered under Medicare Part B or Part D for price negotiations taking effect in 2029, with the potential to also select medicines included in previous rounds for renegotiation.

That upcoming group of drugs will make up the fourth cycle of pricing negotiations, with negotiated prices taking effect in 2029. CMS asserted that the rule gives both patients and drugmakers “a clear path forward as the agency builds on the program’s results and continues negotiating prices for high-cost drugs.”

The initial rounds of price negotiations have already included a slew of prominent brands, including Novo's semaglutide franchise of Wegovy and Ozempic and cancer meds like Bristol Myers Squibb’s Pomalyst, Pfizer’s Ibrance and AstraZeneca’s Calquence.

Meanwhile, the latest tranche of drugs selected earlier this year for negotiation, whose new prices would go into effect in 2028, includes notable entrants like Gilead’s HIV treatment Biktarvy, AbbVie’s Botox and Eli Lilly’s diabetes drug Trulicity, to name just a few.

Beyond codifying the framework for price negotiations in 2029 and subsequent years, CMS has also taken steps to fine-tune several elements of the program.

Namely, the rule finalizes a policy on how CMS classifies certain fixed-combination drugs, with the aim of addressing “potential program integrity concerns” that could encourage drugmakers to evade negotiated prices by steering patients toward new product formulations.

The rule also includes a provision establishing a temporary “small biotech floor” that prevents CMS from offering or accepting a counteroffer below a specified price floor for certain eligible small biotech drugs in 2029 and 2030. It also codifies two policies related to Medicare Part D intended to ensure plan coverage of medicines with a maximum fair price in effect and limit the prices plans pay pharmacies and other dispensing entities.

The IRA and the Medicare price negotiations it empowered have been frequent targets of the biopharma industry, which has broadly alleged that the policy is unconstitutional and that the pricing talks aren’t as voluntary as advertised. But attempts by major drugmakers and powerful trade organizations like the Pharmaceutical Research and Manufacturers of America (PhRMA) to push back on Medicare price negotiations have repeatedly stalled out.

In the interim, the U.S. government has made a separate and more recent push to rein in high prescription drug costs by leaning on President Donald Trump’s “most favored nation” (MFN) strategy.

“President Trump directed us to lower drug costs for Americans using every tool at our disposal,” CMS Administrator Mehmet Oz, M.D., said in a statement announcing the new rule. “This rule provides additional tools to help CMS negotiate better deals, achieve greater savings for taxpayers and beneficiaries, and improve health outcomes for seniors who rely on the Medicare Prescription Drug Program.”

MFN, for its part, has yielded at times, controversial deals with more than two dozen drugmakers, generally involving commitments to price new medicines in line with prices in high-income comparator countries and offer MFN pricing on certain existing drugs through Medicaid.

Through those deals, which have also included investment commitments in the U.S., drugmakers have been able to secure temporary exemptions from the Trump administration’s threatened pharmaceutical import tariffs.

Around this week’s midterm elections, several Big Pharma executives have signaled that they expect little immediate change to their thinking around MFN. Nevertheless, the lack of transparency surrounding the deals has already drawn scrutiny from Democratic lawmakers. With Democrats set to control both chambers of Congress beginning in January, the party may soon press harder to uncover the details of those agreements.
