The 10 Most Ambitious Documentaries of All Time, Ranked

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Yes, documentaries can be a bit formulaic, and there are conventional ones that have interviews, voiceover, and fairly mundane visuals that all serve to depict something objectively, or give the impression that things are being explored objectively, but that’s not every documentary, by any means. You can put that criticism on any genre, really, because there are generic action movies, horror films, comedies, etc.

Documentaries seem to get that criticism the most often, though. It’s not really fair, and maybe one way to combat that sentiment is by highlighting some particularly cinematic and groundbreaking ones. So, the following might go some way toward that, because these feel like some of the most ambitious documentary movies of all time, with the lot of them being interesting for different reasons and certainly unexpected compared to what you might picture when you think of “ordinary” or “normal” documentaries.

Since Apocalypse Now is a tremendously ambitious – and unusual – war movie, it’s inevitable that any behind-the-scenes documentary about its production is going to be, at the very least, also quite interesting. Yet everything that happened behind the scenes with this movie was more chaotic than you'd expect, even if you were expecting chaos, and so Hearts of Darkness: A Filmmaker’s Apocalypse is ultimately a dizzying watch.

It doesn’t mess around in showcasing the hectic production of Apocalypse Now, and it’s about as psychologically intense and even as surreal as a good chunk of the actual movie is. Also, Apocalypse Now is already an easy movie to admire, but watching Hearts of Darkness: A Filmmaker’s Apocalypse is a pretty much guaranteed way to respect the fact that filming was completed – and the final product made some kind of sense – even more.

Tokyo Olympiad is a documentary about the 1964 Tokyo Summer Olympics, or might be better described as a look at said event, kind of neutral and without commentary or much of a narrative. It might sound daunting, just because of its approach and the fact that it’s almost three hours long, but it’s honestly as artistic and mesmerizing as sports footage can get, even if you're not hugely into watching footage of sports.

You notice the three-hour runtime to some extent, sure, but Tokyo Olympiad does remain interesting throughout, with everything captured in unbelievable detail. It's far more than just a clip show, as all the camera angles are a good deal more interesting than what you'd see on a TV broadcast, with the editing playing a huge role in enhancing everything and making it all feel like a largely hypnotic viewing experience.

Historical value/context is important when it comes to Man with a Movie Camera, since the existence of this film is such that the whole documentary format might look entirely different today had this movie never been made. No hyperbole, but it's likely one of the most important and influential documentaries of all time, using inventive visuals and creative editing techniques to show how life was in the Soviet Union during the 1920s.

That really is the premise, though. Man with a Movie Camera is simple, and outside the style, there isn't really a whole to this silent film. Yet it’s the style that makes it dazzling and engaging to this day, doing some interesting enough things with camera tricks and editing to make it feel exciting and very alive, all to the point where it’s worth checking out, even if you might assume that a listless documentary that’s nearly a century old would be dull. Maybe other listless 100-year-old documentaries would be. Not Man with a Movie Camera, though.

Two filmmakers were credited with making The Five Obstructions: Lars von Trier and Jørgen Leth. The former’s the one you're more likely to have heard of, and he does feel somewhat in control for much of this documentary, but that’s largely got to do with Leth being more of the subject here, since The Five Obstructions involves Lars von Trier challenging him to remake one of his old short films five different times, and with absurd rules on each occasion.

Well, technically, the rules get more unusual with each new remake, and then those shorts Leth did make (or seemed to make) are shown in quite a bit of detail, so that’s where his directing definitely comes in. There might be more involvement on his part, as it’s hard to know how much of The Five Obstructions is genuine and how much is two Danish filmmakers playing a feature-length prank on the audience, but in any event, it’s all very engaging to watch, and certainly interesting to think about, too.

Flee is an animated documentary, which is interesting enough on its own (see also another documentary from 2008 that’ll be mentioned a little further down). It’s about a man’s life experiences, basically, including his time as a refugee and how he grappled with his sexuality while surrounded by people who weren't always accepting of it… and the animation itself is a way to protect his identity, essentially.

There's quite a bit of time covered throughout Flee, and also numerous weighty subjects discussed and explored.

It’s simple animation, sure, but it does still lead to interesting presentation, and there’s quite a bit of time covered throughout Flee, and also numerous weighty subjects discussed and explored. The whole thing ends up being rather moving and quite memorable. Though it’s currently in an awkward middle-ground between being a new release and something old enough to be considered a classic, there’s a good chance time will ultimately be kind to Flee.

Rightly sometimes regarded as one of the greatest films of all time, including non-documentaries, Shoah is a mammoth documentary and one that people owe to themselves to watch at least once in their lives, though you do need to devote a ton of time to finishing it. The runtime stands out and has to be addressed, since Shoah goes for more than nine hours, using that length of time to hammer home the enormity of the event it explores.

It’s made up entirely of interviews with people recalling the Holocaust, including survivors, witnesses/bystanders, and even a few perpetrators. There’s no archival footage or much else beyond those interviews, with the approach nevertheless being used to form a sprawling and daunting narrative by stringing together all those different testimonies. Shoah is a challenging watch, but it has to be, also feeling about as essential and historically significant as war documentaries can get.

O.J.: Made in America could well be the greatest crime documentary of all time. However, it's also more than a documentary about one particularly publicized event and subsequent criminal trial, using the story of O.J. Simpson and his much-discussed trial to unpack a whole range of things, including the American dream, the country's history of racial conflict, and, more broadly, investigating American culture throughout the second half of the 20th century.

Sure, it’s seven and a half hours long, which means it has room to do a lot, but it’s still impressive how much is packed into that runtime. Also, it has to be acknowledged that it exists in a strange middle-ground between movie and miniseries, as it was screened theatrically and therefore won an Oscar for Best Documentary, but most would've watched it as a miniseries in five parts, and the Academy Awards changed the rules after O.J.: Made in America won. Oh well… point is, it deserved to win, and it’s easily one of the most monumental documentaries of the century so far.

Depending on where you spend your time online in 2026, you may feel like You Can See Everything is one of the biggest movies of the year. At least in some circles, it’s one of the most discussed, but then also if you want to look at things more objectively, or outside certain circles, it has already earned a lot for a documentary (and, at the time of writing, it’s only just starting to get released outside the U.S.), and the level of critical acclaim has been similarly remarkable.

It’s co-directed by Nathan Fielder, who uses the documentary to build on some of the stuff he was doing in The Rehearsal, which was a show where he built upon the stuff he was doing in Nathan for You, but what Lance Oppenheim contributes here as the other co-director shouldn’t be overlooked (he’s had a good year, since he was also behind the recent Primetime). As for the content of You Can See Everything… it’s wild. It’s best not to say anything about why it’s wild. But it is. It’s a big deal. It’s hard to stop thinking about, once seen, and moves so well for such a long and static documentary, if it can even be called a documentary in the first place.

A film that covers a particular war within an extensive conflict that’s still relevant to this day, Waltz with Bashir is uniquely presented and shattering as a film. It has director Ari Folman taking a personal approach to covering the 1982 Lebanon War, wartime atrocities committed, and attached post-traumatic stress disorder, with the film being about his attempts to recover his memories from his involvement in the conflict as a young man.

Waltz with Bashir explores the conflict – in particular an infamous event known as the Sabra and Shatila massacre – not from the victims’ point of view, which has made it controversial. However, it doesn’t feel like it’s in favor of the attack’s perpetrators, showing the horror of what happened and condemning both it and other atrocities around the same time. It feels distinctly anti-war, with Folman addressing what he feels he can personally speak to, doing so in a way that feels self-reflective and critical. Not everyone agrees with that, but in any event, what this does as a wholly distinctive and unusually presented documentary is impressive, and it does feel like Waltz with Bashir inevitably achieves what it set out to do.

Of all the documentaries here that feel like so much more than “just” documentaries, Koyaanisqatsi is perhaps the most groundbreaking, unusual, and compelling. There isn't anything here by way of voiceover or interviews, and you don’t even get all that much text explaining the things you're seeing and hearing, but Koyaanisqatsi does still get its message across through all its sights and sounds.

Different people will get slightly different things from it, yet the environmental message and the exploration of a conflict between humanity and the natural world are still very much effectively conveyed, not to mention ultimately hard to miss if you pay even the slightest amount of attention. And Koyaanisqatsi is worth paying much more than a slight amount of attention. It’s easily among the most memorable and radically presented documentary movies of all time, and it’s lost none of its power when watched today, more than four decades on from its initial release.

Iraq's oil output climbed to its highest level in seven years, the IEA said

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Gulf crude exports reached 18.4 million barrels a day in October, still 26% below pre-war levels, the IEA said

Iraq pumped 4.6 million barrels of crude a day in October, an increase of 540,000 barrels a day from the previous month, the International Energy Agency said in its monthly oil market report on Friday.

That was the highest level since September 2019, according to Bloomberg.

Iraq's output ran 170,000 barrels a day above its implied target of 4.43 million, the IEA said. The agency put the country's sustainable capacity at 4.87 million barrels a day.

Gulf producers excluding Oman exported 18.4 million barrels a day in October, which is 26% below pre-war levels, the IEA said. Those exports rose by 950,000 barrels a day from the prior month, according to Bloomberg. Producers in the region are sending some cargoes on low-profile tanker runs across the Strait of Hormuz and are shipping other crude by alternative export routes that never touch the strait.

Global oil supply rose by 2.3 million barrels a day to 105.4 million in October, the IEA said. That followed a 3 million barrel-a-day increase in September. The agency said the gains came from high Gulf exports despite continuing attacks on energy infrastructure and tankers.

Saudi Arabia produced 9.99 million barrels a day in October, up from 8.93 million in September, the IEA said. Kuwait produced 2.03 million barrels a day, up from 1.58 million.

The IEA's estimates differ from figures that Gulf producers submitted to OPEC. Iraq told OPEC it produced 4.888 million barrels a day in October, above the IEA's 4.6 million. Saudi Arabia told OPEC it pumped 10.563 million barrels a day, compared with the IEA's 9.99 million.

The recovery in Gulf output follows disruption from the U.S.-Israeli war on Iran, which began in late February.

Travis Barker Reveals He Stopped Smoking Weed After Precancer Diagnosis: ‘I Loved Blunts’

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The Blink-182 drummer opened up about his decision to cut the kush on this week's Big Bro with Kid Cudi podcast.

Travis Barker had a very good reason for giving up weed. During a sit-down with Kid Cudi on this week’s episode of the Big Bro with Kid Cudi podcast, the 50-year-old Blink-182 drummer said he had to stop smoking his beloved blunts after receiving a scary diagnosis.

“I only quit because I got diagnosed with precancer of the esophagus,” Barker told Cudder on Wednesday’s (Nov. 11) episode. “I loved blunts and I would smoke too much.” Barker described trying to be healthier for his kids and taking up running and boxing to get in shape after years of going for a six mile run and then celebrating afterward with a cigarette and a blunt.

And while he misses smoking — ”I was so good at it” — Barker said he asked his doctor if the diagnosis was serious and he assured him it was. “He’s like, ‘This is your warning shot Trav. Don’t f–k around. You gotta stop smoking,'” he said the doc told him about his 10-15 blunt-per-day habit. “Or I would have never stopped. I thought I could be like Willie Nelson and smoke to the day I die. I feel like weed never hurt anybody, it just wasn’t right for me.”

Cudi countered that he had to go to rehab and “reestablish” his relationship with weed because he realized he was overdoing it at a time when he was smoking 15-20 blunts a day. “If I was awake, I was smoking, no matter what I was doing,” he said of his former habit.

Barker, who has a three-year-old son, Rocky Barker, with wife Kourtney Kardashian, as well as son Landon Barker, 23 and daughter Alabama Barker, 20 with his ex, Shanna Moakler — he also helped raise his stepdaughter, Atiana de la Hoya, 27 — admitted that he still thinks about firing up a blunt “all the time … I miss it. I’m not gonna lie.”

Luckily for him, running has helped keep him on the healthy path. “Many years later now, 20 years later, a couple miles a day keeps the demons away,” said Barker, who stopped using prescription drugs and other drugs when he was in a burn center for six months after surviving a deadly 2008 plane crash that killed four people, including his assistant and bodyguard. “My running started getting bigger and more important to me than smoking.”

He added, “It really helped me stay sober ’cause you can’t run you a half a marathon if you’re doing drugs. It’s gonna to be the hardest 13.1 miles of your life.”

Last month, Barker said he had undergone a cubital tunnel release surgery to fix a pinched nerve in his elbow that was causing numbness and weakness in his hands.

Last week, The Pokemon Company released the final Pokemon Trading Card Game set of the year—Delta Reign. It's perhaps a set that encapsulates 2026 as a whole for the Pokemon TCG, or at least the sets released, with some great cards, but not enough.

As an avid Pokemon TCG fan, I've opened more than 100 packs of each of this year's expansions and experienced the highest highs and the lowest lows. Putting the scalping issues aside for a second, here is our ranking of each, from worst to best.

This was the easiest choice on the list— Perfect Order is comfortably the worst set The Pokemon Company released this year. It made sense to feature Mega Zygarde so prominently, given its importance in Pokemon Legends: Z-A, but there are 100s of other Pokemon to catch in Z-A, whereas the Mega Zygarde ex SIR took up one of only four SIR slots in the set. As a TCG, you need cards featuring Pokemon that will sell sets, and Mega Zygarde isn't one of them.

The Meowth SIR is cool, and both the Godzilla Starmie and Clefable SIRs have some nice colors, but it's not a great selection, and the IRs don't save it either. In my preview, I called it the worst expansion since 2024, and I stick by that.

Pitch Black suffers from a lot of the same problems Perfect Order does, it's lacking a killer Pokemon. Had the regular Darkrai been the feature, it'd have been far better, but instead we got the much-maligned Mega Darkrai. The card is killer, but it's not one that makes the set stand out, and as such, it falls low on the list.

Like Perfect Order, Pitch Black features a pretty but underwhelming selection of SIRs, including Morpeko and Mega Chandelure, and a lackluster set of IRs, which is saved only by Slowbro. I called the set "deflating" in my preview, and I think that sums up the Pokemon TCG's recent momentum nicely.

If there is one thing the Pokemon Trading Card Game did really well this year, it's making cards that pop​​​​​​. All of Chaos Rising's SIR's have really nice color schemes, but again, they mostly feature Pokemon nobody cares about. The Mega Greninja ex definitely puts it above the Mega Darkrai and Mega Zygarde. It also has the best IR selection of the three sets comfortably so far.

While this has no bearing on the rankings, during my Chaos Rising preview, I had some staggering pulls and called it the "most astonishing pack opening" I've had in 30 years of collecting.

Delta Reign is the Pokemon TCG's latest set, and it takes the top spot of the four regular expansions released this year. It does something the other three failed to do, and has TWO banger SIRS. I said the Mega Rayquaza SIR would be one of the most expensive cards in a while, and I was wrong, but it's still over $300 and a stunner; the Raikou SIR takes the cake for me, though. Jumping down a stormy mountain, the Gen 2 Legendary looks fantastic.

With IRs featuring adorable Pokemon like Eevee, Growlithe, Zorua, and Azurill, Delta Reign takes the prestigious bronze medal. The Pokemon Company kicked 2026 off with style with Ascended Heroes. It's probably my favorite since 2021's Evolving Skies, and it features some of the best SIRs ever created. The affectionately named "Treekachu" is great, the Mega Gengar is menacing, the Mega Dragonite is beautiful, and all three SIRs featuring the starters are vibrant. There are too many to name-check, and I think that's what makes this set so good—the variety.

The IRs let it down a little, though there are some bangers; the SIRs do so much heavy lifting that it doesn't really matter. Even some of the Mega Attack Rares are peak. In my preview, I may have bemoaned the set size, but I said, "Ascended Heroes, for me, goes down as an all-timer. It has some of the best art created in Pokemon TCG history, and I had a blast getting through my packs."

Hugo Boss CEO Daniel Grieder Steps Down Amid Frasers Takeover

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Hugo Boss has announced that CEO Daniel Grieder will depart the German company, effective December 31. David Epstein — currently the chief brand partnerships officer at Frasers Group — will replace him, effective January 1, 2027.

Grieder was appointed CEO in June 2021, overseeing the company’s Claim 5 turnaround — a strategy focused on improving brand relevance, product innovation, digital output, omnichannel experience, and operational logistics to drive growth. Before Hugo Boss, Grieder spent more than 20 years at Tommy Hilfiger.

His mandate at Hugo Boss was to double company sales to €4 billion by 2025, believing the company had lost its relevance within the fashion space. He decided that while the brand’s heritage remained strong, it needed reframing. In 2022, he divided the parent company into two individual brands: Hugo and Boss — positioning Hugo for Gen Z and Boss for millennials.

Grieder also ushered in an ongoing collaboration with David Beckham, introduced in 2025 as a co-designed capsule. For fiscal 2025, Hugo Boss reported €4.3 billion in group sales.

“Looking back, what makes me proudest is what our teams accomplished over the past years,” said Grieder in a statement. “Together, we have brought Boss and Hugo back on stage, built a 24/7 lifestyle product offering, redesigned our stores worldwide, and renewed our culture and the way we work. I am deeply grateful to everyone who made this possible.”

Grieder continued: “With this strong foundation in place, I am convinced that now is the right time for me to step down and hand over to the next generation of leadership. It has been an honor to lead Hugo Boss, and I will always feel connected to its people and brands.”

Daniel Grieder and Boss ambassador David Beckham.

In May 2026, Hugo Boss sales fell 6% and have declined further since, attributing this to the latest phase in the Claim 5 turnaround strategy, “Claim 5 Touchdown”, which began in December 2025, focusing on brand equity, relevance, and long-term profitability over short-term volume growth. During Q3 earnings, the company shared new guidance for fiscal 2026, forecasting a high-single-digit decline, attributed to weakened demand and ongoing uncertainty.

In June this year, Frasers Group put forward a €2 billion (€38 per share) takeover proposal to Hugo Boss. The German company considered it “financially inadequate”, urging shareholders to reject it. However, Frasers continued to push, progressively upping its stake in the company. In August, Frasers increased its stake to 47.89%, edging closer to majority stakeholder status. Frasers Group CEO Michael Murray assumed the Hugo Boss chair position in September, after Stephan Sturm stepped down.

In October, the British retail conglomerate raised its stake to approximately 50.08% — an additional purchase of 198,666 shares, equating to €7.63 million, according to the stock market filing. The German fashion company became a subsidiary of the Mike Ashley-owned Frasers business. This year, Frasers has continued its aggressive M&A play, buying out Harvey Nichols, acquiring a stake in Under Armour, and upping its stake in Burberry. The Frasers portfolio also includes Flannels, The Webster, Sports Direct, and Slazenger, among others.

“On behalf of the supervisory board, I would like to sincerely thank Daniel for his dedication and his contribution to Hugo Boss,” said Murray in a statement. “Under his leadership, the company sharpened its positioning and renewed the way the brands connect with customers around the world. We respect his decision to step down.”

Four ideal signings for Mauricio Pochettino’s Man Utd in January

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Mauricio Pochettino is set to take the reins at Manchester United and they’ll be hoping for an immediate boost in their fortunes – but they might have to add to their squad at the next opportunity to give themselves the best chance.

United’s season so far has been a disaster. They’ve gone into the international break sitting in the Premier League’s relegation zone. It’s cost Michael Carrick his job as manager, but quite rightly, the blame is more widespread.

United’s owners, directors and players have all come in for criticism, but if anything’s going to change, it’s probably the latter.

It’d be wrong to expect a mass overhaul in January, a notoriously difficult transfer window to do big business in, but it’d be equally daft to expect United to just sit around and do nothing.

A new manager coming in gives them a chance to bring in new ideas and players to suit those ideas.

So, keeping it semi-realistic, who’d be a good signing for them under Pochettino?

It’s tempting whenever a manager gets a new job to look at who’s at his former clubs that he might like to reunite with.

Ironically in this case, the one familiar player that might most appeal from Tottenham isn’t someone Pochettino worked with at Tottenham – it’s been a while, after all – but at Chelsea instead.

Gallagher was Pochettino’s most-used player during his sole season at Chelsea, playing in 48 of his 51 games in charge.

Sure, United already did a lot of work on their midfield over the summer, but they still seem to be missing runners there. And if there’s one thing Gallagher can do, it’s run. And if there’s one thing Pochettino wants his midfielders to do… you get the picture.

On average, Gallagher has covered 12.2km per 90 minutes when playing in the Premier League this season. It’s the highest average in the squad of anyone with three or more starts.

Pochettino used to rave about Gallagher at Chelsea. Exhibit A, this ringing endorsement in February 2024:

“He is a player with a great commitment to the team. He is always trying to compensate in every situation, in offensive and defensive situations. It’s priceless to have a player like him.

“‘He is in the starting XI nearly every game and one of the captains. For me, he is the type of player that the club need to have.

“He runs, he plays, he fights. He does everything to win. He is a really important player that the coaching staff want to have on the team.”

Gallagher hasn’t exactly been a regular starter for Spurs this season, but he only joined them in January, so he might be tricky to prise away.

But hypothetically, he’d get more of a look in from Pochettino in a midfield where Kobbie Mainoo is a top prospect, but Carlos Baleba has had injury issues, Youri Tielemans hasn’t convinced with his energy and Andrey Santos remains an unknown quantity.

Santos spent the 2023-24 season out on loan away from Chelsea rather than as part of Pochettino’s squad.

Gallagher would add to United’s homegrown quota too, but for a Pochettino team, he wouldn’t just be making up the numbers.

Pochettino got the best out of Luke Shaw when they worked together at Southampton, but Luke Shaw then was different to Luke Shaw now.

Back in those days he was a talented teenage prospect. Nowadays, he’s United’s tired only natural option at left-back. Quite simply, they need to freshen it up.

Pochettino will probably be happy to keep Shaw around as a senior, trustworthy figure. But he’ll be wanting more energy from the full-back positions and that means fresh legs.

Some of Pochettino’s best teams thrived when their full-backs bombed on to offer width while the wingers cut inside. Shaw can’t really do that anymore.

One player who can get up and down the flank is Palace’s Mitchell, who might not be the most inspiring choice but could be the best of what’s available.

Palace’s precarious position, coupled with the fact that Mitchell is out of contract at the end of the season, could make January his ideal time to escape.

His attacking output hasn’t always been cited as a strong point, but he can cover plenty of ground up and down the channel. A Pochettino team don’t necessarily need their full-backs to be the ones getting goals and assists, just to offer support and threatening runs.

Mitchell also has a good record in duels and could offer United more stability in defence, as they desperately need. There might be a signing with a higher ceiling to make, but that’s the kind of thing that might have to wait until the summer.

It’s not just at left-back United need to upgrade. The battle between Diogo Dalot and Noussair Mazraoui on the other side is hardly awe-inspiring, but unless they can shift one or the other, finding an upgrade might be a summer job.

At centre-back, though, they might not be able to afford to wait. Getting Harry Maguire back from injury will be a boost when the time comes, but with Lisandro Martinez suffering a sharp downturn in form, a brand new centre-back would help.

We reckon Nottingham Forest’s Murillo would be a pretty smart signing for United. He’s strong and imposing but also good on the ball, which is perfect for Pochettino.

Is he gettable in January, though? Probably not. He signed a new contract in the summer and we know what Forest are like when it comes to selling players. Or stopping them from being sold.

File this one under the dream signing category. Murillo has Premier League experience, plenty of good years still ahead of him and the attributes that Pochettino values highly in his defenders.

Look, we can safely say Pochettino would love to work with Harry Kane again. We know there have been numerous times when United would have loved to sign Harry Kane. It’s just not happening, though, is it?

Ink dry on his new Bayern Munich contract, Kane is off the menu. But a striker is still high on United’s wish list.

By January, Benjamin Sesko will be just about ready to return from injury, but who knows in what state. In his absence, United don’t have great alternatives. Joshua Zirkzee has had enough chances now and not taken them, while Matheus Cunha and Bryan Mbeumo are probably better out wide.

The summer transfer window is usually the more likely option for big striker moves. United could still do with someone to put them on, though.

The right kind of middle-ground between luxury signing and mere stop-gap could be someone like Borussia Dortmund’s Guirassy.

He’s the same height as Kane at 6’2 and boasts some similar strengths, such as his link-up play and chance conversion.

With eight goals from 14 games this season, including four from four in the Champions League, he’s in solid form. It’s no anomaly, looking at his last few seasons: 22 goals last time out, 38 the season before, 30 in the one before that.

At 30 years old, he’s not necessarily the long-term solution. But Kane is 33 and you’d sign him, wouldn’t you?

Guirassy might be one of the best options out there in terms of value for money.
