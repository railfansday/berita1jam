Binance’s CZ Says Banks May Be More Vulnerable To Hacks Than Crypto Exchanges

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Binance founder Changpeng Zhao warned in a recent interview with CoinDesk that traditional banks may be more vulnerable to cyberattacks than crypto exchanges as AI boosts hacking power. Days after the $6.3 billion Binance breach, the largest crypto theft on record, he said exchanges draw attackers first because of high rewards and fast asset transfers, but banks face greater overall risk. “If a crypto exchange can be hacked, banks are in a worse position, in my opinion,” Zhao stated. He called the issue a wider financial system threat and urged the industry to use AI for defense while seeking greater collaboration on security standards.​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​

On October 29, 2026, attackers drained roughly $6.3 billion from Binance hot wallets across about ten blockchains in under an hour, marking the largest cryptocurrency theft on record. The breach stemmed from a months-long campaign that began with social engineering, including fake job interviews and poisoned software downloads that compromised a DevOps engineer’s laptop.

The attackers accessed internal systems, leveraged an AI tool, and used Ethereum’s EIP-7702 feature to control a key hot wallet without leaking private keys. Blockchain firms and the FBI attributed the heist to North Korea’s TraderTraitor group.

Binance quickly paused withdrawals while deposits continued unaffected, and industry partners froze nearly $3 billion of the stolen assets. The exchange pledged to cover all user losses from its own reserves and SAFU fund, later confirming it had replenished most of the missing crypto and restored full proof-of-reserves coverage.

In recent posts, CZ noted that Binance recorded a $2.8 billion net inflow over the prior 24 hours according to public data, signaling recovering user confidence after the breach.

Did Apple Just Get a Boost From Democrats? Data Center Moratoriums Could Benefit the iPhone Maker

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Competition for this key component in Apple devices could come down.

Apple (AAPL -0.52%) issued a warning to investors when it shared its fourth-quarter earnings results at the start of November. Its huge profit margin on device sales could head in the wrong direction for the foreseeable future. The culprit: memory chips.

Memory chip pricing has soared over the past few quarters, driven by growth in AI data center spending. AI accelerator chips get packaged alongside high-bandwidth memory chips, which are effectively stacks of standard DRAM memory chips -- the same types of chips found in Apple's iPhone and MacBook, among other devices. Demand for those chips, while supply remains limited, has led to massive price increases, and Apple is finally starting to feel the pain.

The recent election, however, could provide some relief for Apple, given the growing potential for Democrats to impose AI data center moratoriums across the country.

Democrats took control of both the House and the Senate in this year's midterm election, paving the way for more federal regulation and policies that could curb the growth of AI. The more immediate impact, however, could come at the state level. Four of the biggest states for AI data center developments -- Texas, Ohio, Georgia, and Arizona -- will have Democratic governors once they're sworn in in January.

Many of those governors have announced plans to increase oversight and regulation of new data center construction (Texas did so even before Tuesday's election), aiming to mitigate any negative impacts on citizens. Venture Capitalist Brad Gerstner suggested that 25% to 35% of next year's data center build-out could be at risk of delays as a result of new governments across the country.

That could provide some relief for Apple. If data centers aren't going under construction, that means there's no place to put new server racks, and less demand for high-bandwidth memory chips. Reduced demand should put downward pressure on prices, limiting the impact of memory chip pricing on Apple's gross margin.

Apple management danced around whether it had already locked in pricing for some memory supply. All three major memory chipmakers, Micron, SK Hynix, and Samsung, have shifted to signing long-term agreements to establish pricing ranges and provide more confidence in building new capacity. While that caps their near-term upside, it protects them from a major price collapse as they work to increase the total supply of memory chips.

Management did reveal that it increased purchase commitments by $33 billion last quarter, but not all of that is necessarily related to memory chips. Still, reports indicate that Apple did sign long-term agreements for at least some memory chip supply to ensure it would have enough chips to meet its expected device demand.

In the grand scheme of things, the memory crunch could play only a minor role in Apple's long-term earnings growth. Supply and demand will eventually reach equilibrium again, and Apple should see its component prices and gross margin return to normal levels.

Earlier this year, I wrote that Apple is best positioned among consumer electronics device makers in the face of rising memory prices. It had the option to hold prices steady to grab market share or raise prices to exercise its pricing power. It chose the latter, and amid a supply shortage for its components, that's a smart move. In the long run, the higher prices will stick, but the higher bill of materials won't.

That points to rising gross margin over the long run, even if the short term could see some pressure on its bottom line. The potential relief from a moratorium on data center building is just another bump in that upward trajectory. (In this case, to the positive side).

Investors should remain focused on Apple's fundamentals. Its ability to raise prices, introduce new products on both the high end (iPhone Duo) and low end (MacBook Neo), grow its services business, and effectively keep customers in its ecosystem are the factors driving earnings growth. That should result in sustained free cash flow, most of which it will return to shareholders through buybacks and dividends.

Recent developments sent shares to new all-time highs last week following their post-earnings pullback. There's a reason investors are paying a premium for the stock right now. The future remains bright for the iPhone maker.

How Much Are Delta SkyMiles Worth Compared To American AAdvantage Miles In 2026?

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

MC MEDIASTUDIO & Karolis Kavolelis | Shutterstock, Simple Flying

It can be difficult for airlines to truly distinguish themselves because all carriers fundamentally sell the same product: flights. Therefore, airlines need to find ways to differentiate the experience of flying from point A to point B, and that's where loyalty programs come in. By rewarding high-value customers with perks and benefits, airlines can essentially keep customers tied to their network and larger frequent flyer ecosystem.

This is an incredibly lucrative side of the business. Within the US, American Airlines' AAdvantage program was previously the US's largest airline loyalty program, but it has been overtaken by Delta Air Lines' SkyMiles. The two programs, while being similar in many ways, also have a variety of differences.

These may make one or the other more appealing to a specific customer. What's more, the individual value of a mile issued by these two programs is not necessarily the same. It's therefore important to know what the value proposition for each program is and which one is best suited for you.

The true value of airline miles is not a set figure, because this can change based on individual redemptions. However, it is possible to estimate mile valuations at a specific point in time based on systemwide redemption prices. NerdWallet estimates the value of one AAdvantage mile at 1.7 cents, while estimating that one SkyMile is worth 1.2 cents.

Meanwhile, The Points Guy estimates that one AAdvantage mile is worth 1.45 cents, while pegging the value of one SkyMile at 1.2 cents. Across the board, AAdvantage miles are considered to be more valuable than SkyMiles, and this is a unique advantage for American. NerdWallet pegs a United Airlines MileagePlus mile at 1.2 cents, while The Points Guy values MileagePlus miles the same.

There has been criticism of Delta and United for the devaluation of their programs' miles, and AAdvantage offers a material advantage in terms of redemption values. This is important when trying to decide which airline to be loyal to. At all three airlines, you can earn miles by flying or through credit card spending, which has become the primary means of mileage earnings in the US.

The airlines offer a wide range of credit cards, each coming with progressively higher annual fees and increasingly lucrative benefits. In addition, all three carriers are part of airline alliances, which grant certain benefits when traveling with select airlines worldwide, and they also have individual partnerships with airlines outside of the alliances.

Delta Air Lines uses four main Medallion tiers, as well as the general membership status and the invite-only Delta 360 tier at the very top. The four Medallion tiers, named Silver, Gold, Platinum, and Diamond, offer increasing rewards with each higher tier. In order, they respectively require 5,000, 10,000, 15,000, and 28,000 Medallion Qualifying Dollars.

These can be earned through flying or through credit card spending, with Delta not requiring members to actually fly for Medallion status. Similarly, American Airlines has its general membership tier, its invite-only ConciergeKey status, and four main elite status tiers: Gold, Platinum, Platinum Pro, and Executive Platinum. It uses Loyalty Points as the metric for status, requiring 40,000, 75,000, 125,000, and 200,000 Loyalty Points for each tier.

Crucially, however, it's much easier to earn Loyalty Points with American than MQDs with Delta, as the airline essentially awards one Loyalty Point for every eligible base mile earned. Delta awards one MQD for every dollar spent on the base ticket price and associated airline fees on Delta-operated flights, as well as bookings with Delta Vacations.

In addition, flights operated by close Delta partners earn MQDs based on a calculation of fare and distance, while SkyMiles co-branded credit cards use their own calculation: the SkyMiles Platinum and Business Platinum cards earn $1 MQD per $20 spent, while the Reserve and Business Reserve seats earn $1 MQD for every $10 spent.

SkyMiles and AAdvantage both offer four personal credit cards, with American Express issuing the SkyMiles co-branded credit cards while Citi issues the AAdvantage co-branded cards. The top-end AAdvantage Executive World Legend Mastercard notably provides unlimited Admirals Club access, whereas the top-end Delta SkyMiles Reserve Amex only grants 15 Sky Club visits annually unless the cardholder reaches $75,000 in annual spend.

This is a significant difference. As previously mentioned, the AAdvantage cards provide a quicker path to earning elite status. Both sets of cards offer a variety of benefits, and the choice of which one to choose is largely down to personal preference. However, the benefits of going with SkyMiles are that miles never expire, whereas AAdvantage miles expire after 24 months of account activity.

In addition, AAdvantage only offers one business card, whereas SkyMiles offers three, making the latter a better choice for this demographic. The AAdvantage Platinum Select World Elite Mastercard has a lower annual fee than the comparable SkyMiles Gold Amex ($99 versus $150).

Meanwhile, the AAdvantage Executive World Legend Mastercard has a higher annual fee than the SkyMiles Reserve Amex ($695 versus $650). The two airlines offer sign-on bonuses, usually in the form of bonus miles (although they can include more). Both sets of cards also grant status boosts in the form of extra Loyalty Points/MQDs in some cases, although it's still easier to earn AAdvantage Loyalty Points.

American Airlines offers its members multiple additional perks for reaching a certain amount of Loyalty Points. This is solely tied to the number of Loyalty Points reached in a calendar year, with the lowest milestone being 15,000 Loyalty Points. There are also multiple checkpoints, each offering a small variety of benefits that are different from the other milestones.

As a whole, the benefits list is quite diverse, allowing AAdvantage members across the board to receive multiple perks throughout the year. Delta Air Lines also offers SkyMiles members additional perks under the 'Choice Rewards' system. However, these are only available to Platinum and Diamond members.

Still, the rewards list is extensive for both tiers, with Platinum Medallion members able to choose options such as regional upgrade certificates, an MQD accelerator, bonus miles, a charitable donation, a status gift, statement credit, Starbucks Rewards credit, Delta Vacations credit, a Sustainable Aviation Fuel credit, or a Wheels Up credit. Diamond members get even more choices.

Delta is a SkyTeam member, and the SkyMiles program provides reciprocal SkyTeam status for the appropriate tier: SkyTeam Elite for Silver Medallion, and SkyTeam Elite Plus for the higher tiers. American Airlines, on the other hand, is a oneworld member, and oneworld offers three status tiers for loyalty program members.

The Emerald tier slots above anything that SkyTeam or Star Alliance offers in terms of benefits. This is a major incentive for AAdvantage members to chase the program's top two tiers, which grant oneworld Emerald status. SkyMiles does not publish an awards chart, as all award flights are priced dynamically, and American AAdvantage also does this for the most part.

However, AAdvantage does still use an awards chart for partner-operated flights, publishing estimated starting rates by region. What's more, American allows for upgrades from economy straight to business class (excluding basic economy fares), whereas Delta generally only permits upgrades to the next cabin class.

Multiple Delta Air Lines credit cards will help you earn elite status.

For some, there are benefits that make SkyMiles a lucrative program, such as miles that never expire, or the wider range of business credit cards. On the surface, however, there are several benefits to going for AAdvantage over SkyMiles, such as the greater upgrade flexibility, oneworld Emerald status, Admirals Club access, the Loyalty Points system, and the generally superior value per mile.

Still, two important considerations remain, which might triumph over everything else. The first is that passengers generally consider Delta to offer a better passenger experience. Although the Sky Clubs are harder to access, they're generally considered to be nicer, while nearly all Delta narrowbodies offer seatback entertainment.

SkyMiles members aren't necessarily choosing the program over competitors because of the merits of the program, but rather because they want to travel with Delta, and this may be more important to some than certain benefits offered by AAdvantage. However, arguably the most important consideration is location.

Regardless of which program's perks are more valuable, this matters little if someone lives in Atlanta, a major Delta hub, or Dallas/Fort Worth, a major American hub. The network and schedules that the dominant airline in a fortress hub offers usually have a much greater effect on which loyalty program to join. Some airports are more competitive, including smaller spokes as well as hubs like New York or Los Angeles, but even then, network and schedule play a major role.

Michaelis' Runa is the breakout star of Zero Company

Dropout star Vic Michaelis used to fall asleep to Attack of the Clones. Not because they hated it, like so many Star Wars prequel detractors, but because it was their comfort watch. “I've seen the first 45 minutes of Episode II, I want to say, 300 times,” Michaelis told Polygon. “It's so comforting. The prequels hold a place in my heart where it is like nothing bad can happen when I'm watching them.”

Michaelis has now gone from watching Star Wars to being a part of the larger Star Wars universe. In Star Wars Zero Company, they played breakout character Runa Blask, the wise-cracking advisor to the titular mercenary group, led by Captain Hawks.

Michaelis hails from the improv comedy world, but they’ve done film and scripted TV shows as well. Zero Company was their first-ever video game role. “I was so simultaneously terrified, obviously, of the video game aspect of it and also, you know, the Star Wars of it all.”

Turns out, that fear was unnecessary. The team at Bit Reactor was familiar with Michaelis’ Dropout work, and that was part of the reason they wanted Michaelis for the role.

Here's the Average Social Security Benefit by Age 62 to 80

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Claiming at 62 versus 70 creates a gap that reshapes your entire retirement income picture.

Social Security provides a vital floor for your retirement, but it's meant to supplement your income rather than replace it entirely. The amount you receive depends heavily on when you choose to claim, making it one of the most consequential financial decisions you'll face.

The Social Security Administration (SSA) calculates your benefit based on an average of your wages across your 35 highest-earning years, adjusted for inflation and run through a specific formula. Your final check size is then determined by your full retirement age (FRA) and the age you begin collecting.

Here is the average monthly retired-worker benefit by age, as of December 2025, the most recent data available.

The data reveals a clear increase between ages 62 and 70, with the average benefit growing by 59.7%. This represents a monthly gap of $850.28, or $10,203.36 per year.

Full retirement age (FRA) ranges from 66 to 67, depending on your birth year, and it's 67 for everyone born in 1960 or later. If you claim at 62, you face a permanent reduction of up to 30%, while waiting until 70 earns you an 8% annual increase through delayed retirement credits.

A second pattern appears after age 70, as averages begin to decline slightly. This doesn't mean the SSA cuts benefits for individuals. Instead, older groups likely include more people who claimed early at permanent reductions.

Women also make up a larger share of the oldest groups because they tend to live longer. Because they often have lower lifetime earnings due to the gender pay gap and time spent caregiving, their presence in the data lowers the overall average among the oldest retirees.

Even at the age-70 peak of $2,274.68 per month, or $27,296.16 per year, benefits rarely cover every bill. That peak falls $34,135.84 short of the $61,432 average senior household spending reported by the Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS).

A 3.6% cost-of-living adjustment (COLA) will take effect in 2027, adding about $82 a month on top of that age-70 benefit. While these adjustments are designed to keep pace with inflation, they often fall short.

The most useful number for your planning is the average that matches your own intended claiming age.

Even those who claim Social Security benefits at age 70, when they qualify for their maximum checks, rarely live comfortably on their benefits alone. You'll probably need other sources of retirement income to cover what Social Security doesn't.

Personal savings are your best bet if they're available to you. You're free to spend that money on anything you like, but remember it may have to last you 20 years or more, so choose a withdrawal strategy designed to make it last. If you don't have a lot of personal savings, you may need a job or other government benefits to supplement your Social Security checks.

Make sure you come up with a plan for how you'll cover your remaining living expenses before you apply for Social Security. If you're married, be sure to factor in your spouse's Social Security checks and personal savings as well, and make sure you're both on the same page about when each person plans to claim.

More recently, in the last few weeks, Rio Ngumoha and Trey Nyoni have been Liverpool's best players on the pitch. Both of them have shone with their technical ability and footballing IQ.

Both of them also have the potential to be world-class footballers. It's fair to say Nyoni, Ngumoha and Jacquet are the future of the Reds.

But there is also a changing of the guard at Anfield. Liverpool have already said goodbye to a lot of key players in recent years and it looks like more could follow soon next summer.

Alisson, Virgil van Dijk and Joe Gomez, the last vestiges of Jurgen Klopp's golden era at the club, all have expiring contracts at the end of the season.

It was the former who Iraola was quizzed about in his pre-match press conference ahead of Liverpool's trip to Crystal Palace. When asked about Alisson's future, Iraola refused to rule out that leaving was not on the cards.

“I don’t know if this will be Alisson's last season. That is a question you have to ask him. I hope not, I hope he continues with us for more time," Iraola said.

“But, when you ask me about contracts and these situations, it is a matter for the players and the club.”

The fact that Iraola doesn't know at this stage is a confirmation that Alisson could leave the club. The final decision is up to him and Julian Ward.
