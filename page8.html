Illustrations of Moral Dissonance

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Posted November 25, 2026 | Reviewed by Lybi Ma

The Trojan War was in its tenth year. A soothsayer had foretold the Greeks that they would win the war only by retrieving the famed archer, Philoctetes, whose bow was from Heracles and whose arrows never missed their mark. The major problem, though, was that Odysseus and his men had abandoned Philoctetes on their way to Troy those many years earlier. On a stop before they reached Troy, Philoctetes had inadvertently wandered into a sacred area and been bitten by a snake. He developed a wound so suppurative and foul-smelling and cried out in such agony that no one could stand to be with him, and Odysseus ordered his shipmates to drop him off on the deserted island of Lemnos (Sophocles; Heaney, 1961).

For all that time, Philoctetes, miserable and alone, suffered with a virulent, gangrenous, and oozing wound that did not heal, "an insatiable sickness" (Sophocles). All the while, he nursed a grudge against Odysseus and his men, and he was obsessed with the betrayal (Lansky, 2003; Wilson, 2018).

There are passing references to this myth in both Homer's Iliad and Odyssey, as well as in Ovid's Metamorphoses and Virgil's Aeneid, but the main extant source is Sophocles’ play, Philoctetes. The story has also been immortalized in many works of art.

Odysseus — the clever manipulator and far more devious than depicted in Christopher Nolan's recent film, The Odyssey — devised an elaborate plan: He and some of his men would land on Lemnos with Neoptolemus, the young son of Achilles, and trick Philoctetes into coming, with his magic bow, back with them to Troy.

Commanded by Odysseus and threatened with punishment for treason if he would not comply, Neoptolemus was to befriend Philoctetes and explain that he, too, had a grievance against Odysseus. He had reason, therefore, to abandon the Greeks and take Philoctetes home, all the while plotting to bring him to Troy.

But Neoptolemus was a young man of character: "It is not in my nature to practice treachery...Don't you think it shameful to lie?" he asks. "It goes against all I was ever brought up to believe," he adds. (Heaney). "Not if the lie brings deliverance...Troy can be taken only with his bow," responds Odysseus.

Neoptolemus ultimately agrees to accept his commander's duplicitous plan that includes the possibility of stealing the bow and leaving Philoctetes to starve.

Seeing the extraordinary anguish of Philoctetes moves Neoptolemus, as he says, "My heart is bursting with grief at your pain." With renewed courage and honor, he refuses to acquiesce to Odysseus' deception (Segal, 1995). "What is disgusting is when a man abandons his own true nature and acts shamefully," says Neoptolemus.

Though Neoptolemus confesses his real intentions to Philoctetes, it will take a deus ex machina in the form of the appearance of Heracles to convince Philoctetes that his destiny is to accompany Odysseus, Neoptolemus, and the men to Troy. Philoctetes, as foretold, will have his wound healed and will be "hailed as the bravest of all the Greeks" with the fall of Troy: Philoctetes will kill Paris, and Neoptolemus will kill King Priam (Wilson).

Sophocles’ play depicts conflicts between the individual and society (Segal, 1995) and poignantly illustrates the effects of chronic, unremitting disease for which someone is ostracized and abandoned. Most significantly, it reflects the high moral cost of compromising one's integrity (Rangell, 1974), as Neoptolemus had, and experiencing moral dissonance (ter Heide and Olff, 2023; VanderWeele et al, 2025), or moral injury (Shay, 1991; 1994; 2002; 2011; Griffin et al, 2019; Litz, 2025; Litz and Walker, 2025).

Psychoanalytically, this compromise of integrity results from a conflict between the ego, the rational part of the brain, and the superego, one's conscience (Rangell, 1974).

Jonathan Shay is credited with introducing the term moral injury in the context of the military. It is a betrayal of what’s right and a “shrinkage of the moral and social horizon.” Moral hypocrisy occurs when immoral actions occur in otherwise moral people (Naso, 2006).

Shay compared Homeric soldiers with American soldiers: Homeric soldiers respected, even honored their enemy, without derogatory name-calling or dehumanizing them, as was commonly encouraged by some in the American armed forces.

Shay thought of moral injury as psychological wound contamination, a kind of leadership malpractice. There are three parts to his definition: there is a betrayal of what’s right, a high-stakes situation, and someone who holds legitimate authority over a person (2011). Moral distress results from "culturally imbued shared and internalized values" (Griffin et al.).

Others have extended the use of the term to situations beyond the military (Litz, 2025), though there is no consensus definition or gold standard of measurement (Litz and Walker, 2025). Further, research suffers from small sample sizes and even disagreement about what is a potentially morally injurious event (Griffin et al., 2019).

“The human story is replete with individuals, groups, and nations wittingly and unwittingly perpetrating cruel and pernicious acts of inhumanity against others." Psychological consequences ensue. “People can be consumed and haunted by these inhumane acts” (Litz and Walker). Moral injury now includes acts of commission or omission, being the victim of, or bearing witness to acts “that transgress deeply held moral beliefs and expectations” (Litz and Walker).

Situations that can evoke moral injury include the My Lai Massacre in March 1968, where American soldiers slaughtered at least 300 unarmed Vietnamese civilians (Borch, 2018).

The torture and abuse of prisoners by Americans at Abu Ghraib in Iraq in 2003 is another example.

Though the effects of moral injury can overlap with PTSD, the two are distinct. PTSD is often fear-based and involves flashbacks, exaggerated startle reflex, memory loss, nightmares, and insomnia (Griffin et al.).

The syndrome of moral injury, in distinction, does not require a life-threatening event (Litz, 2024). It is "persistent moral distress" (VanderWeel et al.), more likely involving feelings of guilt, shame, anger, anhedonia, social isolation (Griffin et al.), loss of trust in others or institutions, failure to forgive oneself or others, and loss of meaning in life (VanderWeel et al.).

The damage from moral injury can be irreparable and exceptionally difficult to treat. Cognitive-behavioral coping approaches, such as habituation and reappraisal, often fail to relieve the distress (Griffin et al.).

How To Complete The Final Flush In GTA 6

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

You can get a free stamina upgrade in GTA 6 by helping out a conspiracy theorist, but there's a dark easter egg to uncover after you're done, too.

I'm convinced that Lucia is a clone in Grand Theft Auto 6 — at least, in my playthrough. If you happen on a little side quest called The Final Flush, where you help a conspiracy theorist uncover the secret machinations of Big Sewage, things get a little weird. Weird enough that yes, I really am convinced Lucia is a clone. Just don't let the FIB hear me posting about that on Snapmatic, yeah?

In all seriousness, this is one of those oddities in GTA 6 where you don't really understand what's happening, things veer a little X-Files, and then you're handed a permanent stamina upgrade for your troubles as though you haven't been wading through literal crap.

Like all good encounters, you start The Final Flush by scanning a QR code on a handwritten poster by a toilet that screams "STOP! DON'T FLUSH." I also found the same poster in the storage compartment of a pawn shop, but if you want a reliable way to begin the mission instead of going on a Vice City crime spree until you happen across one, head to the north of Corsair Key in Mariana County and enter the only building on the entire island - the toilet.

Use the WaiNK IT app to scan the QR code (#NOFLUSHERS) and you'll get a message asking you to reply "TRUTH" to join the movement. If you decide to embrace your inner tinfoil hat, you'll get a follow-up message telling you to meet the sender at Frank's Waffles in Vice City, whereupon arriving you'll need to repeat the code: 00-00-02.

Drive to Frank's Waffles, and you'll see an older man muttering to himself at a booth by the toilets. This is the colorful Dr. Merle, a conspiracy theorist with an ingenious plan: swallow a tracker, poop it out, and follow it to the source.

Of course, as Lucia rightfully points out (you can also do this mission with Jason), he could've just thrown the tracker in the toilet and saved himself the trouble. But he waffles about how Node is too smart for that, and that he needs to be stealthier.

If you return to Frank's Waffles and check the toilet later, well, I decided not to include the screenshot.

The point is, supposedly, Big Sewage is harvesting Leonidan DNA through their poop to make clones of its citizens, and Dr. Merle wants to get to the bottom of it.

Immediately after depositing the tracker, Dr. Merle will ask you to drive his van as he yells directions at you. You can take a guess at where the tracker in the sewage pipes leads — the sewage plant.

Fast and Furious fans have an extra special easter egg to find in GTA 6: Paul Walker's Mitsubishi Eclipse from the first film.

Just make sure you have the compass enabled, as you won't get a trail on your mini-map to follow, only sporadic directions from Dr. Merle as he tells you to drive east, north, and in whatever direction the pipes are leading.

Eventually, you will get close enough that a waypoint appears on the beach. Park the van on the coast and follow Dr. Merle on foot along the fence, where he'll start lockpicking a gate to get you inside.

Dr. Merle will ask you to keep watch while he very slowly works a lock that Lucia and Jason could pick in seconds. You can't really do much though, because it's a chain-link fence that security can easily see through.

So, when a van pulls up asking what exactly you're doing, select 'Defuse' to tell the worker you're fixing the gate. They won't believe you, but it will buy you enough time to get inside and start sneaking about.

Once inside, head to the pink area marked on your map. You can either talk to the workers and lie that you're electricians, or sneak past them through the double doors into the adjacent building (like with all interiors, it's marked by a darker shade of blue).

Here, Dr. Merle will break down and ramble about how close he got to finding out the truth, only to be thwarted by concrete, as the tracker is right below your feet. This is where things take a turn. His poop-laden tracker isn't just lurking in underground pipes, there's a secret facility.

Head to the balcony overseeing the room where there's a control panel with three buttons. Turn all the lights on the panel green (press the bottom two) and then interact with the main lever. Right where Dr. Merle is sitting, a secret staircase will be revealed leading to a strange chamber.

Naturally, like with any conspiracy theorist worth his salt, Dr. Merle puts his idea to the test and climbs into the chamber. It quickly fills with a strange, viscous green liquid, and he asks you — Jason or Lucia — to press the button on the control panel and quite literally flush him. Do it.

One of GTA 6's best easter eggs comes with some very unique accessories. Here's how to get them.

The room blares with the sound of the agitated water authority warning you that the police are en route and that the chamber is toxic and should not be entered. But if you want a permanent stamina upgrade, I'm afraid you have to get inside and follow Dr. Merle's example.

You'll enter first-person while it fills with that strange green liquid again, and bizarrely, you wake up outside your safehouse, unclear how you got there, stamina upgrade in hand.

Okay, I'm not only convinced that my Lucia is a clone. I have proof. And this is where the guide takes a turn. After completing The Big Flush, you can find a disturbing little easter egg revealing what exactly happened in the chamber.

Head north to Lummox County and travel to the eastern island. Make sure you don't stumble onto Hyde Island, or you'll end up being hunted for sport.

Near the tip, there's a sealed bunker. Enter the code '3612' to open the door, and head on inside. There's no reward here, just a disturbing revelation that will change how you see the rest of the game.

Through the bunker and down a ladder, you'll see three corpses — so long as you've done the mission first: Dr. Merle, whoever you put in the chamber, and an unknown bald man (I checked his face, it isn't Jason).

Naturally, as you can see in the video embedded above, I brought Jason along so he could find out that the Lucia at home is really a clone, which makes every single text and interaction feel just a little bit off. But hey, a free stamina upgrade is a free stamina upgrade.

Or... maybe these are discarded clones. Who's to say?

Ice hockey prospect Max Calce follows Leon Draisaitl's path, leaving Mannheim for Canada

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The player widely seen as Germany's biggest ice hockey talent to come along in years is leaving the country's top professional league for Canada – changing tracks in his bid to make it to the NHL.

Max Calce made the surprise announcement that he would be moving to the Brantford Bulldogs of the Ontario Hockey League (OHL) in a statement issued on the website of his German Ice Hockey League (DEL) team, Adler Mannheim late on Tuesday.

"I have long had three goals," he said. "To play for the Adler, in the Canadian Hockey League (CHL), and in the NHL. Today is a difficult and emotional day for me, as I say goodbye to my home."

Having started the season in Mannheim, many thought the 17-year-old would finish the season in the DEL.

Wild baboons seem to plan breakfast around a favorite fruit

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Travel  |  Read Source →

Wild baboons stopped visiting their favorite fruit trees after a fire destroyed the crop, suggesting flexible food planning.

Wild Guinea baboons in West Africa travel to the same area each year to feed on a favored fruit. In 2019, this annual pattern was disrupted by a fire that swept through the feeding grounds.

The fire reached the area just as the fruit was beginning to ripen on the trees. Only a single visit to the fruit trees was recorded during February of that year.

The baboons resumed their usual visits the following year, when ripe Natal oranges were available again.

These observations suggest that the animals do more than simply recall where and when food can be found. Their foraging plans also appear to be revised when a familiar food source is lost.

The study was led by Lisa Ohrndorf of the German Primate Center in Göttingen, Germany.

“The year of the fire gave us a unique opportunity: We were able to observe how the animals react when a normally reliable food source suddenly becomes unavailable during their usual season,” said Ohrndorf.

Her co-authors were Roger Mundry, Dietmar Zinner and Julia Fischer. All three are affiliated with the center and with the University of Göttingen.

The baboons inhabit Niokolo-Koba National Park in southeastern Senegal, where several groups have been followed by researchers since 2007.

Earlier work carried out at the same center had shown that these baboons share meat with close companions.

A clear preference for the Natal orange is shown by the animals during the dry season. The fruit is roughly the size of a common orange and is protected by a hard outer shell.

Inside the shell the seeds are embedded in a layer of soft pulp that is eaten by the baboons.

Natal oranges ripen between January and March and reach their greatest abundance in February. Data from recent years indicate that nearly a fifth of February feeding time was devoted to this fruit.

Very few other plants in the park are known to produce fleshy fruit during the dry season. The long journey to the Natal orange trees is therefore likely to be well rewarded.

Most of the Natal orange trees are found in the northern parts of the home ranges of the baboons.

These trees cannot be seen from the riverside forest in which the animals usually spend the night.

In 2023 a series of evenly spaced survey routes was walked by the researchers through the area. A total of 5,847 Natal orange trees was counted along these routes.

The densest stand found in this survey was then used as the target area for the analysis of baboon movements.

Fires are a regular feature of the park and are recorded in every dry season. They normally burn between mid-December and early January and mainly consume the tall grass left after the rains.

The fires of 2019 were unusual because they arrived much later than in most years.

Satellite records indicate that most of the target area burned within a few days at the end of January. By that point the Natal oranges had already begun to ripen.

Nearly all of the ripe fruit was destroyed by the heat of the fire. The trees themselves suffered little lasting damage and plant growth in the following rainy season was close to normal.

Nine years of observation records for seven baboon groups were examined by the team. Each group contained an average of 29 animals.

The baboons were observed on 22 days in February 2019 but entered the target area on only one of them. In other years between eight and 29 visits were recorded in February with an average of about 16.

During the fire year the baboons were seen feeding on the fruit of fan palms. The fruit of a shrubby tree growing in other parts of their range was also eaten.

The baboons visited the area four times in March 2019, at the low end of the range of four to 22 visits recorded in other years.

The baboons may have returned in March to feed on fruit from taller trees, where it hangs higher and is less exposed to the heat of a fire.

By 2020 the Natal oranges had recovered and the usual pattern of visits to the area had resumed.

The researchers also analyzed 2,922 GPS tracks that had been collected over a period of nine years.

Of those tracks, 530 showed a group entering the area where the fruit trees grow.

When Natal oranges were in season the baboons were found to follow more direct routes to the trees. Their average travel speed on these trips was also about 14% higher than at other times of year.

The distance involved in these trips was considerable. From the known sleeping sites the trees lay roughly 1.4 miles (2.2 kilometers) away in a straight line.

Baboons are generally slow to leave their sleeping trees in the morning. More than an hour commonly passes between sunrise and their departure.

On trips to the feeding area, the baboons left their sleeping sites about 40 minutes earlier when the fruit was in season.

Cold weather normally delays departure because the animals tend to sun themselves before setting off.

Mornings in the fruit season were in fact colder on average. Since the baboons still left earlier, warmer temperatures cannot account for the change.

In studies of animal learning a distinction is drawn between habitual behavior and behavior driven by an expected reward. A habit may persist for some time after the associated reward is no longer available.

The direct and rapid trips to the trees could in principle have reflected nothing more than a fixed yearly routine. The response of the baboons to the fire argues against that interpretation.

If the visits had been governed by habit alone the empty trees would probably have continued to be visited. The baboons instead shifted their foraging to food sources located in other parts of their range.

The results suggest that the value of a journey is assessed by these baboons before the journey is undertaken.

Comparable behavior has been described in several other primates. Chacma baboons have been observed leaving their sleeping sites early in order to reach fig trees. Female chimpanzees leave their nests earlier when a preferred fruit is located far away.

Memory for food locations is not restricted to primates. Small birds such as chickadees are able to recall where large numbers of their food stores were hidden.

Responses to fire are also known to vary widely among animals. Some deer return to burned ground within days while certain lizards detect smoke and move away before the flames arrive.

The late fire occurred only once during the study period. Visit numbers could therefore be compared across years but full statistical testing of the fire year was not possible.

GPS positions were recorded at 30-minute intervals during follows that usually ended around 1 p.m. Some movement was missed as a result and the actual routes were probably longer and less direct.

The means by which the baboons navigate through their home range also remains unclear. Repeated use of the same trails and roads and stream crossings points to a reliance on learned routes.

A further open question concerns the way in which the baboons recognize that the Natal oranges are ready to eat.

Day length and temperature or the appearance of other seasonal foods may serve as cues for the animals. These possibilities were not tested in the present study.

The study offers a closer look at the way wild baboons make decisions about where to search for food.

Knowledge of where food grows is clearly useful but knowing when to stop searching may be equally important.

The full study was published in the journal Animal Cognition.

Rolling on a then-new RWD platform developed with oversight by Ferrari engineer Philippe Krief, the Giulia has the goods to compete with the likes of BMW's 3 Series and Audi A4 when the roads get bendy. It's a testament to how good the basic chassis is that it can perform competently with this base four-cylinder model and, with notable tweaks, also with the monster 500-horsepower V6 Quadrifoglio.

Our car has the 1,995cc DOHC MultiAir four under the hood. With variable valve timing and an intercooled turbocharger, that makes an astounding 280 hp and 306 pound-feet of torque. Matched to that engine is a ZF 8HP50 8-speed automatic transmission that powers the rear wheels. While AWD was offered on this model, this car's RWD layout is simpler to maintain and lighter, offering better performance and fuel economy.

According to the ad, this Giulia has 75,000 miles on the clock and, remarkable for any Alfa, especially a first-year model that may still be suffering teething pains, it is claimed to suffer no issues whatsoever. Did I mention it's also really good-looking?

Having established that this is a very attractive car, it's also in very good shape. The pictures in the ad support the seller's claim that it's "well kept," as the Silverstone Gray Metallic paint appears to have no dents, and the darker gray factory alloys show no evidence of curb rash.

This being an entry-level model and with the ad's limited description, the seating surfaces are likely vinyl rather than leather. Some wood on the console warms up the space in an otherwise Germanic, all-black (even the Alfa badge) interior.

That's not to say that the car is parsimoniously equipped, as it has all the basic comfort and convenience features one might expect on a reasonably modern car. Those include power windows, locks, seats and mirrors, dual-zone climate control with eyeball vents for the back seats, and a center stack screen for infotainment and the backup camera display. There's also cruise control, auto headlamps... You name it, it's likely got it.

Per the seller, the car presents in excellent condition, has a clean title, and drives perfectly. What more could one want?

Well, I'm sure one thing we could all ask for is a car that's free and can navigate to a leprechaun's pot of gold when asked. There are certain things we can't have, no matter how much we want them.

This car isn't free, but its $7,999 asking price is a recent reduction from $8,500, and it makes one wonder: just how scared are people about buying a decade-old, out-of-warranty Alfa that even that slightly higher price seemingly couldn't get a nibble?
