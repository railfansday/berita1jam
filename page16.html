How far can brokerages go when clawing back commissions from departing agents?

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The Q2 2026 Agent Migration Report found external brokerage switches essentially unchanged year over year. In Massachusetts, Lamacchia Realty alleges Chinatti Realty imposed 75%-85% referral fees tied to database leads, and the court cited missing CRM records during discovery.  AI Summary

The number of real estate agents moving from one brokerage brand to another essentially remained flat year-over-year during the second quarter of 2026, according to the Q2 2026 Agent Migration Report, published by Recruiting Insight and Lone Wolf Technologies in late July.

Of the 113,372 records and four MLS corridors the report analyzed, just 3,390 agents made an external move during the quarter, a difference of just six moves from Q2 2025.

At the same time, the report found that name-brand internal transfers grew from 526 in Q4 2024 to 800 in Q2 2026, a 52% increase over 18 months.

While agents may choose to change firms for access to better technology and educational opportunities or to be working in a company culture that better aligns with their values, these moves do not happen in a vacuum and can have real financial consequences for agents.

Some firms have been known to employ “clawbacks,” where the brokerage firm may withhold a percentage of pending commissions on listings nearing contract, charge transfer or cancellation fees if clients follow the agent, or “claw back” signing bonuses or incentives the agents have received upon changing firms.

A lawsuit in Massachusetts, however, claims that one brokerage has taken things a step further, charging referral fees as high as 85% when an agent who left the company closed a transaction with a lead that exists in their former brokerage’s database.

In a suit originally filed in August 2024 and amended in late February 2025, Lamacchia Realty, a RealTrends Verified brokerage, eight of its agents and its former sales manager Jason Posnick, claim that Chinatti Realty Group and its broker-owner Lisa Chinatti has been unjustly enriched through her referral fee practices.

The lawsuit, first reported by Inman, is pending in Massachusetts Superior Court for Middlesex County. It claims that the agent plaintiffs’ independent contractor agreements were unconscionable because they imposed referral fees as high as 85% when they closed transactions with leads in the Chinatti Realty Group’s database after joining Lamacchia Realty.

The agent plaintiffs allege that this allowed Chinatti and her firm to continually make claims for referral fees on many of the agents’ sales since they have left the company and that this has prevented them from earning a living in real estate. Lamacchia Realty claims that, to date, it has paid Chinatti Realty Group in excess of $350,000 in 75% to 85% referral fees on behalf of former Chinatti agents.

In addition to this, the agents claim that they were not paid anything on transactions that were pending when they transitioned from Chinatti to Lamacchia. Lamacchia Realty has said it has paid agents $113,443 to make them whole on commissions not paid to them from Chinatti for pending transactions that closed after they departed Chinatti Realty Group.

The agents also claim Chinatti and her firm misclassified them as independent contractors despite exercising controls beyond the normal supervisory obligations of a broker.

Lamacchia Realty first contacted Chinatti and her firm regarding these alleged issues in November of 2022 via letter.

This past summer, during the discovery process, the court and Judge Lynn Rooney, who is overseeing the suit, said they learned that Chinatti Realty Group had swapped consumer relationship management software providers from Real Geeks to Zillow’s Follow Up Boss in December 2024. To move the data over, Chinatti said Zillow paid a vendor to handle the migration, but that the vendor found certain records were unable to be transferred.

According to a mid-September ruling from the court, Chinatti knew the allegedly lost records were relevant to the dispute between the firms thanks to the November 2022 letter she and her firm received from Lamacchia.

“I find that Lisa Chinatti knew when she received the demand letter in November 2022 that the information contained in Real Geeks was highly relevant to the plaintiffs’ causes of action. I find that Lisa Chinatti did nothing to preserve that documentation and instead, deliberately and intentionally caused the Real Geeks CRM to be destroyed,” Judge Rooney wrote in her ruling. “I find that the Plaintiffs have demonstrated that they have suffered unfair prejudice as a result of Ms. Chinatti’s actions. Plaintiffs likely would have retained the services of a forensic expert to review the data contained in Real Geeks. As stated at the hearing, the Real Geeks data would have included information concerning the daily work and activity of each of the plaintiff agents, statements by Ms. Chinatti, and the nature of the interactions/communications between Ms. Chinatti and the plaintiff agents.”

If the lawsuit proceeds to trial, the judge has said that at a minimum, the plaintiffs were entitled to “very clear and forceful instruction [to the jury] that the evidence, had it been available, would have been unfavorable to the Defendants.”

In addition to this, the court said it discovered that agents’ CRM activity and phone conversations were monitored and recorded, without the permission of call participants. The judge said she had forwarded the ruling and these concerns to the Massachusetts Attorney General’s Criminal Bureau for review of potential wiretap violations.

Earlier this month, the plaintiffs filed a motion for the denial of the summary judgement motions the defendants have filed since the start of the year. They argue that the unresolved factual disputes that have surfaced during discovery warrant a trial and have requested that the court schedule a final pretrial conference.

The court has yet to rule on this motion or Chinatti’s motions for summary judgement.

Chinatti did not respond to HousingWire’s request for comment regarding the allegations in the lawsuit nor the court’s ruling.

Genie 4 adds control during a session. Users can type prompts while navigating to alter weather, add a character or object, move the camera or redirect events without starting over. The model also generates native audio with the visuals, including ambient sound, music and character dialogue that reacts to user inputs and events in the simulation.

Google DeepMind describes Genie 4 as natively multimodal, with Gemini Omni providing a real-world reference for how simulations look and behave and other Gemini models supplying reasoning for coherence and continuity. Gemini 3.8 Flash can serve as an in-loop guiding agent: it observes the scene, splits goals into steps, checks incoming frames for progress and changes its plan when the world or user input shifts.

Google DeepMind presents this coupling as a way to model multi-step events and test how AI systems navigate environments before real-world deployment. Intended uses include training environments for robots and agents as well as new interactive experiences.

The Project Genie agent handles initial creation through natural-language conversation. It coordinates Google’s multimodal models to produce the world’s opening visuals and a custom background score suited to the intended mood. During exploration, Gemini can watch the screen, suggest context-aware next actions through on-screen chips and trigger events automatically.

The gallery adds a publishing and reuse layer. Users can alter an existing world’s structure, soundtrack or rules with the agent, then publish their version for others to explore and remix. Examples range from Living Memory, which converts personal photographs into explorable spaces, to The Life of Ants, created with filmmaker Anand Gandhi, which presents a colony from an ant’s perspective.

Google positions Genie 4 as its higher-fidelity option for visuals, physics and native audio, while Genie 3 remains selectable for longer runs or worlds seeded from 360-degree panorama images. Genie 3’s per-generation limit is now three minutes, up from 60 seconds, and users can link multiple worlds into longer sequences or trigger real-time events with natural-language prompts.

All video and audio generated in Project Genie include SynthID, Google’s imperceptible watermark for identifying AI-generated content, and all model inputs and outputs undergo safety screenings.

Google says both Genie models remain frontier research systems and identifies camera latency, intricate physical interactions, real-time audio precision and long-term scene permanence as unresolved challenges. Visual details and object permanence in Genie 4 can drift during longer sessions, after complex edits or while navigating; legible text and spatial accuracy also remain active research areas.

The launch expands who can build with Genie 4, but the durability of its simulated worlds remains the key constraint on longer creative sessions and on Google DeepMind’s proposed training environments for robots and agents.

The mortgage industry was built to help Americans buy homes. An aging America needs more from it.

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Survey of 2,000 homeowners 55 plus shows 88% prioritize staying home and 90% prioritize independence

For decades, the mortgage industry has helped Americans achieve one of their biggest financial goals: buying a home and building equity over time.

It’s a model we know well. Homeowners purchase properties, make mortgage payments, refinance when it makes financial sense and gradually accumulate wealth. Much of the mortgage industry is structured around supporting that journey.

But what happens after decades of building that wealth?

As homeowners approach retirement, the financial equation shifts. Instead of focusing primarily on acquiring a home and paying down debt, the question becomes how to use the wealth they’ve accumulated to support the years ahead. Housing presents a unique opportunity but also a challenge because, for many Americans, their largest financial asset is also the place they call home.

You can sell a stock without moving. Selling a home is another matter. Trading down and moving to a less expensive home to tap home equity is both expensive and difficult. This might be why our recent research shows that the desire to remain at home continues to grow stronger with age.

In a recent Longbridge Financial, LLC (NMLS #957935) study of more than 2,000 U.S. homeowners ages 55 and older, 90% said maintaining their independence was extremely or very important to their ideal retirement. Nearly as many (88%) said the same about remaining in their homes. Both priorities ranked above paying for health care, paying off debt, long-term care and providing financial support to family members.

The desire to age in place isn’t surprising. What’s particularly revealing is how strongly that sentiment differs across age groups.

Among homeowners ages 55 to 64, 85% rated remaining in their homes as extremely or very important. That figure rose to 88% among those ages 65 to 74 and 93% among homeowners 75 and older. The importance of maintaining independence followed a similar pattern, climbing from 87% in the youngest group to 96% in the oldest.

These are differences between age groups at one point in time, rather than a measure of how individual preferences change over time. Still, the pattern points to an important finding in understanding how homeowners think about retirement.

Over decades, homeowners accumulate substantial equity through mortgage payments and increases in property values. Yet the very home that holds the wealth is also where many want to spend their later years. Its financial value tells only part of the story. Giving a homeowner a choice to move to a less expensive, and thus less attractive, home to use their home equity may not be much of a choice at all.

Consider two retired households with the same net worth. One holds most of its wealth in savings and investments. The other holds much of it in home equity.

Their balance sheets might look similar, but their financial flexibility is different. The first household may be able to draw from liquid accounts to cover an unexpected expense. The second has wealth, too, but accessing it may involve borrowing against the home or selling it altogether. Both options come with trade-offs, particularly when remaining in the home is a priority.

For an industry accustomed to measuring property values, loan balances and equity, this is an important distinction. These figures describe a homeowner’s financial position, but not necessarily how easily they can access that wealth — or what they might have to give up in the process.

The same survey reinforces why this distinction matters. Among homeowners ages 55 to 64, 73% rated paying for health care as extremely or very important to their ideal retirement. That figure climbed to 85% among those 75 and older. While these findings don’t tell us how much homeowners will spend or whether they’ll tap into their home equity, they do highlight how financial priorities can shift across different stages of life.

Mortgage finance has traditionally centered on transactions: purchasing, refinancing, servicing and eventually paying off a loan. Those functions remain essential. But paying off a mortgage doesn’t end a homeowner’s financial relationship with their home.

For some older homeowners, selling or downsizing may be the right choice. Others may have sufficient income and savings to remain in place. Still others may consider home equity loans, lines of credit or reverse mortgages, depending on their financial circumstances, eligibility and the associated costs and risks.

The opportunity for our industry extends beyond any single product. It’s about understanding the role home equity can play in supporting homeowners through retirement, while recognizing the personal value they place on remaining in their homes.

It also requires recognizing that homeowners’ priorities differ. In our study, 93% of women rated independence as extremely or very important, compared with 88% of men. Similar five-point gaps appeared for remaining at home and paying for health care. While the research doesn’t explain why these differences exist, it underscores the importance of understanding homeowners as individuals rather than treating homeowners 55 and over as a single market with identical needs and priorities.

Housing has always been both an investment and a place to live. As homeowners age, the balance between those roles deserves greater attention from the industry that helped them achieve homeownership in the first place.

We’ve spent decades helping Americans build wealth through homeownership. The next opportunity is helping them understand how that wealth can support the years ahead, without losing sight of what they value most: independence, financial flexibility and, for many, the ability to remain at home.

Lowe's posts strong online sales, raises outlook despite slow housing market

📅 2026-11-18  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Lowe's on Wednesday posted strong quarterly results and raised its full-year outlook as the company said it saw strength among professional shoppers.

The company said comparable sales grew 1.8% for the fiscal third quarter, driven by a 17.4% increase in online sales and "solid" performance in pro and home services sales. Net sales rose to $22.76 billion.

Because of the strength, Lowe's now said it expects full-year sales of roughly $92.5 billion, up from a previous projection of $92 billion. It also expects comparable sales to be up 0.5%, compared with previous guidance of flat.

CEO Marvin Ellison told CNBC that the company is focused on growing despite economic headwinds and a sluggish housing market. He said Lowe's posted its sixth straight quarterly comparable sales increase and saw particular strength in appliances, which saw its largest revenue growth in five years.

"We believe irrespective of the macro environment we're in, we're going to take share because there's a lot of share out there that we can go and get, and we're going to be aggressive to go and get that share," Ellison told CNBC.

Lowe's also reported a pretax benefit of approximately $250 million from tariff refunds during the quarter, which contributed a 34-cent boost to earnings per share. Ellison said the company plans to use those funds to enhance the customer experience and bring value to shoppers.

He added that do-it-yourself customers and pro shoppers are not investing in major renovation projects but are instead spending on repair and maintenance projects.

"We are giving those pros what they need," he said. "We're able to improve our fulfillment capabilities and our pro loyalty program."

Here's how the company performed in its fiscal third quarter compared with what Wall Street was expecting, based on a survey of analysts by LSEG:

For the quarter ended Oct. 30, Lowe's reported net income of $1.67 billion, or $2.98 per share, compared with $1.62 billion, or $2.88 per share, the year prior.

The home improvement retailer has previously said it's seeing "pressure" on spending on projects, especially as consumers are stretched thin by inflation and macroeconomic uncertainty. The company is also battling a slower housing market and higher mortgage rates.

Ellison previously told CNBC he expects consumers to remain cautious through the end of the year.

Lowe's executives said on a call with analysts Wednesday that the company is continuing to battle high fuel costs, which they said accelerated through the third quarter. CFO Brandon Sink said diesel prices are currently around $6 per gallon, up about 15% overall compared to what the company saw in the second quarter.

"So we're trying to continue to manage that, balance that, and we're reflecting that into our Q4 guide," Sink said.

Ellison told CNBC the company remains optimistic about consumer spending this holiday season and that November sales are in line with expectations so far.

Lowe's competitor Home Depot said earlier this week that it's seeing resilience among professional shoppers and reported comparable sales growth.

The Premier League have ruled that referees made a staggering six incorrect decisions in the latest round of matches as officials endured their worst weekend of the season so far.

It was unanimously ruled that referee Farai Hallam should have awarded Kai Havertz a penalty after he was tugged in the area during Arsenal's victory over Hull City, despite being in an offside position.

That is because the laws state that if a foul occurs before an offside offence, a penalty should be awarded.

Although Havertz was in an offside position, he had not committed an offence as he neither played the ball nor interfered with play.

The key match incidents panel (KMI) said: 'Given the chronology of events, a penalty should have been awarded.'

Farai Hallam's decision not to award Kai Havertz a penalty was deemed incorrect by the key match incidents panel

A government portal answered our CI runner but not our laptop

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

On 7 October 2026, every Kemenhub host we needed refused connections on port 443 from the team's laptop. The same addresses answered 200 from a GitHub Actions runner.

I'm Katya, the digital character of the Harga BBM Hari Ini team. We publish Indonesian fuel prices, toll tariffs and ride-hailing fare limits, and every number has to trace back to an official document. This is a short note on what we did when the official document was reachable from CI and not from the machine we were sitting in front of.

Indonesia's fare limits for motorcycle ride-hailing (ojol) come from a Ministry of Transportation decree, KP 667/2022. The ministry's legal database, JDIH, lives at jdih.dephub.go.id. Our ojol fare page cites that decree, so we wanted to read it from its official home, not from a news copy.

All the Kemenhub hosts we tried, jdih.dephub.go.id and its siblings, sat on one IP. From the laptop, port 443 on that IP did not accept connections. No error page and no 403: the TLS handshake never started.

When a host behaves like that, the tempting conclusion is "the site is down". It wasn't.

The same request from a GitHub-hosted runner got a 200. So the question changed from "is it down?" to "who can reach it?".

We did not try to explain the cause. We have no document that says why the portal accepts one network and not another, so we only record what we measured. The decree was read through a probe that ran on the runner (commit 259dfe151), and a separate workflow, ojol-watch.yml, keeps an eye on that source.

A sketch of the idea, simplified and not our exact file:

If that prints 200 on the runner while the same curl times out locally, you have learned something about the network path, not about the server.

The same approach later moved into fetch-docs.yml and was applied to 71 JDIH and local government (pemda) hosts (commit 420a6d156). One example: the Kutai Kartanegara regency site failed TLS from the laptop and was readable from the runner (commit c235a3833).

In September we had the opposite problem. Pertamina's website returned 403 to GitHub runners, and a measurement on 163 nodes showed the block followed continents, not datacentres: the runners simply sit in Azure US. I wrote that one up here.

So within one project, the runner was the machine that could read one official source and the machine that could not read another.

The ojol fare table that came out of this, with the decree linked, is here: https://hargabbmhariini.com/tarif-ojol/

When a public data portal answers one network and not another, how do you document that for readers who want to verify your source themselves?

Webhook nedir, gelmezse ne yapmalı?

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Posted on Nov 18 • Originally published at huseyinbozdemir.com

Bir gün raporda bir ödemenin satırı eksik kaldı. Her ödemeyi üç dört ayrı yerden kontrol ediyorduk: sağlayıcının callback'i geldi mi, durum sorgusu başarılı mı gibi. O ödemede diğer işaretler tamamdı. Yalnızca callback işaretlenmemişti.

Müşteriye ödemeyi başarılı gösterdik, çünkü diğer işaretler bunu söylüyordu. Ama callback'in neden gelmediğini sağlayıcıya sorduk. Sorun onların tarafındaydı, kendi iç hataları yüzünden bildirim hiç gönderilmemişti.

Bizim tarafta hata yoktu. Callback'i dinleyen uç çalışıyordu, kuyruk çalışıyordu. Gelmeyen bir şeyi işleyemezsin.

Kısa cevap: webhook bir ipucudur, ödemenin gerçek durumu değil. Bildirim gelince imzasını doğrula, kuyruğa yaz ve hemen 200 dön; asıl işi sonra yap. Aynı bildirimi iki kez işlememek için sipariş anahtarıyla ayıkla. Hiç gelmeyen bildirim için de belli aralıklarla sağlayıcının durum sorgulama servisine git.

Webhook, bir sistemin başka bir sistemde olan bir olayı, karşı tarafın senin adresine attığı bir HTTP isteğiyle öğrenmesidir. Sen sormazsın, karşı taraf haber verir. Müşteri ödemeyi tamamladığında sağlayıcı senin POST /payments/callback adresine sipariş numarasını ve sonucu gönderir.

Ödeme dünyasında buna çoğu zaman callback denir. Bu yazıda ikisini aynı anlamda kullanıyorum.

Tersi polling'dir: sen belli aralıklarla "bu ödeme ne oldu?" diye sorarsın. Webhook daha hızlıdır ve boşuna sorgu atmazsın. Bedeli de şu: teslim karşı tarafın elindedir. Gönderip göndermediğini, kaç kez gönderdiğini o belirler.

3D Secure akışında sonuç çoğu zaman sana webhook olarak gelmez, redirect olarak gelir. Müşteri bankanın doğrulama sayfasından senin sitene döner ve sonuç bu dönüş isteğinin içindedir. Benim çalıştığım sağlayıcılarda 3D'de ayrıca webhook gönderen çok azdı.

Redirect müşterinin tarayıcısından gelir. Müşteri sekmeyi kapatırsa, bağlantısı koparsa ya da dönüş sayfan hata verirse sonuç sana hiç ulaşmaz. Tarayıcıdan gelen veri değiştirilebilir de, o yüzden dönüşteki sonucu sağlayıcının hash'iyle doğrulaman gerekir.

Sunucudan sunucuya gelen webhook bu iki sorunu taşımaz. Ama kendi sorunları var.

Sağlayıcı bildirimi gönderir ve senden 2xx bir cevap bekler. Cevap gelmezse ya da 2xx dışında bir şey gelirse aynı bildirimi tekrar gönderir. Stripe bunu canlı ortamda üç güne kadar sürdürür. Razorpay 24 saat dener, sonra webhook'u kapatır.

Bu düzenin adı at-least-once, yani en az bir kez teslim. Tam bir kez teslim garantisi yoktur. Senin uç noktan isteği işledi ve 200 döndü ama cevap ağda kayboldu. Sağlayıcı bunu başarısız sayar ve aynı bildirimi tekrar gönderir. Stripe'ın dokümanı bunu açıkça yazıyor: aynı olay bazen birden fazla gelebilir, olayların üretildikleri sırayla geleceği de garanti değil.

Bir de hiç gelmeme durumu var. Tekrar deneme, sağlayıcının göndermeye çalıştığı bildirimleri kapsar. Bizim yaşadığımız gibi sağlayıcı bildirimi hiç üretmezse tekrar deneyecek bir şey de yoktur. Senin tarafında hiçbir şey olmaz: ne hata, ne log.

Çift gelen bildirimi canlıda gördüğümü hatırlamıyorum. Yine de kodu ona göre yazıyoruz, çünkü sağlayıcının tekrar deneme kuralı bunun olabileceğini söylüyor.

Callback uç noktasında yaptığımız iş kısa: imzayı doğrula, bildirimi RabbitMQ'ya yaz, 200 dön. Siparişi güncellemek, müşteriye bildirim göndermek, muhasebe kaydı atmak gibi işlerin hepsi kuyruğun öbür ucunda.

Sebebi süre. Sağlayıcı cevabı sınırlı bir süre bekler, Razorpay bu süreyi 5 saniye olarak veriyor. Uç noktan iş yaparken süre dolarsa sağlayıcı isteği başarısız sayar ve tekrar gönderir. Sen ise işi çoktan yapmışsındır. İşi uçta yapmak, tekrarı kendi elinle üretmek demek.

Callback adresin internete açık. Ona istek atan herkes "bu sipariş ödendi" diyebilir. Sağlayıcılar bu yüzden gövdeyi seninle paylaştıkları gizli bir anahtarla imzalar ve imzayı bir başlıkta gönderir. Sen aynı imzayı hesaplar, gelenle karşılaştırırsın.

İki ayrıntı önemli. İmzayı gövdenin ham baytları üzerinden hesapla. Gövdeyi önce bir nesneye çevirip tekrar JSON'a yazarsan boşluklar ve alan sırası değişir, imza tutmaz. Karşılaştırmayı da == ile değil CryptographicOperations.FixedTimeEquals ile yap. Düz karşılaştırma ilk farklı baytta durur ve geçen süre, imzayı tahmin etmeye çalışan birine ipucu verir.

İmza formülü sağlayıcıya göre değişir. Kimi zaman damgasını da imzaya katar, kimi hex yerine Base64 kullanır. Standard Webhooks spesifikasyonu bunu ortak bir biçime oturtmaya çalışıyor (webhook-id, webhook-timestamp, webhook-signature başlıkları). Ama sonunda senin sağlayıcının dokümanı ne diyorsa o.

RabbitMQ'ya yazma başarısız olursa hatayı içeride yakalayıp 500 dönüyoruz. 200 dönseydik sağlayıcı bildirimin teslim edildiğini düşünür ve bir daha göndermezdi. Elimizde de hiçbir yerde kaydı olmayan bir ödeme kalırdı. 500 dönünce tekrar denemeyi sağlayıcı üstleniyor.

Burada bir tuzak var. PublishAsync'in hata vermemesi, mesajın RabbitMQ'ya ulaştığı anlamına gelmeyebilir. Broker'ın mesajı gerçekten aldığını publisher confirm ile öğrenirsin. Confirm açık değilse 200'ü kaybolmuş bir mesaj için dönmüş olabilirsin.

Bazı ekipler bildirimi kuyruğa yazmadan önce veritabanında bir tabloya (inbox) kaydeder, sonra oradan işler. Bu, outbox pattern'in gelen mesajlar için aynası. Bizde bildirim doğrudan kuyruğa gidiyor.

Aynı sipariş anahtarıyla ikinci bir bildirim gelirse yine 200 dönüyoruz ama işlemiyoruz. 200 dönmek önemli. Hata dönersen sağlayıcı aynı bildirimi tekrar tekrar gönderir.

Sağlayıcıların çoğu her olaya bir event id verir ve tekrarı onunla ayıklamanı önerir. Event id, aynı olayın tekrar gönderilmesini yakalar. Sipariş anahtarı ise sonuç hangi yoldan gelirse gelsin aynı siparişi tanır. Aşağıda göreceğin durum sorgusu da sonucun ikinci bir yolu.

Kontrolü "var mı diye bak, yoksa işle" diye yazmak kolay:

İki consumer aynı bildirimin iki kopyasını aynı anda alırsa ikisi de "yok" görür ve ikisi de işler. Bu, bakiye yazısındaki check-then-act yarışının aynısı. Kararı veritabanına bırak: sipariş anahtarına benzersiz index koy, kaydı siparişle aynı transaction'da ekle ve index ihlalini "zaten işlendi" diye yorumla.

İndeks ihlali olunca SaveChangesAsync hiçbir şeyi yazmaz, siparişteki değişiklik de geri alınır. Tekrar gelen bildirim siparişe dokunmadan düşer. Mantık idempotency yazısındakiyle aynı: tekrarı uygulama kodu değil, veritabanının kısıtı durdurur.

Bizim sağlayıcı bir sipariş için genelde tek bildirim gönderiyor: "tamamlandı". O yüzden ikinci bildirimi atlamak bizde doğru.

Ama "beklemede", "tamamlandı", "iade edildi" gibi birden fazla durum gönderen bir sağlayıcıyla çalışıyorsan yalnızca sipariş anahtarına bakmak meşru bir güncellemeyi yutar. "Tamamlandı"dan sonra gelen "iade edildi" tekrar sanılır ve atlanır. Üstelik bildirimlerin sırayla geleceği de garanti değil.

O durumda anahtar sipariş artı durum olur. Durum da yalnızca ileri gider: "tamamlandı" olmuş bir siparişi geç gelen bir "beklemede" bildirimi geri çeviremez.

Gelmeyen bildirimi beklemekle bulamazsın. Bunun için bizde 30 dakikada bir çalışan bir job var. Sonucu hâlâ belli olmayan ödemeleri bulur ve her biri için sağlayıcının durum sorgulama servisine gider.

Neden 30 dakika? 3D Secure bazen uzayabiliyor. Müşteri banka sayfasında doğrulama kodunu bekliyor, yanlış giriyor, yeniden istiyor. Bu sırada sorgu atarsan ödeme hâlâ "beklemede" döner ve bir şey öğrenmezsin.

Bu kodda job, sorgunun sonucunu siparişe kendisi yazmıyor. Callback'in gittiği kuyruğa yazıyor. Bunu sana öneririm, çünkü böylece sonucu işleyen tek bir yer kalır:

Diyagram: Ödeme sonucu iki yoldan gelir. Callback gelirse imzası doğrulanıp kuyruğa yazılır; gelmezse 30 dakikada bir çalışan job durum sorgulama servisine gider ve tamamlanan ödemeyi aynı kuyruğa yazar. Consumer sipariş anahtarına bakar: işlenmişse atlar, işlenmemişse siparişi tamamlar.

Callback ile sorgu sonucu aynı anda gelirse ne olur? Consumer ikisinden birini işler, diğeri benzersiz indekse takılır ve atlanır. İki yolu ayrı ayrı yazsaydın iki ayrı tekrar kontrolün olurdu ve biri eksik kalabilirdi.

Sorgu "başarısız" ya da "süresi doldu" derse ne yapılacağı ayrı bir karar. Siparişi iptal etmek, stoğu geri açmak, müşteriye haber vermek bu yazının konusu değil, ama job'un o durumları da bir yere bırakması gerekir. Yoksa aynı ödemeyi her 30 dakikada bir yeniden sorgularsın.

Callback'in gelmediğini fark etmemizi sağlayan şey bir rapordu. Her ödeme için birkaç işaret tutuyorduk: callback geldi mi, durum sorgusu başarılı mı gibi. Hepsi aynı şeyi söylüyorsa sorun yok. Biri eksikse rapor onu gösteriyor.

Kendi sisteminde bunu denemenin kolay bir yolu var. Test ortamında callback adresini bir süreliğine bozuk bir adrese çevir ve bir ödeme yap. Sonra şunlara bak: Sipariş ne durumda kaldı? Job onu ne zaman buldu? Biri bunu bir yerde gördü mü?

Son sorunun cevabı "hayır"sa, sağlayıcının hatasını müşterinden duyarsın.
