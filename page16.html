Applied Materials posted record revenue, up 53%, topping expectations on AI chip demand

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The chip equipment maker expects fiscal first-quarter revenue of $11.1 billion, plus or minus $500 million

Applied Materials $AMAT reported record fiscal fourth-quarter revenue of $10.41 billion on Thursday, up 53% from a year earlier, as demand for semiconductors tied to artificial intelligence grew, the company said.

Net income was $2.72 billion, or $3.40 a share, up 43% from a year earlier. Non-GAAP earnings per share reached a record $4.16, up 92%.

Analysts had forecast adjusted earnings of $4.06 a share and revenue of $10.30 billion, according to The Wall Street Journal.

Semiconductor Systems revenue rose to $7.996 billion from $4.879 billion a year earlier. Foundry and logic made up 58% of the segment's revenue, DRAM 33% and flash memory 9%. Applied Global Services revenue was $1.896 billion, compared with $1.506 billion.

GAAP gross margin was 50.4% and non-GAAP gross margin was 50.6%, up 240 and 250 basis points from a year earlier, respectively.

"AI continues to fuel strong incremental demand for semiconductors," President and CEO Gary Dickerson said in a statement. He said Applied expects its Semiconductor Systems business to expand by more than 40% in calendar 2026, a period the company defines as running from the second quarter of fiscal 2026 through the first quarter of fiscal 2027.

For the fiscal year ended Oct. 25, revenue was a record $34.45 billion, up 21%. Non-GAAP earnings per share were a record $12.89, up 37%.

Cash from operations was a record $3.30 billion in the quarter. Applied returned $2.42 billion to shareholders, including $2.00 billion in share repurchases and $421 million in dividends. Chief Financial Officer Brice Hill said the buyback was the largest quarterly repurchase in more than eight years.

Applied expects first-quarter fiscal 2027 revenue of $11.1 billion, plus or minus $500 million, and non-GAAP diluted earnings per share of $4.35, plus or minus 20 cents, the company said. Analysts had forecast $10.85 billion in revenue and adjusted earnings of $4.24 a share, according to The Wall Street Journal.

Applied scheduled an earnings call for 4:30 p.m. ET on Thursday.

It's pretty funny that a cornerstone of Hitman: World of Assassination's post-launch content has been all about letting you kill celebrities.

Eminem, Gary Busey, and even Conor McGregor aren't safe from a bowl of poisoned mom's spaghetti, but IO Interactive recently branched out into the world of online celebrities with Seán William McLoughlin, better known as Jacksepticeye. He was added to the game on October 29 for a limited-time elusive target mission, putting him squarely in the crosshair of Agent 47—and the team isn't stopping there.

Mission writer Jen Simpkins was recently interviewed by PCGamesN to discuss the Jacksepticeye mission, and how the team honed in on the "latent horror of the content creation space" with his character. During this talk, she was asked whether IO Interactive would consider bringing more internet celebrities into the fold for future elusive target updates, and it's a "big yes."

"The internet is a weird place full of fascinating characters to take inspiration from, so it's a big yes from me," she said."

Who exactly IO Interactive might recruit to be brutally murdered by Agent 47 in ridiculously elaborate ways is another question entirely, but it's always a bit of a gamble with content creators. Overwatch added a skin based on Sinatraa (Jay Won), only for him to be accused of sexual assault; Call of Duty removed the NICKMERCS bundle after his comments on anti-LGBTQ+ protests; and the Payday 2 collab with H3H3 has faced unending backlash after Hila Klein's anecdote about her time in the IDF, and Ethan Klein's recent cheating scandal.

Of course, this is a risk with adding any real person, internet or otherwise. Elusive targets in Hitman are only available for a limited time, which lessens the risk. But even still, the McGregor court ruling in 2024 prompted IO Interactive to remove him from the game and issue a public statement; such is the nature of including big personalities.

Novità importanti sul fronte Lazio, sia per quanto riguarda i biglietti del derby sia per il futuro sponsor tecnico. Marco Canigiani, direttore marketing biancoceleste, ha fatto il punto sulla situazione, soffermandosi anche sulla vendita dei tagliandi per la Supercoppa.

“L’obiettivo ovviamente è preferire la vendita ai nostri tifosi, soprattutto per la Monte Mario; per la Tevere, anche più avanti servirà, la Eagle Card, mentre in Monte Mario partirà la vendita libera, che quindi ci auguriamo sia limitata. “Potenzialmente i possessori di tessere potrebbero riempiro lo stadio, dipende dalla volontà di farlo; anzi potrebbero anche non bastare i posti. TCL Electronics (01070.HK) Reports Significant Growth in Results for the First Three Quarters of 2026

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Adjusted Profit Attributable to Owners of the Parent Surges 54.5% Year-on-Year to HK$2.24 Billion

Global Business Continues to Grow, with Strong Operating Performance in International Markets

HONG KONG, Nov. 13, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- TCL Electronics Holdings Limited ("TCL Electronics" or the "Company", 01070.HK) today announced its unaudited results for the first three quarters of 2026, covering the nine months ended 30 September 2026. During the reporting period, the Company firmly implemented its dual-drive strategy of "Globalisation" and "Mid-to-High-End Premiumisation" and actively deepened cooperation with global channels, achieving strong growth in operating performance. For the first three quarters of 2026, the Company recorded total revenue of HK$95.79 billion, representing a year-on-year increase of 15.7%. Gross profit increased by 29.5% year-on-year to HK$16.26 billion, while gross profit margin increased by 1.8 percentage points year-on-year to 17.0%. The overall expense[1] ratio was 11.9%. Driven by both improved operating efficiency and the realisation of economies of scale, adjusted profit attributable to owners of the parent increased by 54.5% year-on-year to HK$2.24 billion.

In terms of strategic development, the Company continued to advance industrial synergies, the integration of core assets and the optimisation of its business structure. On 31 March 2026 (after trading hours), the Company entered into a transaction framework agreement with Sony in relation to the home entertainment business. The two parties proposed to establish a joint venture to jointly build a new global home entertainment ecosystem, providing important strategic support for the expansion into the mid-to-high-end market. In addition, on 15 July 2026 (after trading hours), the Company announced its proposed acquisition of the TCL air-conditioning business for a total consideration of HK$5.61 billion. The transaction was completed on 2 November 2026, and the relevant business has been consolidated into the Company's financial statements since the completion date, laying an important foundation for the Company to comprehensively implement its "All-category Smart Device" strategy. On 24 August 2026, the Company announced that it was considering the spin-off and separate listing of its photovoltaic business through a distribution in specie. The Company will comprehensively and prudently evaluate the various impacts of implementing this proposal from the perspective of maximising the interests of all shareholders. Looking ahead to the full year of 2026, the Company is confident that it will achieve sustained and steady growth through firm execution of its strategies.

Continued Enhancement of Global Brand Strength, with Significant Improvements in Profitability of Mid-to-High-End and Large-Sized TVs in International Markets

Underpinned by continued enhancement of its global brand strength, efficient channel expansion and ongoing product mix optimisation, the Company's Display Business recorded revenue of HK$62.38 billion, representing a year-on-year increase of 19.1%, and gross profit of HK$11.51 billion, representing a year-on-year increase of 38.4%. Gross profit margin increased by 2.6 percentage points year-on-year to 18.5%. Benefitting from the continued increase in the proportion of mid-to-high-end and large-sized TVs, revenue from the TCL TV business increased by 16.7% year-on-year to HK$52.03 billion, while gross profit margin increased by 3.2 percentage points year-on-year to 19.5%.

TCL Mini LED TVs maintained rapid growth for the first three quarters of 2026, with global shipments increasing by 57.9% year-on-year and their share of total shipments rising by 6.1 percentage points year-on-year to 17.5%. In particular, shipments in international markets increased by 94.5% year-on-year, driving a 4.4-percentage-point year-on-year increase in the gross profit margin of the TV business in international markets to 19.0%. The Company continued to expand its global channel footprint and deepen the development of key channels. These efforts helped TCL TV rank among the top three globally in sales revenue, maintain its second-place ranking globally in shipment volume, and retain its position as the world's No. 1 brand in TCL Mini LED TV shipments[2]. TCL X11L SQD-Mini LED TV was named one of TIME magazine's "Best Inventions of 2026" in the United States, making it the only Chinese television product selected in this category and further demonstrating the Company's leading position in premium display technology.

In the Small-and-Medium-Sized Display business, the Company adhered to the strategy of "prioritising efficiency and focusing on key markets" and continued to deepen its presence in key channels in Europe and the United States. Leveraging differentiated products such as NXTPAPER eye-care display technology, the business achieved growth against the market trend, with both revenue and gross profit outperforming the industry as a whole. For the first three quarters of 2026, revenue from the Small-and-Medium-Sized Display business increased by 32.2% year-on-year to HK$9.32 billion.

International Internet Business Contributes to High-Quality Earnings, While Photovoltaic Business Achieves Substantive Progress in the European Market

During the Reporting Period, the Company's Internet Business recorded revenue of HK$2.70 billion, representing a year-on-year increase of 28.2%. Someone had to do dual MX/HE compatibility properly, and Keychron stepped up to the mark. The Keychron V6 Ultra Hybrid 8K HE offers dual magnetic and mechanical switch compatibility, plus the widest array of magnetic hot-swappability I’ve yet seen. It’s formidable for gaming, beautiful for typing, built well and reasonably priced. It’s a little bland-looking, but that’s a minor point, and we had a small QC issue, although I’d expect that to be fixed in full production units.

The Keychron V6 Ultra Hybrid 8K HE (catchy name, right?) attempts to forge ‘The One Ring’ of keyboards. One keyboard to rule them all, to do and be everything for everyone. The master keyboard.

As such, it’s the latest in a very small selection of keyboards that offer dual mechanical and magnetic capability. If you want to hot-swap from magnetic back to mechanical switches, you can. But the V6 takes things further with, as I alluded to above, an altogether more holistic approach. The V6 Ultra Hybrid 8K HE also packs everything else Keychron has to throw at a keyboard — which essentially means every feature that actually exists right now.

8K polling, tons of magnetic features and customization in the Launcher app, gasket mounting for comfortable typing — all with a reasonable price tag. It also offers my holy grail feature at checkout: a magnetic and tactile switch, for elite gaming and typing without even needing to hot-swap in the first place.

So, like, is this legitimately the perfect hybrid board? The best gaming keyboard and the best mechanical keyboard all rolled into one? I think so. Keychron has done it. Find out more in my full Keychron V6 Ultra Hybrid 8K HE review.

Where to even start? Proper MX/HE hot swap, bags of gaming and productivity performance, reasonable price — this is a Goldilocks keyboard.

The V6 Ultra Hybrid 8K HE’s main feature is denoted by the “Hybrid” part of its (terribly convoluted) name. This board is dual mechanical/magnetic switch compatible, meaning you can swap between the two.

The V6 Ultra Hybrid takes any standard 3-pin or 5-pin mechanical switches. It also supports a wide range of magnetic switches with the N Pole facing downwards — that means Gateron x Wooting Jade switches, Gateron KS-20 switches, and any of Gateron’s ever-growing line of dual rail magnetic switches. Neat.

Now Keychron isn’t the first to do dual MX/HE. Glorious did it a while back with the hilariously overpriced GMMK 3 HE. More recently, Lofree had a pop with the Hyzen, but with typically Lofree results: great looks and typing but rank amateurism in the software department (pivotal to a magnetic board). Keychron, in usual Keychron fashion, is the first to do a proper job of it, with the widest magnetic hot-swap scope while still keeping things affordable.

The long and short is that the V6 Ultra Hybrid has more actual hot-swap potential than any other magnetic board that I know of. Most magnetic boards claim to be hot swappable, but only support a couple of switch variants from the same specific magnetic switch line — often the variants available at checkout. In reality, that usually makes hot-swap claims severely hamstrung at best; meaningless at worst. Not so here. Again, the Lofree Hyzen also supports an impressive roster of compatible magnetic switches, but lacks the professional polish of Keychron’s boards.

The “hybrid” moniker also has another related meaning. The V6 Ultra Hybrid is a hybrid board for both work and play, offering powerful gaming performance while capable enough to handle the rigors of work and heavy typing.

The bit that excites me most are two of the magnetic switch choices available from stock. The V6 Ultra Hybrid ships with one of four Gateron Ultra Fast Lime magnetic switches, two of which are tactile. Yes, tactile magnetic switches — this is the biggest news in my opinion.

All magnetic switches up to now have been linear in operation. Sure, being able to hot-swap those is fine, but swapping out 108 switches from a full-sized keyboard is a ginormous time suck. So it’s not like you can just quickly switch from tactiles for work to magnetic for gaming. If, like me, you enjoy tactile switches but want magnetic gaming performance, you’ve so far been left out in the cold. Not anymore.

The V6 Ultra Hybrid also packs plenty of Keychron’s productivity heritage. There’s a gasket mount, for bouncy and comfortable typing; Keychron’s steeply-raked and deeply-sculpted OSA profile keycaps, which I find ideal for fast typing; and screw-in stabilizers for minimal big key wobble. As you can see, my typing speed and accuracy were both above average on the V6 Ultra Hybrid, helped by paring back the actuation to 2.6mm — my personal sweet spot and where I type-test all magnetic boards. I could (and indeed did) type on this thing all day.

Thanks to Gateron’s magnetic switches, you also get every elite gaming feature under the sun right now: adjustable actuation; rapid trigger; dynamic keystroke for multiple commands per actuation; custom SOCD resolutions for last key prioritization (A.K.A. ‘Snap Tap’). There’s 8K polling too, although on a keyboard this is tantamount to snake oil, if you ask me. All of this is customizable through Keychron’s fantastic web launcher.

I used the board for everything, from competitive gaming on “Counter-Strike 2” and “Wardogs,” through to more relaxed titles like “Total War: Rome II,” “Valheim,” and “Dune: Awakening.” It never put a foot wrong.

Admittedly, if you want a board purely for competitive FPS gaming, you’re probably better off with a plate-, tray-, or top-mounted magnetic board like the NuPhy Air60 HE, NuPhy Field75 HE, or Keychron Q16 HE 8K — these mount styles aren’t as comfortable, but move around less than a gasket mount, making them less likely to cause accidental inputs at super low <0.5mm switch actuations.

If you need an all-rounder for both work and play, though, I seriously can’t think of a better board than the V6 Ultra Hybrid.

As this is a Keychron board, you get all the expected goodies. Aside from some QC issues I’ll cover later, the board is built well, using premium materials. The case is sturdy ABS plastic with very little bend and flex — impressive given this is a long board.

The keycaps are doubleshot PBT, which will stand up well to wear and tear without going shiny, unlike ABS keycaps. PBT is also grease resistant. I did my usual test, typing on the keyboard after eating Monster Munch (a fried corn snack here in the U.K.), and the V6’s keycaps picked up a lot of grease but were easy to wipe down. Not quite as impressive as the caps on the Logitech G Pro X2 Rapid, which completely resisted the same grease altogether — like not even a single smear.

The V6 features a decent 4,000mAh battery, plus triple Bluetooth, 2.4G and wired connectivity, toggleable via a switch on the case. There’s a macOS/Windows toggle, too, but Keychron still supplies separate sets of Windows and macOS command keys rather than dual-labeling its keycaps. This is a longstanding frustration of mine. I switch between the two OS’ constantly for work. It’s great to have the toggle, but it undermines the ease if I have to switch keycaps too. A small issue, but one that’s so easily solvable by dual labelling — as proved by the Lofree Hyzen and Lofree Flow 2.

When I reviewed the original Keychron V6, which I awarded a full five stars, one of my favorite features was the price. It offered major performance for a very reasonable sum. The V6 Ultra Hybrid 8K HE is pricier. It costs $164 versus the $99 basic V6 and the $119 V6 Ultra (which has faster polling, better internal damping, nicer switches, etc — just an all-round upgraded package).

However, the V6 Ultra Hybrid packs a lot more functionality than the lower-tier V6 models thanks to the dual MX/HE switch compatibility, and way more gaming features when using HE switches. An extra $45 over the standard V6 Ultra is fair game for all that, at least in my book.

Let’s look at the hybrid MX/HE competition, too. The Lofree Hyzen costs a whopping $279 — sure it’s made from aluminum and is beautiful to behold, but the software is janky as hell. The Glorious GMMK 3 HE starts at $179, but you can easily get up to near $500 for choice productivity upgrades (and to make it look less ugly than stock). By those two yardsticks, the V6 Ultra Hybrid 8K HE is the best value-for-money hybrid board on the market, and it ain’t even close.

At the time of writing, the V6 Ultra Hybrid 8K HE is in pre-order, after its Kickstarter campaign, with estimated shipping starting December 2026.

As I’ve mentioned in previous reviews, I absolutely hate that Keychron floats its new boards on Kickstarter. Keychron is a major keyboard brand (arguably the dominant productivity market force) that absolutely does not need to crowdfund its boards. Besides, as someone who has tested several of Keychron’s crowdfunded boards, I can tell you they’re already developed and practically finished by the start of the campaigns (because most Keychron boards are 95% the same under the hood). Oh well. That’s just unfortunately how Keychron chooses to operate, but you can rest assured that there’s usually very little chance of a crowdfunding rug pull from this brand.

There’s very little I don’t like about the V6 Ultra Hybrid 8K HE, aside from its ridiculously long-winded name. It’s a little boring to look at, I guess, and I saw some QC teething issues, but nothing that should seriously dissuade you.

Ah, look: black plastic case, gray rounded keycaps, a tiny bit of color on the ESC key. It must be a Keychron! Yawn. I’m not saying the V6 Ultra Hybrid is bad-looking, but it’s the same old Keychron design we’ve seen for years now. And it’s getting kinda boring.

Now, as someone who has owned, tested, used, or edited the reviews of literally hundreds of keyboards, this probably affects me more than you. I see a lot of Keychrons; you’ll probably only see the one you buy.

But man, I wish Keychron would make some more exciting decks in its more affordable V- and K-series sometimes. Pretty boards with plenty of color, like the NuPhy Halo75; or handsome futuristic designs like the Lofree Hyzen and Lofree Block. Come on, Keychron, spice it up a bit.

As Keychron has grown, I’ve seen firsthand its once stellar reputation for build quality start to drop. A few years ago, every single Keychron board we tested was built like a tank. Now we’re seeing frequent issues with sticky switches or other build quality concerns. Not all the time, I must say. And Keychron is always keen to hear from us when this happens, so we always tell its representatives and receive a replacement in quick order. But it’s getting to the point now that I’m seeing a pattern I can’t ignore.

My first V6 Ultra Hybrid review unit suffered both a sticky switch (solved by adjusting debounce time) and a USB dongle port that was far too large with no magnet inside, meaning the 2.4G receiver would fall out. Keychron’s representative has assured me this was a pre-production teething issue, and issued me a full production unit where the problem has been fixed. But as I said above, similar issues are becoming too common now from this once wholly reliable brand, and it’s a problem I need to call out. Keychron, if you’re reading, please step your game back up.

I utterly adore this keyboard. I won’t be using it long-term because I prefer a 75% layout (so I‘m eagerly waiting for a V2 Ultra Hybrid 8K HE). But I absolutely love what this deck signifies.

As is so often the case in the productivity keyboard arena, it takes Keychron to turn its attention to an innovation to truly bring that feature into the mainstream and make it work. Dual MX/HE compatibility is now here, with proper wide-ranging switch support for both mechanical and magnetic. And there’s a tactile magnetic switch on offer to boot.

If you want one keyboard that excels for literally every purpose, without breaking the bank, it’s the Keychron V6 Ultra Hybrid 8K HE. End of story.

Xbox Shares New Fable Trailer that Divides the Fanbase

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

The new Fable trailer looks surprisingly dark

On Nov. 12, Xbox CEO Asha Sharma shared a new gameplay trailer for Fable, the long-anticipated fourth installment in the Fable series, developed by Playground Games. While excitement for the game remains high, many fans shared their negative reactions online, confirming that this will be another controversial release.

Fable is scheduled for release on Feb. 23, 2027. The game has been in development for almost a decade, with first reports emerging in 2018. It's the first game in the series not developed by Lionhead Studios, after the studio was closed by Microsoft in April 2016. Fable is an attempt to revive the beloved franchise created by Peter Molyneux and his team at Lionhead, which released the first three critically acclaimed games between 2004 and 2010.

In Fable, players will return once again to the land of Albion and take the role of the Hero, but the game will not follow the established timeline of its predecessors in the series. Judging by the latest trailer, that won't be the only difference.

The Fable trailer released on Nov. 12 shows a female Hero following the trail of a woman named Isabel, who allegedly attacked her village, turning the inhabitants to stone. The protagonist is accompanied by another Hero, a woman called Arden (voiced by Susan Wokoma), who rejected her heroic calling and now works as a cobbler. Together, they explore a bit of the forest, fight some undead enemies, and then find a mysterious cavern with supernatural phenomena. There is a lot of walking, and a lot of talking. And everything looks bleak.

The first three Fable games had their dark moments (do you remember the Tattered Spire in Fable 2?), but the world of Albion almost always looked bright and colorful, even when it was trying to kill you. The series took inspiration from fairy tales, which often mix whimsy with the unsettling, giving the games their unique tone and feel. This latest trailer for the new Fable shows a world that has discarded whimsy and retained only the darkest parts of the fairy tale (literally, there are very few colors).

Many fans have expressed their dissatisfaction with the footage. Some complained that the "gameplay" trailer consists mostly of cutscenes and characters walking and talking, with Arden acting as a source of excessive exposition. Other comments highlight how the game looks too much like any run-of-the-mill action-RPG.

The trailer does have a combat sequence, which showcases the three combat styles (melee, ranged, and magic) that players can freely switch between. Some users praised the combat seen in the trailer, while others complained that the movement looked stiff. Many fans are simply excited about a new Fable game, and have high expectations for the title.

Overall, the biggest source of disagreement among fans is that the new Fable has a very different aesthetic from previous chapters in the series. While this is fully understandable after a decade, it's also true that the colorful and bombastic nature of Albion was one of the reasons the franchise was so successful. It must be said that the sequence shown in the trailer takes place in a forest twisted by dark forces, and the previous trailer looked a bit brighter.

Fable is scheduled for release on Feb. 23, 2027. It will be available on PlayStation 5, Windows PC, and Xbox.

Grey’s Anatomy’s 17-Year Heartbreak Story Legitimizes Its New Meredith

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Warning! SPOILERS about Grey’s Anatomy season 23, episode 5 ahead.A story entirely unrelated to Meredith unexpectedly introduced the perfect replacement for the Grey’s Anatomy protagonist in season 23, episode 5. Ellen Pompeo scaling back her appearances in Grey’s Anatomy season 19 raised questions about whether an existing character on the ABC medical drama would take over from Meredith for the kind of storylines that focused on her, both in terms of her centrality to several storylines and how she always propelled drama and important themes in them. Over the years, Grey’s Anatomy proposed several Meredith replacements, but they rarely worked perfectly.

Meredith appearing several times throughout each season at Grey Sloan Memorial made finding some kind of replacement superfluous, especially as her visits managed to coincide with either momentous developments in her research and the hospital, or disasters. However, in the day-to-day of Grey Sloan Memorial’s happenings, some characters channeled the Grey’s Anatomy protagonist more than others. Among them were two of Meredith’s mentees, Jo and Simone, who both suffered a lot in their past, had a complicated history with Grey Sloan Memorial, and proved their resilience just like Meredith did before them.

However, Grey’s Anatomy season 23, episode 5 may have just introduced another unlikely contender in the contest for Meredith’s replacement, one whose story might not completely fit within Meredith’s parameters, but who had his fair share of family drama. The arrival of his father at Grey Sloan Memorial in Grey’s Anatomy season 23, episode 4’s ending and what that caused in him made his similarities with Meredith impossible to ignore.

Grey’s Anatomy season 23, episode 5 doesn’t even reveal the name of Benson Kwan’s biological father, but the effect he has on him is evident. Kwan doesn’t want anything to do with him, and yet after learning a little more about him, he still offers to test for compatibility for a potential bone marrow transplant, as a way to help him with his myeloma. Only Jules overhearing his private call reveals that the only reason he had come to Grey Sloan Memorial was to convince Kwan to help him.

Grey's Anatomy season 23's premiere's plane collision highlights a recurring problem of the medical drama that risks decreasing its stakes.

While bearing some differences, including how despicable Kwan’s father is for wanting to simply manipulate his son to save himself, Kwan’s dynamic with his father in Grey’s Anatomy season 23, episode 5 is eerily similar to Meredith’s with Thatcher in season 6. In Grey’s Anatomy season 6, episode 4, Thatcher arrived at Seattle Grace with liver failure. While Lexie wanted to help him, Meredith didn’t want anything to do with him, and yet it was Meredith who was compatible.

Thatcher didn’t want to accept Meredith’s generosity because he didn’t believe he deserved it, proving a better parent than Kwan’s father. Still, Meredith was eventually swayed by Lexie’s request rather than caring about Thatcher’s fate specifically. Kwan might still decide to help his father, although how they left things makes it extremely unlikely. Even if he didn’t, though, his willingness to help his father despite all the hurt that he caused him makes him extremely similar to Meredith Grey, both in his decision and family dynamic with his father.

Meredith didn’t leave a lasting impact on Kwan’s training in the little time they were both at Grey Sloan Memorial. However, Grey’s Anatomy highlighted time and time again the similarities between Kwan and Meredith’s values and professional histories. After all, Kwan was so burned by having to pay for his mother’s medical bills well after her death that he suggested his patient and her husband divorce to help with the insurance. Meredith went to greater extremes by committing insurance fraud in Grey’s Anatomy season 16, but it’s clear they came to the same conclusion that their system was broken.
