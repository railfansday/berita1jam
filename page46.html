Gaming on Mac has come a long way. Gone are the days of limited releases, though not absolutely everything is playable on the platform. Thankfully, community developers are only too keen to plug the gaps.

FX Steam Launcher on GitHub brings SteamOS to Apple Silicon Macs. Specifically, this independent, open-source project is based on the ARM64 Steam Frame version of the operating system. Therefore, you're getting Windows game compatibility on Mac thanks to Proton 11 and FEX, alongside the very swish SteamOS interface (via Videocardz).

Obviously, there are a few things to bear in mind if you want to go all in as a Mac gamer. For one thing, this is not a native installation, and is instead running SteamOS by way of a virtual machine on your Mac. For another, this is a GitHub download to begin with, but once you've got it installed, the launcher directly downloads the official SteamOS image from Valve and then even handles OS updates too. Neat!

As this is an unofficial effort, there are a few more caveats still. The FX Steam Launcher itself currently only supports Apple Silicon Macs running either macOS 15 (from September, 2024) or newer. However, to actually get the latest and greatest running, you may need something much more recent than that.

Bear with me here, but Proton and FEX are essentially only the first hurdle. DXVK is required to run DirectX 9, 10, and 11 games, while DirectX 12 releases rely on VKD3D-Proton. Then either MoltenVK or KosmicKrisp is the next hurdle graphics instructions will need to pass before finally handing off the baton to Apple’s Metal API (though there's some experimental support for DXMT, which allows DirectX 10 and 11 games to run without VKD3D-Proton).

Anyway, all of that is to say KosmicKrisp, a pretty pivotal translation layer in this whole process, requires at least macOS 26 or later to run. That's a fair amount of hoops to jump through, though; if you can get it running, the launcher does allow you to run SteamOS either windowed or fullscreen, while letting you still access macOS either way.

On account of not being a Mac power user, I've not had a chance to test it out myself, but YouTube creator ETA Prime tested the launcher with The Witcher 3, Stellar Blade, and Left 4 Dead 2 on their Apple M4 Max. At high settings, the creator saw performance to the tune of over 70 FPS for each of these games, with Left 4 Dead 2 breezing past 200.

Herniated Discs Are a Common Cycling Injury—Here’s Everything You Need to Know

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Keep your back health on track with these tips from top docs and bike-fit experts.

Sometimes it happens with a bang, like when former Tour de France pro Marty Jemison was racing Tour of Flanders in the late 1990s. His saddle slipped to be nose up. He thought “no big deal” until one of the steep cobbled climbs, where he was in the saddle cranking out maximum wattage.“And my back just popped,” he says.

Jemison finished the race and the others that followed until he retired about 18 months later, though he had pain off the bike and for the first 30 or 60 minutes of riding, and knew something was wrong. That something was a herniated disc that became nearly debilitating after retirement, eventually required two procedures, and still flares up to this day if he’s not meticulous about his bike position and on top of his core work.

Other times, it’s the proverbial straw that breaks the camel’s (or the cyclist’s) back, as was the case with avid long-distance cyclist Mark Elsasser of Philadelphia who developed some sciatica in 2018. He was able to ride through it just fine, but couldn’t sit for any length of time before the pain was unbearable. The diagnosis: a herniated disc in his low back, which he resolved with physical therapy and has kept at bay by changing his position on the bike, going from a 0-rise to 5-degree stem. “My spine is less compromised being a bit more upright rather than nose down on top of the stem,” he says.

Herniated discs are fairly common in the general population, occurring most frequently among 30- to 50-year-old adults, and men more often than women. Interestingly, you may have one right now and not even know it. “We know from radiographical studies that 50 percent of those aged 40 may have some form of disc herniation. We might consider it normal, age-related changes, and many of those won’t be symptomatic,” says bike fitter and sports physiotherapist Bianca Broadbent, co-owner of The Cycling Physios based in the United Kingdom.

In fact, one research review published in the American Journal of Neuroradiology found that degenerative changes in the discs, which include herniated discs, are present in nearly 90 percent of adults 60 and older and often require no intervention. A herniated disc only causes pain if it’s pressing or otherwise irritating a nerve root in the spine

Your spine is comprised of 24 vertebrae. Between those vertebrae are fluid-filled cushions known as discs, which act as shock absorbers for your spinal bones.

“Imagine your spinal discs as jelly-filled doughnuts,” says Andrew Pruitt, Ed.D., the founder of the Boulder Center for Sports Medicine and CU Sports Medicine and Performance Center. “A herniation happens when the jelly inside pops out.”

You may hear people using other terms like “bulging disc,” “ruptured disc,” or “slipped disc.” Technically, a bulging disc is just that: It’s when the jelly inside the donut is pushing against the side enough to bulge out from the vertebrae but isn’t leaking out. A ruptured or slipped disc refers to an actual herniation, where the doughnut cracks, allowing the gel-filled center to squeeze out.

A bulging and/or herniated disc can cause pain by creating pressure on the nearby nerves. There’s some evidence that the leaked fluid from herniated discs also can cause chemical irritation and inflammation of the spinal nerve roots.

Again, it’s possible to have an asymptomatic herniated disc where you feel no pain. When you do have pain from a herniated disc, however, you’ll know it.

Pain from the herniated discs is sharp and radiating. Most cyclists experience herniated discs in the lower, lumbar back (we’ll get to why in a bit). So you may feel burning pain often known as sciatica that radiates from your lower back into your glutes and down one or both legs, sometimes even into your feet. You may feel pain similar to an electric shock when you stand or sit or walk. You may also feel radiating numbness, tingling, and/or weakness in your lower extremities.

So how does the jelly get out of the doughnut and start wreaking havoc on your spinal nerves? In a word: pressure. It can be extreme and sudden, like Jemison cranking on the cobbles. Or, in many instances, it can happen gradually over time, as was the case with Elsasser.

When your spine is in its generally straight, neutral position, the pressure from the top down is uniform on your discs, so the jelly inside those cushions is evenly distributed, Pruitt explains.

“When you bend forward, the jelly is pushed back, stressing the skin of that doughnut,” he says. “If you’re sitting all day, that jelly is pressing back there for eight hours. Now jump on a bike, and you add another two hours in an even more extreme position, pressing that jelly further and further, until—pop—it comes out.”

Though herniation can happen anywhere in the spine, in cyclists it happens most frequently in the lumbar (low back) region close to the base of the spine, or the L5-S1, the bottom-most disc that sits right on top of your tailbone, because that’s where we bend the most, says Menachem Brodie, C.S.C.S., head coach at Human Vortex Training and USA Cycling expert coach.

“For many cyclists, the L5-S1 is where they’re getting their flexion, and the lumbar spine has about six degrees of movement,” Brodie says. So if you’re not hinging from the hips and keeping your spine within its safe, natural range, you’re pushing those limits. Keeping your spine under that kind of stress for prolonged periods of time pushes you closer to the failure point.

Along those lines, research shows that riders with a lower handlebar position and greater flexion in that key lower back region were more likely to have low back pain. Though, importantly, the research also suggests that poor core strength and endurance could contribute to the spinal stress and low back pain in cyclists.

Of course, the bike isn’t the only place you can stress your spine. The gym can be another place riders put their spine under unhealthy stress, says Dane DeLozier, D.P.T., the biomechanics lab director at and co-founder of Revo Physiotherapy and Sports Performance. Maintaining a stable, neutral spine is very important when resistance training, especially when doing big lifts like deadlifts and squats as well as under velocity like with kettlebell swings, DeLozier says.

“When you have your spine under load, you want it to be neutral and very stabilized. You want to be hinging through your hips, not bending and extending your spine under load,” he says.

If you’re unsure of if you’re maintaining a neutral spine, DeLozier recommends setting up your phone and recording yourself (or having a friend video record you) so you can see if you are breaking or flexing under load.

The good news is a 2020 scientific paper, “Slipped disc: Overview,” reports that about 90 percent of people will get relief from their pain and restricted movement within six weeks even if they receive no medical treatment. That’s because the body gets rid of part of the prolapsed tissue or it shifts position so that the nerves aren’t irritated anymore, according to the overview.

You can help that process along with physical therapy, DeLozier says. “The vast majority of folks feel better and have a great outcome by doing mobility exercises for the hip and low back and decompression exercises for the spine.”

The exercises you do depends on your individual situation, but your physical therapist may recommend mobility, stability, and core strengthening exercises such as:

Press Up: Lie facedown with your feet together, toes pointed, and your forearms on the floor palms. Pull your shoulder blades down and in towards your spine and press into forearms to lift your chest off of the floor, keeping your neck long and hips on the floor. If mobility allows, you can press up further by placing hands below your shoulders to come into an up-dog position. Hold for 3 to 5 seconds. Repeat about 15 times.

Hip Flexor Stretch: Kneel with the affected knee on the floor, tighten your glutes, and brace your abdominal muscles while keeping your back straight. Pressing hips forward. Hold this stretch for 30 seconds. Do 3 times.

Bird Dog: Get on your hands and knees with your palms flat on the floor, shoulder-width apart. Slowly raise and straighten your right leg and left arm at the same time. Keep your hips aligned with the torso and not tilted to one side. Hold for 10 seconds. Return to start. Repeat on the other side.

Cycling itself, when done with a healthy riding posture, can also be a great rehab tool to strengthen the discs and surrounding structures, Broadbent says. In fact, a study done on high-volume road cyclists who rode nearly 100 miles a week for five or more years had beneficial adaptations to their discs and psoas (the biggest hip flexor muscle), which were beneficial for spinal health.

If you’re riding through your rehab, Brodie recommends “crocodile breathing” when you get off the bike. To do it, lie facedown with your forehead resting on the back of your hands and let your chest and hips sink into the floor. Inhale deeply into your belly, expanding your diaphragm for four slow counts. Pause for two seconds, then exhale for six slow counts.

Finally, while many herniated discs can be treated conservatively with physical therapy, some require injection therapy like nerve blocks, if the symptoms are severe, Broadbent says. In rare cases, spinal disc herniation can cause extreme pressure and swelling of the nerves at the end of the spinal cord causing a medical emergency known as cauda equina syndrome.

“If your bladder and bowels aren’t working properly, you have saddle numbness (off the bike as well), or you have sexual dysfunction such as unable to get an erection then you should seek urgent medical investigation,” Broadbent says.

Again, cycling can actually improve spinal strength and the health and hydration of your discs, says Broadbent.

Having a good bike position and the right saddle is key. “You want your spine in a healthy position that doesn’t strain those discs in your lower back,” Pruitt says. If you ride a gravel or a mountain bike, you’re likely already in a slightly more upright, more neutral spine position, he says.

An anatomical saddle will also allow you to put your weight on your perineum without putting harmful pressure on those sensitive tissues, so you’re able to reach the bars without bending too far and putting your spine in an unhealthy position. “Saddle technology improvements have really helped reduce degenerative disc disease,” he says.

You also can protect your spine by doing core-strengthening exercises several times a week. Brodie recommends what are known as the “big three” exercises for spinal stability, developed by renowned back pain expert, Stuart McGill, Ph.D., Professor Emeritus of Spine Biomechanics at the University of Waterloo. These exercises stiffen the musculature throughout the core in a way that enhances stability in the spine.

How to do these exercises: The American Council of Exercise recommends performing three sets for each using a reverse pyramid rep/set scheme. Start with a higher number of repetitions for the first set, then decrease by 2 to 6 reps, then decrease by another 2 to 4 reps on the last set. So you might start with 8-6-4 reps and work your way up to 12-10-8 reps as you get stronger and build endurance. Hold each exercise for no more than 8 to 10 seconds.

McGill Curl-up: Lie on your back with one leg extended and the other leg bent, foot flat on the floor. Put your hands under your low back to maintain your natural arch. In one move, lift your head, shoulders, and chest off the floor, keeping your neck and back in the neutral position. Hold. Then slowly return to start. Do half a set. Then switch legs and complete the set.

Side Bridge: Lie on your right side, with your forearm on the floor and elbow underneath your shoulder. Bend your knees at a 90-degree angle. Place your left hand on the opposite shoulder for stability. Lift your hips off the floor so your body forms a straight line from your head to your knees. Hold. Then lower to the starting position and repeat to the other side. Work up to doing the move with your legs straight.

You can make these exercises part of your warm-up, along with dynamic mobility moves before you get into your bigger lifts.

Off the bike, Brodie recommends practicing good spine hygiene by avoiding hunching forward when you sit and not sitting in a static position for prolonged periods of time, but rather shifting positions in your chair every 15 minutes and getting up, moving around, and doing a little standing stretch, extending your arms, reaching overhead and taking a deep breath every 60 to 90 minutes.

Finally, don’t overlook your mattress. A firm mattress with a pillow top will help support your spine while you sleep, Brodie says. “A lot of people overlook this and it can make a big difference in your back health.”

What’s at stake for crypto as the US votes in the 2026 midterms?

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Crypto groups spent about $176 million ahead of the US midterms, with control of Congress and the future of crypto legislation at stake.

US voters head to the polls in midterm elections on Tuesday that could shift control of Congress and shape the future of cryptocurrency regulation.

Crypto industry groups have spent roughly $176 million across more than 100 races this election cycle, according to an Oct. 27 Reuters report.

Coinbase, Ripple and Andreessen Horowitz are among the main financiers of the Fairshake political network.

The results could reshape the prospects for the stalled CLARITY Act and determine which lawmakers lead committees overseeing the industry.

Republicans enter Tuesday’s midterms controlling both chambers of Congress, with 219 seats in the House of Representatives and 53 in the Senate, according to the Blockchain Association’s election dashboard.

Democrats are seeking to overturn those majorities, with prediction markets favoring the party to win the House and Senate, according to Kalshi data at the time of publishing.

Voters will decide all 435 House seats and 35 Senate races, determining which party controls Congress for the final two years of President Donald Trump’s second term.

Democratic control could bring more congressional scrutiny of Trump-linked crypto businesses and regulators’ decisions. Control of the Senate would also affect confirmation of future presidential nominees to regulatory posts.

Ohio’s Senate race between Republican Jon Husted and Democrat Sherrod Brown is a major test of crypto’s political influence. Industry groups have reportedly spent more than $50 million opposing Brown, a longtime crypto critic who could return to a senior Senate committee role.

In Nebraska, Republican Sen. Pete Ricketts faces independent Dan Osborn. Ricketts, a Senate Banking Committee member who has supported crypto-friendly legislation, has benefited from nearly $11 million in spending by Fairshake, according to the Washington Examiner.

Texas is another race to watch, with Republican Ken Paxton facing Democrat James Talarico in a closely contested Senate election. As Cointelegraph previously reported, the Winklevoss-backed Digital Freedom Fund has spent money supporting Paxton.

A shift in congressional control could delay the CLARITY Act, although the crypto market structure bill has already struggled under Republican leadership.

The legislation would divide oversight of crypto markets between the Securities and Exchange Commission and the Commodity Futures Trading Commission, as well as establish rules for intermediaries. The Senate failed to advance it in a 49–50 procedural vote on Sept. 15, short of the 60 votes required.

Democratic Sen. Mark Warner cited insufficient safeguards against Trump profiting from his administration’s crypto policies. Republican sponsors said their final draft incorporated substantial ethics concessions sought by Democrats.

“A change in control of Congress would most likely reset the timetable for the CLARITY Act,” Christian Davies, group head of innovation and digital capital at Relm Insurance, told Cointelegraph.

Ryan Kirkley, CEO of Global Settlement Network, agreed that the challenges extend beyond party control.

“The CLARITY Act was already struggling to get through the Senate with Republicans in control, so clearly the problem goes beyond which party holds the majority,” Kirkley said.

The industry has already secured a federal stablecoin framework through the GENIUS Act, which became law in July 2025.

Bitcoin’s (BTC) price history around US midterms has attracted attention on social media, but analysts caution that past declines offer little guidance on what could happen after Tuesday’s vote.

There haven’t been enough cycles to call it a pattern, and the market is built differently now: ETF flows, institutional portfolios and global liquidity move the price far more than they used to,” Michael Heinrich, CEO and co-founder of 0G Labs, told Cointelegraph.

Their Names Sound Like Legacy Medical Groups. Their Agendas Tell a Different Story.

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

— Newcomer organizations have names that resemble established groups

Several organizations have sprung up in recent years with names that sound very similar to long-standing established nonprofit public health and medical groups, whose science-based policies have withstood the test of time.

Here's a list of some of these organizations whose names, at first glance, may confuse prospective members or donors.

The American Public Health Association, founded in 1872, has some 25,000 individual members and more than 150,000 allied health professionals and advocates for public health science and spending. It publishes the American Journal of Public Health. Its CEO, Georges Benjamin, MD, has been a vociferous critic of the Trump administration's policies. The APHA has filed a lawsuit against its vaccination schedule policies and several others over grant funding.

The Academy of Public Health was founded in February 2025 by Jay Bhattacharya, MD, PhD, the Trump administration's NIH director, and others who questioned some of the ways the U.S. responded to the COVID pandemic, and who advocated the controversial policy of herd immunity. Its founders also criticized publication policies of many mainstream medical journals. It publishes the Journal of the Academy of Public Health.

The American Academy of Pediatrics was launched in 1930. It now has some 67,000 pediatricians as members. It has opposed Trump administration immunization policies as well as statements by HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. questioning vaccine schedules that have led to lower immunization rates among children, and more illness. It supports gender-affirming care for transgender youth.

The American College of Pediatricians was launched in 2002 by several pediatricians who left the AAP to protest the support of allowing same-sex couples to adopt children. It holds that life begins at fertilization. Its website says it is the "leading U.S. medical organization protecting the biological integrity of children against transgender ideology."

The American College of Obstetricians & Gynecologists began in 1951 to focus on issues specific to women's health, including family planning. It now focuses on advocating for maternal health as a national priority. It has about 60,000 members and publishes the journal Obstetrics & Gynecology. It has opposed several Trump administration policies, including warnings about use of antidepressants during pregnancy, suggesting these are linked with autism, miscarriage, and birth defects.

The American Association of Pro-Life Obstetricians and Gynecologists was an ACOG special interest group until 2013 when ACOG ended that designation. It opposes abortion, suggesting it is associated with health risks such as depression and substance abuse, and supports "the physician's right of conscience in medical decision making" regarding abortion.

The American Academy of Family Physicians began in 1947 and now says it is the voice of 124,500 family doctors. It publishes a journal, American Family Physician. It has opposed several Trump administration actions, such as its removal of an AAFP member and other members from the CDC's long-standing Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices.

The American College of Family Medicine began in the spring of 2023. It opposes mandatory abortion training in residencies and maintains that "abortion is never medically necessary and should not be offered or performed by any health professional."

The American College of Surgeons was founded in 1913 to improve surgical outcomes. It has launched numerous surgical quality improvement programs for surgical specialties to learn strategies and compare results, and its website has a surgical risk calculator. It claims 95,000 members globally, and publishes the Journal of the American College of Surgeons.

The Association of American Physicians and Surgeons was launched in 1943 by a conservative group of physicians. It has long opposed the Affordable Care Act, believes physicians are often unfairly targeted with federal fraud accusations, and are too frequently subjected to "illegitimate" hospital peer review. It opposed COVID mask and vaccination mandates and generally believes that healthcare providers are over-regulated. It opposes gun control.

It publishes the Journal of American Physicians and Surgeons.

The Center for Science in the Public Interest began in 1971 as a consumer advocacy group and now advocates for policies that keep dangerous chemicals out of the food supply. It wants to "fix our broken food additive regulations" and improve food labeling.

The Center for Medicine in the Public Interest says it is a non-partisan research and educational organization with a goal to advocate for patient-centered healthcare. It has come under fire for its free-market policies and has been said to be overly influenced by the pharmaceutical industry, including lawsuit immunity for drug manufacturers.

The Children's Defense Fund began in 1973, rooted in the civil rights and women's movements and the fight for equity and racial justice. It works to reduce child hunger and gun violence and improve nutrition, education, and school safety.

Children's Health Defense began as the World Mercury Project, but under Robert F. Kennedy Jr.'s chairmanship -- his picture is at the top of the organization's homepage -- the name was changed in 2018. Through litigation and public messaging campaigns, the group has challenged public health mandates and school vaccine requirements, and has suggested vaccines and some environmental contaminants are the underlying causes for autism, despite evidence to the contrary.

As the Trump administration's Health Secretary since 2025, Kennedy has taken many of CHD's causes and statements and incorporated them into federal recommendations and policies. His actions have prompted numerous lawsuits from states, medical groups, and others.

On its website soliciting donations, CHD acknowledges the potential for name confusion with other organizations. It says, "Please note, we are often confused with Children's Health Fund or Children's Defense Fund, so be careful not to inadvertently select the wrong recipient."

Several of these organizations did not respond to a request for comment, or said they declined comment on whether name similarity had affected their philanthropic donations or membership.

Former CAKE team is back in the kitchen with a new electric motorcycle

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

If the new ReBake Abler electric motorcycle looks like it might share a bit of DNA with Sweden’s dearly departed CAKE, there’s a good reason for that.

ReBake Motors has officially unveiled its first electric two-wheeler at EICMA 2026 in Milan, introducing the Abler as a highly modular, utility-focused electric moped and light motorcycle.

Behind the new Swedish company is founder and CEO Karl Ytterborn, who spent eight years at CAKE from its founding in 2016 through 2024. CAKE famously built some of the most interesting minimalist electric motorcycles and utility bikes on the market before ultimately collapsing after failing to secure additional funding. Ytterborn says ReBake is intended to apply many of the lessons learned during that experience, while eliminating unnecessary cost and complexity.

Its chunky 42mm aluminum tube frame isn’t just a styling exercise. ReBake designed it as a mounting system, allowing cargo accessories to attach directly to the frame at multiple locations. That means riders can configure the bike for everything from carrying groceries and passengers to more serious utility duties.

There are two versions. The Abler45 is an EU L1e-B moped with a 45 km/h (28 mph) top speed, 4 kW nominal motor and 5.12 kW peak output. It starts at €3,990 including VAT.

The €4,990 Abler80 steps up to the A1 light motorcycle category, increasing nominal power to 6.14 kW and peak power to 7.68 kW while pushing the top speed beyond 80 km/h (50 mph). Both versions use a direct-drive rear hub motor.

Energy comes from removable 2 kWh lithium iron phosphate (LFP) batteries weighing 17 kg (37.5 lb) apiece. The Abler45 comes standard with one battery but can carry two, while the Abler80 includes two for 4 kWh of total capacity. With both batteries, ReBake claims up to 160 km (99 miles) of low-speed urban range on the Abler45 or 114 km (71 miles) on the Abler80. Each battery can be charged either on or off the bike from a normal 230V outlet, reaching a full charge in four hours.

The rest of the hardware includes 14-inch cast wheels, 140mm of front suspension travel, dual rear shocks, front and rear disc brakes with combined braking, regenerative braking, and full LED lighting.

Perhaps more interesting than any individual specification, though, is simply seeing another Swedish electric motorcycle startup emerge from CAKE’s ashes.

CAKE demonstrated that there was plenty of appetite for unconventional, lightweight electric motorcycles. Unfortunately, it also demonstrated just how difficult it is to turn that enthusiasm into a sustainable business.

ReBake appears to be getting another crack at that equation – this time with eight years’ worth of lessons already baked in.

The Trump administration's months-long campaign to challenge how states conduct elections and pushback against those efforts came to a head on Election Day on Tuesday.

Those efforts have included lawsuits, an attempt to implement a new U.S. Postal Service rule for mail-in ballots this year, threatening letters to state election officials and a plan to increase the number of poll monitors.

On Tuesday afternoon, The New York Times reported that the Department of Justice had sent letters to about 20 Democrats, among them prominent election lawyer Marc Elias, informing them that they might be targets in a criminal investigation of how a candidate with the same name as incumbent Republican Sen. Dan Sullivan ended up on the ballot of the Alaska Senate race.

NBC News reported that the tally of letters includes both target letters "and preservation requests to third parties to retain records."

Elias has represented Mary Peltola, the Democratic nominee in the Alaska election. In addition to him, at least one official at the Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee was sent a target letter, The Times reported.

"The department is investigating possible donor fraud and conspiracy charges," according to The Times, citing a person familiar with the investigation.

Receiving a target letter does not mean a person has been charged with a crime, and does not necessarily mean that they will be charged.

In a statement, Elias said, "No one should be surprised that President Trump's Department of Justice is targeting me and my law firm on Election Day."

President Donald Trump, during an interview earlier Tuesday on Fox News' "Fox and Friends," blasted Elias.

"You had the Democrat Party headed up by Elias, their lawyer, who was a bad guy, very bad guy — should go to jail, in my opinion, " Trump said. "But you had the Democrat Party look all over Alaska for somebody named Sullivan."

A federal judge on Sunday dismissed the DOJ lawsuit that sought to delay certification of Alaska's Senate election if incumbent Sen. Sullivan loses by a margin that could be attributable to voter confusion from having another Dan Sullivan on the ballot. The DOJ is appealing that ruling.

That case, and the reported letters to Elias and other Democrats, underscore the likelihood that the Trump administration's election-targeting efforts will continue beyond Election Day.

Democratic lawmakers, attorneys general and advocacy groups are poised for battle if the administration tries to interfere with voting sites or challenge ballot counts, particularly in the states that could determine whether Trump's fellow Republicans lose their narrow Senate majority.

Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer, D-N.Y., on Sunday night convened the entire Senate Democratic Caucus for a briefing by him and Elias about what a Democratic aide said was the "current landscape of election threats from Trump and his allies." The aide spoke on condition of anonymity to discuss the private briefing.

"We've been, you know, blocking and tackling and protecting and defending our elections for months and through the last few weeks of the voting window, and it'll all culminate tomorrow," California Attorney General Rob Bonta told CNBC on Monday evening. "And each of Trump's attempts has failed."

"I'm not sure what he has up his sleeves," Bonta said of Trump.

Bonta said he and other California officials, along with the 23 other Democratic state attorneys general for months have been preparing for Election Day and potential actions by the Trump administration "to disenfranchise and suppress votes and voters."

"We game-planned, and ... war-gamed and tabletop- and scenario planned all the possibilities," Bonta said.

Bonta said that if extreme events happened, California officials would quickly seek relief from the court system.

He noted, "We have laws in place right now where it is a criminal offense" to remove ballots from the custody and control of an election official.

On Monday, multiple federal courts dealt with urgent motions in election-related lawsuits and appeals by and against the Trump administration.

The DOJ on Monday asked the Supreme Court to block California from counting about 20,000 mail-in ballots from voters whom the DOJ suggests might not be U.S. citizens. Hours later, the Supreme Court rejected that request without any noted dissents.

In U.S. District Court in Washington, a DOJ lawyer told Judge Reggie Walton on Monday that the department expected to deploy 900 people to monitor polling places at sites in all 50 states.

"In 2024, there were about 100 monitors," the lawyer, Kathryn Barragan, said.

The DOJ on Tuesday said it was deploying approximately 1,000 election monitors and observers across the country. The areas it is focusing on are primarily in urban areas that vote Democratic.

Barragan's statement came at a hearing where Walton was considering an emergency request by the NAACP to bar the DOJ from using election monitors who are federal law enforcement personnel.

John Cusick, assistant counsel at the Legal Defense Fund, a civil rights group, said the administration has used the DOJ, Department of Homeland Security and other federal agencies to target election procedures this election cycle for three main reasons.

"First and foremost, it's an attempt to intimidate voters and election officials," Cusick said in an interview.

"The second component is confusion" about election rules, he said. "And the third, the unfortunate part, is trying to lay the groundwork for challenging the post-election counting and certification of ballots."

He said there has been a "constant barrage" by the administration to target election workers and election officials with unlawful actions.

"That has created such a chilling effect," Cusick said. "There's no doubt that the threats are threats to our democracy."

Attorney General Todd Blanche, when asked on Oct. 22 if there was any scenario in which the DOJ would try to block states from certifying their election results, conceded that was possible.

"I mean, is there any scenario?" Blanche told reporters in Virginia. "Yes, of course. There's scenarios that we could all come up with, hypothetically, you know, some doomsday scenario, for example."

"But, you know, to say that there's no circumstances that we would ever do that? Of course I'm not going to say that."

On Monday, a bipartisan group of former election officials in a filing told the 9th Circuit U.S. Court of Appeals that "increased federal pressure and threats on election officials ... are part of a pattern by the administration of attempting to assert authority over elections and questioning the integrity of state and local officials."

The filing came in a case where the DOJ is appealing an order issued Friday by a federal judge in California barring the Trump administration "from carrying out a series of efforts to actually or attempting to intimidate, threaten, or coerce election workers who aid others in exercising their right to vote."

The judge cited threats by the DOJ to prosecute state and local election officials if they process and count ballots using procedures that are legally required.

Low-Calorie Diet and Supervised Exercise May Reverse Early Onset Type 2 Diabetes

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Follow Healthline on our socials for all things health.

The RESET for Remission trial, presented at the 2026 European Association for the Study of Diabetes (EASD) Annual Meeting and published in The Lancet Regional Health Americas on October 1, found that more than half of adults with early onset type 2 diabetes entered remission after six months on a low calorie diet and supervised exercise program.

Early onset type 2 diabetes is when the condition is diagnosed before age 45.

Those with early onset type 2 diabetes may be at a higher risk of complications like long-term damage to the capillaries (microvascular complications), adverse cardiovascular outcomes, and earlier death.

Typical treatment for early onset type 2 diabetes is often a combination of lifestyle changes, like diet and exercise, and medications.

However, this new study suggests that a supervised low calorie diet and exercise program may help some people with early onset type 2 diabetes enter remission.

The RESET for Remission trial included 96 adults from England and Canada who were between the ages of 18 and 45, had early onset type 2 diabetes, obesity, and were not on insulin treatment.

The participants had an average age of 38 and an average body mass index (BMI) of 35.2 kg/m2. They were randomized into a control group that received usual care alone and the intervention group, which ate a low calorie diet and participated in supervised exercise programs.

During the first 12 weeks, participants in the intervention group followed an 800–900-calorie-per-day diet and completed twice-weekly supervised aerobic and resistance exercise sessions, along with one independent exercise session per week.

For weeks 13–24, they transitioned to a weight-maintenance diet and independent exercise.

Both groups were supervised by physicians, and the intervention group was also closely supervised by licensed dietitians.

The low calorie diet consisted of 30% protein, 50% carbohydrate, and 20% fat. Participants were also encouraged to drink at least 2 liters of calorie-free fluids per day and given access to a fiber-based laxative as needed.

In the first two weeks, all meals were replaced with specially formulated nutritional products (Optifast). Then, in weeks 3–12, the participants maintained the same calorie intake but had more flexibility in their food choices.

This second phase of the diet included one conventional meal per day, with daily intake targets that included protein, fruit, and vegetables. Typically, this meant 60 to 90 grams of protein from all sources combined, one portion of fruit, and two portions of non-starchy vegetables.

At the end of 24 weeks, 54% of the intervention group entered remission of type 2 diabetes, compared with only 4% in the control group. Researchers defined remission as an HbA1c level below 6.5% without glucose-lowering medication for at least 12 weeks.

“This study proves what most physicians have advocated for quite some time. Clearly, diet and exercise can work to improve or even resolve diabetes,” David Cutler, MD, board certified family medicine physician at Providence Saint John’s Health Center in Santa Monica, CA, who wasn’t involved in the study, told Healthline.

Cutler also added that this study provides a clear example of how difficult it can be to enter diabetes remission with diet and exercise alone. He stated that the small number of participants in the study also makes him wonder if these findings can be applied to all populations.

Another limitation of this study, as Cutler pointed out, was that the strict dietary limits imposed on study participants would be very difficult for the average adult to adhere to.

The study also followed participants for only six months, so it’s unclear how many would remain in remission over the longer term.

“It is worth trying diet and exercise first because they can work to achieve meaningful weight loss and remission of diabetes,” he said.

Just a little AI resistance, as a treat

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

AI EMFCamp NaBloPoMo · 3 comments · 750 words · Viewed ~388 times

At the recent EMFCamp, I bumped in to Professor Kate Devlin who was talking a little about the AI Resist List. I think one of the useful things we can do is loudly complain about shitty uses of AI. Here's an example.

As mentioned in a previous post, I'm contacting companies which send malformed calendar invites. One of the organisation I contacted was the British online supermarket Ocado. They don't have an easy way to contact their technical team, and no obvious paths to raise a bug report, so I filled in their usual customer service feedback form. I included a clear statement of the problem and a detailed technical analysis of why I thought it was a problem with their service.

After sending in my message, I received this back from "Lulu" at Ocado:

Thanks for getting in touch and sharing this feedback about our calendar files.

You're entirely right—having a technical glitch pop up when you're trying to organise your delivery is a real pain, so I've made sure to pass your feedback on to our technical and webshop team.

We really appreciate you taking the time to help us improve our service. I hope you have a wonderful rest of your day

I mean, that's AI right? It starts with the classic tell of a sycophantic agreement.

Next up is the em-dash. Beloved by chatbots but almost unheard of in British English style guides.

Finally (and this is hard to quantify) it just doesn't feel like a customer service response. They're usually poorly spelled, have dodgy grammar, and ask a bunch of irrelevant questions. The message gave me no confidence that anyone had looked at the problem and I doubted it had been passed on to a technically competent human.

I immediately got a customer satisfaction survey. Usually I give top marks to humans because, even though Net Promoter Score is bullshit, I don't want customer service agents to suffer the indignity of a bad review. But in this survey I vented my spleen - giving crappy 1 ⭐ scores on every metric and complaining vociferously about how rude it is to use an AI to deal with a genuine complaint.

I was both surprised and delighted to receive this back a few days after my invective was sent:

I saw your survey comment about getting a robotic, AI-generated response previously, and I wanted to reach out personally to apologise. There is nothing more frustrating than pointing out a legitimate bug only to feel like you're talking to a brick wall. I promise you there is a real person typing this out!

Second, I wanted to send a massive thank you for the incredibly detailed diagnostic. It is rare to get feedback that tells us exactly what is broken and how to fix it.

I can assure you that this has been passed to our team to look into. Your feedback is going to make things much smoother for a lot of people, and we really appreciate you taking the extra time to help us get it right.

Thank you again, I do hope that this issue is resolved quickly. If there is anything else I can help with in the meantime, please don't hesitate to get in touch.

Perhaps I'm being touchingly naïve, but that feels a lot more human, right? The grammar isn't perfect, the sentences are a bit more rambly, and it appears to have actually engaged with the contents of my message.

I get that having a million irate customer service messages is a hassle. Using AI to triage them probably makes business sense, summarising the verbose ones could be useful for customer service agents, and totting up general themes is almost certainly a sensible way to gather metrics without overloading the workforce.

But using an AI to reply to a human is just disrespectful. It says the organisation values their customer's time at less than their own.

It's OK to call out shitty AI responses and insist that a human engages with you.
