Michael Carrick clashes with Sky Sports reporter in spiky interview over his future: Man United boss expects to continue despite club being in relegation zone after 4-4 draw with Villa

📅 2026-11-08  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

By CHRIS WHEELER, NORTHERN SPORTS WRITER and LUKE AUGUSTUS, ASSISTANT SPORTS EDITOR

Published: 15:17 EDT, 8 November 2026 | Updated: 16:08 EDT, 8 November 2026

Michael Carrick insists he won't quit as Manchester United boss despite having to see his team spend the international break in the relegation zone.

United staged another incredible fightback to snatch a 4-4 draw with Aston Villa at Old Trafford after trailing by two goals heading into injury-time.

But it wasn't enough to spare the club from the humiliation of being in the bottom three for the next two weeks – the longest United have spent there since 1986.

The manner of the performance and result will only heap further pressure on Carrick and when the future of his own position was put to him by Sky Sports reporter Patrick Davison, the United boss took exception to the question in a spiky interview.

'What do you expect to happen over the next few weeks? You've been asked time and again about your future? I know you have said that you walking away was the best for the club, you'd probably do it,' Davison asked Carrick.

'It's not going to happen, no,' Carrick replied.

With United chiefs Omar Berrada and Jason Wilcox in the stands, Davison pushed further asking: 'What do you expect to happen? Clearly there's going to be conversation?'

This prompted Carrick to quizzically state: 'Is there?' as the pair engaged in a back-and-forth on the subject.

Eventually Carrick replied: 'You know the situation, we haven't won enough games. But I expect to move forward with things we've been working on, showing a lot of the good from today tighten up a few mistakes and win some football matches.'

United's start of one win in 10 games is still their worst since Alex Ferguson replaced Ron Atkinson 40 years ago this week. They conceded twice at the start of each half against Villa, and have now let in 30 goals in all competitions – the joint highest in Europe's top five leagues.

'We're making it hard for ourselves. Mistakes that the boys know shouldn't happen,' added Carrick. 'Two goals again put us on the back foot. We beat ourselves a little bit with the goals that we're giving away in tight clusters. It's hard enough anyway, but we're making it a lot more difficult than it should be.

'I thought we were the better team. We never gave up and never stopped believing. I'm disappointed that we still haven't won the game, to be truthful.'

The United fans kept behind Carrick and his team while once again targeting the club's owners, chief executive Omar Berrada and director of football Jason Wilcox.

However, there were loud cheers when Diogo Dalot was replaced in the 66th minute. It was reminiscent of the treatment of Joshua Zirkzee by the home supporters when he was substituted against Newcastle in December 2024, and the Dutchman made a point of giving Dalot a reassuring hug in the dugout.

'We've got to be careful with that,' said Carrick. 'I understand the frustration and the disappointment of the supporters, so I get it. We're all frustrated, we're all disappointed, and the players are as much as anyone, I guarantee you that.

'Diogo gives absolutely everything. He's played in different positions, he's played left-back, he's played really well at times.

'He gave a penalty away today, he knows that. Maybe he can do a little bit better. He's made a mistake, but he cares an awful lot and he's giving everything.'

Dalot's replacement Patrick Dorgu led the fightback, five days after United had recovered from two goals down to beat Roma 3-2, as he crossed for Zirkzee to pull a goal back and then headed the equaliser in the 96th minute.

'I don't feel happy,' said Dorgu. 'We are in a bad situation and we need to fight together to get out of it. A draw is better than a loss, but everyone needs to look at themselves and ask if they are doing enough. We need to fight for the manager, for the club and get out of this together.

'Of course, it hurts (to be in relegation zone). There are a lot of games and we need everyone to come back sharp from international break and start getting wins.'

Teammate Matheus Cunha added: 'We are so disappointed. Before anything else we need to look inside to us – the players, the staff. It's a huge club and to feel something like this (being in the relegation zone) is not normal.'

It's not the first time Carrick has engaged in an interesting post-match interview this season. In August, he snapped at a reporter's 'ridiculous' opening question after Manchester United's shock defeat at Hull City on the opening day of the season.

Carrick was affronted when asked if his players had lost against the newly promoted club due to a lack of 'passion'.

United's Premier League season got off to a nightmare start as goals from Semi Ajayi and Nobel Mendy - both stemming from set-pieces - put them to the sword in Yorkshire.

Carrick was interviewed afterwards by Viaplay broadcaster Jan Age Fjortoft, the Norwegian who played for Swindon, Middlesbrough, Sheffield United and Barnsley in the 1990s.

Nvidia investigates CEO Jensen Huang's relationship with alleged Chinese spy

📅 2026-11-08  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Nvidia has opened an investigation into co-founder and CEO Jensen Huang's relationship with a businesswoman who intelligence agencies say is a Chinese spy.

U.S. intelligence agencies have expressed concerns that the woman, Alice Huang, who is affiliated with a cloud company and an Nvidia customer called Megaspeed, has been passing information to Beijing, according to reports in The New York Times and the Financial Times.

Huang told Nvidia staff in an email on Sunday that he had an affair with the woman and that Nvidia's board of directors had opened an independent investigation into the spying allegations.

"I was unfaithful to my wife. My actions hurt her and our children. There is no excuse for what I did," Huang wrote in the message. "I am deeply sorry."

An Nvidia spokesperson said that Huang was committed to continuing as CEO of Nvidia without interruption.

In a statement, the Nvidia board said it would investigate the allegations that Huang had been the target of a foreign intelligence effort.

"While the Board has no reason to believe that either national security or NVIDIA secrets were compromised, the Board's inquiry will examine all available information," Nvidia's board said in a statement.

The allegations come as Nvidia has become the center of a trade dispute between China and the U.S. as AI is seen as increasingly critical to national security.

The U.S. restricts exports of Nvidia's most advanced AI chips to China, though it has approved exports of certain older, slower models.

Megaspeed, which Ms. Huang is affiliated with, has been accused of helping restricted Chinese companies acquire Nvidia AI chips. Nvidia representatives previously said that Megaspeed had already been investigated for evading export controls.

Nvidia's Huang has pushed for broader AI chip exports to China, saying it is better for the U.S. if the world uses and builds on American technology. He has also aligned with President Donald Trump on American artificial intelligence policy and its infrastructure buildout.

In his note to staff, Huang said he was "proud and grateful" to be an American.

Three House Republican committee chairs sent a letter to Huang on Wednesday asking him to sit for a closed-door briefing with lawmakers on Nov. 18.

Nvidia reports third-quarter earnings on Nov. 17 and will hold a major conference in Washington, D.C., at the end of the month.

Man United are a rotten, shambolic mess. They lack intelligence, look uncoached and fumbling, failing Michael Carrick is not the man to arrest this trauma, writes IAN LADYMAN

📅 2026-11-08  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Published: 14:32 EDT, 8 November 2026 | Updated: 15:49 EDT, 8 November 2026

Manchester United do not have a football team, more a collection of adrenaline junkies. Over time, it will not be enough.

So deficient are Michael Carrick's players that they surely can't keep their manager in a job for much longer. For the most part United are shambolic, a mess. At times they play football so devoid of intelligence and competence that it's almost impossible to fathom. They look uncoached.

Yet here they were for the second night running at Old Trafford, completing a lap of honour at full-time while their supporters sang the name of Carrick as he completed his own journey in front of the Stretford End towards the tunnel.

United – it's worth pointing out – are third bottom of the Premier League. They haven't won in the league since August and have conceded more goals across all competitions – 30 – than any team bar Crystal Palace in Europe's top divisions.

But at Old Trafford they will cling to anything to help them look in the mirror without laughing and currently the currency is comebacks. There was one against Roma in the Champions League last Tuesday and here – quite remarkably – there were two in one game.

None of it should really mean anything in the grand scheme. United are rotten, capable of playing effectively only when something gives them an energy spike with which to roll. It makes for quite hypnotic theatre but has inadequacy and desperation at its core.

It won't sustain them or indeed their fumbling, failing manager. But for the moment it's what they have and what they are.

Here, they twice trailed by two, at the start of both halves. They were 2-0 down after only four minutes and 4-2 behind after 53. Somehow they managed not to lose.

The two goals United scored in added time at the end of the game came from absolutely nowhere. On the touchline Carrick – free from total humiliation for the second time in six days – raised a single finger to the sky.

In the directors' box – where the TV companies seem to have a camera permanently trained these days – fist pumps were in evidence from those in United blazers.

This was another uncomfortable afternoon for Messrs Ratcliffe, Wilcox and Berrada. A collection of new banners in the crowd made feelings known.

For now, Carrick is being spared any of the blame. That is strange and wrong but tells us much about the confused nature of the modern United. If they blame one of their own then they really do have nowhere left to go in search of self-respect.

United's opening against Roma last week had been traumatic enough. This was even worse but the root of it was desperately familiar – incompetent football.

The hapless United full-back Diogo Dalot was to blame for the first goal as Villa's Brazilian Alysson blasted his shot through the Portuguese's limp challenge. Later – after conceding a second half penalty – Dalot was booed off by his own fans.

That was painful and unedifying but completely understandable. Dalot has played more than 250 games for United yet has never even been good enough.

Once again United were in sink or swim territory. Once again, amazingly, they kept their heads above water – at least for a while.

Boubacar Kamara's 13th minute challenge on Matheus Cunha was stupid as the Brazilian was heading away from goal. But the Bruno Fernandes penalty that followed was calm and precise and the shot of belief United needed was theirs.

For the next 20 minutes or so, Carrick's team played well off the back of it. It was the only part of their game that will look good on the highlights. The United front four pressed and harried the Villa defence to the extent that Unai Emery's team couldn't get up the field.

Villa keeper Zion Suzuki saved well from a Cunha curler while subsequent shots from Fernandes, Kobbie Mainoo and Amad Diallo took deflections and narrowly missed the target.

At some stage it felt as though United's energy must drop. Over time it did. But then they equalised.

Moments after seeing Suzuki save one of his own efforts, Amad found space to pull the ball back for Youri Tielemans who placed his first United goal in from 18 yards against his old club. Carrick's team deserved to be level at that stage and had played some enterprising football to get there. But their self-harm gene surfaced again before long.

Alysson had almost scored with the last big moment of the first half and in the 47th minute he drew an utterly dumb foul from Dalot and John McGinn scored the penalty firmly.

United are beyond compare in the Premier League when it comes to this kind of thing. Who else in the top flight concedes the kind of goals they do? A case in point came in the 53rd minute.

McGinn's free-kick was heading across goal and away when United defender Leny Yoro inexplicably headed it towards his own goal. Lammens somehow touched the ball on to the bar but when it dropped Leon Goretzka poked it in.

That was the 16th goal United had conceded in the last five games and this time the attempt at a fightback was more muted.

There were some half chances – shots from Cunha and Fernandes – and some territory in which to play.

But this was a game Villa were now able to control rather better and they looked set to ease home until Patrick Dorgu crossed for Joshua Zirkzee to ease a goal in from out of nothing in the 2nd minute of added time.

Old Trafford was emptying fast by the time Dorgu headed in a 96th equaliser from a corner. But by then the collective blood was up once more and it was enough.

Villa appealed for a foul on goalkeeper Suzuki and it's true Lammens had gone looking for him. Was the contact between the two 'keepers significant enough to warrant VAR intervention? Probably not. United had their draw and they had a point.

If the season ended today, they would be down. Yet they were cheered off. Some things you just can't make up.

Characterize First, Then Refactor: A Golden-Corpus Harness With a Mutation Gate

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Most refactors break behavior that no test ever covered. Adding more hand-written tests is slow and biased toward what you already suspect. A pinned behavior snapshot is faster, and a mutation gate tells you whether that snapshot can actually fail.

The rule: pin first, prove the pin bites, then change the smallest thing. If the pin never fails on a deliberate bug, you have documentation, not a safety net.

Choose a target that is boring and load-bearing. Small input surface, no hidden globals, callable from a script. Write the choice down in the commit message.

If the function reads the clock, calls the network, or draws randomness, stop. You need a seam before a snapshot. Pin a wrapper with the bad inputs injected instead.

Build a JSONL file of real-shaped inputs. One case per line, stable ids, keyword arguments. No customer data, no tokens, no emails.

Three sources work well. Existing fixture files, call sites in the repo, and log payloads you have already stripped. Twenty to forty cases covers most small functions.

Include the ugly ones: empty string, zero, negative, missing key, duplicate id. The golden file should pin the error paths too, not just the happy path.

Raw output rarely compares cleanly. Dict ordering drifts, floats repeat, timestamps move. Normalize before hashing.

Normalization is a tradeoff, not a free win. Rounding floats hides small drift. Falling back to type name hides everything inside slot-only objects. Log both decisions in the harness header.

The harness fingerprints each case and stores a sha256. Exceptions are pinned by class name and message.

Run it twice. Once to write, once to confirm the harness is stable. A pin that drifts on a second run is not a pin.

This is the step most people skip. A snapshot nobody has ever seen fail is unverified. So break the code on purpose, one behavior at a time.

Keep each mutant as a tiny patch file. Each one must change real behavior, not formatting.

A MISSED line is the useful output. It names a behavior your corpus never exercises. Add one case, re-pin, re-probe. Repeat until every survivor is a mutant you can prove is equivalent to the original.

A SKIP line proves nothing. A patch that fails to apply, or a mutant that fails to import, is not evidence of coverage.

Now refactor. Rename, extract, inline, move. Re-run the check after every commit.

Example output after a clean extract-method commit: ok: 34 cases unchanged. If a hash moves, the patch was not behavior-preserving. Revert it, split it, try again.

One extra rule keeps this honest. Any commit that updates golden.json must also carry a BEHAVIOR: line in the message. That line explains why the change is intended. A golden diff without that line is a refactor that leaked.

After the refactor, run the mutant loop again. Every mutant that was CAUGHT must still be CAUGHT. Coverage can survive a move while silently degrading.

Two parts of this loop are tedious to write by hand. First, the normalization layer, which needs to survive real shapes you have not seen. Second, the mutant set, which needs genuinely different behaviors rather than nine cosmetic edits.

MonkeyCode's free model access and free server option are relevant here, both as operator-supplied availability claims rather than measured benchmarks. A free model is good at drafting normalize branches for shapes you forgot. It is also good at proposing candidate mutants across comparison operators, defaults, branches, and validation paths. The free server option matters because the mutant loop is a matrix of full test runs. Running that matrix in a sandbox keeps your local credentials and your laptop's patience out of the loop.

Disclosure: This article was prepared as part of MonkeyCode's product outreach.

Use model output as a proposal, never as a gate. Every suggested mutant needs a human check that it compiles and actually changes behavior. In my experience with generated mutants, the failure mode is equivalent mutants and non-applying patches, not missing ideas. Reject them loudly; a mutant set padded with noise makes the probe look green while proving nothing.

Skip this workflow if you have no reproducible input source. Skipped if the function cannot be imported without side effects, or if the output is dominated by data you cannot legally freeze. Skip it when the real risk is security or concurrency, where a pinned snapshot is silent. And skip it if nobody will maintain the mutant set. A stale probe is worse than none, because it looks like evidence.

The most useful file you will produce is the MISSED list. Save it, and let it drive the next corpus case.
