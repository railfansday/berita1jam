Pfizer hikes full-year outlook as newer products grow, taps company veteran as new CFO

📅 2026-11-03  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Pfizer on Tuesday reported fiscal third-quarter results that topped estimates and hiked its full-year outlook, as demand for its newly launched and acquired products grows.

Pfizer also Tuesday announced that Cecile Guegan, who has held various finance leadership roles at the company over the last two decades, stepped in as chief financial officer this month. She had served as interim CFO since August, when Pfizer's former CFO, Dave Denton, stepped down to take the same position at Nike.

The company is now expecting full-year revenue to total $62 billion to $64 billion, which compares with a previous outlook of $60.5 billion to $62.5 billion. The low end of that sales range would be roughly flat or down slightly compared with 2025 revenue of $62.6 billion. Still, Pfizer cited its "strong year-to-date performance" as a driver for the guidance hike.

The pharmaceutical giant now expects full-year adjusted profit of between $2.95 and $3.05 per share. That compares with previous guidance of between $2.80 and $3 per share.

That includes a one-time $650 million charge, or a negative impact of 10 cents per share, recorded in the third quarter tied to the company's licensing and collaboration agreement with Chinese cancer drugmaker Innovent Biologics.

Pfizer is working on a comeback story after its massive pandemic-era success waned. As demand for Covid products drops and generic rivals threaten its biggest moneymakers, CEO Albert Bourla is looking to jump-start growth through strategic acquisitions, fresh cancer drugs and a big bet on weight loss treatments through its acquisition of Metsera.

Pfizer in August expanded its multiyear cost-cutting efforts to help offset sharp declines in Covid product sales, and is now targeting roughly $9.7 billion in net savings through 2029.

Apple stock sinks as global memory crisis hurts profits, giving Ternus his first big test

📅 2026-11-03  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

John Ternus' first earnings report as Apple CEO was going fine until it was time to issue a forecast for the company's gross margin, one of the key metrics followed by investors.

Apple reported better-than-expected earnings and revenue on Monday, including in the company's core iPhone unit, and even projected sales in the current period that topped estimates. But just before Ternus opened the earnings call up to analysts' questions, CFO Kevan Parekh came forward with the first real piece of bad news.

Parekh said that for the December quarter, which goes through the holiday season, Apple's gross margin will be between 45% and 46%, trailing the 46.3% average analyst estimate, according to LSEG.

"This includes primarily the impact of higher memory costs, as well as some costs related to the ramp of Siri AI," Parekh said.

Apple shares slid 3% in morning trading on Tuesday.

It's part of an ongoing trend for Apple, which is absorbing some of the soaring costs from the global memory shortage. A margin drop in the fiscal first quarter would be a third straight decline for Apple, following a gross margin of 50.1% in the June period and 47.9% in the September quarter, boosted by 1 percentage point of tariff refunds.

At the midpoint of the forecast range, it would be Apple's lowest gross margin since the fiscal fourth quarter of 2023.

"What we're seeing with memory pricing right now is unprecedented," Ternus said on the call, adding that the company is paying more than it did earlier this year. "Looking beyond the December quarter, we see the pricing for memory purchases continuing to increase."

Ternus, who took the reins from Tim Cook on Sept. 1, is grappling with a problem that his predecessor spent his final months addressing. Cook told The Wall Street Journal in June that the memory situation was a "hundred-year flood." Apple followed by raising prices across the board on Macs, iPads and accessories.

In September, Apple increased the prices of its iPhones by $100, which Ternus said the company did "reluctantly."

Computer and smartphone makers everywhere are suffering. On Friday, Intel had its worst day on the market since the 1970s due to a weak forecast tied to the softening PC market. It's all because of insatiable artificial intelligence demand.

AI server giants like Nvidia and hyperscalers are buying up all the RAM they can find, and they're willing to pay a premium for it, leaving little supply for companies that make smartphones and tablets.

Despite its size and global clout, Apple said it expects supply constraints on iPhones and Macs in the holiday quarter, primarily driven by memory.

On the call, Ternus had some top lieutenants field component-related questions. Sabih Khan, chief operating officer, became the first Apple COO to appear on an earnings call since Cook in 2010, when Steve Jobs was CEO.

"As we have said before, we're seeing less flexibility in the supply chain than normal," Khan told analysts on the call.

In response, Apple has drastically increased its purchase obligations to secure supply, Khan said. Those obligations rose by $33 billion from the June quarter to the September period, Khan said, though he declined to answer questions about whether Apple had signed "long-term agreements" with memory makers.

"We're always working hard to improve supply," Khan said.

Even with the recent margin slide, Apple's gross margins are still well above where they sat earlier in the Cook era, when the number would predictably come in between 38% and 39%. As Apple's profitable services business grew, its gross margin expanded, hitting 40% in 2021 and growing from there.

Analysts see a potential silver lining for Apple in the current market turmoil. Because Apple is more capable of eating a portion of the increased costs, some experts predict the company will take market share from makers of Windows PC and Android phones that don't have such a cushion.

Citing data from a third-party researcher, Ternus said gains are already happening.

"According to IDC, we gained share globally for the quarter and for the full fiscal year," Ternus said. "And we have never sold more Macs in a fiscal year than we did this time, which is a truly remarkable testament to these incredible products."

Measles update: New York reports first measles-associated death; US nears 5,000 confirmed cases" style="max-width:100%; height:auto; border-radius:8px;" loading="lazy">

The New York State Department of Health on Monday afternoon reported its first measles-associated death in 30 years.

The state has counted 210 total cases this year as of Nov. 3. The reported death was a child in the northern Lewis County, according to the department. No information was given about the child's specific illness or the role measles infection may have played in their death.

Sixteen confirmed cases of measles have been reported in Lewis County as of Tuesday, the department said. Many of New York's cases are located among upstate counties, and just eight are in New York City, according to the department.

In a statement, New York Governor Kathy Hochul—who on Oct. 5 declared a Disaster Emergency in response to rising measles case rates—expressed her sympathies to the child's family and told residents that "vaccination remains our best protection against this dangerous disease."

"This is New York’s first measles-related death in 30 years, a devastating reminder of what’s at stake when Washington deliberately erodes decades of progress in protecting Americans from preventable illnesses," the statement reads, echoing criticisms of federal health leadership voiced by Pennsylvania Governor Josh Shapiro regarding measles-associated deaths in his own state.

As of Oct. 29, the Centers for Disease Control reports 4,852 confirmed cases of measles this year across the country, far outpacing 2025's total of 2,289. Of these, 511 have been hospitalized (versus 244 in 2025) and three deaths have been recognized—though health officials in Pennsylvania alone said they have confirmed five measles-associated deaths. The CDC has said its death count may change after it completes work to develop a new standardized case definition for "deaths due to measles."

A spokesperson for the Department of Health and Human Services, in a statement to Fierce Healthcare, said it had deployed its epidemiologists and other experts to New York after receiving a formal request for support. The support is currently scheduled to run through Nov. 13, and includes on-the-ground "surveillance, data analysis, strategic planning and other outbreak response support," they said.

Of the roughly $100 million of additional measles and vaccine-preventable diseases funding the department said it has provided via the CDC since August, nearly $2.4 million has gone directly to New York State and $2.1 million to New York City, they said.

"Secretary Kennedy and CDC Director Dr. Erica Schwartz continue to make clear that the MMR vaccine is the most effective way to prevent measles," they added.

The U.S. is in the midst of its worst measles outbreak in decades, with more than 4,000 cases reported to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention as of early October and a sixth measles-associated death disclosed by local Pennsylvania officials on Monday.

The vaccine-preventable disease was declared eliminated in the U.S. back in 2000—meaning that there was no endemic spread of the disease—but has resurfaced in force during recent years as declining childhood vaccination rates have pushed most states below the 95% coverage bar for school-age herd immunity.

The CDC had counted 2,289 cases across the entirety of 2025, which had been the country’s highest total since 1990, and several public health officials have warned for months that number would likely be surpassed based on the scale and frequency of early-year cases.

Measles-associated deaths have also accompanied the higher case counts, though just how many is a subject of dispute. While three deaths were confirmed in all of 2025, this year logged its first in late August with Pennsylvania, which hosts more than a quarter of the total cases, now reporting six measles-associated deaths as of Oct. 13. The CDC, however, lists two deaths alongside a note that its count is subject to change, and has promised more broadly to develop a new, standardized case definition to be used across all reporting states.

That discrepancy is an example of friction between federal health leadership and the broader public health community that experts say has contributed to this year’s outbreaks.

Pennsylvania’s initial death announcements in August were contested by Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr., who accused Pennsylvania Governor Josh Shapiro of withholding sufficient information for the CDC to confirm the deaths and using his press conference announcing them as “gaslighting the public” for political purposes.

During an ensuing social media feud, the governor and the secretary described a heated phone call in which Shapiro (at the time) rejected federal assistance and accused Kennedy of fueling the outbreaks with only lukewarm endorsement of the MMR vaccine and a broader concurrent campaign to scale back other routine childhood vaccinations.

Numerous state public health officials and experts have lobbed similar criticisms of Kennedy, who is affiliated with anti-vaccine groups, and federal health agencies under his purview.

The CDC, for its part, has reportedly infused at least $100 million of extra public health funding to states for immunization programs and surveillance, and leading officials including Kennedy and new CDC Director Erica Schwartz, M.D., have described the MMR vaccine as safe and effective when asked directly. Still, the agency has not launched the type of broader vaccine messaging and awareness campaigns that it would have in years past, critics are quick to note.

Pennsylvania isn’t alone in raising alarms over rising measles cases. New York Governor Kathy Hochul declared a State Disaster Emergency in early October, the first such declaration in recent years.

Roli Has Fallen Apart—and Left Customers Holding the Bag

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Roli, the company behind expressive, touch-sensitive music devices like the Seaboard and Roli Blocks, has collapsed.

Although it hasn't been officially announced, Roli reportedly ousted its CEO and founder, Roland Lamb, earlier this year. Since then, Roli has all but dissolved. (In August, a new company called Instrumental Intelligence was registered with the UK government under Lamb's name, a potential move by the founder to acquire Roli's assets).

On Monday, Roli emailed users that two licensed insolvency practitioners were appointed joint administrators of Roli on October 20. The company went into administration, the UK process that manages corporate insolvency. This is the second time Roli has gone bankrupt.

That has left a small but bereft community of customers with no word on getting the products they paid for. The Roli community has expressed sadness, anger, or outright confusion. In Facebook groups, the r/Roli subreddit, and other forums, dismayed users have struggled to find recourse for Roli equipment purchases ranging from hundreds to thousands of dollars. The Roli subreddit has been awash with posts from customers who say they paid for orders months or years ago and have received no response or refund since then.

Existing owners also worry about the long-term impact. Roli instruments rely heavily on proprietary software launchers and companion apps to connect, map sounds, and configure settings. While the hardware still functions, users fear that lack of software support will eventually leave their gear bricked.

Roli has not publicly communicated about its future strategy with customers. The company did not respond to WIRED’s request for comment; Lamb did not respond to multiple requests for comment. Roli’s primary financial backers, investment firms TriplePoint Capital and Hoxton Ventures, also did not respond.

Roli has faced financial struggles before.

The company started in 2009 but really took off after musician Pharrell Williams invested in 2017 and Sony’s Innovation Fund in 2018. Musicians like Dream Theater keyboardist Jordan Rudess supported and touted Roli’s gadgets. Ryan Gosling’s character played a Seaboard Grand in the 2016 movie La La Land. In the late 2010s, the tech and the company were hailed as a hit.

That tune changed in 2020. The British government’s Future Fund—a taxpayer-funded relief package issued to help businesses during the pandemic—partially bailed out Roli from a Covid-19-induced financial crisis. Even then, Roli went into administration for the first time in 2021. It survived by relaunching as Luminary Roli and focusing on music-learning devices like the light-up Lumi keyboard and the Airwave, a digital theremin that doubled as an AI piano teacher.

Through all of that, customers have had issues with how the company communicates. Users who ordered the Lumi keyboard found orders unexpectedly delayed, with no indication from the company on what was happening.

“One of the problems they have had ever since I started with the Kickstarter is communication and explaining things—telling people what's going on,” Lluís Gerard, a YouTuber who has reviewed Roli products for years under the handle WasabiNoise, tells WIRED. “There's always been too much secrecy about things, or then you'd realize, oh, there are these delays.”

Former Roli employees—who spoke with WIRED but asked not to be named to avoid jeopardizing future employment opportunities—alleged a company culture that revolved around Lamb’s whims. Every person we spoke with who worked with Lamb compared him to a Steve Jobs-esque character. One person described the experience of being at Roli as being in a movie, where Lamb saw himself as the main character and everyone else as extras or set pieces.

“I love the company, love the culture, love the people—I didn’t love working for him,” the employee said. “Everyone has their story with him. You're buckled into his car—and he’s not going in the right direction.”

In 2021, another former Roli employee, who also asked not to be identified, uploaded files on GitHub that include various notes chronicling Roli as a hostile workplace. One 16-page PDF is titled “Keeping Roland Happy” and includes multiple sections about how to handle interacting with a pathological narcissist. Several former employees independently described what they viewed as Lamb’s narcissistic behavior, describing him as an arrogant visionary.

“Roland is the archetype of a pathological narcissist,” one employee told WIRED. “Unfortunately, that's what he became. Incredibly brittle when challenged, absolutely shameless, quite reckless, incapable of learning or growth, would rather gamble than take any lessons from the previous experience. It was very disheartening to see.”

“The tragedy here is that it was always fundamentally a great company,” another former employee says. “A lot of what made it great was the sort of all-powerful vision of the founder. But that was so obviously the critical flaw.”

Along with other touch-sensitive devices like the Linnstrument and the Sensel Morph, Roli was a key company in the rise of MIDI polyphonic expression (MPE) devices. MPE technology lets players control a note’s pitch and timbre through touch-based movement. Pressing or sliding fingers over the device’s surface produces varying MIDI notes that capture more expressive playing.

While inventive, Roli’s instruments were altogether very niche—a hard product category to build an Apple-sized audience around. Roli certainly tried, partnering with Apple to sell its Roli Blocks in Apple Stores. Alas, the pandemic caused dire supply chain issues, and the company couldn’t keep up with orders. After the pandemic, bankruptcy, and the rebrand, little remained to push the company forward except the founder's will.

“This was one of the leading lights of music technology in the world,” one former employee said. “It was truly an inventing company—the stuff it made had never been made before. So it's a real shame.”

The response to Roli’s collapse hasn’t been pure dismay. After months of delays and frustration, Reddit users have gathered to mourn the company—and to make sure its devices keep working.

Sam Hocking, a UK audio engineer who runs an audio tools company called Demix.me, also owns multiple Roli Seaboard Blocks. Even before Roli went into administration, Hocking had been building software that could keep the equipment working as a capable MIDI device. He shared his vibe-coded configuration tools in the Roli subreddit and on GitHub, so Roli’s Grand, Rise, Airwave, and Block devices can work without the Roli launcher.

Hocking says his Seaboards are nearly 10 years old and still working. Despite the company’s rocky history and poor communication, he still wants to keep his devices. They’re high quality, cost a good chunk of money, and feel somewhat irreplaceable.

He’s not alone. Other users on the subreddit have joined in, adding their own tweaks and fixes to the software that may keep at least a few of these devices alive.

“They're quite a unique product; there's not another keyboard type out there,” Hocking says. “Nobody likes to spend money and then not be able to use it.”

GLP-1s May Help Lower Diabetes, Heart Risks Better Than Standard Prevention

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Follow Healthline on our socials for all things health.

GLP-1 drugs are more effective in helping prevent type 2 diabetes than the commonly prescribed medication metformin, according to a new study.

Researchers said GLP-1s such as Ozempic and Mounjaro were more likely to reduce blood sugar to healthy levels in people with prediabetes and obesity.

They added that people prescribed GLP-1 medications had a lower risk of progressing from prediabetes to type 2 diabetes.

The researchers noted that GLP-1 users also had a lower risk of death from any cause as well as fewer heart attacks, strokes, and heart failure events compared with metformin users.

“The great news with prediabetes is that it’s still possible to reverse the progression of the disease,” said Vinicius de Sousa Barzon Serra, MD, a study author and research fellow in cardiovascular medicine at the Mayo Clinic in Scottsdale, Arizona, in a press release. “This research may add to the case for considering GLP-1 medications earlier for certain patients, rather than waiting until they develop type 2 diabetes.”

Serra noted that metformin still remains an effective treatment for people with prediabetes and type 2 diabetes.

“This is less about replacing one medication with another and more about discussing additional options with specific patients,” he said.

The researchers presented their findings at the American Heart Association Scientific Sessions 2026 in Chicago. The research has not been published yet in a peer-reviewed journal.

David Cutler, MD, a family medicine physician at Providence Saint John’s Health Center in Santa Monica, CA, wasn’t involved in the study, but said the findings were “quite impressive.”

“It elevates the comfort of prescribing GLP-1 drugs as a front-line treatment,” he told Healthline.

Robert Eckel, MD, a former president of the American Heart Association who was likewise not involved in the study, agrees the research provides more options for patients and prescribers.

“GLP-1s provide another option for prevention, perhaps as an add-on therapy. However, costs and access to these medications are important factors,” said Eckel, an endocrinologist at the University of Colorado School of Medicine, in the press release.

For their study, the researchers analyzed the medical records of 256,000 adults in the United States with prediabetes and obesity. About 76% of the subjects were women, and the average age of the participants was 52.

About half of those studied were taking GLP-1 drugs with the active ingredient semaglutide, tirzepatide, liraglutide, or dulaglutide.

The other half had been prescribed metformin, a medication used to help manage blood sugar levels in adults and some children with type 2 diabetes by decreasing glucose production in the liver, reducing glucose absorption from food, and improving the body’s insulin sensitivity.

The researchers reported that the people in the study who were taking GLP-1 drugs had a 1.5 times higher chance of reversing high blood sugar to healthy levels compared to people who were using metformin.

The researchers also noted that GLP-1 medication users had a 31% lower risk of developing type 2 diabetes and a 41% lower risk of death from any cause.

They added that GLP-1 medications were associated with 15% fewer heart attacks, 20% fewer strokes, 25% fewer cases of heart failure, and 8% fewer cases of atrial fibrillation (AFib), a common type of irregular heartbeat.

The researchers cautioned that their study is observational and, therefore, cannot prove a cause-and-effect relationship. They also noted they relied on prescription records, so the frequency and dosage at which patients took their medication was not reflected.

In addition, the GLP-1 drugs were grouped together, so individual differences between medications were not considered.

Eckel said in the news release that the benefits for people taking GLP-1 drugs were probably mainly due to weight loss. Eckel noted that the research results open up a wide range of possibilities.

“This is an important finding because the GLP-1 receptor agonists are commonly used today and this is a population that in the transition from prediabetes to diabetes are at increasing risk for cardiovascular disease,” Eckel said in a taped interview alongside the study findings.

Cutler pointed out that several GLP-1 drugs also target blood sugar levels and insulin production. They could be effective in people with prediabetes who don’t have weight management issues.

Cutler noted that these people, however, might be reluctant to take GLP-1 drugs because they may not be experiencing symptoms of their prediabetes but could develop side effects with the medications.

“They would not be getting any immediate benefits,” Cutler said. He added that metformin, which was first used clinically in 1958, remains an effective alternative. “It’s been proven safe, effective, and it’s very cheap,” Cutler said.

It’s estimated that 40 million people in the United States have diabetes, with more than 90% of those cases being type 2 diabetes, a chronic condition characterized by high blood glucose levels that results from the body’s ineffective response to insulin or insufficient insulin production.

It’s also estimated that 115 million adults in the United States have prediabetes, a condition in which a person has higher-than-normal blood sugar levels but not high enough to be diagnosed as type 2 diabetes.

Around 5–10% of people with prediabetes develop diabetes every year. More broadly, up to 70% of people with prediabetes eventually develop diabetes.

Medication is sometimes prescribed for people with type 2 diabetes and prediabetes. Lifestyle changes can help reverse type 2 diabetes and prediabetes, as well as prevent the progression from prediabetes to type 2 diabetes.

Cutler said the lifestyle changes needed to prevent type 2 diabetes are pretty clear.

“The most important thing for people with prediabetes to know is they should lose weight, reduce their carbs, exercise daily and, if that doesn’t work, then consider medication,” he said.

CFTC Opens U.S. Stock Market to Equity Perpetual Futures

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Perpetual futures on individual stocks can begin trading on U.S.-regulated exchanges for the first time, the Commodity Futures Trading Commission said Tuesday, extending a product that was confined to offshore crypto venues a year ago into the equity market.

In a statement, CFTC Chairman Michael S. Selig said the contracts, which he called “Equity Perps,” were deemed approved under Commission Regulation 40.3 as security futures products.

They may list on exchanges that hold a CFTC designated contract market registration and a notice registration with the Securities and Exchange Commission as a national securities exchange for security futures.

“This expansion marks a significant step towards allowing U.S. market participants, consistent with applicable law, to avail themselves of the advantages of perpetual futures,” Selig said. He said bringing the products inside the agency’s framework means Americans no longer need to “turn to offshore, and often unregulated, venues.”

The contracts fall under the Commodity Exchange Act, federal securities law and the rulebooks of both agencies. The statement names no exchange, lists no contracts and sets no launch date.

Those details are expected to come from the designated contract markets themselves.

Perpetual futures have no expiration date. Instead of settling on a fixed day, longs and shorts exchange periodic funding payments that keep the contract price tied to spot. The structure has dominated crypto derivatives for years, with most volume on platforms outside U.S. jurisdiction.

The CFTC approved the first U.S.-listed bitcoin perpetual, Kalshi’s BTCPERP, on May 29, and Kalshi went live with the product soon after. The same day, staff granted no-action relief that lets Coinbase Financial Markets route U.S. clients into offshore perp and options liquidity. Kalshi added gold and silver perpetuals in September.

In his statement Tuesday, Selig described the equity contracts as an extension of that May decision “beyond digital commodities and into traditional securities.”

The legal basis is older. The Commodity Futures Modernization Act of 2000 created a joint CFTC-SEC regime for security futures products, and the two agencies signed a memorandum of understanding on March 11 to coordinate rulemaking and enforcement.

Selig cited both, and repeated SEC Chairman Paul S. Atkins’s description of security futures as stranded in a regulatory “no man’s land” for lack of harmonization. The agencies had set out a joint crypto asset taxonomy earlier in the year.

Not everyone accepts the CFTC’s reading. CME Grou  sued the agency in June over the Kalshi approval, arguing that a contract built on exchanged funding payments is a swap under Dodd-Frank, not a future.

That case is pending. An equity version adds the SEC’s jurisdiction to the mix and may draw a new set of objections from securities-side participants.

The approval lands five days after the largest theft in crypto’s history. On Oct. 29, attackers that TRM Labs and Chainalysis link to North Korea’s TraderTraitor group drained about $6.3 billion from Binance’s hot wallets in roughly 20 minutes.

Binance reopened withdrawals on Sunday and says it has replenished about 95% of affected assets.

Selig used the breach to press the case he made in Tuesday’s statement.

“The real issue here is that these are unregulated offshore exchanges, and they don’t have the same sorts of safeguards that we have in our financial markets here in the United States,” he told CNBC on Oct. 30.

He said the hack “proves our exact point” about the agency’s October proposal, an advance notice of rulemaking that would create a voluntary federal registration path for crypto exchanges with cybersecurity, proof-of-reserves and custody requirements. Selig has said the CFTC cannot make that registration mandatory without Congress.

The equity perp approval works from the same premise. Perpetuals built the offshore derivatives business; moving them onto dual-registered U.S. exchanges puts the margin, clearing and surveillance rules of two federal agencies around them.

Selig framed it as a choice between regulated access and “being forced to turn to offshore, and often unregulated, venues.”

Portable Business Ontologies for AI Agents

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The term “ontology” is now en vogue. Why? Agents, and the LLMs that power them, need relevant business context.

Before you buy into an ontology, ask the vendor: “When the ontology gets our business wrong, how do our people correct it—and what still depends on you?” The answer will tell you a great deal about whose model of the business you are buying into. It also points to the larger stakes: your ability to improve that model as the business changes, and to keep that investment useful if you change platforms.

It helps to distinguish an ontology from a knowledge graph, even though the two often overlap.

Ontology: What kinds of things exist, what relationships mean, and what rules apply. Equipment, components, teams, roles, processes. For example, an ontology can define an “Equipment” type and a “Contains” relationship to a “Component”. A knowledge graph can then assert, “Pump P-101 contains Seal S-7”: two specific entities and their relationship.

Imagine you tell an agent, “Compute same-store sales growth, but show me a side-by-side where we show all stores, then another where we exclude stores open less than 2 years; then break both sides of that up into domestic, international, and combined.”

That ask depends on details an agent cannot guess:

A policy might exclude stores until they have been open a year; the requested two-year group adds a further filter. Even then, do you compare calendar months as soon as a store qualifies, or include it only after a full prior fiscal year?

The ontology needs to connect those stores to the policy that governs the calculation. Finding related facts helps; applying the right rule is what makes the answer useful.

An agent can use relationships in the graph to retrieve relevant context before answering a question. And no wonder they are useful, when knowledge domains can easily get very complex.

But extraction can also encode mistakes. Teams may use different names for the same entity, creating duplicates. Sources can disagree or become outdated. Even a clean source can be misread by an LLM. Making those mistakes look authoritative does not make them less dangerous.

Those risks grow when a model of the business becomes a system that acts on it. A wrong relationship can then trigger a wrong action, not just a wrong answer.

First, be wary of inflexible models. In the name of making something “batteries included,” a vendor may make its own entities first-class citizens while limiting what yours can do. Customization is not enough if custom types lose search, validation, or action capabilities.

Second, look for a path from discovery to governance. A tool should let you bootstrap a graph from what an LLM sees, then validate it against explicit constraints. You should be able to escalate exceptions to people and preserve exploratory knowledge without treating it as approved business data.

The largest risk is tying business processes to a platform you cannot leave. Your ontology represents your business: its knowledge, processes, and relationships. That investment in curation and business logic should stay useful even if you change tools. Why should a vendor get to put that under lock and key?

As knowledge graphs grow very large, it can be worth choosing a tool or vendor that supports graph embeddings. These are close cousins of the semantic embeddings that power many RAG applications. There are two useful benefits to consider.

First, a few hops through a densely connected graph can yield too many entities to process. By learning numerical representations of entities and relationships, embeddings offer another way to find relevant parts of the graph. For the right task, this can be more efficient than walking the graph.

Second, embedding models can rank candidate missing relationships. The same learning can reveal patterns in the attributes and relationships typical of an entity type, helping to build a more complete graph.

To own your own destiny, avoid lock-in, and model your actual business instead of a vendor-dictated caricature of it, how do you begin without an army of forward-deployed engineers? This is where I think modern AI shines, if the tooling supports a path from discovery to verification.

First, use automated discovery and extraction. From business documents, policies, email, or Slack messages, you can construct an initial view of a domain. The critical part is that you cannot trust it as accurate until it is verified.

Verification is the second part. Your tools must give you a smooth path from extracted candidates to claims grounded in sources, and to knowledge validated by a responsible person or authoritative system. Preserve each claim’s source.

Freshness is ongoing work. The process for handling a trouble ticket, or the answer to “how many stores are there in New Jersey”, may not be the same today as it was three years ago. But updating today’s knowledge must not erase yesterday’s reality. When a fact was recorded and when it applied can differ; a useful ontology needs to support both current answers and an accurate historical view. Someone still must keep that knowledge current.

The scope can be massive. Start with a smaller corpus, map a useful domain, and build toward a comprehensive picture while creating value along the way.

Customer support is one useful slice: inbound calls, emails, a ticketing system, automated resolutions, a support team that can respond to customers, and a path to escalate true bugs to engineering. It connects knowledge to action and exposes the same risks and rewards in a manageable domain.

ServiceNow’s Common Service Data Model illustrates the constraint. Its CSDM 5 white paper warns, “Without this data in prescribed tables, you may not receive the full value of the ServiceNow AI Platform.” The warning itself creates a design and operational constraint: lean into the vendor’s model or accept a risk. What if that means cramming your round peg into their square hole?

Now suppose an ontology platform also supplies ingestion, transformations, actions, and business rules. You may be able to export entities, attributes, and relationships. But can you also port transformations and action pipelines that execute changes in your databases, call webhooks, and create other side effects? Tightly coupled integrations can leave the business itself locked in.

Software is eating the world, and AI is eating software. As teams use AI both to build software and to power it, more business logic can become tied to these platforms. Each useful automation can also become another migration dependency. It’s dangerous to let a vendor stand between you and agility.

It’s an amazing time to be alive! Little wonder people are exploring ontologies to provide context to agents. Extending that context into processes and integrations can create real value. Come back to that opening question. Your people’s corrections should make the model a better reflection of your business. That accumulated understanding is too valuable to be stranded when you change platforms. It’s also a terrible time to be painted into a corner.

New Jersey Drops Coinbase Staking Order After Three Years

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

New Jersey’s securities regulator has rescinded its 2023 cease-and-desist order and $5 million penalty against Coinbase’s staking service.

New Jersey’s securities regulator has withdrawn the 2023 cease-and-desist order that barred Coinbase from offering staking to new customers in the state, ending a three-year standoff that cost residents an estimated $15 million in forgone rewards.

The Bureau of Securities notice, dated Oct. 30 and signed by Acting Bureau Chief Keith A. Alt, rescinds the order and the $5 million penalty that came with it. The withdrawal is without prejudice and without a ruling on the merits.

Coinbase disclosed the development in its third-quarter report filed with the Securities and Exchange Commission, which states that the regulator informed the company of its intent to rescind on Oct. 29.

Coinbase CEO Brian Armstrong said New Jersey users would regain access to staking “very soon.”

As of Monday, the exchange’s eligibility page continued to list New Jersey among the states where customers cannot stake new principal, and no reopening date has been announced.

The order traces to June 6, 2023, when the New Jersey bureau joined securities regulators in nine other states — Alabama, California, Illinois, Kentucky, Maryland, South Carolina, Vermont, Washington and Wisconsin — in a coordinated action against Coinbase’s staking program.

The states alleged the service amounted to an unregistered securities offering under state law.

California led the task force; its action covered more than 600,000 accounts. In July 2023, Coinbase agreed with California, New Jersey, South Carolina and Wisconsin to block customers in those states from staking new funds pending final adjudication. Maryland reached a similar agreement in October 2023.

Customers who had staked before the orders kept earning rewards, but could not add principal or compound the rewards they received.

The state cases ran parallel to the SEC’s June 2023 lawsuit against Coinbase, which named staking among its claims. The SEC dismissed that case in February 2025. Alabama, Kentucky, Illinois, South Carolina and Vermont dismissed, vacated or withdrew their own actions in March and April 2025. New Jersey is the sixth of the original ten states to step back.

Rescinding the order ends the restriction on new staking in New Jersey. It does not resolve the underlying legal question. The bureau made no finding on whether Coinbase’s staking program is a security, and the “without prejudice” language preserves its ability to file a new action. Coinbase’s own filing calls the state matters “preliminary” and says an adverse resolution “could have a material impact” on the business.

The filing describes a planned rescission rather than a completed one, which explains the gap between Armstrong’s statement and the unchanged eligibility page. The change for New Jersey residents arrives when Coinbase updates its systems, not when the notice was signed.

Coinbase has framed the state restrictions as a competitive disadvantage. In an April 2025 post, the company’s Ryan VanGrack argued the orders singled out one provider while residents could stake through other platforms. Coinbase put forgone rewards across the four restricted states at more than $130 million as of October 2025.

Four states remain. California, Maryland and Wisconsin continue to bar new staking on Coinbase while their cases proceed; Washington permits staking but is pursuing a $4 million fine. Coinbase estimates California residents have missed more than $89 million in rewards since 2023.

The decision lands as federal policy has shifted but not settled. The SEC has withdrawn from the registration-based cases it brought in 2023, and the CLARITY market structure bill passed the House in 2025 but stalled in the Senate. State securities regulators are not bound by the SEC’s enforcement priorities, which is why the staking cases outlived the federal one by more than a year and a half.

A future SEC, or a future bureau chief in Trenton, could revisit the question.

Google Expands Guided Learning and Plans Opt-In K-12 AI Access

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Google announced on November 3, 2026 that Guided Learning is globally available in Google Classroom and will be piloted in Search at select U.S. schools later in November 2026, alongside plans to make several AI products opt-in for K-12 student accounts.

Chris Phillips, Google’s vice president and general manager of education, introduced the updates around a design principle Google calls “productive struggle”: keeping students actively challenged without becoming so overwhelmed that they give up. Guided Learning first arrived in Gemini on August 6, 2025, using probing and open-ended questions, step-by-step explanations and multimodal responses such as diagrams, videos and interactive quizzes.

In Classroom, a teacher assigns the activity and the AI acts as a one-to-one study tool anchored in class material. Google’s education report says the activity can be grounded in teacher-uploaded materials. Focus Mode keeps students on task and redirects the conversation toward teacher-specified learning objectives if they try to move off topic. Teachers receive real-time class-level and individual insights, including full chat transcripts, to identify where students need additional support. Google tested the Classroom feature with schools and educators before launch and incorporated their feedback.

For student accounts in Search, certain homework-help requests will trigger step-by-step prompts rather than a direct path to a final answer. Google’s examples include a pasted physics problem and a request for help writing a book report. The pilot will gather educator feedback before the feature expands to other school districts in the coming months.

Access to Gemini, Gemini Notebook, Gemini in Classroom and Google Search will be entirely off for K-12 student accounts until a school administrator explicitly turns each product on. The change is not yet fully in force: Google will share more specifics with schools in the coming months before the controls roll out, giving administrators time to prepare. The revised approach follows feedback from schools, teachers, researchers and policymakers and is intended to let school leaders decide how AI products are used in their classrooms.

For its core Workspace for Education services, Google states that schools own their data and that student data is not sold, used for advertising or used to train generative AI models.

A preregistered randomized trial in Sierra Leone conducted by Google’s LearnLM team and Fab AI enrolled 1,763 students aged 13 or older across 48 Grade 7 and 8 mathematics classrooms in 12 government-supported schools. Classrooms were randomized between Guided Learning and standard instruction, and teachers in the Guided Learning classrooms were asked to provide 12 hours of use. The researchers reported an intent-to-treat mathematics effect of 0.258 standard deviations and likened it to a median student moving from the 50th percentile at baseline to the 60th at endline; 69% of students met the requested usage threshold.

Teachers defined objectives and starter prompts, organized students to work in pairs and led discussions to consolidate learning. The researchers described that teacher-led structure as essential to the intervention and found that students who entered with stronger mathematics skills benefited more, leaving an explicit challenge to improve outcomes for students who start behind.

A separate 10-week UK trial with 1,179 students tested Gemini-powered tutoring on Eedi. Expert tutors reviewed every AI-drafted message before delivery. The researchers reported that tutors approved 94.7% of the 94,385 reviewed drafts without changes and that supervised AI tutoring matched expert human tutoring on proximate and intermediate measures while substantially outperforming standard remediation.

The broad standardized Star Maths assessment produced a different result: students showed similar gains after AI tutoring or standard remediation, with no clear overall advantage for tutoring. The researchers linked that discrepancy partly to tutoring conversations reducing curriculum coverage and said longer studies should examine when immediate gains become broader assessment improvements.

The expansion therefore makes implementation as consequential as access. The Classroom design combines teacher-uploaded materials, teacher-specified objectives and access to chat transcripts, while the forthcoming settings place activation decisions with administrators. The trials produced gains on some learning measures, but they also left broad-test gains, curriculum coverage and benefits for lower-attaining students unresolved. The central open question is whether those measured benefits will carry into wider school use without repeating the unequal gains and reduced coverage documented in the research.
