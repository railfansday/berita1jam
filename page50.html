Not Micron, Not Sandisk. This Is My Top Memory Stock for 2027.

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

SK Hynix is bigger than Micron and Sandisk in the DRAM and NAND flash markets, which is why it has the potential to outperform them next year.

By Harsh Chauhan – Nov 18, 2026 at 2:01PM EST

Micron Technology and Sandisk have sizzled on the stock market in 2026. Both stocks have delivered impressive gains this year, despite witnessing a significant pullback in recent months amid the broader correction in tech stocks.

Micron stock, for instance, has gained 128% in 2026, as of this writing. Sandisk's gains are even bigger at 194%. Importantly, both memory companies can step on the gas once again in 2027, driven by the artificial intelligence (AI)-fueled demand for compute and storage.

However, I think that Micron and Sandisk could be overshadowed by South Korean memory giant SK Hynix (SKHY -1.23%) next year. Let's look at the reasons why.

SK Hynix sells both dynamic random-access memory (DRAM) and NAND flash memory. Counterpoint Research points out that the company controlled 29% of the DRAM market in the third quarter of 2026. It is second only to Samsung in this market. Micron, for comparison, has a 21% share of the DRAM market.

Meanwhile, SK Hynix's 19% share of NAND flash puts it ahead of both Micron and Sandisk, which have market shares of 15% and 11%, respectively. The South Korean company's healthy market shares in both DRAM and NAND flash explain why it reported phenomenal growth in Q3. SK Hynix's revenue surged 328% year over year to $79 billion, easily beating the consensus estimate.

The company's operating profit jumped 632% from the year-ago period and exceeded analysts' expectations. SK Hynix is well-placed to sustain such impressive growth. That's because the company has already sold more than half of its production capacity for 2027 and 2028 under long-term agreements with 14 customers.

Moreover, SK Hynix anticipates that memory demand will outstrip supply through 2030, indicating that the favorable environment fueling the company's stunning growth is sustainable. Also, SK Hynix is ramping up production of its next-generation high-bandwidth memory (HBM) chips, known as HBM4.

It points out that HBM4 has twice the bandwidth of the previous-generation HBM3E chips and 40% higher power efficiency. SK Hynix notes that HBM4 revenue and shipments will surpass those of HBM3E in the current quarter, which should bode well for the company's margins. That's because HBM4 reportedly carries a 24% premium per gigabyte (GB) over the previous generation, HBM3E.

What's more, SK Hynix controls a third of the global HBM market, higher than Micron's 18% share. This is another reason to buy SK Hynix, considering that the global HBM market's revenue could surge to a whopping $641 billion in 2030, according to Bloomberg Intelligence. The firm has significantly upgraded this guidance from its earlier estimate of $260 billion, noting that the average price of HBM will more than double over the next four years.

So, there is a strong likelihood that SK Hynix's revenue growth will outpace Wall Street's expectations.

So, even though SK Hynix stock hasn't made a bright start on the U.S. stock market following its listing on the Nasdaq this year, it remains worth buying on account of its red-hot growth, cheap valuation, and the ability to outperform Micron and Sandisk. In the end, I won't be surprised to see this AI stock's fortunes turn around in the New Year, and buying it while it trades at cheap multiples seems prudent.

Fed officials expect more rate hikes, worry about AI impact on inflation, minutes show

📅 2026-11-18  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Federal Reserve officials expressed confidence in October that further interest rate increases will be needed to stem inflation, though they decided to hold off while gathering more information, according to meeting minutes released Wednesday.Policymakers put artificial intelligence at the core of their discussions, indicating that demand associated with the technology would be "a material source of inflation risk." More broadly, officials said "inflation pressures in the economy were broad based" and would warrant action, with "some" policymakers advocating for a quarter percentage point hike at the meeting. A couple participants even noted the possibility that a larger increase might be needed at the next policy meeting in December.The meeting summary said "almost all participants judged that, under their baseline outlooks for inflation and the labor market, further action to raise the target range for the federal funds rate … would likely be appropriate, in order to ensure a timely return of inflation to the 2 percent objective."In a CNBC interview Wednesday, Fed Governor Christopher Waller reiterated his belief that the Federal Open Market Committee should consider a half-point hike in December.

Officials at the meeting expressed a high level of concern over AI, both as a source of inflation and financial instability. Fed staff members who briefed participants noted "that off-balance-sheet leasing commitments and circular financing arrangements among AI-related firms could amplify losses if returns on AI investment were to disappoint.""Most participants observed that AI-related debt had grown rapidly and that much of this growth had taken place through complex and opaque financing structures," the minutes said. "A few participants remarked that AI-related exposures were becoming increasingly concentrated among a small number of firms."Officials said they still expect inflation to fall off sharply in 2027 and beyond as the impact from the current shocks wear off.Ultimately, the FOMC voted 10-2 to keep its benchmark overnight borrowing rate locked in a range between 3.75-4%. Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack dissented in favor of a quarter-point increase. Those voting in favor of the hold "pointed to benefits in accumulating additional information, which would help in determining the policy stance appropriate to ensure a timely return of inflation to the Committee's 2 percent goal."

Since the meeting, the inflation data has showed that the pace of price increases remains well above target.

The consumer price index in October rose 0.4% for all items and 0.3% for the core that excludes food and energy. Both readings were 0.1 percentage point higher than expected.

While indicating concern over how stubborn inflation has been, most Fed officials who have spoken publicly advocated a patient approach.

However, Waller said Wednesday that the Fed may have to "shock" markets by approving a half-point hike in December. He is the only central banker to espouse that belief publicly, though St. Louis Fed President Alberto Musalem said he anticipates as much as a full percentage point move higher by the spring of 2027. As governor, Waller is a permanent FOMC voter; Musalem will not vote until 2028.

Chicago Fed President Austan Goolsbee said Friday on CNBC that the central bank risks losing its credibility if it doesn't act.

At the core of policymakers' concern is whether rising prices related to the Iran war and President Donald Trump's tariffs, in many cases temporary events that wouldn't prompt a response, are instead becoming more entrenched.

Markets are fully pricing in a quarter percentage point hike at the Dec. 8-9 meeting that includes low odds of a half-point move, according to the CME Group's FedWatch gauge. Futures traders also are pricing a solid likelihood of two more increases in 2027.

Christopher Nolan Reveals Why Watching 'The Odyssey' at Home Is Better than IMAX

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The Oscar winning director explains how repeat viewings reveal new layers in his cast's performances and shares his home theater tips.

The Oscar winning director explains how repeat viewings reveal new layers in his cast's performances and shares his home theater tips.

Christopher Nolan has spent much of his career championing the theatrical experience. From shooting on IMAX film to encouraging audiences to see his movies on the biggest screens possible, the director has made no secret of his belief that movies are meant to be experienced in theaters.

But that doesn't mean Nolan thinks watching a movie at home is a lesser experience. In fact, the filmmaker believes home viewing offers some advantages that even the most impressive theater can't replicate. And with The Odyssey arriving on digital, 4K Ultra HD and Blu-ray on Nov. 17, he's encouraging audiences to take advantage of them.

Speaking with Polygon ahead of the film's home release, Nolan discussed how watching a movie in your living room can change the experience, particularly when it comes to appreciating the work of the actors.

"The fun thing about watching a film in the home is a different kind of concentration," Nolan tells Polygon. "When you go to a movie, you're in the dark, you're really focused for a couple hours. In the home, it's a different type of concentration because you can pause the movie, you can go get a snack, come back."

That freedom means viewers engage with a movie differently than they would during a theatrical screening.

"It does allow you to control the timeline and really look at the detail of things," he said. "And for me, it's really about the performances."

While The Odyssey features enormous ships, mythical creatures, and elaborate action sequences, Nolan believes the film's greatest rewards on repeat viewings may come from paying closer attention to its actors.

"The human element is what connects you with the story," Nolan says. "But I think the work of these actors, this cast, this incredible cast in the film, it rewards multiple viewings."

Nolan knows that better than most, having spent countless hours reviewing footage during the editing process. He pointed to the layers in performances by Matt Damon, Anne Hathaway, Tom Holland, Robert Pattinson, and Samantha Morton as details that audiences might appreciate more fully when watching again.

"There's subtleties and nuances in what they're doing that I, in the edit suite, get to watch again and again and again and sort of pick up on all the different things that Matt or Anne or Tom or Rob, what they're bringing to things, or Samantha Morton," Nolan says. "It's really fun for people to be able to look at that in the home and see all the different layers that these guys have put into the story."

When I mentioned that my wife and I enjoy discussing movies while watching them at home, something that isn't exactly encouraged in a theater, Nolan acknowledged the experience has its own appeal.

"It's a different form of engaging with the story, and it has strengths and it has weaknesses," he says. "Nice to be able to sit on your own couch and stretch out, you know what I mean? It's been really exciting over the course of my career to see the leaps forward in terms of technology, in terms of what you're able to do in the home."

Nolan also appreciates how much home-viewing technology has improved during his filmmaking career.

"It's been really exciting over the course of my career to see the leaps forward in terms of technology, in terms of what you're able to do in the home," he says.

And for anyone interested in bringing a little more of the theatrical experience into their living room, Nolan has a suggestion.

"I'm a big fan of projectors," he said. "You can get a relatively low-cost, pretty high-quality projector, darken your living room, maybe introduce some popcorn into the equation or whatever. Have a mini movie theater and do it on your own timeline. That's tremendous fun."

But Nolan doesn't believe a movie necessarily loses its theatrical identity once audiences begin watching it at home. In fact, he suggests that the excitement surrounding a major theatrical release can follow a film into people's living rooms.

"When you have a movie that's played, as The Odyssey has, at length in movie theaters, even viewed in the home, there's a kind of excitement that comes with an event like that," Nolan says. "Even if people are seeing it for the first time, I think the film sort of carries that with it."

Magic: The Gathering Designer Reveals the Biggest Problem With Marvel’s Infinity Stones

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

“Three of the six stones are about as blue as it comes.”

When Magic: The Gathering‘s Marvel’s Spider-Man set launched in 2025, the inclusion of The Soul Stone hinted at the scope of Wizards of the Coast’s partnership with Marvel. Including one of the six cosmically powerful gems as the set’s headliner card practically guaranteed the rest would eventually be released, a plan that was confirmed with the printing of The Mind Stone in the 2026 Marvel Super Heroes set. It’s a clever strategy, but one that Magic head game designer Mark Rosewater told Polygon demonstrates a problem with Universes Beyond design.

“We wanted to do something collectible, and we just said to ourselves, ‘In the Marvel property, is there anything that's cool as a collectible?’" Rosewater told Polygon at MagicCon: Atlanta 2026. “Well, the collection of six objects has been the cornerstone of multiple comic book series and the movies. The awareness of the Infinity Stones is so high because of the movies. They are just the obvious choice, so we went with them.”

Rosewater was part of the team that crafted the Infinity Stones as a way to connect the Marvel sets. The cards haven’t been finalized yet, giving Wizards flexibility to fit them into sets as they develop, but the colors and core utility have already been set. They’re all two-cost mana rocks that can be harnessed for a much higher cost to unlock a powerful persistent effect.

“A two-mana stone is just good enough that most decks probably can play that if they want,” Rosewater says. “We make the harnessed ability something that's gated so you don't get it right away, but when you do get it, it can be this pretty glorious thing.”

The hardest part was figuring out how to get six stones to fit into Magic’s five colors.

“One of the challenges of UB is we have to make something attached to our color pie that was not remotely designed for the color pie,” Rosewater says. “Three of the six stones are about as blue as it comes: the Mind Stone, the Space Stone, and the Time Stone. They can’t all be blue. The challenge was to divvy them up. They were close enough to their color matching the color of Magic that we finally decided let’s just make the green stone the green one. If you think of purple and yellow being black and white, we found most of them lined up. There are individual choices that might not be perfect, but each stone is the color that it is was the easiest and simplest way to explain it.”

The Mind Stone became white. The Time Stone will be green and be the headliner for next year's Darkhold Destiny, where its bearer, Doctor Strange, plays a key role in the story. The Space Stone will actually be blue when it appears. Rosewater says his team considered making those three stones blue to go with their thematic affinities, but decided against it.

“It caused a bunch of problems,” he says. “Now blue just has more access than anybody else. That means somebody doesn’t get it, so the people that love that color are yelling at us that everybody gets a two-drop except white. Why doesn’t white get it? For balance purposes and audience expectation purposes, we really, really wanted it to be a cycle.”

The team also considered having a bigger disparity between the core functionality of the cards.

“In the end, we really liked the idea that they’re mana stones,” Rosewater says. “They have a functionality in every deck. If you’re playing that color, you kind of want to play it. The extended effect might not be as effective in your deck, so maybe you don’t play it, but we wanted to make it so a lot of decks of that color would at least heavily consider it.”

There are six Infinity Stones and only five Magic colors. Rosewater says that’s not a problem.

“Magic can do six because we can do five colors and colorless, so Magic has a way to do six that’s built into the game,” he says.

While Wizards of the Coast has stopped short of confirming it outright, that implies we’re due for three more Marvel sets, each prominently featuring the Reality Stone, Power Stone, and Space Stone. The real question is whether Thanos might try to collect them all with the Infinity Gauntlet as some kind of culminating set-based event.

7 Window Treatment Fixes Designers Swear By

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Smart solutions for windows that are anything but standard.

You’d be hard-pressed to find a homeowner who complains about having too many windows, but not all windows are created equal. Maybe you’re saddled with a skinny one tucked in the corner of a bedroom, or an unusually shaped one in a bathroom. The right treatment can make even the most awkward window feel intentional—but the trick is knowing which solution works best for each situation.

Here, interior designers Paige Kelly and Lindsey Walsh share the window treatment fixes they turn to again and again, along with a few clever ideas you may not have considered.

If a room is short on vertical space, Kelly recommends mounting your curtain rod a few inches from the top of the wall, rather than right above the window. “Getting the drapery high and wide is going to make a room feel bigger,” she says.

Tall panels draw the eye up, but make sure they are long enough to skim the floor. “Otherwise, they look like high-water pants, and it just looks wrong,” warns Walsh.

TWOPAGES Liz Linen Pleated Drapes are a full-length option with a tailored pleat that gives the finished look a little extra polish.

Walsh points to “pattern drenching” as a way to make a space feel more cohesive and help soften the edges of an oddly placed window or render an awkward proportion less noticeable. When you carry the same pattern across the walls and window treatments, the window becomes part of a larger visual composition rather than a feature that stands out for the wrong reasons.

Walsh notes that TWOPAGES can work with paper-backed fabric to allow the same material to cover both the walls and windows. A wallpaper installer can apply the fabric to the walls, and a window treatment workroom will install it as drapery or shades, carefully aligning the pattern repeat so the two applications meet seamlessly.

Increasing the fullness of drapery panels can trick the eye so that windows appear more substantial. “You can make yours look wider by using the stack back required for a wider window,” says Walsh. “The panel fabric may just be covering a lot of the wall, but it will look like there’s a window there.”

If the window placement is part of the problem, Kelly suggests extending the curtain rod beyond the window and using a one-way draw. “Gathering a drapery stack to one side feels intentional, and can help balance an off-center window,” she says.

Combining shades and drapes lets you tailor the light and privacy to the room. In a bedroom, for instance, “you want a dual functioning system with a blackout layer and a sheer layer,” says Kelly. One blocks light for sleeping, while the other filters sunlight and provides privacy during the day. (For added convenience, opt for motorized blackout treatments that let you greet the morning sun with the touch of a button.)

In rooms where you don’t need full blackout, like kitchens and bathrooms, cafe curtains—which hang in the middle of a window along the check rail—are a great solution. “They cover your neighbor’s dirty yard but you still get to see the sky,” says Kelly. TWOPAGES also sells a variety of ready-to-hang cafe curtains, along with the necessary hardware in a gold or black finish.

Long panels aren’t always practical, particularly when a window sits above a radiator, cabinetry, or a piece of furniture. In those situations, Walsh often turns to roman shades. They’re a streamlined way to introduce fabric and softness without taking up wall space.

For a more decorative look, Walsh loves adding a lambrequin, a fixed fabric-covered frame around the window, with a functional shade tucked beneath it. “It’s a great fix when the interior architecture gets in the way of having the kind of window treatment you want,” she says.

TWOPAGES carries a wide range of shade materials—from linen blends and embroidered cottons to bamboo and woven wood—and offers every fold and lift type possible, so you can find the exact form and function to best fit your space.

Walsh likes to use drapery beyond traditional windows, especially if a room feels stark or echoey because of finishes such as marble, glass, or tile. A portière—a curtain hung in a doorway or cased opening—can create a sense of separation between spaces, soften an entryway, or help buffer cold air near an exterior door.

“Even if they’re purely decorative, drapery and window treatments absorb sound and provide texture and visual interest,” adds Kelly.

The right fabric reinforces that effect. A lighter fabric like TWOPAGES Patti Belgian Flax Linen Curtain adds softness without feeling too heavy, while a drape with a more substantial texture, such as the brand’s Birkin Velvet Curtain, adds visual warmth and a richer feel, perfect for the colder months.

The smallest details can make the biggest difference, even if custom treatments are not in your budget. Instead of a traditional curtain rod with finials on each end, which can look dated, Walsh advises using a French return rod, which is mounted on the wall and curves outward. This allows the end of the panels to sit flush against the wall while still creating space for a stack back.

“A French return rod is by far the most elegant way to fake it and make your drapes look professional,” says Walsh. But stay away from grommets—“they are a dead giveaway you did it yourself,” she says—and use rings with clips instead.

Disney’s Experiences Segment Hit Record Quarterly Revenue of $10.1 Billion as Cruise Days Surged. Can the Momentum Last?

Many consumers long to ride on a large ship these days.

By Eric Volkman – Nov 18, 2026 at 2:00PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Walt Disney ›

It isn't easy for a huge, sprawling global company to increase its key fundamentals at double-digit percentage rates. Walt Disney (DIS +4.74%) achieved this feat with the fourth-quarter and annual fiscal 2026 results it published Tuesday morning.

All three of the entertainment giant's business units posted revenue gains in the mid-teens; arguably the most impressive of the trio was experiences, which notched an all-time quarterly record by drawing more than $10.1 billion in revenue. Let's take a brief overview of how Disney did during the quarter, and drill down into the performance of the experiences unit.

For the period, Disney's total revenue was just under $25.95 billion, a 16% improvement over the fourth quarter of 2025's tally -- although I should note that the most recent quarter included one more week. Regardless, that percentage essentially matched the growth of all three business units (entertainment and sports, in addition to experiences).

With such lift, non-GAAP (adjusted) net income also soared. It headed skyward at a 56% clip to almost $3.35 billion, or $1.77 per share.

Both numbers beat the consensus analyst estimates, albeit not spectacularly. The average prognosticator forecasts for revenue and per-share profitability were $25.21 billion and $1.68, respectively.

Experiences is a close second in terms of revenue among the three reporting units, behind entertainment. Yet it was by far the No. 1 when measured by operating income, with its $2.25 billion trouncing entertainment's $1.33 billion and sports' $1.39 billion. It also happened to be the champ in the year-ago quarter too.

I should note here that, starting in the current (first) quarter, Disney has shifted its consumer products licensing and publishing operations from experiences to entertainment. If the company had made that move before the fourth quarter, experiences would still be the runner-up in revenue. Meanwhile, the adjustment would shift consumer products' operating profit to entertainment, and make entertainment No. 1 in that metric.

Nevertheless, experiences was a crucial driver of growth for those impressive top- and bottom-line improvements. Given that, it's worthwhile to explore why, and devote a little space to whether Disney is positioning it well for further growth.

As its name implies, experiences comprises the unique, in-person Disney attractions. This includes the theme parks, anchored by the endlessly popular Disneyland in Southern California, and comprises the burgeoning Disney Cruise business and travel services (plus the consumer products operation that's now under entertainment's big tent).

What gives the unit its notably higher margins is that many of its operations center around fixed infrastructure. Disneyland and other parks are already built, and the company's cruise ships regularly travel the seas. Yes, there are maintenance costs, but for the most part much of the capex for those assets has already been spent, while the customers keep coming (and paying). In the sports division, content costs are extremely high and seem to be on the rise, so it's more challenging to make a buck. As for entertainment, we can say the same for even a mid-budget movie or a network TV show.

Happily for the company's most profitable unit, we're famously in the midst of an experiences boom, as consumers with money to spend are eager to participate in unique events. This explains the recent, sustained success of the travel industry; have you noticed how often commercial airplanes fly at near or full capacity these days?

One experience consumers long to enjoy is a cruise. Disney was happy to oblige, as its cruise days (total passengers carried times the total duration of sailings) popped by 11%. Management had smartly laid the groundwork for this rise by adding two new ships -- the Destiny and the Adventure -- to its fleet between November 2025 and this past March. The total is now eight ships, and there's clearly enough demand to keep those sailings lively.

The experience boom was gathering steam as the COVID pandemic faded in 2023. That year, Disney announced it planned to almost double its capital expenditures (capex) in the experiences segment, to roughly $60 billion over the decade from fiscal years 2024 to 2033. And part of this was the expansion of the fleet, hence the recent debuts of Destiny and Adventure.

That's still very much a central strategy for Disney. Of the capex splurge on experiences, CEO Josh D'Amaro said on the fourth-quarter conference call, "the plan will continue to expand our capacity and deliver a differentiated entertainment experience as we continue turbocharging our parks and cruise businesses."

All this, of course, leads to the question of whether Disney stock is a buy. After all, judging by Mr. Market's reaction to the earnings -- the shares closed Tuesday down by 1.5% -- a clutch of investors didn't think so. They flagged some negatives, such as the 7% slide in operating income in the international theme park segment and the anticipated 9% annual rise in spending on content in the new fiscal year.

However, as a longtime Disney shareholder and bull (with some down moments over the years, admittedly), I think the fourth quarter's stars well overshadowed the slumps, to the point where the company had scope for its very generous 33% dividend raise. Plus, its focus on experiences is already powering strong growth, and will surely continue to do so. This is one stock I'm absolutely keeping in my portfolio.

An AI Debt Collection Service Exposed 6.6 Million Accounts and 315,000 Call Recordings

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Two vulnerabilities in the India-based AI-driven debt collection platform CreditNirvana exposed 6.6 million accounts and about 315,000 call recordings from the company's agentic call center, according to research released on Wednesday. Owned by the Indian fintech company Perfios, the platform is built to “read a borrower’s full context, decide the next best action, and execute it autonomously, continuously, and within policy.”

The bugs were patched in CreditNirvana's systems in June and were disclosed publicly in October. But independent researcher Eaton Zveare, who found and disclosed them, notes that, while the vulnerabilities were active, they exposed personal information on millions of borrowers. Details about financial collections are inherently—perhaps obviously—sensitive, because they relate to unpaid accounts or debts and often involve extensive documentation of people's personal histories.

“They fixed it in one day, which was great, but this is really a pretty significant case,” Zveare tells WIRED. “Some of the largest fintech firms across India use this company to autonomously collect debts, so they naturally hold data, including credit scores, call recordings, transcripts, court records, and other sensitive documents. There’s just so much in there that makes it a very sensitive issue.”

CreditNirvana and Perfios did not return WIRED's requests for comment. Zveare says he worked directly with India’s Computer Emergency Response Team (CERT-In) to get the vulnerabilities fixed. CERT-In categorized one vulnerability as “high” severity and the other as “critical” severity.

The high-severity vulnerability impacted the call center data, exposing hundreds of thousands of calls as well as recommendations for next steps generated by the platform after calls were complete. It could also have been exploited to allow an attacker to manage AI agents within the platform and direct their own agentic campaigns.

The critical severity vulnerability impacted CreditNirvana's main platform, and Zveare says it specifically exposed the data of three corporate customers—all prominent Indian financial services firms—using CreditNirvana for their collections. The data also exposed millions of individuals’ accounts, which contain credit reports, ID cards, case management details, and legal documents, along with certain types of communications, including millions of emails, texts, and call transcripts related to financial services, unpaid accounts, and collections. The vulnerability also could have been exploited by an attacker to track the location of human debt collection agents and for some administrative privileges within the system.

“Successful exploitation of this vulnerability could allow the attacker to enumerate platform users, establish sessions as arbitrary users including administrative users, access and modify sensitive information with the privileges of the impersonated user,” CERT-In wrote.

Zveare says he is relieved that the issue was resolved quickly, but is conscious that the bugs were potentially present in CreditNirvana's systems for years. As AI is integrated into more and more automated systems, synthesizing data from a greater number of sources and using it to draw conclusions, Zveare says, the episode serves as “an important example of an AI-powered service having a very sensitive data exposure.”

The move brings the niche utility token to more than 15 million fintech app users under the EU’s MiCA regulatory framework.

Retail fintech giant Revolut has added GOMINING, the native utility token of the digital Bitcoin mining platform GoMining, to its in-app cryptocurrency trading service. The listing enables over 15 million Revolut crypto users across the United Kingdom and the European Economic Area (EEA) to buy, hold, and trade the token alongside everyday banking services.

Prior to this integration, GOMINING was primarily accessible through crypto-native exchanges such as Kraken, KuCoin, and Bitget.

The listing builds on an existing payment integration established in 2024 that allowed European users to fund GoMining accounts via Revolut Pay. It also adds GOMINING to Revolut’s cryptocurrency trading service.

Revolut reviews digital assets before listing them, assessing their risks, compliance requirements and potential financial crime exposure. It also monitors listed tokens on an ongoing basis as part of its procedures under the EU’s Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation (MiCA).

“For years, there has been a clear line between crypto and traditional finance, and most people stayed on one side of it,” said Mark Zalan, CEO of GoMining, in a statement announcing the listing. “For GoMining, this listing means our token is no longer only for people who already live in the crypto world.”

GOMINING has several uses within the GoMining platform. Token holders can use it to pay digital miner maintenance fees, with discounts of up to 20%, buy digital miners on the platform’s marketplace and purchase boosts in Miner Wars, GoMining’s mining game.

The token also has a governance function. Users can lock their GOMINING tokens to receive veGOMINING, which gives them voting rights on the allocation of ecosystem rewards.

Revolut customers can now buy GOMINING directly through the app. To use the token on GoMining, they must first transfer it to an external GoMining account.

The Revolut listing gives GOMINING exposure to a wider retail audience through a mainstream financial app. It also provides another way for users to acquire the token without first opening an account on a crypto-native exchange.

However, buying GOMINING through Revolut does not provide direct access to GoMining’s platform features. Users must transfer their tokens to an external wallet before using them within the GoMining ecosystem.
