Earlier this year, I put Google Fi’s $35/month unlimited plan to the test against my Visible service — concluding that it’s a great alternative for anyone who wants a hassle-free setup. In my never-ending quest to find the best cheap cell phone plans, I spent the past 30 days evaluating TextNow’s $35.99/month unlimited plan to see how it holds up and what kind of value it offers over the competition.

I actually signed up for TextNow’s free talk-and-text service a couple of years ago, but it lacked the consistent data connectivity and allotment I needed access to while on the go. Now, the MVNO is stepping up its lineup with an unlimited data plan that runs $8.99 per week, or $35 per month if you want to maximize your savings — directly rivaling Google Fi's entry tier, and for a little more, Visible+ Pro.

If you’re wondering whether it’s worth switching to TextNow’s unlimited plan, I’ll break down the data allowances, extra features, and real-world performance to help you decide if it's worth getting over my experience with Visible and Google Fi.

Unlimited talk & text and 2GB data to/from Canada and Mexico

While swapping eSIMs is often an exercise in futility, the setup process with TextNow was remarkably painless. All it took to get the service running was downloading the TextNow app, logging in, and initiating the eSIM setup. Once I entered my phone's IMEI, it took just a few minutes for the eSIM profile to push directly to my device.

The app also made hardware upgrades effortless: I easily transferred the line from my Motorola Razr Fold over to the Pixel 11 Pro Fold, and it’s now active on my Vivo X500 Pro Max. If your handset doesn’t support eSIM, TextNow can still ship you a physical SIM card. Overall, I’m thrilled I didn’t run into any roadblocks when swapping between daily drivers.

Both Google Fi and Visible also offer the convenience of eSIM activation, with Fi delivering the same frictionless setup as TextNow. Visible, on the other hand, has been hit-or-miss in my experience — with one device swap leaving me stuck with technical support for several hours to get it resolved.

While it’s advertised as an all-you-can-eat offer, TextNow’s plan technically offers unlimited data to chow down on each month — but in reality, it comes with 20 GB of premium high-speed data. I’m a power user and could easily burn through that if I didn’t already have other services to lean on, but it should suffice for most people who primarily stay on Wi-Fi.

On average, I’ve been getting about 80 Mbps down and about 4 Mbps up. These speeds aren’t a home run compared to the 200 Mbps speeds I got while running on Google Fi with ultra wideband connectivity, so you’ll have to wait a bit longer to download massive files onto your phone. Visible offers faster speeds too in my experience, but Google Fi has always been better.

It's worth mentioning the data caps with each: 20 GB for TextNow, 30 GB for Google Fi, and none technically with Visible. In the fine print details, though, Visible says that "in times of congestion, we may temporarily prioritize Heavy Data User's data behind other traffic for the remainder of the cycle." Meaning, it's unlimited data, but "Heavy Data Users" can see their speeds reduced.

In real-world situations, though, TextNow's data speeds were more than enough to stream Instagram and TikTok reels on the mass transit commute home without any buffering.

One of the biggest adjustments is having to rely on the TextNow app for all your calling and texting needs, which requires granting default app permissions during initial setup. Since I tested the service exclusively on Android devices, losing access to my default phone and messaging apps wasn’t a major issue — especially since Google Messages also routes messages over a data network.

The saving grace of being tied to the TextNow app is that the paid tier eliminates the intrusive ads that plagued the free tier when I tested it a couple of years ago. On top of that, the messaging client supports the modern features I rely on, including smooth handling of GIFs, stickers, videos, and emoji reactions.

At this price point, TextNow leaves out two perks I rely heavily on with my primary Visible line: mobile hotspot and smartwatch connectivity. Visible includes both out of the box, while Google Fi’s $35 plan covers smartwatch data.

Even though TextNow technically doesn’t support hotspot functionality on paper, I was still able to toggle the option on my phone and connect in a pinch. Over my 30 days of testing, the connection was never throttled or shut down — though there’s always a risk the carrier could clamp down on unauthorized tethering down the road.

Still, in such a competitive price bracket, it’s tough to overlook these omissions when rival carriers package them in at no extra charge. Visible+ Pro, for example, lumps in both crucial features at no extra cost.

At the same $35 monthly rate, Google Fi simply delivers a more compelling overall package — not to mention free smartwatch connectivity. Add in Fi's dual-network “Super Network” switching to help eliminate coverage dead zones, and it easily stands out as the superior pick.

Visible+ Pro is also a superb option, despite costing a little more at $39 per month. Although, that extra spread does come with welcome additions I simply don't get with either Google Fi Unlimited Essentials and TextNow Unlimited — such as mobile hotspot functionality, smartwatch data, and a robut set of international features.

While TextNow gets the job done, it functions best as a short-term bridge or secondary option. Paying monthly saves you the most money, but having the flexibility to pay $8.99 week-to-week is a solid perk if you only need temporary connectivity in a pinch.

If nothing else, TextNow’s free talk-and-text option remains well worth setting up as an emergency backup for when your primary carrier suffers an unexpected outage.

Your AI agent is only as good as your endpoint data

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

PARTNER CONTENT: Real-time endpoint data keeps AI-driven IT operations accurate, fast and under control

Every so often a flaw comes along that shows how fast the ground can shift under IT and security teams. PHP2Shell, a pair of critical flaws in PHP, was one of them. Details leaked before the planned disclosure, frontier AI helped researchers find the flaws, and PHP shipped fixes the next day. For many teams, though, the patch was the easy part. So what does it take to respond at that speed, and where should teams begin?

Picture a loop. Software finds where a risk lives and ranks what matters most. It proposes a fix, then, inside limits people define, carries it out and checks that it worked. Routine, low-risk actions can run on their own. Higher-impact ones wait for a person to approve. A few are never handed over at all. Each step leaves a record that someone can review and reverse.

Three problems keep coming up. The first is visibility. PHP2Shell reportedly sat undetected for more than a decade. For these two flaws, the fix was a version upgrade. The hard part was finding every copy of PHP, including copies bundled inside other software or extracted by hand onto Windows machines that never registered as installed applications. Then teams had to work out which copies the internet could reach and check for signs of earlier attack. An inventory built from a periodic snapshot can't answer those questions on the day they matter.

The second is tool-hopping. Answering "where does this run, and can the internet reach it?" often means pivoting across an asset inventory, a vulnerability scanner and an external attack surface tool, each with its own view. Every hop adds delay. Every console is another chance to disagree with the last.

The third is pace. Several security firms reported exploitation attempts before PHP released its fixes, and Cloudflare reported early attacks hours ahead of the patch. Frontier AI models helped find PHP2Shell, and in April Anthropic said its Claude Mythos Preview model had found thousands of previously unknown flaws across every major operating system and browser. With models like these in the picture, the old rhythm of disclose, patch and verify assumes a gap that keeps shrinking.

Put an AI agent on top of stale, fragmented data and these problems don't go away. They scale.

Endpoints are where work happens and where many attacks end up, so they are the ground truth for any decision about the estate. Four qualities make that data a sound foundation for autonomous action:

Accuracy: Live data from the endpoints themselves, not a snapshot that was true last week.

Speed: A short path from question to action, with no hopping between tools to reconcile answers.

Control: Approvals and an audit trail, plus the ability to roll back, so any automated action can be reviewed and reversed.

Shared truth: IT operations, security and the system of record working from one view, rather than arguing over whose data is right.

Matt Psencik, a threat research engineer at Tanium, put the visibility problem bluntly when PHP2Shell broke: "The installations that worry me most are the ones a security team doesn't know it owns."

AI sits on both sides of this. It helped researchers find the PHP2Shell flaws, and attackers can use it to build exploits too. For defenders, AI works best as the operator and the interface: it takes a plain-language question, works out what to ask of the data, and proposes or carries out the next step. But it is only as good as the data it is grounded in. A fluent answer built on outdated information is still wrong, just more confidently so. Keeping people in the loop where stakes are high is what makes automation safe to expand.

Teams don't need to hand over the keys all at once. Start here:

Find every copy: Don't stop at the installed-applications list. PHP2Shell showed that software bundled inside other products can slip past it. Compare your CMDB against what is really on your network and record exact versions.

Draw the lines: Rank systems by what the internet can reach, then decide which response actions run automatically, which need approval and which are never delegated. Write it down before an agent needs it.

Pilot one low-risk workflow: Pick something contained, build approval into it, and read the audit trail before widening the scope.

Teams can also shorten the pilot with a partner. World Wide Technology (WWT), a Tanium partner, works with many large organizations on technology rollouts and hosts a Tanium lab in its Advanced Technology Center, where customers can test an approach hands-on before moving it into production.

That second problem, the hopping, is the one Tanium Atlas was built to close. Instead of moving between consoles, an operator asks a plain-language question, gets an answer from live endpoint data, and acts on it inside limits the team sets, with every action recorded. Access to Shanghai Grand Opera House staircase restricted to paid ticket holders

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Photography by Kaia Wang, Yumeng Zhu, Tian Fangfang, Chen Hao

The Snøhetta-designed Shanghai Grand Opera House has introduced a paid, ticketing system for visitors wanting to access the signature spiral staircase and rooftop viewing platform at the recently opened building in Shanghai.

When Dezeen visited the site, we observed a temporary barricade that had been set up to separate the free-to-access, ground-level public from the spiral staircase leading up to the roof. Access to the staircase and roof now costs 50 yuan (£5.60) per person.

Since opening, access to the architectural feature has been restricted with visitors now required to pre-book tickets online, with the staircase open between 10:30am to 2:30pm.  According to the booking site, people with tickets to see a performance inside the opera house are still required to purchase a ticket and the fee includes accident insurance.

Asked about the apparent change in access requirements, Snøhetta said that demand meant that “visitor management is necessary” and that “access arrangements will continue to develop”.

“As with any major cultural institution, operational procedures continue to evolve during the opening period as the Opera House team gains experience with visitor patterns, capacity needs, and the building’s day-to-day use,” the studio told Dezeen.

“Given the exceptional public interest and the limited capacity of the roof and public stairs, visitor management is necessary to ensure a safe and enjoyable experience for everyone,” it continued.

“Access arrangements will continue to develop with operational experience, but the intention remains the same: to create a building that can be widely enjoyed by the public while balancing accessibility, safety, and the visitor experience.”

Following news that a fee had been introduced, the phrase “is it worth spending 50 yuan to visit Shanghai Grand Opera House” trended on Chinese social media.

Some commenters questioned whether charing to visit the attraction was justifiable, while others sympathised with the need for crowd control. Others shared tips on how to take the best photos of the building without paying the entrance fee.

Visitors Dezeen spoke to at the site were concerned about both the fee and the temporary barriers.

“I don’t think it’s worth the money,” Mr Qiu, who paid to climb the stairs, told Dezeen. “Basically you pay to climb a giant stairs. If you want good photos of the stairs, best to take from afar, which you can do for free,”

Ms Ye, who didn’t pay the entry fee, told Dezeen that she was really disappointed that the barricade was in her photos.

“I don’t know what the point of the barricade is – to separate the paid and unpaid? Or for better crowd circulation? But at least they need to put some effort in designing it to match the beauty of the building,” she said.

“It really is an eye-sore for the photos.”

The building, and its accessible roof, are often compared to Oslo Opera House, which was also designed by Snøhetta and is topped with a walkable roof. According to the studio, both projects were designed to “reduce thresholds wherever possible”.

“Buildings like opera houses inevitably create thresholds through walls, doors, and ticketed programs,” said Snøhetta co-founder Kjetil Thorsen at the time of the Shanghai Grand Opera House opening.

“We see it as our responsibility to challenge those thresholds and make the building as much a civic destination as a performance venue. People should be able to enjoy the building without attending a performance. It should become part of everyday life, a place to spend time, meet others, and experience the city,” he continued.

“A successful landmark is not only recognised by visitors, it is also embraced by the people who live around it and make it part of their everyday lives.”

Several other cultural projects in China have been built with publicly accessible roofs. However, the accessibility presented in concepts has often been restricted when the buildings open.

Also in Shanghai, the Start Museum, designed by French architect Jean Nouvel and opened in 2022, features a public roof that can be used for displaying large-scale art work. However, the space has rarely been used.

The Amateur Architecture Studio-designed Fuchun Cultural Museum Complex is defined by a continuous undulating roof to resemble the experience of climbing the local Fuchun Mountain. However, the roof didn’t open to the public for four years and is now only open to public visits for one hour a day, three days a week.

InfoQ Homepage News LinkedIn Replaces Argo CD with a Custom GitOps Engine for Kubernetes Deployments

Nov 12, 2026 4 min read

In a recent post, LinkedIn revealed it has replaced Argo CD with its own GitOps engine. This new system deploys applications across over 250 Kubernetes clusters. It supports more than 4,000 services and manages over 100,000 deployments each week. According to a post, deployment infrastructure failures fell from 925 to 63 despite a 3.7x increase in deployment volume. The median time to detect and conclude a failed deployment dropped from 16.1 to 2.45 minutes.

LinkedIn started with one Argo CD instance per cluster, a topology it chose because Argo CD already managed its cluster add-ons that way. Clusters run up to 5,000 nodes each. A central deployment orchestrator spawned an ephemeral pod per deployment, with Argo CD hidden behind it. The team lists seven problems that emerged at scale. Argo CD doesn't have an observedGeneration, so you can't link sync and health status to a specific deployment. LinkedIn created a Lua health check that uses a tracking annotation to address this. A Synced and Healthy application could also resolve to an unintended revision when stale repo-server cache entries were used.

Two of the issues were architectural. Argo CD maintains one watch per group-version-resource across Applications. LinkedIn’s clusters have low resource-type diversity. This means etcd churn from one app can slow syncs for all apps. In one test, sync took under 3 minutes on a cluster with 60,000 pods and over 10 minutes with more than 80,000. The second was server-side-apply PATCH. Kubernetes truncates managedFields once an object has had more than 10 distinct field managers, and the API server then forgets who owns a field. In LinkedIn's AI workloads, which run multiple containers per Pod, a container removed from a manifest could therefore survive on the live Pod. The team had pruning disabled because destructive operations in production require approval. The remaining problems were CRD schema changes that required controller restarts across the fleet. Some OutOfSync states did not reflect real drift. Error messages were buried in boilerplate.

LinkedIn evaluated Flux but found it shared the same controller paradigm, so it built the engine as a library. Each deployment runs in its pod, with no shared controller state. Updates use PUT for full replacements, not PATCH. They retry on resourceVersion conflicts with exponential backoff. A 3-way diff across the live object, the previously deployed manifest, and the newly rendered manifest detects drift, and genuine drift fails the deployment. The same pod then watches the object's status until it converges or times out, and the API server unwraps errors for the user. Objects missing from the rendered manifest become deletion candidates, subject to delete protection, and convergence logic for LinkedIn's CRDs is built in.

The comparison uses the same calendar month a year apart. The migration ran from July 2025 to April 2026, which makes May 2026 the first clean post-migration month. The infra failure count covers only failures caused by the deployment system and excludes drift errors, since the two systems define drift differently. LinkedIn states that the improvement isn't solely due to the new engine. The orchestrator's error classification and reliability also got better during this time. The median duration for successful deployments is similar for both systems. However, the mean and tail have improved. The team attributes this to a namespace-scoped informer cache.

The engine is internal, and no open-source release is mentioned. Argo CD remains in use at LinkedIn for cluster add-ons and system services. The team had also found that maintaining 200+ Argo CD instances made each upgrade a multi-week project.

Teams running Argo CD at scale can check two things without a rewrite. Count the field managers on long-lived objects, since managedFields truncation can leave orphaned containers that produce no error. Then check whether sync latency tracks total pod count on shared clusters, which points to watch contention. LinkedIn concludes that the build-or-extend decision relies on the operational model. They say that per-deployment isolation, not controller tuning, allows the system to scale with compute.

LinkedIn is not the only large operator to build deployment logic around Kubernetes rather than adopt a general-purpose GitOps controller. Uber’s Up platform assigns each deployment to a dedicated controller. This is the Uber Deployment Controller. It translates the intent into Kubernetes primitives and sends status updates. This helps the workflow move forward. Uber keeps a long-running controller, while LinkedIn runs one ephemeral pod per deployment with no shared controller state.

Airbnb went further back and built its own tooling years earlier. In a talk at QCon London 2019 on InfoQ, infrastructure engineer Melanie Cebula introduced kube-gen. This internal framework creates Kubernetes configuration from one versioned project file. It also includes a deploy tool that applies or replaces objects and restarts pods, so changes take effect right away. Airbnb's deploy tool also returns an atomic success or failure, and a container that restarts for 30 seconds at the start of a rollout counts as a failed deploy. Airbnb was then at roughly 125,000 production deploys a year.

5 new mobile games to try this week - November 12th, 2026

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Fresh mobile picks you should give a go this week

Who doesn't love new games? We certainly do - there's just something about the exhilaration of a new title you can't wait to dive into, as the loading screen pops up and you enter a brave new world.

Of course, new mobile games are a dime a dozen these days, and who has the time to scour the iPhone, iPad and/or Android stores to catch the latest title that drops? Fret not - we've gathered a quick list of 5 new mobile games you should try this week, whether that's an undiscovered indie gem or a high-profile AAA masterpiece.

That said, you can head on over to our NEW WEEKLY MOBILE GAMES hub if you're on the hunt for more curated adventures to obsess over!

Perhaps the release that a good number of people on our team are the most excited about is Warhammer Survivors, and despite not being a Warhammer fan myself, I can totally understand why. This Survivors-like take on the popular franchise is, as Iwan and Will put it, the perfect pair, especially since it was crafted in partnership with poncle of Vampire Survivors fame.

You'll try to survive in this "bullet heaven" shooter using more than 40 characters from Warhammer 40,000 and Warhammer Age of Sigmar, taking down endless hordes of foes across mobile-optimised controls to boot.

One of the most important elements of a classic whodunit is to piece together clues based on what suspects are saying - but what if you had to solve the case across an entirely wordless affair? I suppose that's the biggest challenge that Hillthorn poses to anyone who dares to take it on, as you'll have to put two and two together to get to the bottom of things using purely card-based clues.

Time is of the essence, so you'll really need to put your detective cap on to find a child who's gone missing. There are multiple endings to discover, so it's safe to say that after every short run, you'll likely want to revisit the eerie mysteries of Hillthorn over and over again.

I don't think there's anything else that embodies the pick-up-and-play nature of mobile more perfectly than one-thumb portrait-mode endeavours, and Rocket Rally is right up that alley! It is, as you might expect, a quick arcade racer where you'll need to boost and drift to the finish line in just 30 seconds, and with a wide variety of rally cars you can add to your collection too.

As you progress, you'll unlock upgrades for your car to ramp up your races, all so you can beat your own best time or aim for the top spot in the leaderboards.

I feel like Crossy Road Rush is perfectly suited for Apple Arcade, as the absence of ads and in-app purchases definitely adds to the endless runner's charm - and especially so since Crossy Road is such a staple on mobile! This time around, you'll be running through colourful environments similar to Subway Surfers, with a whole bunch of cool stuff to collect and obstacles to overcome.

There are more than 50 levels to discover and daily challenges to take on, as well as family sessions you can join in on with up to four players. I'd say it's definitely a fresh take on the franchise - after all, where else can you squad up with your fam to take on a giant Octoboss?

The concept of climbing an endless tower is nothing new, but I suppose this is the first time I've ever encountered one where you're meant to crank it up - quite literally. A Fool's Errand was originally made for the Playdate, where you turn a literal crank to help your hero ascend. Now that it's out on mobile, you'll still do the same climbing without the physical crank - but with the same atmospheric visuals and intriguing story.

There are plenty of traps to avoid along the way, and it's really all about the journey as you keep climbing on - as the title suggests - a fool's errand. Whether or not what awaits you at the top is worth it is anybody's guess - and I suppose you'll just have to make the climb yourself to find out.
