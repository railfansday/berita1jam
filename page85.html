Rory McIlroy looking to match Colin Montgomerie's record of eight times topping the DP World Tour Order of Merit; Eugenio Chacarra around 500 points clear of McIlroy ahead of the season-ending DP World Tour Championship; watch live on Sky Sports from 7am Thursday

Rory McIlroy feels he will have to be at his "absolute best" to match Colin Montgomerie's record of eight Race to Dubai titles as he aims to beat Eugenio Chacarra to this year's prize in the UAE.

McIlroy has topped the DP World Tour's Order of Merit for the past four seasons but heads into the 2026 DP World Tour Championship, live on Sky Sports from 7am on Thursday, second in the standings, over 500 points behind former LIV Golf player Chacarra.

The world No 2 hopes his experience at Jumeirah Golf Estates - he has won the season-ending tournament three times and also lost in a play-off to Ryder Cup team-mate Matthew Fitzpatrick last year - will give him the edge.

McIlroy said during his media duties on Tuesday: "The last few years I played well much earlier in the year where I've maybe not had to play at my absolute best to get it done. This week's a little bit different.

"The course has evolved a little bit over the years. It's certainly a lot more penal off the tee this year. The rough is very, very thick. You get a lot of bad lies in the rough.

"I think when you come back to a golf course that you played a lot on and you've done well on, that always gives you a little bit of an advantage starting the week.

"I think there's a little bit there [in having experience of the course]."

On having the chance to emulate Montgomerie, McIlroy, who finished third at last week's Abu Dhabi Aldar Championship, four strokes behind winner Jon Rahm, said: "I'm very excited to be in contention for the eighth. I made some ground up last week, maybe not as much as I wanted, but I felt like it was a step in the right direction.

"It's been a course that's been very good to me over the years, and I have a lot of experience on it and a lot of experience up at the top end of the leaderboard.

"I'm excited to do it all over again, to try to play well enough to give myself a real chance at the weekend."

McIlroy completed the career Grand Slam in 2025 after finally claiming an elusive Masters title - 11 years after his previous major triumph - and then made it back-to-back victories at Augusta National in April of this year.

The 37-year-old told reporters he is "increasingly proud" of his "consistency" and "longevity" over the years as he targets what would be a 45th career title should he win the DP World Tour Championship in the Emirates this week.

Nintendo Adds eShop Gifting to Switch Consoles

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The latest Nintendo Switch system update added support for an upcoming feature that will let users gift digital software purchased through the eShop. The gifting system is set to launch this month, meaning Switch users will be able to add digital games to their letters to Santa this festive season.

Nintendo continues to regularly release updates for the Switch and Switch 2, ensuring both consoles are in the best possible state and progressively improving user experience. Usually, these patch notes mostly focus on system stability, but that was not the case this time around. On Nov. 9, Nintendo released firmware update v23.5.0 for both Switch and Switch 2. Besides the usual technical updates, it added support for a highly requested feature: gifting.

Starting on Nov. 16, players will be able to purchase digital software (games, upgrade packs, or DLC) in the eShop and have it delivered to friends and family. This feature has been a long time coming, and Nintendo decided to add it at the right time — just in time for the holidays.

Once this feature is live, players will be able to tick the "Give as a Gift" box when buying a product on the eShop. Then, all they need to do is select the recipient, pick the wrapping color, and complete the purchase.

Digital gifts on the eShop will be sent immediately and cannot be scheduled, like on Steam. Unfortunately, this isn't the only setback. First, supervised accounts, meaning accounts under parental control, cannot use the gifting system. While details haven't been provided yet, there is a limit on how many gifts a user can send within a given time period. It is currently unknown whether the limit is based on the number of games, the type of software it is (DLC or game), or the value of the gift. Players cannot send gifts to users outside their family group, even if they are Switch friends.

These restrictions make sense, but some players were disappointed when they found out they couldn't gift all of their friends. Nintendo family groups have a maximum of eight members, which certainly makes this feature feel limited. Nevertheless, this addition is a step in the right direction.

Polestar has unveiled a concept that makes you wish it would quietly slip into production tomorrow. The Formula 2030 is low, wide, and muscular, with four doors and enough suggested practicality to help you justify the rather large wheels.

Unfortunately, Polestar has no plans to produce the Formula 2030, but some of what you’re looking at is headed for cars you can actually buy. So this is the first Polestar penned under the brand’s new design boss, Philipp Römers, who took the role in August.

The study measures 184.3 inches (4,680 millimeters) in length and stands just 53.1 inches (1,350 mm) tall, making it lower than the Polestar 5. For reference, that’s about as low as Audi’s last TT sports car, but the concept’s aggressively bulging rear haunches and low stance make it look a lot meaner and sportier.

It features plenty of cool, subtle details you may not notice at first. The headlights, for instance, sit within a front aerodynamic wing, while a single upright windshield wiper screams motorsport. Polestar has ditched the familiar full-width rear lightbar for separate “Single Blade” lights. The 22-inch wheels, splitter, and diffuser sport variations of the same "Nitrogen" exterior color.

Inside, four individual seats have inflatable cushions and bolsters that adjust around their occupants. Rear-hinged rear doors swing open for pillarless access, while exposed structural components and flax-based composite seat backs really highlight the use of this sustainable material.

The coolest thing about the interior, though, is the fact that the cockpit also physically changes depending on the driving mode. Switch to Performance, and the 12.2-inch center screen lowers into the console while the steering wheel and its attached 20-inch driver display move closer. Today’s consumers are spending more on things meaningful to their ‘life, health and happiness’, says Siam Piwat CEO Chadatip Chutrakul

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Retailers across Asia are trying to find new ways to boost sales, whether it’s by giving shoppers more reasons to visit a retail location, or by using new technologies to reduce friction for customers, shortening the gap between attention and sales.

Consumers are spending more on things “that are meaningful to their life, health, and happiness,” Chadatip Chutrakul, CEO of Bangkok-based retail mall developer Siam Piwat, said at Fortune’s Southeast Asia 500 Summit in Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam. “Retail is just a part of it… It’s not [only] about shopping, but creating experiential destinations.”

In October, Siam Piwat announced ICONPHUKET, a retail and entertainment development in Thailand’s Phuket province. Scheduled to open in 2029, the project will include green spaces and health and wellness facilities alongside shops, residences, offices and hotels.

“Shopping malls in Thailand—and I think in Asia—are like a second home to customers,” Chutrakul explained. “So besides shopping, we [also try to] offer everything else.”

Malina Ngai, CEO of global drug and beauty chain AS Watson, agreed that today’s consumers need a reason to choose one particular retailer over another. “Ultimately, the retail game is about relevance,” she said. “It’s not so much about whether you have more products or technology… customers are looking for a brand that they trust.”

Online, social media and influencers are pushing consumers to develop relationships with brands faster.

“[In the past], consumers built a relationship with a brand over [a span of] five years, but that cycle has been cut down to a couple of months now, because people start to adhere to a brand identity very quickly,” Saemin Ahn, managing partner at Rakuten Capital, the venture capital arm of Japanese conglomerate Rakuten Group, said.

Platforms also need to ensure views are turned into purchases. In February, Rakuten Ichiba—the company’s online marketplace—partnered with Google via the YouTube Shopping affiliate program, which allows viewers to tap ‘View Products’ during YouTube videos and navigate straight to Rakuten Ichiba’s webpage to make a purchase.

“In Rakuten Ichiba, there’s about 40,000 to 50,000 merchants that we have to keep happy, and those happy merchants keep the users happy,” Ahn explained.

Physical retailers are also using new tools to help shoppers find what they need. AS Watson is using AI to study how customers shop in different sections of its stores, from supplements to oral and hair care, Ngai said. The aim is to identify where shoppers struggle and change the layout or signage to help them.

“If we know that they spend about 56 seconds on this aisle, and they couldn’t find the product they need, we would have to improve how we arrange our products, put up signages, and work with our suppliers to make it easier for customers to make decisions,” she said.
