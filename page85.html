Peak Halftime Show: The A-List Machine Eventizing Pro Games

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

No longer content with just a Super Bowl splash, pro leagues led by the NFL are increasingly booking major music stars for programming. But it’s a balancing act.

By Alex Weprin, Ethan Millman November 20, 2026 8:56am

Nearly 73,000 fans inside Rio De Janeiro’s Maracanã Stadium rocked out to Ricky Martin and Brazilian DJ Pedro Sampaio on Sept. 27, when the duo performed their song “pikito pikito” live for the first time. But they weren’t headlining a concert — they were playing a halftime show.

The NFL has long held global ambitions, but this is the year it leaned into music to serve as a sort of cultural diplomacy. The Rio game, between the Baltimore Ravens and Dallas Cowboys, may be the best example, combining a global superstar in Martin with a local talent. “There was a lot of preparation and many hours of rehearsals leading up to the performance,” Martin tells The Hollywood Reporter, “but it was one of those experiences where everything came together beautifully.”

He adds, “These are memories I’ll carry with me for a long time.”

In fact, the NFL has seven halftime shows planned this season, kicking off with the Jonas Brothers in Melbourne on Sept. 10, running through The Warning in Mexico City on Nov. 22. It’s also part of a concerted effort to elevate games into cultural events. Just look at Nov. 25, when U2 will perform during a halftime show for Netflix’s Thanksgiving Eve game at SoFi Stadium between the L.A. Rams and Green Bay Packers. “There’s really this gathering momentum about how we can further eventize some of the parts on our schedule and our calendar,” Hans Schroeder, executive vp and chief media officer of the NFL, told reporters in October.

The league is leaning into halftime shows as the sports world overall has been more heavily embracing music programming in games. FIFA for instance, had its first World Cup Final halftime show this summer, with Coldplay’s Chris Martin curating a lineup that included BTS, Madonna and Justin Bieber. The NCAA, meanwhile, tapped EDM act The Chainsmokers to play a “bridge show” between the two Final Four games. The NHL has dug in, too, tapping Benson Boone to headline the first intermission during this year’s Winter Classic on New Year’s Eve.

“The NFL and all these leagues understand that to grow the game internationally and even domestically for the younger generation, they need to find organic ways to get those eyeballs,” says Tyler Stimmel, a booking agent in UTA’s music brand partnerships division. He handled booking on many of the NFL’s international events this year, including Jonas Brothers, Burna Boy in Paris, The Warning and Drew Baldridge in Munich.

“It all stems from the younger generation,” Stimmel says, pointing toward a recent USC Annenberg study that suggested nearly 80 percent of Gen Z respondents in a survey suggested they’d go to a sporting event because of concerts and other brand activations rather than because of the game itself. It’s beneficial to the talent, too, with international acts getting a shot to play before a wide swath of stateside NFL fans who otherwise likely wouldn’t have been exposed to their music. The agent adds, “We live in a highly saturated pop culture ecosystem the NFL wants more ways to cut through. “Music is the most natural way because we already know where to put the show, and you can pick a booking to tap into a version of culture that people within the region can easily relate to.”

In the U.S., that means working with such partners as Netflix to create more event-sized moments, like having U2 perform. “Netflix has leaned in because they see the success of Super Bowl and the Super Bowl halftime show, and it draws more eyes and it brings more attention and it brings more people, and not necessarily all avid football fans,” says Jon Barker, senior VP of global event production and operations for the NFL. “Now we’re tapping into casual fans, now we’re tapping into new fans, and it’s just one more element that opens another door for somebody to walk into the ecosystem of football and participate.”

Unlike the Super Bowl, which is booked by Jay-Z’s Roc Nation (next year’s headliner is Jay-Z himself), the league books its other halftime shows itself, though Barker notes that there is close coordination with its media rights partners. “It gets a little complicated, to be honest with you, in trying to find the right balance,” Barker says of the booking process. “Who is touring and is available? Who can put on a show that can fill a stadium? Who will translate well and do well on television? Who is the right act for the audience that’s inside this area, and who is the right act that will attract new fans that will be interested in what we’re doing?”

Stimmel, for his part, describes the process as sophisticated, saying the NFL has heavily relied on its regional offices throughout the world, identifying artists who are a major draw in each market but who can pull viewers from several verticals. “Burna Boy in Paris in particular is one that checks off a lot of those boxes,” he says. “He was one of the first Nigerian artists to sell out Stade de France, so we know he is popular there, but he can draw from Africa, North America.”

While the increased bookings may bring in more younger viewers, they may not always be a popular offering to older fans, who might prefer to see just a traditional halftime report with pundits rather than a concert. Stimmel acknowledges that critique but adds that “they want this to appeal to everyone, but the leagues are recognizing that in order to sustain the sport, it has to appeal to younger fans.”

NFL exec Barker says the league isn’t done expanding its halftime ambitions, noting that while “there’s probably a limit and the right balance, I don’t think we’re there yet,” even if it maintains the Super Bowl as the ultimate showcase.

From the league’s point of view, the performances are ultimately about growing the game — and reasserting the league as the most culturally relevant piece of American pop culture. “The way that we think about music is the same way we think about art, the same way we think about fashion,” says Barker, adding that the more ways “we can open to bring somebody in and show them the sport, the better off we all are.”

Bitcoin falls below $60K for first time since July amid broader market selloff

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Bitcoin fell nearly 6% as US stocks declined, with the Nasdaq dropping more than 2% and Anthropic shares losing almost 10%.

Bitcoin fell below $60,000 on Friday for the first time since July, extending its recent losses amid a broader selloff in technology stocks and other risk assets.

Bitcoin (BTC) dropped as low as $58,811 before recovering slightly to trade around $59,503, down 5.6% over the past 24 hours as of 16:52 UTC on Friday, according to CoinGecko data. The cryptocurrency has fallen 13.8% over the past week and nearly 30% over the past month.

The decline came amid a broader selloff in US equities. The Nasdaq Composite fell 2.73%, while the S&P 500 declined 1.44% as of approximately 16:27 UTC on Friday, according to Yahoo Finance.

Shares of Anthropic (ANT), which made its Nasdaq debut on Thursday, fell 9.92% to $87.54 as of 16:27 UTC, amid concerns over valuations and spending in the artificial intelligence sector.

US spot Bitcoin exchange-traded funds recorded $582.3 million in net outflows on Thursday, up from $306.5 million the previous day, according to Farside Investors. BlackRock’s iShares Bitcoin Trust (IBIT) accounted for $541.1 million of Thursday’s withdrawals.

Bitcoin’s decline appears to be driven more by the broader selloff in artificial intelligence stocks than by problems specific to the cryptocurrency market, according to James Butterfill, head of research at CoinShares.

Butterfill told Cointelegraph that Bitcoin has increasingly traded alongside other liquidity-sensitive growth assets, with deteriorating sentiment toward AI stocks spilling into crypto as investors reduce risk. The prospect of interest rates remaining elevated has made the broader selloff more severe, he added.

Interest-rate expectations have also shifted toward further tightening. The Federal Reserve held its benchmark interest rate at 3.75% to 4.00% in October, following a quarter-point increase in September. Traders on Polymarket were pricing in a 94% chance of another rate hike by the Fed’s next policy decision on Dec. 9, as of 17:19 UTC on Friday.

Polymarket traders price in 94% chance of another Fed rate hike in 2026. Source: Polymarket

Despite Bitcoin falling below $60,000, Butterfill said the decline has not fundamentally changed his medium-term outlook.

Butterfill said Bitcoin could face further short-term losses if the AI selloff deepens, but should benefit relatively quickly if technology stocks stabilize.

Alexander Blume, founder and CEO of digital asset investment firm Two Prime, told Cointelegraph that macroeconomic instability, interest rates, inflation and a lack of Bitcoin-specific catalysts were contributing to the decline.

However, Blume suggested that selling pressure may be nearing an end.

An Anthropic mathematician using Claude claims to have proven the most famous problem in math — the Riemann hypothesis — in the latest, but potentially most consequential, example of artificial intelligence (AI) producing stunning math results.

AI has steamrolled its way into the math community. Anthropic's proof will certainly and rightly be scrutinized, but rather than picking apart every single result these models produce, we should respond by changing how we evaluate and reward expertise — placing less weight on the volume of results and more on the human judgment required to decide which questions and answers matter.

Without this shift, we risk drowning genuine insight in a flood of plausible but poorly understood mathematics.

Mathematical judgment is the ability to distinguish a result that is correct, but boring, from one that might fundamentally change how we think about the problem or even the whole area of mathematics surrounding the problem.

AI could generate hundreds of thousands of complex variations on a theorem, but these variations might add very little to mathematical knowledge, each one ultimately proving a dead end. A truly consequential result might settle a long-standing unsolved problem or connect to apparently unrelated areas. It is mathematical judgment that allows us to tell the difference between the two.

Earlier this year, an OpenAI system produced a counterexample to the long-standing planar unit distance problem, overturning a conjecture of Paul Erdős that had resisted mathematicians for decades. More recently, OpenAI announced an apparent solution to the Navier-Stokes problem, a celebrated question about whether equations describing fluid flow can develop singularities, or infinite values. It is one of the Clay Mathematics Institute's Millennium Prize Problems — a set of seven well-known and complex math problems, each with a $1 million prize attached.

Whether OpenAI's result ultimately stands up to scrutiny remains to be seen. But what is clear is that AI systems are now operating at a level that many mathematicians did not expect to witness for years, if not decades. OpenAI has since released hundreds of further mathematical results, leaving mathematicians scrambling to assess their correctness and significance.

This does not mean that mathematicians are about to become obsolete. But it does mean we need to carefully reconsider what their precise purpose is.

Traditionally, expertise has been closely associated with production: Experts solve difficult problems, prove theorems, construct models and write papers. If AI can generate proofs, conjectures and probe research areas at unprecedented speed and scale, mathematical production is no longer necessarily the bottleneck. Instead, many pure mathematicians would argue that it is mathematical judgment that will become the scarcer resource.

Human judgment remains necessary because proofs generated by AI are not necessarily illuminating, comprehensible or even reliable. Some proofs may be so long or opaque that checking them requires substantial work by specialists and even more work to reinterpret them in a way that is meaningful to human mathematicians.

A proof can be valid without offering insight into why it is true or how its ideas might be employed elsewhere. The job of human mathematicians will be to become arbiters of the correctness of proofs, but also to scrutinize the reasoning and to extract the underlying ideas, determining whether the result says anything that meaningfully advances the field.

Mathematics, as a research community, needs to react rapidly to this change. Mathematicians and journals must prioritize significance over output. Mathematics involves deciding which results are important as well as generating them. A proof can be correct without being insightful. To give a simplified example, AI might "prove" that the square of a number divisible by two is divisible by four and then prove that the square of a number divisible by three is divisible by nine, and so on with ever larger integers. However, each new "proof" isn't an example of insightful mathematics, just a variation on the same elementary idea.

AI can create arguments faster than the community can verify, interpret or absorb them. Existing safeguards were not designed for that imbalance.

In contrast, a significant proof might settle a long-standing question, reveal an unexpected connection between disparate fields or develop a technique which is useful in the solution of many other problems.

Journals should therefore require authors to explain clearly what is genuinely new, why it matters — and what role AI played. Reviewers should judge whether a result deepens understanding, rather than merely verifying that the argument works. That does not mean rejecting work whose eventual importance is uncertain. The history of mathematics is full of ideas whose significance became apparent only decades later, so journals must continue to make room for foundational, exploratory and curiosity-driven research.

But there is a difference between a result with as-yet unknown implications and one that is unlikely ever to be insightful. Mathematical judgement cannot guarantee that nothing valuable is ever overlooked. It can, however, distinguish work that contains genuinely new ideas or possibilities from the automated production of uninteresting dead ends.

Universities and funders should also stop treating publication counts as a convenient proxy for quality. Careers are built around papers, citations, grants and prizes, but these measures were developed when producing sophisticated mathematics demanded substantial human time and effort. In the age of AI, work that might previously have taken years of careful thought could now be produced in weeks or even days.

Consequently, as the cost of generating mathematical results falls dramatically, those measures of quality become less useful. A decreased reliance on traditional metrics may be no bad thing, but institutions need credible alternatives: assessments of a researcher's most important contributions, evidence of intellectual leadership and explanations of how their work has changed understanding. This is urgent because mathematicians are already struggling to evaluate the sheer volume of material being produced.

Finally, mathematical training must continue to reward understanding. Written work alone will become a less reliable guide to individual capability when papers, reports and applications can be written mostly using AI. Seminars, discussions and collaborations can reveal whether someone understands an argument, can defend it and can connect it to other ideas. Demonstrating understanding has always been a more important quality in university students than simply presenting the answer; education and recruitment should now reflect that principle more explicitly.

Critics might argue that this is an overreaction. Mathematical claims still require proof, peer review already evaluates significance, and AI is simply another tool, like a calculator or computer algebra system. There is a degree of truth to each objection. Yet the explosion in the number of published papers changes the nature of the game. A calculator does not generate hundreds of research claims that specialists must spend precious time checking. AI can create arguments faster than the community can verify, interpret or absorb them. Existing safeguards were not designed for that imbalance.

Recent advances have understandably generated both excitement and anxiety. The right response is neither to reject AI nor to unguardedly celebrate every finding it produces. Journals, universities and funders should adapt their incentives now, before quantity overwhelms judgment completely.

In an environment increasingly capable of automatically generating research-level mathematics, the most valuable experts will be those who know which questions are worth asking, which results deserve trust and which ideas are worth our attention.

In Grand Theft Auto VI, that now also includes hitting the gym. And if you want to progress through the story as efficiently as possible, becoming an in-game gym rat might be the best thing you can do.

Expanding on a gameplay mechanic introduced in Grand Theft Auto: San Andreas, working out in GTA levels up your character to help with progressing through the story. The fitter you become in the game, the more stamina you have to sprint, the more health you have before you’re Wasted, and the more focus you have to take down enemies. Just like in real life, pumping iron makes daily life a whole lot easier.

To get fit, you can use the weights in Jason’s outdoor gym, which features the big three full-body lifts: squats, bench presses, and deadlifts. While your character handles the heavy lifting, all you have to do is carefully time your button presses to complete reps without causing an injury. Once you have enough money, you can purchase memberships at gyms across the city, giving you access to treadmills, free weights, and workout machines. The exercise options are so extensive (you can even do zone 2 cardio) that gamers have already started trading tips to the best GTA 6 workouts for maximizing your character’s gains.

As your character gets stronger, their avatar transforms accordingly, developing more defined muscles, a six-pack, and even a chiseled face. Inevitably there will be many players that skip the gym this weekend so they can pump a little virtual iron with their thumbs. There’s something undeniably ironic about looksmaxxing your GTA character while spending hours sitting on the couch playing a video game.
