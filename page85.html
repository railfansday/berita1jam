1 High-Flying Stock to Buy Hand Over Fist if the Stock Market Crashes

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Moderna's cancer vaccine data were historic, but the stock prices in a flawless future.

By Prosper Junior Bakiny – Nov 20, 2026 at 12:21PM EST

Innovation often pays off. Moderna (MRNA -0.92%) has proven as much this year. The company's shares have soared in 2026 on the back of impressive clinical and regulatory progress. But is there any upside left for the vaccine maker? Not in my view. But Moderna would be a great stock to buy if the stock market crashes. Read on to find out more.

Moderna is pioneering mRNA-based personalized cancer vaccines. These products offer the potential to train patients' immune systems to recognize and destroy cancer cells based on specific mutations present in each patient's tumor. This approach could lead to much greater treatment efficacy than conventional cancer medicines. Personalized cancer vaccines may also more effectively prevent recurrence.

Moderna provided the first positive Phase 3 clinical trial data for an investigational personalized cancer vaccine, intismeran autogene, earlier this year. Moderna is developing intismeran autogene with Merck (MRK +1.59%), and in this Phase 3 study, the personalized cancer vaccine was tested as a combination therapy with Keytruda -- the best-selling cancer drug in the world -- versus Keytruda alone in certain patients with melanoma. Intismeran autogene, combined with Keytruda, led to meaningful improvements in recurrence-free survival. This clinical win sent Moderna's shares up by more than 100% overnight.

The market is seeing beyond Moderna's clinical win. The company proved that personalized cancer vaccines can reach significant clinical success. Intismeran autogene hasn't done so yet, but based on its Phase 3 results, it should eventually generate significant sales. Some analysts expect the personalized cancer vaccine to generate more than $10 billion in revenue at its peak, especially as it earns approvals across additional indications beyond melanoma.

It is being invested in as a treatment across several other forms of cancer. And the rest of the company's lineup and pipeline also looks robust. For instance, Moderna has earned approval for mFLUSIVA, an influenza vaccine, earlier this year. Its coronavirus vaccine is still on the market, and it is developing several other candidates in infectious diseases.

Some of the company's targets look ambitious. Moderna is developing a potential HIV vaccine, for instance. There is a long road ahead for this candidate, but if it is successful, it could be a game changer. That said, Moderna's most important strength isn't any single pipeline candidate or currently approved product.

It is the company's innovative mRNA-based platform that should produce significant clinical milestones over the next five to 10 years. However, there is a problem. The market may have already factored Moderna's success into its share price. Consider that the company's market cap is $82 billion, as of writing. Even assuming Moderna could generate $10 billion in sales next year (which it won't), it would be trading at 8.2x estimated 2027 sales, a fairly expensive valuation for a biotech stock of this size.

True, more clinical wins could jolt the stock, but even with that in mind, I am not convinced the valuation makes sense. After all, even intismeran autogene isn't approved yet, and some other advanced pipeline candidates may not earn U.S. approval for another two years. Adding commercial ramp-up times, we are looking at revenue far into the future even for the company's most advanced programs, and that's assuming they are successful at all.

The uncertainty of these future revenues (and cash flows) makes for a somewhat risky outlook at current levels: Too much could go wrong and sink the stock. I'd like a much more reasonable valuation and a significant margin of error -- considering we are discussing a volatile biotech stock -- before investing. If Moderna's market cap falls below $50 billion in a market crash, I'd definitely consider buying because the business looks strong, the science is impeccable, and the company's future looks bright.

But valuation is a major sticking point. I am keeping Moderna on my watch list for now.

GTA 6's Autosave Is Ruining The Game, But Not For The Reason You Think

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

GTA 6 fans say they're losing many hours of progress, but that's not the whole story

Rockstar's Grand Theft Auto 6 might be the best-rated game of all time, but that doesn't mean players love every single thing about the open-world shooter. Some drawbacks, like the game's wonky controls, can be adjusted. Others, players are simply stuck with. One of the biggest complaints is that GTA 6's autosave feature simply isn't good enough.

The issue began attracting attention after high-profile incidents involving Twitch streamers like JasonTheWeen, who lost 12 hours of progress after accidentally turning off his PlayStation 5. It's reasonable to expect that closing a game suddenly might result in some lost progress, but losing 12 hourswas so frustrating that the streamer burst into tears on camera.

Most people aren't losing half a day's worth of progress. Early reports suggest that streamers accessing the game early via the "New Zealand trick" are encountering unforeseen issues with the workaround. Still, many GTA 6 fans were incensed enough about lost playtime to complain about the autosave feature​​​​​​​ on social media.

"4 hours gone, no autosave, no save file..." one fan wrote on a gaming Facebook group. "What the actual! A billion dollar game and this has broken already!"

"I lost 10 hours to their​​​​​​​ dumbass save system," one Redditor alleged.

"What genius came up with this save feature," another complained.

The problem is partially due to a knowledge gap. GTA 6 does autosave​​​​​​​, typically at the start of a mission or at a checkpoint​​​​​​​. If a player elects to spend hours between missions shopping or cruising the streets, GTA 6 won't autosave any of that game time. Players should regularly pause the game and save manually.

The issue isn't just infrequent saving.​​​​​​​ Progress in GTA 6 is slow-going compared to previous games.​​​​​​​ It's harder to steal cars now, and the map is much bigger, so getting anywhere takes time. The roads are narrower, so it's easier to get into a car crash. Many players find themselves struggling with the game's idiosyncratic controls. It's easy to aggravate an NPC by pressing the wrong button, which can set off a chain of unwanted events. Loading an earlier save to undo the damage might also mean losing significant progress.

Many of GTA 6's systems mimic real life, and the autosave issue can make the quiet yet rewarding gameplay feel tedious. Even if a player loses just 20 minutes, during that time they could havewoken up​​​​​​​, worked out, drunk a protein shake, showered, chosena new outfit, and answered some texts​​​​​​​. They spoke to the neighbor, picked up a new quest, and biked to the grocery store. While buying bread,​​​​​​​ they accidentally pulled out a gun and scared the cashier. The worker called the police.

It wouldn't take that long to do it all over again from an earlier save file​​​​​​​, but repeating everything would be a major bummer. The player could try to outrun the cops — but then getting back to what they intended to do might take twice as long. That's assuming that nothing goes wrong during the chase, which is a tall order in the world of GTA.

That propensity for chaos might explain why Rockstar's current system works the way it does. If GTA 6 saved more often, players would be perpetually stuck with the haywire consequences of their actions. There must be a middle ground, though.​​​​​​​ The game could autosave more frequently, across multiple temporary save files. That tweak still wouldn't change GTA 6's focus on the monotony of day-to-day life, which is what fans are truly struggling with.

The likelihood of an update here seems slim since Rockstar has used a similar system for saves across many of its games​​​​​​​. Perhaps the best GTA 6 fans can do is save often​​​​​​​ — and learn to embrace the pandemonium.

Nvidia co-founder Jensen Huang accused of decades of bullying and workplace abuse in new investigation

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Public humiliation reportedly considered a "rite of passage".

An investigation into the workplace behavior of Nvidia co-founder Jensen Huang, who resigned recently after a series of extramarital affairs came to light, has uncovered decades of harassment, verbal employee abuse and "cultish" behaviour at the company which has long played a major role in PC gaming.

Following Huang's departure, The New York Times has published a report in which "more than a dozen" current and former Nvidia employees discussed their experiences with the former CEO. Speaking on conditions of anonymity, employees alleged Jensen Huang would verbally berate them, reportedly going as far as to say he'd rip their arms off, rip their heart out and stomp on it, or rip their head off and "pee on your brain".

The report includes numerous examples of Huang's alleged abusive behaviour, which apparently stretches back years before Nvidia became the one of the most valuable companies in the world - years before the AI boom. It's claimed Huang on one occasion went around a room of managers telling them he'd kill them. Then, when he reached a person in their 70s, said everyone hoped they'd drop dead.

Huang allegedly referred to these bouts of public humiliation as a "rite of passage" at Nvidia, when questioned by an internal HR employee whom he later fired for not being a "right fit". Huan's stance was apparently to test whether a person could handle the culture there, which is described in the report as "cultish".

It's claimed Huang's influence at Nvidia has meant other senior managers have followed his lead, including his daughter Madison, Nvidia's chief of staff, who it's said often yells at staff, including executives more highly ranked than her.

A spokesperson for Huang denied the allegations in response to a request for comment by the New York Times. The spokesperson, John Fisher, said the claims in the report were "fabrications that are now impossible to disprove specifically, and the thousands of Nvidia employees who continue to speak out for Jensen show they’re false".

Huang resigned from his role as Nvidia CEO earlier this month after a series of extramarital affairs were unearthed - one with a person US intelligence believes to be a Chinese spy, and two others with Nvidia employees. The chair of Nvidia's board, Harvey C. Jones, reportedly said earlier this week that the company "does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO".
