Everton are reportedly interested in signing Palmeiras striker Vitor Roque ahead of the 2027 transfer window, but the Brazilian club are demanding a fee of at least €50 million for his services.

The 21-year-old has attracted attention from several Premier League clubs, and Everton are now considering a potential move as they look to strengthen their attacking options.

According to a report from Brazilian journalist Diego Firmino, Palmeiras are unwilling to negotiate for less than €50m (£44m), despite the striker’s difficult campaign.

The Brazilian forward enjoyed an impressive campaign in 2025, scoring 20 goals and providing five assists in 51 appearances for Palmeiras. His performances attracted considerable attention from English clubs, with Chelsea, Manchester United and Tottenham also exploring a move.

Everton could now provide fresh competition for his signature, although they will need to overcome Palmeiras’ considerable financial demands.

The Brazilian outfit invested €25.5m to bring Roque back from Barcelona, with additional bonuses that could increase the transfer fee. The Spanish giants also retained 20% of his economic rights, while his current contract runs until December 2029.

Roque has struggled to replicate his previous performances this season, managing nine goals and one assist in 39 appearances. An ankle injury required surgery and kept him out of action for approximately three months between April and July.

However, the young attacker has continued to demonstrate his potential since returning, and his previous experience at Barcelona and Real Betis could make him an attractive addition for Everton.

It remains to be seen whether the Merseyside outfit will be prepared to meet Palmeiras’ €50m valuation. The 2027 transfer window gives Roque a chance to rediscover his best form, but Everton will want assurances before committing such a significant amount.

Christopher Nolan’s ‘The Odyssey’ Will Return to Theaters Next Month as Awards Season Heats Up

Universal's epic has collected $626 million domestically for a global cume of $1.8 billion.

The theatrical journey continues for Christopher Nolan‘s The Odyssey.

Universal Pictures‘ acclaimed and starry mythological epic is set for a nationwide theatrical rerelease on Dec. 11 after its record-setting run over the summer. Starring Matt Damon as Odysseus, The Odyssey will return to more than 1,000 North American theaters in all formats, including Imax and Imax 70mm.

With Nolan and Emma Thomas producing for Syncopy, The Odyssey has collected $626 million domestically for a global cume of $1.8 billion. It continues to play in international markets in all formats and recently surpassed 2015’s Furious 7 as Universal’s highest-grossing international release ever. Universal announced in September that the film had surpassed 2015’s Jurassic World to become the studio’s highest-grossing release of all time. It had previously supplanted 2012’s The Dark Knight Rises as the top-grossing feature in the acclaimed filmmaker’s career after opening on July 17 with Nolan’s biggest debut frame internationally and globally.

This rerelease is well-timed as awards season heats up, with The Odyssey expected to be among the top candidates for an array of Academy Award categories, including best picture. Tom Holland, Anne Hathaway, Robert Pattinson, Lupita Nyong’o, Zendaya and Charlize Theron round out the cast for the feature adaptation of Homer’s ancient poem about Odysseus (Damon) making his way home to Ithaca after the Trojan War.

After becoming the first narrative feature to be shot entirely on Imax film cameras, The Odyssey notched the biggest debut frame domestically for Imax 70mm and delivered the studio’s highest-grossing opening for an R-rated title. Nolan’s film continued to hit milestones throughout its run, becoming the highest-grossing R-rated movie ever, in addition to Imax’s highest-grossing feature ever.

The Odyssey stands just outside the all-time global top 10 chart, holding down the No. 11 spot behind Zootopia 2 and its $1.87 billion. Nolan’s film is No. 15 on the all-time domestic chart and remains below Jurassic World’s $652 million for Universal’s biggest North American haul.

Electronic Arts is launching 25 classic games on Steam, including “Anniversary Editions” of the motorcycle racer Road Rash and six Wing Commander titles. These Anniversary Editions preserve “the original look, feel and creative intent of the game” but also have quality-of-life updates to support modern systems and controls and improve performance, according to EA.

The seven new editions were “inspired” by the discovery of a “physical media vault” with “thousands” of Betacam, VHS, and D1 tapes and “other media assets,” EA says. As part of the digitization process, EA was able to recover master FMV sequences and reedited them to restore more than 1,500 scenes from the games. The updated Wing Commander games also officially incorporate some community-made mods.

You’ll be able to get the Road Rash and Wing Commander Anniversary editions for $9.99, and the other classic games, including Ultima and Crusader titles, will be available for $4.99. You can grab them on Steam, GOG, and the EA App.

Jared Verse was upset that Browns teammates didn't react when he took a cheap shot

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Browns defensive end Jared Verse was picked up and thrown to the ground by Steelers guard Mason McCormick on Sunday, and McCormick wasn’t the only person Verse was upset with afterward.

Verse told reporters in Cleveland today that he was upset both by McCormick’s cheap shot and by his teammates’ reaction, or lack thereof. Verse thought the rest of the Browns’ players should have had his back, and didn’t.

“To be quite honest with you, that was the play that made me really angry,” Verse said. “I was already upset about the game, the way things were moving, but at that point it became deep. You’re doing dirty shit, you’re trying to sweep my legs out. That honestly could have put me in a position to not be able to finish the season, put me in a position to change my life in a very bad aspect, so that upset me. The lack of reaction by those around me had upset me a lot. They were focused on the game and everything like that, but a similar play happened to Derek Barnett on the next field goal, and I spazzed, I lost it. Because those are my brothers, those are my teammates, and when you do something like that to them, it upsets me deeply. We have to get better, we have to be aware. These are my teammates, these are my brothers, and we’re not fighting for each other.”

Verse said he addressed his feelings with his teammates. Verse did acknowledge that he brought some of it on himself by the trash talk he was directing at the Steelers, but he still felt that his teammates should have protected him when it escalated from verbal to physical.

“That’s a conversation that we’ve had, we’ve spoken about,” Verse said. “I made my concerns known, I’ve made my issues known about the situation, but also, I didn’t put them in a position to let them know that, I didn’t let them know beforehand I was going to talk to the Steelers and Steeler Nation. I’ve got to be better.”

Verse, the 2024 NFL Defensive Rookie of the Year who arrived in Cleveland in the trade that sent Myles Garrett to the Rams, has been a disappointment this season. His teammates may be wishing he’d focus a little less on talking trash and getting into altercations on the field, and a little more on sacking the opposing quarterback — something he hasn’t done yet this season.

Newcastle United star Amar Dedic was at the centre of a major police operation after he was spotted carrying a bow and arrows at a Northumberland beauty spot – with at least SIX patrol cars involved in the search for the £30million defender.

The Bosnia international sparked alarm when a concerned member of the public reported seeing him and a friend walking with the weapon near a reservoir in the countryside outside Newcastle.

Police dispatched marked and unmarked vehicles to the area in Harlow Hill, with some understood to have been armed response units, before Dedic and his companion were finally intercepted around seven miles away in the exclusive Darras Hall estate.

Sources have described the dramatic swoop on Dedic's vehicle before officers searched the car and seized a bow and arrows, with the 24-year-old questioned about his activities.

In a bizarre twist, Newcastle goalkeeper Mark Gillespie and former defender Paul Dummett arrived at the scene while police were speaking to Dedic. It is understood the pair were in the area by chance and stopped to offer assistance.

After speaking to the two men, officers were satisfied there was no threat to the public, with police subsequently stating that the equipment was believed to have been intended for recreational use.

Amar Dedic played in Newcastle's 1-0 defeat by Everton on Monday evening

Dedic was spotted with a crossbow off Military Road, the 61-mile B6318 in Northumberland

However, enquiries remain ongoing into the incident, which happened on Wednesday afternoon. It is understood that Dedic and his friend had fired some arrows while in the countryside, although it remains unclear what they were aiming at. Hunting any animal or bird with a bow and arrow or crossbow is illegal in the UK.

Dedic joined Newcastle from Benfica during the summer and has impressed at right back despite the club's difficult start to the season.

Daily Mail Sport understands he will not face disciplinary action from Newcastle over the incident, although there is a feeling that the player was naive to believe he could walk around the countryside carrying such weaponry.

Swansea City have hit a poor run of form after a phenomenal start to the season. The Swans were top of the pile after 12 games, but a run of three defeats in four games has seen them drop to 4th, with a midweek defeat to Portsmouth the latest in that run.

During that Pompey clash, summer signing Stephen Welsh went off with a knee problem that appeared serious. However, as they head to Birmingham City this weekend, Vitor Matos has revealed that the injury is not as bad as first thought and he could be in contention to face Chris Davies’ side, as reported by Wales Online.

Matos said: “We will still assess if he will be available for Saturday but it’s not a bad injury. It’s just a knock that is really really painful.”

Welsh has made a great impression since making the switch from Celtic in the summer. Eom Jisung and Moussa Yeo are back available for the Swans this weekend as well, while Ben Cabango is also fit despite a recent knock.

Welsh has quickly established himself as one of Swansea City’s most important players since arriving on a free transfer from Celtic in summer 2026. The 26-year-old signed a two-year contract, with the option of a further year, becoming Vitor Matos’ first summer recruit.

Cameron Burgess’ long-term Achilles injury opened the door for Welsh, who has since made the centre-back position his own. He has started 13 of Swansea’s 14 Championship matches, primarily alongside Ben Cabango, and has impressed with his composure, defensive work and ability to play out from the back.

His passing accuracy, progressive distribution and contribution to Swansea’s defensive organisation have made him an ideal fit for Matos’ possession-based approach. His performances have also earned him his first senior Scotland caps.

With Swansea challenging near the top of the table, Welsh has become a key figure. Securing an experienced defender of his quality on a free transfer is proving to be excellent summer business.

Swansea are short of centre-back options without Welsh, although their defensive depth remains manageable in the short term.

Cabango is the obvious leader, while Jenson Seelt offers the most natural replacement after already featuring under Matos. The Sunderland loanee would be the likely partner for Cabango if Welsh is unavailable.

Filip Lissah provides further cover, having demonstrated his versatility by operating centrally, but regularly relying on him could weaken the right-back position. Cameron Burgess remains sidelined with a long-term Achilles injury, leaving Swansea more exposed if Welsh faces an extended absence.

Victor Mendivil Crashes Latin Albums Chart’s Top 10 With ‘No Hay Ángeles Aquí’

Victor Mendivil earns his first top 10 on Billboard’s Top Latin Albums chart as No Hay Ángeles Aquí debuts at No. 6 on the Nov. 7-dated ranking. The set also opens at No. 3 on Top Latin Rhythm Albums, his second top 10 there.

No Hay Ángeles Aquí is Mendivil’s sophomore release on the Rico o Muerto label, cofounded by singer Oscar Maydón and manager and songwriter Alexis “Chachito” Fierro. The labelmates have linked up on two tracks: “Me Paseo” from Mendivil’s debut album, Tuntankamon, and “Bling Bling,” part of the 23-track album No Hay Ángeles Aquí.

Twenty-year-old Mendivil broke onto the Billboard charts with Tuntankamon, which launched at Nos. 19 and 9 on Top Latin Albums and Top Latin Rhythm Albums charts, respectively, in August 2025. Born in Obregón, Sonora, Mendivil is another one of the new wave of Mexican acts who fuses corridos alterados with trap — a trail also blazed by Rico o Muerto labelmates Maydón, Omar Camacho and Regulo Molina.

No Hay Ángeles Aquí debuts at No. 6 on Top Latin Albums with just over 9,000 equivalent album units earned in the United States during the Oct. 23–29 tracking week, according to Luminate. Streaming drove most of the album’s opening sum, with its songs generating 10 million official on-demand streams.

Notably, Mendivil also breaks through the all-genre Billboard 200, as the album debuts at No. 174.

In addition to the top 10 start on the overall Top Latin Albums chart, Mendivil’s sophomore album gives him his best mark yet on Top Latin Rhythm Albums, where it starts at No. 3, following Tuntankamon’s No. 9 debut and peak.

Two songs from No Hay Ángeles Aquí arrive on Hot Latin Songs — which measures airplay, streams and sales in its formula. “Cansada” with sister Victoria Mendivil joins the chart at No. 35, while “FES,” with Camacho, opens at No. 36. The latter also secures a No. 18 start on the Hot Latin Rhythm Songs chart.

Elsewhere, thanks to “Cansada,” Victoria earns her first entry across three charts. In addition to Hot Latin Songs, the team-up debuts at No. 199 on the Billboard Global Excl. U.S. chart. Plus, achieves her first top 10, as the song debuts at No. 3 on Hot Latin Pop Songs.

On Nov. 5, 2002, NASA's old Galileo spacecraft was almost out of fuel. But the Jupiter mission still had some science to do. Galileo flew by a small moon called Amalthea. Amalthea is small, but it's interesting for scientists.

The Jupiter moon Amalthea is the reddest object in our solar system. It also generates more heat than it gets from the sun. Scientists think Amalthea creates electrical currents as it orbits in Jupiter's huge magnetic field. Near this little moon, strong radiation from Jupiter is dangerous for spacecraft.

The flyby caused some problems for Galileo. The spacecraft went into safe mode only 30 minutes after going past Amalthea. NASA said it was ready for problems, because Galileo was on its last legs, and the radiation near Amalthea was strong. However, NASA quickly fixed the problem and got Galileo working again.

Less than a year later, Galileo threw itself into Jupiter's atmosphere. The spacecraft was crushed by the high pressure in Jupiter's atmosphere, but its data still survives on Earth.

The Galileo spacecraft was running on cosmic fumes by the time it reached Amalthea and the flyby would be the last time the probe would ever swing by one of Jupiter's moons.

While it may not look like it in Galileo's black and white images above and below, Amalthea is tinted red due sulfur contamination on its surface. The moon is small, about 168 miles long, and orbits about 112,468 miles (181,000 km) from Jupiter (much closer to the planet than the Earth and its moon).

Galileo flew within 99 miles of Amalthea during its flyby, which also marked the closest Galileo would get to Jupiter before its death dive in September 2005.

"Scientists studying data from NASA's Galileo spacecraft have found that Jupiter's moon Amalthea is a pile of icy rubble less dense than water," NASA announced in May 2005

"Amalthea is throwing us a curve ball," Torrence Johnson, a study co-author on the Almathea flyby and project scientist for the Galileo mission at JPL, said in 2005. "Its density is well below that of water ice, and even with substantial porosity, Amalthea probably contains a lot of water ice, as well as rock."

Originally known as Jupiter V (because it was the fifth Jupiter moon discovered after the so-called Galilean moons Io, Europa, Ganymede, Callisto), Amalthea was named after naiad, or water nymph, from Greek mythology.

It is fitting that Amalthea was the last of Jupiter's moons to be visited by the Galileo spacecraft. The moon was also last Jupiter moon to be discovered by visual observations when it was first disovered by astronomer Edward Emerson Barnard in September 1892 using the Lick Observatory telescope in California.

The Galileo spacecraft ultimately crashed into Jupiter on Sept. 21, 2005, less than a year after its Amalthea flyby.

Hong Kong: Joshua Wong Gets Nearly 10 More Years in Prison

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Click to expand Image Joshua Wong speaks to protesters near the Legislative Council in Hong Kong, June 17, 2019.  © 2019 Kin Cheung/AP Photo 

(Tokyo) – The Hong Kong High Court on November 5, 2026, sentenced Joshua Wong, a well-known democracy activist, to 10 years in prison on baseless national security charges, Human Rights Watch said today. Two months of the sentence will run concurrently with his current national security sentence. Wong has been in custody since November 2020, when he was 24, and faces imprisonment until late 2036, when he will be 40.

The Hong Kong government should immediately and unconditionally release Wong. Foreign governments should denounce the sentences against him and other wrongfully detained pro-democracy activists and impose targeted sanctions on officials responsible for Hong Kong’s growing repression.

“The harsh sentence on baseless charges against Joshua Wong is cruel and unjust,” said Elaine Pearson, Asia director at Human Rights Watch. “Young people promoting social change are an asset for any vibrant society, but the Hong Kong government puts them behind bars.”

To keep Hong Kong society subdued, and to reduce the chance of an international outcry, the Hong Kong government appears to have adopted the tactic of stacking charges against influential pro-democracy leaders to ensure their prolonged detention, Human Rights Watch said. Other notable Hong Kong figures who have faced similar tactics include Jimmy Lai, founder of Apple Daily, and veteran democracy activists Lee Cheuk-yan and Albert Ho.

The court sentenced Wong, 30, under the National Security Law for “conspiring to collude with foreign forces.” The authorities accused him of “conspiring” with the activist Nathan Law and other “unknown” individuals between 2016 and November 2020 to urge foreign governments to sanction Hong Kong and Chinese government officials as they advocated for Hong Kong’s “democratic self-determination.” 

The prosecution relied heavily on the international lobbying and media advocacy that Wong and Demosisto, the political party Wong co-founded in 2016, carried out before the National Security Law took effect. Wong initiated online petitions, spoke to international media, and met with foreign officials to draw attention to human rights abuses in Hong Kong. He urged governments to sanction Chinese and Hong Kong officials responsible for abuses and to stop selling weapons to Hong Kong police that they use to carry out rights violations.

Wong had mostly stopped these activities before the National Security Law became effective. Demosisto disbanded on June 30, 2020, and Wong resigned from the group. However, the government quoted social media posts by Wong as ostensible evidence that his alleged “conspiracy” with Law to commit “foreign collusion” continued after the law took effect. In these posts, Wong said he “would not change his views” and that he would “continue to seek support from the international community” in his personal capacity, while Law and others abroad would continue their international advocacy.

The government also cited Wong’s subsequent opinion pieces and media interviews, though none directly called for sanctions, which the National Security Law specifically prohibits. It also cited his reposts of Law’s posts broadly calling for sanctions against Chinese officials and Hong Kong police.

The court ruled that Wong’s offense was of a “grave nature.” The National Security Law sets prison terms of 3 to 10 years for “foreign collusion,” or 10 years to life for offenses deemed to be of a “grave nature.” Wong formally pleaded guilty on September 2. A guilty plea normally results in a one-third sentence reduction. But a sentence reduction cannot bring the term below the statutory minimum.

Because of reporting restrictions on pretrial committal proceedings, when a magistrate determines whether to send a case to a higher court for trial, the public did not know the details of the accusations against Wong until the formal plea. The government enacted a law in 2024 to enable the court, on national security grounds, to deny the defendant’s right to ask the court to lift restrictions on reporting about the case.

By restricting media reporting on the pretrial proceedings in national security cases, the government can control the narrative and undermine the fundamental legal principle of open justice.

Since Beijing imposed the National Security Law on Hong Kong in June 2020, Hong Kong authorities have used the new legal regime—along with existing laws—to rapidly dismantle civil society and detain, imprison, or force into exile hundreds of activists and government critics.

Wong has been in custody since November 2020, when he pleaded guilty to two charges of organizing and inciting an unauthorized assembly linked to the 2019 citywide protest movement. After that, he pleaded guilty to five more charges on separate occasions, including for violating an anti-mask law, participating in an unauthorized vigil for the 1989 Tiananmen Massacre, and breaching a court ban to disclose a police officer’s personal information.

While serving these sentences, Wong was charged with two national security offenses, which carry far harsher penalties. In January 2021, Wong was charged with “conspiring to commit subversion” for participating in an unofficial democratic primary in 2020 and sentenced to another four years and eight months in prison. 

Before Wong could complete these sentences, the authorities brought the “foreign collusion” charge against him in June 2025, ensuring that he would remain in prison.

Wong became one of Hong Kong’s best-known democracy activists as a teenager, after leading a massive 2012 protest against a government educational curriculum widely seen as political indoctrination. He became the global face of Hong Kong’s democracy movement during the 2014 Umbrella Movement and co-founded Demosisto with Law and others in 2016. The party disbanded on June 30, 2020, hours before the National Security Law came into effect.

The National Security Law’s sweeping provisions have been used to punish peaceful protest and expression. Its use against Wong and others violates the right to freedom of expression under the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights, which is incorporated into Hong Kong law through the city’s de facto constitution, the Basic Law, and the Bill of Rights Ordinance.

The health system's admissions grew by 0.1% and its adjusted admissions by 2.9%, year-over-year improvements over the second quarter, with net patient service revenue per adjusted admission increasing 0.7% since Q3 2025. Inpatient surgeries dropped by 5% and outpatient surgeries by 0.6% compared to the prior-year period, with Chief Financial Officer Alfred Lumsdaine noting that about half of those drops were tied to the health system's "intentional rationalization of certain lower-margin service lines in targeted locations."

Executives said they were happy with the volumes, noting that a 7% drop in exchange admissions and corresponding self-pay increase, similar to Q2’s 8%, was “manageable and largely contemplated in our guidance.” They particularly pointed to strong showings at the top of their “demand funnel,” or areas like urgent care or physician visits that can lead to downstream utilization. Part of the company’s ongoing improvement efforts are focused on boosting access to these offerings while reducing “friction as we move patients through the journey” to capture more care.

“For us, there's a lot of we'll call it embedded growth or backed-up growth that we just need to unlock by reducing friction and ensuring that we're making the process of converting the top of the funnel to the bottom of the funnel easier,” Lumsdaine said.

On the expense side, executives said Project IMPACT helped pull SWB spending down to 39.9% of revenue during the quarter, a 300-basis-point improvement from last year, and slash contract labor from 3.5% of total SWB to 2%. Professional fee expense growth also declined from Q1 2026’s 12.9% to Q3’s 7.4%, with that slowdown expected to slow further in Q4. Supply growth was “a modest 2.6%,” per Lumsdaine.

The performance and expected efficiency gains led Ardent to adjust its full-year 2026 guidance. While revenues are still anticipated to land on “the lower end of our $6.4 billion to $6.7 billion range,” the target for adjusted EBIDTA rose 1.5% at the midpoint.

On expectations for 2027, they declined to outline any specific number targets but noted well-recognized industry headwinds like Medicaid work requirements, marketplace plan attrition, as well as tailwinds like the Project IMPACT performance improvements and the recently finalized outpatient payment rule.

“I'm extremely proud of the progress our team has made this year, despite very fluid industry and macroeconomic dynamics,” Lumsdaine said. “We continue to strengthen our operating model, and we remain focused on building on our performance, generating strong cash flow and creating long-term shareholder value by aligning our people, capital and assets with the highest value opportunities.”

Ardent Health’s operations include 30 acute care hospitals and over 280 care sites, several of which are run as part of a joint venture structure with other health system partners. In 2025, the company increased its revenues by 6% to $6.3 billion and grew its adjusted EBITDA to $545 million.

Oscar Health is seeing the “strongest year” in its history in 2026, CEO Mark Bertolini told investors during the company’s third quarter earnings call Thursday morning.

“We delivered record profitability for the first nine months of the year, reporting earnings from operations of $1.3 billion and net income of $1.2 billion,” he said. “We also expect to achieve several of our 2024 Investor Day commitments one year ahead of plan.”

Oscar Health brought in $5.1 billion in Q3 revenue, up “roughly 70%” year-over-year, Bertolini said. It beat analysts’ estimates of $4.72 billion.

Its medical loss ratio was 84%, down from 88.5% year-over-year, according to financial results published Thursday morning.

The company ended Q3 with 2.7 million members, up 27% year-over-year, Bertolini said, adding it is entering open enrollment with a “24% larger footprint” with plans to expand into South Carolina. “Our expansion increases our total addressable market to approximately 10 million consumers across 21 states,” he said.

Moreover, Oscar Health is launching a “new wave” of consumer lifestyle products and “addressing affordability through bundled benefits,” Bertolini said.

He also pointed to new artificial intelligence capabilities across the Oscar Health platform, including Lucie. Unveiled in April, the platform is a one-stop shop for individuals seeking health plans and supplemental benefit options.

“No other platform brings these capabilities together in one place, he said. “Lucie is on track to sell approximately 800,000 new policies in 2027, driven by growing broker and consumer demand.”

Based on the company’s performance, CFO Scott Blackley said Oscar Health is raising its 2026 full year outlook. Earnings are expected to be in a range of $850 million to $950 million, Blackley said.

Total revenue is expected to be in the range of $19 billion to $19.3 billion, which Blackley notes is “an increase of $300 million” from its prior outlook.

Blackley continued that the company expects to end the year with 2.5 million members, a figure that reflects “additional disenrollments from the second CMS review.” A full-year MLR of 80.5% to 81.5% is also expected.
