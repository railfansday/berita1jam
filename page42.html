To appoint the next comptroller general of the United States, follow the law

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The new approach being employed to appoint the next Comptroller General of the United States should raise a red flag. The Government Accountability Office (GAO), which is headed by the Comptroller General, plays an important role in helping Congress discharge its responsibilities under Article I of the U.S. Constitution, particularly in connection with oversight of the Executive Branch.

We were shocked to learn that U.S. Sen. Rand Paul (R-KY), Chairman of the Senate Committee on Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs (HSGAC), recently and spontaneously requested that names be submitted to him, under a short time constraint, to be recommended to the President for nomination as the next Comptroller General of the United States.

Under the GAO Act of 1980, a 10-member statutory bipartisan and bicameral Comptroller General Selection Commission was created. Chairman Paul is a member, since he is Chair of the HSGAC, but he is not the Chairman of the Commission. That Commission is required to jointly develop and transmit a list of at least three qualified nominees to the President. That is the law, and that is how the process of appointing the Comptroller General has been conducted since 1980.

The current vacancy for Comptroller General of the United States began on Dec. 30, 2025, and yet the Commission and its members have never met.

Equally shocking is that Sen. Paul took the liberty as HSGAC Chairman to insert an empty placeholder for an unnamed nominee for Comptroller General of the United States for a Nov. 19, 2026, Committee confirmation hearing.

Again, Sen. Paul did so even though the statutorily required Comptroller General Selection Commission has never convened or submitted a list of candidates. Then, on Nov. 16, President Trump submitted a nominee for confirmation. (We don’t want to name the candidate since we do not want to make it a personal issue.)

The General Accounting Office Act of 1980 included a revised method for appointing the Comptroller General. It recognized that the position was in the Legislative Branch and that GAO was the primary agency the Congress uses to conduct oversight of the Executive Branch. Therefore, it provided for a bipartisan and bicameral commission to recommend candidates to the President for possible appointment.

It also provided for a 15-year non-renewable term with extremely difficult removal requirements to help ensure the independence of the Comptroller General. This 15-year term is the longest for any appointed position in the federal government.

To be clear, the 1980 law doesn’t say in so many words that the President must pick from the Commission’s list. The Senate report on the bill said he could, “in his discretion,” look elsewhere, but should give the Commission’s recommendations “great weight.” You can’t weigh a list that doesn’t exist. Every Comptroller General nominated since 1980 came from a Commission list, and the law’s answer to a President who wants other options is to ask the Commission for more names, not to skip it. Henry Wray, a former GAO attorney who helped develop the 1980 law, says the current unprecedented process “violates both the letter and the spirit of the law.”

It is extremely important for Congress to follow the statutory process. Dave Walker and Gene Dodaro (two of the coauthors of this piece) were unanimously confirmed under this process in 1998 and 2010, respectively. Both of these appointments were made under the statutory commission method, which has worked like a charm.

With regard to potential candidates, any Comptroller General should be a highly qualified executive with relevant past experience who will operate in a professional, objective, fact-based, non-partisan, non-ideological, and non-personal manner. The Comptroller General must be independent from the Executive Branch, which the GAO audits, investigates, and evaluates on behalf of the Congress and the American people. In addition, any Comptroller General must have broad bipartisan support. In fact, every one of the past eight Comptrollers General was confirmed with overwhelming bipartisan support. All of these factors are of critical importance for the Comptroller General to be credible and for him or her, and the GAO, to be effective.

Since Senate Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs Committee Chairman Paul and President Trump have not followed the statutory requirements for the appointment of the Comptroller General, the Nov. 19, 2026, confirmation hearing should be canceled, and the normal appointment process should be adhered to.

The Hot Read: The Jaguars have quietly become the NFL’s most complete team

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Each NFL season, there is always a handful of teams that stand above the rest in terms of their dominance. The San Francisco 49ers sport the league's best record at 8-1 and are coming off a 46-point performance against the Dallas Cowboys. Tied with them for the NFL's top record is the Buffalo Bills, who have won each of their last three games by 17+ points. Across the way in the AFC, Joe Burrow and the Bengals just defeated their division rivals, the Pittsburgh Steelers, by 20 points in prime time while Burrow tied an NFL record by throwing four touchdowns in five straight games. And need we not forget about the ever-present Kansas City Chiefs, whose quarterback is coming off his own high-end performance, throwing five touchdowns in a blowout win over the Falcons.

But as the blue bloods of the NFL convene atop their respective standings, standing alone in the corner sipping a Mai Tai with their loose ties hanging from a shirt with the top button loose, are the Jacksonville Jaguars. They don't want to be at this get together. They, frankly, don't care if anyone is talking about them as real Super Bowl threats. They just wanna take the field, kick ass, and go home – which has been their calling card over the last month of the season.

The Jaguars have scored 40+ points in two of their last three games, the third of which featured a 33-point outing. In their last three games, they have outscored their opponents 115-40, and Trevor Lawrence has submitted himself as a MVP candidate for the second straight season.

After finishing fifth in MVP voting a season ago, the former No. 1 overall pick has thrown seven touchdowns to just one interception over Jacksonville's three-game win streak, and he currently sits near the top of the league in nearly every major statistic. Lawrence is fourth in EPA per play among all quarterbacks with at least 100 dropbacks, per Sumer Sports. He is also fourth in success rate, and his 21 touchdown passes are third in the NFL.

It's not just Lawrence that has been fantastic, though. Far from it. Wide receiver Parker Washington has built off a strong 2025 season and blossomed into a true star receiver in 2026. The Penn State product has six touchdowns and is averaging nearly 18 yards per reception as the leader in a stacked Jags' receiving corps. Bhayshul Tuten and Chris Rodriguez have proven to be an electric running back duo, as well, with the two combining for 30 impact plays, 842 rushing yards, and nine rushing scores.

Jacksonville's defense has been terrific, too

. They are the league's best run defense in terms of EPA per rush and rush success rate, and they are one of just three teams who have more interceptions than touchdown passes allowed.

Overall, the Jaguars are the only team in the NFL that is top five in both offensive and defensive EPA. They are the most complete team in the NFL. And, oh by the way, they added Jessie Bates and Jordan Davis at the trade deadline, both of whom are expected to make their Jaguars debuts in Week 11 after sitting out while the Jags dropped 40 on the Titans. And among all this well-constructed talent sits a head coach in Liam Coen who has come in and immediately established himself as one of the game's best. In his first two seasons as head coach, Coen is 20-6. He was the fastest head coach to reach 15 wins, and he is now among the likes of George Seifert and Matt LaFleur as the fastest to reach 20 wins. His presence immediately changed the culture and the ceiling of a franchise that had long found itself among the laughing stocks of the league.

That said, many remain hesitant to mention the Jaguars in the same breath of the current elites in the league until they make a deep playoff run. Every empire needs its first conquest, after all. But the looks of it, Coen's group is well-equipped for a takeover.

That was the cold open – this is The Hot Read.

Now of course things can change between now and January, but as things currently stand, Bengals quarterback Joe Burrow is the 2026 MVP. I'll go ahead and give myself a small pat on the back for predicting as much back in July. I also said he'd throw for 50 touchdowns – he is currently on pace for 51. He has thrown five more touchdowns than anyone else in the league, and if the season ended today, he would be the new NFL record holder for highest completion percentage in a season. His total EPA leads all quarterbacks, and his pass EPA is 15 points higher than second-place Brock Purdy.

It's not even much of a conversation as to who is the top dog in the race. The aforementioned Lawrence is certainly in the conversation, as is Brock Purdy, but after that? Josh Allen, sure, but he only has 13 touchdown passes. And I'm not one who diminishes the rushing touchdowns being from a yard or two away, but at the same time, he's not rushing for a lot of yards with those touchdowns that would justify him being in that top spot. Patrick Mahomes has played second fiddle to Kenneth Walker as the big star of the Chiefs' offense this season, so I wouldn't put him in the picture as of right now. Jordan Love and the Packers have been wildly inconsistent despite his individual brilliance. The clear leader in the race is Burrow. And should he remain on this pace, he'll be a potential unanimous MVP.

A year and a half into DK Metcalf's extension that sees him make $33 million per year, the deal has been an abject disaster for the Steelers. It doesn't get a major spotlight outside of the 412 area code because the Steelers won the AFC North in 2025 and currently sit with a 6-3 record, but their lack of passing game is in large part due to the fact that Metcalf has been a massive disappointment.

The Ole Miss product currently sits with 482 receiving yards, 115 of which came in one game agains the Browns in Week 4. If you take that outlier away, he would have just 317 yards on the season. Five times this season, he's put up 40 or fewer receiving yards, and twice, he's put up 12 or fewer. He has 48 fewer receiving yards than Davante Adams despite the Rams' star playing in three fewer games. He ranks 70th in yards per route run among receivers who have run at least 100 routes, and his target success rate ranks 99th.

Throw in the fact that he has the most accepted penalties of any receiver in the NFL this season, and you get a player who is playing nowhere near the level of someone who is the seventh-highest paid player at his position. Pittsburgh has weathered the storm, largely leaning on Jaylen Warren to be the engine of their offense, while tight ends Pat Freiermuth and Darnell Washington have been relied on as go-to targets in the passing game. However, the lack of vertical passing game is hindering Pittsburgh. Aaron Rodgers is just 9-of-44 on passes 20+ yards downfield, which allows teams to be a bit more aggressive against the short and intermediate passes, as well as the run game – see Cincinnati in Week 10. It's not that Metcalf hasn't played to the level of his contract, it's that he has been a net negative when on the field. And unless something changes drastically between now and the playoffs, the Steelers won't have nearly enough firepower to take on the AFC's top teams.

Like Jake Gyllenhaal and Heath Ledger in Brokeback Mountain, I truly wish I could quit the Houston Texans. After blowing a 17-0 lead against the Cleveland Browns, you would think it would be easy to do that. But then I look at their schedule and see a ton of winnable games still on the horizon. They play the Colts at home on Thursday night, followed by another home game against a bad Ravens team. Two wins get them to 5-6 going into Week 13, where they will travel to Pittsburgh for a Sunday night game that could ultimately decide their season. If they win that game, though, they then face the Washington Commanders, another winnable game. And at 7-6 (presumably) they would then host the Jaguars before trips to Green Bay and Philadelphia and a Week 18 home game against the Titans.

Of their final eight opponents, six rank 18th or worse in offensive EPA, two of which are in the bottom three. And while Houston's defense has underdelivered after being overhyped, their own offense has been quite good, ranking ninth in total EPA and points per game.

The problem for Houston is that their magic number appears to be one. If they get to eight losses, it becomes very hard to see a path to the postseason, especially because the current No. 7 seed Browns hold the tiebreaker. Should Houston want to make another improbable run to the postseason, they'd need to nearly run the table. And even if they do get to 10 wins, there is no guarantee that it would be enough.

In the first game of the Sunday slate, the Detroit Lions and New England Patriots faced off in Germany. At the end of the opening drive, Lions star pass rusher Aidan Hutchinson sacked Patriots quarterback Drake Maye. The 2025 MVP runner-up remained on the turf as Hutchinson jumped in the air to celebrate the sack, and then he, too, went back down to the ground.

It was revealed that Hutchinson tore his ACL on the celebration, ending his season. Maye suffered a fractured ankle, and his season looks like it could be over, as well. Two teams seeing their respective campaigns severely altered, one of which will now need to figure out what it will do at quarterback for the rest of the year. For the Lions, it's the second time in three seasons they've lost Hutchinson to a season-ending leg injury. For a defense that was already inconsistent at best, this will serve as yet another massive obstacle for them to overcome. As for the Patriots, their season was a whirlwind before the Maye injury. Now at 5-5, their playoff hopes are all but dead and gone.

Seattle's offense has just flat out been better with Lock under center. Of all quarterbacks with at least 100 dropbacks, Lock leads the NFL in EPA per play.

Read that again. Drew Lock leads the NFL in EPA per play. He's also yet to throw an interception, and has seven touchdown passes, including a Ben Roethlisberger-esc play that resulted in Cooper Kupp hauling in a long score this past Sunday against the Raiders. Sam Darnold has looked a lot more like Jets and Panthers Sam Darnold than the quarterback we saw in each of the last two seasons. Will Lock keep up this level of play? It feels unlikely, but right now he is the best option for Seattle. Keep riding the hot hand until it stops being hot.

For what it's worth, everyone has been selling on Parker since Week 1, but Sunday was a never-ending charade of inept defensive play. The Cowboys allowed 584 yards of offense to the 49ers, the most yards ever allowed at home in franchise history. The 46 points allowed puts Dallas in second-to-last in points per game (28.2), just 0.7 points ahead of the Cardinals and Raiders. It's rare to see a coach or coordinator last less than one season, but this should be a case where Dallas gives Mike Pettine the interim DC title for the rest of the season.

Kirk Cousins going back to the Vikings is much more than just a good story. In his first start back in the Purple and Gold, Cousins threw two touchdowns and led the Vikings' offense to a 30-10 win over the Packers, its first 30-point outing since Week 1 against… the Packers. With their defense, the Vikings don't need Cousins to be superhuman, they just need him to not be the worst starting quarterback in the NFL, which, collectively, Carson Wentz and Kyler Murray were. We saw the Seahawks win a Super Bowl with this formula a season ago. Who's to say Minnesota can't do it, too?

This is a lifeless team with a soft defense. Credit where it's due, Jacoby Brissett came in and gave the Ravens a chance to win. However, their defense, under the guidance of a quote unquote "defensive genius" Jesse Minter, allowed the Chargers' league-worst scoring offense to put up 23 points – tied for their most in a game this season – en route to Los Angeles finally winning their first game. Now at 4-6, and with Lamar Jackson still a way's away from being able to come back, stick a fork in the Ravens.

3 ways DDoS attacks knock websites offline, from clogged links to busy servers

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Volumetric, protocol and application layer attacks each exhaust a different part of a site. Here is what fails first and why.

A website can go offline without anyone breaking into it. No data is stolen and no password is cracked. The site simply receives more traffic than it can handle, and real visitors cannot get through. Attackers no longer need much skill or equipment to do this. Help Net Security reports that DDoS-for-hire platforms and hijacked internet-connected gadgets, known as IoT devices, make large attacks easier to launch. The danger to a site therefore depends less on who the attacker is than on how much traffic that person sends and which part of the site's connection it is aimed at.

Defending a site is mostly a problem of recognition, not strength. Any operator can block traffic. The hard part is blocking only the hostile traffic, because a flood of requests from an attacker can look just like a rush of real customers after a sale or a news story. An operator who shuts out everyone during an attack ends up with the same outage the attacker wanted, only by the operator's own hand. That is why a crude fix rarely helps. The defender has to keep serving the people who belong on the site while turning away the people who do not, and that has to happen while the attack is still under way.

Cloudflare, which sells protection against these attacks, sorted the main ways attackers use DDoS attacks against different parts of a connection, from the work of generating web pages to the bandwidth that links a site to the wider Internet and the firewalls between them. "The key concern in mitigating a DDoS attack is differentiating between attack traffic and normal traffic," Cloudflare said. That concern applies to every kind of attack, but the failure itself differs from one kind to the next. In one case, "the site went down" means a link too full to carry anything. In another, it means equipment too busy keeping track of connections. In a third, it means a server too occupied answering requests to serve real visitors. Knowing which failure is under way shapes what a defense has to tell apart and how quickly it has to act.

The crudest way to take a site offline never touches the site itself. In a volumetric attack, attackers fill the link that connects a target to the rest of the Internet until it has no capacity left, Cloudflare explains, and requests from real visitors never get through. The attack becomes a contest of raw size. A server can work perfectly and still be unreachable when the attacker sends more traffic than its connection can carry.

Few attackers control enough traffic to win that contest, so they borrow it. Some send requests from botnets, which are networks of hijacked machines that send traffic from many places at once and can't be shut off at a single source. Others use amplification. Floods and amplification attacks that abuse DNS, the Internet's address-lookup system, made up 34.3% of network-layer attacks, according to Help Net Security. Attacks built on DNS are therefore a large part of what defenders face at the network layer. Either method leaves a connection with no room for legitimate traffic.

The typical flood is small and short, and the giant attacks are rare. "Despite the hyper-volumetric growth, the median DDoS attack Cloudflare mitigated in the first half of 2026 remained short and small with 96.62% of network-layer attacks remaining under 500 Mbps and 90.60% ending in under 10 minutes," Cloudflare said, as reported by Help Net Security. The rare cases are becoming more common, and attacks larger than 1 Tbps rose significantly over the same period. A site that handles routine attacks has passed the common test but not the hard one, and that record says little about whether it could withstand an attack large enough to use up all its bandwidth.

A short attack leaves people no time to react. Many attacks were over in less than a minute, according to Help Net Security. An attack that brief can end before any engineer has a chance to step in. A defense against floods has to run automatically and all the time, and it has to decide what to let through without waiting for a person.

Against the bluntest attack, how fast a defender judges traffic matters as much as how much bandwidth it has.

A protocol attack takes a site down by overworking the machines that sit between its Internet connection and its web pages. These attackers don't try to fill the link to the wider Internet, and they don't try to make the server build pages until it runs out of effort. Cloudflare reports that these attacks use weaknesses in network layers 3 and 4, and that they use up the capacity of a site's servers, its firewalls or both. Those layers are the networking machinery beneath the web pages themselves, where traffic gets handled before any visitor sees a page. What gives way, then, is the site's own equipment. The machines run out of room to work, even though nobody has broken into them and nothing has been stolen.

Some of the machines that attackers overload exist to guard and organize the very traffic that swamps them. "Protocol attacks cause a service disruption by over-consuming server resources and/or the resources of network equipment like firewalls and load balancers," Cloudflare said. A firewall meant to screen out hostile traffic, or a load balancer meant to spread visitors across servers, can become the bottleneck itself. That changes how a site operator should read an outage. When attackers exhaust the machines rather than the bandwidth, a site that goes dark isn't simply short of connection space. Buying a bigger connection on its own leaves the weak point in place, because the equipment behind that connection still can't keep up with the work it is handed.

For a site under this kind of attack, the useful question is not only how much traffic arrived but which machine ran out of capacity to handle it.

In an application layer attack, attackers go after the server's own effort to build web pages, not the connection to the Internet or the equipment in front of the server. Cloudflare reports that this attack targets web page generation, the work a server does to answer HTTP requests. Attackers keep those requests coming until the server has no effort left for the people who actually want to use the site. These are the same HTTP requests a visitor's browser sends to load a page, which makes the hostile traffic the same kind as the legitimate traffic. A site under this attack is not jammed. Its server is too busy doing its normal job for the wrong people.

Attackers here count on the cost of answering a request as much as on the amount of traffic. Building a page for a visitor takes work, and attackers aim at that work. "The goal is to overwhelm the target with requests, since a single HTTP request can be expensive for the target server to respond to," Cloudflare said. The danger in this attack depends on how much work each request forces the server to do, not only on how many requests arrive.

Defending against this attack means judging requests, not only limiting them. Rate limiting, a general defense against DDoS attacks, lets a server take only so many requests within a given period. That cap keeps the workload within what the server can handle, but it limits how many requests get through, not which ones. Another general defense is a web application firewall, which filters traffic by rules. That filtering is the step that tries to separate hostile requests from real ones.

Against an attack made of the same requests a real visitor sends, protecting a site comes down less to absorbing traffic than to recognizing it.

Emergency Broadcast System Test Still On For Today, Homeland Security Says

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Politics  |  Read Source →

Executive Editor, Legal, Labor & Politics

Despite a series of technology outages the past week on email systems, aviation communications, airlines, banks, national security networks, and more, the Federal Emergency Management Agency says the long scheduled test today of the Emergency Broadcast System will go ahead.

“This is a nationwide test of the Emergency Alert System, issued by the Federal Emergency Management Agency,” the one-minute long message will states, FEMA said. “This is only a test. No action is required by the public.”

Set to hit at 11:20 a.m. PT/2:30 p.m. ET, the test will appear on “radio and television broadcasters, cable systems, satellite radio and television providers and wireline video,” notes the Department of Homeland Security. According to FEMA, there will be a brief interruption, but “No blackouts, no secret emergencies” connected to the test. Watch on Deadline

FYI – FEMA falls under the jurisdiction of the DHS.

We’ve heard a lot of rumors about tomorrow’s EAS test. 📺📻 Let’s clear things up. It lasts 1 minute, and no action is required. No blackouts, no secret emergencies. Just our legally mandated, routine test on TV and radio at approximately 2:20 PM ET: https://t.co/WGD7xCuRDB pic.twitter.com/HEErLQVKga

“During an emergency, every second is crucial,” FEMA Administrator Cameron Hamilton said in a statement Monday. “FEMA’s alert and warning systems ensure that no second is wasted getting you the critical information needed to keep you safe. Operational tests, like the one to be conducted tomorrow, allow us to identify and fix potential issues before an actual emergency occurs.”

As you might recall from 2023, the feds are obliged by the IPAWS Modernization Act of 2015 to conduct a EAS test every three years. A more localized test of today’s test took place in Alaska with no problems.

A further safeguard is in place that if today’s test encountered difficulties. FEMA explained back in September: “If an actual emergency or significant weather event occurs on test day, FEMA and the FCC may postpone the test. The backup date for the nationwide test is December 3, 2026.”
