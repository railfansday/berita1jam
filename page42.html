Who is Joe Milton III? Eagles turn to QB with 'cannon' arm strength

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Jordan Mailata might have been speaking for the rest of his Philadelphia Eagles teammates – and the public at large – when discussing his initial impression of quarterback Joe Milton.

"Have you seen his arm?" Mailata asked reporters on Wednesday, Nov. 4. "It is a (expletive) cannon, oh my God."

After taking a moment to apologize for his language, the left tackle went on to describe what made the passer such a singular player at the position.

"You see (the arm strength) on TV. You hear about it when he was going through the draft. But then you see it, and it's a work of art, man," Mailata said.

Since he was a high school football standout, Milton has been known for physical capabilities that differentiate him from even his most gifted peers. But after spending the bulk of his three-year pro career on the fringes of the league, the 6-5, 230-pound quarterback will get his chance to step into the spotlight Sunday, when he's set for his first pro start against the New York Giants.

Milton takes over for Tanner McKee, who started the past three games after Jalen Hurts was sidelined by blood clots that later moved to his lungs. Under McKee, an already shorthanded and struggling Eagles offense continued to flounder. The attack ranks last in yards per game (265.3) and 30th in scoring (17.1 points per game).

With the team's losing streak extending to six games, Milton is being called on to provide some kind of spark.

"With everything going on, he provides a good light to it," Mailata said of Milton.

From the time Milton started emerging as a highly sought-after high school recruit in Orlando, word began spreading about his arm strength.

As a 16-year-old, he threw a pass 75 yards to win a high-school competition, he said in 2024. At the Manning Passing Academy in 2023, Milton launched a pass more than 70 yards, with a clip of the feat quickly going viral.

For as easy as he made his throws look, though, college was anything but that for the quarterback. He lost his starting job twice – first at Michigan, then again at Tennessee after an injury.

But Milton put himself back into the NFL conversation with his final season with the Volunteers, during which he threw for 2,769 yards and 20 touchdowns with five interceptions. After again showcasing his unique physical tools during the pre-draft process, he was selected in the sixth round of the 2024 NFL Draft by the New England Patriots, who took fellow quarterback Drake Maye with the No. 4 overall pick.

Stashed as a developmental option, Maye didn't see any action until Week 18 of his rookie year, when he took over for Maye after one series and threw for 241 yards in a win over the Buffalo Bills. He delivered a signature moment when he repeatedly evaded rushers as he darted left before uncorking a 48-yard touchdown strike to Kayshon Boutte.

That performance caught the attention of Dallas Cowboys owner Jerry Jones, who three months later acquired Milton in a trade with the Patriots.

"I really have to pinch myself that we got him after the last game that he had up there, starting there for New England," Jones told 105.3 The Fan in Dallas in August 2025. "I'm just excited that we had a chance to get him. And he hasn't disappointed. ... We're just more and more encouraged by what we're seeing."

But Milton lost the role as Dak Prescott's backup to Sam Howell prior to this season and was waived in late August as part of the team's cutdowns to a 53-man roster. He rejoined the Cowboys two days later, however, when he re-signed to the practice squad.

When the Eagles signed Milton to their practice squad on Oct. 16, one day before Hurts was placed on injured reserve, the move might have largely been an afterthought to some.

Less than a month later, however, he's taking on a starring role for the team at a crucial juncture.

Eagles coach Nick Sirianni initially said on Monday, Nov. 2, that he was not entertaining benching McKee. Two days later, news broke of the switch.

Sirianni later chalked up the mislead to a matter of trying to establish a competitive advantage.

"Every second that goes by in a situation like that, that you can hold onto that information as long as you possibly can, is a little less time for the opponent to get ready for the game," Sirianni said Friday, Nov. 6.

With the news out, however, Sirianni went to bat for his new starter.

"He's talented and been here for a couple weeks now," Sirianni said of Milton. "Very talented arm, very talented athlete, can process and make decisions out there. Lot of good qualities he has. I like his confidence and everything that he brings, and it's been fun getting to know him and working with him."

As Chargers look for answers, who is to blame for 0-7 start?

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

The Los Angeles Chargers' season has proven to be a complete failure.

The Chargers were predicted to be an above .500 team this season, but with a 0-7 start, the team now has very little wiggle room to even make that expectation a reality.

Coming off a bye week, the team did not appear to have taken any significant strides, suffering a 24-9 loss to the Los Angeles Rams at SoFi Stadium on Nov. 1.

The Chargers put together a promising first half, scoring nine points on three field goals.

One of the biggest positives from the half came in the final seconds of the second quarter when quarterback Justin Herbert threw a hail mary 54 yards to Ladd McConkey.

Unfortunately for the Chargers, McConkey was tackled at the one-yard line as time expired and went into the locker room without additional points.

The play didn't help create any momentum for the Chargers, who finished scoreless in the second half.

The Chargers will have the opportunity to turn things around against the Houston Texans on Sunday, Nov. 7, at home.

After seven consecutive losses this season, who is to blame for the disappointing start to the season?

There is a lot of blame that could be passed around regarding the team's recent on-field struggles.

Coach Jim Harbaugh, offensive coordinator Mike McDaniel and quarterback Justin Herbert are among the three in that order that should take on the bulk of that responsibility.

The Chargers moved away from being a pass-heavy offense that proved to be functional and efficient compared to what the offense has presented under McDaniel's watch this season.

Harbaugh added McDaniel to lead the offensive staff with the expectation that he would be an upgrade over what was already in place.

Herbert has been an above-average quarterback during his time in Los Angeles and he should be in the midst of a career year as a veteran and not regress with an uptick in interceptions.

As the head coach, Jim Harbaugh should take the brunt of the blame and responsibility.

He had the opportunity during the bye week to make much-needed changes to the offense but did nothing.

"We're aware of what the issues are, and we're the only ones that can fix them," Harbaugh told reporters on Nov. 1.

Harbaugh continued to get peppered with questions during the media availability but remained fairly vague.

"Our motivation is a dedication to getting it changed, getting it fixed," Harbaugh said when asked about keeping McDaniel.

While there was a pledge that some changes would be made, McDaniel remained as the playcaller through the bye week.

The Chargers have actually doubled down on their support and loyalty for one another.

Offensive lineman Joe Alt said the group remains bought in but admitted his displeasure with the results.

"Just frustrated," Alt said. "You want to come away with a win as a player and as a unit we have to be better. The scoreboard reflects that. … We have to score more points."

The offense has not scored a touchdown in its last nine quarters of play. It's a drought that spans 27 possessions.

Sean McVay vs Jim Harbaugh: Will Rams or Chargers figure things out?

As offensive coordinator with a clear directive from the head coach to score points, McDaniel's offense failed another test.

"We're not scoring enough points," Harbaugh said on Oct. 18 after a loss to the Kansas City Chiefs on the road.

McDaniel talked about collaborating with Herbert to help turn things around during the bye week. The coordinator called the bye week not much of a break, because "there were problems to solve."

The duo spent four hours watching film together throughout the week to "find spots to build on, where you can find spots to improve," but those again didn't necessarily result in touchdowns and a victory.

"In this league you have to produce results, or there's going to be heat, so I don't expect anything else," McDaniel said during a press conference on Oct. 29.

The heat is on, but it remains unclear when Harbaugh may be ready to make a change or if the organization will make it for him.

Last season, the Chargers had a middling offense under coordinator Greg Roman. It ranked 20th in the league with 21.6 points per game. The offense threw 14 interceptions and scored 36 total touchdowns.

This season, the Chargers rank last in scoring offense, averaging 14.1 points per game. The team is also averaging 310.9 total yards per game and has just nine total touchdowns through the first seven games.

Despite all the struggles Herbert has dealt with this season, he remains publicly bought into McDaniel's offense.

"I believe in him," Herbert said. "He's a great coach. He's very smart and it's on us as an offense to make those plays. ... We just have to make plays and score points and score touchdowns and we haven't done that for him."

Herbert completed 24 of 32 passes for 257 yards and two interceptions against the Rams on Sunday. He was also sacked twice.

"Whatever we've been doing hasn't been enough and it's up to us," Herbert said during the postgame press conference on Nov. 1. "Only we in the locker room can change it and work toward it."

Herbert has averaged 24 passing touchdowns and 9.7 interceptions per season through his career. This season has seen remarkable regression.

The star QB has completed 131 of his 212 pass attempts for 1,398 yards with just five touchdowns through the first seven games. He's already thrown 10 interceptions and has been sacked 23 times this season.

To Make a Doc About a Nomadic Cyclist, the ‘Perfect Road’ Filmmakers Spent 3 Months in a Campervan

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Adam and Christian Bonke recall how they and their cinematographer followed Maximilian Schnell in a "not so big" vehicle with "a lot of equipment" to shoot material for the film now screening at Tallinn.

For their documentary Perfect Road, Danish directors and cousins Adam and Christian Bonke followed nomadic cyclist Maximilian Schnell on an extreme journey across some of Europe’s highest mountain passes. The film documents his journey, offers a character study and serves up a meditation on modern life and our priorities and life choices.

Elk Film and Dogwoof produced Perfect Road, with Andreas Dalsgaard, Anna Godas, Oli Harbottle, Miriam Nørgaard serving as producers. Jonatan Mose served as cinematographer, with editing handled by Martin Anthon. Related Stories Movies PÖFF Stands With Ukraine: A Look at Films Featured at Tallinn Made Despite, and Because of, the War Oscars 2027: Int'l Films in Focus 'Hold Onto Me,' Cyprus' First Oscar Hopeful, Stars a Newcomer and a 'Dogtooth' Actor in a Very Personal Father-Daughter Drama

Perfect Road, which world premiered at Zurich, is now screening at the 30th anniversary edition of the Tallinn Black Nights Film Festival (PÖFF) in Estonia. It shows how the road brings freedom and adventure, but also grueling physical and mental challenges. And that is not only true for Schnell but also the directors and their cinematographer.

“We ended up living in a campervan for three months,” recalls Adam Bonke. “It was three grown-up men in a not so big campervan with a lot of equipment. And then we had a small a smaller car, like a satellite, that we could send out on the tiny roads where the campervan couldn’t go.”

Adds Christian Bonke: “It was such a headache because you had to put water into the car, you had to empty the toilet. So when we were done shooting for the day, we had to take care of all that.”

The filmmakers considered various strategies for capturing footage. Initially, they wanted to use several different camera people over the course of the three-month adventure. “But then our DP was kicked out by his girlfriend,” Christian Bonke tells THR. “So he said, ‘I’m going to do all three months. This is perfect for me.’ And Adam and I took turns.”

It may not have been a holiday or super-comfortable, but Adam Bonke tells THR: “It was comfortable enough.” And it allowed the filmmakers to get a better feel for Schnell’s life choices. “He said, ‘I’m trading comfort for freedom,'” the co-director explains. “I think that’s that’s such a nice thing to say and really frames the film perfectly.”

The Danish filmmaker duo actually still fondly looks back at the experience shooting Perfect Road. “We often talk about how we miss those days. It was so beautiful,” says Christian Bonke. “You felt embraced by nature. And after meeting Max, we were completely hit by how conventional and boring [traditional life can be] and how many constraints our lives had at home. So we could get a little glimpse of living his way.”
