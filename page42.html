Model ML raises $250M to automate investment banking grunt work

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Model ML, a London-based startup that uses AI to automate work traditionally performed by junior bankers and consultants, including building financial models, preparing pitch decks, and writing due diligence reports, has raised a $250 million in Series B funding at a $1.5 billion valuation. The new round, which comes a year after its $75 million Series A round was announced, was co-led by General Atlantic and earlier backer QED Investors.

Brothers Chaz and Arnie Englander co-founded the three-year-old startup after previously co-founding two other companies, including Fat Llama, a peer-to-peer rental marketplace acquired by Swedish rival Hygglo for $41.5 million in 2022.

Model ML develops AI agents that integrate with clients’ systems, data, and tools, including Excel and PowerPoint. The startup says its agents understand the full context of deals that bankers and consultants are working on, and can automatically update financial models and presentations when new developments affect their analysis.

Other startups automating the routine, repetitive tasks for investment bankers and management consultants include Hebbia and Rogo, which raised $160 million at a $2 billion valuation in April.

Anthropic and OpenAI have also built tools for financial professionals, but Model ML says it runs on open-source models and that doing so allows it to operate at about 2% of the cost of frontier models while delivering faster results.

James Bond Director Reveals The Most Important Quality Of Robert Pattinson's 007

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Now that Robert Pattinson has officially been cast as James Bond, Amazon MGM's plans for the franchise are coming together. The new owners of the Bond IP are clearly sparing no expense for the upcoming "Bond 26," hiring a major director in Denis Villeneuve to shepherd the project, which will now be led by one of the biggest names in Hollywood. After the missteps that were Daniel Craig's last few films in the tux, it seems Amazon is aiming to re-establish Bond movies as must-see cinematic events. For Villeneuve, however, the star power of his Bond actor is less important than the ability to project the energy of a cold-blooded killer.

In an interview with GQ, the "Dune" director was asked what he looks for in a 007 actor. "A first quality is that I need to believe that this man is a killer," he replied. "Because more than a spy, Bond is an assassin, and I need that kind of strength. I need that kind of anger deep in the eye. It's not everybody who has that." Those who doubt whether Pattinson can do so need only take a glance at the casting announcement video, in which the actor's Bond emerges from the darkness aiming his Walther PPK and wearing a cold, resolute expression on his face. There's a definite edge to his brief appearance in the clip, and you can see why Villeneuve was impressed.

That said, it seems Pattinson wasn't actually the first choice for Bond. According to a recent report, Villeneuve initially settled on "Wake Up Dead Man: A Knives Out Mystery" actor Josh O'Connor, who ultimately dropped out and cleared the way for Pattinson. But that will almost certainly prove to be a positive thing.

Robert Pattinson's casting as James Bond breaks a 60-year tradition of the franchise introducing audiences to relative unknowns in the lead role. But that's a good thing, as Bond is in need of revitalization following the debacle that was 2021's "No Time to Die." Pattinson's star power will help restore the franchise's popularity. But the "Batman" star is more than just an A-lister with a built-in audience. He's easily one of the most talented actors of his generation, having demonstrated an extraordinary ability to completely transform on-screen. As Bond, he'll no doubt exude exactly the kind of subtly sinister energy Denis Villeneuve is looking for and which has been one of the most important aspects of the character since his introduction.

In 1962, "Dr. No" kicked off cinema's most enduring franchise by introducing audiences to Sean Connery in the lead role. Rather than casting an established star with the kind of refined energy you'd expect of a gentleman spy, producers Albert R. Broccoli and Harry Saltzman hired a working class former footballer from Scotland, and it was a masterstroke. 007 debuted on the big screen with an edge that only Connery could bring to the role, and that's been an integral aspect of the cinematic incarnation of the character ever since. Sure, director Terence Young helped round off some of Connery's rough edges, but he'd be nothing without the sense of danger the Scotsman brought to the screen. Thankfully, it seems both Villeneuve and Pattinson recognize that, and it's yet another aspect of this casting that bodes well for "Bond 26."

Denis Villeneuve clearly values the killer instinct aspect of James Bond above everything else. But the director has plenty of other requirements for the role. As he went on to tell GQ, "I need someone who has that kind of unapologetic panache, someone that [...] when he comes into the room everything starts to gravitate around him, that kind of strong charisma." It's a foundational aspect of Bond and a characteristic every actor to portray the spy has possessed. But that's not all Villeneuve is looking for.

The French-Canadian filmmaker went on to say he also looks for "sensuality" in his Bond actor, as well as "dark humor" and an ability to take "pleasure into being in deep s***." It's standard stuff for Bond, but it's reassuring to know Villeneuve has such a solid grasp of the fundamentals. Beyond that, the director described someone "strong, muscular, someone that is a force," as well as an individual with a "deep intelligence in the eyes" and who is "always ahead of us." Sounds about right to me. Thankfully, Robert Pattinson can fulfill every one of these requirements, which makes him one of the most exciting of all the James Bond actors.

Thus far, Bond fans have reacted to Pattinson's casting with positivity, even while there's been the predictable objections regarding his "Twilight" years and physicality. At this point, however, the British star has long since proved his ability to deliver consistently electric performances that always surprise. There's no reason to think his Bond portrayal will be any different. Heck, Villeneuve was convinced, and he clearly seems to know what he's talking about.

Denver-based Frontier Airlines has rapidly cut a total of 16 international routes from its network this year, with Atlanta (ATL), one of the airline's major bases, losing a total of eight routes overall. These network changes have taken place between March and August 2026.

Data from Cirium, an aviation analytics company, shows that the budget airline has reconfigured its international operations. It is redeploying aircraft on more lucrative domestic or other international routes following a difficult year for airlines in the face of high fuel prices and other operational demands. This data has been reviewed against 2026 and 2027 flight schedules.

Frontier is the second-largest operator at Atlanta. However, it is still just a marginal player when compared to Delta, which calls Atlanta home. Frontier operates around 6% of all flights from Atlanta, according to the airport's website. The airline trimmed eight nonstop international routes from the airport in April of this year.

These affected services to Puerto Vallarta (PVR), San José del Cabo (SJD), Santo Domingo (SDQ), Nassau (NAS), Aruba (AUA), Providenciales (PLS), St Maarten (SXM), and San Pedro Sula (SAP). This leaves Frontier with a network of 40 domestic and six year-round international destinations from Atlanta.

The remaining five year-round services from Atlanta to Cancún (CUN), Guatemala City (GUA), Montego Bay (MBJ), San José, Costa Rica (SJO), and San Salvador (SAL). There is also a seasonal link to Punta Cana (PUJ).

Earlier this year, Denver (DEN) lost two international services served by Frontier. The airline withdrew its operations to San José del Cabo (SJD) from the Colorado airport in March, while Denver to Puerto Vallarta (PVR) lost its international link in April. This was followed by Charlotte (CLT) also losing its international route to Cancún (CUN) after the airline pulled service on the city pair in April.

This leaves the airline with no international routes from Charlotte. Now, it only has domestic flights to Baltimore (BWI), Chicago–O'Hare (ORD), Dallas/Fort Worth (DFW), Denver, Detroit (DTW), Fort Lauderdale (FLL), Houston–Intercontinental (IAH), Miami (MIA), New York–LaGuardia (LGA), Orlando (MCO), Philadelphia (PHL), San Juan (SJU), and Tampa (TPA).

Frontier's international route from Raleigh/Durham (RDU) to Cancún was also lost in May. Meanwhile, Orlando's link to St Maarten (SXM) was axed in summer, after the airline pulled the pin in August.

Atlanta loses multiple leisure routes while competitors like Delta expand to absorb sunseeking travelers from the hub.

The decision to consolidate a number of international routes was also seen in both Chicago and Miami, with Frontier terminating two services from each city. Chicago Midway (MDW) lost its service to Cancún in April, while O'Hare (ORD) lost its link to Punta Cana (PUJ) in the Dominican Republic.

This leaves Frontier with 23 domestic routes from O'Hare, and a further nine domestic routes from Midway. Frontier is the second-largest airline at Chicago Midway, the city's second busiest airport after O'Hare. While Southwest operates more than 91% of all flights at Midway, Frontier served over half a million passengers between June 2025 and May 2026, accounting for 3% of all traffic.

Packers fan kicked out of Lambeau Field for spreading loved one’s ashes after loss

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

A Green Bay Packers fan was ejected from Lambeau Field after spreading a loved one’s ashes onto the field following the team’s loss to the Minnesota Vikings.

The incident occurred on Sunday, November 15, after Green Bay suffered a 30-10 defeat at home. Police were called to the stadium after a spectator was spotted dispersing what authorities later confirmed to be cremated human remains.

According to local news station WBAY, officers spoke with the individual and confirmed that the ashes belonged to a loved one. The fan was subsequently removed from the stadium for violating local regulations.

Under Green Bay Municipal Code 24-117, spreading human ashes onto the playing field is considered unlawful conduct at a public event.

The Packers addressed the incident in a statement to WBAY, acknowledging that some supporters want to honor deceased friends and family members at the stadium while emphasizing that the practice is prohibited.

“While we understand the desire of some fans to have their ashes, or those of friends or family, spread at Lambeau Field, it is against the law and prohibited at the stadium,” the team said.

Rather than scattering cremated remains, the organization encouraged supporters to consider alternative ways of remembering loved ones, including its commemorative brick program.

“To honor a loved one’s legacy appropriately at Lambeau Field, we refer fans to the commemorative brick program. We also suggest they can coordinate tributes in other appropriate ways with their funeral home,” the Packers continued.

The program allows supporters to purchase personalized bricks honoring individuals, creating a permanent tribute at the historic stadium without violating its regulations.

9 Modern Spy Thriller Shows To Watch To Prepare For Robert Pattinson’s Bond Era

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

The next era of James Bond is closer than ever, and with Robert Pattinson officially cast as James Bond, there's a lot to look forward to now that Amazon MGM is behind the wheel.

With Denis Villeneuve directing and a script from Steven Knight, the creative team behind the next James Bond film is now complete, with Pattinson tapped as the future 007. Expectations and stakes are high for the coming take on the franchise. As James Bond continues to push boundaries in the espionage genre, it would be smart to look to the best spy thriller TV shows that are capturing the attention of audiences everywhere.

Tom Hiddleston, the star of The Night Manager​​​​​​, has a James Bond energy himself, and he brings this gravitas to the role of Jonathan Pine in the critically acclaimed series. Set in Cairo, The Night Manager follows Jonathan, the titular evening manager of a high-end hotel, as he becomes involved with a dangerous ring of arms dealers and the agents trying to bring them down.

Also featuring Hugh Laurie, Olivia Colman, and Noah Jupe, The Night Manager's sleek style and complex storylines are elevated by the skill of the central cast. Laurie plays a villain worthy of any James Bond movie, and the dynamic between him and Hiddleston throughout the episodes will keep audiences on the edge of their seats.

Enormously popular, sharp, and proof that Apple TV can tackle thriller TV shows as well as science fiction, Slow Horses has been setting the tone for contemporary spy series since 2022. The announcement of Slow Horses' season 9 and 10 renewal doesn't come as a surprise, as the show has found a dedicated audience base thanks to the beloved cast led by Gary Oldman and Jack Lowden.

Also based on a book series, like James Bond, Slow Horses follows a group of disgruntled and low-achieving MI5 agents sent to Slough House to do the drudgery of the agency. However, somehow, the characters find themselves caught up in thrilling investigations. Though they lack the smooth grace of James Bond characters, this makes them even more interesting to watch.

A period drama, neo-noir, and a must-watch non-English-language series for audiences who haven't yet discovered it, Babylon Berlin is set in Berlin beginning in 1929. Following the rise to power of the Nazi Party and the fallout of World War I, Babylon Berlin is a fresh take on this period of history, which has been brought to life onscreen many times before.

Volker Bruch leads the cast as Inspector Gereon Rath, a tortured police officer caught up in a deeper conspiracy than he could ever imagine when he enters the seedy underbelly of the city. Though James Bond has always appealed to international audiences, the warm critical and audience reception of Babylon Berlin makes it clear that viewers are ready to see their heroes in espionage stories complicated and full of anguish.

Zoe Saldaña has made her mark on most of the biggest movie franchises of the 21st century, from The Avengers to Avatar​​​​​​. However, her recent work in the thriller series Lioness shows Saldaña giving one of the best performances of her career. She plays Joe McNamara, the CIA agent heading up the titular Lioness program by recruiting female agents.

One of the most exciting aspects of modern thriller TV shows is how ready they are to put women in leading roles and give them juicy espionage storylines up to par with the legacy of James Bond and other classic spy narratives. As the series has progressed, Lioness has only gotten better, crafting complex storylines and giving Saldaña and her co-stars plenty of opportunities to shine in action-oriented and nuanced emotional moments.

Black Doves season 2 recently dropped on Netflix, and the series' second outing has proven to be just as exciting, cementing it as one of the streamer's best thriller shows of the decade. With Keira Knightley and Ben Whishaw leading the cast as Helen Webb and Sam Young, a pair of spies-for-hire, Black Doves is a twisting narrative of secrets, betrayals, and moral questions that the lead characters are forced to face throughout the seasons.

It's fun to watch Whishaw's performance in Black Doves, as it differs sharply from his work as Q in James Bond. In Black Doves, he gets the chance to step into the action-oriented side of spy work. Photographer Captures Eerie Images of Looming Data Centers in Virginia

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Photographer Captures Eerie Images of Looming Data Centers in Virginia

A photographer has set about documenting data centers in northern Virginia — an area that has the highest concentration of data centers in the world.

Stephen Voss’s unnerving photographs show giant box buildings looming over housing developments, baseball fields, and cemeteries.

“Three years ago, I was driving back from a photo shoot in Virginia when I noticed a data center for the first time, in all its drab, boxy glory. Here was the physical reality of something we’ve somehow agreed to call ‘the cloud’,” Voss tells PetaPixel.

The disconnect, Voss says, stuck with him. Over the next three years, he made over 100 trips to data centers in northern Virginia, exploring how these facilities intersect with the communities and places where people live their lives.

“Where does a data center end and someone’s neighborhood begin? What does it look like when the infrastructure behind our digital lives becomes the view from someone’s backyard?” Voss asks.

The project became Every Past and Tomorrow, a book Voss intends to self-publish via Kickstarter.

“Most of us rarely think about the infrastructure behind our online lives — where our photographs are stored, our music is streamed, or an AI prompt gets answered,” Voss says.

“For people living near data centers, those buildings are part of their everyday lives and I don’t think we should discount what it means to live right near one.

“In some neighborhoods, the sounds of cooling fans and natural gas turbines can be heard inside of their homes. People watch their kids play baseball at fields with 75-foot data center walls just past the outfield fence.”

During the course of his project, Voss says he’s witnessed forests cut down, empty fields become enormous buildings, and places where he once made photographs closed off behind fences.

“Every time I return, I see a landscape changing and felt this urgency to document as much of it as possible,” he adds.

Voss says the landscape is changing so much that Google Maps can’t keep up.

“Street View and satellite imagery often show an empty field where there’s already a building,” he says. “So, I spend hours driving around, searching through online permits and county records, and returning to places again and again to see how they’ve changed over time.”

Over the course of the project, Voss gained a drone license so he could take to the air to better show the relationships between data centers and the neighborhood.

However, bringing massive nondescript buildings to life is no easy task, and he started to see the giant blank walls as a canvas.

“I dug through AI research papers, terms of service, and old science fiction,” Voss explains. “At night, I projected words from those texts onto the data centers themselves — returning the language of this technology to the structures that make it possible.”

Voss says that more than once security guards came and spoke to him as he projected text onto the buildings, making him hard to miss.
