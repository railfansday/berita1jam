Dividend Stocks for 2027: Kimberly-Clark vs. McDonald's

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Kimberly-Clark trades at a noticeably lower multiple than McDonald's, but the franchise giant's richer price tag raises a question about which premium is justified.

By Jack Delaney – Updated Nov 18, 2026 at 8:00PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Kimberly-Clark ›

Investors often seek stability in market giants that dominate daily life. Deciding between a consumer essentials powerhouse like Kimberly-Clark (KMB -0.84%) and a global restaurant titan like McDonald's (MCD -0.65%) requires weighing income against growth.

Kimberly-Clark provides essential hygiene products that consumers buy regardless of the economic climate. McDonald's leverages a massive franchise model to generate consistent cash flow through the fast-food market. Comparing these two involves looking at how defensive positioning in household goods stacks up against the high-margin, scalable nature of the global restaurant business.

Kimberly-Clark sells essential hygiene and health products to households and professionals among consumer staples stocks worldwide. Its brands, including Huggies and Kleenex, reach customers in more than 175 countries through supermarkets and e-commerce platforms. Walmart (WMT -0.13%) is its largest customer, accounting for nearly 16% of net sales, and customer concentration like this adds a layer of risk to the business.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached nearly $16.6 billion. The company reported net income of close to $3.3 billion during this same timeframe. Kimberly-Clark has total cash of $1.8 billion, with total debt of $6.5 billion. Over a 12-month trailing period, it has operating cash flow of $3.3 billion.

McDonald's operates a global fast-food empire by primarily franchising its locations to independent owners rather than running them directly. This model allows for high net margins compared to many traditional retail-focused businesses. It also maintains a delivery partnership with Uber Technologies (UBER -0.07%) to help boost volume and customer loyalty across more than a dozen global markets.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached approximately $27.8 billion, which shows revenue growth of nearly 5.8%. Net income for this period was roughly $9 billion, demonstrating the company's ability to turn sales into significant bottom-line profit. The steady expansion of the franchise system and delivery infrastructure supports these consistent financial results.

Kimberly-Clark faces intense competition from other branded household products and lower-priced private label manufacturers. It is sensitive to price changes in volatile commodities like cellulose fiber and petroleum-based materials used in manufacturing. Additionally, the company must manage the integration of its acquisition of Kenvue while navigating global regulatory and sustainability reporting requirements.

McDonald's deals with fierce competition and depends heavily on the financial health of its individual franchisees. Labor shortages and rising wage costs can pressure the system, while food safety concerns or public perception issues can damage the global brand reputation.

McDonald's carries a significantly higher premium than Kimberly-Clark based on both its forward price-to-earnings (P/E) and its price-to-sales (P/S) ratio, which measures market cap relative to sales over the past twelve months.

Both Kimberly-Clark and McDonald's are well-run companies, and while McDonald's is known for its singular brand, Kimberly-Clark is known for multiple brands, ranging from Kleenex to Band-Aid. Both are also dividend stocks, helping investors generate more income. Kimberly-Clark's dividend offers a higher yield of 4.7%, while McDonald's is 2.8%.

That said, between the two, I'd rather own Kimberly-Clark. That's because in tight economic times, Kimberly-Clark offers items people will keep buying, whereas people may limit how often they go out to eat. We are seeing a bit of that play out as McDonald's figures out how to keep prices lower amid rising costs. For its 2026 third-quarter results, restaurant sales in the United States decreased by 0.2%.

In comparison, Kimberly-Clark has more predictable demand, which is nice to have with all the uncertainty swirling around the markets. In the first nine months of 2026, Kimberly-Clark reported a 1% increase in net sales to $12.4 billion. That 1% increase may not sound exciting, but again, it's about consistency. Also, the acquisition of the consumer healthcare company Kenvue gives Kimberly-Clark even more essential products to sell, adding brands like Tylenol and Listerine to its portfolio. While consumers may cut back on going out to eat and ordering burgers and fries, they won't hesitate to buy Huggies or Tylenol if they need them.

If you expect the same or a higher tax rate in retirement, convert traditional IRA or 401(k) money to a Roth after a market drop. You owe income tax on what the shares are worth the day you convert, so the lower price shrinks the bill, and any rebound grows tax-free inside the Roth.

Say every dollar you convert is taxed at your 22% federal rate, and shares in your traditional IRA worth $50,000 in January fall to $40,000 in a downturn. Converting them after the drop, rather than in January, adds $40,000 to your taxable income instead of $50,000, so the federal tax comes to $8,800 instead of $11,000.

If the shares climb back to $50,000, your Roth holds the same $50,000 a January conversion would have left you, for $2,200 less tax. That $10,000 rebound comes out tax-free after 59½, once five tax years have passed since you first put money in a Roth.

Whether converting pays at all depends on your expected tax rate in retirement, which also decides between a Roth and a traditional IRA. Conversions have no income limit, which is what lets high earners use a backdoor Roth.

With a 401(k), convert inside the plan if it offers that option, or roll the money into a Roth IRA once the plan lets you take it out, such as after you leave the job or reach 59½.

Pay the tax from savings outside the IRA or 401(k), with an estimated tax payment or extra withholding from your paycheck. Tax withheld from the conversion itself never reaches the Roth, and if you’re under 59½, it counts as an early withdrawal, which can cost a 10% penalty.

Convert in pieces, too. A conversion made since 2018 can’t be undone, so if the shares keep falling after you convert, you still owe tax on what they were worth that day. Spread the conversion across a few dates, and stop each year’s total at the top of your current tax bracket, since every dollar past it is taxed at the next rate.

Each conversion also starts its own five-year clock, counted from January 1 of the year you convert, and taking converted money out before it runs out can cost anyone under 59½ a 10% penalty. So convert only money you can leave alone for five years.

Japan bond yields surge to 3.75%, hiding a quiet Bitcoin trap

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Demand for Japan’s 20-year bonds fell sharply as yields rose, putting yen-funded Bitcoin trades under scrutiny.

Demand fell at Japan’s Nov. 18 auction of a 20-year government bond, with investors offering to buy 3.64 times the amount the Finance Ministry accepted in the competitive auction, putting pressure on traders who borrow yen to hold Bitcoin.

The average accepted yield rose, while the yen barely moved between the Bank of Japan’s two 5 p.m. Tokyo readings.

In the Nov. 18 results, the ministry recorded ¥1.914 trillion in competitive tenders for issue 198 and accepted ¥525.8 billion. At the October auction, it received ¥3.139 trillion and accepted ¥537.1 billion.

Demand fell by much more than the amount sold in the competitive auction, and the average accepted yield rose 3.3 basis points to 3.756% from 3.723%.

Competitive coverage fell from 5.84× on Oct. 20 to 3.64× on Nov. 18 for Japan’s 20-year issue 198.

Source: Finance Ministry, Oct. 20 result · Finance Ministry, Nov. 18 result

At 5 p.m. in Tokyo, the midpoint of the Bank of Japan’s dollar-yen quote was 159.35 yen per dollar on Nov. 18, up one hundredth of a yen from 159.34 on Nov. 17.

Money-market broker Tokyo Tanshi reported a weighted average unsecured overnight call rate of 1.227%, unchanged from the previous day. The rate measures one-day borrowing in Japan’s call money market, for which the Bank of Japan publishes a daily series.

A trader borrowing yen short-term to hold Bitcoin faces higher costs to renew the loan if funding rates rise. If the trader sells Bitcoin for dollars and leaves the yen debt unhedged, a stronger yen makes repaying the loan in dollar terms more expensive.

Bank for International Settlements researchers documented that feedback during the August 2024 unwind in leveraged currency and equity trades.

Bitfinex reported that aggregate Bitcoin futures open interest fell to 645,750 BTC over Nov. 14 and 15, 8% below Nov. 11. The futures total shows how much exposure was outstanding but gives no breakdown by funding currency.

BIS research showed that although it identified $271 billion in yen-denominated bank loans to non-bank borrowers outside Japan in the first quarter of 2024, it could not determine how much was used for carry trades.

The Finance Ministry plans to offer about ¥300 billion at the Nov. 25 reopening of a 40-year bond, the next test of long-dated demand.

A stronger signal for Bitcoin would be rising short-term yen borrowing costs or a sustained strengthening of the yen, alongside shrinking futures exposure and evidence that traders cutting positions had yen liabilities.

How GTA 6 Is Leaning Into Advanced Physics For More Realistic Car Crashes

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Has this crazy timeline finally found balance? We can't know, but "Grand Theft Auto 6" is actually an honest-to-goodness playable game now. Sure, the longtime PC-only "BeamNG" became available on PlayStation 5 a month before GTA 6, but those "We got X before GTA 6" jokes can finally be put to rest. Thanks for 13 years of meme-ories.

With GTA 6, you'll become the ultimate Florida Man, as the gameplay actually extends beyond Miami-esque Vice City and into Rockstar Games' equally fantastic renderings of the Everglades, the Florida Keys, and elsewhere. For something escapist, though, GTA 6 is unapologetically realistic. Not only do vehicles in GTA 6 require fill-ups, your characters will even be tracked via Flock-like cameras. Factor in the realistic-looking crashes and you might just end up forgetting you're in-game and calling 911. Okay, maybe it's not quite that immersive, but the much-improved physics highlighted in vehicle collisions certainly add to the overall hyper-realistic visual effects.

There's no shortage of car racing games and driving simulators available at our fingertips. But as fun as, say, the Mario Kart franchise is, racing as a cow while riding a boombox with wheels is hardly ideal, let alone realistic. Don't even bother asking about crumple zones. Showcasing soft-body-style dynamics, however, adds so much more to the gameplay and is an impressive feat all its own.

When it comes to simulations, particularly collisions, how the vehicles crash and come apart generally falls into two categories: rigid body and soft body. As the names suggest, rigid bodies don't deform or change shape on-screen, no matter how severe the impact. With soft body physics, the causes and effects are shown. Sheet metal is compressed and contorts in a collision, for example.

With driving games, "BeamNG" was at the forefront and even shares a simple explanation of how it developed its soft-body physics simulation software model. For GTA 6, the driving dynamics and subsequent physics are previewed in the video game's trailers, especially in the mature-content extended version. While they may not be the same soft-body physics that "BeamNG" uses, the visual effects come close.

During chase scenes, bullets damage vehicles as they would in real life. You'll see tires blowing out, bullets ricocheting off grilles, and holes left in door panels. In other scenes, car windows actually shatter when smashed, fuel tanks rupture, and body panels deform. Other notable visual effects include functional dash gauges as well as dust and dirt that kick up as cars pull away.

Most impressive is that all of this is happening in real time during actual gameplay — these scenes aren't all preset. GTA BOOM reported that former Rockstar Games animator Mike York described how two different simulator systems can operate simultaneously through Rockstar's RAGE engine. Movements, impacts, and damage are calculated as they happen while thousands of physics computations per second occur in the background.

It's the little things that you might not have noticed, but now can appreciate. But did we have to wait so long? Well, at least we're not having to reenlist in the Army.

Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella explains why he asked 60K employees to quit

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella explained Wednesday why he offered buyouts to 60,000 of his employees in October, essentially urging them to voluntarily quit instead of reducing the workforce via another massive layoff.

Short answer: AI, but not, he insists, in the way you might expect. He’s not saying he’s replacing employees with AI, especially software engineers. He’s saying he’s reorganizing Microsoft into a company where everyone uses AI to do their work, and their jobs become focused on managing that AI.

The buyout was “to make sure that at least everyone felt like they had agency in making choices,” he told Andrew Ross Sorkin during the DealBook Summit.

“Let’s take software engineering. We went from, ‘Well, we don’t buy that AI can ever do software.’ To suddenly, ‘Wow, it can do all software, and why do we need software engineers?’ To now rediscovering that software engineering is very much alive and, in fact, is going to thrive, except the way the team is organized and the work that a software engineer does has now got a new operating point,” he explained.

For some people, “that reorganization and change is sort of hard, right?” he added, “but, I mean, it has to be done.”

He offered the example of the team that builds Cowork, the AI agent in Microsoft 365 apps. He said the team consists of just 11 developers who have mastered using AI to do their jobs. “They build a software factory that builds the product. They don’t build the product.”

He believes that every employee will one day similarly become a manager of agents, rather than a person directly doing the work. “That level of direction is what, by the way, is going to happen in all jobs.”

But Microsoft, as a 51-year-old company, didn’t grow up as an AI-first company. “So the question is, how quickly can an incumbent transform the work, the workflow, and the work artifact, and with all the wage support that goes with it?” he said. “It’s going to take a bit more time than anyone expects. And there is going to be in all of this, you know, people who may say, ‘Hey, I have some other choices I want to make.’”

When asked about this same topic — why ask 60,000 people to voluntarily resign — last week by Gergely Orosz on his The Pragmatic Engineer podcast, Nadella further explained, “We have created enormous anxiety in people sort of feeling that they don’t have control of their own destiny because the way we have talked about this AI revolution.”

He called out talk about the singularity (the hypothetical point at which AI surpasses human intelligence) and predictions that AI will take away all jobs. “All of that leads anyone who even wants to change to be sort of anxious … You have to sort of walk the hard walk of really making the structural changes, getting people slotted, giving them opportunity.”

In other words, the buyout was the message to employees: You can get with the new AI-first program, which will radically change your job, or you choose to get on with your life. Elsewhere.
