I Took Olive Oil Shots Every Morning For A Month—Is The Health Hype Real?

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Delish editors handpick every product we feature. We may earn commission from the links on this page.

I’ll try just about anything that promises to make me look and feel better, but drinking olive oil? That one gave me pause—until I read up on the impressive benefits and found out Hailey Bieber does it every day. Suddenly, I was down to ditch my green juice habit to sip this alleged liquid gold.

But is there any real advantage to drinking it straight versus incorporating it into your regular diet? Or is it just another TikTok fad that sounds better on your FYP than it works IRL? I decided to take pre-made olive oil shots for an entire month to put the hype to the test. Here’s what happened.

If you didn’t already know, extra-virgin olive oil is a nutritional powerhouse! It’s rich in monounsaturated fats and antioxidants, and research has linked consumption to lower LDL (aka the “bad” cholesterol), better cardiovascular health, and reduced inflammation. It may also support healthy blood sugar levels and brain health, which is pretty cool considering it’s an ingredient we all probably have in our kitchen cabinets.

“Olive oil also contains polyphenols, which are natural plant compounds that help protect our cells from oxidative stress and support a healthy inflammatory response,” Maura MacDonald, M.S., R.D., C.S.S.D., registered dietitian at Thorne, tells Delish. “These compounds can benefit gut health too by interacting with the bacteria in our digestive system and helping to support microbial diversity and a healthy gut microbiome. It’s one of the reasons olive oil is such a staple of the Mediterranean diet, which has long been associated with better overall health and longevity.”

Listen, I knew that chugging olive oil wasn’t going to be a pleasant experience, but I wasn’t expecting it to be quite so, ahem, interesting. My main gripe was with the consistency: It was just a little too thick and viscous to go down easily, and every shot required a full pep talk.

I didn’t notice much difference after day 5, which left me feeling discouraged. I had hoped for an immediate click—glowing skin, boosted energy levels, less bloating, something!—but the only tangible evidence of my participation in the experiment was a glue-like coating that clung to my tonsils after every morning shot.

By week two, I called an audible and started adding lemon juice to the olive oil. Anything to cut the oiliness just a little—plus, the citrus brings its own nutritional perks! And while it wasn’t a perfect fix, it made the process a little more bearable.

Midway through the month, I started to notice improvements. My skin seemed more hydrated, my digestion was more regular, and I wasn’t dealing with the same post-meal bloating I’ve dealt with for years. I also found myself filling up faster, and I didn’t feel the same pull to go back for seconds. Whether it was the olive oil or a placebo effect, I’m not entirely sure, but at least I finally felt like the morning routine was doing something.

As the experiment progressed, I noticed my energy levels improve. I wasn’t exactly jumping out of bed like a morning person (let’s not get carried away here), but I was able to avoid the usual afternoon slump. I had fewer cravings too and wasn’t automatically reaching for snacks between mealtimes. My body just felt like it was functioning better.

I’m so glad you asked! Yes, yes, it does. According to MacDonald, it actually makes a big difference.

“Extra-virgin olive oil is generally the best choice for maximizing health benefits because it’s cold-pressed and unrefined, meaning it retains more of its natural antioxidants and polyphenols,” she explains. “This is the one you’re likely eating at a restaurant when they serve the bread.”

Regular virgin olive oil is also cold-pressed and offers similar benefits, but it has a slightly higher acidity level and milder flavor, while refined olive oil goes through additional processing that reduces even more of those benefits.

“I'd recommend reaching for extra-virgin olive oil whenever possible, especially formaking salad dressings, drizzling over vegetables, or adding to meals,” MacDonald adds. “It's an easy way to get the most nutritional value out of the olive oil you're already using.”

While, for the sake of the experiment, I drank it straight, MacDonald says that it’s actually not the most practical way to consume it.

“I'd recommend incorporating extra-virgin olive oil into your everyday meals instead,” she says. “Not only is it easier on digestion, but it can also help your body absorb fat-soluble vitamins from other foods.”

She doesn’t have to tell me twice. I’ll drizzle it on salads and roasted veggies, mix it with lemon (but for a dressing this time!), and add it to fish and whole grains.

“Using olive oil, a source of fat containing both mono- and polyunsaturated fats, in place of saturated fats like butter is another easy way to support heart health,” MacDonald continues. “It's really about making olive oil part of a balanced eating pattern, rather than treating it as something you need to incorporate each morning separately.”

While it’s not a downside per se, MacDonald points out that a typical serving of olive oil (a.k.a. one tablespoon) has about 120 calories and 14 grams of fat, so a two-tablespoon shot would total about 240 calories and 28 grams of fat. The TL;DR? While it’s a great source of heart-healthy monounsaturated fats and antioxidants, it’s also calorie-dense.

“Taking a shot every morning can add up quickly, especially if you're adding it on top of your usual diet—which may already include using olive oil or other fats in your meals and cooking processes,” she says. “Rather than thinking of olive oil as something extra you need to consume, I’d recommend using it in place of saturated fats, like butter or coconut oil, when applicable, as part of your everyday meals.”

I did notice changes in how I felt overall after taking olive oil shots every day, especially with my digestion. But given MacDonald’s advice that you can reap the same benefits from simply incorporating it into your everyday diet, I think I’ll retire the morning shots in favor of something I’ll actually enjoy—like O.O. on a salad or with a fresh piece of sourdough. I’ll get my daily dose the old-fashioned way, and reserve the shot glasses for my Friday nights.

On day 5 I woke up feeling like a nervous wreck and unsure what to do with my day. I knew rain was coming the next day and was worried about the already muddy forest. I decided to just go loosely with my original plan – to get to the Krishna Sanctuary and then make a call about whether I’d climb halfway up to the Raetea summit to camp with some of my friends. That led to my first time hitching, a mere two minutes into walking, a woman pulled up in her ute with her very cute dog and loaded us into the car and drove us all the way to the Krishna sanctuary. Meeting her and being at the sanctuary was like a full reset for my nervous system. I stayed the night there on the floor of a cabin, massaged my tight calves and nursed my remaining blisters from the beach.

By the time my alarm went off at 5:15am I was feeling good. A little nervous for this boogeyman of a hill but physically strong and ready for a big push. I was so excited to be off the beach.

The day was hard, but getting up the hill went quickly, I walked with two couples I had met the night before and they were fast up and down the hills. We laughed a lot as people slipped in mud and the boys sang and did gollum impressions. By the time we reached the summit I was feeling triumphant, this hill was not so bad!! But then the rain really set in and the descent began. Steep, deep mud for 8km. Eventually I lost the couples, preferring to not rush and navigate the mud alone. I got stuck up to my mid-shins and had to dig myself out with my poles. But overall I was feeling great, my usually-a-problem knee was holding up fantastically and I had only fallen once.

Then, about 5km from the end I slipped and my left leg folded under me and bashed into some sharp roots. It was one of those falls where your whole brain sets off an alarm and you have to count to ten before you can start assessing if you’re really hurt. The sharp pain from the sicks had me wondering if my sock had ripped (luckily no) and I stood and tested my ankle – it seemed ok so I kept walking. I didn’t have much choice.

By the time I made it out of the forest, the rain was getting lighter and I meandered through a misty field trying to find the trail markers.

I was so close to the gravel road to camp when I had my third fall of the day, walking down what I thought was regular dirt but was actually extremely slippery mud, toward a gate that would let me onto the road. I fell in slow motion, swearing at the universe. This fall didn’t hurt my body so much as my ego. I threw my pack over the gate and climbed over the stile before hobbling my muddy butt up the road to the campsite.

I was so incredibly happy to see my friends and the sun peaking through the clouds. After nine and a bit hours I had made it! There was a stream for washing and collecting water and I did both, using my muddy shorts to wash both themselves and my legs. I got my tent up and airing out, and someone gave me pre-boiled water for a hot chocolate. Everything was looking up!

But what goes up must come down I guess, and my left ankle started to swell. Turns out the fall and bashing it on those sticks did hurt. I massaged it and did my best to keep off it and by morning it felt walkable, so off we set.

This day was one of emotional ups and downs, it was supposed to be the chillest, easiest day of the section. Mostly gravel roads for 23km and a dairy (convenience store) 6km in. Unfortunately, my ankle was GRUMPY.

There was a mobile voting place at the dairy so I could vote right on trail and have an ice cream at the same time! The ladies at the voting place were so kind and I told them it was the first time I was able to vote in NZ so this was a big deal for me. I cast my vote then went to the dairy and bought myself an icecream and a canned iced coffee. Glorious.

The next 10km or so up a forestry road flew by. Then my ankle decided it was once again in a bad mood. I hobbled down the forestry road to reach a stream about 1.5km out from camp and stood in its very cold waters for ages to get the swelling down. It was gorgeous and a relief.

Camp this night was much more established, with a water tank, toilet and a shelter. I decided that if my ankle was still sore in the morning I’d stay, I had plenty of food, battery charge, and I knew more people would turn up the next day.

I felt a bit sad at this prospect but I knew I’d feel stupid if I pushed it and had to get off trail. The magic of thru hiking struck this night, and we had a lovely time chatting in the shelter and eating the whole block of Whittaker’s chocolate my friend brought. I got to see a ruru (a native NZ owl) for the first time ever. We all had a good laugh when someone’s tent got raided by a possum. It’s all ups and downs.

My ankle was, indeed, still sore this morning. One of my day-one friends decided to stay with me for the day as she also had tons of food and wanted to take some time to sort out her gear and the blisters on her toes. I managed to get some messages out to Fraser and the trail angel who was going to pick me up to let them know that I was okay but delayed.

We spent the morning slowly drinking our tea and coffee, and eating breakfast. I washed some of my clothes, read with my feet up in the air to help with swelling and munched on our over-abundance of snacks. To be honest, it was exactly what I needed.

Another group of hikers turned up in the afternoon and one was a physio (the trail provides!) she assessed my ankle and did a lymphatic drainage massage on it. She was a life saver. In the morning I felt way better but decided it wasn’t smart to continue on the rougher track ahead and backtracked to the road where I was picked up by a trail angel. This is NOT how I wanted this section to go, I really was looking forward to the river sections, but I needed to be smart and recognize that even with some improvement on my ankle, it was likely an overuse injury and needed real rest and to be checked by a physio in town.

To say I was disappointed is an understatement, I have never had an issue with my ankle like that so it really blindsided me. But taking a break is not quitting. And boy will it make for a good comeback story when I’m recovered and back on trail. For now, it’s several rest days and hoping I don’t get too stir crazy.
