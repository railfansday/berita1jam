Dropout’s Vic Michaelis Wants to Toss Their Star Wars Character Out a Window

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Michaelis' Runa is the breakout star of Zero Company

Dropout star Vic Michaelis used to fall asleep to Attack of the Clones. Not because they hated it, like so many Star Wars prequel detractors, but because it was their comfort watch. “I've seen the first 45 minutes of Episode II, I want to say, 300 times,” Michaelis told Polygon. “It's so comforting. The prequels hold a place in my heart where it is like nothing bad can happen when I'm watching them.”

Michaelis has now gone from watching Star Wars to being a part of the larger Star Wars universe. In Star Wars Zero Company, they played breakout character Runa Blask, the wise-cracking advisor to the titular mercenary group, led by Captain Hawks.

Michaelis hails from the improv comedy world, but they’ve done film and scripted TV shows as well. Zero Company was their first-ever video game role. “I was so simultaneously terrified, obviously, of the video game aspect of it and also, you know, the Star Wars of it all.”

Turns out, that fear was unnecessary. The team at Bit Reactor was familiar with Michaelis’ Dropout work, and that was part of the reason they wanted Michaelis for the role.

Their improv background helped influence their performance, though they weren’t changing lines or jokes. “The improv is so much about the preparation in a way that I think people don't really know.” Instead, all of Runa’s best quips were already in the very strong script. “All of my favorite lines that Runa had were written or pitched lines from the writers,” Michaelis said.

Among those lines were many instances of Runa saying “wizard,” which basically means “cool” in Star Wars. “Runa's very tall, everything is ‘wizard,’” Michaelis said with a laugh, joking they must have said the word “500 times.”

Fans have glommed onto Runa for her constant jokes and ribbing of Hawks. “I'm seeing a lot of people being like, ‘Runa, I love her, and I wanna throw her out a window.’ Which I really resonate with, and I think was part of what we were going for,” Michaelis said. “Big sister vibes.”

Runa can’t be thrown out a window. She actually can’t be harmed at all. While the game features a permadeath feature for its operators, Runa doesn’t go on combat missions and doesn’t leave The Den, Zero Company’s headquarters.

“I did start seeing people devastated and heartbroken that some characters that they put a lot of time and energy into didn't make it through,” Michaelis said. “Do I wish people sort of mourned me in that way? Maybe. I don't know. I’m going to be thinking about that.”

Before Runa was a member of Zero Company, she was a self-described “freelance consultant to the top corporations and families in the galaxy.” AKA, a Separatist financial fixer. She desires a fortune that working for Zero Company will never grant her. As she tells Hawks, if she had that fortune, she’d want to buy a moon and run a resort. Michaelis has much humbler aspirations if they were to live in the world of Star Wars. “I would want to be a small business owner,” Michaelis said, and started describing what would make for a very fun Disney Plus show.

“I want to do a comedy night at my bookstore and all of a sudden, you've got a bunch of interplanetary politics or some red tape of why I can't do this. Then I'm marching up to the Republic with my civil city ordinances, trying to get this comedy night happening, and I'm accidentally now involved and implicated in a sort of a greater plot,” Michaelis said. “And all I wanted to do was try and get a couple of standups at my bookstore at night so I could sell wine after hours.”

If Star Trek can have a critically acclaimed animated sitcom, why can’t Star Wars have a series about a store owner just trying to get a comedy night off the ground? Your move, Disney.

The Lincoln Lawyer Season 5 Successfully Replaces Harry Bosch

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

While The Lincoln Lawyer season 5 won’t feature the title character of Prime Video’s Bosch after all, this could actually be good for the final chapter of Netflix’s legal drama. When Prime Video’s Bosch debuted in February 2014, it is fair to say that even committed fans of detective dramas could not have predicted how big an impact the series would have on the genre in the years that followed.

Adapted from Michael Connelly’s novel series of the same name, Bosch followed Titus Welliver’s titular LAPD homicide detective as he investigated murders while also keeping a close eye on his department’s own corruption issues. While HBO’s True Detective season 1, which arrived within a month of Bosch’s debut, is often called the most influential detective drama of the 2010s, Bosch’s franchise gives the critical hit a run for its money.

Without True Detective, viewers might never have gotten Sharp Objects, Mare of Easttown, and a slew of other acclaimed dark psychological thrillers. However, after Bosch’s seven-season run ended, the Connelly adaptation spawned the three-season sequel series Bosch: Legacy, the two-season spinoff Ballard, and the upcoming prequel Bosch: Start of Watch. The popularity of Connelly’s series even resulted in Netflix turning one of the author’s other novel series into a show with The Lincoln Lawyer.

Staring Manuel Garcia-Rulfo, The Lincoln Lawyer marks the second time Connelly’s troubled antihero, Mickey Haller, made it to the screen. Previously played by Matthew McConaughey in 2011’s standalone movie The Lincoln Lawyer, Haller is a conflicted former addict who gets his nickname thanks to his unconventional decision to run his legal practice out of the boot of his flashy Lincoln town car. Throughout The Lincoln Lawyer’s four seasons to date, Haller has slowly gotten his life back together, while always showing up in the courtroom.

Like Bosch, Haller can pull himself out of his personal troubles when his job calls for it. Netflix’s upcoming The Lincoln Lawyer season 5 was set to make the comparisons between the pair even more obvious, as the outing is based on Connelly’s 2025 novel Resurrection Walk. In the shared fictional universe of Connelly’s novels, Bosch and Haller are half-brothers, and Bosch spends much of Resurrection Walk assisting Haller in his latest case.

Sadly, the Bosch franchise belongs to Prime Video, while Netflix owns the rights to The Lincoln Lawyer. As such, the odds of an in-universe crossover were always vanishingly unlikely, and made even more implausible when Welliver moved on from the role. Although he played Bosch in a guest star capacity during 2025’s Ballard season 1, Welliver’s busy schedule means he won’t even be providing a voice-over for Bosch: Start of Watch.

As such, The Lincoln Lawyer season 5 ended up replacing Bosch’s role in Resurrection Walk with Cobie Smulders’s Emi, Mickey’s half-sister who was introduced in season 4. Emi is set to receive a spinoff of her own after The Lincoln Lawyer ends with season 5, meaning this is the perfect time to get viewers invested in her story.

Speaking to the Spokesman Review as part of the Northwest Passages conversation series, Connelly explained that Bosch became Mickey’s half-sister Emi in season 5’s reworked version of the novel’s plot, and said of the change that “it worked out pretty well.” Considering how much TV adaptations of beloved detectives like Karin Slaughter’s Will Trent often drastically alter their literary source material, it makes sense that Connelly isn’t worried about the change.

It would always have been near-impossible for Bosch and The Lincoln Lawyer to cross over, and not just because of the legal, practical questions of competing streaming services owning the rights to the characters. Bosch’s dark, grim neo-noir tone would clash massively with the melodramatic, sunny fun of Netflix’s The Lincoln Lawyer, a soapier, self-consciously sillier series with none of the former’s patient pacing.

Ultron settles the debate and reveals which of the Avengers is truly the most powerful. The debate over who deserves the title of the strongest Avenger has fueled decades of intense arguments in and out of universe. Strength in the Marvel Universe is rarely a simple measurement of how much weight a hero can lift, after all. The answers vary drastically depending on the battlefield, the status of each hero's power, and whether weapons and energy-based power can be weighed equally against sheer physical strength. Scarlet Witch can neutralize Thor using her magic, but Swordsman can technically take down Scarlet Witch if he catches her with her guard down.

Marvel Comics and the Marvel Cinematic Universe have presented several conflicting answers to this question. Wanda Maximoff has reshaped reality and rewritten the lives of everyone in the universe, while Hyperion is strong enough to stop clashing universes using his bare hands, and Sentry effortlessly rips Ares' body in half. The MCU playfully turned this debate into a running joke in Thor: Ragnarok, where Tony Stark’s computer security recognizes Hulk as the "strongest Avenger" while relegating Thor to "Point Break." And yet, Thor and Captain Marvel quickly claim the title in Avengers: Infinity War and Avengers: Secret Wars.

The "strongest Avenger" debate has many possible answers, one of which gains more authority when supported by one of the Avengers' biggest villains.

Avengers: Twilight takes place in a possible future where superheroes have been outlawed following a catastrophic event known as H-Day, which resulted in countless deaths and the destruction of Boston. During a televised discussion, an older Steve Rogers and James Stark, Tony Stark's son, revisit the long-running debate about which Avenger deserves to be called the strongest. The young Stark points out that the individual responsible for the highest death toll was the Hulk, who had been placed under Ultron's control. When discussing the incident, Stark confirms Hulk is the strongest Avenger, as Ultron chose to weaponize the Green Goliath rather than Thor or Captain Marvel to destroy the city.

James Stark's assessment measures strength through destructive potential over isolated displays of power. Thor is a literal god who has fought entities like Hela and Surtur, and Captain Marvel can singlehandedly defeat entire alien armies, but Hulk offers a terrifying level of strength that rises indefinitely alongside his rage. Once Ultron gained control of Banner's alter ego, the robot turned one of the Avengers' best defenders into a living weapon capable of destroying an entire city as a starter feat. While Thor, Captain Marvel, and even Iron Man could technically stop Hulk at his base level, nobody can stop the Green Goliath once he crosses a certain anger threshold.

James Stark's declaration that the Hulk is the strongest Avenger in Avengers: Twilight #1 becomes increasingly difficult to dispute. The most famous example is World War Hulk, when the Green Scar returns to Earth in search of vengeance against the Illuminati and illustrates how he could singlehandedly defeat all of Earth's superheroes in a gladiator tournament. A similar demonstration takes place during Incredible Hulks #632, when Hulk's transformation into Worldbreaker threatens to unleash enough energy to destroy North America. Besides, the Illuminati choose to banish the Hulk to outer space precisely because they understand that he's one of the biggest threats to Earth, and their preemptive decision ends up backfiring.

It's been seen on the page and debated for years: which classic Marvel Comics team would win in a straight-up fight? The Avengers or the X-Men?

More recent Marvel stories have taken this terrifying concept even further. In the new Ultimate Universe, Earth-6160, a considerably weaker Hulk oversees horrific experiments on Gamma Island and leaves the resistance team seemingly dead. On Earth-616, the Infernal Hulk defeats the Avengers, the Fantastic Four, and the X-Men, and he transforms many of them into monsters for his army. Parallel to The Infernal Hulk, Avengers: Armageddon explores a similar nightmare with Thunderbolt Ross, whose Red Hulk transformation turns the longtime Hulk hunter into the harbinger of a potential World War III and destroys an entire city in Europe, just like Avengers: Twilight's Ultron-manipulated Hulk.

Here's the Average Social Security Benefit by Age 62 to 80

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Claiming at 62 versus 70 creates a gap that reshapes your entire retirement income picture.

Social Security provides a vital floor for your retirement, but it's meant to supplement your income rather than replace it entirely. The amount you receive depends heavily on when you choose to claim, making it one of the most consequential financial decisions you'll face.

The Social Security Administration (SSA) calculates your benefit based on an average of your wages across your 35 highest-earning years, adjusted for inflation and run through a specific formula. Your final check size is then determined by your full retirement age (FRA) and the age you begin collecting.

Here is the average monthly retired-worker benefit by age, as of December 2025, the most recent data available.

The data reveals a clear increase between ages 62 and 70, with the average benefit growing by 59.7%. This represents a monthly gap of $850.28, or $10,203.36 per year.

Full retirement age (FRA) ranges from 66 to 67, depending on your birth year, and it's 67 for everyone born in 1960 or later. If you claim at 62, you face a permanent reduction of up to 30%, while waiting until 70 earns you an 8% annual increase through delayed retirement credits.

A second pattern appears after age 70, as averages begin to decline slightly. This doesn't mean the SSA cuts benefits for individuals. Instead, older groups likely include more people who claimed early at permanent reductions.

Women also make up a larger share of the oldest groups because they tend to live longer. Because they often have lower lifetime earnings due to the gender pay gap and time spent caregiving, their presence in the data lowers the overall average among the oldest retirees.

Even at the age-70 peak of $2,274.68 per month, or $27,296.16 per year, benefits rarely cover every bill. That peak falls $34,135.84 short of the $61,432 average senior household spending reported by the Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS).

A 3.6% cost-of-living adjustment (COLA) will take effect in 2027, adding about $82 a month on top of that age-70 benefit. While these adjustments are designed to keep pace with inflation, they often fall short.

The most useful number for your planning is the average that matches your own intended claiming age.

Even those who claim Social Security benefits at age 70, when they qualify for their maximum checks, rarely live comfortably on their benefits alone. You'll probably need other sources of retirement income to cover what Social Security doesn't.

Personal savings are your best bet if they're available to you. You're free to spend that money on anything you like, but remember it may have to last you 20 years or more, so choose a withdrawal strategy designed to make it last. If you don't have a lot of personal savings, you may need a job or other government benefits to supplement your Social Security checks.

Make sure you come up with a plan for how you'll cover your remaining living expenses before you apply for Social Security. If you're married, be sure to factor in your spouse's Social Security checks and personal savings as well, and make sure you're both on the same page about when each person plans to claim.

TL;DR: TeraWulf’s November 6 earnings release shows $63 million in third-quarter high-performance-computing lease revenue, around 83% of its $76.2 million total. The company is increasingly an AI infrastructure business, although large non-cash gains and long-dated construction plans require careful reading.

TeraWulf is becoming a clear example of how the economics of Bitcoin mining can lead an infrastructure operator into a rather different business. In its third-quarter results published November 6, the Nasdaq-listed company said high-performance computing, or HPC, generated $63 million of lease revenue in the three months to September 30.

That represented about 83% of its $76.2 million quarterly revenue. Investors who still describe the firm primarily as a Bitcoin miner risk missing where its business is now concentrated and what its future costs may look like.

The company operates existing mining infrastructure at its Lake Mariner campus but is repurposing portions of that site to support contracted computing workloads. TeraWulf said 102 megawatts of revenue-generating critical IT capacity were online at Lake Mariner at quarter-end, with another 336 megawatts under construction.

These numbers are not interchangeable with power agreements or theoretical campus capacity. A contract for future electricity or land does not mean an equivalent amount of paying data-centre capacity is already operating. The distinction matters when a company is projecting revenue from facilities that require years and significant capital to complete.

TeraWulf also reported net income of approximately $709.9 million for the quarter. But it attributed a significant benefit to a non-cash change in the fair value of warrants, while adjusted EBITDA was negative at about $12.8 million. That makes the headline profit a poor substitute for analysing recurring rental revenue, financing costs and operating cash flow.

The company’s release described a 20-year Anthropic lease covering approximately 401 megawatts at its Justified development in Kentucky, representing around $19 billion in contracted revenue over the initial term. It also described a Google credit backstop supporting anticipated project financing and a definitive engineering, procurement and construction agreement with Fluor. These are material commercial commitments, not a claim that $19 billion has already been received.

TeraWulf expects phased service availability at Justified to begin in late 2027 and continue into 2028. At Muskie, it said contracted power capacity had expanded toward one gigawatt, subject to regulatory approval. Schedules, financing and utility infrastructure remain central risks.

NewsBTC has previously examined the mining industry’s move towards AI infrastructure. TeraWulf’s fresh results make that theme measurable: a majority of present revenue now comes from leases rather than mining.

A miner switching to AI can reduce its exposure to Bitcoin production economics, including changes in difficulty, coin price and power cost. It can also create demand for the same grid connections, facilities and financing that historically supported mining. That transition need not be immediately positive or negative for Bitcoin’s network; it depends on which machines are retired, which are redeployed and whether remaining miners take up capacity.

How a bottleneck calculator for your GPU and CPU works

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

They're a quick way to help you check component compatibility before you build or upgrade a system.

Troubleshooting computer components can be daunting. After the physical labor of installing a new part, you'll want to stress-test it to make sure your replacement works properly. Finding a faulty part is relatively straightforward, but what if the component is limiting the rest of your system? During a stress test, you might see your CPU at 100 percent while your GPU is practically idling.

This is considered a bottleneck. When one component holds back the rest of the system, it hinders performance until you resolve the bottleneck. For example, if your CPU is at 100 percent while your GPU is barely drawing power, we'd classify that as a CPU bottleneck since it's impairing your GPU's performance. Knowing which component is bottlenecking your system helps you decide what to upgrade or troubleshoot next. Before you buy a new GPU or any expensive component, check for bottlenecks first.

Some websites and software offer bottleneck calculators to help you troubleshoot these issues without stress-testing your system. While they can help you quickly spot a hardware mismatch, they're rarely accurate. Many online calculators can't tell the full story of how your system behaves. That said, a bottleneck calculator can help you check component compatibility before you build or upgrade a system, saving you valuable time while troubleshooting.

A bottleneck calculator takes your chosen CPU and GPU and compares their performance at your target resolution— common test outputs are 1080p, 1440p and 2160p. If both components are rated to perform adequately at your target benchmark, you'll receive a bottleneck score around ten percent or less. However, if one component is ill-equipped to handle the benchmark, it will receive a high percentage, indicating it's a bottleneck. The lower the percentage, the better the score.

Typically, the CPU is the culprit behind most PC bottlenecks. The CPU's job is to send information to the rest of your system in a timely fashion. If it's too slow, other components will wait for the CPU to finish. This is usually what causes stutters in video games. However, if your GPU is hovering around 95 percent usage while struggling to render the application, you may have a GPU bottleneck; GPUs tend to struggle when rendering graphics well above their intended resolution or frame rate. A bottlenecked GPU can cause screen tearing, low frame rates and inconsistent performance.

That is why bottleneck calculators ask for your target resolution: your GPU's performance depends heavily on how many pixels it renders each second. Paradoxically, this clinical approach to calculating performance is what makes bottleneck calculators so unreliable: they rely on spec sheets and theory, not real data from your PC.

Bottleneck calculators can't give you truly accurate data because of how they work. Since they reference manufacturer spec sheets, they omit variability from other system components and the application(s) you're testing. Not every graphics card is made equal, just as not every video game uses your GPU as effectively. A newer Intel GPU may struggle to run an older DX11 application that most Nvidia or AMD cards would run flawlessly. A powerful workstation CPU like the Ryzen 9 7950X won't perform as well as many X3D Ryzen chips for gaming because they have smaller L3 caches. These nuances are completely lost in these bottleneck calculators.

While they may not be ideal for detailed system troubleshooting, bottleneck calculators help with system-build planning. They're a convenient way to quickly check if your chosen CPU and GPU can handle your target resolution out of the box. They can also be a fun way for hobbyists to check strange hardware combinations you wouldn't normally see.

But for those looking for accurate tests on their PC hardware, performance monitoring software will always yield more reliable results. Bottleneck calculators can't account for your installed drivers, background apps you should disable, or performance issues in the game you're benchmarking. A simple bottleneck percentage doesn't tell the full story.
