Rabiot then laid the ball off to Modric, who immediately played it into the space vacated by Cittadini. Under pressure, Frosinone inadvertently turned the ball into their own net to give Milan a 1-0 lead.

Frosinone looked to commit numbers into Milan’s half, creating ball-sided overloads to progress through the pitch. Their two wingers maintained wide positions to stretch Milan’s wing-backs and create more space in the central areas.

Out of possession, Frosinone adopted a 3-4-3 to match Milan’s 3-4-2-1 in possession. They also maintained a very high defensive line, looking to press Milan aggressively and win the ball back as high up the pitch as possible.

Milan allowed Frosinone to dictate possession, dropping into a deeper defensive block to invite them into their half. Once Frosinone advanced, Milan looked to win the ball back and launch quick counter-attacks.

Frosinone’s lack of a clear attacking structure and, more importantly, an organised rest defence left them vulnerable in transition. Milan exploited this disorganisation repeatedly, with the next four goals all benefiting from the spaces Frosinone left behind.

The second goal perfectly illustrated Milan’s approach in transition. Frosinone had committed ten players into Milan’s half, leaving very little defensive cover behind.

Ramos won possession, and Musah immediately played a vertical pass to release Moreira through on goal. Moreira and Rabiot then drew three recovering defenders towards them before creating the space for Ramos to finish.

Frosinone’s high-risk approach briefly paid off with Masini’s goal. They drew Milan into central areas before pressing aggressively to recover possession. With their players positioned close together, they were able to combine quickly and progress the ball towards Kvernadze.

A deflection off De Winter then took the ball into Ghedjemis’ path, allowing him to set up Masini for the finish.

Frosinone consistently targeted Milan’s left flank, particularly the space around Bartesaghi. The individual duel between Ghedjemis and Bartesaghi became a key feature of the game.

Ghedjemis repeatedly beat his marker in 1v1 situations and posed a threat. Recognising the problem, Amorim took Bartesaghi off at half-time.

After the break, Jashari and Pulisic began making coordinated counter-movements. Pulisic dropped deeper, drawing his marker away and creating space in the wide attacking area, which Jashari then exploited with an advancing run.

The goal was very similar to the movement Jashari made against Venezia, highlighting a recurring pattern in Milan’s attacking play.

Both Cisse’s and Pulisic’s goals came from a counter-attack following a Frosinone corner, for which they did not leave many men back.

For Cisse’s finish, his body orientation suggested he was going to shoot towards the goalkeeper’s right, prompting the keeper to adjust his stance accordingly. Cisse then went the other way, finding the opposite corner.

The biggest limitations of ChatGPT

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

From fabricated citations to forgotten instructions, ChatGPT still has some frustrating blind spots.

It's no secret that ChatGPT can write up emails, become your software troubleshooter and even dumb down complicated questions in an instant. But it's also pretty capable of making up responses so polished that you might not think to verify the shaky facts behind them. If only most of us had the confidence that ChatGPT has when it is hallucinating. Even after OpenAI brought its advanced GPT-6 model to all ChatGPT users in October 2026, the chatbot still has the tendency to fabricate supporting evidence, misread an ambiguous question and even forget instructions amid a lengthy conversation.

Some shortcomings are tied to the model or version of it that you're using, along with any plugins you've added. And just because you're paying for ChatGPT Plus or the $100 ChatGPT Pro plan doesn't mean you're guarded against the chatbot's biggest limitations. To be fair, this isn't a problem unique to OpenAI, as most of these shortcomings are common in AI assistants we compared, like Claude.

Topping the list of ChatGPT's biggest quirks is its bad habit of hallucinating, or crafting details that seem legitimate but are entirely made up. Packing all the confidence of a subject-matter expert, ChatGPT will cite studies that don't exist and make them almost believable with imaginary quotations too.

OpenAI is quite upfront about this and warns that ChatGPT could invent quotes and cite fake sources. Using ChatGPT Search does make things better, but just because the response has some live links doesn't make the final statement correct. While an expert on the topic could probably catch the counterfeit sources upon closer inspection, this could be troublesome for someone looking for references in uncharted territory.

ChatGPT can also follow detailed instructions and acknowledge a mistake you've pointed out, only to ignore it a few prompts later. One user on OpenAI's community forum reported that ChatGPT reused earlier details in a report and presented them as verified, then repeated the mistake after admitting it.

But then, that's one person's experience and not exactly strong evidence of this being a problem across the board. That said, researchers found that the reliability of language models tends to go down as input length increases, even if the tasks aren't inherently complex. The researchers tested 18 different models, using older versions of OpenAI's GPT, Google's Gemini and Anthropic's Claude, so the findings may not be an accurate depiction of how GPT-6 performs. And just because you've fed your AI model an enormous amount of context doesn't guarantee it will make the most of it.

If you're working on a lengthy project across several days, being mindful of the changes requested and repeating the given instructions is part of the play. Sure, it can get frustrating that a tool that should be saving you time needs so much babysitting, but the alternative is far more time- and labor-intensive. OpenAI also acknowledges that ChatGPT's Memory feature doesn't retain every detail from every conversation anyway.

SFA could open ‘huge can of worms’ with ‘two-game’ Hyun-jun Yang ban after Celtic scenes – Keith Wyness

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Celtic winger Hyun-jun Yang could potentially face a “two-game” ban after he appeared to dive to win a free-kick during last weekend’s 4-1 win over St Mirren.

That is according to former Aberdeen chief executive Keith Wyness, speaking exclusively to Football Insider, who believes that the South Korean could potentially face retrospective punishment.

Yang went down midway through the St Mirren half in the 32nd minute, with referee Matthew MacDermid awarding a free-kick, which Alex Oxlade-Chamberlain eventually scored the opening goal from.

However, Wyness believes that the SFA must set a new precedent of banning players for dives outside of penalty incidents if they are to punish Yang.

Fans on social media have been in uproar over the incident, while former Premier League referee Dermot Gallagher has pointed out that VAR couldn’t intervene as it wasn’t a penalty incident.

Yang’s incident has prompted plenty of debate in recent days, but Wyness is sceptical as to whether the 24-year-old will face any punishment.

Aberdeen’s former chief Wyness – who served as CEO at Pittodrie between 2000 and 2004 and now runs a consultancy business advising elite clubs – believes that the SFA would be opening a “huge can of worms” if they handed a ban to Yang.

The Scottish businessman pointed out that it would then set a precedent for dives to be reviewed all over the pitch, not just in the penalty area.

Speaking on the latest edition of Football Insider’s Inside Track podcast, Wyness explained that it is not a straightforward decision as to whether Yang should face punishment.

“Well, this is probably a little bit more interesting in terms of the situation here because there have been retrospective actions on penalties, but this was for a free kick,” he said.

“He could see up to a two-game ban. But what that would mean is opening up a whole new situation of simulation for free kicks, which could be all over the pitch as opposed to penalty kicks.

“Now that's a big difference. And so we'll see how they actually handle that one. It may not be an area they want to go into in terms of opening up simulation for free kicks everywhere because that does open a huge can of worms.

“Penalty kicks I understand but I think this may be just too big a bridge for them to travel on this one.”

Wyness recently told Football Insider that Celtic fans will be unhappy to see the club exploit their “glorious past” with their latest release of watches ahead of the Christmas period.

The Bhoys are selling special edition Lisbon Lions watches at £299, with only 1,967 set to be sold, the year of their Champions League triumph.

However, Wyness only expects the club to make around “£100,000” in profit from the new scheme.

He also feels that Celtic may have handed their fanbase another stick to beat them with, as the discontent at Parkhead continues.

iRestore Elite Laser Hair Growth System Review: Husband-Approved

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

The Aging Process is hard. It’s jarring, for example, when the luscious, flowing locks of your youth start to disappear before your very eyes. But there's nothing to do about this natural hair loss—or is there? While still the subject of ongoing research, red-light therapy has been used to treat inflammation, pain relief, and wrinkles, and—as it turns out—it may also help with hair regrowth. It’s also a much more accessible option than flying to Istanbul for a full-on hair transplant.

I learned about iRestore’s Elite Laser Growth Hair System after it came across my TikTok FYP. My algorithm likely served it to me because of my own hair woes (scalp irritation and eczema). Upon learning more about the system, I hoped it could help me with cell regeneration and kill off the bacteria causing flares on my scalp.

However, an even better test subject stood before me: my sweet husband, who is dealing with a rapidly receding hairline. So I roped him into testing this laser helmet—for science, of course. And to better understand said science, I spoke with Zakia Rahman, MD, FAAD, a clinical professor of dermatology at Stanford University School of Medicine.

Red-light therapy didn’t start as a treatment for antiaging and recovery. It actually came about as an accidental solution for hair loss in the 1960s, when Hungarian scientist Endre Mester was researching whether exposure to red light could treat cancer in hairless mice. Imagine his surprise when the mice started growing hair.

Red-light therapy has come a long way since then. According to Rahman, red-light wavelengths are thought to activate the mitochondria in skin cells, which may encourage an uptick in the production of adenosine triphosphate (ATP) to promote new growth. Red-light therapy may also improve blood flow to hair follicles.

Importantly, iRestore's devices are FDA-cleared—a safety designation from the US Food and Drug Administration indicating that a product is “substantially equivalent” to another legally marketed device. There are three hair-growth devices in iRestore's lineup: the entry-level Essential ($425), the mid-tier Professional ($799), and the Elite ($1,699). Each contains iRestore’s proprietary Lumitech technology, which combines LED lights and medical-grade lasers. These laser diodes, called Vixo lasers, operate in the range of 655 to 680 nanometers (nm). (Preliminary studies with mice have shown that using red light in the 600- to 660-nm range, and infrared wavelengths in the 800- to 850-nm range, can promote cell regeneration, pain relief, and more.)

The idea is that this dynamic duo of lights and lasers can penetrate deeper into the skin than ordinary red light with lasers, while the LEDs ensure light is distributed across the scalp for maximum efficacy. Together, they reach a maximum of 2,500 milliwatts (mW) in the Elite, compared to the Professional's 1,410 mW and Essential's 600 mW.

The Elite is iRestore’s top-of-the-line offering and has the most LEDs of the three laser hair-growth systems, with 500 diodes in total—that's 300 lasers and 200 LEDs. The Essential has 51 lasers and 69 LEDs for a total of 120 diodes, while the iRestore Professional has 82 lasers and 200 LEDs for a total of 282 diodes. The Elite was also designed to cover more of the scalp than the other two models and requires 12 minutes of daily use, while the Professional and Essential require 25 minutes every other day.

If price is not a factor, the Elite is the no-brainer choice due to its higher-concentrated light therapy, shorter use time, and potentially faster results.

The helmet arrived in hefty red packaging containing the helmet, a portable battery pack (annoyingly, this is a $99 add-on purchase), a charging cord, and a red-and-black zippered travel case. It feels very fragile, and I wouldn't throw it around—then again, you probably wouldn't plan to shove such an expensive device in the back of your bathroom vanity.

During the daily 12-minute sessions with the Elite helmet, you'll feel as if you're sitting under bright, hot sunlight. The remote displays a countdown of how many minutes you have left, and it beeps once the cycle is complete. Because I was testing the helmet to treat my dry scalp and eczema, I grew increasingly concerned with every passing minute of feeling warmer and warmer. (Sweating is a big no-no for eczema, so I wasn’t thrilled to experience this side effect.) With time, my husband was able to get used to the heat-lamp effect, as it’s not painful, just initially uncomfortable.

Within the first two weeks of continuously using the helmet, I was thrilled to see baby hairs begin to pop up around my husband's hairline. The growth continued through two months of regular use. The top of his crown had more volume and overall hair density, while new growth appeared in the more drastically affected areas near his forehead. While the additional hair didn’t completely cover his scalp, the change was stark enough for old college friends and family members to remark on how good his hair looked.

The effects aren't permanent, though. Unfortunately, my husband stopped his iRestore routine after a few months due to travel. While the iRestore Elite comes with a case that makes it super portable, my husband was nervous about misplacing it while we were on the go. (Also, the thing takes up a decent amount of space.) Unfortunately, stopping the treatments reversed the progress in a matter of weeks. Just like that, he was back to square one.

Rahman likens this phenomenon to the importance of a commitment to a healthy daily diet and regular exercise; one instance of either won't make a difference in your health. Rather, consistency is required to drive results. When I told her that my husband's hair growth had fallen off, she strongly encouraged us to pick it back up.

In addition to using the red-light therapy helmet as recommended, iRestore suggests adding thickening haircare products (shampoo, conditioner, supplements, and serum) to your regimen to get the best results. The star ingredient in the serum and shampoo is plant-based Redensyl, which is meant to fortify hair follicles during their regrowth. Additional elements—like the shampoo's castor oil and biotin, and the conditioner's vegan keratin and argan oil—act as natural strengtheners.

The supplements contain natural dihydrotestosterone (DHT) blockers derived from saw palmetto. These blockers work to prevent the enzyme (5-alpha reductase) from transforming testosterone into DHT. In 2022, Iraq’s National Museum in Baghdad reopened after being closed for three years following political unrest. Then in 2024, the museum closed again, this time for renovations. Now, it’s scheduled to open on December 1.

The Art Newspaper reported that the museum will open with several new galleries, such as the Abbasid and Islamic galleries. There will also be specially curated wings.

The museum will display artifacts including winged bulls (lamassu) and stone carvings depicting legendary Assyrian monarchs, ancient clay tablets and historical prisms recording legal codes, calligraphic friezes, stone carvings, glassworks, and mosaics that have been recovered and restored. Once-struggling retailers are entering the holiday quarter with improved performance and sales momentum as turnaround efforts begin to resonate with shoppers.

A handful of earnings reports on Tuesday pointed to a mix of factors behind the strong results, including a resilient consumer, stronger execution and demand for the right products at the right price.

Kohl's reported comparable sales growth of 1.2% for the period ended Oct. 31 and raised its full-year outlook as CEO Michael Bender said customers "have remained resilient" but are "migrating to value with their purchases."

He tied improvement to the company's work on product assortment, store experience and value.

Best Buy also on Tuesday posted its highest comparable sales growth in five years, at 6.1%, which prompted the electronics retailer to raise its full-year outlook.

"We have a tremendous amount of momentum," CEO Jason Bonfig told CNBC in an interview. "The fact that we saw pretty much broad-based growth across almost all of our categories is a lot of momentum that we're very excited about."

The company also credited execution, market share gains and a compelling product cycle alongside growing customer demand.

At Dick's Sporting Goods, quarterly comparable sales rose 2.6% at the core business and 0.5% at Foot Locker. CEO Lauren Hobart said "all income demographics grew" and the company "didn't see trade-down from best to better or better to good."

She similarly pointed to new products and market share gains within the category as contributing to the strong quarter. She also highlighted continued improvement at Foot Locker.

All three companies have been undergoing efforts to boost sales and get the right products on shelves to meet customer demand.

Target, likewise undergoing revitalization efforts, last week reported comparable sales growth of 5.6% in the third quarter, fueled by a 5% increase in comparable traffic. The retailer raised its full-year outlook.

CEO Michael Fiddelke at the time called the traffic gain "a healthy sign that guests are responding to our changes and choosing Target more often."

Even Gap CEO Richard Dickson told CNBC the company has moved from "rebuild mode" to "turnaround mode" at its Old Navy banner, a sign of progress, albeit not a broad-based portfolio recovery.

Michael Baker, a retail analyst at D.A. Davidson, told CNBC he sees both a resilient consumer and company-specific improvements as driving sector strength.

"Retail sales have been more resilient than most think," Baker said. His analysis showed retail sales, excluding autos and gas, rose 5.1% in the third quarter on a nonseasonally adjusted basis — the strongest quarter since the pandemic.

But he cautioned that growth appears to be coming more from transaction size than traffic, with inflation, promotions and higher units per transaction driving up spend.

Dollar Tree, which reported better-than-expected earnings on Tuesday morning, posted a 3.8% increase to traffic, accelerating from 0.4% growth in the prior quarter, suggesting more shoppers may be bargain hunting. Comparable sales at the retailer increased 4.6% during the period.

"Consumers are under pressure, and they are increasingly selective about where and how they spend," said Dollar Tree CEO Michael Creedon on the company's earnings call. "When under pressure, shoppers prioritize value and buy closer to need."

Comparable sales of everyday essentials rose 8.9% at Dollar Tree stores in the third quarter, while sales of nonessential items were essentially flat, the company said.

The number of regions where patients have limited choices for Affordable Care Act plans is on the rise, according to a new analysis.

The Robert Wood Johnson Foundation report found that for the 2027 plan year, there are more than 200 counties in which there is only one insurer available, and close to 1,000 where there are just two plans available for consumers to choose from.

This makes for an increase of 50% from the 2026 plan year, with more than a third of counties having limited options on the ACA's marketplaces for 2027.

Most states (39) will have at least one county where plan choices are limited for enrollees. For about half of these states, this is the norm and aligns with 2026, but for the rest, this marks a significant shift, the RWJF researchers wrote in the report.

"For the individual market as a whole, the decline in participation is sobering but not a fatal blow," per the report. "The presence of federal and some state subsidies provides an important backstop that will likely prevent a complete meltdown."

"Yet the prospect of a growing number of counties with little insurer participation is grim, and likely will not bode well for premium increases, morbidity or enrollment going forward," according to the report.

The foundation cites a recent analysis from Wakely that found the individual market declined by 16% in the first six months of 2026. The end of the enhanced premium tax credits, cost pressures and growing administrative burdens are likely to contribute to worsening trends in this space.

For instance, ACA marketplace exits from Cigna, CareSource and Medica are having a significant impact in Iowa and Indiana for the coming plan year, the researchers found. In the latter case, most Indiana counties have two or fewer plan choices.

However, there are some bright spots, RWJF researchers wrote. There are certain insurers participating in the exchanges who have beefed up their footprints, and there is a smattering of new entrants. Oscar Health, for example, is planning the largest growth, entering a new state and adding new counties in some of the states it already serves.

Some insurers, like Centene and UnitedHealthcare, are making strategic exits in some markets while entering others, for a neutral impact on their footprints overall.

Insurers like Antidote Health and Peak Health are also entering new regions—even if on a limited basis in some cases—which can help staunch the bleeding.

Blue Cross Blue Shield plans also often play a key role in limited markets, as in close to 80% of counties with a single insurer, that payer is a Blues plan. In addition, in 12% of counties, the single insurer is Elevance Health, which operates plans as Anthem Blue Cross Blue Shield.

"This suggests that were it not for the somewhat unique role of Blues plans as a backstop, the threat of a bare county might be higher," per the report. "Single-state Blues, in particular, are under social and political pressure to participate in much if not all of their state, and do not feel as free to exit unprofitable counties as are publicly traded insurers."
