Tough-tackling Schlager proves his worth for Forest

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Xaver Schlager has made more tackles than any other player in the Premier League and it helped Nottingham Forest beat Manchester City. With insight from his coaches past and present, Adam Bate examines why the unsung Austrian is quietly impressive...

Football Features Writer @ghostgoal

Xaver Schlager has been quietly impressive for Nottingham Forest this season but what was missing was that big moment. It came in the 3-1 win over Manchester City on Saturday when he dispossessed Elliot Anderson for Liam Delap's first goal.

The free transfer from Leipzig nipped in ahead of the £116m man that he has replaced in Forest's midfield and set Delap on the way to doubling the lead. Schlager is no Anderson but he has his own qualities and they made the difference at the weekend.

"It was a top team performance and it was a top performance by Xaver Schlager," said Forest boss Oliver Glasner in the press conference afterwards. He pointed out that "it must have been a fantastic performance" because City's midfielders are world-class.

Please use Chrome browser for a more accessible video player

Schlager was better. "He is so smart, anticipating and getting balls, one of the most for interceptions in the league. And he was physically so strong in his 50-50 balls. He always stayed on his feet and won these balls, because he can then play forward."

In fact, despite only coming on as a late substitute in Forest's opening defeat at home to Leeds, Schlager has made more tackles than any other player in the Premier League this season. It is a good illustration of the combative style that is his trademark.

Not every supporter has been enamoured by Schlager and perhaps that is inevitable given the team's underwhelming start to the season. When searching for explanations, new arrivals come under scrutiny and the 29-year-old Austrian is not one for the future.

But he has had a fine career. Before Leipzig, he worked with Glasner at Wolfsburg, having emerged at Red Bull Salzburg before that. Oscar Garcia spotted him in their feeder team Liefering, another of Glasner's former clubs, and gave him his senior debut.

"He had very good potential," Oscar tells Sky Sports. "Some players in midfield left and we wanted to give him the chance to play. In the structure of Red Bull there were other players ahead of him but we saw that he was a player who could do almost everything."

Not that the self-deprecating Schlager would admit that. Speaking in the summer, he acknowledged his limitations, revealing that "you will not see me too much" as he keeps the game simple. "I do the dirty things you do not recognise but someone has to do it."

Aside from his standout moment that was impossible to miss, that was exactly what he did in the win over City. In what was a particularly intense game, Schlager was the only outfield player on either side to win possession of the ball more times than he lost it.

As a result, the player who in that amusing introduction to the fans admitted that he was "not the quickest" and "not as skilful as some" is actually crucial. Maybe this was the key line? "The most important thing is I want to make my teammates around me better."

James McAtee, Schlager's partner in midfield for much of the season so far, is enjoying the best form of his Premier League career. Ryan Yates took the plaudits on his return to the starting line-up on Saturday. Schlager is a facilitator, a true team player.

Speaking to his former coach Oscar about him, he remains a huge admirer. "In midfield, he can have the ball, he can make the last pass, he can play with the left, he can play with the right, and he can recover the ball very well when the team is gegenpressing."

The passing aspect is not obvious but the statistics show that while his pass completion rate is 84.5 per cent, his expected completion rate is just 77.6 per cent given the difficulty of those passes under pressure. He is in the top 20 for overperformance on this metric.

In other words, Xaver Schlager is tidy on the ball and working incredibly hard off it. On Saturday, he ran 11.92 kilometres, more than anyone else on the pitch. He is not Elliot Anderson. But he is contributing. That big moment against City highlighted it perfectly.

‘The Odyssey’ Has Delivered Record Profits for IMAX

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

‘The Odyssey’ Has Delivered Record Profits for IMAX

IMAX reported its strongest quarterly financial performance in the company’s history last week, with record box office, revenue, and profits for the third quarter of 2026.

Christopher Nolan’s The Odyssey drove the results, becoming the highest-grossing IMAX release of all time.

THR reports that the company generated $712 million in global box office during the quarter, a 90% increase year over year and a significant rise from the $372 million recorded during the same period last year.

The Odyssey accounted for more than $550 million of that total in worldwide IMAX box office, contributing more than 30% of the film’s overall theatrical earnings. Its success was supported by premium screenings, including 70mm presentations that attracted audiences to IMAX locations.

Revenue increased 64% to $175 million, compared with $106.7 million in the third quarter of 2025. Net income climbed to $117 million, up from $22.6 million a year earlier.

“These results demonstrate the significant operating leverage and opportunity as we further expand the network and grow Imax box office,” says IMAX CFO Natasha Fernandes.

Content solutions revenue increased 110% to $94 million, reflecting the rise in global box office, while technology products and services revenue grew 28% to $77 million.

IMAX CEO Rich Gelfond says the results represented a broader shift in audience demand for theatrical experiences.

“What IMAX achieved goes beyond simply a ‘great quarter’ and transcends the many records we shattered in the period. What we believe we are witnessing is an awakening to the power and potential of our platform to impact global culture, unlock new commercial opportunities, and inspire a new level of demand and awe in audiences around the world,” he says, per THR.

“Christopher Nolan’s The Odyssey is a historic cinematic achievement crafted entirely with IMAX technology and its record-breaking success marks a new era of opportunity for our company. The record margins we generated in the quarter demonstrate the unique strength of our model, and the growing value of scarce events and experiences in an increasingly AI-driven world underscore a powerful opportunity for our business, as we look ahead to a remarkably strong slate over the next several years.”

The company expects to significantly exceed its previous full-year 2026 box office guidance of $1.4 billion, which would itself represent a record. Its expansion programme is also continuing, with more than 100 agreements for IMAX systems signed worldwide so far this year. Approximately 65% of those agreements involve new locations.

THR notes that IMAX is also exploring new auditorium formats, including smaller premium venues designed to offer audiences an immersive viewing experience through larger aspect ratios, greater depth of view and improved sound. Additionally, the company is considering adding between 10 and 15 new 70mm film locations in response to demand and the upcoming film slate.

DOJ lawsuit says Lyft routinely refuses service to passengers with disabilities

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Travel  |  Read Source →

The company settled with the DOJ over similar allegations in 2020.

The Justice Department has filed a lawsuit against Lyft, accusing the company and its drivers of "routinely" refusing service to passengers with disabilities. Its complaint focuses on individuals who use mobility devices like stowable wheelchairs, as well as those who have service animals trained to perform tasks related to their conditions. The actions of Lyft and its drivers violate Title III of the Americans with Disabilities Act of 1990 (ADA, which prohibits transportation companies from discriminating based on disability, the department said in its announcement.

In addition to refusing service, the agency accuses Lyft of imposing cancellation fees on passengers who've been refused service, as well as of refusing to modify its policies and practices to avoid discrimination against those passengers. One example is denying passengers with mobility issues the choice to sit in the front seat when needed.

This isn't the first time Lyft has had to face the same allegations from the Justice Department. In 2020, the company reached an out-of-court settlement with the agency to resolve allegations that it violated the ADA. Back then, the department launched an investigation into the ride-hailing service's practices after a wheelchair user in Los Angeles filed multiple complaints over being refused rides. Lyft agreed to pay damages and to revise its wheelchair policies, but the DOJ has clearly concluded that any change it has made since then isn't enough.

The lawsuit is seeking $53 million in damages for passengers with disabilities who have filed complaints to Lyft or the department itself. It's also asking the court to order Lyft to train its drivers on the ADA. The company denies the allegations in a statement it sent to the New York Times.  "Discrimination has no place in the Lyft community," it said. "We have longstanding policies requiring drivers to accommodate riders who travel with service animals or use foldable mobility devices, and drivers who violate these policies face serious consequences, including permanent deactivation."

Last year, the DOJ also sued Uber (again) over disability discrimination. Similar to its complaint against Lyft, the department said back then that Uber and its drivers "routinely refuse to serve individuals with disabilities." Both lawsuits were filed in the US District Court for the Northern District of California.
