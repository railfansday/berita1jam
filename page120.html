Nvidia has recently delivered particularly impressive growth given how large its revenue base is relative to AMD's, but the market isn't giving the chipmaker credit for it.

With 2026 nearly wrapped up, it's OK for investors to start looking ahead to 2027.

Next year, Wall Street analysts expect Nvidia's revenue to grow at a 67% pace. While some may discount that growth rate given what's going on in the C-suite, I don't think that's a wise investment move. The reality is that Nvidia's largest clients already know what they're going to spend on chips next year, and they've let Nvidia know so these products are ready to go when they need them. During the Q2 earnings call, management gave guidance for 70% revenue growth in 2027. It's rare to see a company issue guidance for the upcoming year so far in advance. The only reason why Nvidia would do that is if it had high visibility into revenue, and nothing that has happened over the past few weeks will likely change its outlook for 2027.

According to the analyst community, AMD's growth rate is expected to exceed Nvidia's. Analysts project AMD will grow its revenue at an 84% clip. That would be a strong outperformance if AMD can pull it off, but considering its much smaller sales base, it's not growing nearly fast enough to make a big dent in Nvidia's lead.

Regardless of what transpires next year, the reality is that AMD's expected growth has already been priced into its stock, while Nvidia's hasn't.

AMD is over three times more expensive than Nvidia based on next year's earnings projections. That's a huge price mismatch, and I think it's a bit absurd that AMD trades at that much of a premium when their growth rates are fairly similar. While I agree that AMD deserves a premium right now because its CEO is still in place, Nvidia has technological advantages and a massive market-share lead that cannot be discounted. Given how expensive AMD stock is, I forecast that its stock will be flat over the next year and will still trade at around just under $600 per share at the end of 2027.

Meanwhile, I wouldn't be surprised to see Nvidia's price-to-earnings ratio double to 20, which would also imply a stock price double that of today's level, about $330 per share.
