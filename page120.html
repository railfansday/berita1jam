Sophie Cunningham’s Post-Election Message Sparks WNBA Debate

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Sophie Cunningham knows how to stir the pot, and she did it again right after the U.S. midterm elections. The Indiana Fever guard posted a cryptic message on her Instagram story that left fans guessing. She did not talk about the election results directly. Instead, she shared a long excerpt from entrepreneur Andy Frisella about personal excellence being the foundation of a great country. This move stands out because other WNBA players often take very direct political stances. Cunningham chose a different path, focusing on individual behavior rather than policy.

Cunningham has faced heat before for her comments on transgender women in sports. During the season, she made waves about her views. "I want to protect young girls in a locker room, or young girls in sport who shouldn't have to go against biological men," Cunningham told ESPN. She later added, "I was asked a question, and that's simply what it was. I wasn't trying to make a statement." This history makes her recent post even more interesting. People are connecting the dots between her views on sports and her views on the country.

The full text she shared from Andy Frisella was quite long and specific. It read, "A culture of personal excellence is the foundation of a great country. There is no election, politician, policy, or law that can substitute for that … and the best thing about that is the same behaviors that make you a better American also make your own life better quickly. If we want a better America … It starts with better Americans. There is no other way. Personal excellence is the Ultimate Rebellion." This message shifts the focus from what the government does to what each person does.

U2 Weigh In on Ed Sheeran and Macklemore Tour Controversy

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The Edge and Larry Mullen Jr. defended the rapper's right to speak out, while the Edge said Sheeran "should not be vilified".

U2 have weighed in on Macklemore’s removal from Ed Sheeran’s U.S. tour, backing the rapper’s right to speak out while cautioning against blaming Sheeran.

“We totally support Macklemore’s right as an artist to say what he wanted to say,” the Edge said in an interview with BBC News. “I don’t think Ed should be vilified,” he added, acknowledging the difficult position Sheeran had been in. The guitarist also praised the artists who left Sheeran’s tour in solidarity, framing the dispute as a matter of free expression. Drummer Larry Mullen Jr. went further. “Artists can choose whether they want to be politically active or not. It’s a human right,” he said. “But we as a band will fight for the right to speak your mind as long as it doesn’t incite violence. And even if we disagree with it or don’t like it, nobody has a right – no billionaire, no promoter, no owner has the right to censor anybody. They will definitely not be censoring us ever.”

Macklemore was dropped from the North American leg of Sheeran’s Loop Tour in September after two opening sets at MetLife Stadium in New Jersey, where he said “Free Palestine,” spoke about Gaza and the West Bank, and performed his protest song “Hind’s Hall.” Promoter Messina Touring Group said venues on the remaining dates would not allow the shows to go ahead with him on the bill.

New England Patriots owner Robert Kraft confirmed he barred the rapper from Gillette Stadium, citing his comments and what Kraft called “a broader history of antisemitic rhetoric and imagery.”

Sheeran said the decision belonged to the promoter. Several acts left the tour in response, including Finneas, Lukas Graham and Irish folk band Beoga, which had been performing with Sheeran each night. Sheeran later opened a show in Philadelphia by apologizing for “mistakes” in his handling of the situation.

Macklemore has since played three sold-out Free Palestine benefit concerts in Dublin, Paris and London, with guests including Hozier, Kneecap and Beoga, raising money for organizations supporting Palestinians.

U2 release Carnaval De Luz, their 16th studio album and first full-length of new material in nine years, on Nov. 13. The album closes with “Torn,” a duet with the late Dolly Parton that the band says was her last recorded vocal performance.

Simple Plan Are Meeting a Whole New Generation of Fans, 25 Years In

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Drummer Chuck Comeau talks TikTok, Good Things Festival, and why a farewell tour is "not in the cards"

Simple Plan have been playing the same songs for 25 years. Lately, they’re playing them to a lot of people hearing them live for the first time.

“Every night we ask people, ‘Hey, how many people are here for the first time?’” drummer Chuck Comeau tells Billboard. “Sometimes it’s like 85% have never seen the band.”

The Montreal pop-punk band is drawing fans who weren’t born when “I’m Just a Kid” arrived in 2002, alongside the original listeners who are now bringing their own kids to shows. Some of the younger crowd have even started calling Simple Plan “classic rock.” Related Deep Purple's Ian Gillan Shares Encouraging News After Undergoing Surgery Richard Lloyd, Television Guitarist, Dies at 75 Benson Boone Brings Young Fan Battling Cancer Onstage at Brisbane Concert Explore Simple Plan See latest videos, charts and news

“And you’re like, ‘What are you talking about?’” Comeau says.

“There’s nothing cooler than what’s happening to our band right now,” he adds.

Much of that can be traced to “I’m Just a Kid” going viral on TikTok, where it soundtracked a challenge encouraging users to recreate their childhood photos. Comeau calls the trend a “gigantic gift,” but says meeting fans online has always come naturally to the band, which was filming backstage antics and sharing them through its website and DVDs long before TikTok existed.

“It’s the same thing,” he says. “It’s just a new platform.”

The renewed attention builds on a strong early run. The band’s 2004 album Still Not Getting Any… debuted at No. 3 on the Billboard 200, while “Perfect” and “Welcome to My Life” reached Nos. 24 and 40, respectively, on the Billboard Hot 100.

Next up is a return to Australia for Good Things Festival, where Simple Plan join a bill led by Limp Bizkit and featuring Spiritbox, the All-American Rejects, Bad Religion and MxPx. The festival hits Melbourne’s Flemington Racecourse on Dec. 4, Sydney Showground on Dec. 5 and Brisbane Showgrounds on Dec. 6, marking the band’s third trip Down Under in three years.

“We love Australia. What can I say?” Comeau says. “Any excuse, we come back.”

The lineup mixes pop-punk with nu-metal, hardcore and heavier rock, the kind of bill Comeau admits might once have made him nervous. “Being on this lineup would have been a bit more intimidating 25 years ago,” he says.

He credits streaming with breaking down the scene boundaries that once divided fans, making it easier for younger listeners to move between genres. The band has seen it firsthand at European festivals, sharing stages with acts like Slipknot.

“At first, you’re a little terrified,” he says. “You’re like, ‘How’s that gonna go?’ And then you actually realize that people are stoked.”

He has his own Good Things wish list. Bad Religion, whom he calls his favorite band, are near the top, along with Swedish punks Millencolin, who are playing Pennybridge Pioneers in full. He’s also keen to finally see Limp Bizkit live. “I’ve seen a lot of crazy videos right now that are popping up everywhere,” he says.

Meanwhile, Simple Plan have started writing their seventh studio album, with work set to continue into 2027. There’s no release date, but Comeau says the band has a batch of songs it’s excited about, and no intention of rushing.

“We’ve been around for 25 years and we only have six albums,” he says. “So we take our time.”

The record will follow 2022’s Harder Than It Looks, and Comeau says the aim is to refine the band’s sound rather than overhaul it. “We know who we are,” he says. “We’re not going to turn around and make a black metal record.”

The harder challenge is competing with the band’s own past. Songs that soundtracked fans’ teenage years carry a weight that new material has to earn.

“When you get to this stage of your career, you’re almost playing against yourself,” Comeau says. “You’re playing against your history.”

“You’re not the new shiny young band anymore,” he adds. “So you’ve got to work harder to have people pay attention.”

That’s why he won’t put a deadline on it. “There’s never a good time for a bad record, and there’s never a bad time for a good record,” he says.

Next year also marks 25 years since the band’s debut album, No Pads, No Helmets…Just Balls. Comeau says a special-edition vinyl and a few anniversary performances are on the table, but not a full album tour.

“I think we’re bigger than just one album,” he says. “But that album was really consequential for us. It’s the one that started it all.”

Another milestone arrives March 9, 2027, when the band releases its first children’s picture book, I’m Just a Kid: The Story of Simple Plan, illustrated by Jomike Tejido. Comeau says it grew out of watching the band’s original fans grow up and start families of their own.

“We were with them throughout their difficult moments,” he says, recalling how songs about breakups, school and friendships became part of listeners’ teenage years. “Fast-forward 20 years, 25 years, now they’re starting families, they’re having kids. We want to be there with them in this new chapter of their lives.”

The book explores friendship and perseverance and includes nods for longtime fans. “It’s as much for the parents as it is for the kids,” Comeau says.

For all the anniversaries and nostalgia, Simple Plan have never broken up, gone on hiatus or staged a reunion, which is rare among their peers. Comeau believes the years since the pandemic have been some of the band’s best, with bigger crowds and a sharper focus on the live show.

“When we were young, you were going out and you were partying,” he says. “Now it’s like, no, we don’t do that anymore. We’re all doing yoga and stretching.”

“We’ve always just kept going,” he says. “I feel like there’s a lot of work to be done.”

“There’s no talk about a farewell tour,” Comeau says. “Not in the cards for now.”

9 Near-Perfect Dragon Age Replacements For Fans of Fantasy RPGs

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Messy, morally gray RPGs wherever you look

BioWare’s Dragon Age is a fantasy RPG series about warring factions, world-ending threats, and, yes, the occasional dragon – but that’s the first-glance take. Get into one Dragon Age game (start with Origins!) and you’ll see that the true heart of this series is the people you drag into hell with you.

The choices you can make in Dragon Age are messy. Your companions get legitimately attached to you — or not; your dating life can complicate your mission; and innocuous decisions you make in hour one can affect the sweeping political options available to you in the series’ biggest moments. Whether you seek more of that exact mix of party banter, branching loyalty quests, and a world that remembers you, or you’re intrigued by the same bones in a different skin — sci-fi crew instead of fantasy party, perhaps — here are 9 games to add to your queue.

Why you’ll like this if you like Dragon Age: The cast of well-written characters and intricate combat system (along with Larian Studios’ whimsical charm)

Where to play: PS4, PS5, Windows PC, Xbox Series X

Although Divinity: Original Sin and its sequel are set 1,000 years apart, in both, you’re protecting your fantasy world, Rivellon, from a magical energy (Source) and monsters emanating from the series’ overarching Void. But, luckily, you’re not tackling that alone. Each game gives you the opportunity to gather a party of intrepid explorers to save the world, but, more importantly, bicker constantly as you do.

This dynamic within Divinity: Original Sin and especially Divinity: Original Sin 2 makes the characters the true reason for replay after replay. Sure, you’re solving a murder mystery in Divinity: Original Sin and escaping imprisonment, then surviving the wilds in Divinity: Original Sin 2 — but those are details. Wait until you meet Sebille. You’ll either be best friends or unable to stand each other immediately.

Why you’ll like this if you like Dragon Age: It’s a 2002 game that helped form today’s RPG genre of real-feeling companions and branching questlines

Where to play: Windows PC, PS4, PS5, Switch, Windows PC, Xbox Series X

Before Dragon Age, there was Neverwinter Nights — a fellow BioWare title that paved the way straight to Dragon Age. At the start of the game, a fatal plague called the Wailing Death (chill; normal) is striking the city of Neverwinter. You’re in quarantine, but you still need to graduate from the Neverwinter Academy and figure out how to help Lady Aribeth create a cure for the plague. People are panicking. There are riots to navigate. Everything’s absolute chaos behind the city’s closed walls.

Since Neverwinter Nights is nearly a 25-year-old-game, some parts of it are harder to love than others, but it’s worth a play for Dragon Age players seeking a nostalgic title.

Why you’ll like this if you like Dragon Age: It’s got similar-feeling companion depth and combat primed for a party tactician

Where to play: Windows PC, PS5, Xbox Series X, Switch

In Warhammer 40,000: Rogue Trader by Owlcat Games, you’re exploring uncharted space…specifically, the Koronus Expanse. But while the setting differs from mostly land-based Dragon Age, the problems your characters face are similar. Here, you’re navigating a succession crisis and working to claim your place within the Von Valancius family. Much like Dragon Age’s Blight and Darkspawn, Rogue Trader has its share of existential corruption for you to weather (here, it’s Chaos and Warp mutations) while you also have to deal with the possibility of much more immediate betrayal.

Dragon Age players will recognize Rogue Trader’s starting class and character background system – will you be a Warrior who was a military officer, or a priest-turned-Operative? – but the turn-based combat will be a change of pace.

Why you’ll like this if you like Dragon Age: Everywhere you turn, there are fiendishly difficult narrative choices. Buckle up

Where to play: PS4, PS5, Switch, Windows PC, Xbox Series X

Welcome to Baldur’s Gate 2 — surprise, you’re trapped in an underground laboratory. You’re Gorion’s Ward, a Bhaalspawn (e.g., kid of the murder god Bhaal). There’s a devilish wizard, Jon Irenicus, who’s torturing you – ahem, conducting tests to study your essence. You need to escape, rescue your also-trapped childhood friend Imoen, and then rest and recover…as long as nothing happens to interfere with that plan, of course.

In Baldur’s Gate 2, as in the Dragon Age series, you’re learning about the world around you and figuring out who you can trust or recruit to help you accomplish a goal that’s both very personal and potentially world-ending. But here, there are vampires.

Baldur’s Gate 2 is another BioWare title from before the company moved away from D&D, so the two games also share developer DNA. (As a bonus, if you’re a Dragon Age person and you’ve not played Baldur’s Gate 3…please do.)

Why you’ll like this if you like Dragon Age: It’s got the same BioWare choice-and-companion architecture. Just swap your sword for an assault rifle

Where to play: PS4, PS5, Windows PC, Xbox Series X

Mass Effect and Dragon Age are both BioWare RPG franchises with relationship building, storytelling, and party- or squad-based tactics. In each, your choices shape the narrative. In each, you can use save data from one game to influence the next. In each, you have a base — a camp or a spaceship — where you can regroup with your companions.

But that’s where the similarities end. In Mass Effect, you start as newcomer Commander Shepard aboard an advanced starship. You’re working to investigate a beacon on a human colony world…until things may or may not very suddenly take a turn for the worse (brace for attack!). But at least you’re fighting alongside friends. Getting to hang with the sullen, sarcastic Garrus Vakarian might be one of the greatest joys in the series.

Why you’ll like this if you like Dragon Age: It’s got party-based tactics and large-scale kingdom management.

Where to play: PS4, PS5, Windows PC, Xbox Series X

If the political games you could play within Dragon Age: Origins were the draw for you, Pathfinder: Kingmaker has that in spades. With even more management systems layered on top. Here, you start the game as a group of adventurers gathered by one Lady Jamandi Alordi. You’re going to the Stolen Lands to bring back the head of the Stag Lord. If you do, you get to found your own barony. If not…well, let’s not contemplate that alternative, shall we?

While this game isn’t just routine kingdom management – there are mysterious fey threats and an ancient curse getting in the way of your day-to-day – steering Pathfinder: Kingmaker’s plot via being a decent lord to your barony is a big focus in this game. But, if you’re looking for explicitly similar structure to feel anchored here, Dragon Age: Origins players rejoice: the combat is real-time-with-pause, which should feel just like home.

Why you’ll like this if you like Dragon Age: It’s got grudge-holding factions, possibly-loyal companions, within a Colonial-era setting replete with political intrigue.

Where to play: Windows PC, PS4, PS5, Windows PC, Xbox Series X

Less polish, smaller budget, rougher edges, but a lot of the same charm – GreedFall feels like Dragon Age in that, here, you’ve got real-time combat, a world where your choices matter, and the ability to deeply bond (or not) with your companions. In GreedFall, though, instead of battling the Blight, you’re working to save the old continent from a plague, the Malichor. Your character is a Legate tasked with traveling to an island untouched by the plague to discover the cure, but you’re not the only one with that idea. As multiple colonial factions – including the religious nation Thélème and the fond-of-science Bridge Alliance – fight over the island, your job is simple: Protect the island, search for the plague’s cure, and fend off political disaster. That does sound simple, right?

Why you’ll like this if you like Dragon Age: It’s a crunchier, more tactical game: Think Dragon Age run through a true Dungeons & Dragons ruleset.

Where to play: PS5, Windows PC, Xbox Series X

As Solasta: Crown of the Magister begins, your party of four is called upon to investigate the Badlands – a land infested with monsters, but you’re prepared for that, right? You do find the titular Crown of the Magister along the way, an artifact that might just be able to help you save your world.

Dragon Age players who loved the series’ focus on deep character writing may find Solasta a bit lacking. Solasta’s focus is not on story. Instead, you’re managing turn-based combat, organizing inventories, and figuring out how to use your class abilities to their greatest effect.

To the “less of a story” point, though, Solasta’s sequel is in development. It’s theorized that the team’s leading harder into story this time around, so stay tuned.

Why you’ll like this if you like Dragon Age: While this game doesn’t have traditional team management – you’ve got pawns, not true parties – it does have the same dark fantasy tone and ingrained class build system.

Where to play: PS4, PS5, Windows PC, Xbox Series X

A gigantic dragon has just attacked the fishing village of Cassardis. When you step up to defend, the dragon rips out your heart, eats it, and leaves you for dead. Oh no! Game over. Except it’s not: You wake up as an Arisen. You’re now bound to the dragon’s fate. You need to find it and get your heart back. Have fun!

Dragon’s Dogma is very different mechanically from Dragon Age, but it sets a similar scene and feels in-genre anyway. You won’t find deep companion writing here, but what you will find are dragons. Did you play Dragon Age for dragons? Extremely understandable. You’ll love Dragon’s Dogma. We cannot overstate how many dragons there are.

With Dragon Age, BioWare helped build a genre of games where stories and companions come to life on a grand scale – but there’s still the quintessential battle RPG focus on skill progression and party tactics. Interested in saving a fantasy continent from the plague? More interested in building your own barony? Regardless, there’s something here to play. And replay, with different choices. And replay, as an evil overlord. And then replay again, just for fun.

Netflix's Upcoming Sci-Fi Spinoff Takes Exciting Step Forward With New Update

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Netflix is preparing for the arrival of one of its highly anticipated spinoffs.

Netflix's upcoming Manifest spinoff series, Arrivals: A New Manifest Mystery, has reached another important milestone as development continues on the science fiction drama. After the original show concluded its run three years ago, the franchise is preparing to introduce an entirely new story within its universe. However, while the plot might be new, the spinoff will maintain its connections to the lore that made its predecessor successful.

Created by Jeff Rake, Manifest originally premiered on NBC in 2018 and followed the passengers of Montego Air Flight 828. Shortly after landing, the characters realized that more than five years had passed despite nothing seeming wrong with their flight. They begin experiencing mysterious events known as Callings, leading them to uncover disturbing secrets about their situation.

Although NBC canceled the series after three seasons, Netflix brought it back for a fourth and final season, which ended in June 2023. The show's popularity eventually led to another installment in the franchise, Arrivals, which Netflix officially announced in August 2026. Now, Rake has revealed that Arrivals: A New Manifest Mystery has secured a director for its first episode. The creator announced the news on his Instagram as part of his Showrunner Diaries series, which provides viewers with behind-the-scenes information.

While Rake confirmed that someone has been selected to direct the debut episode, the director's identity has not been disclosed at the time of this article's publication. The update also revealed that everything is progressing very smoothly across several departments. The creator explained that they are beginning to form Arrivals' production team, with several positions already filled.

Arrivals: A New Manifest Mystery was first announced by Netflix on August 28, 2026, with the date paying homage to Flight 828. The streaming platform confirmed that the upcoming project would feature new characters and storylines while staying true to the Manifest universe.

The spinoff series won't be a direct continuation of the original show's finale. Arrivals: A New Manifest Mystery will explore an entirely different incident within the franchise's world. The story reportedly begins when an unidentified aircraft makes an unexpected crash landing in Denver. The people aboard are unable to remember who they are or why they were on the plane in the first place.

There is no confirmed release date for Netflix's Arrivals: A New Manifest Mystery at the time of this article's publication. However, industry speculation predicts that the series will be released on the streaming platform in 2028.

A supernatural drama series created for NBC, Manifest is an investigation of a fictional flight, Montego Air Flight 828, that went missing for five years after a moment of turbulence in the sky. However, the passengers and crew only felt a moment pass despite the rest of the world assuming they were all gone. The "survivors" now try to reacclimate to society as the world has moved on without them - but voices from beyond seem to beckon them somewhere unknown.

10 Deadliest Weapons in Transformers History

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →
