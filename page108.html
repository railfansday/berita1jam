‘Rivals’ No More: Research Reveals Rationale Behind Disney-Channel 4 Deal

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

EXCLUSIVE: New research has spotlighted how the Disney–Channel 4 deal could be a match made in heaven.

Last month, the Mouse House and Gogglebox network struck a landmark pact that will see multiple Channel 4 shows head to Disney+ day-and-date including Bella Ramsey-starrer Maya, hundreds of hours made available on-demand such as Russell T. Davies’ Tip Toe, and the Channel 4 linear network drop on Disney+ in its entirety.

Disney+’s UK catalog is comprised of just shy of 10% UK originals, originals that account for an 8.7% share of viewing in the nation, Digital-i found. This contrasts with Netflix and Prime Video, which both over-index. Netflix has a 12.7% share of UK-produced content in its catalog accounting for 17.9% UK viewing share, while the same figures for Prime Video are 13.4% and 18.6% respectively.

Disney+ UK shows include the likes of Rivals, Alice and Steve and Oasis doc Don’t Look Back in Anger, while upcoming are Mosquito starring Nicholas Hoult and Daisy Edgar-Jones, a just-revealed adaptation of Bob Mortimer’s The Satsuma Complex and a Blind Date reboot.

Gaining access to a Channel 4 back catalog that includes the likes of Friday Night Dinner, Taskmaster, Derry Girls and Peep Show, all of which have previously shone on Netflix, is a smart move, Digitial-i suggests. According to the firm, these four shows alone accounted for 5.3% of all viewing of UK-produced content on Netflix UK during the first half of 2026. The data therefore cites how Channel 4’s existing streaming audience is particularly valuable to platforms seeking to build viewing frequency and engagement.

When the Channel 4-Disney deal was struck, both Channel 4 CEO Priya Dogra and Disney+ EMEA chief Karl Holmes stressed that their audiences are complementary.

At the time, Holmes said Disney+ viewing skews younger than public service broadcasters like Channel 4, adding that around 40% of Disney+ viewing over the past year had come from 16-34 year olds, ahead of Netflix (32%), Prime Video (27%) and Paramount+ (23%). According to ratings agency Barb, UK viewing hours to all Disney+ content rose 4% for the year to September 2026, while Prime Video was down 7% and Netflix 13%.

The Channel 4 deal is one of several of its ilk Disney has struck in Europe recently and the data gives some insight into this strategy. “We’re not competing with free-to-air partners; we’re helping them reach a broader audience,” Holmes told us a few weeks back.

Disney+ has committed big to international originals of late, with a plan to triple volume over the next three years. This strategy was reiterated in Disney’s earnings earlier this week.

Digital i chief analytics officer Matt Ross said: “Channel 4 brings Disney+ far more than a library of recognisable British hits. Our data shows its programming attracts an audience that already demonstrates significantly higher engagement, streaming more frequently and spending more overall time on streaming platforms.”

Dozens missing after boat carrying 100 migrants sinks off Senegal's coast

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The damaged boat washed ashore on a beach

Senegalese authorities are continuing search operations after a boat reportedly carrying about 100 migrants capsized off the coast.

Initial reports said that one person had died, but some local media have reported that at least four bodies have been recovered. Dozens of people are still missing.

Personal belongings were found on the badly damaged boat, which washed ashore near the village of Yenne, south of Dakar. Search operations continued throughout Wednesday and were due to resume on Thursday.

This comes less than two weeks after a shipwreck off the coast of the north-western town of Thieppe killed at least 39 people, in one of Senegal's deadliest migration disasters.

Oumar Sow, a representative of the village chief, said the latest tragedy happened around 02:00 local time (02:00 GMT).

"Cries could be heard coming from the pirogue that were audible even from the beach," the AFP news agency quoted him as saying.

A survivor questioned by the police said there were about 100 people on the boat, including five women, AFP reported.

Police recovered identity documents, several mobile phones and personal belongings from the wreckage, including a school enrolment document.

At a cabinet meeting on Wednesday, President Diomaye Faye expressed concern about a renewed increase in irregular departure by migrants hoping to reach Europe.

He instructed the interior ministry to prepare a report examining the factors driving the dangerous journeys and proposing measures to tackle the problem.

Following last month's tragedy, Faye had urged Senegalese people not to risk their lives attempting the dangerous sea crossings.

Many thousands of people from West Africa have died or gone missing trying to flee conflict, authoritarian rule or poverty in recent years.

The UN's migration agency IOM said last month that it had recorded at least 454 deaths and disappearances off the West African coast this year alone.

It warned the figures were likely to be an underestimate, as many boats disappear without leaving survivors or a trace.

As it has become more difficult to reach Europe through North Africa, due to tighter controls and increased co-operation with European countries, migrants are increasingly targeting Spain's Canary Islands.

They often travel in wooden boats known as pirogues that are frequently overloaded and not suited to such long voyages across the Atlantic Ocean.

Most migrants arriving in the Canary Islands this year are from Senegal, according to the UN.

They are driven to leave home by limited employment opportunities, family pressures and established migration networks, according to the IOM.

On 7 November, El Hierro, one of the Canary Islands, declared a state of emergency following a surge in migrant arrivals.

It said more than 3,300 people had reached the island in about 30 boats since the beginning of the month. This amounts to almost a third of the island's population.

CleanSpark’s Harry Sudock expects lower-cost miners to dominate Bitcoin mining as margins shrink.

Bitcoin miners have sold 108,247 BTC worth $8.08 billion in 2026 amid growing profit pressures.

AI data centers could generate four times higher returns per megawatt than Bitcoin mining.

CleanSpark Bitcoin miner CBO Harry Sudock expects Bitcoin mining to become less profitable over the next decade as rising operating costs and miners face lower profit margins. Hints of it are already emerging, as major Bitcoin miners are selling BTC like never before, over 108,247 BTC ($8.08B) in 2026 alone.At the same time, more miners are moving power and data centers toward AI, where returns can be much higher.

Speaking in an interview, Harry Sudock said Bitcoin mining will follow the pattern of other commodity production businesses, where profitability becomes harder to maintain over time.

“Like with any commodity production business, it should get harder and thinner margin over time, with the lowest-cost miners likely to survive.”

He also expects global hash rate to grow more slowly over the next 10 years than it did in the previous decade. He believes mining could spread into regions such as Central America and Africa, where operators can access cheaper electricity.

The move toward AI is also changing how miners use their money and power. Sudock said large mining sites may now earn more by running AI data centres than by mining Bitcoin alone.

For years, listed Bitcoin miners held much of the BTC they mined. That trend has changed sharply in 2026.

Lookonchain data shows 14 major listed miners, including MARA, Riot, CleanSpark, Hut 8, American Bitcoin, and Cango, have sold a total of 108,247 BTC worth around $8.08 billion in 2026.

MARA led the sales with 40,581 BTC, followed by Riot with 20,264 BTC and Bitdeer with more than 10,553 BTC. Several other miners have also reduced their Bitcoin holdings.

The biggest reason behind the sell-off is squeezing mining profits. As of November 19, the average global cost to mine one Bitcoin was about $76,669, while Bitcoin traded near $63,200. That puts average production costs roughly 23% above the market price.

Even mining profitability, measured by hash price, has also fallen to around $30 per PH/s per day.

And that pressure is pushing 70% of bitcoin miners toward AI data centers. Industry estimates show that 1 MW of power can generate around $7.9 million over five years from AI/HPC data centres, compared with just $1.9 million from Bitcoin mining.Core Scientific plans to end Bitcoin mining by Q4 2026 and focus on AI data centres, backed by 1,120 MW of power capacity from CoreWeave (590 MW) and AMD (530 MW). Riot Platforms signed a 15-year, $28 billion lease for 910 MW with Anthropic, while Hut 8 secured a 15-year, $7 billion AI deal.

IREN, TeraWulf, and Cipher Digital are also shifting more capacity to AI, which could bring 70% of IREN’s and TeraWulf’s revenue by year-end.Therefore, CleanSpark’s Harry Sudock expects smaller miners with cheaper power to take a bigger share of Bitcoin mining as large public companies move toward AI.

Musk accuses Zelenskyy of lying about Starshield speed limits in Ukraine

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Zelenskyy says limits on the military satellite network still hamper Ukraine’s air defence. Musk says the US government controls Starshield.

Elon Musk at VivaTech in Paris, 16 June 2023

Elon Musk has accused Ukraine’s President Volodymyr Zelenskyy of lying about speed limits on Starshield, SpaceX’s military satellite network. Musk made the comments at The New York Times’ DealBook Summit on Wednesday, CNBC reported.

“I don’t want to blow your mind, but Zelenskyy is not always truthful,” Musk said when asked about the claims.

Earlier on Wednesday, Zelenskyy had said speed restrictions on Starshield were still in place. He said they make it harder to shoot down Russian missiles and drones, according to CNBC.

SpaceX runs two satellite systems. Starlink is its commercial internet service. Starshield is a version built for governments and militaries. Ukraine gets Starshield access through a deal with the US government. It uses it to fly spy drones and to stop Russian attack drones.

At the summit, Musk said Starshield is controlled by the US government. He said Starlink has a speed limit because it is not meant for attacks, CNBC reported. Musk said Zelenskyy really wants to use Starlink to attack.

The dispute dates back to October. Ukraine said Starshield cut out on fast-moving drones, which hurt its air defence. Musk said then on X that the US military and the president set any limits.

The Wall Street Journal later reported that Musk had ordered the slowdown, citing US officials. The Trump administration has denied ordering it.

Zelenskyy has said the service got better after October. But he has asked whether Musk could have limited Starshield on his own. TNW has not independently verified either side’s account.

They have also left locals with ample choice of United hubs and leisure destinations, making Cleveland an ever more important part of the airline’s network.

But there is a sense of irony in the move. United largely turned its back on the airport, unceremoniously winding down operations 12 years ago. This cut what was a major hub in itself from United’s network. So with the carrier now appearing to bet on the site once again in what looks to be a full-circle moment, the question is: What has changed?

United’s move back to Cleveland is particularly noteworthy because of its historical relationship with the airport. After a merger with Continental Airlines in 2010, Cleveland became one of nine hubs for the expanded carrier, which retained United’s name. Unfortunately for the airport, this would turn out to be just one of a host of factors that would see it fall out of favor with the new, combined entity.

Not only was Cleveland the smallest of the nine hubs, but it was also relatively close to United’s existing gateways at Chicago O'Hare International Airport (ORD) and Washington Dulles International Airport (IAD), as well as another former Continental base: Newark Liberty International Airport (EWR). While these fit nicely into the larger network and remain in use as hubs by United to this day, Cleveland failed to fill a significant role in the wider operation. Not least was Chicago—a huge airport in comparison, serving a far greater market—being only 315 miles (275 km) down the road.

Behind all of this was the fallout from the 2008 financial crisis, which had hammered profitability across the industry. After United and Continental joined forces to cut costs, the unviable, loss-making Cleveland base became an easy target in the now-unified United and Continental brand’s drive towards efficiency. This came in 2014, with its de-hubbing (which was actually the second time in its history that United moved away).

The de-hubbing did not mean United left Cleveland altogether, though. Rather, the carrier drastically scaled back on departures and rerouted connecting services elsewhere. Under plans laid out at the time, United said average daily departures would be cut by around 60%. Some 72 peak-day flights serving 20 destinations nonstop were to be retained, it added, down from 200 and 61 beforehand, respectively.

These fell further with time, too. By 2025, just 30 or so United flights were departing Cleveland daily. That is, until the carrier opted to expand its offerings from the base once again this year. Las Vegas’ Harry Reid International Airport (LAS), which United last flew to from Cleveland in 2024, would be served year-round with five flights a week starting in September, an announcement said in May. A winter service, running between December and April next year, would then see Miami International Airport (MIA) reappear on United’s list of destinations from the airport for the first time since 2014.

* Myrtle Beach: 11 flights from June 4, 2027.

** San Juan: 26 flights from December 17, 2026, to January 11, 2027.

*** Sarasota: 4 flights from March 13 to April 3, 2027.

Owing to the additions, United’s winter schedule for this year going into next is set to cover 14 nonstop routes from Cleveland. This will be the most among any of the 10 passenger airlines that regularly operate out of the airport during the period. It will also leave United flying out of Cleveland almost 40 times a day in the meantime, taking capacity of the airport to its highest in over a decade. No doubt, this is in response to strong local demand.

The carrier is betting big on Cleveland's travel market with the announcement of two new nonstop routes to Miami and Las Vegas.

As Scott C. Carr, a City of Cleveland Department of Port Control assistant director, commented on the news of United’s route additions in May.

“Strong demand for leisure travel continues to create opportunities to grow service and connect Northeast Ohio travelers to destinations they want most.”

This is by no means new, however, with the airport having enjoyed progressively stronger footfall for some time now.

In fact, the airport’s own data shows a clear trend of improving traffic in almost every year since United’s large-scale departure in 2014, aside from an obvious slump during the pandemic. Though passenger numbers naturally dipped as United slashed services, it only took a few years for other airlines to move in, pick up the slack, and push the figures higher once more.

Among the flurry of airlines to begin or expand operations in response were low-cost carriers Frontier Airlines and Spirit Airlines, as well as close United rivals Delta Air Lines and American Airlines, to name a few. Simply put, demand was always there, but capturing it without losing money required a different approach than the one United had committed to thus far.

Prior to 2014, Continental — and later United following their merger — primarily used Cleveland as any other airline would: to funnel passengers originating elsewhere onto flights headed elsewhere. But such an operation is complex and can come with caveats.

A major consideration is whether what are known as banked connections can be sustained. Here, airlines effectively sort their arrivals and departures into waves so that passengers have time to disembark one flight and board another. The downside is that all aircraft involved have to remain grounded for a period to give travelers time to get through the airport, which can be costly for a carrier. Alongside this, airport infrastructure and ground crews must be in place to support sudden spikes in activity as planes come and go in waves.

Clearly, the practice was ultimately deemed unsustainable at Cleveland by United post-consolidation with Continental. Instead, a scaled-back, arguably simpler operation targeting those originating in or traveling to the area has taken its place. The bonus for United is that the fewer moving parts in a point-to-point system, compared to a hub-and-spoke model, make reacting to demand simpler — especially when the personnel and infrastructure to accommodatechanges are already in place.

Newark is the main United hub, while it has other major hubs in other airports across the country.

Indeed, United drastically cut back on services from Cleveland, but it did not fully scrap them. In fact, the airline remained its largest by passenger traffic, which gives some context into the weight of the de-hubbing decision on the airport. Either way, that meant United retained everything it needed to fly from the site — including a pilot base, cabin crew domicile, and maintenance facility.

But despite having the means in place to easily expand its schedule from Cleveland, United has found itself in a far less dominant position there today. Yes, it is still the airport’s primary carrier, but that is by no means as clear-cut as before the de-hubbing.

United accounted for a 28.84% share of Cleveland’s passengers over the year to August 2013, according to the US Bureau of Transportation Statistics. ExpressJet was the airport’s next-largest operator at the time, with a 14.60% share. Come the 12 months to August this year, United’s share was a more moderate 23.77%, while the gap to the second-largest - now Frontier with 21.47% - had narrowed drastically.

In other words, competition has heated up, and a string of nonstop routes passengers can choose from in Cleveland are serviced by more than one carrier — hence the risk United has had to accept in moving back in. But another factor may have contributed to the decision.

Spirit took the axe to its network prior to its collapse this year. This involved closing its base in Cleveland, where it had historically held a near 10% market share and from which it had even flown to the likes of Miami and Las Vegas. How much that has influenced United’s decision-making is debatable. Regardless, it has no doubt left a gap to be filled.

5 Funniest ‘Far Side’ Comics About the Meaning of Life

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

If I were forced to find the meaning of life in a daily newspaper comic strip, the first ongoing series I’d probably consult would be Calvin and Hobbes. Despite the fact that Calvin was just a six-year-old, Bill Watterson infused the boy with a deep thoughtfulness and insight that sometimes wrestled with the biggest questions about life, including its very meaning.

My second choice would probably be Peanuts, as Charles Schulz captured that slice-of-life realism better than anyone else. His characters, while not quite as philosophical as Calvin, would sometimes stumble onto something brilliant in the same way that Charlie Brown’s puny tree could somehow perfectly embody the spirit of the Christmas holiday.

I most certainly would not consult The Far Side.

Gary Larson’s single-panel comic strip was no less brilliant than those aforementioned comics, and Larson was no less a genius than​​​​​​​ Watterson or Schulz, but pondering life’s big questions is just not what the guy did. Gary Larson was a gag man, a joke maker, a humorist, not a philosopher. His humor was incredibly dark and maybe even nihilistic at times. The Far Side was smart, often subtle, but not deep and certainly not the kind of outlet where you’ll discern the meaning of existence. It’d be like reading Garfield in the hopes of finding healthy eating habits.

Still, having already located the deeper, meaning-of-life-type comics done by Watterson and Schulz, I thought it'd be an interesting exercise to attempt the same feat with The Far Side. The results only confirmed what I already thought — and loved — about Gary Larson’s humor.

To find Larson’s views on the meaning of life, I thought it would make sense to check out the comics where he depicts God, as there are a number of them. This one pretty much seems to sum up the seriousness Larson has towards the Almighty. His “Acts of God” depicts God putting on performances like a third-rate vaudeville star, doing things like juggling and getting shot out of a cannon. If you compare this with Linus’ sermons from A Charlie Brown Christmas, you’ve got two very different comic strip creators there.

In this comic strip, Larson depicts God at his computer, watching a man walk under a piano, as he’s about to tap the “smite” button on his keyboard. While the previous entry depicted God as a desperate entertainer, this one somehow seems worse as it portrays God as a disconnected, indifferent deity who deals out death with frightening casualness.

And this comic strip takes it to yet another level, going from indifference to outright cruelty, with God adding jerks to the world “just to make it interesting.” While I don’t think the meaning of life can be discovered in this strip, it does make you wonder why are there so many jerks in the world? And if God does exist, why did he create so many jerks?

This one, I think, gets more at what Larson actually thinks about life more than his depictions of God do. It shows two ants sitting on a mushroom, looking up at a starry night sky. One of the ants says to the other, “Just look at those stars tonight. … Makes you feel sort of small and insignificant."

The way I read this joke has a couple of different levels to it. First, I think the surface-level joke is, “haha, they’re ants, their lives are meaningless and they are small.” However, I also wonder if the ants are supposed to represent us. In other words, we’re​​​​​​​ the insignificant little ants staring up at an indifferent universe above.

Maybe there’s more depth to Larson than I gave him credit for.

The way most Far Side comics worked was to have the image work as the setup to the joke and the punchline was the caption below. Because of that, this comic appears to have no punchline at all. It’s just two scientists staring at a complex equation on a chalkboard, with one scientist saying,​​​​​​​ “No doubt about it, Ellington — we’ve mathematically expressed the purpose of the universe. God, how I love the thrill of scientific discovery!”

That isn’t funny and it isn’t the punchline.

This is one of those Far Side comics where you have to go looking for the joke, and I’m convinced the joke is that this complex equation ends in “=0.” Zero, of course, means nothing​​​​​​​, which I’m pretty sure is Larson’s way of saying that there is no purpose​​​​​​​ to the universe and that existence is meaningless — which certainly is a possibility.

Along with making the pavilion a building used by the public again, the studio aims to return the building’s core to its Edwardian look by removing later additions. It will also add glazed facades to the wings on either side of the central pavilion.

“The beachfront facade will see a new glazing system and colonnade to each of the building’s wings, with a new sheltered archway forming a new seating terrace at the building’s heart,” it continued.

The Sky Today on Thursday, November 19: Mercury moves into Libra

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Looking for a sky event this week? Check out our full Sky This Week column.

November 18: The Leonid meteor shower peaks

The solar system is a dynamic place. Let’s return to the predawn sky today to check out the inner planets’ progress relative to the stars of Virgo. An hour before sunrise, both Mercury and Venus are again well above the eastern horizon, with the stars around them still visible before twilight brightens too much.

Compared with earlier in the week, Venus now sits to the upper left of Spica, rather than directly above it. Venus will pass 1.8° due north of Spica later today at 6 P.M. EST. Now moving steadily east, it will track along the ecliptic, slowly headed for Libra. Mercury has already reached the Balance, sitting within its domain and to the far lower left of Kappa (κ) Virginis, as opposed to the pair’s close proximity a few days ago.

Pull out your telescope to see how the planets’ appearances have changed, too. Mercury is now 55% lit and 7” across, while Venus is 18% lit and has shrunk more noticeably, now 47” across.

Cardinale admits Milan’s NBA Europe project could be worth €5B+: “Hottest market in Europe”

📅 Nov 19, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Gerry Cardinale spoke at a conference last night and it’s clear that the AC Milan owner has a lot to say on the NBA Europe matter.

The NBA Europe project has been discussed for some time now, but there has been little movement. As reports have suggested, Milan were one of the first brands/clubs to throw their hat into the ring and that interest has never died.

Now, it seems that some progress could be made with the plans to develop the San Donato land into an arena. However, it still feels like we are a way away from seeing an NBA team in Milano.

Speaking at the SBJ Dealmakers conference, Cardinale sat alongside the NBA Deputy Commissioner and discussed the ongoing discussions about a new European league, as MilanNews relays from Gazzetta dello Sport.

“We will continue on our path with or without an agreement because we know that in the end, we will be the ones left standing. I honestly cannot understand how we can operate for 26 years and not be able to make a profit.

“Adam Silver and Mark Tatum [who sat alongside him] are doing everything they can to reach an agreement, but it takes two people to compromise. I am convinced that in the end, the NBA will prevail.”

The American also discussed the position that Milan are in and the future of the basketball arm of the club.

“Milan is currently the hottest market in Europe. When I arrived four years ago, I had two goals, besides winning: one was the stadium, which will finally be built, and the other was to invest in basketball.

“In fact, I had conversations with Olimpia Milano well before the advent of NBA Europe. Where will NBA Europe be in 10 years? Let’s say that if the new NBA franchise in Las Vegas is valued at $13-14 billion, I think that in 10 years, NBA Europe clubs will be worth at least half that.”
