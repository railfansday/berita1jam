American Airlines is updating how stranded passengers get hotel rooms, meal credits and rides during flight disruptions. An internal November memo I reviewed lays out automatic offers for eligible overnight delays and a new way for agents to arrange Uber rides without making passengers download the app. It also warns that employees may see fewer hotels to choose from. Agents can issue food and beverage credits for individual reservations affected by qualifying delays, cancellations and missed connections. The system uses flight information to evaluate eligibility and tells agents what qualifies. October 27: automatic offers for eligible overnight delays. The system sends hotel offers and applicable food and beverage credits when its eligibility and automation criteria are met, reducing the need for an employee to initiate the offer. Planned Uber Central rollout. At participating airports, authorized agents would arrange a ride on the customer’s behalf through the same system. The passenger receives a text message or WhatsApp link to start the ride, without downloading Uber. This should now be available but I haven’t seen passenger reports of the broadly available rollout yet (though customers reported it in limited release previously). Hotel inventory changes, date undetermined. American is changing the hotel inventory feed and selection process. Employees are told they may see fewer hotel choices. Stockport County vs Tranmere Rovers - prediction, team news, lineups

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

By Matthew Cooper | 08 Nov 2026 16:58, Last updated: 08 Nov 2026 17:00

Stockport County will be looking to continue their good form when they welcome Tranmere Rovers to Edgeley Park on Tuesday in the EFL Trophy.

The hosts have already secured their place in the knockout stages, while the visitors could join them with a win.

Stockport currently sit second in League One with 26 points from their opening 14 matches and are among the favourites to earn promotion to the Championship this season.

Jimmy McNulty's side beat Wimbledon 2-1 in their most recent league game, with Jack Diamond and Ethan Ennis scoring in the second half as Stockport came from behind to earn an important win.

The Hatters have also enjoyed an excellent EFL Trophy campaign, recording a 6-3 victory over Everton Under-21s before beating Shrewsbury Town 2-0 to secure their spot in the knockouts.

It is also worth noting that Stockport have only lost to Tranmere twice since 2010, including beating them 3-1 in their most recent meeting in the FA Cup last season.

Tranmere, meanwhile, are 11th in League Two with 19 points from 14 matches and they travel to Greater Manchester following a disappointing FA Cup exit on Saturday.

Darrell Clarke's side were beaten 3-0 by Barnet, with Kane Smith, Nnamdi Oforbh and Damola Ajayi all scoring in the final stages of the game.

However, the Rovers are unbeaten in their last three league games, having picked up a 3-1 win over Cheltenham Town in their most recent fixture.

In the EFL Trophy, Tranmere have only played one game so far, which saw them beat Shrewsbury Town 1-0 thanks to a Dylan Jones strike.

Stockport County form (all competitions):

Luca Ashby-Hammond has been Stockport's EFL Trophy goalkeeper, but he was sent off against Cray Wanderers on Saturday and Oliver Whatmuff is expected to start instead.

Mickael Mandron is likely to start up front, with top scorer Kyle Wootton rested, while Oliver Norwood and Lewis Fiorini are set to continue in midfield.

Tranmere will be hoping top scorer Dylan Jones can continue his good form after he bagged a brace against Cheltenham.

Will Vaulks and Tom Conlon are expected to continue their midfield partnership, while Tom Ince is set to start in the No.10 role.

Stockport County possible starting lineup:

Whatmuff; Dacres-Cogley, Wilson-Brown, Jobe; Ennis, Norwood, Fiorini, Adigun; Glover, Mandron, Wood

Tranmere Rovers possible starting lineup:

McGee; Senior, Faulkner, Nevett, Slater; Conlon, Vaulks; Jones, Ince, Whitaker; Ironside

Stockport are in better form than Tranmere and should have enough quality to pick up the win even if they decide to rotate.

‘Godzilla Minus Zero’ Powers $31M in Historic Box Office Launch Against Subdued ‘Cat in the Hat’

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The sequel to 2023's 'Godzilla Minus One' made noise as 'Ramayana' and 'Wild Horse Nine' also debuted.

Godzilla Minus Zero is king of the box office in its opening weekend as the sequel feature tallies the biggest launch in its distributor’s history with nearly three times the debut of predecessor Godzilla Minus One.

Hitting 3,254 North American theaters from GKids and Toho, Godzilla Minus Zero conquered $31.6 million domestically for the weekend. This is being touted as the highest-grossing live-action foreign-language opening at the box office since 2004’s Hero launched with $17.8 million. (It’s behind the debut of last year’s animated Demon Slayer: Infinity Castle with $70.6 million, while 2004’s The Passion of the Christ and its massive $83.8 million opening is not included in the category as an American-produced project.) Godzilla Minus Zero notches the highest-grossing opening weekend for a GKids release, topping the $13 million for 2023’s Oscar-winning The Boy and the Heron. Godzilla Minus Zero added $31.4 million internationally, including $17 million in Japan. Filmmaker Takashi Yamazaki‘s sequel to 2023’s Oscar-winning Godzilla Minus One earned an A CinemaScore from audiences and performed well above the distributor’s projected opening in the mid-$20 millions.

Ryunosuke Kamiki, Minami Hamabe and Min Tanaka star in the new movie that carries a reported budget in the mid-teens. It centers on postwar Japan dealing with attacks from the three-headed Ghidorah and the possibility of Godzilla’s return. Godzilla Minus Zero holds an impressive 90 percent critics approval on Rotten Tomatoes, along with a 96 from audiences, with The Hollywood Reporter chief film critic David Rooney calling it a “thrilling jolt of marauding monster action.”

The sequel is making a bigger footprint than Godzilla Minus One, which opened in December 2023 with $11.4 million domestically and reached $57.1 million for the run, adding $56.9 million internationally. The film rode its massive breakout success to become Japan’s first feature to win the best visual effects Oscar.

Overall, the weekend showed an increase of around 11 percent over last weekend, with the studios avoiding new releases for the Halloween frame, and a roughly 40 percent uptick from the equivalent 2025 weekend that saw the debut of 20 Century’s Predator: Badlands, according to FranchiseRe. This October had trouble establishing hits that can continue to play deep into the fall.

Warner Bros.’ The Cat in the Hat grossed a sleepy $20.5 million domestically from 4,003 North American locations, on par with the studio’s projection. The two previous animated Dr. Seuss adaptations had far higher debuts, with Universal’s 2018 The Grinch swiping $67.5 million in its opening, while that studio’s 2012 launch of The Lorax claimed $70.2 million. The studio hopes for word of mouth to help the $92 million-budgeted The Cat in the Hat play through the Thanksgiving holiday and into December after earning an A CinemaScore, with competition on the horizon from Disney’s Hexed on Nov. 25.

Dr. Seuss films historically haven’t made a big splash overseas, and this one has staggered its international release, with around half of the territories set for later debuts throughout the remainder of the year. The Cat in the Hat added $10 million internationally for the frame.

Directors Erica Rivinoja and Alessandro Carloni’s animated adaptation of Dr. Seuss’ classic 1957 children’s book centers on the titular feline being given a final chance to prove his worth by helping a pair of siblings. Bill Hader leads the voice cast that also includes Matt Berry, Quinta Brunson and Xochitl Gomez. The movie marks Warner Bros. Pictures Animation’s first title since Bill Damaschke took over as president and chief creative officer in 2023.

Critics have been a bit aloof on the film, as it holds a 46 percent Rotten Tomatoes score from reviewers and an 87 percent score from audiences, with THR critic Frank Scheck calling it “more taxing than enjoyable.” It’s faring better critically than Universal’s 2003 live-action The Cat in the Hat starring Mike Myers, which holds an 11 percent score on Rotten Tomatoes. That movie opened to $38.3 million domestically en route to a North American cume of $101 million against a $109 million budget.

Sony Pictures released Prime Focus Studios’ Ramayana: Rise of a Legend in 1,433 North American locations, with the film grossing roughly $8.6 million for the weekend and $12.1 million for the four days. It added $32 million in India and an additional $11.5 million from 37 overseas markets. Boasting the widest domestic launch ever for an Indian movie, director Nitesh Tiwari’s religious epic focuses on the life of Prince Rama, with the two-film project’s budget reported at $420 million and beyond. It holds a 76 score from critics on Rotten Tomatoes, along with an 89 from audiences.

Also debuting in the top five is Angel Studios’ Drummer Boy, with directors Ben and Joel David Smallbone’s historical drama grossing a strong $9.3 million from 2,850 North American locations. Earning an A CinemaScore, the faith-based musical focuses on an unlikely bond during the American Revolution and stars Joel David Smallbone in the title role. On Rotten Tomatoes, it has a 50 critics score and a 94 from audiences. Angel was uniquely positioned to open the film in a major way after having established itself with the faith-minded audience.

“It has been eight long years of failure and triumph to bring Drummer Boy to the screen,” Joel David Smallbone said in a statement to celebrate the opening. “So as you’d imagine, I find myself deeply impacted by the fact that this personal story has found its way into hundreds of thousands of your hearts.”

Academy Awards hopeful Wild Horse Nine opened in 1,100 North American theaters with an estimated frame of $6 million. John Malkovich, Sam Rockwell and Steve Buscemi lead writer-director Martin McDonagh’s comedic thriller about two CIA operatives who travel from Santiago, Chile, to Easter Island in 1973. The Searchlight Pictures release holds a 94 critics score on Rotten Tomatoes and 91 from audiences, with Rooney’s review describing it as “uniquely affecting.”

Likely rounding out the top 10 is Black Bear’s Wicker, which declined just 23 percent in its third weekend with $3.3 million for a domestic cume of $19.8 million, along with an additional $9.1 million internationally. Olivia Colman and Alexander Skarsgard lead directors Eleanor Wilson and Alex Huston Fischer’s romantic fantasy that continues to build on enthusiastic word of mouth, holding a 94 critics score on Rotten Tomatoes and 92 from audiences.

Expected to hit just outside the top 10 is Burns & Co and Fathom Entertainment’s Jimmy, with the Jimmy Stewart biopic opening to an estimated $2 million from 1,470 North American theaters. Scheck’s review calls star KJ Apa “top-notch” but deems director Aaron Burns’ film “heartfelt to a fault.”

Another Oscar hopeful launched in platform release as A24 debuted Club Kid on six screens, with the Cannes standout grossing $390,583 for an average of $65,097 per screen. Making his feature directorial debut, Jordan Firstman also stars as a party promoter who learns he has a 10-year-old son. Set to expand into Thanksgiving, it boasts a sterling 100 score from reviewers on Rotten Tomatoes, with THR critic Richard Lawson heralding it as “winning and clever.”

Amazon MGM Studios releases I Play Rocky in seven locations, and it grossed around $345,000 for a per-screen average of $49,200. Anthony Ippolito stars as Sylvester Stallone in director Peter Farrelly’s making-of story for the 1976 classic Rocky. In his review, Rooney wrote that the movie — which holds an 88 Rotten Tomatoes score from critics — creates “a wave of warm nostalgia.”

Among holdovers, A24’s much-discussed Elizabeth Holmes documentary You Can See Everything looks to pull in around $8.8 million for the weekend as it jostles with Ramayana and Drummer Boy to round out the top five. The domestic cume for directors Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim’s feature stands at $79.7 million.

Next weekend brings the Johnny Depp studio comeback vehicle Ebenezer, along with How to Rob a Bank and Paper Tiger.

Tom Hardy’s Epic 6-Season Crime Series Is a Near-Perfect Netflix Binge

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

From HBO’s The Sopranos to Paramount+’s MobLand, dysfunctional mob families make for great television — but no other group of criminals is quite as cunning, conniving, and calculating as the Shelby family from Netflix’s Peaky Blinders. Starring Oscar-winning Cillian Murphy as the notorious Shelby ringleader Thomas “Tommy” Shelby, Peaky Blinders takes viewers back to Small Heath, Birmingham, in 1919, the town that would witness the Shelbys’ rise, peak, and eventual downfall.

Violence and bloodshed are expected in the series, but Peaky Blinders is just as much a family drama as it is a crime epic. Blood may be thicker than water, but Tommy is a businessman first and a family man second. Across six seasons, Peaky Blinders delivers no shortage of shocking plot twists to keep audiences hooked, made all the more surprising by the fact that many come from the people closest to Tommy.

Inspired by the real-life street gang, Peaky Blinders follows Tommy, fresh out of serving in World War I, as he returns to Small Heath with plans to turn his family’s small horse-betting operation into a legitimate business. His opportunity comes when his men stumble upon a crate of stolen military weapons, setting his bigger plans for the Peaky Blinders into motion. From blackmailing police authorities and taking territory from rival gangs to expanding into bigger cities, each season sees Tommy taking another step toward legitimizing Shelby Company Limited.

Four Netflix characters, four completely different ways of showing up for somebody. This is the one who fits how you actually do friendship, and what having them in your life would be like.

You do not do friendship at half volume, and neither does he. What you want is somebody who says yes to the plan before you have finished describing the plan.

You are the friend who makes things happen, and so is she. Together you would be mildly exhausting to everyone around you and completely delighted with yourselves.

You have no patience for pleasant lies, and she has no ability to tell them. What looks like coldness from the outside is the most reliable friendship on this list.

You look after whoever is having the worst day. So does he. It is the least strategic way to move through the world and the best possible quality in a friend.

As Tommy’s ambitions grow, so do the risks. Every season also follows the seemingly untouchable Tommy pushing his luck further, making increasingly questionable decisions to keep his empire growing. He doesn’t even hesitate to use his own family members as collateral, insisting that it’s all for a greater purpose. That's part of the appeal of Peaky Blinders: watching just how long Tommy can get away with his schemes before his luck finally runs out. When a man who has spent his entire life building an empire finds himself with nothing left, the question becomes whether he can rise from the ashes or if this is finally where his story ends.

Peaky Blinders has no shortage of seasoned actors bringing its characters to life. Besides Murphy’s cutthroat performance as the morally ambiguous and frighteningly intelligent Tommy, the rest of the Shelby family are just as gutsy as their leader, with some arguably even more so. From Paul Anderson as Tommy’s violent but troubled older brother, Arthur Shelby, to Helen McCrory as the wiser and more level-headed Aunt Polly Gray, each family member has a distinct personality that makes them fascinating to watch. Put them all in the same room, though, and their explosive chemistry makes audiences wonder how they haven't killed each other yet.

The series also boasts an impressive roster of recurring characters, some of whom become regular fixtures in Tommy's life while others only appear for a season or two. Even with limited screen time, these roles attract plenty of talent. Anya Taylor-Joy, Stephen Graham, and Adrien Brody are just a few of the big names who join the cast in later seasons. Then there's Tom Hardy as Alfie Solomons, the leader of a London-based Jewish gang. Appearing across five seasons, Hardy makes for one of Tommy's most entertaining counterparts, with their unpredictable alliance and rivalry providing some of the series' best moments.

Pierce Brosnan and Helen Mirren also star.

The original Peaky Blinders series ended in 2022, seemingly sealing Tommy’s fate after six seasons of chasing one impossible high after another. However, the story didn't end there. Meet the 27-Year-Old Who’s Trying To Bring Housing Back to Rural Ohio

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

When it comes to the hottest locations that attract young 20-somethings, Mansfield, OH, probably isn't the first place that comes to mind.

For years, Mansfield’s nickname was “The Fun Center of Ohio” because of its family-friendly attractions and its status in the 1970s as a mini-bike manufacturing hub.

More recently, the town of 47,000, located halfway between Columbus and Cleveland, earned the local nickname of “Danger City” after the Westinghouse electric plant left in 1990 and the town fell into deep economic decline.

The 27-year-old serial entrepreneur, who started a painting and landscaping business as a teenager, relocated to Mansfield from Seattle a year ago, hoping to revitalize rural housing through his vertically integrated housing development company, Western Dominion Land.

Vertical integration is a development model in which a single company controls the full life cycle of a housing project.

“In other countries, they’ve figured out that vertically integrating construction means you’re not paying a margin out to every single step," Goehner explains. "If you own the system—from a lumberyard, to producing panels or modular, to buying the land, to developing the land, to building the homes—you're not paying everyone’s markup along the way, and you can actually deliver that home at a reasonable price.”

Goehner is building a factory in Mansfield to produce structured panels that he says will cut single-family home construction from months to weeks and allow him to charge much less than other builders. The first panels are expected to roll off the production line in early 2027.

Currently, it takes 8.8 months to construct a home from start to finish, according to the National Association of Home Builders. Goehner says his process allows a home to be built and move-in-ready in 40 days.

Western Dominion has already built about 500 housing units across Ohio, starting at $200,000 for a single-family home.

Western Dominion focuses on what it calls "attainable housing"—homes priced below what the median household income in the area can afford but not income-limited, meaning they work for the vast majority of families and buyers.

In Mansfield, where the median listing price is $229,000, he says, "single-family homes at $215,000 sold instantly. At $260,000, they sat for months."

As he sees it, 25% of the homes on the market are priced for 60% to 70% of buyers, while the rest of what’s being built is at the top end of the market. Furthermore, only 8% of construction happens in rural areas, despite 20% of the population living there.

"The government is too focused on low-income housing" and low-income housing tax credits, he says. “And the last few housing bills they passed were only structured to deliver essentially more urban multifamily units and incentivize builders to build more low-income housing with tax credits."

In rural communities, he adds, “We have a terrible amount of aged housing inventory. Most of our homes are from before 1970, which would be fine if it didn't cost so much to remodel.

“From the '90s to the 2000s, we’re building a good amount of homes. As a country, we were doing about 1.6 million a year. And then in rural America, we were doing about 220,000 homes a year. And then 2008 hits, and that drops down to about 60,000 homes a year in rural America."

Along the way, smaller builders disappeared, and larger builders were disincentivized from building in rural areas because of the red tape involved.

"If you want to build in rural America and you go to a bank, unless you have all the cash, you have to show comps," he says. "You've got to be, like, ‘All right, we think these homes are going to sell for this much because these guys sold for this much.' And if no one's built for five to 50 years, which is the case in a lot of these communities, that can be difficult."

The added costs of building in rural areas, like having to pull utilities, "mean you're spending $25,000 to $50,000 per lot before you can even start putting a house on it or connecting your utilities to the lot."

Since the COVID-19 pandemic, home prices in rural communities have risen sharply. A Realtor.com® housing analysis of home prices from November 2019 to November 2025 found that median listing prices increased more than 70% in rural counties while in-metro counties saw home prices increase around 30% during the same time period.

"A lot of the politicians we talk to, they go, 'I didn't know that that county needed housing. I didn't know rural needed housing because the population is shrinking.' But then when you look at why the population is shrinking, it's because they haven't gotten any new homes for 20 to 40 years."

"You've got this huge demand. But on paper, on the surface, it looks like there's no demand because the population's declining," he continues. "And that's how you get cities like Holiday City."

Holiday City, near the Ohio border with Indiana and Michigan, has a population of just 48 people, but it is home to three large businesses—Chase Brass & Copper Co., 20/20 Custom Molded Products, and a Menards Distribution Center—which draw around 1,500 commuters from out of state.

"Let's put up, you know, 300 homes, and a portion of the workforce can actually stop commuting an hour or two every day," Goehner says.

Goehner hopes Ohio's new slate of elected officials will help advance rural building by offsetting some of the infrastructure-related costs of building in rural areas. In exchange, he would sell homes at his “attainable” housing rates.

"The government needs to stop spending thousands of dollars a month per unit to subsidize rents when they could simply spend a little bit upfront to make it viable to build at a reasonable price point again," he says.

Vitalik Buterin Declares Ethereum (ETH) a Store of Value, Not Fee-Burning Token

Ethereum's intrinsic economics is rebuilding itself in motion with tech bugs and the macroeconomic picture overlaying each other in the span of 48 hours. The week wraps up for a major alternative crypto project with a sudden Glamsterdam hard fork stop after testnet launch revealed a dangerous bug in the fee recalculating mechanism, brutally breaking the logic in smart contracts.

As the Ethereum community debated the ability of the network to sustain on old power, Vitalik Buterin showed up with a manifesto which completely zeroes out all the panic around mainnet's decreasing revenue. The "red line" behind Vitalik's updated vision is that the community should stop being fixated on the idea of ETH as "fee-burninng token". For a basic blockchain layer worth $270 billion, attempts to build evaluation on the amount of gas burned are a dead end. For Vitalik, the novel of "ETH as store of value (SoV)" is much more viable amid all the global uncertainty around.

This U-turn, taken by a key visionary of the ecosystem, happened at $2,240 for ETH and may drastically change all the plans developed with a focus on Glamsterdam architecture. The hard fork was initially projected for the logic of blockspace surplus, not the deficit. Ecosystem builders are already readying expansion of L1 capacity with the help of EIP-8037 (tariffs calibration) and EIP-7928 (parallel production) with an aim to raise gas limits up to 200 million units. The logic is that the excess of free space will cheapen the transactions, while token burning rates will gradually be cut.

The market saw a risk of deflation in this concept. Vitalik took a front-step. By his estimates, the historical peaks of burning through EIP-1559 "never were as strong as they seemed". They were dependent on inner cyclical activity of crypto speculations. And it itself was falling once every bear market started.

Attempts to squeeze the premium out of the protocol by any means would push the system toward overly aggressive management and interventions, says Buterin. Ethereum would be required to become a centralized commercial project with inflated costs on research and development in other words. What the market needs meanwhile, amid the rise of AI and regulatory storms, is not a "corporate shitcoin", but what Vitalik calls CROPS - "censorship and capture resistance, open source, privacy and security".

EIP-1559 is not going anywhere, and Vitalik openly says "don't be sad" if the volume of gas burned will forever stay below the 10% mark. The source of value for Ethereum lies in completely another dimension.

Solana's co-founder, Anatoly Yakovenko, unexpectedly aligned with Buterin on this matter. "You can't focus on fees as the products", he said, calling such an idea "absolutely meaningless". According to Yakovenko, the blockchain network must generate real value for the end users, not control the price of getting into the block.

Vitalik is absolutely right that you can’t focus on fees as the product. That’s absolutely meaningless. You need to pick a product, the thing that brings value to people, and do that really well.

There is lots of technical stuff and philosophy around, but what the missed deadline on the testnet and Vitalik's manifesto reveal is that Ethereum opts for long-term, secure, and even a bit boring stability of the basic layer. Infra doesn't try to flirt with the market through deflationary records.
