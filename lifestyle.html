Noni Madueke snatched a 2-2 draw for Arsenal after Newcastle produced a stunning second-half turnaround to claim a deserved point against the Premier League leaders.

Eberechi Eze put the champions in front and Madueke wasted a couple of chances to increase the lead before half-time, with Matthias Jaissle’s side jeered off following a listless first 45 minutes.

They rallied in stunning fashion on the resumption, with Harvey Barnes scoring Newcastle’s first goal at St James’ Park since September 19 before Malick Thiaw put them ahead after the hour from close range.

Former Newcastle captain Bruno Guimaraes underwhelmed on his return to Tyneside, his every touch booed before he was brought off after 70 minutes, but his new employers claimed a share of the spoils thanks to Madueke.

Arsenal’s five-match winning run in the league was halted, although they extended their lead at the top of the table to six points over second-placed Brighton, who could halve the deficit at Hull on Sunday.

Newcastle came into the fixture with significant concerns about where their goals were coming from and they were up against the most miserly defence in the league, with Arsenal conceding just five times in their previous 10 games.

Newcastle were given an early warning when Madueke cut inside and left England team-mate Dan Burn trailing in his wake, with the Arsenal forward’s low shot to the near post hitting the outside of it.

Arsenal’s press meant there was no let-up for Newcastle goalkeeper Lukas Hornicek, who made a series of distribution errors, and it was a heart-in-mouth moment for the home side’s fans when Guimaraes got ahead of Barnes but the Brazilian could not direct a sliding volley on-target.

The Magpies’ luck ran out in the 33rd minute when Kai Havertz dispossessed Lewis Hall before sliding a perfectly-weighted pass to Eze, who went in behind Amar Dedic to finish coolly past Hornicek.

Matters might have worsened before half-time as more loose play allowed Madueke to pick his pocket. Madueke bore down on goal but took one touch too many before his shot was saved by Hornicek.

Hall made partial amends to then deny Madueke’s shot while Eze’s free-kick was saved as Arsenal ended the half on top, prompting Jaissle to make a triple substitution at the break, with Jacob Ramsey, Anthony Elanga and Tino Livramento replacing Jacob Murphy and Dedic and Sean Steur.

Newcastle exploded out of the traps and needed just four minutes after the restart to draw level, with Martin Odegaard nudged off the ball by Joe Willock and the ball dropping to William Osula, who teed up Barnes to fire beyond David Raya with aplomb.

It was Newcastle’s first goal at home in 312 minutes and with the crowd now firmly behind them, they remarkably took the lead with Arsenal at sixes and sevens.

The unmarked Burn headed Hall’s corner on-target and a goalmouth scramble between Willock and Arsenal duo Gabriel and Raya led to Thiaw poking home from a yard out in the 62nd minute.

Arteta was forced into a substitution with Raya suffering a shoulder injury, while Guimaraes was also hooked alongside Eze with 20 minutes to go to the delight of Newcastle’s supporters as Kepa Arrizabalaga, Christos Tzolis and Myles Lewis-Skelly were introduced.

Newcastle continued to pile forward but were undone in the 78th minute when a fluid move forwards from Arsenal ended with Madueke cutting onto his left foot and dispatching through Hornicek’s hands and in.

Both sides had chances to claim victory in a frenzied atmosphere but neither could manage another breakthrough before Guimaraes, the first Newcastle captain to lift a trophy in 56 years following their historic 2025 Carabao Cup triumph, was cheered at full-time when he completed a lap of the pitch.

Miles Morales’ New Spider-Man Redesign Released By Marvel

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Lifestyle  |  Read Source →

Miles Morales gets a new Spider-Man costume that complements his iconic black-and-red suit with extra accessories. Miles Morales achieved the remarkable but overlooked feat of turning his sleek black-and-red Spider-Man costume into an unmistakable pop-culture staple nearly three decades after Peter Parker’s legendary black symbiote suit first debuted in 1984's Secret Wars. Creating an alternate Spider-Man uniform that stands on its own without feeling like a temporary variant is notoriously difficult, yet Sara Pichelli’s 2011 design immediately resonated with a new generation of readers. The striking contrast of matte black fabric accented by crimson webbing gave Miles a distinct identity that stands apart from Peter’s classic red-and-blue attire.

Not content to stop at a single memorable color scheme, Miles Morales has pushed Spider-Man fashion into bold territory with his many streetwear accessories. Miles' Spider-Man: Into the Spider-Verse look became an instant phenomenon after it layered an oversized hoodie, basketball shorts, and Air Jordan 1 sneakers directly over his spandex suit. In the comics and games, Miles has continued to experiment with standout looks, including his high-collared tenth Anniversary pink-and-navy streetwear suit, his Vibranium armor gifted by Black Panther, the Bodega Cat backpack outfit, and sleek cyberpunk or symbiote-inspired ensembles that reflect his Brooklyn roots and evolving heroic confidence.

Miles Morales has arguably many more options for creative costume redesigns than Peter Parker, and Marvel's new variation of his suit proves it.

After a first wave of gorgeous character redesigns, InHyuk Lee's Streetverse variant covers return with a new batch of stylish Marvel characters. Black Cat wears a fancy all-white outfit with long black boots, the Punisher dons a beautiful black-and-red coat that makes him look like a vampire hunter, and Miles Morales shows off a multi-layered Spider-Man costume. Clearly, InHyuk Lee's new wave of Streetverse designs are geared specifically for winter, and Miles Morales proposes the most practical outfit of all.

Peter Parker has famously worn civilian clothes over his Spider-Man suits, particularly during the cold New York winter or during tough flu episodes. Both The Amazing Spider-Man 2 and Spider-Man: Brand New Day adapted these visual twists, while Spider-Man: Into the Spider-Verse integrated Miles' hoodie into his first official Spider-Man costume. Perhaps in the future, Earth-616's Miles could put even more stylish twists on the classic winter Spider-Man outfit trope, potentially drawing closely from InHyuk Lee's Streetverse design in Marvel Comics canon.

Miles Morales’ Spider-Man: Into the Spider-Verse costume and InHyuk Lee’s acclaimed Streetverse variant design succeed as a natural streetwear extension of his personality. Into the Spider-Verse's oversized hoodie, basketball shorts, and sneakers over Miles' classic black-and-red spandex tells an immediate story about a teenager still growing into his heroic role and refusing to abandon his everyday style. InHyuk Lee’s Streetverse cover artwork takes the same concept and adds sharp urban tailoring that complements Miles’s signature crimson-and-black palette. Meanwhile, Insomniac’s similar attempt at an "Evolved Suit" in Marvel’s Spider-Man 2 clashed with Miles' style, as the jarring electric-blue-and-white toothpaste color scheme and the open-top mask felt out of place.

Through twenty years of cinema, Spider-Man's iconic red and blue suit has seen many variations. Each one is unique in its own way. What are they?

Jackets, hoodies, sneakers, and layered streetwear accessories work so effortlessly on Miles because they capture his youthful energy and his artistic background. Unlike Peter Parker, whose classic superhero aesthetic grew out of 1960s acrobatic simplicity, Miles belongs to a modern era where fashion, music, and self-expression go hand in hand. The addition of a cropped jacket and a pulled-up hood breaks up the rigid uniformity of traditional superhero spandex and gives his agile movements extra visual momentum in both comics and animation, as long as those layers enhance his iconic palette instead of burying it under distracting and unnecessary details.

This week at the CMA Awards, Taylor Swift’s nails were so scarlet, they were practically “maroon.” The singer sported short nails painted a shade of deep red that evoked a tall, thin wine glass, and the manicure went with all three of her outfits of the night. It’s not the first time she’s given us nail inspiration—she recently had “gold fairy dust nails” that stole the show at the 2026 Academy Museum Gala. But this new color is the perfect shade for cooler weather.

“I would describe this as a classic burgundy manicure,” says Vikki Swan, award-winning nail educator and founder of Vikki Swan Nail Academy in Los Angeles. “The deep wine-red shade creates a timeless, elegant look. It’s a great example of how a simple, solid color can make a statement without any additional nail art.”

The color also isn’t completely opaque—but it also isn’t translucent. Jin Soon Choi, manicurist and founder of Jinsoon, describes the polish as a “crelly.” “The crelly finish combines the richness of a cream [nail polish] with the softness of a jelly,” she says. “It gives this classic color a youthful, modern twist while keeping it soft and effortless.”

A Party to End All Parties—Fashionable London Fetes the Life of Antony Price

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Lifestyle  |  Read Source →

Antony Price always said that if anyone should ever think about giving him a memorial, “Make it a fabulous party!” The legendary and beloved British designer’s wishes were honored by his friends in London on Wednesday night. With glamour on the invitation and his gasp-making shows and images playing on vast screens, the man who forged the look of Roxy Music and Duran Duran and conjured couture dreams for decades was fully celebrated in an epic Antony Price appreciation night thrown for him at 180 Strand.

Tributes flowed from his multi-generational fans and closest people. Among them were models who’d walked his London shows, or rather stalked and prowled them, per his direction: Marie Helvin and Yasmin Le Bon, as well as Marilyn Cole, who brought along a glorious reminder of her role on the cover of Roxy Music’s 1973 album Stranded, printed on a t-shirt. There she was, lying looking seductively sweaty in a jungle wearing an artfully shredded red dress that Antony Price had concocted. Thanks to Price’s creative direction and styling (terms unheard-of then), the lives of a generation of British teenagers were changed, just from staring at the fantasies of Jerry Hall as a blue-painted mermaid, Amanda Lear as a gleaming patent-leather dominatrix, and Kari-Ann Muller as a 1950s pin-up on Roxy album covers. It went from scruffy long-haired hippy to sexy slicked-back glamour overnight.

Thirty years later, Alexander Fury discovered the credit ‘Antony Price: clothes, makeup and hair’ on the back of the Roxy albums as a student at Central Saint Martin’s. “I didn’t care about the music—it was the clothes, the look! Who was this genius? ” It started an obsession that led Fury to fashion journalism; he also became an Antony Price collector and friend. From the podium, he contextualized Price’s contribution as the first London designer to fuse fashion, music, and spectacle together in live events—selling tickets to the public. His 1970s fashion shows, dramatically stylized productions staged with supermodels in music venues like Camden Palace and the Hippodrome, “were pinnacles of that approach, early examples of fashion breaking out of its niche to be truly democratic. He was a revolutionary, a pioneer,” Fury said, but added, “yet he was also, and he remains, the most criminally overlooked designer in British fashion history.”

Nick Rhodes of Duran Duran was celebrating his friend’s life in the crowd, while his fellow band members Simon Le Bon and John Taylor circulated elsewhere. Rhodes remembered how he and Taylor came across Price’s wildly stylish store Plaza in the King’s Road, and how that led to Price making the band the vividly colored suits they wore on a yacht slicing through the Caribbean on the video for their massive 1982 hit “Rio.”

“ John and I went in there and blew our entire EMI clothes budget for our whole album on one suit. As soon as we told him we were planning a shoot in the Caribbean, he said, “Oh, all those beautiful pastel colors! And he started dyeing things in his bath. Everything was hand-made with Antony. When you hear people saying, he was a true artist—well he was. He did everything, could do everything. He was up there with Thierry Mugler in Paris, and John Galliano or Alexander McQueen, after him.”

Price was an ace tailor, sculpting broad-shouldered, narrow-hipped suits based on his own rediscovery of matinee idols and screen goddesses in 1940s Hollywood movies when he was a Royal College of Art fashion student in the 1960s. Born in Keighley in the idyllic Yorkshire countryside in 1945, he went to Bradford school of art, just after David Hockney, then followed him to the Royal College of Art at the time when London was swinging. He was the son of “a Spitfire pilot who left my mother with me and my sister and twins,” he once told me. “We were not a straight-up-and-down family. My sister was a poor man’s Sophia Loren. I’ve always loved that curvaceous type.” A lot of that background stuck with him and imprinted his tastes for life. One thing was his liking for uniform; he’d style himself “like a Star Captain beamed down to earth for an evening’s rock ’n’ roll, often in a military cap ,” as I wrote once in a British Vogue profile.

Then there was his passion for birds, keeping and breeding peacocks and fancifully-feathered chickens. He never said whether his idea of molding clients’ torsos from chicken-wire (as couture dress-forms) had cross-fertilized from this practice, though it was a suspicion. Philip Treacy saluted Price, who sculpted bombshell dresses in black velvet for his millinery shows, remembering just what a brilliant collaborator he was. One season, the young Treacy talked about his idea to use feathers to finish an upcoming collection. “So Antony bred a hundred white Yokohama chickens—from the egg. Then brought them live to my studio so I could clip the tail-feathers off before putting them on the hats,” he laughed, adding, “no chickens died for fashion.”

Price delighted in collaborating with younger creative people, who adored him back for his genius, his delight in making women look more amazing than they’d ever felt, his mind-blowing techniques, and, yes, his “lethal mix of humor and pessimism,” as Treacy put it. There were, indeed, decades when—though he was still operating a couture service for private clients—Price would moan at all and sundry that he’d be forgotten. That was not to be. In what was to be the last year of his life, Marco Capaldo, the young designer of 16Arlington, a fan who idolized Antony Price’s glamour, asked him to collaborate on a show of 16 curvy demi-couture looks. On November 17 last year, they were flamboyantly posed in high-1980s style by a whole new generation, including Lily Allen, wearing the ‘Avenge’ dress, Lila Moss in the feathered ‘Pollo,’ and Lara Stone in the zebra-stripe ‘Jerry.’ The warmth of the ovation as Capaldo brought him out at the end finally showed Price just how much he was adulated and loved.

Kit Butler in 16Arlington and Antony Price

Isaac Ferry, son of Bryan, grew up seeing melees of creative people coming in and out of the house. Price was his godfather. Four years ago, while Price was living in a cottage in Sussex with Isaac, his now wife Elizabeth and brother Tara, he witnessed the couturier’s tireless routine in action. “He’d rise at five, go straight down to the workroom, ‘because I’ve got to get on,’ and he’d be down there, watching the early birds at the feeders. He’d sit at his machine or cutting table, working away while watching Cleopatra, Spartacus, or some other Hollywood spectacular.”

Shortly after the 16Arlington show, Antony Price passed away at his sewing machine at the age of 80. Wednesday’s party was an emblematic finale to a legendary life, toasted with oceans of Moet & Chandon by the constellation of stars of pop, London club-life of the high glam years, clients of two generations who partied for him in exactly the way he might have dreamed up himself. Now, just one thing remains to memorialize the full stature of Antony Price: An exhibition and/or a documentary about him are long overdue.

Michelle Obama, Hillary Clinton, and Jill Biden Come Together at Gloria Steinem’s Joyous Celebration of Life

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Lifestyle  |  Read Source →

The former first ladies coordinated in elegant, high-fashion ensembles to honor the feminist activist

Gloria Steinem’s life, work, and legacy were celebrated this week, as many gathered at a memorial service for the feminist activist in New York City.

The celebration of life took place at the Park Avenue Armory and came two months after Steinem’s passing at age 92.

Among those who came together to pay their respects were former United States first ladies Michelle Obama, Hillary Clinton, and Jill Biden—as well as Chelsea Clinton, Diane von Furstenberg, Meg Ryan, Lucy Liu, Emma Watson, Marisa Tomei, and Mariska Hargitay. In total, there were about 1,000 invited guests, all who were encouraged to donate to Gloria’s Foundation, which aims to preserve Steinem’s Upper East Side brownstone on East 73rd Street as a permanent archive and center for feminist organizing.

Rather than a somber service, the memorial was a joyful, music-filled celebration titled “It’s Steinemite!” Cynthia Erivo sang John Lennon’s “Imagine,” Cyndi Lauper performed “Girls Just Want to Have Fun,” and Kathy Sledge of Sister Sledge closed the event with “We Are Family,” joined by Lauper and Gloria’s Choir.

For the evening, attendees coordinated in all-black ensembles—but it certainly wasn’t your average funeral attire. Clinton wore an elegant pantsuit—one of her favored looks. Obama was so chic and cool in a cropped blazer and a pleated skirt with a silky sash and golden accents. And Biden went for a fun, high-fashion ensemble that included Schiaparelli’s black Corsini suit jacket with asymmetric gold buttons.

If she could’ve looked into that crowd of incredible women, Steinem would’ve been endlessly proud.

It's the first Yeezy runway show since 2022.

Yeezy season has returned. Ye (formerly Kanye West) had the runway back on for the Yeezy Season 01 fashion show on Wednesday (Nov. 4) in Los Angeles, which served as his first Yeezy show since 2022’s controversial Paris Fashion Week exhibit, at which he wore a “White Live Matter” shirt.

Plenty of star power was on hand to support West — who continues to face backlash for his years of antisemitic hate speech, though he took out an ad in the Wall Street Journal to apologize in January — including Big Sean, Kid Cudi, 2 Chainz, Natalie Nunn, Sab Zada, designer Jerry Lorenzo, model India Love, his wife Bianca Censori, along with Ye’s four children with ex-wife Kim Kardashian: North, Saint, Psalm and Chicago.

There were also tan boots, along with red pants, sweaters, T-shirts and bomber jackets, with other minimalistic looks resembling earlier Yeezy aesthetic.

A second song featured a blaring siren on the production side. “I went through your phone/ Who the hell is Tone?/ I went through your phone,” Ye repeats on the haunting track.

West posed for photos in the rare public appearance with all four of his children. North sported her signature neon blue hair, while Saint rocked a gray hoodie and Psalm and Chicago opted for more earthy tones.

The 49-year-old has a pair of shows left this year, as he’s set to take over AT&T Stadium in Dallas on Saturday (Nov. 7) before heading to Glendale, Ariz., on Nov. 21.

Met Gala 2027: Costume Institute Announces New Theme After John Galliano Controversy

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Lifestyle  |  Read Source →

The 2027 Met Gala has a new focus. On November 5, Vogue, alongside the Metropolitan Museum of Art Costume Institute, announced its new spring 2027 exhibition and Met Gala theme. And its new focus is on the “extraORDINARY: Fashion and the Poetics of the Everyday,” which will be curated by Andrew Bolton alongside Stephanie Kramer and Emily Mushaben. This pivot comes after designer John Galliano pulled out of the 2027 Met Gala months after political and civic leaders raised concerns over his previous racist and antisemitic comments that led to his termination as Dior’s creative director in 2011.

The exhibition will be “devoted to the undersung beauty and allure of quotidian garments—think the aforementioned t-shirts and jeans, plus raincoats, sweatshirts, puffer jackets, and more. It will ask visitors ‘to see the everyday anew, and come at its subject from two perspectives: First, exploring how designers have reinvented and elevated archetypal garments to runway-worthy creations and then, secondly, highlight how humble, even ‘disposable’ materials—like paper, aluminum, and plastic—are transformed into wearable art.”

“It was really important to come up with an exhibition that wasn’t just a masterwork show or something that we could do a little bit more easily,” Bolton also said. “That was really important to me: to come up with an idea that wasn’t a substitute. And it had to have some sort of contemporary resonance.”

Before this switch, the museum stated that the original exhibit, John Galliano: Horizons, was meant to “consider how artistic achievement should be understood alongside ethical accountability.” So now, this exploration of the extraORDINARY seems to be more focused on design and creation from a more sustainable viewpoint.

So now that fashion’s biggest night is back on track with a new theme in hand, it will be interesting to see how all our favorite celebrities interpret this new theme. The Met Gala 2027 will take place on May 3, and the exhibit will open to the public on May 9, 2027, and run through January 9, 2028.

After a 16-year long dev period, Faraway, a shooting star arcade game about siphoning the energy of other stars, is out now

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Lifestyle  |  Read Source →

Lord almighty, video games take a while to make, 'ey? Especially if you're just one person working on it by yourself. Such is the case with Faraway, out today, an arcade game that reminds me of the early days of PS3 indies "about a shooting star that creates constellations to stay alive," all made single-handedly by creator of cult-classic mobile game Eliss, Steph Thirion. Did I mention that Faraway took 16 years to make? Like I said, game development is a lengthy process.

Looking at Faraway, and knowing how long it took to produce, it doesn't surprise me to come from a dev who managed to release a mobile game in the early days of the platform, long before free-to-play got its chokehold on the industry. It also really does feel like it should be sitting amongst the likes of PixelJunk, this thing should be something I'm desperate to play on my PS3 while I wait for my controller to charge.

As mentioned, you are a shooting star, a "spark meant to shine and vanish in a blink." But still you persist, connecting to and wrapping yourself around starts to "harvest their energy," in turn lengthening your own life, and of course, in arcadey fashion, racking up a nice score. It looks like game design as poetry, everything you're looking at procedurally generated to bring freshness to every run. I can fully envision this as a "one more run" kind of arcade game, the best kind, especially in an age long past the need to shove in another of your hard earned coins.

Faraway conceptually charms me because it taps into a style of game we don't get to see much of any more. What I'm saying is, I'm just a teensy bit sick of how many roguelikes there are, but that doesn't mean I don't want a game I can just hop in and out of at my own whims. Sure, arcade games might not be in vogue, I just miss them a bit. In any case, Faraway is available to pick up on Steam right now. Seems like it'll be worth a punt!
