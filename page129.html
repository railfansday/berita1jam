Carrie Coon Speaks Out About Women’s Representation at ELLE’s Women in Hollywood Event

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Carrie Coon had a question for her fellow Women in Hollywood honorees: “Can we start a studio?”

On Monday night, the actress took the stage at ELLE’s 2026 Women in Hollywood celebration in Los Angeles, where she called for women in the entertainment industry to come together and create more opportunities for themselves.

Coon was introduced by her Gilded Age co-star Louisa Jacobson, who celebrated her recent career achievements.

“Carrie is a crusader, always fighting tirelessly for artistic integrity in a landscape that seems to value high gloss and profit over honest, nuanced storytelling,” Jacobson said. “And if you know Carrie, you know that she is completely allergic to superficiality.”

In her acceptance speech, Coon reflected on the importance of women’s stories, drawing inspiration from feminist literature and End Times Fascism, a book by Naomi Klein and Astra Taylor.

“Patriarchy, capitalism...these are hierarchical systems of domination that rely on fear,” she said. “And in this paradigm, women and girls, like our natural resources, are a disposable commodity—and as such, according to philosopher Kate Manne—our stories are interchangeable and of equal power. But of course, they’re not. That’s not true. And we’re not a monolith.”

Coon went on to discuss the performances of her fellow honorees, arguing that their work represented something extraordinary. “In these women, I see the fight for the living world. I see moral clarity. I see imagination. I see courage,” she said. “And these are, in fact, the antidotes to fascism. That’s how important this storytelling is.”

Toward the end of her speech, Coon questioned how women in Hollywood could create lasting change beyond events celebrating their individual achievements.

“How do we forge our collective safety and solidarity in an ethic of communalism?” she asked. “Okay, so this is the part of the speech where I don’t actually have the answer to that question, but I make the appeal that nights like these shouldn’t be one-off nights.”

She concluded with the aforementioned proposition for her peers: “And so I’m going to start with a really stupid question, but I’m not afraid to ask a stupid question: Can we start a studio?”

9 Classics Where the Audiobook Version Is Better

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Audiobooks were technically born as far back as 1877, with Thomas Edison's invention of the phonograph. However, it wasn't until 1932 that the American Foundation for the Blind officially named them "Talking Books," introducing them as a medium for visually impaired people to enjoy literature as an alternative to Braille. Throughout the entire 20th century, and even well into the 21st, many classics have been turned into audiobooks so phenomenal that they're arguably even better than the original book.

Audiobooks and physical books are, of course, vastly different media that fulfill vastly different purposes, so comparing them can feel a bit like comparing apples to oranges. Still, sometimes, an audiobook is so good that it becomes entirely reasonable to say it provides a better experience than the original. These are audiobooks that anyone who enjoys classic literature should consider essential listening material. "Classic" is defined for the purposes of this list as any book that was originally published before the 21st century.

Folkloric vampires predate Bram Stoker's Dracula by hundreds of years. Inspired by such stories, as well as by Irish folklore and historical figures like Vlad the Impaler, Stoker wrote the 1897 classic that is nowadays widely remembered as the most foundational pillar of modern vampiric fiction. It's undoubtedly one of the best horror books of all time, which made the announcement of an original Audible audiobook-slash-audio-drama all the more worthy of skepticism.

In the end, however, Bram Stoker's Dracula ended up being one of the greatest horror audiobooks that readers have been treated to in years. With a stacked cast that includes the likes of Ella Purnell, Jonathan Bailey, and Aimee Lou Wood, the audiobook takes the classic epistolary novel and makes it feel even more immersive, cinematic, and well-paced. It's worth noting that it's an abridged audio drama rather than a full reading of the original novel, which is not ideal, but those interested in such a concept should love it.

Originally serialized between 1875 and 1877, Russian author Leo Tolstoy's Anna Karenina is widely regarded as one of the most foundational pieces of world literature in history, a must-read masterpiece whose daunting 1,000-page length shouldn't deter anyone from considering it essential reading. Since it was one of her favorite books of all time, Maggie Gyllenhaal agreed to narrate an Audible original audiobook that really does justice to one of the best epic books of all time.

Tolstoy's novel was such a monumental artistic achievement that the author was notoriously drained and exhausted by the whole experience by the time he was done, and reading such a vast book can itself also be a massive undertaking. With the raw immediacy and many emotional variations of Gyllenhaal's narration, listening to Anna Karenina can be a far more engaging and far less tedious experience than many may initially think. At over 30 hours long, it's definitely a time commitment, but oh so worth every minute.

Upon publication back in 1997, J. K. Rowling's Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone (published in the US as Harry Potter and the Sorcerer's Stone) was an immediate commercial and critical hit, kicking off one of the biggest multimedia franchises in history. The book's first audiobook version came out in 1999, and the performers varied by region. Whereas Jim Dale narrated the book in the US and Canada, it was Stephen Fry's voice that was heard in the British and international releases. If there is any one Harry Potter book that's better heard than read, it's Fry's performance of Philosopher's Stone.

As a whole, this is one of the most essential fantasy books to read before kicking the bucket, but there's nothing quite like hearing it through Fry's rich, velvety baritone voice. On the page, Philosopher's Stone is beautifully imaginative and cozy, but at times feels like an overly simple debut novel in terms of Rowling's prose. Fry elevates every word and makes the entire story feel like a wonderful fairy tale being told by the fire on a cold weekend night outdoors.

One of Stephen King's first novels was 1983's Pet Sematary, published before he had fully established and cemented himself as the true King of Horror. Fans had been begging for an audiobook for years, and once the project finally materialized, it was Michael C. Hall of Dexter fame who publisher Simon & Schuster picked as the right voice to narrate the story. The result is one of the best Stephen King audiobooks to date, and considering just how many great ones there are, that's saying a lot.

Pet Sematary was already absolutely legendary well before 2018, widely regarded by many—including King himself—as the author's most genuinely terrifying work. However, once the horror aspects really pick up steam after a relatively slow-burning first few pages, Hall makes it hit even harder. Getting an actor well-known for projects as death-related as Six Feet Under and Dexter to narrate a horror novel all about death and grief was not only brilliant meta-casting, but also hugely beneficial for the story as a whole.

Multiple books are almost always brought up when the endless debate of what "The Great American Novel" is gets brought up, but John Steinbeck's East of Eden is the most noteworthy. This family saga was likely key to Steinbeck being awarded the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1962, and it more than lives up to its reputation as one of the most perfect American books ever written. The first audiobook version of the classic ever recorded was narrated by respected character actor Richard Poe.

East of Eden was already widely recognized as one of the most perfectly written masterpieces of all time, and it's definitely a must-read, but there's something about the way Poe complements the narrative that makes his version arguably even more unforgettable. Steinbeck wrote East of Eden in such a sweeping, almost Biblical voice that it almost feels like the narration of a legend, and Poe's resonant yet deeply human voice allows that narration style to feel all the more intimate and personal.

The book that earned Harper Lee a Pulitzer Prize was 1960's To Kill a Mockingbird, one of the most essential and widely discussed American classics of the 20th century. Though certain elements of its narrative are undeniably dated by modern standards, its heart will forever be in the right place, with its critiques of racial prejudice and systemic injustice being as timely and hard-hitting today as they were back in the '60s. In 2014, legendary actress Sissy Spacek was tasked with narrating the audiobook version of the story.

The reasons why this is one of those great books that are even better as audiobooks are aplenty, and even though the Pulitzer-winning original remains absolutely foundational, Spacek brings a level of nuance and authenticity to the book that feels like a much-welcome sense of modernity. The actress' beautifully melodic Texan drawl makes the small-town Alabama setting feel all the more evocative and atmospheric, and the way she flawlessly captures Scout's personality as both a child and an adult is incredible.

Stephen King's 22nd book was 1986's It, still remembered by many as the author's best and most iconic work to date. At over 1,100 pages in length, it's an absolute brick of a horror novel, but its epic scope still works marvelously all these many years later. To narrate the 2016 audiobook, Simon & Schuster decided to go for an actor who already had a pre-existing tie to the Stephen King universe: Steven Weber, who played Jack Torrance in the 1997 three-part miniseries version of The Shining.

It was already one of those horror books where every chapter is perfect—something quite admirable for such a thick novel—but Weber took that literary doorstop and somehow turned it into an even bigger, more immersive 45-hour juggernaut of an audiobook. The actor gives each of the Losers a distinct voice both in their child and adult versions, and he goes all in on Pennywise without ever teetering too far into distracting camp. Perfectly paced and intense to the core, It is a must-listen-to audiobook through and through.

American poet, essayist, and civil rights activist Maya Angelou was a towering figure in modern American literature, completely reshaping how Black American identity was expressed in the written word. The first of her seven published memoirs, 1969's I Know Why the Caged Bird Sings, broke new ground and became one of the first major autobiographies by an African American woman to achieve global bestseller status. When an audiobook version was released in 2011, it was Angelou herself who narrated it.

Taking one of the best memoirs of all time and turning it into an audiobook worthy of its legacy was always going to be a tough task, so who better to achieve it than the author herself? By having Angelou narrate her own story with her unmistakably theatrical contralto voice, I Know Why the Caged Bird Sings feels even more honest and intimate than it already does on the page, balancing poignancy and humor to absolute perfection.

Well before he wrote what many still consider the greatest and most influential fantasy epic of the 20th century, J. R. R. Tolkien published the fantasy children's classic The Hobbit, almost inarguably the most perfect fantasy novel of the 1930s. There are multiple audiobook versions of The Hobbit available, but one reigns supreme as perhaps the audiobook that most elevates the experience of the original text: the 2020 version narrated by Andy Serkis.

It's easily one of the best audiobooks of all time, with Serkis not only delivering a much-expected powerhouse performance as Gollum, but also giving each of the other characters a distinct voice that makes them even more memorable than they are on paper. The Hobbit is also filled with songs and poems, and Serkis brings them to life in a way that reading the physical book simply can't match. In Serkis' voice, The Hobbit really does feel like the wonderful myth that it was always meant to be.

Nvidia’s interim CEO Colette Kress takes on export compliance as Washington turns up pressure

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Nvidia’s national security and trade compliance teams will report directly to interim CEO Colette Kress and a new board oversight committee on day-to-day operations as the AI giant faces heightened scrutiny from Washington. Compliance has become a critical issue for Nvidia and  for Kress, who is also the company’s longtime finance chief, as questions swirl over the potential diversion of its advanced AI to China.

The company said Tuesday that its board has formed a National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee to oversee national security policies, export-control compliance and global trade risks. Suzanne Nora Johnson will chair the panel, joined by directors Stephen C. Neal and John O. Dabiri. The committee will meet at least quarterly and work with independent experts.

Board Chair Harvey Jones said the committee would work to establish and advance a robust framework to “safeguard national security while supporting continued technological innovation.”

The move comes amid heightened U.S. scrutiny of access to advanced AI chips. The Commerce Department’s Bureau of Industry and Security announced Monday an interim final rule addressing remote access to advanced computing technology through overseas data centers. The rule, developed at the direction of the Treasury Department and the National Security Council, aims to prevent companies in restricted countries from bypassing export controls by accessing computing power remotely through data centers in third countries.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has publicly criticized Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick over the department’s handling of Megaspeed, a company suspected of facilitating the diversion of Nvidia AI chips to China. Megaspeed has also come under scrutiny following revelations about former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang’s relationship with Alice Huang, a former Megaspeed executive whom U.S. authorities suspect of having ties to Chinese intelligence.

“Treasury, War, USTR, and State were in favor of putting Megaspeed on the entity list for months,” Bessent told CNBC on Friday. “But Commerce has the pen.”

The central export-control question is whether advanced Nvidia chips supplied through Megaspeed were diverted to China. U.S. rules have allowed certain data centers outside China to provide remote computing services to Chinese customers, but that is distinct from the unauthorized transfer of restricted physical chips, Fortune reported.

On Sunday, Bessent further intensified scrutiny of Nvidia’s compliance practices, questioning whether potential violations of U.S. export restrictions were isolated incidents or reflected broader problems within the company.

“I can tell you that if this were a bank and they hadn’t complied like this, the OCC [Office of the Comptroller of the Currency] would be in there right now and a lot of the executives would be removed,” Bessent said on Fox News’ Sunday Morning Futures.

Nvidia’s 2026 proxy statement, published in May, says the company “does not have a chief compliance officer.” However, that does not mean it lacks a compliance structure.

The proxy describes an enterprise risk management process reviewed by the CEO, other named executive officers reporting directly to the CEO, including Kress, Debora Shoquist, executive vice president of operations, and Timothy S. Teter, EVP, general counsel and secretary, as well as other senior management members. These leaders are responsible for managing and monitoring risks within their respective areas.

Nvidia’s Nominating and Corporate Governance Committee also has explicit board-level oversight responsibility for trade compliance, regulatory matters and related risks, including issues at the center of the Megaspeed controversy. The five-member committee includes board chair Jones and is chaired by Neal.

The newly established National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee adds another layer of board-level scrutiny, and puts export controls and national security risks firmly on the agenda as Nvidia navigates mounting pressure from Washington.

Sheryl Estrada is a senior writer at Fortune, where she covers the corporate finance industry, Wall Street, and corporate leadership. She also authors CFO Daily.

The Social Reckoning Digital Release Date Revealed For David Fincher Companion Thriller

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

After its October theatrical release, The Social Reckoning is coming home to digital platforms later this month.

The Aaron Sorkin-directed and -written film is a companion to David Fincher's hit 2010 biopic The Social Network. It was released in theaters on October 9 but didn't have the same financial or critical impact as its predecessor. The Social Reckoning only grossed $18 million at the box office and proved to be very divisive among critics, who gave it a 54% on Rotten Tomatoes (though audiences were much more receptive with a 71% score).

For those who didn't see it in theaters, the movie will soon be available to watch from the comfort of home. The Social Reckoning's digital release date is set for November 24 and then will come out on Blu-ray on January 12, 2027. Sony is also releasing a two-pack bundle that includes both The Social Network and The Social Reckoning.

Both the Blu-ray and digital releases will include special features like a four-part series called Moving the Needle — The Making of The Social Reckoning, featuring the cast and crew, and True Integrity — Meet Frances Haugen, with the whistleblower opening up about her life and what led her to speak out about Facebook. The Blu-ray will also have a digital code that can be redeemed on Movies Anywhere.

The first film, The Social Network, was met with great fanfare when it was released in 2010. With Jesse Eisenberg playing Facebook founder Mark Zuckerberg in the early years of the social media platform's existence, alongside a cast that included Andrew Garfield, Justin Timberlake, Armie Hammer, Brenda Song and Rashida Jones, the film earned overwhelmingly positive reviews and is Certified Fresh on Rotten Tomatoes with a critic score of 96% (and audience score of 87%).

While in theaters, it grossed $225 million and went on to win Best Adapted Screenplay, Best Original Score and Best Film Editing at the Oscars. The Social Network also won Best Motion Picture – Drama, Best Director and Best Screenplay at the Golden Globes.

In the years since then, Facebook and parent company Meta have become much more controversial. A lot of those scandals are the center of The Social Reckoning, with Facebook engineer Frances Haugen becoming a whistleblower and revealing internal documents and secrets to journalist Jeff Horwitz about what's really going on at the tech company.

Eisenberg did not return as Zuckerberg for the companion film. Instead, Jeremy Strong stepped into the shoes of the controversial figure, with Mikey Madison playing Frances Haugen and Jeremy Allen White starring as Jeff Horwitz. Other cast members include Wunmi Mosaku, Betty Gilpin, Billy Magnussen and Bill Burr.

Behind the scenes, Fincher didn't return to the director's chair. Sorkin, who wrote the script for the first film, handled both screenwriting and directorial duties this time around. Lauren Lohman, Roger McNamee, Ellen Goldsmith-Vein, Broderick Johnson and Andrew A. Kosove served as executive producers, with Sorkin, Todd Black, Peter Rice and Stuart Besser as producers.

Even when a film doesn't perform well in theaters, it always has a chance at redemption when it's released on digital platforms and streaming services. The Social Reckoning has major star power both in front of and behind the camera, which could help draw interest in the film when it's available to buy and rent at home, despite an underwhelming theatrical run. Time will tell if the biopic hits the streaming charts and has a redemption arc.

The Social Reckoning will be released on digital platforms on Tuesday, November 24, 2026, and on Blu-ray on Tuesday, January 12, 2027.

Kalshi paused Trump mention markets at CFTC's request over manipulation risk

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The CFTC has stayed certification of some Kalshi mention contracts, citing possible manipulation, two months after a staff advisory

Kalshi halted trading on several contracts centered on President Donald Trump's remarks late last week after the Commodity Futures Trading Commission asked it to, according to CNBC.

The paused contracts invite traders to bet on Trump's use of nicknames ahead of 2027, his word choices in November, and the countries and companies he mentions before this month closes, according to CNBC.

Brooke Nethercott, a CFTC spokesperson, told CNBC the agency has put a hold on certifying several of the mention markets Kalshi has proposed. She pointed to a contract on which key words Trump would utter ahead of a set deadline, one that Kalshi self-certified in February 2025.

"The CFTC stayed the certification of these contracts because they may be susceptible to manipulation, which is consistent with the staff advisory the CFTC put out two months ago," Nethercott said, according to CNBC.

Staff of the CFTC's Division of Market Oversight issued the advisory on Sept. 22. It covers contracts that settle on whether an individual will say certain words, attend an event or interact with another person. The staff said they may view such contracts as presumptively readily susceptible to manipulation and expect exchanges to make a heightened showing to list them. The advisory states it represents only the views of staff and does not create new obligations.

The advisory says those closest to the outcome often have advance knowledge, such as access to scripts or prepared remarks, and can be targeted by others seeking to influence the result.

Kalshi and the White House did not respond to CNBC's requests for comment.

The CFTC was looking into mention markets internally in August, according to CNBC. The agency asked Kalshi to pull its mention markets tied to sports; CNBC describes the company as the sole U.S.-regulated exchange that lists this kind of contract. Kalshi complied.

A mention market tied to Trump came under scrutiny in July, when a White House teleprompter operator was found to have traded on the president's comments, according to CNBC. In a settlement, the CFTC ordered the operator to stop trading for three years and pay a penalty of more than $172,000.

A mention market on what Vice President JD Vance will say at the New York Times DealBook Summit on Wednesday was active as of Tuesday morning, according to CNBC.

Why iPhone Duo wasn’t for me (but might be for you)

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

I’ve had the iPhone Duo for nearly two weeks now, and have officially decided to return it. Here are the three main reasons why the Duo didn’t work out for me, but might for you.

I use my iPhone one-handed all the time, and based on my experience, iPhone Duo is clearly intended to be used with two hands. You can use it one-handed, but for me it’s not nearly as comfortable as the iPhone 18 Pro. It’s too wide and too heavy to be used for long.

Perhaps over time I’d get used to needing two hands for most iPhone interactions. But that would be a pretty significant shift for me, as there are so many situations when I don’t have both hands free.

If you already use two hands with your iPhone regularly—perhaps because you have a Pro Max model—then the Duo’s one-handed experience likely won’t bother you. But the more I used the Duo, the more I found myself missing my iPhone 18 Pro.

The biggest value proposition of a foldable is that it’s a 2-in-1 product, letting you have a standard phone but also a tablet-sized display that fits in your pocket.

I totally get the appeal, and fully expected to love that hybrid design. But after two weeks of trying, I still struggled to find times when I actually wanted to unfold the Duo. And to be clear, that’s a me problem, not an issue with the Duo itself.

I tried a bunch of apps on the inner display, and struggled to find use cases that—for me—made it worthwhile to open the Duo. But based on the feedback I’ve seen from most Duo owners, I’m very much in the minority.

I know lots of people love the Duo for video, but I don’t watch much video on my iPhone. I almost never watch YouTube, TikTok, or Instagram Reels. And for shows and movies, I stick with my TV screen and an Apple TV 4K.

Multitasking support on iPhone Duo works really well. But when I found myself needing to multitask, I still reached for my iPad Pro with Magic Keyboard attached. A keyboard-free product can’t replace my main computer (nor would I expect it to), and the Duo screen was too small to comfortably work on it.

Last year I bought an iPhone Air and absolutely loved it. Initially, the Air’s less capable camera system didn’t bother me, it was “good enough.”

After a few months though, I started missing the Pro camera system more. Then I learned that my wife was pregnant with twins, and I knew I wanted the best possible iPhone cameras to capture their early weeks and months. So I bought an iPhone 18 Pro in September.

I’ve been very impressed by iPhone 18 Pro’s Main camera upgrades, and it’s great having a Telephoto lens again too. Since having the best camera system is so crucial for me right now, I knew iPhone Duo couldn’t be my only iPhone.

I initially planned to dual-carry the two models, using iPhone 18 Pro solely as a camera. But over and over I found myself wishing it was my only iPhone.

Is the iPhone Duo camera good enough for most people? Probably so. But for this year at least, with my twins aging rapidly, it’s not quite right for me. If I loved the Duo for other reasons, I would just use both models. But since I didn’t, I’m happy to stick with the iPhone 18 Pro.

In closing, I want to explicitly state that I don’t think the iPhone Duo is a bad product, and this isn’t a “bad review.”

The iPhone Duo just isn’t for me. I get the sense, though, that it’s lots of people’s favorite iPhone in years. Apple created its most unique new iPhone model since the original, and there’s plenty to love about it. I’m very curious to see how popular foldables become over time compared to slab models, because each clearly offers pros and cons. But for me, at least right now, the iPhone 18 Pro is my model of choice.

The failure of a Vulcan Centaur rocket carrying a full load of internet satellites for Amazon was caused by an anomaly in its first-stage booster, rocket maker United Launch Alliance said today (Nov. 17).

That failure — which led to the loss of 40 Amazon Leo internet satellites — was recognized about 10 minutes after liftoff, but the problems began much earlier than that, ULA said in a statement.

"While we appear to have lost telemetry and the mission at approximately 10 minutes into flight, early indications are that just after liftoff the launch vehicle experienced low booster performance, resulting in stage separation occurring when the vehicle was low in altitude and below minimal velocity to reach orbit," ULA wrote in today's update.

The two-stage Vulcan Centaur is the successor to ULA's workhorse Atlas V rocket, which will retire after just six more flights. Vulcan debuted in January 2024 and now has a total of five launches under its belt.

The new rocket has experienced problems on three of those missions. On both its second and fourth flights — which launched in October 2024 and February of this year, respectively — the nozzle of one of Vulcan's strap-on solid rocket boosters (SRBs) fell off about 30 seconds after liftoff.

On both occasions, the rocket overcame the problem and successfully delivered its payloads to orbit. However, after the second occurrence, the U.S. Space Force paused national-security launches with Vulcan until ULA addressed the SRB nozzle issue. (The February 2026 flight was a Space Force mission.)

That work led to "design and process improvements to the nozzle," ULA said in an Oct. 28 update. And those tweaks apparently worked.

"All indications are that the solid rocket booster performance was nominal, following nozzle improvements implemented for this launch," the company wrote in today's update.

Vulcan's Centaur V upper stage, which features two RL-10C engines, did its job as well "but was unable to recover from the [booster] performance loss, resulting in loss of mission," ULA added.

Vulcan's first-stage booster is powered by two BE-4 engines, which are built by Amazon founder Jeff Bezos' Blue Origin. Today's update did not identify the exact problem with the booster; doing so will require more time, as the investigation has just begun.

If that investigation finds a serious issue with the BE-4, there could be broad ripple effects across the American space program. After all, that engine also powers the first stage of Blue Origin's New Glenn rocket, which is a key piece of hardware for NASA's Artemis program of moon exploration. (And New Glenn is currently recovering from a failure of its own — a massive explosion on the test stand that occurred on May 28.)

NASA plans to integrate the Centaur V upper stage into its Space Launch System moon rocket, the vehicle that launches Artemis astronauts to space. So the fact that the Centaur performed nominally on Sunday is a bit of good news for the space agency.

Vulcan's grounding is a big deal, no matter what the anomaly investigation ends up finding. The U.S. doesn't have a surfeit of powerful rockets, after all, and two of them — Vulcan and New Glenn — are out of commission at the moment.

There's considerable pressure on both Vulcan and New Glenn to bounce back strong, for the nation faces an impending launch crunch in the next few years. SpaceX plans to retire its Falcon 9 and Falcon Heavy rockets and shift its launch load over to the fully reusable Starship megarocket. SpaceX is expected to prioritize its own needs in Starship's early days, using the vehicle primarily to build out its next-gen Starlink broadband constellation and Starmind AI data center network.

The Vulcan failure also impacts Amazon — specifically, the company's plan to build out Amazon Leo, which will compete with SpaceX's Starlink. Amazon has booked about 100 launches on a variety of rockets to build its constellation, which will eventually consist of more than 3,200 satellites. Thirty-eight of those bookings are for Vulcan; Sunday's launch was the first of those.

The other rockets Amazon has booked are the Atlas V, New Glenn, Falcon 9 and Arianespace's Ariane 6. About 400 operational Amazon Leo satellites have reached orbit to date across 14 launches (not including Sunday's failure).
