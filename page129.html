Koopmeiners scores at former club as Juventus beat AZ 2-1 despite trailing on expected goals

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Travel  |  Read Source →

Juventus won 2-1 at AZ Alkmaar on a night when Teun Koopmeiners faced his old club, even though the hosts dominated the expected-goals count. The result strengthens their league-phase position ahead of Sunday's Serie A trip to Fiorentina.

The trip to Alkmaar returned Teun Koopmeiners to his former club. His brother Peer was in the AZ side that Juventus then held off. UEFA picked out the family meeting in its matchday 4 round-up.

Kolo Muani supplied the pass for the opener. Nick Woltemade was credited with the assist for the second goal. Kolo Muani earned a rating of 8.61 from the data provider, with Koopmeiners on 8.2.

AZ pulled one back in the 88th minute. Mexx Meerdink scored from an Elijah Dijkstra assist. The hosts could not find an equaliser in the remaining minutes.

The home side had 56% of possession. They completed 523 passes to Juventus’s 378. Eleven of AZ’s attempts were blocked, against three of Juventus’s.

Both teams put three shots on target. Vicario made two saves for Juventus. AZ’s expected goals total points to the chances the hosts created without converting them.

The figures are consistent with a Juventus side defending a first-half lead. Spalletti’s team committed 20 fouls to AZ’s 13. Andrea Cambiaso, Jeff Ekhator and Juan Cabal were booked for Juventus. Wesley Hoedt was the only AZ player shown a yellow card. Damian Sylwestrzak refereed the match.

The victory was Spalletti’s second in a row in all competitions, following the league win over Napoli. It came after a run that included a 0-2 defeat to Genoa and a 0-3 loss to Lazio. Koopmeiners now has four goals for Juventus in all competitions in 2026, one more than the three he scored for the club in 2025, Sky Sport Italia noted.

For AZ, the result was a third straight defeat in all competitions. Losses to Ajax and Twente came before Thursday’s game.

This season’s league phase has 36 clubs, and each plays eight different opponents. It ends on Thursday 28 January 2027. Juventus host Omonia Nicosia on 26 November at 21:00 CET, while AZ travel to Sparta Praha. Juventus then visit Hapoel Beer-Sheva on 10 December and Ferencváros on 21 January 2027, both at 18:45 CET. They finish at home to Real Sociedad on 28 January 2027 at 21:00 CET.

Serie A comes first. Juventus travel to Fiorentina for matchday 11 on Sunday 8 November, with kick-off at 20:45. AZ host Groningen in the Eredivisie on the same day.

Leeds United remain interested in signing Liverpool midfielder Harvey Elliott after missing out on him over the summer, and the Yorkshire club are confident that a deal could be struck in January, according to Football Insider.

Elliott is currently on loan at Valencia, joining the Spanish club on transfer deadline day at the start of September after it was made clear to him that he isn't part of Andoni Iraola's plans at Anfield. The 23-year-old has made six appearances for Los Che so far, failing to contribute to a goal whilst being sent off in his last outing against Villarreal, which led to him receiving a 0/10 rating from one publication.

Valencia aren't said to be impressed with their loanee, and are reportedly planning to terminate his temporary spell in Eastern Spain once the winter window opens.

Leeds are preparing to swoop for Elliott, who is understood to be keen on a permanent move to another Premier League club.

Elliott has endured a turbulent few years since Jurgen Klopp left Liverpool. The creative midfielder was a regular under the German, making 53 appearances in the celebrated manager's final season on Merseyside.

However, Arne Slot was far less fond of the Fulham academy graduate, using him sparingly in his first season as Reds boss. The Dutchman granted Elliott just 18 Premier League appearances in the 2024/25 season, before sending him out on loan to Aston Villa for the duration of the following campaign.

Elliott had a miserable time in the West Midlands, appearing just nine times under Unai Emery, who effectively froze him out for much of the season.

He returned to Liverpool in the summer acutely aware that he wasn't wanted by new manager Iraola, but he only found an escape route from Anfield at the eleventh hour of the window, joining Valencia on loan.

Leeds were heavily linked with Elliott, but the Whites weren't able to agree a deal with the Premier League giants. Daniel Farke and the recruitment team at Elland Road haven't given up hope of landing the playmaker, however.

Football Insider report that Leeds are optimistic about their prospects of signing Elliott in January. They continue to monitor his situation at Valencia, which isn't said to be harmonious.

Top 12 most fouled players in the EFL Championship

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

With an ever-increasing emphasis on utilising set pieces throughout the footballing landscape, a player's ability to draw fouls and win menacing dead-ball situations is only growing in importance and the trend has not avoided the Championship.

Players who frequently win free kicks, of course, are often among the chief danger men for their side and strike the sort of fear into opponents that makes foul play a near-unavoidable necessity, and there are some at Championship level that routinely end up on the receiving end of some almighty tackles.

There are, however, some high-volume foul-winners that may just surprise you, too, as it's not just the lightning wingers, inventive playmakers or troublesome frontmen who draw the most illegal challenges in the second-tier.

With that in mind, then, Football League World has decided to explore the TWELVE most fouled players across the Championship right now, using data-led website PlayerStats for all accompanying statistics.

Getting the ball rolling in proceedings is Watford playmaker Iker Bravo, who has established himself as a rare bright spark at Vicarage Road and a thorn in second-tier defences aplenty since joining the Hornets from Udinese over the summer.

Still aged just 21, the Spanish youth international and ex-Bayer Leverkusen prodigy has translated some of his pedigree into a struggling Watford side and, with one goal and three assists on his stat-line to date, is often a threat for Championship defenders.

His close control and confident ball-carrying skills are two of Bravo's strongest qualities, and they can be too much to live with as the Hornets technician has won 23 fouls at an average of just over two per 90 minutes.

Another Spanish attacking midfielder lands next up in the form of Fer Lopez, with the Wolves sensation having lit up the Championship this season following the Old Gold's relegation from the Premier League.

Lopez, who moved to Molineux in a reported £19 million deal from Celta Vigo in the summer of 2025, is quite evidently too good for the level with six goals and three assists from his opening 13 appearances, and the classy creative kingpin's tricky flair, deft touches, line-breaking passing and venomous ball-striking mean he's one of the most feared forward players in the league right now.

Through AI, we'll rank the top 25 most intimidating football stadiums in British football, but which stadium comes out on top?

It's no surprise, then, that opponents are often desperate to prevent the Spaniard wreaking havoc, which is rarely quite enough in truth, and he's currently averaging 2.05 fouls per 90 minutes from 23 in total - representing the most marginal advantage over his fellow countryman in Bravo.

Glen Kamara, by contrast, is not of the maverick number-10 genre akin to Bravo and Lopez, and that makes it somewhat surprising that he's winning even more fouls than the gifted Spanish pair.

He still likes to get forward, of course, and the former Leeds United promotion winner's ability to dictate the tempo, shield possession, drive forward and break lines from deeper areas offers a welcome contrast to the steely presence of Martinique international Jonathan Varane in QPR's two-man midfield engine room.

Those attributes are why midfield counterparts frequently look to nullify Kamara before he makes something happen or distributes possession to one of the Hoops' key difference-makers in the final third, with the experienced midfielder having also won 23 fouls at a higher average of 2.24 p90.

The QPR star hasn't drawn quite as many fouls as his namesake down at Portsmouth, though, with Abu Kamara surfacing as one of the most fouled Championship players so far this season by being brought down on 24 occasions to date.

Interestingly, Kamara's average of 1.94 represents the lowest of the 12 listed players on a per-90 minutes basis, and the winger has endured mixed fortunes since sealing an emotional return to Pompey in the summer.

With zero goals and just one assist from 14 matches and more than 1,000 minutes of league action, the 23-year-old needs to refine his final ball but still strikes fear into opposing full-backs with his electric pace and quick, tricky feet.

Among the most fouled Championship players in recent years, it's no surprise to see Callum O'Hare make the cut after switching from Sheffield United to Wrexham at the end of the previous window.

Cunningly creative with the ability to ghost past opponents in tight pockets of space, the 28-year-old playmaker loves travelling upfield in possession and can win fouls as if they're going out of fashion - having already drawn 24 at an average well in excess of two per 90.

O'Hare, who recorded 16 goal involvements during an influential final campaign at Bramall Lane last time out before joining Wrexham for £7 million, often attracts strong challenges from opponents in their desperate efforts to prevent the talented playmaker from making things happen, although he's now set to spend the "foreseeable future" on the sidelines after recently suffering ankle damage.

One of just two defenders to feature is a former teammate of O'Hare's in Sam McCallum, whose resurgence at Sheffield United over the last twelve months has made the dynamic left-back increasingly difficult to face off against for his opposite number.

McCallum's forward-first approach, marauding overlapping runs and searing pace up and down the left-hand side mean his inclusion is perhaps less surprising than you would first think, and despite balancing minutes with Harrison Burrows, the ex-Coventry City defender is among the most frequently-fouled Championship players by drawing 24 at a superior average to O'Hare.

An ex-teammate of McCallum's and a current colleague of O'Hare at Wrexham, the aggressive nature and towering presence of Kieffer Moore has always rendered the evergreen frontman as a complete foul-magnet.

He's rarely guilty of committing them, and loves to engage in the dark arts of the game with physical battles and the odd stray elbow for good measure.

Crucially, though, Moore knows exactly how to win fouls and refuses to moderate his brawny all-out approach at the ripe old age of 34, receiving 27 fouls in fewer than 1,000 minutes of football.

Ironically enough, Jack Moylan could end up becoming the first Cardiff City player to score 20 times in a single season since Kieffer Moore's goal-laden debut campaign for the Bluebirds back in 2020/21, and much like the Welshman, he also knows a thing or two about drawing fouls.

Moylan, however, wins them through different means, with the Republic of Ireland international's ability to hold onto possession, drive forward and dumbfound defenders with no shortage of flicks and tricks simply proving too hot to handle in the Championship thus far.

A summer signing from Lincoln City, the versatile attacker has already cemented his worth among the second-tier's top dogs with nine goals - including a recent hattrick against title favourites West Ham United - and his high-volume shooting tendencies mean that defenders invariably try and stop him before it's too late.

Despite Moylan drawing close to 30 fouls and more than two per match, though, nobody in the Championship can contain the Irish sensation right now.

Next up, it's fellow Cardiff star Perry Ng to feature with an ever-so-slightly higher average than his teammate, and the defensive stalwart's inclusion admittedly represents more of a surprise.

Ng has the technical quality and composure on the ball to frequently advance forward from right-back and join attacking moves, often acting in tandem with lightning-quick winger Ollie Tanner in what is very much a possession-centred approach under Brian Barry-Murphy despite lacking the raw pace to burn past opponents.

He's very streetwise, however, and alongside being a regular customer for bookings, the 30-year-old excels in the dark arts and has quite the education when it comes to winding up opposition players and drawing fouls as a result.

That's why Ng, while resembling a different profile to many of his listed counterparts, is the joint-fourth-most fouled player throughout the Championship, having won no fewer than 28 at a rate of 2.19 p90.

There are no such surprises when it comes to Leo Scienza, who was the most fouled player in the entire division during the previous campaign and is right up there yet again this term.

One of the most technically-gifted footballers outside of the Premier League, the Brazilian winger's ability to glide past opposition full-backs with consummate ease makes him a joy to watch and often lights up Tonda Eckert's promotion-chasing Southampton side and, more often than not, subjects his unfortunate rival number to a 90-minute nightmare.

Scienza, who perhaps would've hoped to boast a greater production than his two goals and two assists apiece at this moment in time but has played the second-fewest minutes of anybody on this list, remains just as difficult to defend against when he gets going with his trademark breezing runs down the left flank, and has been fouled on 29 occasions at an average of just under three times a game.

Swansea City loanee is ready to break into the side after slow start: Opinion

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Swansea City have made a great start to this season and are one of the early title contenders. However, some defensive injury concerns have seen them fall away in the last four, losing three in that process.

Centre-back duo Ben Cabango and Stephen Welsh are both currently nursing knocks. Just as Cabango looks to be over those woes, Welsh went off with a knee injury in the defeat to Portsmouth, which is a blow but not as bad as first believed.

The Swans injury problems have created an opportunity for Jenson Seelt, and the early signs suggest the Dutchman has the attributes to make the most of it. The 23-year-old arrived on loan from Sunderland in August, providing Vitor Matos with some much-needed depth.

However, there is potential for the move to become much more significant for all parties involved.

Seelt is a physically imposing centre-back who offers aerial strength, aggression and reasonable composure in possession. Those qualities are particularly valuable in Matos’ system, which demands defenders who can contribute to the build-up while remaining comfortable defending aggressively and winning their individual battles.

The Swansea boss has already highlighted Seelt’s physicality, aerial dominance, composure and reading of the game. His early numbers suggest those qualities are translating onto the pitch.

According to FotMob, he ranks in the 79th percentile for aerial duels won and the 92nd percentile for defensive contributions among centre-backs. Across six Championship appearances and 230 minutes, Seelt has won 12 aerial duels at a success rate of 63.2 per cent.

He has also recorded 22 clearances, seven interceptions, 11 recoveries and 34 defensive contributions. His 82.4 per cent passing accuracy, alongside 16 line-breaking passes, also suggests he has the ability to contribute when Swansea are building from the back.

There is still plenty of work to do, however. Seelt has only started twice and needs a sustained run of games before any definitive conclusions can be drawn. Nevertheless, his parent club Sunderland clearly believe there is considerable potential to unlock.

The Black Cats handed Seelt a new contract until 2030 before allowing him to join Swansea, with director of football Florent Ghisolfi making clear that the defender remains part of their long-term plans.

If he can establish himself under Matos, improve his consistency and become a dependable presence in a side competing towards the top end of the division, he could return to Wearside in a much stronger position.

Swansea have acquired a defender whose physical profile suits their immediate needs, Seelt has the platform to prove himself, and Sunderland retain control of a promising young player. This loan could prove to be an extremely smart piece of business for all three parties.

Doc Rivers shares criticisms of Giannis Antetokounmpo's game − and how he can improve

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

If you ask Doc Rivers, Giannis Antetokounmpo is in the perfect place to take the next step in his career.

In the most recent episode of "The Bill Simmons Podcast," Rivers dished on his time with the Milwaukee Bucks, how he navigated the ill-fated Damian Lillard trade and the highs and lows that came with coaching the Greek Freak.

"I liked Giannis a lot. I liked him more as a dude," Rivers told Simmons. "He's interesting. He's not easy. He plays at this crazy downhill pace. He's really a difficult guy because he has this great passing ability, but he doesn't necessarily have super IQ at the same time. It's really a strange thing, and he really has one way. Downhill. Force, force. Every play, he wants to force it."

The number one thing Rivers emphasized was execution. Preparing the right way and setting his teammates up for success is the area the Hall of Fame coach believes Antetokounmpo fell short in during their time together. But now, with Antetokounmpo on the Miami Heat under Erik Spoelstra, Rivers is hopeful that will come to fruition.

"I wish he learned how to execute better," Rivers said. "He's one of those guys − you get a lot of them from the U.S., AAU guys − that have never needed to execute because they can do it on their own. But that doesn't help the team. And I told Giannis this, the thing he needs to still learn is the value of execution. How important it is when you're in practice and shootarounds to lock in on execution. And now he has a coach who is as good as there is in execution."

Antetokounmpo landed in South Beach this summer after a blockbuster trade that put an end to the long-running standoff between the 10-time All-Star and the Bucks front office, but it's been anything but greener pastures early on. The Heat are 3-6 in their first nine games despite him averaging 28.3 points and 12.5 rebounds a night.

The Golden State Warriors pulled away from a tie game against Miami at halftime to earn a comfortable 120-100 win Thursday, thanks in no small part to the ability of Draymond Green, Al Horford and Charles Bassey to contain Antetokounmpo to just one point in seven fourth quarter minutes down the stretch.
