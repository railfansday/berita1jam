TRX Tron (TRX) treasury firm Tron Inc. posted a higher third-quarter net income, as it continued to accumulate TRX tokens and saw rising staking income.

The company booked a net income of $14.9 million in Q3, up 22% year-on-year, according to its latest quarterly report released Tuesday. Its nine-month net income reached $39.4 million, up 203%.

The company's digital asset holdings rose to $257.8 million at the end of September. It increased its TRX holdings to 723 million TRX ($229.3 million) as of Tuesday, according to a post on X.

Notably, its unrealized staking income rose 41% year-over-year to roughly $3.18 million in the third quarter. For the first nine months of the year, its unrealized staking income more than quadrupled to $9.51 million.

"Unlike unrealized gains resulting from changes in digital asset market prices, staking income reflects rewards associated with the company's participation in Tron Network staking activities," the company said.

"Our strategy is expanding beyond holding digital assets to providing additional value-add services for the Tron network. Our election as a TRON Super Representative in October represents an initial step in positioning Tron Inc. for further infrastructure integration within the TRON ecosystem," Tron Inc. CEO Rich Miller said in the report.

Tron Inc. rebranded from SRM Entertainment in July 2025, shifting its focus from toy design and entertainment to crypto treasury management. It brought on Tron founder Justin Sun as an advisor.

TRX, which has a market capitalization of $30.1 billion, traded flat over the past 24 hours at $0.3172, according to The Block's price page. It has declined 5.5% over the last five months but remained up 11.5% year-to-date.

Meanwhile, Nasdaq-listed shares of Tron Inc. closed flat at $1.45 on Tuesday. The stock has risen 17.9% over the past month but remained down 33.5% over the past six months.

EU Seeks Experts to Help Set Tokenisation Standards as Financial Markets Move Onchain

TL;DR: European authorities are inviting nominations for a new Tokenisation Standards Group, with applications due on November 27. The effort is about practical coordination across the financial system, not a new approval for cryptocurrency tokens or a promise that all tokenized assets will be regulated alike.

Europe is trying to solve a problem that has followed tokenized finance from pilot projects into real markets: moving an asset onto a blockchain is only part of the job. Trading, ownership records, custody, corporate actions, payments, and reporting still need to work together.

In a November 9 announcement, the European Securities and Markets Authority said the EU was seeking nominations for a European Tokenisation Standards Group. The deadline for candidates is November 27, 2026, meaning the process remains in its membership-building stage.

A token might provide a fast record of a transfer, but its legal and operational meaning depends on the system behind it. If the asset represents a bond, someone must still ensure that the person receiving it has enforceable rights, that payments reach the correct holder, and that the records remain accurate when a key is lost or a transaction is reversed under applicable law.

Those tasks become harder when different issuers, custodians and trading venues use incompatible methods. Common standards can reduce integration work and make it easier for supervised firms to compare and connect systems. They do not eliminate risk, but they can help clarify who is responsible when something goes wrong.

That is why a coordination effort deserves attention even if it does not announce a flashy new chain or a major token launch. The goal is to make the plumbing of tokenized markets more predictable.

The NYSE’s proposed tokenized-securities venue is an example of how conventional-market institutions are exploring blockchain-based records while maintaining established securities functions. It is a different jurisdiction and project, but it points to the same practical issue: tokenization has to coexist with financial-market rules and infrastructure.

At the same time, Securitize’s work to expand tokenized assets across networks shows why interoperability matters to firms operating in more than one technical environment. A tokenization service that works on several networks can still face fragmented onboarding, settlement and compliance processes.

For European banks, asset managers and infrastructure operators, the payoff from consistent standards would be less duplicated work and more confidence in how instruments move between systems. Whether those efficiencies materialize depends on the resulting proposals and industry adoption.

This announcement should not be read as the EU approving all forms of real-world asset tokens, replacing securities law, or selecting a particular blockchain as a winner. Nor does a call for nominations itself establish binding technical requirements. It is an early process intended to bring stakeholders into the conversation.

Different asset classes could continue to require different handling. A tokenized money-market fund, for instance, has obligations that a privately issued collectible does not. Regulation, transfer restrictions and investor eligibility remain important regardless of how assets are represented.

November 27 is the immediate date to watch. After nominations are considered, the composition of the group and the work it chooses to prioritize will give a clearer indication of what practical standards could emerge.

The significance for crypto markets is less about speculative prices and more about infrastructure. If regulators and financial firms can agree on reliable operating conventions, tokenized finance may have a better chance of expanding beyond isolated tests and into services that ordinary institutions actually use.

Cognizant zet meer dan 25.000 professionals in voor de implementatie van Claude en introduceert een model voor beheerde diensten om Claude centraal te stellen in bedrijfsactiviteiten

📅 Nov 11, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

TEANECK, New Jersey, 11 november 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Cognizant (Nasdaq: CTSH) heeft vandaag de oprichting aangekondigd van de Claude Frontier Capability Group, een groep van meer dan 25.000 professionals die zich inzetten om Claude bij klanten te implementeren. De groep richt zich op een hoogwaardige uitvoering, transformatie en het succes van klanten en beschikt over de capaciteit om AI binnen de kernactiviteiten van ondernemingen van de pilotfase naar de productiefase te brengen.

Ondernemingen hebben toegang tot geavanceerde AI-technologie, maar vinden het nog steeds lastig om deze technologie om te zetten in concrete bedrijfswaarde. Cognizant heeft binnen zijn hele personeelsbestand expertise op het gebied van Claude opgebouwd. Meer dan 44.000 medewerkers in bedrijfsonderdelen en ondersteunende afdelingen zijn inmiddels opgeleid of gecertificeerd in het gebruik van Claude. De nieuwe groep is een gespecialiseerde organisatie die systemen op basis van Claude ontwikkelt en beheert binnen de bedrijfsomgeving van klanten.

Cognizant introduceert ook een model voor beheerde diensten voor Claude. Binnen dit model neemt Cognizant namens klanten het beheer van systemen op basis van Claude in productieomgevingen op zich, zodat ze zich kunnen richten op de bedrijfsresultaten die deze systemen mogelijk maken. Beide initiatieven bouwen voort op het Frontier-personeelsmodel van Cognizant, waarmee het bedrijf Frontier Certified Engineers en Frontier Business Operators opleidt die rechtstreeks door ontwikkelaars van geavanceerde AI-modellen worden gecertificeerd.

"Klanten willen een partner die de verantwoordelijkheid voor de uitvoering op zich neemt zodra AI binnen hun bedrijf wordt ingezet", aldus Ravi Kumar S, CEO van Cognizant. "We zetten meer dan 25.000 van onze medewerkers in voor Claude en nemen via beheerde diensten het beheer ervan voor onze klanten op ons. Daarmee investeren we fors in Claude en in de mogelijkheden die deze technologie ondernemingen biedt binnen hun kernactiviteiten."

"Alle ondernemingen proberen AI zo snel mogelijk in hun organisatie te integreren, maar medewerkers met de vaardigheden om AI in de praktijk binnen een bedrijf te laten functioneren, zijn steeds moeilijker te vinden", aldus Paul Smith, Chief Commercial Officer bij Anthropic. "Cognizant helpt dit tekort aan te pakken door meer dan 25.000 medewerkers in te zetten om klanten te ondersteunen bij het gebruik van Claude binnen hun hele organisatie."

Een model voor beheerde diensten voor Claude

Het model voor beheerde diensten van Cognizant ondersteunt klanten bij het gebruik van Claude in productieomgevingen. Het combineert gespecialiseerde medewerkers, toegang tot Claude op basis van licenties en het intellectuele eigendom van Cognizant, waaronder het AI Delivery Operating System. Het model biedt klanten beter voorspelbare AI-uitgaven en zorgt ervoor dat alle projecten worden uitgevoerd binnen beveiligings- en governancekaders op bedrijfsniveau, afgestemd op de specifieke vereisten van elke klant op het gebied van connectiviteit, architectuur en risicobeheer.

Vier prioriteiten, van het herontwerpen van bedrijfsactiviteiten tot AI-native softwareontwikkeling

De groep zal zich aanvankelijk richten op vier gebieden: het herontwerpen van bedrijfsdomeinen rond AI, het ontwikkelen en moderniseren van de ondersteunende systemen, het veilig en op grote schaal inzetten van AI-agenten en het integreren van AI-native softwareontwikkeling als kerncompetentie voor de dienstverlening. Voor de modernisering zet Cognizant speciaal ontwikkelde Claude-agenten in die de analyse en herstructurering van verouderde softwarecode en mainframecode versnellen. Samen zorgen deze vier gebieden ervoor dat Claude zich ontwikkelt van een experimentele technologie tot een integraal onderdeel van de bedrijfsinfrastructuur.

Deze verschuiving is al zichtbaar in de bedrijfsactiviteiten van klanten. Bij een wereldwijde farmaceutische fabrikant hebben door Cognizant ontwikkelde Claude-agenten voor de productievloer de stilstand met 35% verminderd en de tijd voor het oplossen van problemen teruggebracht van 90 tot 120 minuten naar 10 tot 15 minuten. Voor een media- en entertainmentbedrijf gebruikte Cognizant Claude om het opstellen van specificatiebladen met vereisten te automatiseren, waardoor de tijd tot marktintroductie werd verkort van 3 tot 4 weken naar ongeveer 15 minuten. Bij een Noord-Amerikaanse verzekeraar heeft automatisering op basis van Claude het opstellen van campagnebriefings en de ondersteuning bij pensioenregelingen met 40 tot 60% versneld.

Cognizant beschikt over meer dan 26.000 Claude-certificeringen en staat wereldwijd op de eerste plaats voor de certificering Claude Certified Architect: Foundations. Zoals aangekondigd in november 2025, rolt het bedrijf Claude uit onder zijn medewerkers, onder meer in ondersteunende afdelingen en teams voor engineering en dienstverlening.

The WandaVision Saga has come to a close with the final hour of the Disney+ series “VisionQuest.” But it seems as though the story of the ultrapowerful Scarlet Witch, the synthezoid Vision and their superpowered children will continue in the MCU.

The finale episode sees Paul Bettany’s Vision reunite with his and Wanda Maximoff’s (Elizabeth Olsen) grown-up sons, Ruaridh Mollica’s Tommy Maximoff (who was key to the series) and Joe Locke’s Billy Maximoff (whose eleventh-hour arrival completes the family reunion). Then, the post-credit scene replays the end of 2022’s feature film “Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness,” where Wanda eradicated the Darkhold from all of the multiverse before (seemingly) being crushed from within a mountain. However, this time, the scene continues with the appearance of Robert Downey Jr.’s Doctor Doom, who lifts the rubble off Wanda and addresses her.

“Scarlett Witch, awaken — your power will be mine,” Doom says, as Wanda gasps and opens her eyes.

The coda confirms fan speculation that Olsen’s Wanda would return as the Avengers face off against Doom in future Marvel projects, which could include “Avengers: Doomsday,” out Dec. 18.

Ahead of the finale, Marvel tweeted a message written in binary code, which fans were quick to decode as a quote from “WandaVision.” Though the tweet was written with a series of 0s and 1s, it translated to: “We could never truly leave each other, even if we tried.” (The line is from the show’s 2021 finale.)

Olsen first appeared as Wanda in the post-credits scene for 2014’s “Captain America: The Winter Soldier,” which set up her full-fledged debut in 2015’s “Avengers: Age of Ultron.” Despite her wide-ranging powers of telekinesis and mind manipulation, Wanda’s first forays in the MCU were laden with tragedy, including the death of her brother Pietro (Aaron Taylor-Johnson), the devastation of her home country of Sokovia in “Age of Ultron,” and then the death of her beloved Vision (Paul Bettany) in 2018’s “Avengers: Infinity War.”

That trauma led to 2021’s “WandaVision,” Marvel Studios‘ first TV series for Disney+, in which Wanda’s overwhelming grief led her to take over an entire New Jersey town and transform it into a recreation of American sitcoms — complete with a reconstituted Vision and two twin sons, Billy and Tommy. By the end of the series, she had to let go of Vision and her sons, all while her interactions with the witch Agatha Harkness (Kathryn Hahn) revealed Wanda to be the Scarlet Witch, one of the most powerful beings in the Marvel universe. Agatha’s witchy, post-“WandaVision” escapades continued in the 2024 Disney+ series “Agatha All Along,” which introduced Joe Locke as Wanda and Vision’s grown-up son Billy Maximoff.

Wanda’s story continued in 2022’s feature film “Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness,” in which her grief from the loss of her children led her to turn fully evil as the film’s central villain. She brutally killed anyone standing in opposition to her desire to find her boys within the multiverse, fueled by the corrupting book of magic, the Darkhold. By the end, Wanda understood all the harm she had caused, and she eradicated the Darkhold from all of the multiverse before (seemingly) being crushed from within Wundagore mountain.

When asked by Variety in 2022 whether she hoped to play Wanda again, however, Olsen sounded optimistic.

“It’s weird that I’m expecting to return, but no one’s told me I’m doing anything!” she said with a laugh. “But in my mind, I’m just making the assumption that they’ll have me again. I don’t know to what capacity, but I hope I’m back. I hope there’s also more fun to be had in something different.”
