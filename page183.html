Where does Kane rank among all-time England greats?

📅 Nov 12, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Harry Kane is now England's most-capped men's player as well as his country's record goalscorer, but harder to quantify is where these achievements place him among the Three Lions' greatest ever players.

This is a tricky debate with so many variables and a timescale as old as the game itself and, to my mind, not merely a determination of a player's individual ability but also a measure of their impact upon the nation's psyche.

Ballon d'Or winners Stanley Matthews, Kevin Keegan and Michael Owen would surely have a shout if the criteria was such, not to mention genius technicians such as Glenn Hoddle, Chris Waddle and John Barnes.

Likewise, if any England boss managed to extract the best from Paul Scholes, Steven Gerrard and Frank Lampard they would be surely making a classic late dart from midfield.

But to qualify as one of the greatest England players of all time, you need the above ability combined with an impactful overall contribution - those memorable moments and an element of longevity - in a Three Lions shirt.

It might be controversial then to leave out Geoff Hurst, who produced the most recognisable performance in England history, while the man he replaced, Jimmy Greaves, would be a shoo-in were it not for injury earlier in the tournament.

It is also hard to rank the contribution of those whose best football came outside of major tournaments and before the dawn of continental competitions - see Eddie Hapgood, Tom Finney, Nat Lofthouse, Dixie Dean et al.

There are only 10 spots, after all, so here we go.

Spice Boy. Celebrity. Galactico. There is no argument David Beckham is one of the most iconic England players of all time, but he backed it up on the pitch, too.

Beckham was the face of the Three Lions for a generation, and to his credit managed to overcome the vicious fallout of his red card against Argentina in 1998.

At times he seemed to single-handedly carry England, most notably scoring a 93rd-minute free-kick against Greece to qualify for the 2002 World Cup.

Beckham's former team-mate Gary Neville called him the "most influential player out of England in transforming football".

It's true. On top of 115 caps, Beckham was part of Manchester United's 1999 Treble-winning outfit - finishing Ballon d'Or runner-up - before joining Real Madrid.

His profile was truly global with spells at LA Galaxy, AC Milan and Paris St-Germain.

David Beckham scored 17 goals for England, including five free-kicks

There's arguably no England star fans forged an emotional attachment with more than Gazza - crying with him in 1990, rejoicing in his spectacular effort against Scotland and desperately willing him to connect during that Euro 96 semi-final defeat by Germany.

Gascoigne won 57 caps and scored 10 goals. At his peak, he was captivating - transcending football as a cultural phenomenon during the summers of '90 and '96, having joined a star-studded Serie A between those tournaments.

Creative, unpredictable, vulnerable. He was a loveable but flawed genius.

He suffered long-term injuries as England missed out on Euro 92 and the World Cup in the USA, but helped the Three Lions qualify for France 98 before being dropped from the final squad, famously wrecking boss Glenn Hoddle's office.

Gascoigne, Poborsky , McCoist - Goals that defined Euro 96

Goalscorers often grab the headlines but I've tried to credit players across positions and eras. From a defensive point of view Ashley Cole, arguably England's best-ever full-back, was close to making it.

But instead it's the great Billy Wright, the first men's player to surpass a century of international caps, including a remarkable 70 successive appearances over eight years.

Wright captained his country on 90 occasions, a joint-record recently broken by Kane, taking in three World Cups with impressive longevity for his era.

He was a true one-club man, winning three league titles, the FA Cup and becoming unofficial "world champions" with Wolves in 1954 - helping inspire the birth of the European Cup - while finishing Ballon d'Or runner-up to Alfredo di Stefano in 1957.

Billy Wright captained England 90 times - a record shared with Bobby Moore until Harry Kane recently surpassed the pair

It feels a little premature to include a 23-year-old with the bulk of his England career ahead of him, but that shows the impact Jude Bellingham has already made for England and Real Madrid.

A Champions League winner, the midfielder has also appeared at four major tournaments for the Three Lions, becoming the youngest player to do so at the delayed Euro 2020, aged 17.

But his role in helping carry England to the final two years ago and a third-place finish at last summer's World Cup really make him stand out.

Bellingham has delivered those moments a nation collectively remembers. The last-gasp overhead kick against Slovakia in 2024, a quickfire double at the Azteca against Mexico this summer and 93rd-minute quarter-final winner against Norway.

His seven goals at this World Cup were more than any previous Three Lions player in a single edition, the solo effort against France a thing of beauty.

The former Birmingham and Dortmund man doesn't turn 24 until June but has already collected 61 England caps and netted 14 times. As Roy Keane put it, Bellingham has the potential to be one of the "greatest of all time".

Ten of Bellingham's 14 England goals have come at major tournaments

In terms of natural ability, Wayne Rooney is among England's greatest ever talents.

Bursting on to the scene at Euro 2004 as an electric 18-year-old, having become England's youngest player and scorer the previous year, convinced fans and media the teen sensation could carry the Three Lions to glory in Portugal.

He bounced World Cup winner Lilian Thuram around in England's opener against France in a glimpse of his precocious strength, before scoring twice against both Switzerland and Croatia.

Who knows how far England would have gone had Rooney not been injured against Portugal in the quarter-final?

He was unable to replicate the impact of his first Euros - a red card at World Cup 2006, suspension for the Euro 2012 group stage, seven goals across six major tournaments - but was an all-rounder who brought more than just goals.

Rooney also won it all with Manchester United, becoming the club's all-time top scorer, and finished his 15-year England career with 53 goals from 120 caps.

Rooney burst on to the scene as an 18-year-old at Euro 2004

There has to be a place for a goalkeeper, and Gordon Banks is the safest pair of hands.

A World Cup winner in 1966, his save from Pele four years later against Brazil is one of the most iconic of all time. Pele called him a "goalkeeper with magic".

Banks spent the majority of his career at Leicester and Stoke, winning a League Cup with each, and earned 73 England caps overall between 1963 and 1972.

His incredible athleticism and reflexes saw the shot-stopper named Fifa's best goalkeeper for five successive years between 1966 and 1971.

Banks and Pele became good friends following the goalkeeper's iconic save in 1970

Gary Lineker's early club career did not deliver the silverware as his goals deserved, netting 40 times but finishing league and FA Cup runner-up in his one season at Everton before joining Barcelona.

He scored a Clasico hat-trick and won the Copa del Rey and Cup Winner's Cup, however, before adding an FA Cup with Tottenham in 1991.

Lineker netted 283 club goals (and famously no cards) in total across spells with Leicester, Everton, Barcelona, Spurs and Nagoya Grampus Eight.

But as a player Lineker is most renowned for his contribution in an England shirt, winning the Golden Boot as they reached the World Cup quarter-finals in 1986 - finishing Ballon d'Or runner-up that year - before netting another four as the Three Lions reached the semi-finals in 1990.

Lineker equalised against West Germany before England crashed out on penalties.

However, having missed a spot-kick against Brazil, he finished one short of Bobby Charlton's elusive goal record when his international career ended after Euro 92.

Gary Lineker scored 48 goals in 80 games for England

Harry Kane is undoubtedly England's most important and influential player of a generation - it is hard to imagine how this Three Lions side functions once the skipper hangs up his boots.

The Bayern Munich striker, complete with Ballon d'Or, has been scoring goals for his country for almost 12 years, the first just 79 seconds into his debut after coming off the bench against Lithuania in March 2015.

Rooney's goalscoring benchmark is long in his rear view mirror - you wouldn't bank against Kane doubling his nearest rival's figure once his England days are over.

They have come on the biggest stage, too - 21 in 36 appearances from three World Cups and European Championships apiece, winning the World Cup Golden Boot in 2018.

Kane has come closer to winning a major tournament than any England men's captain since Bobby Moore - losing two World Cup semi-finals and back-to-back Euros finals. Team-mate Bellingham called him the "greatest player that has ever played for England".

"I think we'll appreciate him even more in another 10, 15, 20 years when you look back and see what he has achieved," said ex-England goalkeeper Paul Robinson.

In many ways, Bobby Moore would be the complete modern-day defender. Cool as you like under pressure, elegant in possession, a great passing range off both feet and, as his West Ham and England team-mate Geoff Hurst said: "Nobody out-ran him and nobody out-jumped him."

Moore's positioning and timing was perfect, that iconic tackle on Jairzinho at the 1970 World Cup the finest example of his poise and precision when defending.

He was also a composed and influential leader. A player built for the big occasions. England boss Sir Alf Ramsey called him: "My lieutenant on the field, a cool, calculated footballer I could trust with my life."

The Barking-born centre-back won the FA Cup and European Cup Winners' Cup with West Ham but his finest moment was leading England to glory in 1966. He was only 25 at the time, having become his country's youngest captain three years earlier.

In 1970, after the Bogota bracelet incident, Moore was imperious as England reached the quarter-finals before bowing out to West Germany.

It was, however, the final group game against Brazil, the most exciting side on the planet, that produced some of the most recognisable and renowned images in sporting history - not only Moore's tackle and Banks' save but the defender swapping shirts with Pele after the final whistle.

"The greatest defender I ever played against," was the Brazilian's verdict.

Bobby Moore is the only man to captain the Three Lions to World Cup glory

For more than a generation, Sir Bobby's England goalscoring record remained unmatched. It took 55 years from his 49th and final strike against Colombia in 1970 until Rooney surpassed him with his half century in 2015.

Charlton was, as Alan Shearer put it, "not only a great goalscorer but a scorer of great goals" and played a pivotal part of the Three Lions' World Cup success in 1966, netting three times on the way to the final.

A scampering run from the halfway line followed by a trademark long-range strike into the corner at Wembley gave England's campaign lift-off against Mexico, before he scored twice against Eusebio's Portugal in the semi-finals.

Manager Ramsey called the buccaneering midfielder his "lynchpin" as England triumphed over West Germany in the showpiece and those performances helped Charlton win the Ballon d'Or later that year.

He'd be runner-up in successive years after that, too.

Charlton won 106 England caps and was a star for Manchester United, with Rooney also the one to pip his club-record 249 goals for the Old Trafford outfit.

A survivor of the Munich air disaster on 6 February 1958, which killed 23 people including eight players and three members of the club's staff, Charlton won three league titles, an FA Cup and most poignantly the European Cup in 1968.

"England's greatest ever player," Lineker previously told BBC Radio 5 Live.

"He was unique. Wherever you go in the world, even if they didn't speak the language, they knew two words - Bobby Charlton."

An Unemployment Labs analysis by Will DePue, published November 12, 2026, found initialization patterns consistent with NVIDIA’s export-controlled Blackwell GPUs in six open-weight models from DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, MiniMax and Ant Group.

The analysis associates DeepSeek-V4-Flash and MiniMax-M3 with initialization on B200 or B300 chips, and DeepSeek-V4-Pro, Kimi K3, Kimi K3.1-Pro and Ling-3.1-flash with GB200 or GB300 chips. The matches identify the hardware layout behind the starting weights; whether those chips also performed training is a separate inference.

Successive releases place the apparent changes in time. DeepSeek-V3 produced a Hopper reading in December 2024, followed by the two Blackwell-reading V4 models in April 2026. Moonshot AI’s Kimi K2.5 read as Hopper in January 2026 before K3 in July 2026; MiniMax-M2.7 and M3 followed the same pattern in April 2026 and June 2026, respectively; Ant Group’s Ling-3.0-flash and Ling-3.1-flash did so in August 2026 and October 2026.

The pattern was not universal. The newest readable models from Alibaba, Zhipu, Tencent, Xiaomi and StepFun produced Hopper rather than Blackwell readings.

The technique begins with weights, the numerical parameters that training adjusts. Before training, the PyTorch/cuRAND setup examined uses the Philox generator to produce repeatable values from a seed. GPU threads distribute those values among weight positions, and a sufficiently large initialization uses 2,048 threads per streaming multiprocessor (SM). Changing the SM count therefore changes where the same generated numbers land.

In the configurations tested, Hopper H100 SXM, H200 and H800 chips have 132 SMs and 270,336 threads; B200 and B300 chips have 148 SMs and 303,104 threads; GB200 and GB300 chips have 152 SMs and 311,296 threads. A reading identifies one of these groups, not a specific chip within it.

Weights reserved for words or symbols absent from training can preserve their initial values almost exactly, while trained weights may retain a weaker statistical relationship to those values. The investigators select a seed and layout on one set of weights, then test that unchanged combination on a separate set excluded from the search.

Under its statistical model, the report estimates that roughly 6 trillion seed-and-layout combinations would produce an expected strongest chance match of about 8 sigma, a statistical measure of distance from random variation. The strongest results for the identified models range from 49 to 1,385 sigma, and every individual test included reaches at least 20 sigma.

DePue’s physical-chip reproductions provided another check. Using DeepSeek’s recovered seed, an H100 reproduced DeepSeek-V3’s preserved values but not V4-Flash’s, while a B300 did the reverse. On the B300, unmodified PyTorch reproduced 461 of 469 selected V4-Flash rows with correlations above 0.99, including 445 complete bit-for-bit row matches. A rented GB200 reproduced all 13 preserved Kimi K3 rows with Moonshot’s seed; changing either recovered seed by one produced no matching rows.

The report says the method agreed with all 24 cases where a developer publicly named a specific model’s hardware, while cautioning that 11 involve step-zero checkpoints and related models are not independent. That makes the comparison a consistency check rather than a measured accuracy rate.

Kimi K3.1-Pro uses K3’s seed and may be a K3 checkpoint with further post-training. Assuming the initializing hardware also performed training, DePue estimates five distinct final runs at roughly 500 to 3,000 Blackwell GPUs each, generally over two to three months. The calculation divides training compute by chip throughput at 35% utilization, using DeepSeek’s disclosed token counts and weight-based token estimates for the other models. Applying the same arithmetic to DeepSeek-V3 produced 2,000 to 2,500 chips, compared with DeepSeek’s disclosed 2,048.

A match concerns initialization alone; it cannot inventory every processor used during later training. A lab could create initial values on one system and transfer them, mixed clusters could leave only some hardware traces, and a Hopper result does not exclude subsequent Blackwell training. Nor can weights establish chip ownership, location or acquisition. DePue’s report notes that Blackwell systems can be rented outside China and says this evidence alone establishes neither smuggling nor an export-control violation.

AMD’s MI300X is the identified hardware alternative. It normally uses 304 processing units but can be divided into two groups of 152 or configured to use 148, reproducing the layouts in physical tests. For DeepSeek-V4-Flash, 363 of 377 preserved values that differ between vendors matched NVIDIA and none matched AMD. That numerical test did not settle the other five models, although the report considers a specially configured AMD cluster very unlikely.

The report states that B200, B300, GB200 and GB300 exports to China require a U.S. license and that no such licenses have been reported; it also says the MI300X is controlled. The method itself requires public weights and a recognizable hardware-dependent initialization pattern. CPU initialization leaves no GPU layout, and Baidu’s PaddlePaddle generator does not carry the examined chip layout.

The analysis gives policymakers technical evidence of hardware access without supplying an enforcement conclusion: public weights can expose an initialization pattern, but determining whether export controls were breached would still require evidence about where the hardware was used, who owned it and how access was obtained.

The Justice Department and Europol said Thursday that Operation TorNado has produced 354 arrests and the seizure of more than $234 million in cash and virtual currencies, in what the FBI called the largest takedown yet of dark web drug vendors and buyers.

The announcement came from the department’s Joint Criminal Opioid and Darknet Enforcement team, or JCODE, an FBI-led group created in 2018. Officials said Operation TorNado is the sixth, and most extensive, internationally coordinated action since then, and that it builds on last year’s Operation RapTor. The sweep ran from May 2025 to October 2026.

Europol, which coordinated work with agencies in 14 countries, published a parallel statement the same day. The operation targeted vendors and buyers who had been active on five dark web markets already seized by law enforcement: Archetyp, Bohemia, Nemesis, Kingdom and Tor2Door. Officials also identified accounts on DrugHub and on forums, single-vendor shops and encrypted messaging services.

The seizures included about 5.7 metric tons of illicit drugs worldwide, among them fentanyl and other opioids, methamphetamine, heroin, cocaine, ecstasy and MDMA, plus 38 pill presses and 209 firearms. Neither the Justice Department nor Europol split the $234 million between cash and cryptocurrency, and neither named bitcoin or any other coin in the official totals.

Europol put the European share at more than €60 million in cash and cryptocurrencies, alongside 950 kilograms of drugs and 57 firearms, and the U.S. share at more than €150 million, or $166 million, in cash and cryptocurrencies, plus more than 4.8 metric tons of drugs and 152 firearms. Those regional money figures still combine cash and crypto. Europol said its forensic and blockchain analysis teams traced illicit transactions as part of the work.

The closest official window into how the vendors were paid comes from the cases the Justice Department highlighted and from court papers posted with the announcement.

Alexander Green, 32, of Jacksonville, North Carolina, was arrested in June and charged in the Southern District of Ohio with conspiracy to distribute controlled substances, including fentanyl. The department said he allegedly operated a vendor called STARLABS on DrugHub, which it described as a Monero-only dark web market, and that the group has been linked to two overdose deaths. A criminal complaint says undercover purchases from STARLABS were made “in exchange for cryptocurrency.” If convicted, he faces up to 20 years in prison.

In a separate Florida arrest, brothers Stanislav Chernyshov, 30, and Vladislav Chernyshov, 35, are charged in the Eastern District of Virginia with conspiracy to distribute Schedule I synthetic opioids. Prosecutors say they allegedly ran the vendor “BarbaraWhite,” selling counterfeit oxycodone pills laced with nitazenes and shipping more than 1,000 parcels across 49 states. A search-warrant affidavit describes bitcoin transfers tied to that account and the Nemesis market. They each face up to 20 years if convicted.

Operation TorNado led to 132 arrests in the United States, 59 in the United Kingdom, 45 in Hungary and 41 in Germany, along with arrests in France, New Zealand, the Netherlands, Slovenia, Austria, Ireland, Czechia, Australia and Finland. A number of investigations are still ongoing to identify the people behind remaining dark web accounts, the department said.

FBI Director Kash Patel called Operation TorNado “the largest interagency takedown of Dark Web drug vendors and buyers to date.” IRS Criminal Investigation Chief Jarod Koopman, whose agency was part of the JCODE group, put the financial work at the center: “Criminal networks may operate across borders and in the shadows of the Dark Web, but they cannot operate without money.”

Federal prosecutions are underway in more than 20 districts. An indictment or complaint only alleges that crimes were committed, and the defendants are presumed innocent until proven guilty.

What the announcement does not settle is how much of the $234 million was cryptocurrency, and how much of that was bitcoin. Officials have not said. The record on Operation TorNado shows a combined cash-and-crypto seizure, a Monero-only market in at least one featured case, and bitcoin transfers in court papers, without a breakdown of the total.

Is it a bird? Is it a plane? Is it a... flying Cybertruck? Nope, it's a modular flying car that will take to the skies next year.

When I first laid eyes on the Aridge flying car at the IFA consumer electronics show in Berlin this past September, I didn’t fully understand what I was looking at.

Taking pride of place on the stand belonging to Chinese robotics company Xpeng, there was a van that bore more than a passing resemblance to a Cybertruck. It didn’t have any wings, and it looked far too dense to fly. Sitting next to it was a kind of oversized drone with a cabin just about large enough to fit two people.

The two vehicles seemed like totally separate entities. It never would’ve occurred to me to assume they were two halves of one system – much less that the drone, with its substantial wingspan, could fold up to fit inside the van.

I now understand that this modular two-part flying car system is known formally as the Aridge Land Aircraft Carrier. The ground vehicle, also known as the mothership, is a chunky 5.5-meter-long van that can be driven with a standard license and fits in most parking spaces. It’s designed to store, carry and deploy the electric vertical takeoff and landing vehicle, or eVTOL, including its six foldable rotors, which can fly as high as a helicopter for 30km (about 19 miles).

This remarkable creation is the work of Deli Zhao, founder and president of Aridge, which is backed by robotics and auto company Xpeng. Speaking at Web Summit in Lisbon on Tuesday, Zhao presented himself as a man who was inspired to pursue a career building flying cars by Harry Potter and the Green Goblin, despite his lack of formal education.

“Dreams are very important,” Zhao said, as a video of him flying the eVTOL played on a screen in the background. Anyone can learn to fly the vehicle with just five minutes of training, he added. Or if you’re too tired to use the simple joystick, the eVTOL can fly itself autonomously.

“Everyone here can be a pilot,” Zhao said. “Everyone can be a captain.”

This sounds like a story from the very kinds of science fiction that sparked the creation of the Land Aircraft Carrier in the first place – and yet, it isn’t. It’s far from the first time I’ve seen a flying car concept at a tech event. They’ve been showing up for almost a decade at this point. But what marks this one apart is that Xpeng says it has already taken 17,000 orders for the vehicle across China, the UAE and other Asian markets.

“Our advantage is we’re closer to commercialization,” Zhao said. Xpeng has established the world’s first mass-production factory for flying cars in Guangzhou, with trial production beginning in November last year. Right now, it’s ramping up capacity and improving quality with the aim of eventually being able to build a flying car in 30 minutes.

Aridge has worked through many different concepts, including flying motorcycles, to finally find one ready for commercial production (plus it has a six-seater air taxi and integrated flying car in the works for the future too). If you have $300,000 going spare, the Land Aircraft Carrier could soon be yours.

The vast majority of preorders are from individuals, according to Xpeng, but the vehicle has also been designed for use in tourism and search and rescue. Zhao said it solves multiple problems with existing eVTOLs simultaneously: storage (it lives in the back of the mothership), recharging (it charges as the mothership is driving), transportation to and from appropriate flight sites, and ease of use.

“I hope to make flying accessible to everyone as soon as possible,” Zhao said. Right now, it takes around 20 to 30 hours of training to acquire the sport pilot license you’d need to fly the eVTOL. In China, Zhao said he’s in conversation with the regulator to reduce this, emphasizing his vehicle’s ease of use.

Zhao said he’s learned a lot from the aviation sector in China, where the Land Aircraft Carrier has gone through extensive safety testing and will likely take to the skies first as a consumer-owned product as soon as next summer. Growing its low-altitude economy, which encompasses drone delivery and eVTOLs, is national policy for the country – in spite of calls for more regulation after a deadly crash in Beijing this summer.

Xpeng is also eyeing the European market, although the regulatory hurdles are likely to be substantial. “Europe’s aviation sector is highly developed,” Zhao said, expressing his respect for the region’s strict rules, but adding that the market was important. The company will also start exploring certification in the US next year, he said. Actual progress will depend on the outcomes, he added.

In spite of Zhao’s boundless optimism, it’s difficult to buy into the hype around his flying car without witnessing it in action in person. I’ve now seen the Land Aircraft Carrier twice in different locations and videos of it in operation, but I want to observe the eVTOL’s deployment from the mothership and its airborne skills for myself.

At one point during his Web Summit talk, Zhao climbed into the cockpit of the eVTOL on stage. For a moment it seemed as though he might fly it right then and there inside Lisbon’s cavernous ME Arena. The audience raised their phones in unison, only to drop them again in disappointment when they realized Zhao wasn’t actually going to take off.

No matter how idiot-proof it might be to fly, it turns out European health and safety rules still apply.

California housing fights are increasingly happening before a vote, with nonprofits sending letters that cite HAA, density bonus law, and CEQA reforms. The goal is to warn officials and create a record that can be used if a city rejects or reduces a project.  AI Summary

A 135-townhome project cleared the Rancho Cucamonga Planning Commission on Tuesday on the planning department’s recommendation.

That is the outcome state reforms are meant to produce: more housing, including affordable units. Warmington Residential Homes’ project includes seven for very-low-income households. Still, the meeting showed how these disputes now run. Before a decision, a pro-housing nonprofit reminds the city to follow the law, creating a record it can cite if officials ignore the warning.

In Rancho Cucamonga, the California Housing Defense Fund sent a three-page letter citing the Housing Accountability Act, the state Density Bonus Law and last year’s California Environmental Quality Act reform. Rejecting the project or cutting its size, it warned, would require written findings of a specific health or safety hazard.

The Rancho Cucamonga letter was one of at least five CalHDF sent that day, along with letters to Santa Clarita, Whittier, Escondido and Twentynine Palms. Four concerned projects of 17 to 146 units. One addressed an ADU ordinance.

Those letters are a fraction of the total. James Lloyd, the organization’s director of planning and investigations, told HousingWire the number reaches about 100 a year.

They serve as a first step toward making sure cities implement state housing reforms properly, according to the organization.

Broadly, the letters pair legal warnings with a pitch to local officials: new housing helps a city’s finances and existing residents while meeting the state’s need for more homes.

“While no one project will solve the statewide housing crisis, the proposed development is a step in the right direction,” the organization writes.

Lawmakers have spent years trying to speed housing construction to improve affordability. Gov. Gavin Newsom signed Assembly Bill 130 to reform CEQA and SB 131 in 2025, exempting qualifying infill housing from environmental review and narrowing review for projects that nearly qualify. Legislators also tightened the Housing Accountability Act, which limits when cities can deny or shrink projects that meet zoning.

Neighbor suits have not disappeared. They have shifted to different arguments. Attorneys at the legal publisher CEB expect litigation to focus on whether a project qualifies for a CEQA exemption at all, rather than on the adequacy of an environmental report.

Builders and advocates pose a growing risk for cities that resist state law. Cities frequently lose when they test the rules in court, and the losses can be costly. Under Senate Bill 1037, penalties can reach $50,000 per unit for willful Housing Accountability Act violations.

La Cañada Flintridge refused to process a builder’s remedy project. A judge ruled against the city in March 2024, and the city dropped its appeal a year later rather than post a $14 million bond. It paid CalHDF $1,262,500 in fees and costs last year.

A small project in Pacifica shows the stakes. Neighbors sued over an eight-townhome project, arguing it needed a full environmental impact report instead of the lighter review the city used.

A trial court sided with them, rescinded the approvals and awarded nearly $1.3 million in attorney’s fees. In December 2025, an appeals court vacated the fee award, saying the judge had wrongly discounted the project’s housing value. It sent the question back to the trial court.

Neighbors of the Rancho Cucamonga project voiced concerns at community meetings this summer, raising traffic, wall height and privacy. Staff added conditions on construction parking and frosted windows.

But when it came to a recommendation, staff tracked the laws CalHDF cited. They wrote in their staff report that the project qualified for waivers under the state density bonus law, which limits when a city can deny them. Staff also noted the project was exempt from environmental review under CEQA guidelines.

The Trump administration is extending its moratorium on new Medicare enrollments from hospice and home health agencies.

The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services said Thursday that it will extend the moratorium by six more months as part of its efforts to combat fraud, waste and abuse in the Medicare program.

CMS said that the initial moratorium window has allowed the agency "to conduct targeted investigations and deploy advanced data analytics" to identify potential cases of fraud. An extension was deemed necessary to continue that effort, as the "circumstances warranting the imposition of the moratoria have not yet abated."

The agency said that hospice and home health have long been considered high risk areas for fraud.

“We’ve made real progress over the past six months, but fraud in hospice and home health persists and our work isn’t finished,” said CMS Administrator Mehmet Oz, M.D., in the announcement. “Medicare beneficiaries deserve protection from these bad actors, and American taxpayers need to know the programs they rely on aren’t being bled dry by fraudsters.”

Like the original moratorium, the extension applies nationwide. However, the extension will allow currently enrolled hospice providers or home health agencies to add new practice locations to their existing enrollment in Medicare. Providers currently enrolled in Medicare will not be impacted and can continue to provide services to beneficiaries.

The Trump administration has issued a six-month moratorium on hospice and home health agencies enrolling in Medicare as part of its efforts to combat fraud.

The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services said in an announcement on Wednesday morning that the "data-driven" decision targets a key source of fraud activity. It follows a similar announcement made earlier this year of a moratorium on durable medical equipment, prosthetics, orthotics and supplies companies.

During the six-month window, CMS said it will focus on identifying and investigating bad actors and deploying advanced analytics that help accelerate the removal of hospice and home health providers that are likely perpetrators of fraud.

CMS added that recent action under the broader White House fraud push has suspended payments to 773 hospice providers and 23 home health agencies in the Los Angeles area, totaling about $70 million in funds.

“We’ve seen systemic and deeply troubling fraud in the hospice and home health space, with bad actors exploiting some of our most vulnerable Medicare patients and stealing money from the American taxpayer,” CMS Administrator Mehmet Oz, M.D., said in the agency's press release. “Today we’re shutting the door on fraud—preventing new bad actors from entering Medicare while we aggressively identify, investigate and remove those already exploiting them."

"This is about protecting patients, restoring integrity and safeguarding taxpayer dollars," Oz said.

CMS said that the moratorium will not affect providers currently enrolled in Medicare, and they can continue to offer key services to members. It will apply, however, to new applicants for participation and "certain changes in majority ownership," which CMS said can be used to "obscure control by bad actors."

The moratoria will also prevent suspected fraudsters from evading punishment by moving across state lines, CMS said.

The announcement, the agency said, builds on a number of steps CMS has taken of late to address fraud in hospice and home healthcare, including increased oversight in states with elevated risk of fraud, such as Arizona, California, Georgia, Ohio, Nevada and Texas.

CMS is also conducting hospice site visits across the country to monitor operations and potentially identify suspicious behavior. In addition, it expanded a demonstration across several states—Florida, Illinois, Oklahoma, Ohio, North Carolina and Texas—that allowed for pre- and post-claim reviews of submissions from HHAs, which may stop improper payments before they're made.

The National Alliance for Care at Home, in a statement responding to the news, said it welcomes the administration's scrutiny and appreciates that "providers will still be able to conduct face-to-face recertification visits via telehealth during the enrollment moratorium, which will help avoid unnecessary care disruptions for patients and families." Still, it said the action raises concerns over access to care in high-demand areas and urged CMS to focus its efforts on "high-fraud markets" or actors without catching the rest of the sector in its net.

“CMS must use data-driven, risk-based program integrity measures and focus resources on boots-on-the-ground surveys and enforcement of existing oversight mechanisms that root out the blatantly bad actors without potentially limiting patient access to care or punishing high-quality providers operating in good faith," Alliance CEO Jennifer Sheets said in the statement.

MedHaul, a platform for coordinating medical transport, has raised $4.1 million in an unnamed round, Fierce Healthcare has learned exclusively.

The strategic round, which brings MedHaul’s total raised to date to $7 million, was led by Black Ops VC, with participation from Pier 70 Ventures, Seae Ventures, Stanford University, 450 Ventures (BCBS of Alabama), LaunchTN and American Cancer Society’s BrightEdge.

The latest capital will be used to expand deeper in oncology, which represents 60% of rides booked on the platform, and to support a forthcoming Epic integration.

Millions of Americans are affected by transportation barriers to care, and the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services estimates that these barriers account for a quarter or more of missed clinic appointments. Missed appointments are associated with mortality among patients with chronic conditions and cost the system billions annually, according to one 2017 estimate.

MedHaul is a B2B platform working with providers and payers. Its tech helps providers match patients with medical transport according to acuity and accommodation needs, such as a wheelchair or stretcher. Care teams can use MedHaul to schedule and track transportation, with same-day and advanced bookings available. They can also manage billing, communicate with patients and identify those at greater risk of missing an appointment. MedHaul also rolled out a newer clinical trial product designed to support CROs and pharma clients.

The existing medical transport ecosystem is “very antiquated” and “heavily manual,” MedHaul founder and CEO Erica Plybeah told Fierce Healthcare. Many transport companies are small businesses that are not tech-savvy, or that need business development support. When MedHaul first entered the market, in 2019, some vendors didn’t even have a website or a social media page, Plybeah noted. And manual back-and-forths between care teams and ride providers means it can take upwards of two hours just to book a ride.

MedHaul’s network spans 17 states, primarily serving patients in the cancer, kidney disease, maternal health and behavioral health spaces. It also supports hospital discharges and rural patients. It has about 600 transport companies in its network.

MedHaul has also launched an enterprise platform, designed for large health systems and multi-location practices. Through a partnership with Uber Health, ride share and non-emergency medical transport bookings are available in one place, on the platform. The platform also provides real-time analytics into patient mobility, enabled by AI.

"There are opportunities in healthcare where AI is bridging the gap to solve for unmet needs—and that’s exactly what Erica and her team are doing at MedHaul,” James Norman, co-founder & managing partner at Black Ops VC, said in a statement. The user-friendly interface and focus on high-acuity communities “make MedHaul an attractive investment, and we’re proud to help them scale.”

Plybeah, who grew up in the Deep South with a grandmother who had diabetic and a double-leg amputee, saw how transportation issues affected her access to care. Throughout her career in clinical and research informatics, including at Epic Systems and St. Jude Children’s Research Hospital, Plybeah “saw how transportation impacted institutions, when patients don’t show up on time, don’t show up at all, show up at the wrong facility … and it brought me back to being a little girl in the Mississippi Delta.”

Over half of MedHaul’s rides require mobility assistance, and 42% live in rural communities. MedHaul’s riders averaged eight rides per year in 2025.

Patients typically don’t pay anything for the rides booked on MedHaul. Instead, the MedHaul client—a provider or payer, for instance—sponsors the rides, often from an existing medical transport budget. In specialties with high transport volumes, providers may also use a patient’s benefit to find rides.

Same-day rides are available within 30 minutes to an hour, depending on acuity. For higher-acuity patients, a booking can take two to three hours, depending on the complexity of the transport required. MedHaul sees a 97% ride completion rate. Providers using MedHaul have reduced missed appointments by 25% and improved patient appointment timeliness by 52%, the company claims.

MedHaul has long used AI across its business. It has a conversational AI agent for older adults who prefer phone calls to texting. It also leverages AI agents to book rides within seconds for highly complex patients, and offers care teams MedHaul GPT, which they can use to ask questions about their programs and patients.

“We really prioritize good implementation,” Plybeah said, noting that there has been an explosion in digital health point solutions, but implementation is a whole different question. “We want this process to be as easy as possible for the end users.”

The company, which launched in 2019, is a public benefit corporation and a certified B Corp. While it’s still a for-profit business, its structure requires it to pursue a stated public benefit alongside financial returns. “We wanted our mission to extend beyond profit and to attract the right partners, investors, employees and customers who shared that commitment,” Plybeah said. “Our social mission is embedded in how we operate, build technology, serve patients and choose the people and organizations we work with.”

Plybeah is excited that there is more industry awareness of the social determinants of health. In the early MedHaul days, “folks seemed to think of transportation as a separate service,” she said. “And now I am grateful to see that folks are really considering transportation as part of the patient’s care journey, because it is.”
