BMW's First U.S.-Made EV Is Now Rolling Off The Assembly Line

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Browse this and many other websites without banner ads, personalized tracking and video ads.

The first American-made electric BMW is now being assembled at the company’s plant in Spartanburg, South Carolina. It’s a big moment for the German automaker in the U.S., but it’s just the beginning of a much larger plan, which will include no fewer than six U.S.-built EVs by the end of the decade.

Roughly 70 iX5 electric SUVs are rolling off the assembly line every day, alongside gas, diesel, and hybrid-powered models. All X5 powertrain versions are based on the same architecture, enabling the car manufacturer to share the same assembly line for multiple fuel types.

2027 BMW iX5 production in Spartanburg, South Carolina

Initially, BMW’s goal was to build no fewer than 100,000 iX5s in 2027 in Spartanburg, but that target has reportedly been revised to 60,000, according to Automotive News. The automaker has not provided production volume estimates for the iX5, but a company representative said that roughly one in four X5s will be electric for the near future.

“Currently, the market is looking like 20-25 percent EVs for the immediate three months,” said Kavit Antani, general manager of vehicle assembly. “As the market sees these vehicles come through, it might shift.”

Suppliers have reportedly been given a target of 175,000 EVs in 2028 and 2029, but that’s still a long way from now, and judging from what has happened in the American electric car market in the previous two years, the target could shift significantly.

The batteries that power the new iX5 come from a new plant 15 miles away in Woodruff, where the first mass-produced packs started their journey to Spartanburg to be fitted to finished chassis earlier this week.

The first Gen6 cylindrical cells used in completed packs will be sourced from AESC in China, BMW confirmed for Automotive News, but the German automaker will switch suppliers toward the end of the decade. LG Energy Solution will supply the car manufacturer’s facilities in the U.S. with locally made cells, while Samsung SDI will supply European factories.

BMW is not backing down from its plan to build six EVs in America by 2030. The iX5 is the first, and it will likely be followed by the iX7, iX6, and a performance-oriented iX5 M, according to BMW Blog.

The 2027 BMW iX5 is powered by a 144-kilowatt-hour battery that can be recharged from 10% to 80% in 22 minutes. Its estimated range sits at an impressive 450 miles, while its dual-motor powertrain delivers 570 horsepower and 594 pound-feet of torque. Pricing starts at $79,800.

Kirk Cousins, Vikings reunion could be just getting started

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Quarterback Kirk Cousins will spend the rest of the 2026 season with the Vikings. That may not be the end of Kirko 2.0.

The Vikings have already expressed interest in keeping Cousins beyond the current season, we’re told. As we understand it, the sentiment was articulated casually and in passing before his rousing debut at Lambeau Field. And it was more of an indication that there will be discussions.

Of course, there will be a greater motivation to do something, now that it’s clear that Cousins is exactly what the doctor ordered for an offense that was ready for a visit from Dr. Kevorkian.

Even though Cousins is under contract through 2028, the deal was structured to spark a release after one season, with Cousins getting a $10 million payment on the back end aimed at sticking the Falcons with $8.7 million in 2026.

A base salary in 2028 of $78.655 million becomes fully guaranteed on the fifth day of the 2027 league year, along with a $1.345 million base salary in 2027. Although that works out to $80 million over two seasons (which puts the average closer to the bottom of the veteran quarterback pay scale than the top), the current structure imposes a ridiculously high cash and cap obligation for 2028, when he will be 40.

So the deal will need to be revised. And Cousins will have leverage, if he continues to do what he did on Sunday.

That said, he possibly will realize that it’s not the time to maximize his leverage. He did that in 2024, when he opted for a multi-year financial commitment that he mistakenly viewed to be a multi-year playing commitment. And then the Falcons pulled the rug out from under him by drafting Michael Penix Jr. roughly six weeks after the ink dried on the new deal.

And, frankly, the Vikings could decide to devise a plan for swallowing the proverbial poison pill and paying $40 million per year for two more season. The impact of Cousins’s return on the Minnesota offense would justify trying to devise a solution.

The best result would be for the two sides to work something out, at least for 2027. After 2023, the Vikings wanted to take a year-to-year approach with Cousins. Cousins wanted something more than that — even though the Rams and Matthew Stafford have been using the year-to-year thing for a while. In hindsight, a compromise would have been much better for both sides.

That lesson from the past should inform the future. As one source put it, sometimes it takes a breakup for everyone to appreciate each other.

It’s clear that, one week after Cousins returned to Minnesota, both sides have significant appreciation for each other. Which gives both sides a very strong motivation to find a way to keep it going at least for 2027.

Unless, of course, the Vikings win the Super Bowl this year and Cousins decides to walk off into the sunset with the trophy that will fit in that spot in his house in Michigan he has saved for it.

That would throw the Minnesota quarterback position back into disarray for 2027. But if it comes with the first Super Bowl win in franchise history, the Vikings would take that.

Swansea City backed for Roberto Martinez or Pablo Hernandez move

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Swansea City have had to take a double-hit in recent weeks, with a dip in form and interest in their manager from elsewhere, but FLW's Swans fan pundit is optimistic over the identity of his replacement.

Swansea City's ascent to the top of the Championship was one of the surprises of the start of the season in the second tier. Seven wins from their first ten games took them to the top of the pile, while other below them struggled to find the consistency required to dislodge them.

That, however, all came to a juddering stop in the month building up to the international break. Four defeats in their last five have dropped them to sixth place in the table, and there was more bad news for them with reports that head coach Vitor Matos is in the sights of Ligue Un club Strasbourg.

With Matos's departure for France imminent, Swansea are in the hunt for a new manager, and with the top half of the Championship table looking extremely tight this season and tricky matches coming up, whoever the new incumbent is will need to get the team back on form to build on their excellent start.

Football League World have been in contact with our Swans fan pundit Will Hughes about the changing of the guard at The Swansea.com Stadium, and in particular about the possibility of the former West Bromwich Albion manager Carlos Corberan coming to his club, and Will believes that his club currently enticing enough to attract that calibre of manager: "I think Corberan would be good. He's certainly a name that should be floating around there."

Will believes that his club is a more attractive option as a result of changes behind the scenes over the last couple of years: "Honestly, I don't know who Matos's replacement is going to be. I'm not sure how big our pull is now, but I'd definitely say that the Swans job is a lot more desirable now managers now than what it was this time last year, for where we are in the league, the outside noise with over goings-on at the club, with the ownership of Modric and Snoop Dogg, and the fact that we've got more finance now. We've got a better squad. It's definitely a more desirable club."

Will is setting his hopes high over the identity of the next Swansea boss, with one former manager being someone he'd love to see back at his club: "This time last year I certainly wouldn't have been saying some of the names that are floating about now. One name that's been floated by Swans fans has been Roberto Martinez. I would love it if he came home, but dare I say maybe he's a bit above us after managing Portugal and Belgium, two very high profile jobs. Would he see this as a step down? Maybe he doesn't want to ruin his legacy here. I can't see it happening, but I'd love it."

And he had another suggestion with a Swansea connection who he feels could be a realistic target for his club: "Pablo Hernandez has been noted. Castellon are in the same bracket as us under Jamestown Analytics, Tony Bloom's company. I don't know whether they have a clause that we can't import another Jamestown club's manager, but going by the outside noise and the statistics that we see from Castellon, he could be a good fit for us."

Will sees the replacement as potentially an opportunity for his club, and is grateful to Matos for his role in improving Swansea's profile: "I don't whether we'd be looking higher. For me, I'd love it if we upgraded on Matos. Matos came in and he did his job. When he came in, Swans were floundering near the bottom. He kept us up and got us to a mid-table finish, which is what we were expecting, and now this year we've had a really good start, he's got us in the play-offs, near the top of the division. Whether he'd have kept us there we'll never know, but he got us to that point so he deserves credit for making the job more desirable."

Our Swansea correspondent is also aware of the importance of the new manager stepping straight in and getting the team back on track: "So the question is can we get someone to keep us there or maybe take us a little bit further up. You'd want someone who's going to come in and hit the ground running. I know it's not as easy as that, but you don't want to bring in someone where you're thinking, "Maybe it'll work, maybe it won't." You want to upgrade. I don't know how far our pull is now, with Jamestown on board, whether we can look at the bigger leagues, at clubs and managers we wouldn't have over the last few years. There's a few out of work. You've mentioned Corberan, I've mentioned Roberto Hernandez. Kieran McKenna would be good, but I've heard we're priced out on him."

And he has one particular fixture in mind, which is coming up on the 29th November: "It's typical that the new manager's first game is probably going to be the South Wales derby. Hopefully it'll be another Garry Monk masterclass; 3-0 in his first game. But we'd hope it doesn't go the way it did afterwards towards the end of his tenure, because it turned sour in the end. I keep saying we'll wait and see. I'd an expect an announcement today that Matos has gone, but we've got Leon Brittan and Chris O'Leary, two legends, looking after us for two games and you'd think hopefully we'll get a new manager in next week, and we can go from there."

Will feels that while the Cardiff game is extremely important, Swansea's season won't be defined by his team's matches against these opponents: "All I would say to Swans fans is not to get on the new manager's back if we don't get the result we want against Cardiff. As much as we all want to beat them, it's a marathon not a sprint. We may lose to them but beat them away from home. Let's not jump on the new manager's back, or Leon Brittan or the club if we don't win that game. It's not the end of the world. It may feel like it, believe me. I've got into some stinking moods when we've lost to Cardiff in the past."

And he believes that the best thing that his fellow supporters can do is get fully behind the team, no matter who the opponents: "But whoever's in charge, let's not get on their back. It was different when we had someone like Michael Duff or Luke Williams when they lost their last South Wales derbies because of the situations that were going on, Luke Williams flirting with West Brom and Michael Duff saying what he said about the derby, so that's fair enough. But let's not get on their back, let's get behind them and hopefully start with a win on Saturday. It's a tricky game against West Brom, but forget about the fact that we haven't got a manager or where they are in the league, it's a game we've got to try and win to stop the rot of our poor form."

Whoever it is who ends up filling the shoes of Vitor Matos at The Swansea.com Stadium will have one immediate job; to turn around the form of a team who were flying at the start of the season. Given that Swansea City haven't finished above 10th place in the Championship in the previous five years, few were expecting them to get near the top of the table this time around, but they managed this on the 1st September and stayed there for most of the next six weeks.

The names mentioned in connection with the vacant position all have something to recommend them. Carlos Corberan has Championship experience from spells with Huddersfield Town and West Bromwich Albion. Pablo Hernandez has a previous Swansea connection from having played there from 2012 to 2014. And Kieran McKenna built a strong reputation from taking Ipswich Town from League One to the Premier League.

And whoever the identity of the new Swansea manager might be, he'll have a tough start. West Bromwich Albion are the Swans' next opponents, and they're one place above them in the table. Next after that come Middlesbrough, who are in second place in the table. And then comes the big derby game against Cardiff City.

So the question facing the club is that of which was the blip? Were Swansea City just on an explosively good run of form at the start of the season which is now settling towards where most people would have put them in the summer, or did Vitor Matos build a squad that is capable of going the entire gruelling length of a 46-game season near the top of the table?

Whichever the answer to that question turns out to be, what is undeniable is that Swansea City demonstrated their potential over the opening two months of the season. If the new Swansea manager can capture that form again, the Swans could yet find themselves pushing to end their nine-year exile in the Championship by its end.

This Is the MVP of the Lord of the Rings Trilogy

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Peter Jackson’s Lord of the Rings trilogy has many heroes, as it perfectly captures the spirit of J.R.R. Tolkien’s classic books. Maybe you cheer whenever Aragorn (Viggo Mortensen) walks on screen, or laugh as Gimli (John Rhys-Davies) and Legolas (Orlando Bloom) compare kill counts. One character, however, quietly stands out from the crowd. He may not have any fancy titles or extravagant weapons, but his pure intentions and devotion to leaving a better place mean that Samwise Gamgee (Sean Astin), Frodo’s gardener-turned-companion, becomes the most valuable member of the Middle-earth saga.

Throughout all three movies, he is the unassuming constant of the mission, even if he wasn’t intended to be part of the Fellowship. Sam is Frodo’s (Elijah Wood) gardener and closest friend, who eavesdrops on his conversation with Gandalf (Ian McKellen) about the One Ring’s power. Discovered by the wizard, Sam is ordered to accompany them to Rivendell where Frodo volunteers to take the Ring to Mordor, after which Sam volunteers to accompany him.

From there, on their epic journey across Middle-earth, Sam is Frodo’s protector, nurse, counselor, and friend. As the Fellowship divides into separate groups, the two friends stick together through unimaginable horrors. As Gollum (Andy Serkis) slithers into their lives trying to trick the Ring out of their possession, Sam defends them from his schemes even as Frodo refuses to see the subterfuge. His humility, bravery, and commitment are key to achieving their goal and saving the world.

A former child star transitioning into adult roles, Astin put everything into the performance that would define his career. He had played underdogs before in films like The Goonies and Rudy, but in Sam, he took that spirit to a different level. Keeping a watchful eye over Frodo, his mission is laid out at the closing of The Fellowship of the Ring, where Frodo seeks to head off alone with the Ring. “Go back, Sam. I'm going to Mordor alone,” he says, to which Sam replies: “Of course you are. And I'm coming with you.” Venturing across a deep river despite not being able to swim, he tearfully declares: “I made a promise, Mr. Frodo. A promise. ‘Don't you leave him Samwise Gamgee.’ And I don't mean to. I don't mean to.”

That’s what makes him so special, and so vital to Frodo’s mission. Sam is a good man with a pure heart, and as Frodo slowly loses himself to the seduction of the Ring, his friend remains a lighthouse in the darkness, a reminder of who he is and why he should cling to hope. Even when all seems lost, he reminds his friend: “There's some good in this world, Mr. Frodo. And it's worth fighting for.” Frodo may have to carry the Ring, but in more than one way, Sam carried him.

The theme at the heart of the trilogy is temptation. The rise of Sauron came about because Isildur could not resist the power of the One Ring, and his allies grew from men who craved the influence it promised. The heroes of this story do not seek fame and fortune, preferring a quiet, anonymous life. Aragorn, the rightful king of Gondor, shies away from his destiny, while Gandalf refuses temptation because he is aware of what he would be capable of with the Ring on his finger. Frodo himself doesn’t seek adventure, but puts his body on the line to do what’s right.

If one accepts that the good people in Tolkien’s books are the ones who cannot have their head turned by power, then Sam is the ultimate example of good. The Ring tries to tempt him, but he is one of the few characters who gives it back willingly, because he is not fighting for an empire, or for vengeance, or anything other than friendship. His humble dreams are as large as the garden that he wishes for in Hobbiton, because he is someone who follows his heart, not his desires. He is the greatest hero of them all because he wishes to protect the home and people that he loves, not take it over.

No actor received an Oscar for their performance in the Lord of the Rings trilogy, with only Ian McKellen earning a nomination. Retrospectively, there is an argument that Sean Astin deserved to be the actor to win that accolade for the ultimate supporting performance. He is the heart and soul of Jackson’s fantasy masterpiece, and the more times you watch the films, the more evident that becomes.

The Matrix was part of a three-movie deal that the Wachowskis landed with Warner Bros., and the rights to the franchise​​​​​​​ remained firmly in the hands of the studio, recently absorbed by Skydance in a much-discussed merger. But things may change in the future.

As revealed by Eriq Gardner in an article for Puck, the Wachowskis have formally notified Warner Bros. that they are exercising their copyright termination rights. Astronomers Discover a Chilling Preview of Our Solar System’s Future

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Astronomers have discovered an extraordinary ring system surrounding a dead star, offering a possible preview of what awaits our Solar System billions of years from now. Using the European Southern Observatory’s Very Large Telescope (ESO’s VLT), researchers identified two separate rings of planetary debris with a distinct gap between them. The unusual structure may be maintained by two planetary bodies that survived the death of their star, including one potentially as large as Pluto or even the Moon.

“We have known for some time that planets may survive beyond the transformation of their central star, but this is the first example of a complex system with a double debris disc and potentially two surviving planetesimals,” says Nicola Gentille Fusillo, an astronomer at the Università degli Studi di Trieste in Italy and lead author of the study published in Nature Astronomy.

At the center of this remarkable system is J1328−1450, a white dwarf. These incredibly dense stellar remnants are what remain when stars similar to our Sun exhaust their nuclear fuel and reach the end of their lives.

When a Sun-like star approaches the end of its life, it swells dramatically before shedding its outer layers. During this transformation, nearby planets can be swallowed and destroyed. Other rocky objects may survive the initial upheaval, only to break apart later and create rotating discs of debris around the star’s remaining core.

Astronomers have observed such debris discs around white dwarfs before, but J1328−1450 stands out. Instead of a single ring of vaporized planetary material, it has two concentric rings of gas separated by a well-defined gap.

“The structure we see in this system is particularly intriguing because the two gaseous rings are remarkably stable and remain separated by a gap,” says co-author Felipe Lagos-Vilches, an astronomer at the University of Warwick in the UK and at the Universidad Técnica Federico Santa María in Chile.

“The system is a rare glimpse of the violent, chaotic process by which a planetary system meets its end, and it hints that dead stars can have disc structures far more complex than previously realized,” adds Gentile Fusillo, who is also affiliated with the INAF-Astronomical Observatory of Trieste.

What keeps the two rings separated? The researchers suspect that two surviving planet-like bodies, traveling along different orbits, are helping maintain the unusual arrangement.

The smaller object, associated with the inner ring, could be comparable to a typical asteroid, measuring just a few kilometers across. The larger body, associated with the outer ring, may be roughly the size of Pluto or even Earth’s Moon. Although their presence has not been directly confirmed, these objects could explain why the debris remains divided into two stable rings.

Until now, astronomers had identified debris discs around white dwarfs through a characteristic observational signature associated with a single, flat, rotating disc. However, observations of J1328−1450 revealed something entirely different.

Using the X-Shooter instrument installed on ESO’s VLT in Chile’s Atacama Desert, the researchers detected the distinctive signature of a double disc, showing that planetary debris around dead stars can form more complicated structures than previously recognized.

The rings also contain important clues about the worlds that once orbited the star. Astronomers detected calcium, oxygen, magnesium, and iron, elements commonly found in rocky planetary material throughout our Solar System.

Even more intriguing, the two rings have noticeably different chemical compositions. The inner ring contains abundant magnesium gas, while the outer ring has very little. This difference suggests that the debris feeding the rings may have come from two separate planets with distinct compositions.

Another possibility is that the material originated from different layers of the same planetary body. For example, one ring could contain debris from a planet’s crust, while the other holds material from its core.

The discovery may also reveal something unsettling about our own cosmic future. Billions of years from now, the Sun will undergo a similar transformation, expanding as it nears the end of its life before eventually becoming a white dwarf. Earth could be destroyed during this process, while other planetary bodies may survive and leave behind rings of debris.

The unusual system around J1328−1450 suggests that the aftermath of a star’s death can be far more complex than a simple cloud of shattered planetary remains. Even after a planetary system experiences catastrophic destruction, some of its original bodies may endure and continue shaping the wreckage.

“The system therefore offers a remarkable glimpse of what the future of planetary systems, including systems like our own, might look like,” concludes Lagos-Vilches.
