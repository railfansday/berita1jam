British fintech Revolut slashes AI coding costs 10x by cutting out Claude and going self-hosted

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Turns out cheap tokens get engineers doing things that save time, like debugging and tackling Jira tickets

Revolut says that in less than a year it's managed to slash its AI coding inference costs by roughly 90 percent by making self-hosted models its default instead of relying primarily on commercial services like Claude.

Speaking at the AI Engineer conference in New York last month, David Abitbol, ops and infrastructure lead at the British fintech, described how it saw a 60 percent improvement in coding time and a 70 percent improvement in lead time following its rollout of AI coding tools to roughly 2,000 engineers. Revolut initially adopted GLM-5.1 before moving to GLM-5.3, which it now serves using 56 Nvidia B200 GPUs.

Prior to its switch to GLM, Revolut had been experimenting with AI, deploying Gemini in early 2025 and Claude Code in early 2026. "We were highly skeptical about the cost of AI. We were seeing engineers spend $1,000 a month, $10,000 a month with not so much ROI, and so when we launched it we launched it with strict budget limits," Abitbol said.

Because of this, engineers only had access to a limited number of tokens, so the company began investigating self-hosting iyd own models. At $0.30 per million input/output tokens, self hosting GLM 5.1 cost about a tenth of using OpenAI's Codex or Anthropic's Claude Code. And, as it turned out, when tokens are cheap, you stop worrying about how many they use or what they use them for.

While Revolut did see improvements to coding speed, Abitbol noted the biggest bottleneck wasn't actually the code. Instead, he says the biggest gains came from letting the AI models deal with things like debugging and Jira and Confluence tickets. "When you have unlimited tokens, you're free to do that," he said. "You no longer have to save your tokens for the end of the quarter or the most difficult task."

Developed by Chinese open weights model dev Z.ai, GLM 5.1 isn't exactly a small model. At roughly 754 billion total parameters (40 billion activated for each token generated), the model requires roughly 380 GB of GPU memory just to hold the weights and another 6-26 GB per user to maintain context depending on precision.

To serve GLM-5.3 at scale, Revolut deployed 56 Nvidia B200 GPUs, equivalent in GPU count to seven eight-GPU DGX B200 systems. But as Abitbol noted, the greatest performance gains didn't come from adding GPUs, but rather tuning the model's hyperparameters along with switching to NVFP4 quantization, implementing disaggregated prefill and decode, and tuning context lengths.

One key takeaway was that supporting a one-million-token context window doesn't necessarily make such a large window useful or efficient. "Long context is a scam. You really need to be mindful of that," Abitbol said. "Even though you can serve one million tokens, 256 [thousand] is really what you need."

With these optimizations, Abitbol says that the company was able to achieve sub 3-second time to first token on context windows of around 256,000 tokens, and generation speeds of around 200 tokens a second, both of which he notes are the sweet spot for the company's software engineers.

"What you want to aim for is a sub-3 second time to first token. This is empirical evidence. As soon as you exceed 5 seconds, my Slack inbox starts to blow up," Abitbol said. "In terms of throughput, it doesn't really matter as much. We serve, in terms of interactivity, 200 tokens per second. People are fine [with that]. You don't need to go higher. The way people work has changed. We no longer look at the stream of thinking tokens. People run tasks in parallel and check in when the task is done."

And if throughput is a concern, he notes engineers who sign in after 5 pm are getting closer to 800 tokens a second.

However, it's worth noting that GLM hasn't replaced GPT or Claude entirely. The Chinese model is text only, so it isn't ideal for things like UI work. So instead, Revolut used a model router to direct vision queries to models like Gemini or GPT Luna while keeping code generation running on GLM.

"Open source is now a priority at Revolut, and our economics are favorable. The billing is flat, we don't have any bill shock at the end of the month, and the frontier is the exception," Abitbol said.

Having said that, Abitbol acknowledges that Chinese open weights models may be a concern for privacy and security reasons, but emphasizes that the major concern there is accessing them via APIs running out of China. When run on-prem or on regional cloud resources, there's really nothing to worry about. ®

Eight weeks into the NFL season, we're starting to get some answers about the 2026 first-round rookie class. Some players look exactly like we thought they would when they came out of college. Others have been pleasant surprises. And then there are those still struggling with some of the same issues that showed up on their college tape.

We evaluated these rookies after four weeks on the With the First Pick podcast – it was our most popular episode this fall – and Arvell Reese and Sonny Styles were among the early standouts. David Bailey was still finding his footing, Rueben Bain Jr. was battling injuries, and Dillon Thieneman looked like one of the league's most promising young safeties.

A month later, some of those evaluations look a little (and, in some cases, a lot) different.

These are our first-half grades for all 32 first-round picks from the 2026 class, based on everything we've seen through eight weeks. Production matters, but so do injuries, expectations and what the tape reveals. We're also revisiting our original scouting reports from the 2026 draft cycle to see which traits have translated, which concerns have followed these players to the NFL, and where we might have missed something. And because good players aren't limited to the first round, we've also graded 10 rookies drafted on Days 2 and 3 who have already made their mark.

Mendoza finally got on the field in Week 5 mop-up duty vs. the Patriots. He was a tidy 6 of 8 for 48 yards. For now, veteran Kirk Cousins remains the starter, but as the losses pile up, Mendoza will likely get his chance to be QB1.

Bailey has taken a noticeable step forward after an uneven start, highlighted by a three-sack performance against Cleveland. I loved his explosive first step, speed-to-power, inside spin and relentless motor at Texas Tech, and we're seeing all of it in the NFL. What's been particularly encouraging is his development against the run, where Jets coaches have praised his discipline and ability to handle responsibilities beyond rushing the passer. He's becoming the complete defender New York envisioned when it drafted him second overall.

Love hasn't looked like the explosive, home-run threat we watched at Notre Dame, but there's a very simple reason: He's been battling an ankle injury since the preseason, and it cost him two games. At Notre Dame, I loved his ability to accelerate through the smallest crease and turn ordinary runs into big plays, but Love has acknowledged that he hasn't been able to cut or explode the way he did in college (not yet, anyway). This grade really doesn't mean much until we see what a healthy version of Love looks like. And that won't be for at least another month with today's trip to the IR.

Tate is becoming a dependable target for Cam Ward despite Tennessee's offensive struggles, and that's hardly surprising given how polished he looked at Ohio State. His ability to manipulate DBs, separate at the top of routes and make difficult catches look easy gave him a chance to contribute immediately. Tate continues to get more comfortable in Tennessee's offense, and he's showing the makings of a No. 1 receiver.

Remember all those concerns about Reese's pro day workout? Seven months later and he's one of the easiest evaluations in the class because he's doing exactly what made him such an exciting prospect at Ohio State. Instincts, range, and insane athleticism, while learning on the job, have all made him an immediate difference-maker, and Giants coaches have praised how quickly he's hit the ground running.

Delane looked like a grizzled All-Pro from the moment he stepped on the field. The only issue has been staying healthy. He was one of the most technically sound CBs in the draft and that has immediately translated – even when he's "allowed" receptions while in coverage (show me the human who is stopping JSN – also, look at Delane's coverage!). The Chiefs traded up for Delane and so far, it appears that they nailed this pick.

Washington already trusts Styles to relay defensive calls and make adjustments before the snap, impressive responsibilities for a rookie still relatively new to playing off-ball LB. His instincts and athleticism stood out at Ohio State, and he's carried those strengths into the NFL. He's been particularly effective in coverage vs. TEs and RBs, and he's shown consistent improvement against the run. There have been occasional missed tackles, but through eight weeks, he's been one of the best players in this rookie class.

People over the centuries have referred to spiritual companionship and assistance as a relationship with a “spiritual friend.” The Gaelic word anamchara refers to the spiritual friend as a “soul-friend.” A soul-friend is an assisting force on our journey. This is the reliable confidant with whom we can share our innermost spiritual conundrums, visions, and realizations.

As St. Aelred (1111-1167), the Cistercian abbot of Rievaulx Abbey in medieval England, wrote in On Spiritual Friendship: “It is no small consolation in this life to have someone who can unite with you in an intimate affection and the embrace of a holy love. Someone in whom your spirit can rest, to whom you can pour out your soul, to whose pleasant exchanges, as to soothing songs, you can fly in sorrow. To the dear breast of whose friendship, amidst the many troubles of the world, you can safely retire. A person who can shed tears with you in your worries, be happy with you when things go well, search out with you the answers to your problems, whom with the ties of charity you can lead into the depths of your heart. A person who, though absent in body, is yet present in spirit, where heart to heart you can talk to him, where the sweetness of the Spirit flows between you, where you so join yourself and cleave to him that soul mingles with soul and two become one.”

In Buddhism we find the idea of Kalyana-mittata, “virtuous friendship.” The spiritual friends support one another’s practice with candidness, wisdom, and trustworthiness. In the Upaddha Sutta, Buddha explained to Ananda, his disciple, cousin, and friend, that such spiritual companionship can be considered the whole of the Dharma as the “holy life.” Buddha went on to say that when we have Kalyana-mittata in place with friends, they cheer and encourage us on the path, and when our friend is Buddha, we are fully freed from suffering. In the Dighajanu Sutta, Buddha says that when a practitioner is committed to dialogue with a variety of upstanding people, he learns to imitate their commitment to the Dharma, their compassion, their discerning wisdom. They have become allies of enlightenment to one another.

Another text, the Anguttara Nikaya, lists the qualities of a monastic spiritual friend: he is amiable, venerable, able to give good counsel, patient in listening, respectful, capable of speaking in depth about the Dharma, and serious enough to share on a depth level. A teacher is a prime example of what is required in spiritual friendship. He, she, or they offer guidance, challenges, comfort, and cheer on the path to virtue and awakening—all the scaffolding of authentic support. A support group, a faith community, or a sangha, a coterie of practitioners of Buddhism, are examples.

Peer companionship is certainly valuable on the spiritual path. Our spiritual practice and our psychological health are enlivened when we have a trusted friend to whom we can speak openly about ourselves. This will not be the “friend” who supports and cheers our ego and its inflation—a narcissistic style of alliance. The ally of our higher Self supports our journey toward generosity, humility, enlightenment, and loving-kindness. This ally warns and saves us from what in Buddhism are called the three poisons: greed, hate, and the illusion of separateness. These are descriptors of the inflated ego at its worst. Spiritual friends will be the ones who show and lead us from greed to generosity, from hate to love, and from ignorance to wisdom.

“Ubuntu” is an African term meaning “humanity toward others.” It is also translated as “I am because you are” or “I am what I am because of who we all are.” Ubuntu tells us that all beings can be allies, contributing to our becoming what we can best be in this moment. Ubuntu is also a spiritual calling to reach out to and care about all beings as relatives in the family of humanity. Ubuntu transcends race and creed. All diversity becomes, or rather is, only one reality, just what the Buddhas and mystics in all traditions came to realize. We feel an intense, ineradicable sense of belonging and we pass on to others that same joyous sense. We remind ourselves that we are not stand-alone beings; we exist only with everyone else, the solidarity that Thich Nhat Hanh calls “inter-being.”

We notice unanimous agreement among the assisting-counseling forces in all times and traditions: We can be allies to one another only if there are no boundaries to our assisting outreach. Indeed, such boundaries are really barriers. The wise, courageous assisting forces in all traditions have made the journey from insulation to universal embrace. They see diversity as a path to unity. Their caring, committed connection has no frontier.

"Covert Contracts" Might Be Wrecking Your Relationship

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

If you’ve ever thought “after everything I do,” therapists say you might have one.

You did bedtime solo every single night this week. You did the baths, with all the splishing and the splashing and the bubble anarchy. You fielded the lightning round of existential questions at 8:47 p.m. (“Mommy, what happens to doggies when they die?”). You tucked everyone in and then did your regular closing-time routine to get the rest of the house buttoned up for bed.

So when Saturday morning rolls around, you’re planning to sleep in. You fully expect your partner to wake up and handle the kids, the breakfast, the early morning dog walking duties. The whole shebang. Tit for tat.

Then your kids are in your face before you can even pry your eyes open, wondering where their soccer cleats are or if you can get more juice. Cue the rage, right? OK, so here comes the uncomfortable gut-check… did you communicate your expectations to your partner? Or did you just sort of assume it was a done deal?

This scenario has been dubbed a "covert contract," and therapists say it can lead to a whole lot of resentment building up in a relationship over time. Here’s what experts want you to know about where this behavior comes from, how to spot it in your own actions, and how it differs from the imbalance of the invisible load.

“A covert contract is a deal only one person signed,” says Kristyn Carmichael, Esq., a family mediator, certified divorce financial analyst, and founder of Couples Solutions Center. “It sounds like ‘If I do X, you’ll do Y,’ but the other partner never saw the terms, never agreed to them, and usually doesn’t know the contract exists.”

And she often has a front-row seat to what happens when those deals go unaddressed for a prolonged period of time. “In mediation, I see the end stage of these: one spouse arrives with years of unpaid ‘invoices,’ and the other is honestly confused about what they owe.”

Cheryl Groskopf, an anxiety, trauma, and attachment therapist who authored The Somatic Healing Workbook, likens it to writing a big ol' agreement and assigning your partner an equally big role in it. Somehow, your partner is the only person unaware of said agreement.

But your partner being unaware doesn’t change the way you feel. The hurt is just as real as if they’d actually broken a promise. “In your brain, they did,” Groskopf explains.

Um, yes. One hundred percent. This was something every expert agreed on! However, the need itself isn’t the issue here; it’s the delivery method.

“The need underneath is reasonable, but if it’s not articulated overtly, then it’s unreasonable to think your partner would be a mind reader,” says Dr. Jenni Skyler, Adam & Eve’s resident sex and relationship therapist.

Already, as a busy working mom who happens to be the default parent, I instinctively start to feel myself bristle at this idea. No one tells me these things or what needs to be done — I just notice and do it because someone has to. The experts say that sort of thinking is a fast path to frustration.

“‘I want my partner to make an effort on my birthday’ is a totally reasonable desire,” Groskopf says. “‘I shouldn’t have to tell them what I want because if they really loved me, they’d already know’ is when it becomes problematic.”

Now, the experts were also abundantly clear that none of this means moms should just silently accept an unfair split.

“One person also shouldn’t have to become a household manager simply because they are more organized, quicker to notice what needs to be done, or less comfortable leaving things unfinished,” says Ilona Phillips, a psychotherapist and founder of Lotus Counseling in Ann Arbor, Michigan. “Expecting shared responsibility is reasonable. Expecting a partner to recognize an unspoken sacrifice and reciprocate exactly one-for-one can get partners in trouble.”

“If I ask for a break and you say no, that’s a direct rejection,” Carmichael says. “If I never ask, I’m protected from that, and I keep the moral high ground.” And, yes, there’s also that belief so many of us carry that “if I have to ask, it doesn’t count,” she adds, “as if a gesture only proves love when it’s unprompted.”

That belief tends to run deep, too (ask me how I know). Says Groskopf, “If you grew up learning that your needs were inconvenient, asking can feel incredibly vulnerable.” Instead of just asking, we end up creating these little litmus tests our partners don’t even know they’re taking.

She uses flowers as an example. If you ask for them, you know they showed up because you asked. But if your partner brings them on their own, "There’s this feeling that you were appreciated, that they noticed you, that you MATTER. And for someone who grew up feeling overlooked or emotionally neglected, that distinction can feel huge.”

Oof. The catch here is that this strategy often just backfires.

“There is a fear that if we directly communicate what we need, and that need is not met, we will be hurt,” explains Dr. Paulette Didia, a licensed clinical psychologist in New York City. “However, if we do not communicate what we need, our partner will not know, our partner will not do it, and we will be hurt anyway. We think hurting in our own mind ‘hurts less,’ and that is not true.”

Admittedly, the lines are a bit blurry. When you’re doing the lion’s share of invisible work in your home and family, it’s hard not to “keep score” or have a running tally in your head of what your partner did versus what you did.

Unfortunately, that scoreboard warps how both partners see each other.

“Once you develop a narrative that your partner isn’t doing enough, it becomes very easy to fill that bucket with examples of how they’re falling short. Meanwhile, your spouse feels like they’re always in the doghouse and starts to wonder if it’s even worth trying. One person is thinking, ‘Don’t you see how hard I’m working?’ while the other is thinking, ‘What did I do wrong now?’” says Mary McLaughlin, LCSW, a couples therapist and discernment counselor.

So while it’s fair to feel frustrated by an invisible (or visible) load imbalance, a little communication can go a long way. That’s true on both sides of the line. “Even if the score is unequal, if all parties know the score and acknowledge it and offer gratitude, this can offset resentment,” says Skyler.

Well, obviously this is tricky since you probably don’t know you’re making them. However, there are a few tells you can look for, according to the experts:

First and foremost, resist the urge to try settling years of “back pay.” You can’t Uno Reverse this one. Instead, focus on rewriting the terms and making sure you and your partner are, in fact, on the same page. Here are a few practical pointers from our pros on how to pull that off.

Didia asks couples to fill out a simple weekly worksheet together that includes tasks along with who does them and when. She has couples do this at the same time each week so that it doesn’t become one more thing Mom has to track, and they post the list where both can see it so no one becomes the default reminder.

If you want to follow suit, start by listing every task (big and small). Next, let each partner choose what they’ll take, and split the ones nobody wants. Very important info: The person who owns a task decides how and when it gets done. “Maybe you think this should be done by Monday, but your partner indicates it will be done by Wednesday,” Didia says. “Since it is their task, they get to decide and, by Tuesday, you will not already be annoyed.”

Phillips encourages a weekly check-in where partners take turns asking: What’s been working well between us? Is anything feeling off? Extra difficult? Frustrating as hell? Unacknowledged? And, finally, how can I be a better partner to you this week?

You may be tempted to stop doing certain things, like washing the dishes, hoping that someone will notice. That’s just a covert contract in a different outfit, says Phillips. And even if your partner does pick up the task, resentment can still linger. You might then think, Why did I have to stop before they noticed?

“Relationships change. What worked when you were first dating, before kids, 10 years ago may not work anymore,” reminds McLaughlin. “Couples need to renegotiate instead of assuming the old arrangement still works.”

When you realize you’re locked in a pattern that won’t budge, it’s probably time to see a couples therapist. Carmichael’s advice is to get help sooner rather than later. By the time couples land in her office, she says the covert contracts show up even in how they divorce, i.e., “I gave up my career, so I deserve more.”

At the end of the day, adds McLaughlin, “Resentment paired with avoidance is a particularly risky combination for couples.” And that means the fix, both annoyingly simple and incredibly hard, is saying what you need out loud… and holding each other accountable to those needs in a way that feels fair and equitable to you both.

Can the Tour Be Pogačar-Proofed? The 2027 Tour de France Route Is Designed to Keep the Racing Close.

📅 Nov 03, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

More time trialing and fewer summit finishes could give Remco Evenepoel, Jonas Vingegaard, and the rest of the peloton a chance to take the fight to the five-time champion.

It’s early November, which means the Halloween candy is on sale, the piles of leaves in my yard are getting harder to ignore, and the routes for the 2027 men’s and women’s Tour de France have finally been unveiled. After weeks of refreshing Tour rumor sites, here’s what stood out to me about the men’s route.

First, let’s address the Slovenian elephant in the room: There’s little anyone can do to defeat a healthy, in-form Tadej Pogačar (UAE Team Emirates XRG), who will head into the 2027 Tour as the overwhelming favorite to take a record-breaking sixth overall victory. Certainly, the organizers seem to feel the same way.

Rather than create a race designed to defeat him, which currently doesn’t seem possible, they’ve laid out a course that could at least prevent the five-time champion from running away with it in the first week. The 2027 Tour packs more than 53,000 meters of elevation gain and 28 second-, first-, or hors catégorie climbs into its 21 stages. But there are only three summit finishes, and the first doesn’t arrive until the end of Stage 9, atop the Grand Colombier.

That means fewer chances for Pogačar and his team to jump out to a huge early lead on the Tour’s general classification and then ride defensively, slowly wearing down the opposition until the final week. That’s when the Slovenian usually delivers the coup de grâce, adding a few more stage wins to his ever-growing tally.

Yes, there are a couple of punchy uphill finishes in the opening week, at the end of Stages 4 and 5, and Pogačar will do well on them. But the time gaps shouldn’t be huge, so I expect a tighter bunch of riders at the top of the GC heading into the first individual time trial on Stage 7.

Speaking of time trials, the 2027 Tour features 71 kilometers of them across Stages 7 and 16, the most we’ve seen since 2012. It’s hard not to see that as a nod to Belgium’s Remco Evenepoel (Red Bull–Bora–Hansgrohe), the four-time world time trial champion who finished second in the Tour this past July.

If he can improve his preparation for this year’s Tour, and he’s only 26, Evenepoel could use those time trials to his advantage. The first might even give him a chance to give Pogačar a scare before the mountains arrive at the end of the opening week.

But waiting until Stage 9 for the first mountain stage doesn’t mean the opening week will be easy. A slew of long, tricky stages will be tough for one or two teams to control. And at least until the Stage 7 time trial, plenty of riders should be in contention to spend a day or two in yellow and willing to take a few risks and big swings to make it happen. That’s the downside of designing a route that attempts to delay the GC fireworks until the second week. More riders feel they have a chance to wear yellow, which can lead to more risk-taking and, unfortunately, the possibility of more crashes.

Throw in tight roads, punchy climbs and descents, some rain during the three opening stages in the United Kingdom, and perhaps a few days of crosswinds, and you’ve got the exact right mix to create unpredictable and downright chaotic racing.

If Pogačar is going to run away with another Tour, I expect we’ll know by the end of Stage 11, the final day in the Alps and the second of the race’s three summit finishes. If his rivals, including Evenepoel, Denmark’s Jonas Vingegaard (Visma–Lease a Bike), and France’s Paul Seixas (Decathlon CMA CGM Team), can somehow limit the damage on Stages 9, 10, and 11, we could have a much more compelling Tour than what we’re used to.

A run of sprint and transitional stages follows before the final week, when the second time trial and two days in the Pyrenees await. With any luck, we’ll still have a GC battle worth watching by then.

That’s what the organizers appear to be hoping for with this route. But given what we’ve seen from Pogačar and his UAE squad in recent Tours, I’m not sure it will happen. While the time trial kilometers seem to acknowledge Evenepoel as one of Pogačar’s greatest threats, I also see the route as a not-so-subtle gesture to Seixas, the young Frenchman many consider the rider most likely to be Pogačar's successor.

Seixas, now 20, raced his first Tour this past summer and finished fourth overall. The 2027 course should give him a chance to continue growing as a Tour contender without throwing him in at the deep end the way this year’s route did. The French would be thrilled to see him finish on the final podium, and with the Tour’s most intense stages broken into two- or three-day chunks, with time to recover and regroup in between, that kind of result looks well within his reach.

I’m also eager to see how two-time Tour champion Vingegaard tackles the race. Recent routes have forced Vingegaard and his Visma–Lease a Bike team to burn a lot of matches early, leaving them powerless when Pogačar and his UAE teammates throw down in the second and third weeks. The 2027 course should allow Vingegaard to ride a bit more under the radar, hopefully keeping him fresh for the Stage 7 time trial and the first summit finish on Stage 9. If all goes to plan, the Dane could enter the third week ready to challenge Pogačar in a way we haven’t seen since Vingegaard won his second Tour in 2023.

As for specific stages, I’ve already marked a few as days I won’t want to miss.

The Tour begins in the United Kingdom with three stages that should generate some intense battles among sprinters and Classics riders. Of the three, Stage 3 looks like the hardest. Its 223 km route includes a slew of punchy climbs, making it a perfect day for riders like Mathieu van der Poel (Alpecin–Premier Tech), Wout van Aert (Visma–Lease a Bike), and Tom Pidcock (Pinarello–Q36.5 Pro Cycling Team).

Then there’s Stage 10, a 171 km ride from Annecy to Sallanches that features six categorized climbs and previews the course used in August’s world championship road race. Coming right after the Tour’s second rest day and sandwiched between summit finishes on Stages 9 and 11, this should be an exciting and hard-to-predict day for the GC contenders.

Stage 14 takes the riders 199 km from Montpellier into the Cévennes, covering many of the roads immortalized in Tim Krabbé’s The Rider, one of the best cycling novels ever written. Seriously, if you’ve never read it, find a copy and enjoy! The stage ends on an airstrip just past the summit of the Côte de la Croix Neuve, a famous climb that always generates an exciting finish.

After taking a back seat in recent Tours, the Pyrenees close out the mountain racing in 2027, highlighted by Stage 18. The Tour’s “queen stage” runs 174 km from Pau to Saint-Lary-Soulan, with 5,400 meters of elevation gain and perhaps the two toughest climbs in the entire race: the Col du Tourmalet and a summit finish on the Col du Portet. If the race hasn’t been decided heading into Stage 18, it should be by the end, even with one relatively milder day in the Pyrenees still to come.

And what about Stage 21? Maybe you heard the pre-announcement rumors that the organizers would skip the cobbled Côte de la Butte Montmartre. Thank goodness they proved false. Adding a few trips over the climb to the final stage in 2025 has proven to be one of the greatest innovations in Tour history, and I’m thrilled they kept it in for another year.

Overall, the 2027 men’s Tour stacks up like this: 3,479 kilometers (2,162 miles) spread over 21 stages, with seven flat stages, six hilly stages, six mountain stages, two individual time trials, and three rest days.

With about eight months to go before the riders hit the road in Edinburgh, a lot can still happen. Most of the top contenders haven’t even confirmed their 2027 programs, which means there’s a chance, however unlikely, that at least one of them might not participate.

But as long as Pogačar is racing, the result, for better or worse, might be a foregone conclusion. And that’s not necessarily a bad thing. He is a once-in-a-lifetime talent, resetting the record books of the sport the way Eddy Merckx once did.

But as the saying goes: it’s the journey—not the destination—that matters here. Yes, we should enjoy speculating about who will and who won’t win the Tour de France—and how and when they manage to do it. But we can’t forget that the Tour de France offers at least 21 chances for something exciting and unexpected to unfold. Next year’s route might not prevent the inevitable, but it will certainly produce lots of great racing along the way. And I can’t wait.
