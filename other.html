GTA 6: How to Escape Police & Clear Heat

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

If you've ever played a GTA game prior to Grand Theft Auto 6, you're probably already familiar with the concept of Heat: commit a crime, and get chased by the cops. The more heinous your crimes, the greater your Wanted level — and the more forceful the police will be in taking you down.

But Heat and police pursuit work a little differently in GTA 6. While previous games make it fairly easy to escape the cops — just stay out of sight until they give up — there's a lot more at play in the latest entry. The police will track certain details about you, and recognizing what they are and how to change them could save you a lot of trouble.

Firstly, you generally don't get a Wanted level in GTA 6 by committing a crime. There are only a select few cases where this is true: if you enter a high-security area, like a military base or steal a plane from an airport, people will know immediately, and the cops will begin pursuit.

In order for you to get a Wanted level for the majority of your crimes in GTA 6, though, someone will actually have to report them, and the police will have to respond. This means you may be able to get away with murder in more isolated areas, but even a minor crime in a densely populated area could result in the cops being called immediately. Using weapons with suppressors can make it harder for bystanders to notice.

So, say the cops are chasing you: you'll see one to six stars in the top-right corner of the screen, reflecting the intensity of the police response. On your mini-map, you'll likely notice that you're within a dark red circle. To end the pursuit, you'll need to get out of this circle and stay out of it until it disappears, thus breaking the line of sight.

Looking at the color of the stars in the top-right corner of your screen can help you understand what the police are doing:

So, when the stars turn white, you want to run until they turn fully red, then stay hidden until they're only outlined in red or disappear completely. You'll also hear the cops say that they've lost you over your in-game police scanner.

In general, the best way to do this is by driving. If you're in a fast, maneuverable vehicle, you may just be able to lose the cops by driving in a straight line. But if you continue straight for long enough, cops may try to trap you by setting up a barricade. Your best bet is to swerve down side streets and change up your direction to confuse them.

Generally, it's not advisable to try and fight the police directly; you'll always be outnumbered and outclassed. The more crimes you commit in front of them, the higher your Wanted level will get, and the more squad cars, SWAT teams, and helicopters they'll throw at you.

Now, things get a little more complicated. In GTA 6, the cops don't give up looking for you after you escape the search area; they'll continue actively investigating around the area where the crime was committed. If you're seen again, the chase will resume. You could lay low and avoid the area until their search is over, but you'll eventually have to get used to clearing your Heat so you can get around freely.

So, after you've gotten away from the cops, look below the stars in the top-right corner of your screen. You should see a series of red circles with icons in them — so-called "Heat Icons." These tell you what, if any, details the police will recognize about you. If you change them, you'll have no problem going incognito.

However, if the area is still under active investigation, there's likely a larger police presence than normal. Any further crimes committed there will likely build on your previous Wanted level, and cops will start chasing you immediately. Better to keep your head down until the investigation is over.

This Everyday Vitamin May Ease Depression In Some People

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Could correcting this vitamin deficiency make a difference for people already receiving treatment for depression?

Could correcting this vitamin deficiency make a difference for people already receiving treatment for depression?

Vitamin D supplements may ease symptoms in some people with depression, according to a review of the evidence.

Several small trials found improvements, including in people who started out with low vitamin D levels.

Other trials found little or no benefit, and one large study found that supplements did not lower the chances of developing depression.

The link is plausible because vitamin D may influence inflammation and chemical signalling in the brain, both of which are relevant to mood.

Depression itself could also lower vitamin D levels if it leaves people with less energy to go outdoors.

That makes it important to ask two separate questions: whether correcting a deficiency can help someone who is already depressed and whether taking vitamin D can prevent depression in the first place.

Researchers at the University of Oradea reviewed studies measuring vitamin D levels in people with and without depression, as well as trials testing whether supplements changed their symptoms.

One small trial recruited people who had both major depression and a vitamin D deficiency.

Both groups continued with their usual care, but after about three months, depression scores were lower in the group given a vitamin D injection than in the placebo group (Vellekkatt et al., 2020).

Differences in who took part, the doses they received and how symptoms were measured made the trials difficult to compare.

A trial designed to test prevention followed more than 18,000 adults aged 50 and over who had no clinically relevant depressive symptoms when they joined.

Over about five years, participants who took daily vitamin D were about as likely to develop depression or clinically relevant symptoms as those who took a placebo (Okereke et al., 2020).

Vitamin D receptors are found in parts of the brain such as the hippocampus, which is involved in memory and the response to stress.

The vitamin can also influence immune signals involved in inflammation.

This is one route through which it might affect mood in some people with depression.

Vitamin D levels also reflect habits that can change during depression, including what people eat and how much time they spend outdoors.

Someone who stops going for regular walks, for example, may get less sunlight and make less vitamin D as a result.

A blood test taken during an episode of depression cannot tell which change came first.

The question now is whether correcting a vitamin D deficiency can improve symptoms in people already receiving treatment for depression.

Answering it will take larger trials that recruit people with both conditions and follow them long enough to see whether any improvement lasts.

The study was published in the journal Pharmaceuticals (Roșian et al., 2025).

National Gallery of Canada receives major gift of modern and contemporary glass art

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Edward Hald, The Fireworks Bowl, 1930s. Collection of Charles and the late Andrea Bronfman © Edward Hald Estate. Photo: NGC

The National Gallery of Canada (NGC) in Ottawa is embarking on a new chapter in its collecting history with a promised gift of 56 modern and contemporary glass works from Canadian American businessman and philanthropist Charles Bronfman and his late wife, Andrea Bronfman. Accompanied by an endowment to establish the institution’s first curatorial position dedicated to decorative arts and design, the donation will help broaden the museum’s collection and increase its scholarly contributions.

“This is the gallery’s first gift of modern and 20th-century glass,” Jean-François Bélisle, director and chief executive of the NGC, tells The Art Newspaper. “It significantly expands our understanding of art history, bringing together artistic expression and material and technical innovation.”

The gifted Bronfman Collection spans more than a century of glass experimentation, with works by 29 artists from Canada, Europe, the United States and Asia. The collection traces the medium’s evolution from the sinuous forms of Art Nouveau and Arts and Crafts to the streamlined aesthetics of Art Deco and Modernism. Early 20th-century examples by Tiffany Studios, Émile Gallé and the renowned studio Daum demonstrate how artists pushed glass beyond traditional decorative applications.

Daum Frères, Lamp, around 1905. Collection of Charles and the late Andrea Bronfman Photo: NGC

The works also chart the emergence of studio glass as an independent discipline in the post-war period. Additional artists with works in the donation include Dale Chihuly, whose gravity-defying installations are in museums worldwide, as well as the studio glass pioneer Toots Zynsky and the well-known Canadian American artist Laura Donefer.

A selection of pieces from the collection is currently on view in the NGC’s European galleries (until 30 April 2027). Highlights of the installation include Edward Hald’s The Fireworks Bowl (around 1921), alongside architectural works by Tiffany and Frank Lloyd Wright.

“Harvey Littleton’s Red Crossed Form (1990) is another notable work that reflects the development of studio glass as an independent artistic medium,” says Anabelle Kienle Ponka, the NGC’s senior curator of European, American and Asian Art. “The presentation brings together very different approaches to glass, from the decorative traditions of the early 20th century to the formal and technical experimentation of contemporary artists.”

Tiffany Studios, Lamp, around 1900. Collection of Charles and the late Andrea Bronfman Photo: NGC

The international scope of the donation highlights the exchange of technical traditions, particularly the influence of Venetian glassmaking on successive generations of artists, Kienle Ponka says. Together, the works demonstrate the medium’s versatility across sculpture, architecture and ornamentation.

Equally significant for the NGC is the accompanying endowment, which will fund the museum’s first curatorial position dedicated to decorative arts and design. The NGC has not shared the sum of the endowment, but notes that the post will support research, exhibitions and future acquisitions, establishing a foundation for sustained institutional engagement with the field.

“[The donation’s] significance extends well beyond this gift,” Bélisle says. “The position will allow us to pursue further acquisitions, develop exhibitions and scholarship, and build relationships with artists and institutions.”

Installation view of works by Lino Tagliapietra and Harvey Littleton from the Bronfman Glass Collection, National Gallery of Canada, Ottawa, until 30 April 2027. Photo: National Gallery of Canada, Ottawa

The donation builds on a long history of the Bronfman family’s philanthropy in Canada's cultural sector. “My late wife Andy and I were admirers of the art of glass making and wanted to share this love with other Canadians,” Charles Bronfman said in a statement. “With the planned celebrations marking the upcoming 100th birthday of my sister, Phyllis Lambert, founder of the Canadian Centre for Architecture, and the 50th anniversary of the Canada Council for the Arts’ Saidye Bronfman Award, named after my mother, I am truly delighted to be contributing in this meaningful way to the national collections.”

The NGC has received other transformative gifts in recent years, including a 2025 donation of 61 contemporary works valued at C$22.8m ($16.8m) from collector Bob Rennie and his family. “The Bronfman gift is significant in a different way,” Bélisle says. “Its importance lies not only in the quality and range of the works, but also in the accompanying endowment, which will provide the expertise and resources to develop decorative arts and design as a sustained area of collecting and scholarship.”

To start, make your way south to Pelican Valley in the Gullet Marsh region. Located around the center of the swamp (circled above) is a lone research station that you can dock your boat next to. If you're struggling to find it, I recommend looking for the building with all the tall antennas sticking out of it, as this can act as a visual marker for where the station is.

There's no combat or anything required to enter, as you won't actually be fighting the mutated crab.

If you want one of GTA 6's best shotguns for free from the start of the game, look no further.

Once you arrive at the research station, don't bother heading inside just yet. Instead, go around the back of the large house and look for a breaker box on the wall. Approach and interact with the box to restore power to the building. Now go inside the research station and head down the short hallway into the scientist's bedroom.

On the floor near the door are some words carved into the wood. Hold down the Left Trigger when near it to examine the writing and make note of the word CLEAVES that is at the bottom. Once you've examined it, return to the living room and approach the computer in the corner.

Fight it off via mashing the Square (PlayStation) / X (Xbox) button, and then leave the main building. Go back to where the breaker box was and look for a shack behind the house. Inside, you will find a bunch of terrariums where the crabs were being studied.

Specifically, look for a room with a much larger terrarium and examine the clipboard on the table next to it. You'll see that crab 36 was the one that was a success, meaning the code to the safe inside of the research station is 0072.

We get this number because the note on the computer states that the code is DOUBLE SUCCESS, meaning you need to double the number of the crab that survived the experiment. Inside the safe, you will find another note about an emergency protocol and $1,100 to spend.

We're not done yet. After you open the safe, head outside of the main building and look for a metal gate you can walk through along the wall (shown below). Follow the path to another shack on the water and enter the code 0212 at the door to open it.

Next to the body of the scientist, you'll find a Stash Bag with $10,000 Marked Bills and the Crabshack VF5 legendary submachine gun. If you're visiting here early, leave the Stash Bag to grab once you can sell the Marked Bills. You can then open the garage door, allowing you to drive the jet ski out!
