Matthew Hayden drops 'more smoke' remark on Sandpapergate, questions Australia's handling of 2018 ball-tampering scandal

📅 Nov 22, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Eight years after the infamous Sandpapergate scandal rocked Australian cricket, the controversy surrounding the 2018 ball-tampering incident refuses to die down. Fresh revelations from Cameron Bancroft and Usman Khawaja have once again brought questions of accountability and transparency into focus, with former Australia opener Matthew Hayden now calling for a fair and independent assessment of the entire episode.Hayden believes several questions surrounding the Cape Town Test against South Africa remain unanswered and has urged Cricket Australia to establish a transparent process that allows everyone involved to present their version of events."It's just undeniable that there is a lot more smoke to come out of this fire," Hayden said while speaking on JioStar's 'Sandpapergate: The Unsettled Debate'.The former opener stressed that Australian cricket needs to move beyond the original sanctions and establish whether the full extent of the incident was ever investigated. He also insisted that reconciliation and trust would remain difficult as long as doubts continued to surround the scandal.

Hayden argued that Cricket Australia should take ownership of the process and consider an independent assessment to establish whether further investigation is warranted."Whether it's with Cricket Australia or an independent assessment process, I think we need to ask specific questions, and we need to identify if there are grounds for further inquiry, and then proceed with a clear scope and a fair process for everyone involved. There has to be some ownership over the process. Reconciliation is never going to be there for as long as there are question marks over integrity and trust," he said.The 54-year-old also emphasised that the process should not be restricted to current players, adding that those who were part of the 2018 South Africa tour and have since retired should also be given an opportunity to speak in an official capacity.He specifically mentioned David Warner and Usman Khawaja, along with other former players who have recently spoken about the controversy."It's unimaginable to think that the game can't actually get itself into a position where it at least understands a little bit further around the seriousness of this matter, and then come up with some sort of process that entitles not only the current players, but also the players that were involved in that series, who have now subsequently retired, David Warner and Usman Khawaja, to have their say in an official capacity, and the other three that have come out in support of it as well. I think as long as that gate remains open, there will be a distrust issue," Hayden added.

The debate has resurfaced following recent comments from Bancroft and Khawaja, who have questioned whether knowledge of the ball-tampering plan was limited to the three players who were initially punished.During the third Test against South Africa in Cape Town in March 2018, Bancroft was caught on television cameras using sandpaper to alter the condition of the ball. The incident triggered one of the biggest crises in Australian cricket and resulted in suspensions for Bancroft, then captain Steve Smith and vice-captain David Warner.In a recent column for The West Australian, Bancroft reportedly detailed conversations with members of the Australian bowling group about generating reverse swing. His account included discussions involving Pat Cummins and suggested that awareness of the plan may have extended beyond the three players who were sanctioned.Bancroft also claimed that his actions had been encouraged and celebrated within the team, adding another dimension to the long-running controversy.Khawaja has similarly raised questions about how much the wider Australian dressing room knew about the incident. Cummins, however, has strongly rejected the allegations, describing them as "utter nonsense".

Hayden also questioned Cricket Australia's response to the latest revelations, arguing that the governing body must address the lingering doubts if it wants to restore public confidence.He acknowledged that the International Cricket Council (ICC) had strengthened its regulations following the incident, including expanding its rules and empowering on-field umpires to deal with ball tampering. However, he believes the broader questions surrounding the Australian team's conduct still need to be addressed."Until there is some further position on this from the authority, and that's Cricket Australia in this case, because the ICC have had their say. They've gone, 'Right, our rules have now expanded. We've got new policies. We've got empowerment of our umpires.' That's a really good thing that's come out of this, because we don't want to see unnecessary tampering of the ball. But Cricket Australia have taken a very definite position on this, and nothing is going to change public opinion," Hayden said.Drawing attention to the importance of a cricket ball to fast bowlers, Hayden questioned the suggestion that members of the bowling group could have been unaware of any plans to alter its condition."If you talk to a first-grade cricketer who's a fast bowler, and you tell him that he doesn't know what ball he's got in their hand, just like you heard Irfan say before, they will say, 'Mate, you are kidding. That'd be like someone tampering with my bat. And I know exactly every centimetre of that bat. It's just undeniable that there is a lot more smoke to come out of this fire," he added.

When Dirk Kuyt shamelessly stole Luis Suarez’s wondergoal v Man Utd

📅 Nov 22, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

When you think of Liverpool’s wins over Manchester United, Dirk Kuyt’s hat-trick often springs to mind. However, in the process, the Dutchman took away one of the great Premier League goals from Luis Suarez.

The 2010-11 season was hardly a vintage one at Anfield. Roy Hodgson had been put out of his misery in January with the Reds down in 12th, and the remainder of the campaign was mostly about restoring a bit of pride.

A great deal of that began in the winter transfer window with a £35million move for Andy Carroll, but it was the other mid-season attacking arrival who hit the ground running.

While Liverpool’s away form left a bit to be desired all season, their displays at Anfield picked up under Kenny Dalglish, thanks in no small part to the contribution of Luis Suarez.

The Uruguayan had already scored on his debut against Stoke, adding to a phenomenal record at previous club Ajax, to help ensure Liverpool were riding a four-game unbeaten home run when they welcomed United in February.

Then, with around half an hour on the clock, he came centimetres from scoring the kind of goal which would make any player an instant hero… only for Kuyt to take that moment away from him.

At the time, Manchester United weren’t used to players approaching them with no fear. They were unbeaten in the league until early February and had made it through their Champions League group at a canter with just one goal conceded.

Yet as Suarez took on one defender, then another, then another still, none of that seemed to matter.

He almost uses Rafael as a prop to begin with, but he’s not just holding off the right-back – this is a game of 4D chess where he’s not only thinking about the next move but about the next board.

For this reason, Michael Carrick’s intervention never stood a chance of affecting the run. He’s stuck between getting to where Suarez was and where he should be and realises too late that neither of these should be the target.

More strikingly, though, Suarez’s movement and subtlety were enough to cause Wes Brown to lose his mind.

Broadly speaking, United’s defenders weren’t all that keen on being embarrassed. As such, you wouldn’t expect the England international to dive in unless he was absolutely certain of winning the ball.

Still with us? Good. You might be trying to figure out how that theory sits with the manic desperation of the actual slide, but the answer is simple: Brown was certain of success. He’d just never before encountered anyone capable of overriding this certainty with such certainty of their own.

Suarez had essentially ripped out Brown’s soul and handed it to him on an ever-elusive platter. The defender only played three more games for United after that trip to Anfield, and even that feels generous on the back of this.

If you think all of that deserved a goal, you’re right. But, with the benefit of hindsight, few neutrals will care.

🗓️ #OnThisDay in 2011, @Kuyt netted a memorable hat-trick vs @ManUtd… 💪🔴 pic.twitter.com/HGEuMot1Hm

If we were looking at this in neutral terms, it would be the act of a selfish goal-hanger ruining a newcomer’s moment of glory. But you can’t not watch it through the prism of what we now know of Kuyt and Suarez.

You’d be hard-pressed to find someone who doesn’t have time for Kuyt, whose everyman qualities turned him into someone we had no trouble rooting for. As for Suarez? Well, we all know what happened in the years after this game.

Compare it to a similar goal, during Portugal’s game against Spain, where Nani took the Kuyt role and the part of Suarez was played by Cristiano Ronaldo. The fury displayed by Ronaldo gave us life and was enough to let anyone forget the surrounding context.

Sometimes the begrudging credit for a divisive superstar isn’t close to registering on the same scale as the pride you take in seeing them brought back down to earth.

Gemma Arterton's 'Secret Service' Is a Twisty Spy Thriller That Never Quite Sticks | Review

There's something so mesmerizing about a good spy story. Double lives, high stakes, tense action, not knowing who to trust; all of these elements keep you on the edge of your seat in all the best ways. The genre has continued to thrive on television, with 2026 being an exceptional year for British spy thrillers in particular. New seasons of Slow Horses, Black Doves, and The Night Manager have kept fans entertained — not to mention the announcement of a new James Bond, and the frenzy that's caused has revealed the level of viewers' investment in these stories.

MGM+ is adding to the year of spy thrillers with its series, Secret Service, starring Gemma Arterton. Like Slow Horses, Secret Service is adapted from the Tom Bradby book of the same name, part of a trilogy centered on its protagonist, Kate Henderson. While the new adaptation succeeds in delivering a season with twists and intrigue, it doesn’t do enough to keep you engaged with its characters beyond what the plot requires.

Secret Service revolves around Kate Henderson (Gemma Arterton) — wife, mother of two, and senior MI6 officer in charge of the Russia Desk. At the start of the series, she's leading an investigation into the head of Russian intelligence. Through her team's work, they uncover that a high-ranking UK politician is working as a Russian asset and is ready to move into the Prime Minister's role. At first, this information is met with skepticism, but when Prime Minister Anthony Fletcher (Steven Elder) announces his pancreatic cancer and his resignation, it becomes a race against time to figure out who the Russian asset is — pushing Kate to the brink and forcing her to question who she can even trust.

Throughout its five-episode mission, Secret Service keeps the narrative moving forward, reflecting the urgency of the situation at hand. The story pops back and forth between London and Malta, as Kate's team keeps tabs on their informant, Lena (Alma Prelec), a nanny to the Director of the SVR. The tension ramps up when Lena is discovered by one of Igor Borodin's (Miglen Mirtchev) operatives as a mole. Kate finds herself split, fighting a war on multiple fronts: uncovering the Russian asset, extracting Lena out of Malta, and being present with her family when she's home. With its short episode count, Secret Service is on a mission to keep its story tight, and the show does a great job of not padding its runtime.

At the center of Secret Service is Arterton, who has proven her acting prowess within the spy genre in films like Quantum of Solace and The King's Man, and it remains true here. Her performance anchors the series, as Kate does what she can to not let the weight of the situation break her, only allowing herself to express vulnerability to her husband, Stuart (Rafe Spall), and her mentor, Rose Trewen (Alex Kingston). Of the characters in the series, Kate is the most well-rounded and complex, yet despite this, moments of depth in her character and relationships are cut short in favor of the plot. There's very little to anchor audiences to her or even remember her after the credits roll in the finale.

Without Kate, everyone else around her falls flat. Kyril Markov (Oleg Levin), one of Borodin's operatives, definitely ratchets up the tension once he realizes that Lena is a mole, but he's also not nearly as intimidating as he could’ve been, especially since Markov has a reputation within the Russian Desk. In the same vein, there's much to be desired from Stuart. Beyond his job and working relationship with the Foreign Secretary, Imogen Conrad (Amaka Okafor), there's very little audiences are getting with him. We’re treated to glimpses of Kate and Stuart's marriage, heavily leaning on the settled domesticity of this point in their relationship. To really bring this home, Alex Kingston isn't given nearly enough when Rose has so much potential. Sure, there are plot reasons as to why this is, but it's not enough to remain attached to the characters beyond Kate.

The show has several good twists to hold your attention as Kate attempts to identify the Russian asset. Like a good mystery, it doesn't overstay its welcome, nor does it dumb itself down for the audience.

In a TV age where there are too many options to choose from, not every show is going to stick with you. Sometimes, all you need to be entertained is a good plot that keeps you hitting play on the next episode. That's the case with Secret Service — it's engaging in the moment for the thriller it is; no more, no less. If this is all you get from the series, it's still time well spent. Viewers won't be left in the dark; the show wraps up its story with very few loose ends.

Should Secret Service be picked up for a second season, one that adapts either of the remaining books in Bradby's trilogy, it would be advantageous to use the show's middle chapter to dive deeper into Kate's psyche and the remaining relationships in her circle. There's potential to enhance the narrative, for the cast to really stretch their acting muscles, and to further explore the political consequences. Otherwise, Secret Service will just come and go as you scroll through your streaming services to find what to watch next.

15 Scent Control Strategies for Deer Hunting That Actually Work

📅 Nov 22, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Early Black Friday deals are here — Shop hunting gear, fishing tackle, optics, and more!

Published Nov 22, 2026 10:00 AM EST

A heavy-antlered 12-point buck materializes on the trail. He’s only five steps from bow range, but then he throws his nose in the air, goes rigid with nervousness, and waves the white flag. Game over. That’s all, folks.

We all know that any successful deer hunt starts with not getting busted by a whitetail’s incredibly powerful sense of smell. And today there’s a whole collection of gear that’s marketed as being able to beat a deer’s nose. Many of the scent control gear and strategies are gimmicks, but some are not.

With careful scent control, you can give yourself a slight edge that just might mean the difference between getting busted and getting a shot. Here are scent-reduction efforts that work, with 15 steps that hunters can use to minimize scent before, during, and after the hunt.

Hunters must understand they will never completely neutralize the scent they emit. Furthermore, they will never completely beat a whitetail’s nose. However, a hunter can minimize their scent.

This won’t keep a deer from smelling you, but it might buy you a little extra time or a few more yards. It can also make all the difference in a non-target deer just moving along rather than alarm snorting.

You don’t have to use all of the following strategies just to shoot a deer. But, implementing at least some of them will certainly make you more effective in the stand.

Deer hunters should consider a long-term scent control mentality. Rather than just preparing the day of the hunt, start mitigating scent long before. During hunting season, consider cutting the smelly shampoos, soaps, colognes, deodorants, and aftershaves out of your routine. This will help keep your clothes smelling more natural, too.

Washing your hunting clothes is the next step. Treat with scent-reduction laundry detergent. Such laundry detergent doesn’t remove all scent, but it knocks out a lot of it and it doesn’t add unnatural scents. (Also, on a deer vision note, ensure that the detergent does not contain UV brighteners.)

Once your hunting clothes are clean, store them in a dedicated container. A portable plastic tote that has a place at home, but easily loads into a vehicle, is the best choice. This should help minimize odors that might contaminate the garment fibers. A lot of hunters like to put a pine branch in their tote to keep clothes smelling fresh and piney (and not like plastic).

In addition to hunting clothing, hunters should also clean their other gear. Backpacks and boots absorb the scents around them. So, store these and other hunting items in areas that aren’t stinky. Use a dedicated storage bin for this gear, too.

The day of the hunt, take a shower with shampoos and soaps that are designed to reduce human odor without adding artificial scents. This should help neutralize some (but not all) of the existing surface scent on your hair and skin.

Brush your teeth, but not with a super-smelly toothpaste. Instead, use something with a natural scent, or no scent at all. Then, eat an apple to help further mitigate scent that might originate from the mouth and throat. Avoid strong-smelling drinks and foods, such as coffee or spicy grub which lead to bad breath.

A lot of hunters use ozone in the field (like Ozonics), but it’s also applicable for treating hunting clothing. Some products are designed to work in conjunction with boxes to clean clothing and remove unwanted scent.

Used improperly, Ozone can destroy clothing. So Wildgame innovations recently introduced a ZeroTrace tote and device that is designed to treat gear without destroying it.

To minimize unwanted scent particles from making it onto your hunting clothes, consider dressing once in the field. This keeps away scent from handling pets, visiting gas stations, or other stops between home and the hunt. Getting dressed in the field during a cold or rainy day can be a hassle, but it’s worth it.

Rubber boots can contain some scent, but they only achieve this if kept uncontaminated from foreign scents. Don’t wear your deer hunting boots around town where they might pick up unwanted odors.

Hunters have many reasons for field wipes. But in terms of scent control, wiping off sweat with a field wipe will certainly help. Just make sure the version you use is scent-free.

Even better than wiping away sweat, is to not break a sweat at all. Sweat just adds an entire fresh new layer of scent. In the field, do everything possible to prevent or reduce sweating. Wait to put on your coat until you get to your stand.. Walk slowly. Remain in the shade. Take more time to get settled in the treestand.

Perhaps the most important aspect of scent control is managing the direction that your scent travels. This means choosing good entry and exit routes, and ensuring your wind doesn’t carry over into areas that deer frequent (especially bedding areas and core feeding areas).

Those who hope to minimize scent even further should deploy hunting blinds that are completely enclosed. Those which have completely sealed doors and windows can hold most, if not all, scent within the blind. Keeping windows closed until it’s  time to shoot is an excellent way to bottle up scent.

Some hunters might cleverly use wind blockers, which are land features that keep deer from getting downwind. Examples include hunting along the edge of a bluff, river, hinge-cut, or lake. Similarly, you can make a mock scrapes on either side of your scent cone, which might stop a buck just long enough before he gets downwind of you.

The rising and falling thermals can be helpful or a huge pain in the butt. When used wisely, and with the right stand locations, thermals will carry scent up and away from deer. For example, hunting up high on a ridge when morning thermals carry scent upward makes it very difficult for deer to smell hunters. Likewise, hunting down low in a bottom when afternoon thermals carry scent downward can achieve the same result.

Remember, no matter how far you take scent control strategies, deer will eventually smell you. It’s important to take all precautions, and implement each step outlined above. Ultimately, careful stand selection that puts the wind in your favor will be the most important step for not getting busted.

But don’t get discouraged when the inevitable happens. Just keep hunting. After all, you can’t really beat a deer’s nose, and it wouldn’t be fun if we won every time anyway.
