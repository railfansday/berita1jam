West Ham fans’ furious reaction raises an uncomfortable question

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

As the Hammers subsided to their first away defeat in the Championship, long suffering supporters, who’d undertaken the 480 mile round trip to be served up a pretty spineless team performance, decided to let their team have it with both barrels.

After last week’s displeasure culminating an Hammers fans singing ‘sacked in the morning’ to their own manager, this weekend brought the players into the crosshairs as the next logical target for their ire.

..With Nuno Espirito Santo out of the job and with Scott Parker just 72 hours in, the 7,500+ travelling fans turned on their stars.

Under no illusions after the result – and the away fans’ response – yesterday: Scott Parker

..As well as booing their own goal keeper pretty much throughout, if the reports on the radio were correct.

Traditionalists will likely be aghast at the thought of fans undermining their own team in this manner, whilst those who participated no doubt feel it was the only way to show their contempt for the perceived lack of commitment and effort which those eleven representatives  of the Claret and Blue heritage displayed.

Not to mention the hundreds of pounds it costs each supporter, every game, merely to arrive at the turnstiles in support of our club. But is it the ‘right’ thing to do?

For me – it’s a no. I started attending games with my son when he was ten years old and however bad it got (it was pretty dire in that season of 2003-4) we never left early, never booed our own players (Luis Boa Morte excepted, I must confess to once letting my frustration with his indolence bubble over) and always, always applauded those who’d put in a shift, regardless of outcome. And I’d not even heard ‘you’re not fit to wear the shirt‘ sung by West Ham fans in my couple of decades’ worth of attending games.

I just don’t see it helping team morale or positively influencing the final outcome. Which is, after all, the point of ‘supporters’.

Giving ‘The Sun’ another gleeful headline to point out our sufferings sticks in my throat too.

But don’t get me wrong, each has absolutely the right to their own response. Just don’t see it helping is all.

I still watch YouTube repeats of the Cup Final of 2006 hoping in vain that Shaka Hislop grows another six inches and stops Steven Gerrard's injury time equaliser. Can tell I'm getting old knowing I watched both Mark Noble's debut and his last game at West Ham. Several players needing bounce-back performances: The five key battles in Genoa vs. Milan

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

AC Milan will make the trip to the Stadio Luigi Ferraris to face a Genoa side in good recent form in a Sunday matinee.

For Milan, it is a familiar feeling of having to bounce back at the weekend after a midweek defeat. The Bournemouth loss was followed up by a win over Udinese, then the collapse against Bologna preceded the derby win.

The most recent setback against Ferencvaros on Thursday has left another bitter feeling, so now Ruben Amorim must galvanise his team again. It will not be an easy one by any stretch though, given Genoa have won four of their last five under Daniele De Rossi, and three in a row.

Speaking of needing a bounce-back, Adrien Rabiot was disappointing in the loss against the Hungarian side. Nonetheless, the Frenchman has bagged five goals and three assists in his first eight league appearances this season. Sergej Milinkovic-Savic was the last to get as many in the first nine Serie A games back in 2022-23, with 10 contributions (three goals and seven assists).

Rabiot has been involved in a goal in each of his last five appearances, and the last Milan midfielder to do better in Serie A was Kaka in 2004 (seven). His opponent today will be Morten Frendrup, someone that Milan have been repeatedly linked with over the years. The Dane remains Genoa’s metronome in midfield, and the shield in front of the back three.

Summer signing Milutin Osmajić has been Genoa’s main threat in quite a low-scoring side, with four goals to his name in Serie A. He is keeping Lorenzo Colombo out of the side, though the Italian has provided a couple of assists for the Montenegrin when they have been fielded together in a front two.

After a disastrous performance against Ferencvaros, Koni De Winter will be hoping for a much better outing this afternoon. The Belgian has been in the headlines for rejecting a Belgium call-up meaning he will stay over the break, and he will aim to channel that same display against Marcus Thuram in the derby.

In addition to Rabiot, the other player to establish himself as a major threat for Milan is Diego Moreira. The Belgian has been directly involved in seven goals (four goals and three assists) in his first six Serie A appearances for the Rossoneri, and that came after a bit of a slow start too. He was decisive in the derby, with two assists and a beautiful free-kick goal.

He is expected to have quite a free-ranging role on the right, but what will be very important is getting players around Goncalo Ramos to try and unlock the Portuguese. So, Moreira will likely come up against the left-sided centre-back Johan Vasquez, someone who has become a mainstay in the Genoa side having entered his fifth season there. He does have three bookings in his first nine games, so that could be something to target.

Speaking of Goncalo Ramos, the concerns about his scoring form (or lack thereof) are beginning to grow into full-blown panic. He still has only two Serie A goals to his name so far, one of which was a penalty, and his overall involvement in the Rossoneri’s attacking play seems to be fluctuating between very little and non-existent.

Amorim and the team must get him firing, or it will continue to look like €70m+ spent very badly. Today will not be an easy duel for the former PSG man either as he faces Leo Ostigard, someone who will be well up for the physical battle. As we have seen, Ramos can get frustrated when he faces players who man-mark him, so he will have to be smart and sharp with his movement, ideally getting some more service too.

With an honourable mention going to Christian Pulisic vs. Alessandro Marcandalli, we had to end by mentioning Stephan El Shaarawy. He scored 21 goals in 83 league appearances for Milan, becoming a fan favourite during his time. The winger has also become the main creative force for the Grifone, having provided an assist in each of his last two league appearances.

Given his propensity to cut inside in order to create danger, both Mario Gila but especially Yunus Musah will have to watch the 34-year-old very closely indeed. Milan have been exposed in transition several times this season, and there is no doubt El Shaarawy would love to make a statement against his old club.

He’s also keen to finally see Limp Bizkit live. “I’ve seen a lot of crazy videos right now that are popping up everywhere,” he says.

Meanwhile, Simple Plan have started writing their seventh studio album, with work set to continue into 2027. There’s no release date, but Comeau says the band has a batch of songs it’s excited about, and no intention of rushing.

“We’ve been around for 25 years and we only have six albums,” he says. “So we take our time.”

The record will follow 2022’s Harder Than It Looks, and Comeau says the aim is to refine the band’s sound rather than overhaul it. “We know who we are,” he says. “We’re not going to turn around and make a black metal record.”

The harder challenge is competing with the band’s own past. Songs that soundtracked fans’ teenage years carry a weight that new material has to earn.

“When you get to this stage of your career, you’re almost playing against yourself,” Comeau says. “You’re playing against your history.”

“You’re not the new shiny young band anymore,” he adds. “So you’ve got to work harder to have people pay attention.”

That’s why he won’t put a deadline on it. “There’s never a good time for a bad record, and there’s never a bad time for a good record,” he says.

Next year also marks 25 years since the band’s debut album, No Pads, No Helmets…Just Balls. Comeau says a special-edition vinyl and a few anniversary performances are on the table, but not a full album tour.

“I think we’re bigger than just one album,” he says. “But that album was really consequential for us. It’s the one that started it all.”

Another milestone arrives March 9, 2027, when the band releases its first children’s picture book, I’m Just a Kid: The Story of Simple Plan, illustrated by Jomike Tejido. Comeau says it grew out of watching the band’s original fans grow up and start families of their own.

“We were with them throughout their difficult moments,” he says, recalling how songs about breakups, school and friendships became part of listeners’ teenage years. “Fast-forward 20 years, 25 years, now they’re starting families, they’re having kids. We want to be there with them in this new chapter of their lives.”

The book explores friendship and perseverance and includes nods for longtime fans. “It’s as much for the parents as it is for the kids,” Comeau says.

For all the anniversaries and nostalgia, Simple Plan have never broken up, gone on hiatus or staged a reunion, which is rare among their peers. Comeau believes the years since the pandemic have been some of the band’s best, with bigger crowds and a sharper focus on the live show.

“When we were young, you were going out and you were partying,” he says. “Now it’s like, no, we don’t do that anymore. We’re all doing yoga and stretching.”

“We’ve always just kept going,” he says. “I feel like there’s a lot of work to be done.”

“There’s no talk about a farewell tour,” Comeau says. “Not in the cards for now.”

Prime Video’s 3-Season Gritty Crime Thriller Is The Perfect NCIS Replacement

After more than two decades and over 500 episodes, NCIS has practically perfected the weekly crime investigation formula. From murders involving Navy personnel to the chemistry between its Major Case Response Team, there's a reason the CBS procedural has stayed popular for so long as one of the best crime dramas. Still, even longtime fans might want something darker than the usual case-of-the-week format. That's where Prime Video's Psycho-Pass comes in.

With three seasons and six feature films from Production I.G, Psycho-Pass offers a different take on the crime thriller. Written by Gen Urobuchi, the mind behind Fate/Zero and Puella Magi Madoka Magica, the series moves its investigations to a disturbing version of 22nd-century Japan. The government doesn't just punish criminals after a crime. Its nationwide Sibyl System monitors citizens' mental states, stress levels, and personalities to calculate their chances of becoming criminals through a score called the Crime Coefficient.

That's what sets it apart from NCIS. The Public Safety Bureau's Criminal Investigation Division still tracks dangerous suspects, but its Dominator handguns can decide whether someone gets arrested or executed based on that score. Sometimes, that judgment comes before a crime has even been committed. It's familiar detective work with a much darker idea of justice.

What makes Psycho-Pass such a good fit for NCIS viewers is that it gets the appeal of a proper investigative team. The story follows 20-year-old rookie Akane Tsunemori, who joins Division One of the Public Safety Bureau’s Criminal Investigation Department after topping her civil service exams. Much like Timothy McGee or Eleanor Bishop joining NCIS as fresh faces, Akane has to find her footing in an established team. The difference is that her new colleagues operate under a much darker system.

Division One has two ranks: Inspectors and Enforcers. Inspectors like Akane and her strict senior partner, Nobuchika Ginoza, oversee investigations, while Enforcers are latent criminals with Crime Coefficients above 100, released from lockup to help track down other offenders. They are essentially the Bureau's hunting dogs, and the Dominator handgun makes their work even more disturbing. It can switch from a Non-Lethal Paralyzer to a Lethal Eliminator depending on a suspect's Crime Coefficient.

Akane sees the system's cruelty on her first night when a terrified hostage is marked for execution after her trauma pushes her score into the lethal range. Akane stops her own lead Enforcer, Shinya Kogami, from pulling the trigger, establishing the show's central conflict between machine-made justice and human judgment.

The procedural appeal is just as strong. Shion Karanomori handles forensic analysis, Tomomi Masaoka brings old-school police experience, and Kogami uses sharp instincts to get inside suspects' heads. Like Abby Sciuto, Kasie Hines, Ducky Mallard, and Gibbs, each member brings something different to an investigation. The big difference? NCIS solves crimes after they happen; Psycho-Pass investigates people the system believes might commit them.

What makes Psycho-Pass worth watching for NCIS viewers is that it doesn't ditch the things that make a good crime procedural work. There's still a tight investigative team, forensic experts picking apart evidence, detectives following leads, and criminals who keep the department on its toes. The difference is that the crime scenes are in futuristic Tokyo, and the technology used to catch killers is just as disturbing as the crimes themselves.

And don't let the animation fool anyone into expecting something lighthearted. Psycho-Pass carries a TV-MA rating, with brutal murders, graphic violence, and some genuinely disturbing scenes. Across three seasons, it has plenty of room to build its characters and cases beyond a single mystery. It's a lot darker than NCIS, but that familiar mix of team investigations, sharp detective work, and dangerous recurring villains makes it an easy recommendation for crime thriller fans.

Michael Carrick said Bruno Fernandes’ attitude is “as good as ever” and he has no doubts about his commitment again after their falling-out.

The United manager dropped his captain after Fernandes criticised his tactics during the 4-1 defeat to Como and chose not to bring him on in the 3-0 loss to Bournemouth.

But Carrick and Fernandes held talks last week and the Portuguese returned to the team at Chelsea on Saturday before playing his part in their comeback win over Roma, scoring their first goal with a twice-taken penalty and providing the assist for Matheus Cunha’s equaliser.

“I think by his actions, the way he's played and how he dealt with even retaking the penalty and frame of mind he was in for that, I think that tells everything,” said Carrick. “Yeah, Bruno's as good as ever.”

The 45-year-old has not revealed if Fernandes apologised to him, adding: “What's actually said in that discussion, you'll be pleased to know I'll keep that to myself.

“But I think it's pretty clear we've moved in a good direction, and Bruno's commitment is undoubted for me of what he brings to this team and this club, and I think he showed that again this week.”

Carrick has challenged Cunha to replicate his catalytic display against Roma, when he won a penalty and scored two goals, to double his tally for the season.

“He has to repeat that performance,” the United manager said. “He was fantastic and that's close to his best, and that's the level where we want to see.”

Carrick has said before that he has not sought reassurances about his position during United’s slump and they have only won one of their last 10 games in all competitions.

He has not been told he will remain in charge next week. “No, no,” he replied, adding that his plans were unchanged. “Just carry on as absolute normal.”
