Travis Barker Reveals He Stopped Smoking Weed After Precancer Diagnosis: ‘I Loved Blunts’

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The Blink-182 drummer opened up about his decision to cut the kush on this week's Big Bro with Kid Cudi podcast.

Travis Barker had a very good reason for giving up weed. During a sit-down with Kid Cudi on this week’s episode of the Big Bro with Kid Cudi podcast, the 50-year-old Blink-182 drummer said he had to stop smoking his beloved blunts after receiving a scary diagnosis.

“I only quit because I got diagnosed with precancer of the esophagus,” Barker told Cudder on Wednesday’s (Nov. 11) episode. “I loved blunts and I would smoke too much.” Barker described trying to be healthier for his kids and taking up running and boxing to get in shape after years of going for a six mile run and then celebrating afterward with a cigarette and a blunt.

And while he misses smoking — ”I was so good at it” — Barker said he asked his doctor if the diagnosis was serious and he assured him it was. “He’s like, ‘This is your warning shot Trav. Don’t f–k around. You gotta stop smoking,'” he said the doc told him about his 10-15 blunt-per-day habit. “Or I would have never stopped. I thought I could be like Willie Nelson and smoke to the day I die. I feel like weed never hurt anybody, it just wasn’t right for me.”

Cudi countered that he had to go to rehab and “reestablish” his relationship with weed because he realized he was overdoing it at a time when he was smoking 15-20 blunts a day. “If I was awake, I was smoking, no matter what I was doing,” he said of his former habit.

Barker, who has a three-year-old son, Rocky Barker, with wife Kourtney Kardashian, as well as son Landon Barker, 23 and daughter Alabama Barker, 20 with his ex, Shanna Moakler — he also helped raise his stepdaughter, Atiana de la Hoya, 27 — admitted that he still thinks about firing up a blunt “all the time … I miss it. I’m not gonna lie.”

Luckily for him, running has helped keep him on the healthy path. “Many years later now, 20 years later, a couple miles a day keeps the demons away,” said Barker, who stopped using prescription drugs and other drugs when he was in a burn center for six months after surviving a deadly 2008 plane crash that killed four people, including his assistant and bodyguard. “My running started getting bigger and more important to me than smoking.”

He added, “It really helped me stay sober ’cause you can’t run you a half a marathon if you’re doing drugs. It’s gonna to be the hardest 13.1 miles of your life.”

Last month, Barker said he had undergone a cubital tunnel release surgery to fix a pinched nerve in his elbow that was causing numbness and weakness in his hands.

BBC Boss Confronted By Angry Staff Over “Banning” Of Trans Rights Symbols In TV Shows

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

EXCLUSIVE: BBC director general Matt Brittin has told staff that the UK broadcaster is “not an anti-LGBTQ+ organization” after facing angry questions about the removal of transgender rights symbols in TV shows.

During an internal town hall meeting on Thursday, Brittin was asked at least a dozen questions about the corporation’s approach to LGBTQ+ issues and content.

It follows the BBC blurring out the trans rights slogan, “Protect the Dolls,” on the T-shirt of Bethany Antonia, the House of the Dragon actress who competed on Strictly Come Dancing. Later in October, the BBC digitally removed LGBTQ+ pride flag pins being worn by contestants on University Challenge, the quiz show hosted by Amol Rajan. During the BBC town hall meeting, one employee contrasted the removal of the LGBTQ+ symbols with the broadcaster’s insistence on presenters wearing Royal British Legion Poppy badges, honouring members of the armed forces. Watch on Deadline

In the comment, which was reviewed by Deadline and received more than 100 upvotes, the staffer added: “Is this not hypocritical and suggests the BBC is an anti-LGBT organization (and particularly the T part of LGBT)?”

Per an audio of the meeting obtained by Deadline, Brittin responded: “We are definitely not an anti-LGBTQ+ organization, and we want to be inclusive of everyone.”

He continued: “There is a challenge, I think, for an organization which has editorial due impartiality at its heart, between how we do our journalism and reporting, and what the standards we hold our presentation of news in particular to, and the inclusive values of an organization that is one where everyone can thrive. And sometimes those are in conflict.

“We have very high bar editorial guidelines, which is where things like the poppy wearing comes from … But that’s something which we always need to kind of have a look at. We have to evolve our standards in line with how society is evolving.”

Brittin’s response did not seem to satisfy some insiders. “Given that the BBC isn’t an anti-trans organisation, why do so many trans people and allies think it is?” one asked.

Another added: “There are people aged 25+ who refuse to pay the BBC license due to the BBC’s stance on transphobia — can you explain what these guidelines are and why trans symbols of support are banned? The initial response did not seem to speak to a worry [that] the BBC silently supports transphobia.”

Others agreed. “How can we in good conscience expect LGBT audience members and their loved ones to pay for the ‘service’ of having their lives treated as a controversy?” an employee asked. Another added: “‘Protect The Dolls’ is not an editorial guideline to be followed or not. It is a fact, and anything less IS anti-LGBTQ+.”

During the internal meeting, Brittin referenced work the BBC is doing to roll out anti-semitism and anti-Islamophobia training for employees. This prompted one insider to ask: “When might we have anti-transphobia training at the BBC too? Trans people are being actively demonised and attacked, including by the BBC, although there is no focus on this at all.”

One employee questioned the BBC’s recent decisions to cancel RuPaul’s Drag Race UK and queer dating show I Kissed a Girl/Boy. They said it was “really damaging to LGBTQ+ members of the BBC.”

Another person commented on the BBC’s news output on transgender matters: “How many people in senior editorial roles believe that being trans is a political/debatable issue? I am seeing a growing dehumanisation in how trans people are referred to in news copy etc. We seem to have decided that readers are too stupid to understand the concept of a trans person.”

The removal of LGBTQ+ symbols is the latest flashpoint between the trans community and the BBC. In 2021, more than 20,000 people signed an open letter accusing the BBC of failing in its reporting on transgender matters. Trans rights advocates protested outside the BBC headquarters in 2021 and 2022, with demonstrators calling for a rethink of how the broadcaster represents the community.

Janet Jackson also presented Daddy Yankee with the person of the year honor.

Spanish singer Rosalía took home the top prize of album of the year at the 2026 Latin Grammys on Thursday night for her 2025 album Lux.

She opened the 27th annual Latin Grammy Awards, live from Sin City, with an emotive medley of her Lux tracks “La Rumba Del Perdón,” “La Perla” and “Dios Es Un Stalker,” joined by angelic dancers ornamented with halos atop a stage meant to look like heavenly clouds.

It was a reminder of the expansiveness of her album: an offbeat, full-hearted embrace of her classical training through huge orchestral movements, inspired by the lives of female saints. It was one for the history books: There were outfit changes, set changes and his native Puerto Rico always at the forefront as he ran through “Ella Me Levantó,” “Con Calma,” “BOTZ,” “Despacito,” “Lo Que Pasó, Pasó,” “Sonríele,” “Rompe,” “Pokito” and, of course, “Gasolina” — joined by Bizarrap, Bad Gyal, Feid, Jay Wheeler, French Montana, LL Cool J and Diljit Dosanjh.

When it came time for Daddy Yankee to receive his honor, it was delivered to him by Janet Jackson “with admiration and respect,” as she said in her speech. When he accepted the award, he concluded by saying “Puerto Rico, recuerda. … Puerto Rico no es pequeño. Pequeño el mapa.” In English: “Puerto Rico, remember. … Puerto Rico is not small. The map is small.”

The award show took place live from Las Vegas’ MGM Grand Garden Arena. It aired live on Univision, UniMás, Galavisión and ViX and hosted by actor, producer and musician Roselyn Sánchez.

For the first award of the night, Pablo Alboran and Backstreet Boys’ Howie D presented Juanes with best pop/rock album for Juanesteban.

Performances were at a high level: Karol G was joined by a full orchestra and 12 Mariachis for her música mexicana ballad “Ese Hombre es Malo.” Mon Laferte broke hearts with her soulful “Femme Fatale.” Silvana Estrada’s “Dime” bridged culture and heritage with her signature Venezuelan cuatro, a four-stringed instrument similar in structure to a ukulele.

Kany García stunned with her acoustic, aflamencado single “La Niña Que Fui.” The Uruguayan-born Jorge Drexler’s rendition of “Las Palabras” felt like theater. And Argentine rapper Milo J performed “Solifican12” and “Gil,” joined by the Latin American folk icon Gustavo Santaolalla.

Carín León brought the house down with “Desde Que Te Tengo,” as did Juanes and Vivir Quintana with “Humano.” Elena Rose was joined by sword-wielding dancers for “Alma.”

Brazilian superstar Anitta did a medley of her “Equilibrium” hits — “Desgraça,” “Choka Choka” and “Meia Noite” among them — an eclectic mix of Brazilian funk, samba, bossa nova, semba, reggae, electronic pop performed in Portuguese, Spanish and English.

Soon after, she was back on stage accepting the trophy for Portuguese language contemporary pop album. She used her speech to shine a light on the Black culture of her native Brazil.

There was a touching tribute to the late ranchera icon, Mexican singer-songwriter José Alfredo Jiménez, produced by Edgar Barrera: The Warning covered “Te Solté la Rienda,” Reik did “La Media Vuelta,” Sofía Reyes and Yami Safdie dueted “Si Nos Dejan” and Camila Fernández brought it home with a big belting “El Rey.” Not soon afterward, she headed back to the stage to perform a full-throated “Serpiente De Cascabel.”

It was the first of two tributes: The second was for Willie Colón, the social activist and Grammy-nominated architect of urban salsa music, who died in February of this year. His son, Miguel Colón, as well as Nathy Peluso and Luis Figueroa and others performed.

Awards were handed out quickly, too. Karol G took home the trophy for urban music album for Tropicoqueta. Xavi won contemporary Mexican album for Dosis, his first Latin Grammy. Fonseca was presented the trophy for contemporary tropical album shortly thereafter for his album, Antes Que El Tiempo Se Vaya.

Best new artist went to Colombian singer Aria Vega, who gave her acceptance speech through tears. This year, the Latin Recording Academy made changes to the category so that only artists with fewer than three albums or 25 singles released were eligible to win.

Most of the evening’s awards were handed out during a pre-televised Premiere Ceremony. That included Bobby Pulido winning the Latin Grammy for best Tejano album for his release Bobby Pulido & Friends: Una Tuya y Una Mía (Vol. 2/En Vivo). Just days earlier, Pulido made history in the U.S. midterm elections by securing a seat in the House of Representatives for the Democrats by flipping a Texas district seat.
