James Bond Director Reveals The Most Important Quality Of Robert Pattinson's 007

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Now that Robert Pattinson has officially been cast as James Bond, Amazon MGM's plans for the franchise are coming together. The new owners of the Bond IP are clearly sparing no expense for the upcoming "Bond 26," hiring a major director in Denis Villeneuve to shepherd the project, which will now be led by one of the biggest names in Hollywood. After the missteps that were Daniel Craig's last few films in the tux, it seems Amazon is aiming to re-establish Bond movies as must-see cinematic events. For Villeneuve, however, the star power of his Bond actor is less important than the ability to project the energy of a cold-blooded killer.

In an interview with GQ, the "Dune" director was asked what he looks for in a 007 actor. "A first quality is that I need to believe that this man is a killer," he replied. "Because more than a spy, Bond is an assassin, and I need that kind of strength. I need that kind of anger deep in the eye. It's not everybody who has that." Those who doubt whether Pattinson can do so need only take a glance at the casting announcement video, in which the actor's Bond emerges from the darkness aiming his Walther PPK and wearing a cold, resolute expression on his face. There's a definite edge to his brief appearance in the clip, and you can see why Villeneuve was impressed.

That said, it seems Pattinson wasn't actually the first choice for Bond. According to a recent report, Villeneuve initially settled on "Wake Up Dead Man: A Knives Out Mystery" actor Josh O'Connor, who ultimately dropped out and cleared the way for Pattinson. But that will almost certainly prove to be a positive thing.

Robert Pattinson's casting as James Bond breaks a 60-year tradition of the franchise introducing audiences to relative unknowns in the lead role. But that's a good thing, as Bond is in need of revitalization following the debacle that was 2021's "No Time to Die." Pattinson's star power will help restore the franchise's popularity. But the "Batman" star is more than just an A-lister with a built-in audience. He's easily one of the most talented actors of his generation, having demonstrated an extraordinary ability to completely transform on-screen. As Bond, he'll no doubt exude exactly the kind of subtly sinister energy Denis Villeneuve is looking for and which has been one of the most important aspects of the character since his introduction.

In 1962, "Dr. No" kicked off cinema's most enduring franchise by introducing audiences to Sean Connery in the lead role. Rather than casting an established star with the kind of refined energy you'd expect of a gentleman spy, producers Albert R. Broccoli and Harry Saltzman hired a working class former footballer from Scotland, and it was a masterstroke. 007 debuted on the big screen with an edge that only Connery could bring to the role, and that's been an integral aspect of the cinematic incarnation of the character ever since. Sure, director Terence Young helped round off some of Connery's rough edges, but he'd be nothing without the sense of danger the Scotsman brought to the screen. Thankfully, it seems both Villeneuve and Pattinson recognize that, and it's yet another aspect of this casting that bodes well for "Bond 26."

Denis Villeneuve clearly values the killer instinct aspect of James Bond above everything else. But the director has plenty of other requirements for the role. As he went on to tell GQ, "I need someone who has that kind of unapologetic panache, someone that [...] when he comes into the room everything starts to gravitate around him, that kind of strong charisma." It's a foundational aspect of Bond and a characteristic every actor to portray the spy has possessed. But that's not all Villeneuve is looking for.

The French-Canadian filmmaker went on to say he also looks for "sensuality" in his Bond actor, as well as "dark humor" and an ability to take "pleasure into being in deep s***." It's standard stuff for Bond, but it's reassuring to know Villeneuve has such a solid grasp of the fundamentals. Beyond that, the director described someone "strong, muscular, someone that is a force," as well as an individual with a "deep intelligence in the eyes" and who is "always ahead of us." Sounds about right to me. Thankfully, Robert Pattinson can fulfill every one of these requirements, which makes him one of the most exciting of all the James Bond actors.

Thus far, Bond fans have reacted to Pattinson's casting with positivity, even while there's been the predictable objections regarding his "Twilight" years and physicality. At this point, however, the British star has long since proved his ability to deliver consistently electric performances that always surprise. There's no reason to think his Bond portrayal will be any different. Heck, Villeneuve was convinced, and he clearly seems to know what he's talking about.

Packers fan kicked out of Lambeau Field for spreading loved one’s ashes after loss

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

A Green Bay Packers fan was ejected from Lambeau Field after spreading a loved one’s ashes onto the field following the team’s loss to the Minnesota Vikings.

The incident occurred on Sunday, November 15, after Green Bay suffered a 30-10 defeat at home. Police were called to the stadium after a spectator was spotted dispersing what authorities later confirmed to be cremated human remains.

According to local news station WBAY, officers spoke with the individual and confirmed that the ashes belonged to a loved one. The fan was subsequently removed from the stadium for violating local regulations.

Under Green Bay Municipal Code 24-117, spreading human ashes onto the playing field is considered unlawful conduct at a public event.

The Packers addressed the incident in a statement to WBAY, acknowledging that some supporters want to honor deceased friends and family members at the stadium while emphasizing that the practice is prohibited.

“While we understand the desire of some fans to have their ashes, or those of friends or family, spread at Lambeau Field, it is against the law and prohibited at the stadium,” the team said.

Rather than scattering cremated remains, the organization encouraged supporters to consider alternative ways of remembering loved ones, including its commemorative brick program.

“To honor a loved one’s legacy appropriately at Lambeau Field, we refer fans to the commemorative brick program. We also suggest they can coordinate tributes in other appropriate ways with their funeral home,” the Packers continued.

The program allows supporters to purchase personalized bricks honoring individuals, creating a permanent tribute at the historic stadium without violating its regulations.

To appoint the next comptroller general of the United States, follow the law

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The new approach being employed to appoint the next Comptroller General of the United States should raise a red flag. The Government Accountability Office (GAO), which is headed by the Comptroller General, plays an important role in helping Congress discharge its responsibilities under Article I of the U.S. Constitution, particularly in connection with oversight of the Executive Branch.

We were shocked to learn that U.S. Sen. Rand Paul (R-KY), Chairman of the Senate Committee on Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs (HSGAC), recently and spontaneously requested that names be submitted to him, under a short time constraint, to be recommended to the President for nomination as the next Comptroller General of the United States.

Under the GAO Act of 1980, a 10-member statutory bipartisan and bicameral Comptroller General Selection Commission was created. Chairman Paul is a member, since he is Chair of the HSGAC, but he is not the Chairman of the Commission. That Commission is required to jointly develop and transmit a list of at least three qualified nominees to the President. That is the law, and that is how the process of appointing the Comptroller General has been conducted since 1980.

The current vacancy for Comptroller General of the United States began on Dec. 30, 2025, and yet the Commission and its members have never met.

Equally shocking is that Sen. Paul took the liberty as HSGAC Chairman to insert an empty placeholder for an unnamed nominee for Comptroller General of the United States for a Nov. 19, 2026, Committee confirmation hearing.

Again, Sen. Paul did so even though the statutorily required Comptroller General Selection Commission has never convened or submitted a list of candidates. Then, on Nov. 16, President Trump submitted a nominee for confirmation. (We don’t want to name the candidate since we do not want to make it a personal issue.)

The General Accounting Office Act of 1980 included a revised method for appointing the Comptroller General. It recognized that the position was in the Legislative Branch and that GAO was the primary agency the Congress uses to conduct oversight of the Executive Branch. Therefore, it provided for a bipartisan and bicameral commission to recommend candidates to the President for possible appointment.

It also provided for a 15-year non-renewable term with extremely difficult removal requirements to help ensure the independence of the Comptroller General. This 15-year term is the longest for any appointed position in the federal government.

To be clear, the 1980 law doesn’t say in so many words that the President must pick from the Commission’s list. The Senate report on the bill said he could, “in his discretion,” look elsewhere, but should give the Commission’s recommendations “great weight.” You can’t weigh a list that doesn’t exist. Every Comptroller General nominated since 1980 came from a Commission list, and the law’s answer to a President who wants other options is to ask the Commission for more names, not to skip it. Henry Wray, a former GAO attorney who helped develop the 1980 law, says the current unprecedented process “violates both the letter and the spirit of the law.”

It is extremely important for Congress to follow the statutory process. Dave Walker and Gene Dodaro (two of the coauthors of this piece) were unanimously confirmed under this process in 1998 and 2010, respectively. Both of these appointments were made under the statutory commission method, which has worked like a charm.

With regard to potential candidates, any Comptroller General should be a highly qualified executive with relevant past experience who will operate in a professional, objective, fact-based, non-partisan, non-ideological, and non-personal manner. The Comptroller General must be independent from the Executive Branch, which the GAO audits, investigates, and evaluates on behalf of the Congress and the American people. In addition, any Comptroller General must have broad bipartisan support. In fact, every one of the past eight Comptrollers General was confirmed with overwhelming bipartisan support. All of these factors are of critical importance for the Comptroller General to be credible and for him or her, and the GAO, to be effective.

Since Senate Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs Committee Chairman Paul and President Trump have not followed the statutory requirements for the appointment of the Comptroller General, the Nov. 19, 2026, confirmation hearing should be canceled, and the normal appointment process should be adhered to.

What exactly are Flock cameras and how can you spot one?

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Flock cameras are everywhere, and they’re easier to miss than you think.

Basic descriptions of Flock cameras fall woefully short of their true capabilities and potential uses. Most people are familiar with the automated license plate reader (ALPR) version, usually pole-mounted and paired with a solar panel. If that were all there was to it, this would be a very short article. But Flock Safety (Flock Group Inc.) has become deeply intertwined with the defining terminology of ALPR cameras.

In truth, there are more than a dozen manufacturers and vendors of ALPRs; their products just aren't called Flock cameras, and each has its own aesthetic characteristics. For all intents and purposes, however, they are identical. If you see Motorola on the side of one, it likely does much of what a Flock does: read plates and log vehicles.

Most are vertically oval, narrow in width and fairly small, roughly the size of your hand. They're designed to capture your license plate and the time and place you passed by. Most are also accompanied by a solar panel. Leonardo and Genetec make the most visually distinct versions. Leonardo's are blockier, rectangular in shape, with some elongated versions. Genetec's are horizontally oval, with the same narrow width as Flock's variations, only flipped. Motorola manufactures a mix of the two.

The problem is that some Flock cameras can easily be mistaken for a standard traffic camera and vice versa. Because of this, it's difficult to accept the absolute accuracy of a project like DeFlock, which uses volunteers to identify Flock cameras visually. There's no centralized or formal verification system to independently confirm the sightings.

Flock also sells more than ALPRs. Depending on what the customer wants, the system can log details like roof racks on cars and bumper stickers. Some setups have microphones and others can recognize things like clothes and beards.

Flock cameras usually turn up where traffic funnels. They're often mounted on poles at intersections and busy traffic areas. Since many homeowners associations (HOAs) are major Flock camera customers, it shouldn't be a surprise to find them at neighborhood entrances. Shopping centers aren't off the list of potential locations either. The aesthetics aren't irrelevant, but since several manufacturers make them, there are a few other telltale signs to look for as well.

Flock cameras are meant to capture license plates, so look at where they're aimed. Check for Flock's bird logo on the camera and a solar panel above it. Because most are self-powered, there's usually little to no additional equipment nearby. Red light and speed cameras are typically larger and wired to traffic signals or enforcement poles. White, elongated cameras are another lookalike, but they mostly help traffic signals adjust to traffic flow. Some states also mount signs next to speed cameras so they're easy to identify.

Keep in mind that there are other variations of Flock cameras out there. The Flock Condor is a mounted sphere that rotates 360 degrees and tracks people. Naturally, these are mounted near areas with heavy pedestrian traffic. Flock also makes a mobile security trailer, a gunshot detector and a drone-as-first-responder. Because companies are using video integration tools to convert standard IP cameras into plate readers, it will be increasingly difficult to identify Flock or Flock-type cameras.

As a rule of thumb, if it's pointed at you, it's potentially watching you. But for a broader view, check the Atlas of Surveillance, a searchable database from the Electronic Frontier Foundation that shows what technology law enforcement agencies use. Search your town or county to see whether police are using plate readers.

“Neither Brody Harshbarger nor Eric Murphy called the landowners to request permission to retrieve the six-point bull and did not make a reasonable effort to retrieve the six-point bull,” according to investigation documents obtained by the Idaho Capital Sun. “Neither individual had permission to be shooting onto the private property in violation of Idaho Code.”

Harshbarger had already filled his elk tag the weekend before the two bulls were shot. His hometown, Ashton, is in the same county where the violations took place. That same county, Fremont, borders Yellowstone National Park and is in the region Harshbarger represents on the state’s seven-person commission. That commission sets statewide hunting, fishing, and trapping seasons plus bag limits and other regulations based on recommendations and research supplied by Idaho Department of Fish and Game scientists.

Each member of the commission, most often a resident hunter and/or fisherman, is appointed by the governor for a term of four years with a two-term maximum. The commission meets at least four times a year. Harshbarger hasn’t attended those meetings since his case opened after a tip received through the department’s Citizens Against Poaching in December. He hasn’t resigned and the Governor hasn’t dismissed him, but his profile has been removed from the commissions website.

“The Governor’s office is aware of Commissioner Harshbarger’s plea agreement,” Gov. Brad Little’s spokeswoman Emily Callihan told the Sun. “Gov. Little will be working with the Idaho Department of Fish and Game and stakeholders in the coming days to ensure the responsibilities of the position are fulfilled.”

Harshbarger was appointed to the commission in 2024. His sentencing for illegal activities in 2025 is scheduled for Dec. 9. With the new plea deal and the number of charges reduced, the fine is expected to be $500 plus a one-year loss of hunting privileges.
