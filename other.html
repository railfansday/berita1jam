Consob e Commissione Atelli firmano un protocollo: più controlli sui conti dei club di Serie A e B

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

La Consob e la Commissione indipendente per la verifica dell’equilibrio economico e finanziario delle società sportive professionistiche hanno sottoscritto oggi un Protocollo d’intesa con l’obiettivo di sviluppare, sulla base delle rispettive competenze, una collaborazione attiva nei settori di comune interesse.

Le due Autorità si coordineranno attraverso un tavolo tecnico al quale è affidato il compito di valutare le questioni che richiedano una trattazione congiunta, condividere le informazioni sulle rispettive iniziative, segnalare i casi in cui emergano ipotesi di anomalie. Il tavolo tecnico, che si riunirà almeno una volta l’anno e ogni qual volta sia ritenuto opportuno, è composto dai responsabili delle unità organizzative competenti in relazione alle materie trattate. Il Protocollo prevede inoltre la collaborazione nell’ambito di indagini conoscitive e proposte normative su materie di interesse comune.

“Una vigilanza efficace nello sport è indispensabile per garantire la sostenibilità a lungo termine di ogni disciplina”, ha detto il Presidente della Consob, Guido Stazi, sottolineando come “senza un giusto equilibrio tra partecipazione di base, corretta gestione delle risorse economiche e benessere degli atleti, lo sport rischia di smarrire il suo insostituibile valore sociale ed educativo. Questo Protocollo ci consentirà di attivare le più ampie sinergie nell'azione di controllo sulle società sportive quotate e sui revisori delle squadre di calcio di Serie A e B, rafforzando la trasparenza e la solidità dell'intero sistema. I miei più sentiti auguri di buon lavoro a questa nuova Autorità, chiamata a svolgere un ruolo fondamentale per la tutela di un ecosistema sportivo sano”.

Per il Presidente della Commissione indipendente per la verifica dell’equilibrio economico e finanziario delle società sportive professionistiche, Massimiliano Atelli, “il Protocollo firmato oggi, oltre a dare attuazione ad una specifica disposizione della legge istitutiva della nostra Commissione, rappresenta un passaggio essenziale nella definizione del nuovo modello italiano di vigilanza economico-finanziaria sulle società sportive professionistiche. Un modello basato sul coordinamento tra i diversi attori istituzionali, e con specifico riferimento al segmento dei club quotati, a quello tra le risalenti attribuzioni della Consob e quelle della nuova Autorità indipendente che rappresentiamo".

Mosseri made the statement on Threads, where he also said that he came to the conclusion the more he looked at the details of the device.

A user responded, saying the best Instagram experience on the foldable is in portrait mode when the bigger screen is open, because the image takes up most of the display. In another response, Mosseri explained that Instagram was designed for tall vertical screens. The Duo's form factor is far from typical, even when folded, as it's wider and shorter compared to most smartphones. As an example of an issue Instagram is trying to address, he said that it makes total sense for the navigation buttons on the right-hand side of the screen when it's closed as it's ergonomic that way. However, that's also where the Like, Comment and Share buttons are located in Reels. "Now we've got this double stack of buttons that doesn't feel great and that we need to reconsider," he said. "It's a lot of that kind of thing."

The Instagram app that was designed for the iPhone Duo has been out globally since October 23. However, it's gotten quite a bit of flak for its interface since then, with people specifically mentioning the double stack of buttons Mosseri was talking about. This could be Mosseri addressing the criticisms, or him trying to set people's expectations for when Instagram rolls out the next versions of its app for the device.

What Do We Do With Leftover Love?

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

After years or decades, problems and patterns can become deeply entrenched. Be a fly on the wall during a sample couples therapy session and witness the slow, deliberate work of changing those patterns for a more enduring love.

Posted November 16, 2026 | Reviewed by Ekua Hagan

He was everything you had ever wanted. Kind, gentle, thoughtful. Almost too good to be true. You imagined a future together, a home, a family, a lifetime of ordinary mornings. Then, one day, while making eggs for breakfast, you saw the messages. The pan was still warm. The sun was coming through the window. Nothing around you had changed, yet it's all gone. In a matter of seconds, the man you thought you knew disappeared. The future you had imagined vanished with him. But the love you felt still lingers.

You can throw the eggs into the bin, but the feelings cannot disappear as quickly. Love involves several interconnected brain systems. Dopamine-rich reward pathways contribute to desire and anticipation (Fisher et al., 2010); the brain's natural opioid system helps make closeness comforting and social rejection distressing (Hsu et al., 2013; Nummenmaa et al., 2016); and limbic regions, particularly the amygdala, help connect emotional experiences with memory (Cahill et al., 1996). The truth can change what you think in seconds, but it cannot instantly erase what your brain has learned over months or years, so do not let people tell you that you are "co-dependent" or "weak." You have a normal brain that might play tricks on you.

You have leftover love, and that leftover love can be weaponised against you (Lesiak & Gelsthorpe, 2025). He may use the feelings that remain to persuade you to let him stay, asking you to ignore what you have discovered, silence your judgment, and compromise the very values that define who you are.

He has already betrayed you. Now he is asking you to betray yourself for him.

You are the one expected to swallow your anger, silence your judgment, and call it "forgiveness." Look at the bargain. He gets to keep the woman who loves him. And you are expected to give up the woman who knows the truth.

Decades ago, psychiatrist Judith Herman wrote about it in Trauma and Recovery (1992):

His ultimate goal appears to be the creation of a willing victim. Hostages, political prisoners, battered women, and slaves have all remarked upon the captor’s curious psychological dependence upon his victim. George Orwell gives voice to the totalitarian mind in the novel 1984:

“We are not content with negative obedience, nor even with the most abject submission. When finally you surrender to us, it must be of your own free will. We do not destroy the heretic because he resists us; so long as he resists us we never destroy him. We convert him, we capture his inner mind, we reshape him. We burn all evil and all illusion out of him; we bring him over to our side, not in appearance, but genuinely, heart and soul.”

Read those last words again. Heart and soul.

It is not enough that you stay. You must want to stay. It is not enough that you forgive him. You must learn to see his betrayal as something you can live with. You must look at the evidence of your own eyes and persuade yourself that it no longer matters.

And if keeping him means abandoning everything you believe in, what exactly are you trying to save—and what's left—when you do that?

Some wounds are easier to bear because you know you stood by your values while receiving them. In Kraków, officers Jakub Kulik and Artur Morański were seriously wounded confronting a mass shooter. Poland calls them heroes, and people across the country are grateful for their courage and service.

Then there is another kind of wound. One that no surgeon can stitch. Amy Winehouse sings it in "You Know I'm No Good": “I cheated myself, like I knew I would.” By the end of the song, she is no longer simply confessing what she did. She is passing judgment on herself.

Imagine waking up beside a man you no longer respect, living a life you never wanted, having persuaded yourself to accept things you once knew were unacceptable. You kept him, perhaps. But somewhere along the way, you lost the ability to trust your own judgment.

That is the deeper cruelty of weaponised love (Lesiak & Gelsthorpe, 2025). He does not need you to believe he is innocent. He needs you to stop caring that he isn't.

And if manipulation or coercion brought you to that point, the shame belongs to the person who engineered that surrender, not to you.

Name the man, not the fantasy. The betrayal wasn't simply another woman. It was the months of lies that made you believe you were building a future together while he was living another life. He needed you to trust him because, had you known the truth, you might have walked away. So he made sure you didn't.

That is what a programme of deception does. It robs you of the freedom to make informed choices about your own life. When used to dominate and subordinate, deception becomes a tool of coercive control and psychological abuse. Stop reaching for words that make him sound better. A liar is a liar. A traitor is a traitor. A backstabber is a backstabber. Grieve the man he pretended to be, the image he created to win your love and use it to control you. But don't defend the man behind the mask.

The eggs went into the bin that morning. The relationship ended. The feelings are disappearing. You'll be ok! Probably you are already more ok now than when you were cheated on, lied to, and abused.

Leading up to the long-overdue end of Two and a Half Men, there was rampant speculation about whether or not Charlie Harper would return. Midway through the eighth season, Charlie Sheen had been fired from the show for substance abuse issues, and the producers charged on for four more seasons with Ashton Kutcher as their new lead. There were ups and downs in the Kutcher years, but let’s face it: Two and a Half Men died with Charlie, and they should’ve just cut their losses and ended the show after Sheen’s departure.

In its final season, Two and a Half Men finally started to claw its way back to its original premise of dysfunctional co-parenting, as Walden adopted a little boy and he and Alan clashed on how to raise him. But in its final episode, it threw that storyline out the window so that co-creator Chuck Lorre could rehash his public feud with Sheen, four years on.

All throughout the final episode, we get hints that Charlie’s death was faked, and he’s come back for revenge (not on the stalker who trapped him in a well for the past four years, but on the family who loved and supported him and never gave up on him — that makes sense!). In the end, Charlie Harper made an appearance, but Charlie Sheen didn’t. A bowling-shirt-clad body double, shown from behind, walks up to the front door of the beach house, rings the doorbell, and gets flattened by a falling piano.

Lorre then breaks the fourth wall to say, “Winning!,” before a second piano falls on him. It torched the legacy of the show, and the last remaining shred of goodwill in the fan base that stuck around for 12 years, in the name of a petty rivalry that had been put to bed years earlier.

Charlie’s “return” in the Two and a Half Men finale was in extremely poor taste. Lorre reopened his old feud with Sheen without giving Sheen the opportunity to give his side. He spends the entire episode torturing Sheen’s character, then drags him out of the well to crush him with a piano. After the credits, Lorre’s vanity card reveals that Sheen refused to return for the finale because he found that ending for his character to be insulting, which is perfectly understandable.

We all predicted that Two and a Half Men was going to do something spiteful and vindictive with Charlie’s character, but even with that expectation, the finale went over the line. Just the fact that Two and a Half Men spent its entire series finale obsessing over Charlie, after trying to make so many other characters and storylines work since he left, goes to show that the series never moved on from Charlie. They brought in guest star Kathy Bates to play his ghost, they brought in Amber Tamblyn to play his long-lost daughter, and, of course, in the finale, they brought him back from the dead, had him vacuum up cocaine in a weird animated segment, and then pancaked him with a piano.

The whole finale just served as a reminder that they should’ve canceled Two and a Half Men after Sheen left. It’s no surprise that Lorre and Warner Bros. decided they could no longer work with Sheen, as his dependencies worsened, but they shouldn’t have tried to keep the series going without him. Kutcher was fine, but Two and a Half Men was the perfect star vehicle for Sheen, and it felt incomplete without him. The Office faced the same problem when it lost Steve Carell, but at least they got the real guy back for the finale.

Ariana Grande Over the Moon About Her Four Grammy Nominations: ‘Such a Sweet and Unexpected Honor’

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The singer snagged her first song of the year nod and seventh notice for best pop vocal album when nominations were announced on Monday (Nov. 16).

Ariana Grande woke up weeping for the very best reason on Monday morning (Nov. 16). The singer was celebrating her four Grammy nominations after this year’s list of nominees was announced, sharing her feelings in a lengthy Instagram post touting the good news. “hellow, good day, crying !!!!!!!!!!! petal !!!!!!!!! i am so overwhelmed, it is hard to find words at the moment. but for now, simply… thank you so, so much @grammys for recognizing my sweet Petal and ‘Hate That I Made You Love Me in this way,'” she wrote. “i’m so irrevocably proud of this body of work and it is such an honor to receive this acknowledgment from you. ♡.” Related Westlife Sets 2027 North America Tour: Exclusive Lily Collins Pays Tribute to Phil Collins After Rock Hall Induction Watch Olivia Dean, Kehlani, Juanes & More Revealing 2027 Grammy Nominations Here

The celebration came after Grande landed her first song of the year nod for the single “hate that i made you love me,” as well as her record seventh nomination for best pop vocal album for her 8th studio LP, this year’s Petal. The nominations pushed Grande past Taylor Swift and Kelly Clarkson, who’ve each received six nominations in the latter category; Grande is also nominated for best traditional pop vocal performance for her Wicked: For Good collaboration “For Good” with Cynthia Erivo and best video of the year for “hate that i made you love me.”

Grande’s post also shared the love with her Petal collaborators, producers and “dear friends” Ilya Salmanzadeh and Max Martin. “i have loved every moment of doing this with you both, growing in so many new directions and sprouting new leaves together over the past 12 years…,” she said. “thank you for supporting my curiosity as an artist and for championing me as a songwriter and producer the way that you do. you two are the greatest there is in every way. ♡”

She also gave props to director Christian Breslauer, who helmed her “hate that I made you love me” visual and for “being able to bring these songs to life in a way that only you can. thank you for caring the way you do and for taking the time to make sure that every single frame of each video we do together shines a light on the truth of each song. it’s an honor to create with you every time. ♡”

Grande, who announced that she’s taking a break from music after wrapping up her Eternal Sunshine tour in September, concluded her post with a summary of how important Petal was and is to her. “everything about creating and releasing this project has been such a meaningful experience for me already and this acknowledgment is such a sweet and unexpected honor and an extraordinary addition to that,” she said. “so thank you, thank you, again… petal forever. ♡”

Breslauer appreciated the shout-out, commenting, “🥹🥹🫶🏻 congrats and thank you my brilliant friend!”

To date, Grande has racked up 24 Grammy nominations and three wins, including best pop vocal album in 2019 for Sweetener, best pop duo/group performance in 2021 for “Rain on Me” with Lady Gaga and best pop duo/group performance in 2026 for “Defying Gravity” with Cynthia Erivo.

Her four nods this year come during a Grammys season when women are the dominant nominees, with RAYE leading the pack with 11 nominations, followed by Rosalía with nine nominations and Phoebe Bridgers, Miranda Lambert, Ella Langley and Olivia Rodrigo with seven each.

The 2027 Grammy Awards will take place on Feb. 7, with the prime-time broadcast airing at 7 p.m. ET/4 p.m. PT on ABC and streaming live on Disney+ and Hulu; the Premiere Ceremony, where the majority of the 100 prizes will be handed out, will take place earlier in the day.
