Mass Effect 3’s criminally underrated multiplayer mode has been restored and added to the Legendary Edition as a special N7 Day treat from a skilled group of fans.

Today, November 7, should be a very special occasion for Mass Effect fans. Back when the series was alive and thriving, BioWare coined N7 Day as a time to celebrate the series and share updates about its future. Of course, with BioWare and the next big game in the series, Universe, recently being shut down by EA, the atmosphere is a little dire.

As the leaked trailer for Universe and all of the new merchandise have kept proving, BioWare had some big plans in store for this year’s N7 Day. Even though the future of Mass Effect is a complete unknown and everyone is still in mourning, fans have come together to try and give the series a worthy send-off, which an impressive modding project has certainly done.

As pointed out by ResetEra user Lord Vatek, renowned Mass Effect modding group ME3Tweaks decided to cheer us all up on this rather depressing N7 Day by releasing Legendary Edition Multiplayer. The mod, which is currently live on Nexus Mods, is a “full game conversion” that adds Mass Effect 3’s multiplayer mode to the remaster.

Although Mass Effect 3’s multiplayer was a little divisive at launch, it’s gone on to be a bit of a cult classic for fans and was considered a notable omission from the Legendary Edition. It’s something that’s still talked about to this day, making its comeback more than five years later a pretty big deal. Especially considering it’s N7 Day.

The mod doesn’t just recreate the original multiplayer, though, as ME3Tweaks actually built on it in some pretty big ways. Heavy weapons like the Reaper Blackstar and Cain are now available, as are an overhauled photo mode, configurable aim assist, and improved FOV settings. New banners, accessibility options, and improved textures round everything out as an impressive expansion of the original mode.

As if all of that isn’t enough, ME3Tweaks aims to add matchmaking server statistics, a proper multiplayer store, and a total rework of the challenges screen.

There are already plenty of impressive Mass Effect mods out there, including some of this modding team’s previous work, but Legendary Edition Multiplayer has to be up there as one of the best. It’s a damn good way of showing just how passionate the Mass Effect community is, even as the series gets put through hell and back.

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

It was not pretty, but Martin Odegaard's goal was enough to secure the 1-0 win that ensured Arsenal go into the international break as the Premier League leaders.

No team has enjoyed more 1-0 wins since Arteta was appointed back in 2019, with the Gunners winning their past 10 home games in all competitions, keeping eight clean sheets in that time.

Odegaard's goal though, bundled home from close range, may well have been a relief to those at Pro Ref, too.

A controversial video assistant referee (VAR) decision, which denied the Gunners a first-half penalty, threatened to be the story as a largely uneventful game drifted by.

When referee Farai Hallam was sent to the pitchside monitor for a VAR review for a shirt pull by Nobel Mendy on Kai Havertz, surely everyone inside the Emirates was expecting a penalty.

The irony would not be lost on Liverpool fans, who were denied a VAR penalty for a shirt pull last weekend when Arsenal's Jurrien Timber tugged back Alexander Isak.

Unlike Isak, Havertz made sure everyone - including VAR Michael Oliver, was aware that a defender had hold of his shirt.

Indeed, the cheers had already begun when Hallam started to describe the foul to the crowd - but Gunners supporters were stopped in their tracks. The referee pivoted to an offside against Havertz, and play restarted with a free-kick to Hull.

Had Arsenal not mustered that 72nd-minute winner from their captain, much of the attention would, once again, have been on VAR.

If Arsenal had dropped points, it wouldn't have been the first time this weekend that a match would have been dominated by discussions about the video officials.

On Friday, Coventry City were denied what appeared to be a clear penalty against Everton for a hair pull by James Tarkowski on Caleb Yirenkyi. Then in the second half, when Thierno Barry scored to put the Toffees 2-1 up and the ball appeared to come off his hand.

The controversy continued in Saturday's early kick-off between Leeds and Tottenham. First, Leeds wanted a penalty for handball by Mateus Fernandes, though replays did show the ball hit the Spurs player's shoulder.

Then came the potential red card for a challenge by Jayden Bogle on Mathys Tel, which has split opinion. The VAR, Tony Harrington, eventually decided after a four-minute VAR check that the foul did not have enough force.

That Tel was forced off injured, and referee John Brooks did not even show a yellow card on the field, only added to the frustration for Tottenham.

So on to the Emirates, and again the debate was around VAR.

It's a situation few would question. After all, an offside player winning a penalty surely goes against the very logic of the law?

If an offside player is running to the ball and is fouled before they attempt to play the ball or challenge an opponent, then the foul is considered to come before the offside offence.

The law seeks to prevent situations whereby defenders have carte blanche to foul attackers, just because the opponent is offside.

But is a complex area of law and is very much open to interpretation.

In February 2019, the season before VAR came in, an offside Harry Kane was awarded a late penalty in a north London derby after being dragged down by Shkodran Mustafi.

Two years ago, Newcastle were awarded a spot-kick against Bournemouth through a VAR intervention when Adam Smith tugged back an offside Fabian Schar.

Yet last December, after being awarded a penalty against Leeds, Brentford's Dango Ouattara was ruled to be offside and the spot-kick ruled out through VAR.

So which interpretation is correct? Kane and Schar, or Havertz and Ouattara?

Ouattara was somewhat different, as he appeared to be engaging with Gabriel Gudmundsson as the ball was played.

The Havertz example is more like the one involving Schar, who was running forward when his shirt was tugged back.

But there is an additional consideration, and this is where Oliver's thinking will lie.

Officials are asked to consider the likelihood of the attacker becoming involved in the play from a second phase. Let's say the ball could be first played by a team-mate, which then makes them onside. Then they are not purely benefitting from being offside.

In the case of Schar and Kane, they could have benefitted from that second phase.

Havertz was the only attacker who had any chance of being involved in the play, so the VAR determined offside was the fair outcome.

At least tonight most of the talk will be about another victory for Arsenal - and not VAR.

They have the best defence in the league and arguably Europe but goals are also seeming hard to come by at the other end.

Arsenal have not scored more than twice in the league since the opening day of the season, when they put three past Coventry.

The same can be said for Kai Havertz too.

The 27-year-old picked up a hamstring injury playing for Germany and, even though he scored the winner against Liverpool, his performances have not reached the same level.

Viktor Gyokeres is firmly second choice this season and Arteta is still working out how to get the best out of the maverick Eberechi Eze, while Noni Madueke is working to fill the gap left by Saka.

Arteta was asked about the number of single-goal wins this season and what he can do to improve that.

"To be more efficient," was the answer the Gunners boss gave. "We are one against one against the keeper. That's happened twice today.

"There is a penalty there, there is another situation inside the box where the ball is bouncing. So many shots that have been deflected, blocked. We need to find ways to be more efficient, sometimes even more pragmatic.

"Sometimes my feeling is that we are trying to score a perfect goal against these low blocks. We need to be much more chaotic than that, and that's it. And open, I think, score much earlier because obviously that's going to change the course of the game."
