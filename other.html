Report - Disaster: Agents of Liverpool star now in talks to leave, Reds desperate to keep him

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Liverpool could lose yet another star. His agents are now in talks with a UEFA Champions League side as a potential exit is on the cards.

Liverpool have lost a lot of truly great talent over the last few years. Not all of it has been choice, either, though some of it absolutely was. Mohamed Salah leaving this year, for instance, was down to the club as much as the player.

There have been plenty of sales, too. Curtis Jones departed for Inter in the summer while homegrown talent like Caoimhin Kelleher and Jarell Quansah were both sold to starting positions elsewhere.

But then there are the ones that aren't by choice. Trent Alexander-Arnold was offered a new contract but rejected it to join Real Madrid. One year later, Ibrahima Konaté did literally the exact same thing.

And it likely won't stop there. That doesn't mean players will leave next year to join Real Madrid (but don't rule that out) but there are stars who could get their heads turned.

Bayern Munich were surprisingly linked with a move for Rio Ngumoha in the summer. They were supposedly willing to put up quite a bit of cash to make it happen, though Liverpool never entertained the idea.

But Christian Falk reports that they haven’t actually stopped looking. He claims that Bayern remain in talks with representatives of Ngumoha, though not directly the player himself.

They’re eager to find the player who could take over from Luis Diaz on the left wing. Ngumoha is certainly a fit for that - even if a successor to the Colombian is the exact same role that Liverpool want him for.

Now, the reason these talks are important is that Ngumoha’s contract expires in 2028 and Liverpool are in their own talks to get a new one signed. If they fail, there will be immense pressure to sell next summer while they can still get a fee for him.

That then becomes even more complicated because Chelsea get 20 per cent of any sale. Essentially, there’s no good outcome here other than Ngumoha re-signing with Liverpool.

Bayern are going to do all they can to make sure that doesn’t happen, though. We’ll probably refrain from predicting how this one goes, given the exits over the last couple of years, but we know Liverpool are pushing hard.

If you want some help with this quest, here's where to find Princess Ruto in Zelda Ocarina of Time.

Once you talk to King Zora in the southeastern side of Zora Domain, he will tell you that his daughter is missing and he has searched for her as far as Lake Hylia — the exact place you must go. You just need to figure out how to get there.

But an OG in cosmetic procedures is seeing a resurgence in men, too.

Long viewed as a treatment limited to the ladies, it’s increasingly becoming another form of male upkeep. Now we’re learning that men are outspending women on the popular procedure. According to an October 2026 report by Guidepoint Qsight, a medical aesthetics data and intelligence provider, men spent an average of $529 per neurotoxin visit in Q3 of 2026, compared with $455 for women. (The figures include not just Botox but also competitors like Daxxify and Xeomin.)

The potential explanation is simple: Botox is an easy way to look a little younger, more well-rested, and even less angry or severe. (As a built-in bonus, it’s virtually painless—especially compared to the barbaric looksmaxxing trends circulating as of late.) According to a 2025 survey of 2,900 aesthetic plastic surgeons, men accounted for over 15% of all Botox cases that year. It was also their top non-surgical treatment by a landslide.

Nancy Samolitis, MD, a board-certified dermatologist in Los Angeles and co-founder of facile, has been treating men for years but has recently seen an uptick in male clientele, aged anywhere from 35 to 70. His goal isn’t to change his face but to slow down wrinkles before they become obvious. He gets Botox for his forehead lines and crow’s feet, and recently started treating his neck lines, too.

But it’s not always about looking younger. Men are also using Botox in less obvious places. David Kim, MD, board-certified dermatologist and the founder of Soho Dermatology in New York City, treats the chin for more projection, as well as the underarms to combat excess sweating. Bell Yoo, NP, a board-certified nurse practitioner and the founder of Mimo Aesthetics in West Hollywood, adds that she injects the masseter (and sometimes temporalis) muscles for guys who struggle with jaw clenching or teeth grinding.

Aesthetically, men usually aren’t looking for the same thing from Botox as women. For starters, many want to soften their lines without erasing them. All that subtlety comes at a price. Men may end up paying more than women to treat the same areas, but the higher price tag is actually expected: Men often have larger, stronger facial muscles, which require more units to achieve their desired results, Yoo says.

Yoo says that most of her male Botox patients are pretty discreet. “I think there’s still some stigma around men receiving cosmetic treatments, and many would rather keep it to themselves,” she says. Waldrop, however, is an open book. “I don’t really see it as something worth hiding or feeling that weird about,” he says. “Guys are already spending money on skincare, haircuts, hair transplants, working out, and all sorts of things to look and feel better, so I don’t really see why Botox should be treated any differently.”

For Waldrop, Botox is less a dramatic intervention than another item on his grooming checklist. He doesn’t see it as something that needs to be announced or apologized for. At the end of the day, “it’s really not that deep,” he says.
