Indiana basketball coach Darian DeVries would 'love' to see Kentucky series continue

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

INDIANAPOLIS — Indiana baskettball coach Darian DeVries doesn't want the Kentucky series to go back into hibernation.

The schools are midway through a four-year scheduling pact, but DeVries told reporters after Friday's night's 90-76 win over the Wildcats at Lucas Oil Stadium that there should be no end date to the border rivalry that used to be an annual fixture for college hoops.

"I would love to keep it going," DeVries said. "I think it's great for college basketball. It's fun games for everybody."

More: Indiana basketball's Darren Harris gets second chance to fulfill his hoop dreams

In 2011, former Indiana forward Christian Watford, who attended Friday night's game, helped send the series into an extended hiatus by sinking a buzzer-beater 3-pointer to upset then No. 1 Kentucky.

Former Wildcats coach John Calipari resisted scheduling a home-and-home series against the Hoosiers for years.

UK didn't play IU outside of a pair of meetings in the NCAA Tournament (2012 and 2016) until the series resumed in 2025. The Hoosiers' win on Friday was the first matchup between the programs in the state of Indiana since Watford's heroics.

Kentucky will host next year's matchup at Rupp Arena before the teams return to Assembly Hall in 2028. DeVries wants to reward the passion he witnessed from the 23,078 fans who attended Frdiay night's game by keeping the series alive.

"The fanbases, the proximity, to have two historic programs like this to continue to play in the non-conference, yeah, I would love to continue it," DeVries said.

COME ARRIVA IL VICENZA - Momento complicato per la formazione guidata da Fabio Gallo, reduce da tre sconfitte consecutive contro avversarie d'alta classifica (Modena, Palermo e Cremonese) e ferma a 9 punti (2 vittorie, 3 pareggi, 6 ko). ‘9-1-1’ Is Officially Switching Protagonists, but There’s a Big Problem

📅 Nov 21, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Editor's Note: The following contains spoilers for 9-1-1 Season 10, Episode 6.9-1-1 is just six episodes into its tenth season, but the show already just aired its mid-season finale. Any network procedural fan knows it's standard for each main character to get a couple solo storylines during a shorter half-season like this, except for the show's leads – which, since the beginning of 9-1-1, were Athena (Angela Bassett) and Bobby (Peter Krause). Since Bobby's death, Athena has been 9-1-1's main lead, while the other characters each get a significant storyline every few episodes. Buck (Oliver Stark), however, has been getting more storylines than everyone else this season.

9-1-1 continues to go in circles with Buck's character in Season 10, making him regress to the point of impulsively giving up the child he chose to foster instead of actually working through his trauma and self-worth issues. Five episodes have been dedicated to the Theo (Lincoln and Theodore Sykes) storyline this season, and every Buck and Eddie (Ryan Guzman) storyline this season has been about Theo. The latter wouldn't necessarily be a problem, except that 9-1-1 is still giving Buck way too much focus without real growth, while unfairly relegating Eddie to the supporting role.

After spending pretty much all of his non-work screen time this season supporting Buck, Eddie got to make a bold move in last week's episode when he chose to foster Theo. Even knowing that Buck would take this as a personal slight, Eddie prioritized the needs of Theo over the second most important relationship in his life – barring that with Christopher (Gavin McHugh), who cares deeply about Theo and even wrote Buck an angry letter for giving him up. 9-1-1 set up something compelling here, where viewers could see how Eddie adjusted to raising a second child, and how he's been feeling about this entire situation. This was the perfect opportunity to get a rare insight into Eddie's emotions, and to see the pain that Buck's cruel remarks caused him.

Instead, Eddie only gets a little bit of focus in the mid-season finale. 9-1-1 finally delivers the long-anticipated first scene between Eddie and Maddie (Jennifer Love Hewitt), but the whole scene sees Maddie treating Eddie coldly and accusing him of fostering Theo to teach Buck a lesson. Maddie hasn't been afraid to call Buck out in the past, like when he hurt Eddie's ankle in "Buck, Bothered and Bewildered." Here, though, she takes his side when Eddie isn't even acting against him, and everyone from the 118 except for Ravi (Anirudh Pisharody) also backs Buck up. Eddie doesn't get to talk about his feelings or his new role as a foster parent at all, and he instead oddly emphasizes the apparent importance of Buck's role as Theo's biological parent.

The Buck and Eddie pairing became so beloved by fans because of their deep friendship and care for each other. They always supported each other through the toughest moments and showed up for each other – until now. Eddie tried to give Buck helpful and honest advice earlier this season, but Buck just lashed out at him. Eddie later stepped up and did something really thoughtful to help both Buck and Theo, and Buck blew up at him again. Instead of having each other's backs like they promised they would before they even became friends, Eddie's been consistently monitoring Buck's back, while Buck can't so much as be bothered to turn around and look at him. Everyone this season is coddling Buck except for Eddie, but Eddie's still been exceptionally gentle with him, refusing to even accept his lackluster apology without also unfairly blaming himself.

Becoming a foster parent has the potential to be an amazing storyline for Eddie, but not in the way 9-1-1 is currently setting it up, where Eddie is just a stand-in parent for Theo until Buck is ready to take him back. 9-1-1 should focus on Eddie's journey of parenting a second child, as well as the memories that this will inevitably bring up for him from Christopher's childhood. Then, either Theo needs to stay with Eddie, or Buck and Eddie need to find a solution that works for them – because it would do Eddie a tremendous disservice to have him simply give Theo back to Buck after choosing to parent him. Considering the show has neglected Eddie's storyline all season, it's about time that he gets something more than just the crumbs of a storyline from Buck.

This Everyday Vitamin May Ease Depression In Some People

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Could correcting this vitamin deficiency make a difference for people already receiving treatment for depression?

Could correcting this vitamin deficiency make a difference for people already receiving treatment for depression?

Vitamin D supplements may ease symptoms in some people with depression, according to a review of the evidence.

Several small trials found improvements, including in people who started out with low vitamin D levels.

Other trials found little or no benefit, and one large study found that supplements did not lower the chances of developing depression.

The link is plausible because vitamin D may influence inflammation and chemical signalling in the brain, both of which are relevant to mood.

Depression itself could also lower vitamin D levels if it leaves people with less energy to go outdoors.

That makes it important to ask two separate questions: whether correcting a deficiency can help someone who is already depressed and whether taking vitamin D can prevent depression in the first place.

Researchers at the University of Oradea reviewed studies measuring vitamin D levels in people with and without depression, as well as trials testing whether supplements changed their symptoms.

One small trial recruited people who had both major depression and a vitamin D deficiency.

Both groups continued with their usual care, but after about three months, depression scores were lower in the group given a vitamin D injection than in the placebo group (Vellekkatt et al., 2020).

Differences in who took part, the doses they received and how symptoms were measured made the trials difficult to compare.

A trial designed to test prevention followed more than 18,000 adults aged 50 and over who had no clinically relevant depressive symptoms when they joined.

Over about five years, participants who took daily vitamin D were about as likely to develop depression or clinically relevant symptoms as those who took a placebo (Okereke et al., 2020).

Vitamin D receptors are found in parts of the brain such as the hippocampus, which is involved in memory and the response to stress.

The vitamin can also influence immune signals involved in inflammation.

This is one route through which it might affect mood in some people with depression.

Vitamin D levels also reflect habits that can change during depression, including what people eat and how much time they spend outdoors.

Someone who stops going for regular walks, for example, may get less sunlight and make less vitamin D as a result.

A blood test taken during an episode of depression cannot tell which change came first.

The question now is whether correcting a vitamin D deficiency can improve symptoms in people already receiving treatment for depression.

Answering it will take larger trials that recruit people with both conditions and follow them long enough to see whether any improvement lasts.

The study was published in the journal Pharmaceuticals (Roșian et al., 2025).
