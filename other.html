Philippine Police Detain Hundreds Protesting Trump Visit

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Click to expand Image Protesters attempt to march to the ASEAN Summit venue in Manila but are stopped by hundreds of police officers, November 17, 2026. © 2026 Lian Buan/Human Rights Watch

Police in the Philippines arrested and detained at least 245 people protesting the visit of US President Donald Trump to Manila for the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) summit. 

Protests in Manila started on November 15 opposing the Philippine government’s plan to build a technology manufacturing hub in Capas, Tarlac province, under the US government’s “Pax Silica” initiative. The construction will displace hundreds of Indigenous people and small farmers from their homes and farmlands.

An estimated 6,000 people joined the protests despite police efforts to contain demonstrations by banning them everywhere except in “freedom parks” – designated areas where rallies can be held without a permit.

Yet police arrested 236 people on the outskirts of Metro Manila who were traveling from the Southern Tagalog region to the main protest site. Among those detained were 29 farmers including members of the Indigenous Mangyan tribe, 19 journalists, 9 environmental defenders, and 6 human rights workers. Ephraim Cortez of the National Union of Peoples’ Lawyers said 19 children were detained. 

“This is the largest mass arrest we’ve seen under [President] Marcos,” protest leader Renato Reyes told Human Rights Watch. “Our whole delegation was arrested and taken to a police camp where lawyers were not given immediate access.”

Detainees are being held inside a police camp in a hall with poor ventilation, insufficient drinking water, and inadequate restroom facilities, according to activists and lawyers.

The protesters have been detained for three days without charge. Lawyers representing them expressed concern that they could face criminal charges of illegal assembly, alarms and scandals, and disobedience to authority. 

Police detained without charge another nine protesters at the main protest site on November 15 for attempting to march to the ASEAN summit venue. News footage shows police officers slapping and kicking a protester on the ground. 

International human rights law protects the right to peaceful assembly. Law enforcement may not carry out indiscriminate mass arrests even if a protest violates local laws or becomes disorderly. Arrests need to be based on individualized grounds and comply with the requirements of legality, necessity, and proportionality. Police can arrest people responsible for specific unlawful conduct but not just for taking part in a protest. All those arbitrarily detained for exercising their basic rights should be immediately released.

Caramelized Cabbage Pasta

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

This pasta is the easiest way to cram an entire head of cabbage into one comforting dinner. The secret? Cooking it in butter over a long, relaxed period of time until it softens into a decadent, caramelized tangle. Paired with spaghetti, heavy cream, and some Dijon mustard and lemon zest to brighten everything up, this dish is a celebration of how versatile cabbage can be. Feel free to top your bowl with lots of fresh dill, or leave it unadorned with nothing more than an extra sprinkle of cheese.

Cook 12 oz. spaghetti in a large pot of boiling generously salted water, stirring occasionally, until very al dente, about 1 minute less than package directions. Drain, reserving 2 cups pasta cooking liquid. Set aside.

Melt 4 Tbsp. unsalted butter in same pot over high heat. Add 1 large head of green cabbage (about 3 lb.), cored, thinly sliced, and 6 garlic cloves, thinly sliced, and cook, stirring often, until cabbage is very tender and browned in spots, 18–25 minutes.

Add zest of 1 lemon, 2 oz. Parmesan, finely grated (about 1 cup), 1½ cups heavy cream, 2 Tbsp. Dijon mustard, and reserved pasta to cabbage and toss to coat. If sauce is too thick, add pasta cooking liquid a splash at a time, tossing constantly, until glossy. Taste pasta and season with kosher salt and freshly ground pepper.

Divide pasta among shallow bowls and top with finely chopped dill if desired.

Premier League KMI Panel unanimously admit to another refereeing error which wronged Liverpool

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The Premier League’s Key Match Incidents (KMI) Panel has adjudged that Liverpool had a penalty wrongly given against them in a recent fixture.

The Reds won their last game before the international break, beating Crystal Palace 3-1 at Selhurst Park, although Alisson Becker was denied a clean sheet by a stoppage-time spot kick awarded to the home side and converted by Eddie Nketiah.

The penalty was awarded for what referee Tony Harrington deemed to be a foul by Milos Kerkez on Dario Osorio when the two players unfortunately clashed heads, and Andoni Iraola said afterwards that he was ‘very upset‘ over that decision.

The Premier League’s KMI Panel has now published its findings from the most recent top-flight matchday (via premierleague.com), and all five independent assessors adjudged that the penalty award against Liverpool was incorrect.

However, there was also unanimous agreement that VAR was right not to intervene, as it was decreed that no ‘clear and obvious’ error had taken place.

The panel concluded: ‘The panel unanimously felt that the referee’s call of a penalty for a challenge by Kerkez on Osorio was incorrect, with the contact being inevitable given the jumping actions of both players.

‘However, the non-intervention of VAR was supported on the basis it was not a clear and obvious error.’

Astronomers Discover a Chilling Preview of Our Solar System’s Future

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Astronomers have discovered an extraordinary ring system surrounding a dead star, offering a possible preview of what awaits our Solar System billions of years from now. Using the European Southern Observatory’s Very Large Telescope (ESO’s VLT), researchers identified two separate rings of planetary debris with a distinct gap between them. The unusual structure may be maintained by two planetary bodies that survived the death of their star, including one potentially as large as Pluto or even the Moon.

“We have known for some time that planets may survive beyond the transformation of their central star, but this is the first example of a complex system with a double debris disc and potentially two surviving planetesimals,” says Nicola Gentille Fusillo, an astronomer at the Università degli Studi di Trieste in Italy and lead author of the study published in Nature Astronomy.

At the center of this remarkable system is J1328−1450, a white dwarf. These incredibly dense stellar remnants are what remain when stars similar to our Sun exhaust their nuclear fuel and reach the end of their lives.

When a Sun-like star approaches the end of its life, it swells dramatically before shedding its outer layers. During this transformation, nearby planets can be swallowed and destroyed. Other rocky objects may survive the initial upheaval, only to break apart later and create rotating discs of debris around the star’s remaining core.

Astronomers have observed such debris discs around white dwarfs before, but J1328−1450 stands out. Instead of a single ring of vaporized planetary material, it has two concentric rings of gas separated by a well-defined gap.

“The structure we see in this system is particularly intriguing because the two gaseous rings are remarkably stable and remain separated by a gap,” says co-author Felipe Lagos-Vilches, an astronomer at the University of Warwick in the UK and at the Universidad Técnica Federico Santa María in Chile.

“The system is a rare glimpse of the violent, chaotic process by which a planetary system meets its end, and it hints that dead stars can have disc structures far more complex than previously realized,” adds Gentile Fusillo, who is also affiliated with the INAF-Astronomical Observatory of Trieste.

What keeps the two rings separated? The researchers suspect that two surviving planet-like bodies, traveling along different orbits, are helping maintain the unusual arrangement.

The smaller object, associated with the inner ring, could be comparable to a typical asteroid, measuring just a few kilometers across. The larger body, associated with the outer ring, may be roughly the size of Pluto or even Earth’s Moon. Although their presence has not been directly confirmed, these objects could explain why the debris remains divided into two stable rings.

Until now, astronomers had identified debris discs around white dwarfs through a characteristic observational signature associated with a single, flat, rotating disc. However, observations of J1328−1450 revealed something entirely different.

Using the X-Shooter instrument installed on ESO’s VLT in Chile’s Atacama Desert, the researchers detected the distinctive signature of a double disc, showing that planetary debris around dead stars can form more complicated structures than previously recognized.

The rings also contain important clues about the worlds that once orbited the star. Astronomers detected calcium, oxygen, magnesium, and iron, elements commonly found in rocky planetary material throughout our Solar System.

Even more intriguing, the two rings have noticeably different chemical compositions. The inner ring contains abundant magnesium gas, while the outer ring has very little. This difference suggests that the debris feeding the rings may have come from two separate planets with distinct compositions.

Another possibility is that the material originated from different layers of the same planetary body. For example, one ring could contain debris from a planet’s crust, while the other holds material from its core.

The discovery may also reveal something unsettling about our own cosmic future. Billions of years from now, the Sun will undergo a similar transformation, expanding as it nears the end of its life before eventually becoming a white dwarf. Earth could be destroyed during this process, while other planetary bodies may survive and leave behind rings of debris.

The unusual system around J1328−1450 suggests that the aftermath of a star’s death can be far more complex than a simple cloud of shattered planetary remains. Even after a planetary system experiences catastrophic destruction, some of its original bodies may endure and continue shaping the wreckage.

“The system therefore offers a remarkable glimpse of what the future of planetary systems, including systems like our own, might look like,” concludes Lagos-Vilches.

Scattered Spider Hacker Sentenced to 6 Years Over $11.6M in Stolen Crypto

A Scottish hacker who was part of the Scattered Spider hacking collective has been sentenced to six years in U.S. federal prison for his role in a scheme that stole at least $11.6 million in crypto.

Tyler Robert Buchanan, 24, of Dundee, was sentenced to 72 months by a federal judge in Santa Ana, California, on Tuesday, the U.S. Attorney's Office for the Central District of California said. The judge set a restitution hearing for December 11. Buchanan began hacking as a teenager and was part of Scattered Spider, the group of young, English-speaking hackers blamed for a string of high-profile cyberattacks, the BBC reported.

Buchanan pleaded guilty in April to conspiracy to commit wire fraud and aggravated identity theft. At the time, prosecutors said the scheme had stolen at least $8 million in crypto. That figure has since risen to at least $11.6 million.

Between September 2021 and April 2023, Buchanan and his co-conspirators sent hundreds of phishing text messages to employees of at least a dozen companies, including telecoms, technology firms and crypto companies, according to the indictment and Buchanan's plea agreement. The messages linked to fake websites that captured employees' login details, which were sent to a Telegram channel run by Buchanan and another co-conspirator.

The group used information stolen from those companies to identify individual crypto holders, then carried out SIM swaps, tricking mobile carriers into moving victims' phone numbers to devices they controlled so they could intercept two-factor authentication codes and empty victims' wallets.

In the largest single theft, one victim lost $1.67 million in December 2022, according to the BBC. When authorities searched Buchanan's home in Scotland in April 2023, they found the names and addresses of numerous victims, and a text file containing crypto seed phrases and login details for one victim's account, prosecutors said.

"Those thefts had severe, life-altering consequences for many victims," prosecutors argued in a sentencing memo.

Buchanan is the fifth and final defendant to be sentenced in the case, which the Justice Department charged in November 2024. A Justice Department spokesperson confirmed at the time that the defendants were suspected members of Scattered Spider, the group behind high-profile attacks including the 2023 breach of MGM Resorts. Buchanan was arrested at Palma de Mallorca Airport in Spain in May 2024 while trying to board a private flight to Naples, the BBC reported, and has been in U.S. custody since April 2025.

His co-defendant Noah Urban, of Palm Coast, Florida, is serving 10 years in prison, while Ahmed Hossam Eldin Elbadawy and Evans Onyeaka Osiebo were each sentenced to 45 months, and Joel Martin Evans to two years.

U.S. authorities have continued to pursue alleged Scattered Spider members. In July, a 19-year-old suspect was extradited from Finland over an $8 million crypto ransom demand against a luxury jeweler.
