Air Fryer Croque Madame

Want to treat yourself to five-star brunch without making a ton of dishes—or taking off your pj’s? Hack the classic croque madame by making it in the air fryer. Here, crispy toast is topped with an egg and smothered with ham, cheese, and béchamel for a simpler version of the classic French dish that will transform the comfort of your sofa into a fancy brunch spot in five seconds flat.

How is this a croque madame?A classic croque madame is a toasted ham and cheese sandwich that’s topped with a layer of béchamel, broiled until bubbly, and then topped with a fried egg. This air fryer version takes all those components—toasty bread, fried egg, béchamel, ham, and cheese—and reconfigures them into a form that cooks much easier in the air fryer. You might say it almost looks like a classic egg-in-the-hole, but don’t worry! If you close your eyes, you’ll still taste croque madame.

The microwave béchamel:There’s nothing wrong with making béchamel in a pot on the stove, but making it in the microwave means fewer heavy dishes to clean—and it’s also a great hack to know if you’re somewhere with limited (or no!) stove space. Rather than standing over a pot, whisking constantly, you’ll simply whisk the flour into the melted butter, microwave until it’s bubbling, then whisk in the milk and microwave again. Once it’s thickened, you can fold in the cheese. Though I don’t recommend microwaving after the cheese goes in—that’s a sure way to break your sauce—if it absolutely needs it, you can microwave it for very short bursts just till it melts together.

Delish Official:Eggland's Best Eggs are the Official Eggs of Delish. That means they're the only eggs we're using in our recipe content.

Make-ahead & storage:Once cooked, the air fryer croque madame should be eaten immediately. That said, the béchamel can be made 2 days ahead and stored in an airtight container in the refrigerator. I recommend gently reheating it before using. I typically do this in the microwave in 15-second increments, stirring in between—this prevents the sauce from breaking.

Ice hockey prospect Max Calce follows Leon Draisaitl's path, leaving Mannheim for Canada

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The player widely seen as Germany's biggest ice hockey talent to come along in years is leaving the country's top professional league for Canada – changing tracks in his bid to make it to the NHL.

Max Calce made the surprise announcement that he would be moving to the Brantford Bulldogs of the Ontario Hockey League (OHL) in a statement issued on the website of his German Ice Hockey League (DEL) team, Adler Mannheim late on Tuesday.

"I have long had three goals," he said. "To play for the Adler, in the Canadian Hockey League (CHL), and in the NHL. Today is a difficult and emotional day for me, as I say goodbye to my home."

Having started the season in Mannheim, many thought the 17-year-old would finish the season in the DEL.

The announcement came after Calce, who had been a regular in Mannheim's lineup this season, had been left off the team sheet for Tuesday night's game in Straubing.

This comes less than a month after he became the youngest-ever player to score a goal for the German men's national team – against Slovakia in Germany's final game of the annual Deutschland Cup invitational.

"It was only a matter of time; he's an incredible player," said former NHL player Tobias Rieder, who frequently played on a line with Calce during the tournament.

"The most impressive thing is that, at 17, he plays a very mature style of hockey—he almost always makes the right decision."

Calce is also the youngest goal scorer in DEL history, having found the net for Mannheim in September 2025 at just 16 years and 100 days old.

In his statement, the centerman thanked Mannheim, which has produced a number of NHL players over the years, including Leon Draisaitl, Moritz Seider and Tim Stützle.

"The trust, support, and experiences I gained through my coaches, teammates, and everyone within the organization have played a major role in my development—both as a player and as a person."

This season, his second in the DEL, Calce has six goals and two assists in Mannheim's first 19 games.

"We believe in preparing young players to take opportunities at the highest level of the sport," Dallas Eakins, Mannheim's head coach and sporting director said.

"As much as we would have loved to continue to have him play for us, we respect and welcome his ambition."

On the surface, the notion of moving from a men's professional league to a junior league may seem like a step backwards, but Calce's move to the OHL will give Calce increased exposure to NHL scouts. The OHL is one of the top three junior leagues in Canada and according to figures released by the CHL last season, they produce more NHL players than any other developmental system.

In doing so, Calce is now following the path of Edmonton Oilers superstar Draisaitl, who left Mannheim's youth program at the age of 16 to pursue his NHL dream, joining the Western Hockey League's Prince Albert Raiders.

This, of course is not the only path to the NHL; Moritz Seider of the Detroit Red Wings and the Ottawa Senators' Tim Stützle were both first-round draft choices plucked directly from Mannheim. Calce is hoping to be taken in the first round of next summer's NHL Entry Draft.

Born to Russian parents in Mannheim, Calce changed his birth surname, Penkin, to that of his mother and stepfather last summer.

Today's Nice Price or No Dice Alfa Romeo has looks that could make you fall in love, but also a brand reputation for tempestuousness that might cause pain — financial and otherwise. Legend has it that, though the execs at Land Rover tasked company stylist David Bache with designing the bodywork of the in-development Range Rover, they were so taken with the styling of the engineering mules that, with minor tweaks, that became the truck's first and final design.

I'd like to get one thing out of the way up front: I find today's 2017 Alfa Romeo Giulia incredibly good-looking, with no bad angles from which to view it. Alfa had to get the Giulia's looks just right, a necessity that went so far as to delay the car's launch. Fiat boss Sergio Marchionne kept rejecting designs Alfa's internal Centro Stile Alfa Romeo designers offered during the car's development, claiming they weren't good-looking enough. Why Alfa didn't go the route of having a design house like Pininfarina pen the car is a mystery, although traditionally Alfa handled saloon styling in-house while farming out coupes to such esteemed external designers, so there's that.

Regardless, Alfa got the Giulia's looks right, and didn't screw up the car's underpinnings either. Rolling on a then-new RWD platform developed with oversight by Ferrari engineer Philippe Krief, the Giulia has the goods to compete with the likes of BMW's 3 Series and Audi A4 when the roads get bendy. It's a testament to how good the basic chassis is that it can perform competently with this base four-cylinder model and, with notable tweaks, also with the monster 500-horsepower V6 Quadrifoglio.

Our car has the 1,995cc DOHC MultiAir four under the hood. With variable valve timing and an intercooled turbocharger, that makes an astounding 280 hp and 306 pound-feet of torque. Matched to that engine is a ZF 8HP50 8-speed automatic transmission that powers the rear wheels. While AWD was offered on this model, this car's RWD layout is simpler to maintain and lighter, offering better performance and fuel economy.

According to the ad, this Giulia has 75,000 miles on the clock and, remarkable for any Alfa, especially a first-year model that may still be suffering teething pains, it is claimed to suffer no issues whatsoever. Did I mention it's also really good-looking?

Having established that this is a very attractive car, it's also in very good shape. The pictures in the ad support the seller's claim that it's "well kept," as the Silverstone Gray Metallic paint appears to have no dents, and the darker gray factory alloys show no evidence of curb rash.

This being an entry-level model and with the ad's limited description, the seating surfaces are likely vinyl rather than leather. Some wood on the console warms up the space in an otherwise Germanic, all-black (even the Alfa badge) interior.

That's not to say that the car is parsimoniously equipped, as it has all the basic comfort and convenience features one might expect on a reasonably modern car. Those include power windows, locks, seats and mirrors, dual-zone climate control with eyeball vents for the back seats, and a center stack screen for infotainment and the backup camera display. There's also cruise control, auto headlamps... You name it, it's likely got it.

Per the seller, the car presents in excellent condition, has a clean title, and drives perfectly. What more could one want?

Well, I'm sure one thing we could all ask for is a car that's free and can navigate to a leprechaun's pot of gold when asked. There are certain things we can't have, no matter how much we want them.

This car isn't free, but its $7,999 asking price is a recent reduction from $8,500, and it makes one wonder: just how scared are people about buying a decade-old, out-of-warranty Alfa that even that slightly higher price seemingly couldn't get a nibble?

With that in mind, what's your take on this base-but-far-from-basic Giulia and that new, lower $7,999 asking price? Is that a handsome deal on this beauty of a car?
