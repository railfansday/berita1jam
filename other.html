Philippine Police Detain Hundreds Protesting Trump Visit

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Click to expand Image Protesters attempt to march to the ASEAN Summit venue in Manila but are stopped by hundreds of police officers, November 17, 2026. © 2026 Lian Buan/Human Rights Watch

Police in the Philippines arrested and detained at least 245 people protesting the visit of US President Donald Trump to Manila for the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) summit. 

Protests in Manila started on November 15 opposing the Philippine government’s plan to build a technology manufacturing hub in Capas, Tarlac province, under the US government’s “Pax Silica” initiative. The construction will displace hundreds of Indigenous people and small farmers from their homes and farmlands.

An estimated 6,000 people joined the protests despite police efforts to contain demonstrations by banning them everywhere except in “freedom parks” – designated areas where rallies can be held without a permit.

Yet police arrested 236 people on the outskirts of Metro Manila who were traveling from the Southern Tagalog region to the main protest site. Among those detained were 29 farmers including members of the Indigenous Mangyan tribe, 19 journalists, 9 environmental defenders, and 6 human rights workers. Ephraim Cortez of the National Union of Peoples’ Lawyers said 19 children were detained. 

“This is the largest mass arrest we’ve seen under [President] Marcos,” protest leader Renato Reyes told Human Rights Watch. “Our whole delegation was arrested and taken to a police camp where lawyers were not given immediate access.”

Detainees are being held inside a police camp in a hall with poor ventilation, insufficient drinking water, and inadequate restroom facilities, according to activists and lawyers.

The protesters have been detained for three days without charge. Lawyers representing them expressed concern that they could face criminal charges of illegal assembly, alarms and scandals, and disobedience to authority. 

Police detained without charge another nine protesters at the main protest site on November 15 for attempting to march to the ASEAN summit venue. News footage shows police officers slapping and kicking a protester on the ground. 

International human rights law protects the right to peaceful assembly. Law enforcement may not carry out indiscriminate mass arrests even if a protest violates local laws or becomes disorderly. Arrests need to be based on individualized grounds and comply with the requirements of legality, necessity, and proportionality. Police can arrest people responsible for specific unlawful conduct but not just for taking part in a protest. All those arbitrarily detained for exercising their basic rights should be immediately released.

NCIS Season 24’s Most Dangerous Story Is Paying Off

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Cher Thompson is a Senior Staff Writer at Screen Rant, where she covers everything from explosive reality TV moments to cast interviews about the twists and turns of scripted dramas. With a background in creative writing, marketing, and teaching, Cher knows how to spot both the storytelling genius and the guilty pleasures in pop culture. Her work blends wit with insight, making readers feel like they’re chatting with a friend who just happens to know way too much about TV.

Warning: this article contains spoilers for NCIS season 24 episode 6, "Ride or Die."

Throughout the run of NCIS, there have been some incredibly dangerous missions that the MCRT has taken on, and this season's most dangerous story is finally beginning to see some payoff. Throughout NCIS season 24, the MCRT has been dealing with some major changes. With Tony DiNozzo (Micahel Weatherly) returning to take on the NCIS director role just being the start of the season, there's been a lot of things to get used to for the larger MCRT, including some big shifts from one of their own.

After having kept big parts of his backstory close to the chest for years, Nick Torres (Wilmer Valderrama) had to face a dark piece of his former life as a gun for hire earlier this season. With his undercover persona, Dante Navarro, becoming the focal point of one of the MCRT's investigations, Nick struggled with the fact that he enjoyed the dark work he'd taken part in earlier in his career. After a case reawakened his feelings, Nick decided to go undercover again behind the MCRT's back as a side gig, but there wasn't much more development to his story in the episode of NCIS that followed, leaving viewers in the dark about what came next.

Thankfully, NCIS season 24 episode 6 made it clear that Nick's undercover storyline is far from over. In fact, it's getting more dangerous, more complicated, and more exciting as it continues to build into the larger plot of the season, making it NCIS season 24's most dangerous story yet.

While the introduction of Nick's undercover work seemed like it may be against what the MCRT was looking for him to do, NCIS season 24 episode 6, "Ride or Die," threw a wrench into the game. When DiNozzo authorizes Nick to go undercover on an operation on his own, the rest of the MCRT have no choice but to trust that he'll be okay, despite the lack of consistent check-ins. Alden Parker (Gary Cole) is the most vocally against the idea of having Nick go rogue within his own team, but Tony makes it clear that it was his call to let Nick run loose.

Throughout the episode, the stakes surrounding Nick's case are increasingly more difficult, especially as the rest of the MCRT gets more involved. Seeing Nick running point within the larger scope of the case is exciting, but it's clear that what he's doing is more dangerous than if he were simply investigating a case in the way he's done for years. While things ultimately get resolved, feeding into Nick's need to go undercover without the NCIS-sanctioned label seems to be helping with his feelings of stagnation, but it may be costing the team more than expected.

While there haven't been any details surrounding the side work that Nick seemingly agreed to do for his former handler, Walt Keller (Mike O'Malley), the fact that Tony put him out on a mission without Parker's okay makes it clear that he knows there's a bit of unrest brewing in Torres. Throughout the last few seasons of NCIS, Torres' future felt uncertain, and though he's now doing something that puts him in grave danger, it may not be too far-fetched to think this is the most dangerous spot he's been in during his time on the MCRT as a whole.

Watching Nick go into cases blindly, working undercover without having eyes on him from the rest of the team, appears to be fulfilling a side of his character that needs to dive into danger. Even so, the future that Torres has with the MCRT seems like it's on less-stable ground because of his insatiable appetite for work that puts him in the line of fire. Despite being a part of NCIS and working on some of the most difficult, intense investigations in the country, Torres' need to put himself into the belly of the beast may cost him the most stable position he's ever had.

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

EXCLUSIVE: ABC is going all in for the season finale of Dancing with the Stars.

Deadline understands that the Disney-owned network is going to air the Season 35 finale live around the country for the first time in its twenty-one-year history.

The move will see ABC air the finale on November 24 at 5pm PT/8pm ET. Normally, viewers on the west coast are forced to wait until 8pm PT to watch their favorite dancers. The three-hour finale will also re-air at 8pm PT and stream via Hulu the next day.

The most significant impact of the move is that viewers on the west coast will be able to vote live while watching the show for the first time.

This comes as the semi-final airs this evening with six celebrities left to waltz across screens: Tatyana Ali, who is dancing with Jan Ravnik, Ezra French, who is dancing with Daniella Karagach, Amber Glenn, who is dancing with Pasha Pashkov, Maura Higgins, who is dancing with Mark Ballas, Harry Shum Jr., who is dancing with Jenna Johnson, and Connor Wood, who is dancing with Rylee Arnold. Only one of these couples is expected to be eliminated this evening with the remaining five couples competing for the Len Goodman Mirrorball Trophy next week with a Judges’ Choice Dance, the return of the Instant Dance Challenge and a Freestyle.

The show, which also airs on Disney+, is hosted by Alfonso Ribeiro and Julianne Hough with Carrie Ann Inaba, Bruno Tonioli and Derek Hough as judges.

It comes as Dancing with the Stars continues to defy the odds and grow its audience, 21 years after launching on ABC. On Super Bowl Night, the show scored a nine-year ratings high among adults 18-49. The episode drew 6.59 million live + same-day viewers, according to Nielsen’s Big Data + Panel, up 2% from the week prior.

Its opening episode, which saw only the male celebrities and their pro partners compete, also saw a 24% increase on the previous season.

The show also scored its first Emmy nomination in the Outstanding Reality Competition Program category for the first time in a decade.

"I think it's more how I was raised [by] my parents," Alvarado said. "Treat people how you want to be treated. Sometimes the situation isn't going to be what you want. You can't control certain things and control what you can and that's your attitude, your energy every day. So I try to do that.

In a viral moment from Sunday's victory over the Raptors, Alvarado was filmed comforting a visibly dejected Mikal Bridges, who was struggling and on the bench.

To appoint the next comptroller general of the United States, follow the law

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The new approach being employed to appoint the next Comptroller General of the United States should raise a red flag. The Government Accountability Office (GAO), which is headed by the Comptroller General, plays an important role in helping Congress discharge its responsibilities under Article I of the U.S. Constitution, particularly in connection with oversight of the Executive Branch.

We were shocked to learn that U.S. Sen. Rand Paul (R-KY), Chairman of the Senate Committee on Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs (HSGAC), recently and spontaneously requested that names be submitted to him, under a short time constraint, to be recommended to the President for nomination as the next Comptroller General of the United States.

Under the GAO Act of 1980, a 10-member statutory bipartisan and bicameral Comptroller General Selection Commission was created. Chairman Paul is a member, since he is Chair of the HSGAC, but he is not the Chairman of the Commission. That Commission is required to jointly develop and transmit a list of at least three qualified nominees to the President. That is the law, and that is how the process of appointing the Comptroller General has been conducted since 1980.

The current vacancy for Comptroller General of the United States began on Dec. 30, 2025, and yet the Commission and its members have never met.

Equally shocking is that Sen. Paul took the liberty as HSGAC Chairman to insert an empty placeholder for an unnamed nominee for Comptroller General of the United States for a Nov. 19, 2026, Committee confirmation hearing.

Again, Sen. Paul did so even though the statutorily required Comptroller General Selection Commission has never convened or submitted a list of candidates. Then, on Nov. 16, President Trump submitted a nominee for confirmation. (We don’t want to name the candidate since we do not want to make it a personal issue.)

The General Accounting Office Act of 1980 included a revised method for appointing the Comptroller General. It recognized that the position was in the Legislative Branch and that GAO was the primary agency the Congress uses to conduct oversight of the Executive Branch. Therefore, it provided for a bipartisan and bicameral commission to recommend candidates to the President for possible appointment.

It also provided for a 15-year non-renewable term with extremely difficult removal requirements to help ensure the independence of the Comptroller General. This 15-year term is the longest for any appointed position in the federal government.

To be clear, the 1980 law doesn’t say in so many words that the President must pick from the Commission’s list. The Senate report on the bill said he could, “in his discretion,” look elsewhere, but should give the Commission’s recommendations “great weight.” You can’t weigh a list that doesn’t exist. Every Comptroller General nominated since 1980 came from a Commission list, and the law’s answer to a President who wants other options is to ask the Commission for more names, not to skip it. Henry Wray, a former GAO attorney who helped develop the 1980 law, says the current unprecedented process “violates both the letter and the spirit of the law.”

It is extremely important for Congress to follow the statutory process. Dave Walker and Gene Dodaro (two of the coauthors of this piece) were unanimously confirmed under this process in 1998 and 2010, respectively. Both of these appointments were made under the statutory commission method, which has worked like a charm.

With regard to potential candidates, any Comptroller General should be a highly qualified executive with relevant past experience who will operate in a professional, objective, fact-based, non-partisan, non-ideological, and non-personal manner. The Comptroller General must be independent from the Executive Branch, which the GAO audits, investigates, and evaluates on behalf of the Congress and the American people. In addition, any Comptroller General must have broad bipartisan support. In fact, every one of the past eight Comptrollers General was confirmed with overwhelming bipartisan support. All of these factors are of critical importance for the Comptroller General to be credible and for him or her, and the GAO, to be effective.

Since Senate Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs Committee Chairman Paul and President Trump have not followed the statutory requirements for the appointment of the Comptroller General, the Nov. 19, 2026, confirmation hearing should be canceled, and the normal appointment process should be adhered to.

What exactly are Flock cameras and how can you spot one?

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Flock cameras are everywhere, and they’re easier to miss than you think.

Basic descriptions of Flock cameras fall woefully short of their true capabilities and potential uses. Most people are familiar with the automated license plate reader (ALPR) version, usually pole-mounted and paired with a solar panel. If that were all there was to it, this would be a very short article. But Flock Safety (Flock Group Inc.) has become deeply intertwined with the defining terminology of ALPR cameras.

In truth, there are more than a dozen manufacturers and vendors of ALPRs; their products just aren't called Flock cameras, and each has its own aesthetic characteristics. For all intents and purposes, however, they are identical. If you see Motorola on the side of one, it likely does much of what a Flock does: read plates and log vehicles.

Most are vertically oval, narrow in width and fairly small, roughly the size of your hand. They're designed to capture your license plate and the time and place you passed by. Most are also accompanied by a solar panel. Leonardo and Genetec make the most visually distinct versions. Leonardo's are blockier, rectangular in shape, with some elongated versions. Genetec's are horizontally oval, with the same narrow width as Flock's variations, only flipped. Motorola manufactures a mix of the two.

The problem is that some Flock cameras can easily be mistaken for a standard traffic camera and vice versa. Because of this, it's difficult to accept the absolute accuracy of a project like DeFlock, which uses volunteers to identify Flock cameras visually. There's no centralized or formal verification system to independently confirm the sightings.

Flock also sells more than ALPRs. Depending on what the customer wants, the system can log details like roof racks on cars and bumper stickers. Some setups have microphones and others can recognize things like clothes and beards.

Flock cameras usually turn up where traffic funnels. They're often mounted on poles at intersections and busy traffic areas. Since many homeowners associations (HOAs) are major Flock camera customers, it shouldn't be a surprise to find them at neighborhood entrances. Shopping centers aren't off the list of potential locations either. The aesthetics aren't irrelevant, but since several manufacturers make them, there are a few other telltale signs to look for as well.

Flock cameras are meant to capture license plates, so look at where they're aimed. Check for Flock's bird logo on the camera and a solar panel above it. Because most are self-powered, there's usually little to no additional equipment nearby. Red light and speed cameras are typically larger and wired to traffic signals or enforcement poles. White, elongated cameras are another lookalike, but they mostly help traffic signals adjust to traffic flow. Some states also mount signs next to speed cameras so they're easy to identify.

Keep in mind that there are other variations of Flock cameras out there. The Flock Condor is a mounted sphere that rotates 360 degrees and tracks people. Naturally, these are mounted near areas with heavy pedestrian traffic. Flock also makes a mobile security trailer, a gunshot detector and a drone-as-first-responder. Because companies are using video integration tools to convert standard IP cameras into plate readers, it will be increasingly difficult to identify Flock or Flock-type cameras.

As a rule of thumb, if it's pointed at you, it's potentially watching you. But for a broader view, check the Atlas of Surveillance, a searchable database from the Electronic Frontier Foundation that shows what technology law enforcement agencies use. Search your town or county to see whether police are using plate readers.

While some services like MapQuest can help you find Flock cameras in your neighborhood, be wary of crowdsourced maps. DeFlock uses OpenStreetMap data collected from volunteers, but there's no official public list, so a missing pin doesn't mean there isn't a Flock camera sitting there.

Pins can also be wrong, since it's easy to confuse a Flock camera with the countless security and traffic camera variations. A Flock camera doesn't have to belong to a public agency. Flock and Flock competitors also sell to HOAs and retailers.

If you want to find every Flock camera in your city, county or state, you have to put in the work. Submit a public records request, attend the next city council meeting or ask your HOA and local businesses.

Gorley earned his 13th ASCAP country songwriter of the year honor.

Evan Bartels opened the show with a rendition of “In the Middle of It All.”

ASCAP president/chairman Paul Williams, CEO Elizabeth Matthews and other ASCAP staffers announced honorees and presented winners with commemorative plaques honoring their top-performing songs over the past year. ASCAP’s country song of the year went to “Just In Case,” cowritten by ASCAP writer Jacob “JKash” Hindlin and recorded by Morgan Wallen.

Ashley Gorley picked up his 13th ASCAP country songwriter of the year honor. Taking the stage, Gorley reflected on the win and his years of making a living by writing songs. He said he was “super grateful to have done this at this level for this long,” noting that one thing that hasn’t changed is “love for the music.” Gorley added: “I’m so thankful to ASCAP for recognizing me for so many years.”

Sony Music Publishing was named ASCAP’s country publisher of the year for songs including “Bottle Rockets,” “Be Her,” “Better Me for You (Brown Eyes),” “Somewhere Over Laredo” and “Til The Last One Dies.” Sony Music Publishing Nashville leader Rusty Gaston praised Sony staffers who support songwriters every day. And to the songwriters gathered there, Gaston encouraged, “You have the power to make magic. It is the biggest honor to get to champion your songs every day, because your songs matter.”

Capitol CMG Publishing was named Christian music publisher of the year for publishing songs including “Heaven on My Mind,” “Still,” “Can’t Steal My Joy” and “Demons.” The honor for ASCAP Christian song of the year went to “Your Way’s Better” recorded by Forrest Frank and cowritten by ASCAP writer Pera Krstajic.

Matthew West earned his seventh ASCAP Christian songwriter of the year, for his work in writing songs including “No Fear” and “God Story.” Josiah Queen was honored with the ASCAP Christian songwriter-artist of the year accolade for his hits “Can’t Steal My Joy,” “Demons” and “Dusty Bibles.”

Other artists in attendance and picking up wins included Kelsea Ballerini, Shaboozey and George Birge.

In closing out the evening, Wynonna Judd received the Golden Note award. Ballerini presented the accolade to Judd, and praised her strength, creativity and vulnerability. In accepting the honor, Judd said, “Music has saved my life more times than I can count.” She told the songwriters gathered, “To this day, I thank you … because without your songs, I wouldn’t have anything to say and the fans wouldn’t come that have been in my fan cub for 35 years. Thank you, ASCAP, for honoring my journey, and thank you, songwriters, for helping me find my voice.”

Judd also performed a rendition of “The Girl Who Could Sing” from her recent album The Hard Truth.

For a full list of winners, visit ascap.com.

Why iPhone Duo wasn’t for me (but might be for you)

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

I’ve had the iPhone Duo for nearly two weeks now, and have officially decided to return it. Here are the three main reasons why the Duo didn’t work out for me, but might for you.

I use my iPhone one-handed all the time, and based on my experience, iPhone Duo is clearly intended to be used with two hands. You can use it one-handed, but for me it’s not nearly as comfortable as the iPhone 18 Pro. It’s too wide and too heavy to be used for long.

Perhaps over time I’d get used to needing two hands for most iPhone interactions. But that would be a pretty significant shift for me, as there are so many situations when I don’t have both hands free.

If you already use two hands with your iPhone regularly—perhaps because you have a Pro Max model—then the Duo’s one-handed experience likely won’t bother you. But the more I used the Duo, the more I found myself missing my iPhone 18 Pro.

The biggest value proposition of a foldable is that it’s a 2-in-1 product, letting you have a standard phone but also a tablet-sized display that fits in your pocket.

I totally get the appeal, and fully expected to love that hybrid design. But after two weeks of trying, I still struggled to find times when I actually wanted to unfold the Duo. And to be clear, that’s a me problem, not an issue with the Duo itself.

I tried a bunch of apps on the inner display, and struggled to find use cases that—for me—made it worthwhile to open the Duo. But based on the feedback I’ve seen from most Duo owners, I’m very much in the minority.

I know lots of people love the Duo for video, but I don’t watch much video on my iPhone. I almost never watch YouTube, TikTok, or Instagram Reels. And for shows and movies, I stick with my TV screen and an Apple TV 4K.

Multitasking support on iPhone Duo works really well. But when I found myself needing to multitask, I still reached for my iPad Pro with Magic Keyboard attached. A keyboard-free product can’t replace my main computer (nor would I expect it to), and the Duo screen was too small to comfortably work on it.

Last year I bought an iPhone Air and absolutely loved it. Initially, the Air’s less capable camera system didn’t bother me, it was “good enough.”

After a few months though, I started missing the Pro camera system more. Then I learned that my wife was pregnant with twins, and I knew I wanted the best possible iPhone cameras to capture their early weeks and months. So I bought an iPhone 18 Pro in September.

I’ve been very impressed by iPhone 18 Pro’s Main camera upgrades, and it’s great having a Telephoto lens again too. Since having the best camera system is so crucial for me right now, I knew iPhone Duo couldn’t be my only iPhone.

I initially planned to dual-carry the two models, using iPhone 18 Pro solely as a camera. But over and over I found myself wishing it was my only iPhone.

Is the iPhone Duo camera good enough for most people? Probably so. But for this year at least, with my twins aging rapidly, it’s not quite right for me. If I loved the Duo for other reasons, I would just use both models. But since I didn’t, I’m happy to stick with the iPhone 18 Pro.

In closing, I want to explicitly state that I don’t think the iPhone Duo is a bad product, and this isn’t a “bad review.”

The iPhone Duo just isn’t for me. I get the sense, though, that it’s lots of people’s favorite iPhone in years. Apple created its most unique new iPhone model since the original, and there’s plenty to love about it. I’m very curious to see how popular foldables become over time compared to slab models, because each clearly offers pros and cons. But for me, at least right now, the iPhone 18 Pro is my model of choice.

Trials Raise Questions About Perioperative Immunotherapy in Bladder Cancer

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

— Experts weigh negative KEYNOTE-866 and ENERGIZE findings against positive NIAGARA trial

In this exclusive roundtable video from MedPage Today, three expert leaders in the field of bladder cancer discuss the latest research presented at the European Society for Medical Oncology (ESMO) congress.

Moderator Matthew Galsky, MD, of the Icahn School of Medicine at Mount Sinai in New York City, is joined by David Aggen, MD, PhD, of Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center in New York City, and Stephanie Berg, DO, of the Dana-Farber Cancer Institute in Boston.

In this second of four episodes, they examine the disappointing results from the KEYNOTE-866 and ENERGIZE trials, how those findings compare with the positive NIAGARA trial, and what they mean for perioperative treatment decisions in muscle-invasive bladder cancer.

Following is a transcript of their remarks:

Galsky: Okay, so let's talk a little bit about the next set of studies. And we saw two studies at ESMO, really along the same theme, and that is should we add immune checkpoint blockade to cisplatin-based therapy for cisplatin-eligible patients with muscle-invasive bladder cancer? We saw the results from KEYNOTE-866 and the ENERGIZE study, and I might add the long-awaited results from both of those studies. Previously, we saw the results of a similarly designed study called NIAGARA, which showed an improvement in essentially every endpoint, path CR [pathologic complete response] rate, event-free survival [EFS], and overall survival [OS].

But these studies were disappointing. There were some improvements in path CR rate versus the control arm. However, there was not an improvement in the time-to-event outcomes. And that raises some questions about cisplatin-based chemotherapy plus immune checkpoint blockade in the perioperative setting.

And now we have data with these different regimens that are mixing and matching components, so it's harder to say that it's just the class of IO [immunotherapy], PD-1 or PD-L1 blockade, or it's harder to say that it's the duration of treatment because duration of treatments were pretty similar. So, Dr. Berg, what do you make of these studies?

Berg: I agree. I kind of forgot they were around until when they were presented at ESMO. And I think ENERGIZE was really interesting because I didn't really know a lot about that compound. But in the end, they both were kind of negative and really aren't going to change practice in my mind.

But I think it was something where we saw, like you mentioned, some path CR gains, but it's also with the design of the studies and what's going to actually happen in current practice compared to what we were just discussing within ADC [antibody-drug conjugate] and IO. I'm not sure they're not going to really change practice, but it was something that, again, they were finally out and we are definitely abandoning cisplatin chemo.

Galsky: So these studies might not change practice themselves, as you point out, but there is another study that showed benefit with this type of approach, which is NIAGARA. And that really makes one wonder if all three studies were presented at the same time, whether or not the general interpretation of the studies would be different than having the positive study come out first and well before the others.

So, Dr. Aggen, what do these studies tell you in terms of how to interpret NIAGARA in retrospect?

Aggen: I think you can't compare because these trials sort of happen in different eras. Perhaps in the control arm of the more contemporary studies like ENERGIZE, there was more use of adjuvant IO that may have narrowed the difference. It's also quite striking in ENERGIZE, it enrolled a very high percentage of patients who had clinical T2 disease. And so maybe it was a slightly lower-risk population as well.

I think the question though is sort of obsolete given the striking results from KEYNOTE-905, KEYNOTE-B15, and now VOLGA that say that you should probably be giving ADC-IO to patients. I think that there may be a small subgroup with either variant histologies or other tumor features that needs durvalumab [Imfinzi] in combination with cisplatin. We don't really have the granularity of who that is.

And so at the moment I still think ADC-IO is our backbone of perioperative therapy. It is an interesting academic question though that you have two phase III trials with chemo-IO that show perhaps higher path CR rates than NIAGARA, but not the same benefit in terms of EFS and OS. And I don't think we're going to get crystal answers as to why those differences exist.

Galsky: So, Dr. Berg, Monday morning, any patient walking in the door who is going to be an appropriate candidate for cisplatin-based chemotherapy plus immune checkpoint blockade versus EV [enfortumab vedotin (Padcev)] plus immune checkpoint blockade?

Berg: I do agree with Dr. Aggen. I think the non-bladder or non-urothelial histology, perhaps even upper tract urethra, since they weren't enrolled on these studies, you would really discuss about cisplatin perioperative therapy. Even though those other studies too with variant histology, we normally go right to cystectomy.

But again, we can consider a perioperative therapy for them and I think it's still appropriate and active in those subsets, but I think more is going to come out with ADC and the non-urothelial histology. So there's some work being done in that center, but I think that's what I would use right now. I would still consider it pretty appropriate.

SAG-AFTRA has tentatively re-upped its solo contract with Netflix.

The guild revealed Tuesday that a tentative agreement had been reached on a new bargaining agreement, which is now subject to approval by the national board.

That vote is expected “in the near future,” per the union, and no details from the deal are expected in advance of that review.

The performers’ union has had a standalone contract with Netflix Studios since 2019. Before that, the streaming giant dealt with the guild on a production-by-production basis. Guild officials said at the time that, with few exceptions, the contract’s terms followed the guild’s Codified Basic Agreement and Television Agreements.
