The traditional back squat has long been the staple of those who want to crush their quads, but standing under a heavy barbell is not for everyone. Mature gym goers, or anyone with mobility issues, may find that the downward force of carrying a heavy bar across the shoulders leads to stress on the spine. Fortunately, IFBB Pro and physique transformation coach, Tanya Johansen swears by the Spanish squat.

“Try a Spanish squat,” recommends the Bikini Pro competitor. “It fries your quads… when done correctly.”

The Spanish squat is a popular movement utilized for rehabbing the knees, but it also pounds the quadriceps, providing the perfect environment for muscle growth. To try it for yourself, stand in front of a cable station or power rack and anchor a resistance band around the support, looping around the back of your knees. You can stand inside a single loop, or loop the band around each knee, as demonstrated by Johansen.

Next, move back a little to make sure there is tension on the band. Now, you should be able to squat by sitting back with the band keeping you upright. Keep your shins vertical, and place emphasis on the quads as you lift and lower your body.  “Band behind your knees, sit back into it, stay tall, let your knees come forward,” summarized the coach, who specializes in physiques that are over 40.

Because there is no barbell compressing your body, the Spanish squat can be executed without overloading the back and knee joints. The quads can still be worked hard with no additional weight, and for those looking to improve their range of mobility, Spanish squats are awesome for building knee strength because they rehab the patellar and its related tendons.

“Over 40? Even better reason,” advises the coach of the Spanish squat. “You don’t need a heavy bar on your back to grow your legs. Forget heavy. Make the muscle do the work.”  And, for those who use the barbell as a crux for their ego, this IFBB Pro has some real talk. “You’re not in there to impress anyone,” she explained. “You’re in there to make sure your quads earn it.”

To make this move more challenging, you can bring in an isometric hold at the top of the squat, pausing before descending back down. You can also slow down the lowering phase for additional time under tension. Aim for 3 sets of 8 to 12 reps.

“It’s not the weight. It’s the work,” concluded Johansen, who’s own physique is proof positive that training smart can bring serious progress.

Spinach Artichoke Baked Potatoes

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

This baked potato stuffed with spinach-artichoke dip is the ultimate solo comfort meal. Classic twice-baked potatoes require two trips to the oven to deliver a pillowy, gussied-up filling and crispy skins all in one bite. Here, I’ve streamlined the workflow with a microwave. By zapping your spuds to start, you can reduce the overall baking time without sacrificing texture. Pick a large russet (or Idaho) for the best results; their relatively high starch and low moisture content helps them bake up airy and light, not gummy.

The rich, velvety filling that melts over top comes together in moments while your spud cooks. Cream cheese and mozzarella pack in enough dairy richness for a satisfying cheese pull and mellow out the subtle bitterness of baby spinach and nutty tang of artichoke hearts. (It’s important that your cream cheese is room temperature before you mash it in with the veggies. If it’s still got a chill from the fridge, it won’t fully incorporate.) The Worcestershire sauce is optional, but just a few shakes from the bottle bring instant savory depth to the filling.

Most recipes serve multiple people. In Me, Myself, and Dinner, each dish serves one and only one. That's you! Because whether you live solo (almost 30% of Americans do) or are enjoying a night to yourself, sitting down to a meal should be something to look forward to.

Preheat oven to 425°. Poke 1 large russet potato (about 12 oz.) all over with a fork; rub with avocado or vegetable oil to coat and sprinkle with kosher salt. Microwave potato on a microwave-proof plate on high 5 minutes. Turn potato over and microwave on high until fork-tender, about 5 minutes more. Let potato sit until just cool enough to handle.

While potato is cooling, microwave 5 oz. baby spinach with 2 Tbsp. water in a medium microwave-proof glass bowl until wilted, about 2 minutes. Carefully press leaves to release as much liquid as possible, then drain off liquid. Coarsely chop spinach.

Mix 2 Tbsp. cream cheese, room temperature, in a medium bowl until smooth. Add spinach, 4 quartered artichoke hearts (from a 14-oz. can), coarsely chopped, 2 Tbsp. finely grated Parmesan, 2 Tbsp. coarsely grated mozzarella, ½ tsp. garlic powder, and ¼ tsp. Worcestershire sauce (if using) and fold to combine; season with salt and freshly ground pepper.

Slice potato in half lengthwise and lightly fluff flesh in each half. Set on a small foil-lined rimmed baking sheet. Divide spinach mixture between potato halves, piling high (don’t pack too tightly). Sprinkle more Parmesan over. Bake potato halves until very tender when squeezed and skin is wrinkled and crisp, 20–25 minutes. Let cool slightly before eating.

There’s some bad news for Gian Piero Gasperini. Devyne Rensch will be sidelined for approximately three weeks following the injury sustained against Manchester United.

According to Sky Sport, tests have revealed a minor tear in his right adductor, which will force the Dutch defender to miss Roma’s matches for the next three weeks.

Rensch was forced off after just 21 minutes of yesterday’s match against Man Utd, making way for Emanuele Lulli.

Initially, it was thought he had a muscle strain, but tests carried out in the hours that followed revealed the extent of the injury.

The Dutchman will certainly miss the match against Sassuolo, scheduled for Saturday, November 7th at the Olimpico, and his return is therefore unlikely to come until after the November international break.

With God on Their Side: Bob Dylan & Pope Leo XIV Commune at the Vatican

📅 Nov 04, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The musician is set to play a show at the Palazzo dello Sport in Rome.

This week, Bob Dylan is knock, knock, knockin’ on heaven’s door — well, arguably the closest earthly thing to it — with the folk-rock icon meeting Pope Leo XIV at the Vatican on Wednesday (Nov. 4).

The Catholic headquarters revealed that the two figures had linked up by sharing a photo on Instagram of them shaking hands. With the pope smiling warmly in his usual traditional white robes, Dylan wears a hooded black zip-up underneath a jacket and looks at the Bishop of Rome intently.

“Pope Leo XIV met with American singer-songwriter Bob Dylan in a private audience on Wednesday, November 4,” the caption simply reads.

Other snaps from the meetup show the two men in conversation while sitting together at a table and posing together in front of a piece of artwork. Another photo features them standing and talking as Dylan leans against an umbrella.

According to Vatican News, their meeting was “brief, consisting of greetings and a few lighthearted exchanges.” The pope also apparently gifted Dylan a rosary, while the musician returned the favor by giving him a copy of his Chronicles: Volume One memoir.

Pope Leo XIV ascended to the religious institution’s top job in May last year following the death of Pope Francis, becoming the first American-born cardinal to do so. He recently made headlines — and history — by voting via mail-in ballot in the United States midterm elections, which ended with Democrats reclaiming power over both the House of Representatives and Senate.

His meeting with Dylan comes one day ahead of the 10-time Grammy winner’s planned performance at the Palazzo dello Sport, the latest stop on his Rough and Rowdy Ways Tour. He’s charted 81 entries on the Billboard 200 over the course of his career, topping the tally five times.
