Manhunt in western Turkey after eight killed in series of shootings

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

The shootings took place in rural areas northeast of the city of Afyonkarahisar (pictured)

A manhunt is under way for a suspect in western Turkey after eight people were killed and one seriously injured in a series of shootings.

The attacks took place in the Afyonkarahisar province on Thursday, with victims found in several locations in three separate vehicles, according to local reports.

CCTV footage published in Turkish media showed a man in a black jacket, white trousers and glasses clutching a long weapon as he stood by a red minivan.

Turkey's state-run Anadolu Agency reported that three people were killed near Ayazini village and three others near Kiyir village. In Yukaritandir, two women were found injured and later died in hospital.

Two teachers were reportedly among the dead.

Local media said roads leading to the affected villages were closed by police, while officers launched a large-scale operation to find the suspect, who fled the scene.

The newspaper Milliyet reports that a vehicle allegedly taken by the attacker was later found stuck in mud, and that the suspect had abandoned it before fleeing on foot.

In a statement posted on social media, the Afyonkarahisar Governorate said "all relevant institutions of our state have been working in coordination since the very first moment and are meticulously carrying out the necessary work."

It added: "We pray to God for mercy on the deceased citizens and offer our condolences and patience to their grieving families and loved ones."

The U.S. government is warning of an “ongoing cyber threat” facing industrial refrigeration units, after reports of cyberattacks targeting freezers and fridges in supermarkets around the world, U.S. military bases, and at least one blood bank.

U.S. cybersecurity agency CISA, the FBI, and several other federal government departments warned in a joint statement on Thursday that hackers are targeting and compromising internet-connected refrigeration and cold chain monitoring systems in grocery stores and cold storage facilities. The statement said that the hacks are hitting critical infrastructure, such as the food, agriculture, transportation, and medicine-shipping industries.

“If successfully exploited, the actors could change the temperature and possibly cause physical impacts such as fire hazards, loss of inventory and changes to the service life of the system,” the agencies warn.

The warning comes after several waves of cyberattacks targeting industrial refrigeration units across the United Kingdom and Europe in recent weeks. Two separate waves of attacks allowed hackers to crank up the temperature on freezers and chilling units in at least a dozen countries, spoiling defrosted food and destroying produce. Some of the affected systems included remote-controlled refrigeration units made by Danish company Danfoss, which had stopped providing security updates to its customers’ freezers.

Hackers also targeted a blood bank in France, causing its refrigerated units to boost temperatures in its cold units containing donated blood supplies — but were stopped before the attack could compromise blood stores. The U.S. Department of Defense is investigating a spate of hacks targeting freezers run by the military, according to Reuters. The hacks were first reported by Signal and Silence.

The U.S. has not attributed the hacks to a particular group or government, but security researchers have broadly attributed the attacks to Iran; U.S. officials also reportedly suspect Iran is the likely culprit.

CISA, the FBI, and the Departments of Agriculture, Health and Human Services, and Transportation urged affected industries to remove remote control systems from the internet, change and strengthen passwords, apply patches where possible, and retire equipment that’s no longer supported by the vendor.

In a separate fact sheet, the departments said that hackers may attempt to “force units into continuous heating and defrost cycles, which can cause widespread inventory loss and equipment hazards.”

“Every organization that depends on refrigeration and monitoring systems should assume it could be a target,” said CISA’s acting cybersecurity chief Chris Butera.

Hyundai is recalling nearly 30,000 Kona Electric vehicles due to an airbag software defect

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Hyundai issued a recall affecting nearly 30,000 Kona Electric vehicles due to a software error that could cause the airbag to fail to deploy in a crash.

In a letter sent to the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration on November 4, Hyundai said it was recalling certain 2019 to 2023 Kona Electric and 2019 to 2022 Veloster N vehicles.

The recall impacts 37,901 vehicles in total, including 29,058 Kona Electric and 8,843 Veloster N models.

According to documents submitted to the NHTSA, the issue stems from a software error with the airbag control unit (ACU) that can cause the airbag malfunction indicator light (MIL) to come on and the airbag system to deactivate under certain driving conditions.

The data may become corrupted during a system reboot or power interruption while the system is writing to the ACU’s memory. When this happens, the airbag MIL will illuminate to warn drivers.

As of October 22, 2026, Hyundai is aware of 331 alleged incidents related to the issue. However, none have resulted in a crash, fire, or injury.

Owners can take their vehicle to the closest dealer, where it will update the ACU software, free of charge.

Hyundai expects to mail out owner notification letters on January 1, 2027. In the meantime, owners can contact Hyundai’s customer service at 1-855-371-9460 with any questions or concerns. Hyundai’s recall number is 311.

You can also contact the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration Vehicle Safety Hotline at 1-888-327-4236 or visit the NHTSA.gov website for more information. The NHTSA campaign number is 26V728.

Saudi-Backed LIV Golf, After Five Years of Trying to Challenge PGA Tour, Is Shutting Down

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

LIV Golf, the pro-golfing startup venture whose biggest investor had been the sovereign wealth fund of Saudi Arabia, is shutting down after it couldn’t reach an agreement with a new investor on a bankruptcy reorganization.

The league was founded in 2021 by a group of pro golfers, led by Phil Mickleson and Greg Norman, who were dissatisfied with the sport’s biggest player, the PGA Tour. Saudi Arabia’s Public Investment Fund, which was LIV Golf’s primary backer, invested more than $5 billion into the venture — before the PIF in April 2026 said it was ending its funding. Related Stories NewsNation Plans Fast-Paced Election Night Focused on Hot Data, Not Hot Air MS NOW Has 2026 Midterm Elections Coverage: Here's How to Watch Election Night Results Live Online Free

After the Saudis exited, LIV Golf had proposed a recapitalization transaction with BC Partners Advisors through its voluntary Chapter 11 bankruptcy proceedings in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of New Jersey. However, according to a statement from the league, “the relevant parties were ultimately unable to reach a consensual resolution of certain outstanding terms of the transaction.”

As such, LIV Golf announced that it intends to liquidate its assets and wind down the business, but added that “the company will remain open to, and continue to consider, any value-maximizing alternatives with third parties that may arise.”

BC Partners Credit had proposed investing $300 million into LIV Golf but deal talks fell apart. In a statement, the financial firm said, “BC Partners Credit supported a transaction that aligned the interests of players and investors, offering a credible path toward building a sustainable future for LIV Golf. While there was great support for the league, we are disappointed that mutually acceptable terms could not be reached among the various stakeholders.”

In January 2025, Fox Sports and LIV Golf announced a multiyear media rights agreement for live coverage of the league’s competition in the U.S. Under the pact, Fox carried live coverage LIV Golf League’s 14-tournament global schedule with events in the U.S., Asia, Australia, Europe and Latin America; LIV Golf tournaments had previously aired on Nexstar Media Group’s CW network. A rep for Fox Sports did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

The top-ranked golfers in the LIV Golf League this season included Jon Rahm, Bryson DeChambeau, Joaquin Niemann, Lucas Herbert, Tyrrell Hatton, Thomas Detry, Anthony Kim and Sergio Garcia.

LIV Golf CEO Scott O’Neil said in a statement Thursday, “Building a global platform designed to bring new, younger, and more diverse fans to the sport of golf has been the round of a lifetime for all of us. LIV Golf was fun, demanding, inspiring, at times challenging — but always rewarding. … We created memories that I hope will inspire a new generation of fans to follow, play, and fall in love with this great game.”

In LIV Golf’s liquidation, Gibson, Dunn & Crutcher is serving as lead legal counsel, Cole Schotz is serving as co-legal counsel, David Orlofsky of AlixPartners is serving as chief restructuring officer, Ducera Partners is serving as investment banker, and C Street Advisory Group is serving as strategic communications adviser.

BioWare Documentary Slated for Release in 2027

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Daniel is a lifelong gamer with a love for various genres and gameplay styles who enjoys exploring games and playing from various angles.

It’s been a few weeks now since the sudden announcement of EA’s closure of BioWare, longtime developer of the fan-favorite Mass Effect series. The major fervor surrounding this landmark closure, as well as the cancellation of the next Mass Effect, has gradually started to die down, and all that’s left is a sad little hole in our hearts where hope for a new spacefaring adventure once lived.

In spite of its unfortunate denouement, it doesn’t change the fact that BioWare was, is, and shall always remain a beloved studio, whose stories and worlds touched many a heart. In fact, BioWare and its staff were something of hometown heroes to its origin in the city of Edmonton, Canada. Because of this, a production effort has been spurred forth by the Canadian entertainment industry to properly document the studio’s 30-plus years of history.

For an in-depth look at how we lost one of the gaming greats, this is how the fall of BioWare was years in the making.

According to a Deadline exclusive published today, a documentary outlining the history of BioWare, from its founding in 1995 to its closure this year, is in production now, with hopes to have it up on screens sometime next year, including a run through all of the major film festivals. This will be the definitive BioWare story, told not just by its staff, but the many fans whose lives were changed by its productions.

The documentary, titled “Massive Effect: The BioWare Story,” is being written by Canadian writer and director Akash Sherman, best known for his 2018 sci-fi film, Clara. Interestingly, the actual filming process for this documentary began about two years ago, which means everything was already well underway the moment the decision came down from EA to shutter the studio. Sherman and his crew have captured the moment the BioWare story came to an end, as well as the rippling effects it had on the gaming community.

The actual filming process for this documentary began about two years ago.

The headliners of the documentary are BioWare’s three original founders, Ray Muzyka, Greg Zeschuk, and Augustine Yip, though it’s also slated to feature appearances by a variety of other game industry alumni, including Lucasfilm Games Vice President Douglas Reilly, Star Wars: Knights of the Old Republic director Casey Hudson, Game Awards creator Geoff Keighley, and even EA’s infamous former CEO, John Riccitiello.

A ranking list of all the games BioWare has ever developed in the history of the studio.

“When the production team asked for our blessing to tell the BioWare story, we were honored and deeply moved,” Muzyka and Zeschuk said in a statement obtained by Deadline. “BioWare was never just about the logo or the building. It was about the stories brought to life by the amazing people on our dev teams, and the fans who defended those worlds, championed them and carried them forward.”

The film’s creation and production is something of a love letter from BioWare’s home turf of Canada. In fact, its creation was commissioned by Canada’s Super Channel, with the Canada Media Fund offering support throughout the production.

“We are thrilled to bring Massive Effect: The BioWare Story to Canadian audiences,” Kim Ball, EVP of Content & Communications for Super Channel told Deadline. “As an Edmonton-based company, we’re especially proud to celebrate BioWare’s extraordinary hometown story and the people, creativity and groundbreaking storytelling behind some of the most beloved games of a generation.”

The film’s producers are inviting BioWare’s fans to contribute photos, videos, and stories of how BioWare’s games have affected their lives, with a select few slated to be featured in the film. “BioWare’s fans have been part of its journey since the very first game,” said one of the film’s producers, Adam Scorgie. “It’s only right that they help us tell the rest of the story.”

Sherman himself has similar reverence for BioWare, saying that their games are what helped ignite his initial creative spark, and that it was his “privilege and responsibility to share the journey of underdogs who came together to create deeply beloved storytelling.”

Portuguese Media React to Cristiano Ronaldo's International Retirement

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

At the ripe old age of 41, Cristiano Ronaldo has retired from international football with Portugal. The iconic forward departs having scored a record 146 goals in 234 caps, most notably winning Euro 2016.

In an emotional farewell message, he said: "To the Portuguese people, the day has come to return the number 7 jersey of our national team. I never felt it was exclusively mine. But for 23 years I had the privilege and responsibility of wearing it, defending it, and taking it with me to stadiums all over the world. Today the moment has come to hand it over to the future. It wasn't an easy decision, because there are decisions that aren't made only with the head. They are also made with the heart."

Of course, Ronaldo departs as one of the greatest football players of all time, but he also leaves under a bit of a cloud. His final appearances with the national team before making the decision to retire were rather controversial. Indeed, the Al-Nassr striker walked away from the camp after falling out with manager Jorge Jesus over game time.

Many have felt Ronaldo should have retired years ago, and that he was actually holding the team back due to his diminishing quality. With that in mind, it's interesting to see how those in Portugal have reacted to the big news.

Portuguese boss Jesus has naturally given his thoughts on Ronaldo's retirement. He distanced himself from the decision and praised the player's career, stating:

"This is all his decision. We already had the opportunity to talk about this before the first call-up. I asked him to stay, to stay with me because I needed him to be by my side, but I have to accept his decision"

Jesus added: "He is a world reference, a reference for Portugal, a reference for the National Team. He is a player who, throughout these years, has given a lot to the National Team. He is a symbol for Portuguese youth. Most of his fans are young. I had the opportunity to share the experience of working with him for a year at Al Nassr . Therefore, he will always be a reference for our National Team, for our people, for our history."

With Ronaldo's falling out with Jesus in mind, media outlet O Jogo compared the player's exit to Lionel Messi, noting how the Argentine left international football in much happier circumstances: "Cristiano Ronaldo and Lionel Messi marked an era in world football, starring in one of the greatest rivalries in the history of the sport for about two decades. However, the end of their respective international careers is proving to be very different: while the Argentinian had a farewell in a festive and celebratory atmosphere, the Portuguese ended his connection with the National Team without fans anticipating that they would never see him again in the national jersey."

Duarte Gomes, the former referee and former technical director of refereeing at the Portuguese Football Federation (FPF), called it a 'shame' that things ended on such bad terms, noting:

"Cristiano Ronaldo will always be a unique figure in the history of Portuguese and world football. His legacy, his career, and everything he offered to the country deserved a different ending. Hardly anyone could have imagined that such an extraordinary connection could end like this. Not even him.

"What saddens me most is not the decision – inevitable in the short term – but the certainty that it should never have happened this way. This outcome is the culmination of a succession of thoughtless, avoidable, and poorly managed episodes, where a glaring inability of everyone – I repeat, everyone – to find a different solution stood out. Regardless of the underlying reasons that may have guided some and others (now irrelevant), one conclusion seems clear to me: somewhere in the process, an adult was missing from the room. A shame."

It's worth noting that the FPF are now planning a final game for Ronaldo to celebrate his career, as they 'refuse to let this farewell take place without due tribute.'

For the most part, however, media outlets across the country were keen to celebrate Ronaldo's special career, one of the most decorated in football history. A Bola, for example, spoke in fond terms of the departing legend, writing:

"Cristiano Ronaldo's farewell to the National Team marks the end of the most golden and impactful era in the history of Portuguese football. Twenty-three years of pure devotion, impossible goals, unprecedented titles, and global records transformed Portugal's status in the world. From his hopeful debut in Chaves in 2003 to his abrupt retirement in Copenhagen, the eternal captain bids farewell with the weight of legend."

Speaking to A Bola in a separate article, former Portugal manager Luiz Felipe Scolari – who gave Ronaldo his international debut in 2003 – insisted that this was the right moment to step away from the national team. He said: "Ronaldo, you did well. You're calm, you made the right decision," before adding: "You were one of the athletes that Portugal will never forget. And I, as your friend and as someone who admires you, will never forget you either, for everything you did with me and with your teammates."

Adding to that, as quoted in O Jogo, former Portugal and Manchester United teammate Nani said: "Congratulations, brother, on this beautiful journey! You inspired us all and made us believe that, as Portuguese people, we could be the best in the world. You made us feel proud of ourselves, as a people and as a team."

"You are and will always be a role model for all of us who follow you and who have shared wonderful moments with you, both on and off the field. The national team is the pinnacle of our careers, and I am incredibly proud to have been a part of your story. For me, you are the best of all time, not only for the player you were and everything you gave to our national team, but also for what you represented to all of us. Even when we often disagreed, we were always growing, improving, and learning. And we owe that to you as well. For all of this, I can only say: thank you very much, Captain! What you did for Portugal and for all of us will never be forgotten."

French authorities investigate claim of fraud over Picasso plates and medallions on sale

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Pablo Picasso with a silver plate, in 1957. The artist had commissioned François Hugo to produce repoussé metal pieces from his designs © 2026 Succession Picasso/DACS, London; © Photo: Edward Quinn, © edwardquinn.com

A criminal investigation amid a family crisis casts a dark shadow over the illustrious history of the French studio Ateliers Hugo, which produced jewellery and silverware created by major artists of the 20th century. According to a legal source, the court of Aix-en-Provence in the south of France has opened an investigation following a complaint by the Picasso family.

On 10 March, Paloma Ruiz-Picasso, representing the nine heirs of the Spanish artist, filed a complaint alleging “fraud, counterfeiting, forgery and concealment of these felonies”. The file does not name the alleged perpetrators, and their identity is not presently known. The following month the Picasso family published a formal warning in the Gazette Drouot, titled “Hugo silverware”. It urged “all art market professionals and buyers to exercise the utmost vigilance”, following the appearance of what it described as “a large number” of illegitimate silver plates and gold medallions, valued at millions of euros.

Paloma Ruiz-Picasso, the daughter of Pablo, has filed a legal complaint on behalf of nine family members of the artist about the allegedly unauthorised works © Studio Cabrelli

The title refers to Ateliers Hugo, a goldsmith based in Aix-en-Provence. It was founded in 1933 by François Hugo (1899-1981), the great-grandson of the famous French author Victor Hugo. In 1955 Pablo Picasso, who since 1947 had created a variety of ceramics in Vallauris on the French Riviera, commissioned François Hugo to produce plates and dishes from similar designs in fine metals, using the traditional technique of repoussé. Later, the workshop reduced them into medallions and earrings. With the help of his brother, the painter Jean Hugo, who introduced him to artistic circles, François made jewellery designed by Salvador Dalí, Max Ernst, André Derain, Jean Arp, Jean Dubuffet, Roberto Matta, Jean Lurçat and Dorothea Tanning. In 1975, his son Pierre Hugo (1947-2023) took over the company. Pierre was followed by his son Nicolas (born 1989).

Members of the Picasso family and their lawyer, Jean-Jacques Neuer, who filed the complaint, declined to comment to The Art Newspaper. But their complaint alleges that “it has been discovered that the moulds of Picasso’s works were reused for unauthorised productions of silver and gold pieces, followed by duplicates, some of them bearing three times the same number, and unnumbered specimens”.

According to a source close to the investigation, the Picasso Administration, which represents the family, says it has discovered at least 17 complete or almost complete suspicious collections, amounting to hundreds of pieces. The first red flag was raised by the Museum of Old and New Art (Mona), a private Australian museum in Tasmania. According to this source, in May 2023 the museum informed the Picasso Administration of its doubts over plates it had bought at Sotheby’s in London. An auction catalogue of 13 October 2021 describes a “complete set of 24 silver plates” bearing the signature of the artist, completed with an additional “unnumbered proof of Visage aux mains”. The pieces were “accompanied by a certificate of authenticity issued by Pierre Hugo”. The lot was sold for £1.4m. Sotheby’s declined to comment for this story.

According to the legal files, the seller was the British dealer Mark Maurice, from a now dissolved gallery named Masterpiece. The source close to the investigation says the dealer allegedly produced documents showing that he had commissioned a set of plates and a set of medallions from the Ateliers Hugo between 2017 and 2020. Mark Maurice indicated he was “unable to respond” to The Art Newspaper’s inquiries “due to strict non-disclosure agreements in place”.

In summer 2023 the ceramics museum in Vallauris, Musée Magnelli, exhibited another collection of 24 silver plates as well as gold medallions attributed to Picasso.

In 2023, the Musée Magnelli in Vallauris exhibited plates and medallions attributed to Picasso Travelworld

According to the legal files, the Picasso Administration protested, having found that they bore no number, mark or punch, as legally required. Nicolas Hugo acknowledges these facts, but tells The Art Newspaper that the works exhibited at Musée Magnelli were so-called “author’s copies, made for the exhibition, duly indicating in the catalogue that they were not for sale”. He adds that he “recovered them and had them melted after the show”. Representatives for the museum did not respond to a request for comment.

In June 2023, as reported in the legal files, the French branch of the auction house Bonhams made a request to the Picasso Administration for the right to reproduce silverware for a catalogue. The auction house was planning a sale of 95 lots from the Ateliers Hugo collection, including works by Ernst, Derain and Cocteau, valued between €400 and €50,000 each. The most valuable were 59 pieces, “made by” or “after” Picasso.

On 26 September 2023 Bonhams was informed by the Picasso Administration that these pieces were considered to be “unauthorised” editions. The auction house then decided to “delay the whole sale”, just two days before it was due to take place. Nicolas Hugo acknowledges he “did initiate, in the wake of good results obtained at Bonhams UK, this sale of pieces”, produced, he says, by his “father and grandfather”. But, he adds, “When we were informed by dealers of the presence of possible duplicates, I agreed to withdraw the sale and collaborated with the Picasso Administration to identify them.”

Bonhams declined to comment, but according to the source close to the investigation, the lots attributed to Picasso were sequestered when a British financial company, Suros Capital, tried to recover them as collateral for a €256,000 loan. A spokesperson for Suros confirmed the operation to The Art Newspaper, adding that “it was secured against not only the Picasso pieces, but a range of assets which are progressively realised, so the company expects to recover the loan in full”.

In November 2023, according to the source close to the investigation, a representative for Bonhams France informed the Picasso Administration that a Spanish collector contacted the auction house to sell 24 silver plates, estimated at £900,000. Again, the auction house refused on the basis of Picasso Administration’s doubts about the objects’ authenticity. In the legal files, the Picasso Administration claims the same collector had sold another set of 24 silver plates and one of 24 gold medallions in private sales through Bonhams in London in 2022. Neither the British auction house nor the Spanish collector made any comment.

Nicolas Hugo, who says he has not been interviewed by the investigators, claims he has no knowledge of the proceedings and “is unaware of the content of the complaint” issued by the Picasso Administration. But, he says: “The criminal investigation can only deal with facts related to the time of my father and grandfather, therefore falling under the statute of limitations and concerning persons now deceased.” He adds that “the rights to reproduce the items disappeared with them, so I have never minted any of Picasso’s creations”, and he does not have the legal status of a goldsmith. However, he says: “I have signed some certificates of authenticity, but only to confirm my father’s own signature.”

In interviews with The Art Newspaper, Nicolas Hugo describes himself as a “self-taught art dealer”. When he was around 20 years old, he started to work as an assistant in galleries in Paris, before moving to London. He is now an agent for several artists and a curator. In 2022, the French magazine Les Hardis described him as a “mix of British and Saint-Germain-des-Prés charm”. “My father became very ill in 2018,” he told the magazine. “I had an epiphany. I decided to take over the workshop. I immersed myself completely,” and started to train in manufacturing jewels.

“From 2019,” he tells The Art Newspaper, “I became involved in the history of his workshop and helped my father to showcase this heritage, for memorial and commercial reasons. I set up exhibitions in places like Barcelona or Vallauris and initiated this sale of the fonds d’atelier at Bonhams Paris. I have not taken over my father’s company and was not his partner.”

Nicolas Hugo says: “There have been some duplicates on the market, dating from my father’s period, but I have no idea who had made them in the workshop.” He says he has “no idea who put a collection on sale at Sotheby’s in 2021” and knows “nothing of the three sets proposed by this Spanish collector to Bonhams in 2022 and 2023”.

The criminal investigation is still at the preliminary phase. It comes at a time when the company is already in limbo. Pierre Hugo died in October 2023 and his five children have turned down his inheritance. Nicolas Hugo’s brothers and sisters declined to comment to The Art Newspaper due to the ongoing investigation.

However, sources close to the case say that Pierre Hugo’s heirs’ inheritance came with a €2.5m tax debt. According to these sources, three of the Hugo siblings said in police interviews they were “not at all” aware of the conduct of the workshop by their brother Nicolas, who had succeeded their ailing father in 2019. The sources said the three Hugo siblings were on alert after learning that Bonhams Paris declined to sell the collection from the Ateliers Hugo in September 2023. According to the same sources, when Pierre Hugo died a month later, the three siblings went to the workshop and took 21 moulds of plates that they decided to hand over to the Picasso Administration. Three moulds from the set were missing and they did not find those for the medallions.

According to these sources, the three Hugo siblings explained to the investigators that, “knowing that the rights of reproduction were extinct, they wanted to be sure this would be respected”. The siblings also asserted that they “did not want in any way to be implicated in these operations and made it clear to the Picasso Administration”, according to the sources.
