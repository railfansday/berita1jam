Official: Latest Italy U21 squad includes Inacio, Mane, Lulli and Bartesaghi

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Silvio Baldini has named a squad of 26 Italy U21 players including the likes of Samuele Inacio, Filippo Mane, Emanuele Lulli and Davide Bartesaghi for a pair of international friendlies against France and Belgium in November, as the Azzurrini prepare for the European Championships coming up this summer.

Like the senior Italy squad, the U21s will also be in action against France and Belgium in November, although the Azzurrini will face France away and Belgium at home: The opposite of the senior Azzurri squad.

These will both be friendly matches, following the completion of U21 European Championship qualifying last month.

The Italy U21s beat Armenia 1-0 last month, before an important 6-2 victory over Poland, which saw the Azzurrini leapfrog their opponents into first place in their qualifying group, earning them a guaranteed spot at the final tournament in the process.

The latest Italy U21 squad features several players who have returned after being named in the senior squad last month: Massimo Pessina, Eddy Kouadio and Inacio.

The squad also features a handful of players who had been considered for the senior squad this time around, but have been left with the U21s for another camp, including Milan’s Bartesaghi and Roma’s Lulli. Cher Ndour has also stayed with the U21s.

Regular centre-forward Antonio Raimondo is unavailable through injury. There are still several other Frosinone representatives, though, namely Lorenzo Palmisani, Gabriele Calvani, Matteo Cichella and Seydou Fini.

Luca Koleosho, a regular fixture of the U21 side during their European Championship qualifying campaign, has been promoted to the senior squad for November.

Goalkeepers: Giovanni Daffara (Parma), Diego Mascardi (Torino), Lorenzo Palmisani (Frosinone), Massimo Pessina (Bologna);

Defenders: Davide Bartesaghi (Milan), Gabriele Calvani (Frosinone), Fabio Chiarodia (Borussia M’Gladbach), Pietro Comuzzo (Torino), Eddy Kouadio (Monza), Emanuele Lulli (Roma), Filippo Mane (Borussia Dortmund), Brando Moruzzi (Avellino);

Midfielders: Lorenzo Amatucci (Deportivo La Coruna), Tommaso Berti (Cremonese), Matteo Cichella (Frosinone), Matteo Dagasso (Venezia), Giacomo Faticanti (Avellino), Luca Lipani (Sassuolo), Cher Ndour (Fiorentina);

Forwards: Alessio Cacciamani (Torino), Francesco Camarda (Milan), Luigi Cherubini (Benevento), Jeff Ekhator (Juventus), Seydou Fini (Frosinone), Samuele Inacio (Borussia Dortmund), Dominic Vavassori (Palermo).

As two US professors of political science wrote for Foreign Affairs magazine this week, past American presidents, like Trump, who become "lame ducks" — that is, a president who loses control of Congress and can't run for another term — have often ramped up international interventions.

"As they think about their legacy, lame ducks often turn to foreign policy, where the power of the presidency allows them to act unfettered by pesky domestic politics," they explained. And one of the places where Trump is most likely to act is Iran, the professors argued.

On the other hand, Trump also told several media outlets that he was in "no rush" to start bombing Iran again.

Opinions on Iran's next moves are also divided.

The Iranians have said they're ready for any attack and rhetoric from senior leaders indicates they may be willing to strike a wider range of targets than previously. That could include regional energy and economic infrastructure and even undersea telecommunications cabling or data centers.

If Iran thinks there is an existential threat to its leadership, then "all bets are off," Ross Harrison, a senior fellow at the Washington-based Middle East Institute, or MEI, and an expert on Iran, said during a virtual briefing hosted by MEI this week. "I think they will target differently in the Gulf states [this time] … so it really depends on how all this unfolds."

But other experts believe Iran was deliberately holding out to pressure Trump before the midterms. Now the election is over, the Iranians may be more willing to negotiate a peace deal.

All this leaves the Gulf states, who have been the main targets of Iranian attacks since the conflict began, in a difficult position.

"We find ourselves drawn within a crisis that we worked diplomatically very hard to try to avert," Anwar Gargash, a foreign policy adviser to United Arab Emirates leadership, said at a security conference in Bahrain last weekend, the Manama Dialogue hosted by the International Institute for Strategic Studies, or IISS. "We are clearly seeking a political off-ramp."

Qatar has been trying to negotiate between the US and Iran but has also been conducting emergency drills at its airport. The Emiratis have been doing similar drills. And Saudi Arabia is looking at a war on two fronts: The oil-rich state is already under attack from the Houthi militia in neighboring Yemen. Even Egypt has reacted, reportedly strengthening air defenses around the Suez Canal.

Still, no matter what happens next, the US-Israel-Iran war has already made Gulf states evaluate defense partnerships with the US. Rather than making them safe, US bases have made them a target.

"There is widespread disappointment across the region with the US over the folly of the war, the erratic nature of Washington's military and diplomatic conduct, and its decision to prioritize Israel's defense over that of its Gulf partners," Hasan Alhasan, a senior fellow at the IISS, told news agency Bloomberg this week. "If the US liberation of Kuwait in 1991 was the moment that established America's role in the region, the 2026 war may be remembered as the moment that undid it."

Gulf states are widening their outreach with, for example, the UAE getting closer to Israel and the Saudis entering the Mecca defense pact with Pakistan and Turkey.

"The Gulf states have already moved toward strategic autonomy and self-reliance," Andreas Krieg, a senior lecturer at the School of Security Studies at King's College London, wrote for online outlet Middle East Eye this week. "A lame-duck Trump will speed that process."

That's even though a Democrat-led Congress might make the US a slightly more reliable partner again.

Krieg argues that Gulf states should come together to pressure the US not to attack Iran and then negotiate a non-aggression pact with Iran — without US involvement.

Whether that kind of unity is possible in the Gulf is dubious, Ryan Crocker, a former US ambassador to the region and distinguished fellow with MEI, replied to a question from DW during the MEI's online briefing.

"I'm afraid that meaningful Gulf unity is not coming to a theater near us anytime soon," Crocker said. The Qataris remain untrusting because of how other Gulf states acted against them in 2017. And, he added, "there are well known differences between Saudi Arabia and the UAE we see in Sudan [and] in Yemen, and, of course, the profound differences … on relations with Israel. So I would not expect any meaningful moves toward [Gulf] security cooperation."

What's more likely in the long term is Gulf states eventually coming to some kind of understanding with Iran.

"The Gulf states understand that they probably can't buy their security only from Washington, and that they're going to have to buy it, to some degree, by some kind of a relationship with Tehran," the MEI's Harrison added. "It's inevitable. Geography reigns."

What that sort of non-aggression pact looks like remains to be seen, he added. That's why one of the big concerns for Gulf leaders now, is the conflict escalating again to a point where the path back to some sort of diplomacy is lost, Harrison concluded.

The Bluetooth Group has announced a new standard. Here's how it works and when you can expect to benefit from it.

Senior Editor / Home Security and Smart Home

Senior Editor / Home Security and Smart Home

Wireless tech is about to get a whole lot snappier. On Thursday, the Bluetooth Special Interest Group, the trade association that oversees Bluetooth, officially unveiled Bluetooth High Data Throughput, a flagship upgrade set to arrive as part of the Bluetooth Core 7.0 standard. The revision supercharges Bluetooth Low Energy by pushing maximum bit rates to 7.5 Mbps, effectively quadrupling the throughput of the existing LE 2M technology. Compatible devices are slated to hit store shelves starting in 2027.

That’s good news for phone users, tablet havers, laptop groupies, smart speaker stans and more. Bluetooth is still the most popular medium-to-short-range wireless standard for connecting devices. But this is a big rollout, and the changes will start with premium wireless, such as earbuds, with other applications following.

Those other applications could include spatial computing, exchanging AI-related info with local devices, connecting to health wearables and a host of smart home applications, like playing Spotify through an Amazon Echo Studio or dimming a smart bulb.

“Imagine a smartwatch that syncs hours’ worth of sensor-rich training data to your phone in seconds, smart glasses that deliver richer AI experiences by offloading data for processing, or a smart home product that completes a firmware update with virtually no user-visible downtime,” Øyvind Strøm, executive vice president of the short-range business unit at Nordic Semiconductor, said in a statement. “Bluetooth HDT paves the way for more sophisticated, data-intensive Bluetooth applications.”

A representative from the Bluetooth Special Interest Group didn’t respond to a request for comment.

The biggest change in this update is a new physical layer for Bluetooth. Layers are like different sections of the Bluetooth protocol, and the physical layer is the bedrock of the standard, the foundational layer involved in transmitting and receiving data signals.

Improvements in the physical layer can have very significant effects on all Bluetooth communications. In this case, new modulation schemes let the physical layer handle data more efficiently and transmit more data at once, which is why the speed increases are so significant.

These improvements also make the new standard more efficient, saving battery life in many applications. So your smart lock may last longer before you need to exchange the AA batteries, earbuds won’t need to go back in the case as fast, and so on.

We know the first flagship devices are expected to appear with the technology in early 2027, but don’t have an exact date.

All we know about which specific devices will get the technology first is that it will start with wireless audio.

With protocol changes like this, it can take some time for new products to reach the market, and manufacturers may take a long time, even years, to adopt it.
