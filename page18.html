📅 2026-11-25  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

In a discussion on CNBC's Halftime Report Wednesday, the question came up: Why can't Federal Reserve Chairman Kevin Warsh hold the line on interest rates the way his icon Alan Greenspan did at the beginning of the dot-com era?

Greenspan famously leaned against inflation hawks on his committee in 1995 and 1996, holding the line on interest rates amid strong growth, because he believed in the productivity promise of the internet revolution.

It would be nice if Warsh could follow the same playbook amid the artificial intelligence build-out and keep rates steady until the productivity arrives. Unfortunately, a quick look at the numbers shows just how different the situation is for Warsh when it comes to inflation, the magnitude of the capital-investment boom and the monetary and fiscal stimulus boosting the economy.

Bottom line: the Fed looks like it has to hike — and maybe substantially — to be anywhere close to the position where the Fed was in Greenspan's time at the dawn of a major technological revolution.

It may be that the massive capex spending on AI will mean more productivity and less inflation down the road. But the front-end build-out is proving to be more inflationary, and more challenging, to a Fed already dealing with high inflation. In many ways, that's the opposite of what Greenspan had to handle.

Macy's on Wednesday announced it's postponing its Thanksgiving Day Parade, originally scheduled for Thursday, due to severe weather.

The parade, held each year on Thanksgiving morning, will now take place on Saturday at 8:30 a.m. ET, according to the company. The New York City area is expected to have heavy rain and thunderstorms on Thanksgiving.

"The safety of our participants and spectators is our top priority, and after close consultation with our city and agency partners, we have made the decision to move this year's celebration," Macy's said in a statement.

This year's event marks the 100th Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade. The annual event has never been canceled due to weather, though it was suspended between 1942 and 1944 for World War II.

The parade's viability this year had been in question after concerns arose over strong winds that could become dangerous for the massive balloons that the event is known for. Many of the balloons had already been inflated as of Wednesday afternoon.

The postponement also means thousands of volunteers and artists slated to perform will have to adapt to Saturday's schedule.

Nvidia's billion-dollar board bet: Who's protected as lawsuits mount?

📅 2026-11-25  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Jensen Huang is back as CEO of Nvidia, after President Donald Trump urged the company's board to rehire Huang. But investigations into the company, its executive leadership and its board continue as outside criticism mounts.

"Clearly they caved to external pressure," said Betsy Atkins, an author and longtime public-company director. "The board failed," she said, when it initially allowed Huang's removal. "The board's role is to think of all stakeholders."

California Attorney General Rob Bonta is investigating whether Nvidia misrepresented information to investors. "There is classic corporate securities law on the books in California that prevents investors and the public from being misled by companies," Bonta said on CNBC's "Squawk on the Street" on Tuesday.

Directors are already defendants in a shareholder lawsuit accusing them of failing to oversee Nvidia. A separate securities class action targets the company and Huang, its co-founder.

Now the board’s insurance choices are part of the story. A risk advisor with direct knowledge of Nvidia’s coverage says the company built an approximately $1 billion D&O tower, insurance jargon for directors and officers insurance policies from different companies that combine to cover one massive risk.

Nvidia "loves insurance," the advisor said. “They buy insurance as if they have no balance sheet.” A billion-dollar tower would essentially mean Nvidia "bought the market," purchasing all the coverage it could get, according to an industry expert with 25 years working in financial insurance. The expert asked not to be named out of concern for past or potential future clients.

But Nvidia directed all that coverage to Side A only, according to CNBC's source. Side A protects directors and officers personally when the company cannot indemnify them. Side B covers the company for legal defense costs or settlements on behalf of directors and officers, and Side C protects the company when it's named as a defendant in a suit such as a securities claim.

The risk advisor says Nvidia believed it had "the war chest" to protect itself against a securities class action suit.

That choice is raising questions about whether the board protected itself at the company’s expense. CNBC asked Nvidia for comment but received no response.

An insurance CEO characterized a billion-dollar policy as a lot of coverage, but said a Side A-only allocation was a surprising move because it results in imbalanced coverage, protecting only directors.

That structure is not automatically a governance failure, according to a D&O insurance expert with long experience in coverage for banks, drugmakers and other businesses that often face litigation. Side A-only programs are common among companies with the capital to manage the corporate risk internally.

"For a company with Nvidia’s resources, retaining the company-side risk can make financial sense," the advisor said. It's Side A D&O that's "imperative to corporate governance."

"It is a capital-allocation decision," the expert told me. The company decides that buying insurance for its own balance sheet is not worth the premium. In other words, Side B and Side C are considered too expensive for the coverage the company gets.

The question for shareholders is whether the board’s decision to hold that risk was a sound use of the company’s resources and whether the company can absorb the costs if the claims grow.

A shareholder derivative complaint filed Nov. 11 names Huang, directors and other officers. It alleges that they failed to oversee risks involving Huang’s relationship with Megaspeed executive Alice Huang and made misleading statements. It also alleges that some defendants sold roughly $2 billion in Nvidia stock while holding material nonpublic information. Those remain allegations, not findings.

A separate securities class action, filed Nov. 9, names Nvidia and Huang. It seeks damages for investors who bought Nvidia shares between Oct. 9, 2025, and Nov. 8, 2026, alleging the company and Huang misled investors.

The distinction matters: The derivative suit is brought on Nvidia’s behalf, while the class action seeks recovery for investors.

"Insurance is already at work," says Michel Léonard, chief economist at the Insurance Information Institute, describing standard policy mechanics, not Nvidia’s specific policy. "Once the suit is filed, D&O starts paying defense costs, long before anyone proves anything."

A claim still has to meet the policy’s terms, and defense costs can reduce the remaining limits. Nor does a drop in Nvidia’s market value by itself establish an insurable loss. Plaintiffs would need to prove legally recoverable damages; the policy would respond only to covered losses.

The board has already taken steps it says are aimed at accountability. Nvidia announced an independent investigation on Nov. 8, and independent directors retained outside counsel two days later. Huang resigned as CEO and a director on Nov. 11; the board appointed CFO Colette Kress interim CEO. Nvidia said it had no reason to believe national security or company secrets had been compromised by Huang's extramarital relationships.

Atkins, who joined the board of Wynn Resorts in 2018 after founder Steve Wynn resigned, argues that allegations are not conclusions and that boards should also weigh governance concerns against the value a founder brings to a company’s innovation. Nvidia's investigation is continuing even as Huang resumes leadership.

“Will companies’ hyper-accelerated innovation speed be the same without them? I don’t think so,” said Atkins, author of "Don't Waste a Board Seat: A Decision-Maker's Guide to Choosing Directors Who Create Value."

A Manhattan judge dismissed landlords’ challenge to the Rent Guidelines Board decision to freeze rents on 1-year and 2-year stabilized renewals, a move Mayor Zohran Mamdani publicly supported. The court found a rational basis in the board record while criticizing campaign promises and a draft shared with a mayoral aide.  AI Summary

On Thanksgiving eve, Judge Brendan Lantry dismissed a landlords’ challenge to the Rent Guidelines Board’s late June decision to freeze rents. The ruling upholds the city’s first-ever freeze on both one- and two-year leases.

With the national real estate industry watching, the rent freeze became a major test of what a mayor can do to force affordability.

Owners of rent-stabilized buildings argued that Mamdani stacked the board with loyalists after promising a freeze, making the outcome predetermined. They also accused the board of following a campaign promise instead of the data on rising landlord costs. Lantry rejected both claims.

The ruling comes three weeks after an appeals court revived the city’s pied-à-terre tax rollout on second homes valued at $5 million or higher. Mamdani posted on social media that the city has billed owners of those homes more than $500 million.

That lawsuit ran parallel to the rent freeze lawsuit landlords filed in late July. It originally was in a Staten Island court but was moved to Manhattan in August at the city’s request.

The rent freeze took effect Oct. 1 while the lawsuit was pending and covers leases on more than a million apartments. The board had frozen one-year leases in 2015, 2016 and 2020.

Because the board acts in a quasi-legislative role, its orders stand if they have a rational basis, and courts may not substitute their own judgment, Lantry wrote. He found that the board’s 42-page explanatory statement showed it considered each factor the Administrative Code requires, including the industry’s economic condition and the cost of living.

Lantry said appointees who share the mayor’s views did not show collusion. He noted that “elections have consequences.”

But Lantry did not let Mamdani completely off the hook.

“Regarding Mayor Mamdani’s campaign for Mayor of New York City, the court would be remiss not to state that such comments, promises, and guarantees to freeze the rent were reckless and irresponsible,” he wrote. “The Mayor of the City of New York has no authority to unilaterally raise, ‘freeze’ or otherwise adjust the rent of stabilized units in this City – that responsibility is within the power of the Board and the Board alone.”

He called an email in which the board’s chair shared a draft of the explanatory statement with a mayoral aide before the vote “troubling.” He said it cast a “dark shadow” over the board’s independence. Without more, he wrote, the email did not justify overturning the decision under Article 78, the state law that lets courts review government agency actions.

The judge noted that petitioners agreed to end court-ordered discovery, which produced about 1,000 documents, and never sought depositions.

Lantry acknowledged the freeze could make it harder for owners to refinance or renovate. But he wrote that the legal question is not whether the policy is wise.

Hong Kong: Fire Anniversary Underscores Growing Repression

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Click to expand Image A memorial near the site of the deadly fire at the residential complex at Wang Fuk Court in Tai Po, Hong Kong, December 2, 2025. © 2025 Ng Han Guan/AP Photo

(London) – The Hong Kong government should ensure meaningful accountability for the devastating fire that killed 168 people one year ago and end its suppression of peaceful criticism, Human Rights Watch said today.

On November 26, 2025, Hong Kong’s deadliest fire in decades engulfed seven residential high-rise buildings at Wang Fuk Court, Tai Po. The authorities initiated an independent investigation and arrested three dozen people implicated in crimes. The Housing Minister resigned. But the authorities have also threatened and arbitrarily arrested those engaging in criticism and activism concerning the fire.

“The Hong Kong government responded to the horrific Wang Fuk Court fire by using Beijing’s tactics of shutting out the voices of the victims and promising an elusive accountability,” said Elaine Pearson, Asia director at Human Rights Watch. “The authorities need to recognize that the only way to prevent similar tragedies in the future is to allow people to speak out safely and hold those responsible fully accountable.”

The authorities have engaged in apparent acts of repression of free expression in the weeks ahead of the anniversary:

The media reported in November that the police made inquiries with Catholic churches planning masses to commemorate victims of the fire.A church in Tai Po subsequently removed references to the fire from its announcement and instead described the service as a mass for “the brothers and sisters who died in the past year;” hundreds nevertheless attended.A priest at another Catholic church posted on Facebook that three police officers had inquired about a planned memorial mass marking the anniversary, even though the church had not publicly announced the service.On November 22, a group working with migrant workers who survived the fire cancelled a book launch and drama event scheduled ahead of the anniversary, citing “unforeseen circumstances.” On November 23, the online news platform Boom News said it would remove some videos featuring interviews with survivors, quoting the whistleblower Edward Snowden on “censorship and fear.” The Boom News founder, Tang Ho-wing, had been arrested in September for alleged “sedition” for reporting on another politically sensitive anniversary.

Police Commissioner Joe Chow said police routinely asked organizers about upcoming public events, while decisions on whether to proceed “rested with the organizers themselves.”

Amid continued restrictions on freedom of expression and peaceful assembly in Hong Kong, Hong Kong communities around the world have planned commemorations of the fire.

Deep concerns surrounding the 2026 anniversary follow a year-long government crackdown on criticism, reporting, and civil society-organizing related to the fire. Immediately following the fire, hundreds of people gathered near Wang Fuk Court to donate and distribute supplies to victims, but as the volunteer effort grew, police told them to leave and government workers took over the site.

Authorities targeted people calling for accountability. Just four days after the fire, national security police reportedly arrested a university student, Miles Kwan, for “seditious intent” after he initiated an online petition calling for an independent investigation into the fire, and two others on suspicion of “inciting hatred against the government.”

Hong Kong and Beijing authorities subsequently accused “foreign forces” and “anti-China destabilizing forces” of using the fire to “incite hatred against the government.” Within hours of these announcements, two prominent online commentators, Ellie Yuen and Hailey Cheng, who had been frequently quoted in foreign media, said they would stop reporting on the fire.

National security police also arrested the online commentator Wong On-yin in December after he planned to hold a news conference about the Wang Fuk Court fire and discussed on YouTube his subsequent questioning by police. The authorities prosecuted him for allegedly obstructing a national security investigation and publishing “seditious material.” He remains in custody awaiting a verdict in January 2027.

Between April and May 2026, the authorities barred Wang Fuk Court residents from displaying banners from their homes or being accompanied by journalists as they were briefly permitted to return to their apartments to retrieve belongings.

Also in May, the authorities arrested the former Wang Fuk Court owners’ committee member Kong Cheung-fat and his wife on allegations of fraud and money laundering. Kong had joined other owners in seeking a meeting to discuss insurance and other post-fire issues. While out on bail, he said he would no longer give interviews or comment publicly. Some residents said his arrest made them more cautious about speaking publicly.

An independent committee, set up by Hong Kong Chief Executive John Lee, published a report on October 29 that found wide-ranging regulatory failures across four government bodies, including inadequate oversight of renovation work and fire safety, weaknesses in building maintenance regulation, and supervisory failures by contractors and other professionals. That day, Lee and senior officials formally apologized to the victims of the fire and their families while promising to implement “systemic reforms.” Housing Secretary Winnie Ho later stepped down from office while the authorities arrested 35 people in connection with the fire and possible corruption in the renovation project.

“An apology and promises of reform are no substitute for ensuring that people can freely question and criticize the authorities,” Pearson said. “The Hong Kong government should act on the independent committee’s recommendations and provide adequate compensation and other support for victims and their families.”
