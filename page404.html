Netflix's spy thriller Black Doves wouldn't be half the show it is without its excellent cast, and season 2 wouldn't have been nearly as exciting without a few major newcomers. While Keira Knightley and Ben Whishaw lead the cast of Black Doves, they're hardly the only stars in the show. A wide range of Oscar, Emmy, and Olivier nominees and winners from every corner of the United Kingdom came together, and it's largely because of that ensemble cast that reviews for Black Doves have been so positive.

The cast of Black Doves has also only grown more important in season 2. As the show starts to expand the list of members of the Black Doves and the reach of the private espionage organization, as well as introduce a new shadowy organization named Kestrel, the world of the show is getting bigger by the minute. From new handlers to new members of the "psychotic girl gang" of assassins, the cast of Black Doves is hugely important.

The cast of the show could also easily influence how Black Doves season 3, if it is renewed, plays out. On top of it all, Black Doves is renowned for its complicated plots filled with intrigue, red herrings, mysteries, and plenty of complicated figures. Keeping track of all the characters, how they know one another, and who's who is supremely important to understanding one of the best shows on Netflix.

Oven-Roasted Turkey

📅 Nov 14, 2024  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The perfect Thanksgiving turkey is easier to make than you might think. Whether you're a first-timer planning a Friendsgiving dinner for your closest pals or the one whose house the whole family gathers at year after year, this recipe will help make your turkey the star of the dinner table. Let this be the year you ace Thanksgiving hosting and find your annual go-to guide for everything—roasting time, thawing, the secret to perfectly browned skin—on how to present the perfect roast bird.

Remove the giblets, trussing, and pop-up thermometers from the turkey. Pat the turkey completely dry inside and out with paper towels. If you plan to cut the wishbone out now, face the turkey neck opening toward you and locate the V-shaped wishbone in the neck cavity. With a sharp and flexible boning knife, using a short but firm swiping motion, gently cut the bone from the breast meat by making long incisions on both sides of the bone. Pry the bone loose with a paper towel or clean kitchen towel and discard. Using your fingers, separate the turkey breast from the skin.

In a spice grinder or mortar and pestle, grind all of the spices until they're finely ground and well combined. This will allow the spices to adhere to the surface of the turkey, especially the dried thyme.

Cover the entire turkey with the seasoning mix, turning the turkey over to season the back as well. Be sure to season under the breast skin and in the cavity. Transfer the turkey to a roasting rack. Refrigerate, uncovered, until cold, at least 12 hours and up to 2 days.

Arrange a rack in the lower third of the oven with no rack above it; preheat to 450°. Gently pat the turkey with paper towels to remove any excess moisture. Place the shallot, lemon, garlic, fresh thyme, and sage in the turkey cavity and neck opening. Be sure not to overfill the turkey. Err on the side of not stuffing everything in if it doesn’t fit. The turkey cavity needs airflow to cook evenly, and blocking that airflow will result in a longer cook time and a drier turkey. Drizzle the turkey with oil. Insert a leave-in probe thermometer (if using) into the turkey thigh or breast and transfer the roasting pan to the oven.

Roast the turkey for 30 minutes. Reduce the oven temperature to 350°. Continue to roast until the temperature in the thickest part of the breast registers 155° and the thigh registers 165°, about 1 hour and 30 minutes more, up to 2 hours more, rotating once during cooking to help the skin brown evenly. Let the turkey rest, uncovered, for 45 minutes before carving.

The current Fleet Admiral of the Navy, Sakazuki, is as twisted as he is powerful. His devil fruit gives him the scary power of becoming lava. Although he was no match for an angry Whitebeard, he dealt serious damage to him and contributed to his death.

Given that his devil fruit power is heat-based, Akainu might also be Luffy's ultimate enemy, with his magma able to seriously wound the Straw Hat captain no matter how far he stretches.

Homemade Mac &amp; Cheese

The perfect mac & cheese will have you dreaming about its cheesy goodness for days after you eat it. Obviously, the cheese has to be top-notch, but the real secret to the best version is the perfect crunchy topping. My recipe delivers on all of that and more, so you won't even think about reaching for another mac & cheese recipe for the rest of your gatherings. From weeknight dinners to potlucks and holidays, this is the classic side dish everyone will be reaching for.

First, you’ll boil the pasta. Start with a big pot of salted water (and I mean super salty, it should taste like the ocean), then add your pasta and cook it until before al dente—I’m talking still-has-a-ring-of-hard-white-pasta-in-the-middle-when-you-bite-in uncooked. You want it to meld and absorb with your cheese sauce in the oven, not turn into mush. Undercooking now guarantees the perfect bite when you pull it out of the oven.

Then it’s cheese sauce time, and that means making a béchamel. You’ll start by melting your butter in a pan over medium heat, then sprinkling the flour over the top and stirring everything together until it starts pulling away from the pan slightly. You’re looking for what’s called a “blonde roux,” which has a slightly yellow tint and should smell a bit like baked goods. You don’t want it super dark (so keep an eye on the heat!), but you definitely don’t want it as light as it started.

Next, add the milk in slowly, stirring constantly so you don’t get thick lumps. Want to shortcut this process a bit? Heat your milk ahead of time to room temperature or even slightly warm. You’re going to cook it until it’s thick and bubbly (about 2 minutes), so having your milk warm will help you get there faster. It’s going to get a LOT thicker when you add the cheese, so if it’s still a little thin but you want to get on with it, that’s okay too.

Once you’re ready to add the cheese, it’s very important to take it off the heat before so that the sauce doesn’t start to separate. That said, if you’ve been at it for a minute and are still having trouble getting your cheese to melt, pop your pot back on the heat for just a few seconds to bring the temperature up and help the process along.

Once it comes together, you can stir in the macaroni until every noodle is coated.

At this point, you could eat this mac & cheese right now, and it would taste wonderful. But! If you want exceptional mac & cheese, you’ve gotta bake it. So pour it into a pan, then get ready for the best part—the topping. To make it, you’ll combine some breadcrumbs and the remaining half cup of Parmesan in a bowl, then add the olive oil and mix it all up. Sprinkle it on top of the mac and cheese in the casserole dish.

All you have to do is pop it into your preheated 375° oven and bake it until it's golden brown on top. Go ahead, snag a bite before it's fully cooled—you'll really be able to taste all your extra effort.

When ready to serve, add the Parmesan and panko mixture, bake, and serve. If baking from frozen, allow to thaw in the refrigerator overnight before baking.

If you have any leftovers, store them in an airtight container in the fridge for around 3 days.
