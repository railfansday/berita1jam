Arsenal are keeping a close eye on Middlesbrough striker Will Lankshear as Mikel Arteta continues to assess potential additions to his attacking options.

The 21-year-old has been one of the Championship’s standout performers since leaving Tottenham in the summer, scoring at a remarkable rate for a Middlesbrough side pushing for promotion.

His form has now attracted attention from several Premier League clubs, although Boro have little intention of weakening their squad midway through the campaign.

According to TEAMtalk, Arsenal are among a growing list of clubs monitoring Lankshear after his superb start at the Riverside Stadium.

The England Under-21 international has scored 15 goals in 16 appearances across all competitions, including 13 goals in 13 Championship outings. Manchester United, Brighton, Everton, Newcastle and Borussia Dortmund have also watched him, while former club Tottenham retain matching rights on any future offer.

Crucially, Middlesbrough have decided they will not entertain a January sale. Lankshear only joined for a deal worth around £20million and is considered central to their push for Premier League promotion.

A related CaughtOffside report confirms Arsenal’s interest and notes that Lankshear is happy at Middlesbrough despite the increasing attention surrounding his future.

Lankshear is an intriguing target because Arsenal already know him well. He began his youth career with the Gunners before eventually developing through Sheffield United and Tottenham.

His goalscoring record suggests he has taken a major step forward since leaving Spurs. At 21, he also fits Arsenal’s preference for signing players who can contribute immediately while still having significant room to develop.

There is an obvious question over whether Championship form would translate instantly to a title-chasing Premier League side, particularly when competition for minutes would be much stronger at the Emirates.

That is why Arsenal should probably monitor rather than force the issue in January.

Middlesbrough are top of the Championship and have no financial or sporting incentive to sell their leading scorer halfway through a promotion campaign. CaughtOffside also reports that a recent hamstring issue is not considered particularly serious.

If Lankshear keeps scoring and Boro eventually become open to negotiations, however, Arsenal should remain involved. A young, homegrown striker with his penalty-box instincts could become a very useful long-term addition to Arteta’s squad.

Homophobia on the rise in football - what is your club doing about it?

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Rainbow laces were a common sight for several years across football and other sports

Warning: this article contains offensive language

Homophobia and transphobia are on the rise in English football, with new data revealing record numbers of discrimination reports in the game - so what is the Premier League doing about it?

Last season, with little fanfare, the league introduced a new LGBTQ+ celebratory activation in February, Premier League With Pride.

The lowkey launch contrasts with the landscape of a few years ago, when England internationals like Jordan Henderson and Conor Coady were proudly proclaiming their allyship.

"The one thing it [Premier League With Pride last season] was lacking was engagement from players," said Jon Holmes, founder and lead of news organisation and campaign group Sports Media LGBT.

"Not only would you have the rainbow armbands, but clearer allyship from players - that was more around the late 2010s. That has been in decline."

A report last month from anti-discrimination charity Kick It Out says they received a new high of 1,744 discrimination reports during the 2025-26 season, a 25% increase on the previous campaign. In this, reports of homophobia rose 46% to 203.

Kick It Out said it had received 142 reports of discriminatory mass chanting during the season, a 41% increase on 2024-25 and the highest number recorded. These reports included increases in homophobic and transphobic chants.

A prime example is the 'rent boy' chant predominantly aimed at Chelsea players and fans, for which incidents had dropped since it was criminalised in 2022. However, the combined 24 reports of homophobic chanting in 2025-26 season were the most in three years.

According to Kick It Out,, external many of the incidents over the past few seasons can be linked to domestic and world events, such as the Supreme Court ruling on gender leading to the FA's ban on transgender women in the women's game, which it says led to a spike in transphobic abuse.

The first Premier League With Pride event included rainbow branding at grounds - but nothing involving players

BBC Sport asked all 20 Premier League clubs what diversity policies they have regarding the LGBTQ+ community, and how much of their budget is dedicated to such schemes.

Nine clubs responded to the BBC request. None said how much money they invest in these areas.

A Nottingham Forest spokesperson told BBC Sport: "The club commits significant funding to this important area, and has an Equality, Diversity and Inclusion lead whose role is dedicated to ensuring inclusion is at the heart of everything we do."

Some clubs do visibly engage with their local LGBTQ+ community. Leeds United have been a sponsor of the city's Pride celebrations since 2018. Luke Ayling, then Leeds captain, marched in the Pride parade in 2022.

Brighton are annually represented in their city's extensive Pride events, as are the Manchester clubs.

Manchester City host an annual Progress flag-raising ceremony, while Manchester United said staff marched in the city centre parade. They also ran a flag competition with LGBTQ+ supporters' club Rainbow Devils, with the winning design displayed at Old Trafford for all matchdays.

Tottenham said that at the start of 2024, Spurs in the Community helped launch a weekly football session for members of the LGBTQ+ community.

And for the first time this season, all 20 current Premier League clubs have dedicated fan groups for LGBTQ+ supporters.

"It feels like there is a renaissance in the LGBT fan movement," said Holmes. "There is a rise in homophobia, but it does seem like the authorities are sitting up and taking notice. We can feel a degree of optimism."

But it is difficult for Premier League teams to encourage LGBTQ+ allyship and diversity, when representation within clubs is a mixed bag at best.

According to Kick It Out, which gathered data on the senior management teams from 17 out of the 20 current Premier League clubs, just 0.3% of people in those roles openly identify as LGBTQ+ - well below the UK population average according to latest national census data, with 4% of the British population identifying as LGBTQ+.

At a senior coaching level, from 11 clubs which responded there is no-one from the LGBTQ+ community.

Kick It Out says, comparing the dataset to relevant census data, that if true diversity in football's workforce were to be achieved, over 100 more people from the LGBTQ+ community would have to be employed at Premier League clubs.

The Premier League previously had a high profile association with LGBTQ+ charity Stonewall's Rainbow Laces campaign - but this was ended before the 2025-26 season

Rainbow Laces started in 2014-15 as a collaboration between Stonewall, Paddy Power and the Premier League. For two game weeks, usually around the end of November, players would wear rainbow-coloured laces or armbands, and the colourful branding would be seen in grounds and on broadcast idents.

Holmes said by the end it was doing more harm than good.

"I think the novelty of Rainbow Laces wore off," he told BBC Sport. "It also became more newsworthy to talk about the controversies; it became more difficult for players to engage without difficult questions.

"Also, club media departments are more risk averse, given growing toxicity on social media.

"It has been misconstrued that players were forced to engage, I think it was because Rainbow Laces became de rigueur, and it was perceived that captains would need to lead with the armband and allyship."

Rainbow armbands were introduced in 2017 for captains, having first been worn by MLS club Orlando City following the mass shooting at Pulse, an LGBTQ+ nightclub, in the city in 2016.

But the Premier League held its final Rainbow Laces campaign from 29 November to 5 December, 2024, and it was surrounded by controversy.

Marc Guehi, then Crystal Palace captain, wrote 'I Love Jesus' on his armband in what was widely viewed as a protest against wearing the armband (which was not mandatory).

Ipswich captain Sam Morsy refused to wear the armband, while Manchester United scrapped plans for a Rainbow Laces-themed jacket during the pre-match ceremonies following an objection by Noussair Mazraoui.

Simon Blake, CEO of Stonewall, admitted to BBC Sport that by the end of its 12-year lifespan in the Premier League, Rainbow Laces in that form had run its course.

"When it started, getting a few people to wear the laces was success," he said. "When it ended, when a few people didn't wear them there was criticism.

"If a campaign no longer has a positive vibe, perhaps it is time to do something different.

"Everything must evolve and change, but visibility still matters, it is important not to throw the baby out with the bathwater."

Stonewall held its first ever Rainbow Laces Day on 8 October this year, to encourage LGBTQ+ people to engage with sport. It had no connection with the Premier League.

Marc Guehi and Crystal Palace did not face any action after he wrote on his rainbow armband

It is notable that the players most ardently against Rainbow Laces were also vocal in their religious beliefs.

Guehi has spoken of the importance in his life of his faith and was one of several devout Christian players in the Crystal Palace dressing room. Both Morsy and Mazraoui are practicing Muslims.

Both Holmes and Blake acknowledge that this may have had an impact on Rainbow Laces.

"The influence of religion is clearly quite significant, but I think more understanding is coming in too," said Holmes.

"We have seen many parts of life less willing to engage with Pride, and football reflects that. The nature of LGBT campaigns is peaks and troughs, and we are in a trough."

"We don't have any specific data about what people's beliefs and views are, but we are in a world where people have different views, even within religions themselves," added Blake.

"The important thing is about behaviours, and we must learn to live with people who have these different beliefs and respect them.

"I don't want to speculate, as it would be too easy to assume people of faith would not be inclusive, I know from my work across 30 years that is not true; I have worked with many religious people who are LGBTQ+ supportive. Freedom of belief is important."

The current picture is a difficult one for gay and trans people in football at all levels.

The grassroots game is where the bulk of the increase in abuse has occurred, says Kick It Out, which claims it received 140% more reports last year than in the 2024-25 season. Three quarters of these reports are from senior grassroots games, where the players are over 18.

Away from football, research by Stonewall published in October found 11% of LGBTQ+ people in the UK avoid the gym, exercise classes or sports groups for fear of discrimination. Among trans people, the figure rises to 29%.

It said 15% of LGBTQ+ people who had been to a live sporting event, or watched sport in a bar, in the past year had experienced discrimination there. This rose to 26% for trans people.

"What we know is that there is a lot of work going on around equality and diversity; we also know homophobia and transphobia continue to be rife," Blake said.

"We are in a world, whether at Premier League or grassroots football, where views that may have been perceived to be unacceptable are now being expressed freely. That influences peoples' relationship with sport."

Premier League With Pride will return in 2026-27.

"The matchday activations take place in February in line with LGBTQ+ History Month, when the league and clubs will showcase their commitment to LGBTQ+ inclusion," a Premier League spokesperson told BBC Sport.

"There will be two match rounds dedicated to Premier League With Pride. This means all 20 clubs have a home game during that period, providing an opportunity to highlight and celebrate the LGBTQ+ community in football and showcase the work undertaken to make the game more inclusive.

"There is no centralised activation lined up for November."

One transfer insider has claimed that Liverpool may be ‘quite optimistic’ over agreeing a new contract for a high-profile first-team player.

Since turning 18 in August, Rio Ngumoha has been eligible for a longer-term deal at Anfield and, with it, a significant wage increase; and Fabrizio Romano indicated in October that ‘active conversations’ are ongoing over a prospective extension for the teenage winger.

Lewis Steele hinted that a new deal for the youngster should be a matter of when and not if, and the player himself said in an interview last week that it was a ‘no-brainer’ to swap Chelsea’s academy for the Reds two years ago.

Speaking on the Transfer Insider podcast for Football Insider, Pete O’Rourke also talked up the probability of Ngumoha signing a new contract at Liverpool, with both parties likely to want that conclusion.

The journalist said: “Ngumoha is happy at Liverpool. Liverpool are very happy with him. He came out in an interview earlier this week saying that his move to Liverpool was a no-brainer, that he was happy that he made that decision to leave Chelsea for Liverpool.

“I would imagine there’s a willingness from all parties to get a new contract done, so I’d imagine Liverpool are quite optimistic that they will get this sorted.”

Was the Seahawks’ bizarre play really a penalty? Here’s what the NFL rulebook says

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

When the Las Vegas Raiders kicked the ball off to the Seattle Seahawks with a tick over two minutes left in the fourth quarter, the idea was to kick the ball through the back of the end zone so that they could preserve the two-minute warning and effectively have three timeouts remaining.

After a long conversation with Klint Kubiak and head referee Bill Vinovich, an announcement was made after the two-minute warning that the Raiders had declined the penalty. What penalty, you may ask? Illegal bat, which the Seahawks know a thing or two about from way back in the day…

Rules analyst Gene Steratore said on the CBS broadcast that "I don't think it's a foul. It's a kick that never hits the ground. I believe you can bat a kick in the air provided that it has never hit the ground yet […] it's a first for me though, fellas."

Well if the veteran official can't commit to a ruling, maybe the rulebook provides some clarity.

Based on Rule 12, Section 5, Article 1 of the current NFL rulebook, Horton illegally batted the ball. What I've highlighted is the most important part of the rule:

No doubt that Horton "batted" the ball to keep it in play and it was in the direction of the Raiders end zone. And for those wondering about the "loose ball" rule… a kickoff in the air is still considered a loose ball.

So that's an open-and-shut case to me. Horton batted the ball forward and it didn't matter that the ball hadn't hit the ground yet. However, I want to shout out this comment we received from Leonson1 that made me think a little bit more.

Notice that the rule that Horton (presumably) violated was "loose ball in the field of play." By the NFL's definition, the "field of play" is only from goal line to goal line. The end zone is NOT part of the field of play, which is why certain plays are governed differently in the end zone than anywhere else on the field.

Horton batted the ball in his own end zone aka not the "field of play." So does the bat occurring in the end zone therefore negate the illegality? Is a loose ball in flight technically in the "field of play" because it never hit the ground? It can easily be interpreted that Horton batting the ball is legal because it occurred outside the field of play. Perhaps a loose ball in flight is officiated differently in terms of "field of play," in which case maybe it was a penalty.

This is such a rare, perhaps never before seen play that it's actually understandable that this is not something that can be so quickly and accurately adjudicated. I suspect the competition committee may look at some clarifying language next offseason.

The Raiders were never getting the time back on the clock because of the penalty, which is why it was declined. The Sahawks accomplished their mission of taking away the two-minute stoppage. Seattle was backed up by five yards but it didn't matter one bit. If you can fault the Raiders for anything, it's not booting the ball out of bounds along the sidelines, because it's not like starting at the 40 vs. 35 is all that big a deal.

Ultimately, what Horton did was fantastic and making sure that Rashid Shaheed picked the ball up and got out of the end zone (which is when the clock is supposed to start) was also great thinking. No one else on the field is allowed to move until the ball is touched, so I doubt the Raiders would've hauled ass from the Seattle 40 to somehow get to the loose ball before Shaheed did. It was not as a risky as it was made out to be, and the Seahawks were able to get to victory formation in part because of Horton's heroic heads-up moment.

8 Standout Moments From the Governors Awards: Glenn Close Finally Gets Her Oscar, Diane Warren Pitches Olivia Wilde a Christmas Song and More

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

The Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences presented honorary Oscars to Glenn Close, Ridley Scott and Floyd Norman at the 17th Governors Awards on Sunday night at the Ray Dolby Ballroom at Ovation Hollywood. Killer Films co-founders Christine Vachon and Pamela Koffler received the Irving G. Thalberg Memorial Award, becoming the first all-female producing team to take home the honor.

Mahershala Ali, John Malkovich, Matt Damon, Todd Haynes and Julianne Moore served as presenters, while Tom Cruise, Pedro Pascal, Olivia Wilde, Diane Warren and Andrew Garfield were among those working a room packed with legends and Oscar hopefuls. Related Stories 'Adults' Renewed for Season 3 at FX

Lynette Howell Taylor, AMPAS President, set the tone, praising the honorees for “the years of determination behind them, the risks that didn’t pay off, the doors that didn’t open.” Then she asked the crowd how many times in the last year they had found themselves referencing “Terminator 2.”

Here are eight moments that stood out during the evening.

Glenn Close Finally Gets Her Oscar and Reframes Eight Losses

Close arrived as the night’s sentimental center. Her eight nominations, from “The World According to Garp” (1982) to “Hillbilly Elegy” (2020), tie her with Peter O’Toole for the most acting nods without a win. The cruelest came for “The Wife” (2018), when she swept the Golden Globe, SAG and Critics Choice Awards, then lost to Olivia Colman for “The Favourite.” When Malkovich finally handed her the statuette, the room erupted.

Her speech was touching and full of gratitude. Close revealed she spent the ages of 7 to 22 “under the thumb of a restrictive cult,” escaping into Disney films before enrolling as a 22-year-old freshman at the College of William & Mary, where she was cast as Olivia in “Twelfth Night.” “It was like water on the desert,” she said.

Then she addressed the losses head-on. “I have always felt that the great honor is to be in the room,” Close said. “Over the past 43 years, I have been in the room eight times.”

She thanked her longtime agent Kevin Huvane, who once sent flowers to her Village apartment at 10 p.m. with a note that read, “Don’t let the motherfuckers get you down” and answered Howell Taylor’s “Terminator 2” quip with a mission statement. “We are not machines,” Close said. “We are flawed. Our imperfections are our salvation.”

She closed with her daughter, Annie, who told her at age 3, “I want you. I want all of you.” Said Close: “The pain of leaving is always relieved by the joy of coming home.”

Diane Warren Pitches Olivia Wilde a Christmas Song

Navigating the dinner hour can be tricky, but not for someone who’s done it a more than 20 times. As the contenders started walking into the room, I found myself chatting with “The Invite” star and director Olivia Wilde, who was flying back out to London tonight to finish shooting her film “Naughty,” which stars Jennifer Aniston, Peter Dinklage and Regina Hall.

In the middle of it all, Diane Warren, herself a 2022 honorary Oscar recipient, walked up and introduced herself, but of course, Wilde knew who she was. After they embraced, Warren asked what she was working on and she said she was shooting a Christmas film. Warren took her shot and asked whether she could write a Christmas song for the holiday movie. Wilde said, “Absolutely,” but I still don’t know if I witnessed just talk or if a meeting got made.

If the Governors Awards is the unofficial start of Oscar campaigning, this year’s floor made the case. Holland and Zendaya arrived together, and Holland stopped to say hello to Timothée Chalamet and Kylie Jenner. Cruise (“Digger”) caught up with “Being Heumann” director Siân Heder, while “Obsession” director Curry Barker was introduced to Christopher Nolan, the DGA president, whose “The Odyssey” is one of the season’s heavyweights.

“The Odyssey” star John Leguizamo crossed paths with “Michael” breakout Jaafar Jackson, and Andrew Garfield (“Artificial”) mingled his way through the crowd, alongside Monica Barbaro, Yura Borisov and director Luca Guadagnino.

John Malkovich Ranks Close’s Greatest On-Screen Deaths

Close’s “Dangerous Liaisons” co-star opened his tribute with a theory. “Personally, I think the mark of a great actor is how spectacularly they die on screen,” Malkovich said. By that measure, Close is in a league of her own, from a falling chandelier in “Mars Attacks!” (“extra points because the chandelier was once owned by Nancy Reagan”) to the bathtub finale of “Fatal Attraction.”

He also had the line of the night about her range. Of her dishwashing scene in “The Big Chill,” the kind of moment that would anchor most actors’ highlight reels, he cracked, “For her, it’s a Tuesday.” And of her turn in “The Wife,” he said, “She roars like the fucking lion she is.”

Two-time Oscar winner Mahershala Ali (“Your Mother Your Mother Your Mother”) presented the night’s first award to Norman, 91, who joined Disney in 1956 and is widely recognized as the studio’s first Black animator. “In his role as animator and story artist, Floyd was not eligible to win an Oscar for his work on these individual films,” Ali said. “But tonight, he is receiving one.”

Norman started as an in-betweener on “Sleeping Beauty” before Walt Disney himself spotted his gag drawings and moved him to the story team on “The Jungle Book.” “Lucky for me, I didn’t make Walt Disney angry,” Norman said. “Apparently, I made him laugh.”

His tribute package delivered a perfect Hollywood twist. When Norman was brought in to help a struggling “Monsters, Inc.,” the first-time director was Pete Docter, the son of Norman’s high school friend. “So I was literally working for my friend’s son,” Norman said.

He then delivered the evening’s best kicker, recalling a critic who panned a documentary about him because his life lacked drama. “I can only remind that critic that on occasion, even movies have happy endings,” he said. “And this special evening, here’s my happy ending.”

All five of his children were in the room to see it.

Ridley Scott Accepts for His Brother Tony

Matt Damon presented Scott’s honorary Oscar with a mock grudge, rattling off “Gladiator,” “Black Hawk Down” and “Alien: Covenant” as Scott films “that he did not cast me in, even though I was totally available.” He then recalled walking into his first meeting with Scott planning to pass on “The Martian,” fresh off playing a stranded astronaut in “Interstellar.” Scott answered in five words: “No one gives a shit.”

“It is about time we all saw him with an Academy Award in his hand,” Damon said of the four-time nominee, who has never won competitively.

Scott, 88, accepted remotely and skipped the greatest hits. His remarks opened on the last shot of his first film, “Boy and Bicycle,” made for 65 pounds in 1962 and starring his 15-year-old brother, Tony. “Both of us sharing our first film together,” Scott said. “It seems just like yesterday.”

He closed by accepting the honor on behalf of his brother, who died in 2012. “His great work would certainly have earned him this recognition.”

Before the Thalberg, Haynes and Moore delivered a surprise. Neil Patrick Harris, one of Broadway’s Tony-winning Hedwigs, performed “The Origin of Love” from “Hedwig and the Angry Inch,” one of the many films Vachon and Koffler helped bring to the screen.

Haynes followed with the story of meeting Vachon in college, when she was working the campus grill in a T-shirt spattered with grease and egg yolk. When a prankster handed her orange juice instead of an egg order, she tossed it on the grill. “Black smoke everywhere. ‘Fuck you, grill’s closed,’” Haynes recalled. “She was terrifying. She was awesome.”

Moore offered the budget-conscious version of the Killer Films legend. “Still Alice,” which won her the Oscar, was made so cheaply that the production couldn’t shoot indoors past 5 p.m. because it “couldn’t afford a bigger light kit.”

The S&P 500 Looks Cheaper Than It Has in Over 3 Years, but There’s a Catch

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Earnings expectations are rising faster than stock prices this year.

Despite some bumps in the road, the last three years have been fantastic for stock investors. The S&P 500 (^GSPC -0.40%) index is up about 80% since the start of November 2023, fueled by the big tech stocks at the forefront of the artificial intelligence (AI) industry. Despite the surge in stock prices, the index looks just as cheap by one measure as it was over three years ago.

The S&P 500 saw its forward P/E ratio fall to 17.7 in the postmidterm rout on Nov. 5. That now sits well below the five- and 10-year average forward P/E ratios for the index (19.8 and 19.1, respectively), and it's the lowest level the metric has reached since Nov. 1, 2023, according to data and analytics company FactSet.

Stocks have recovered a bit from the recent sell-off, and their valuations remain relatively attractive compared to where they've traded in recent years. Before investors expect another 80% climb in stock prices over the next three years, though, there's a big catch they should be aware of.

The forward P/E is a measure made with imperfect information. While we can know the price of stocks (P) with 100% certainty, the earnings (E) half of the equation is based on analysts' best guesses. When those guesses change as we gain new information, the P/E ratio can change significantly. For example, the S&P 500's forward P/E ratio fell from a relatively high 22 at the start of the year to less than 18 today, even as the index climbed.

2026 earnings came in well above expectations at the start of the year, prompting analysts to raise their outlooks for future earnings growth. Actual earnings between the third quarter of last year and the second quarter of this year exceeded analyst expectations by an average of 13.8%. For the third quarter, companies have reported earnings about 2.7% above estimates, which is less than the usual earnings surprise. But analysts already have high expectations of about 30% earnings growth.

Those high expectations carry into the fourth quarter, when analysts expect 28.4% earnings growth, and slow down slightly in the first quarter of next year to 19.5%. Second-quarter forecasts fell to just 3% earnings growth, as some big companies face tough comparables due to one-time investment gains in 2026, but analysts expect 16.1% full-year earnings growth for the S&P 500 in 2027.

Analysts have raised their growth expectations considerably from the start of the year, pushing the forward P/E ratio lower. The big catch, of course, is that future earnings expectations are far from knowable. Even after the stellar earnings results of the last few quarters, the S&P 500's trailing P/E (using actual financial results) of around 25 remains above the five- and 10-year averages (24.4 and 23.7, respectively).

While it's impossible to know if earnings growth can match the torrid pace analysts currently expect, stock investors earn higher returns over the long run because they're willing to accept that risk.

Some argue that analysts' earnings expectations still underappreciate the massive growth opportunity for artificial intelligence. With big tech companies piling growing amounts of cash into their data center build-outs, further revenue growth acceleration could be on the horizon as income-producing assets come online.

On the other side of the coin, some see significant hurdles for further growth among the AI stocks, with capital markets getting tighter and increased pressure from regulators. That could slow down the build-out and bring revenue growth to a halt.

While big AI stocks have the biggest impact on the S&P 500's overall earnings expectations, there are hundreds of companies that are mostly unaffected by the artificial intelligence build-out. Investors willing to dig into individual stocks and their earnings outlooks could find underappreciated opportunities as everyone focuses on AI.

Most importantly, with stock prices relatively low compared to earnings expectations, there's room for error. Stocks still look attractive after an 80% climb since they were last valued this low, but it's important to note that earnings growth expectations are higher now than they were three years ago. That leaves more room to disappoint.

Four volunteers spent 378 days inside a windowless NASA Mars habitat

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Four NASA CHAPEA volunteers left a 3D-printed Mars habitat in Houston on Oct. 31, 2026, after 378 days.

Ross Elder, Ellen Ellis, James Spicer and Matthew Montgomery walked out of NASA’s Mars habitat in Houston on Saturday, Oct. 31, 2026. They had gone in on Oct. 19, 2025, and stayed 378 days with no windows and no live calls home.

Ellis, the crew’s medical officer, later told NASA that on most days she did not feel isolated. She said she relied on her three teammates every day to get through the mission.

The year inside was CHAPEA Mission 2, NASA’s second yearlong simulation of life on the surface of Mars. NASA posted the crew’s reflections on Nov. 9. The health data are still to be interpreted in two weeks of post-mission evaluations.

The habitat is called Mars Dune Alpha. It was 3D-printed, covers roughly 1,700 square feet (158 square meters) and sits inside a building at Johnson Space Center.

On entry day in October 2025, the crew rode to the habitat in the Space Exploration Vehicle, a prototype pressurized rover, according to NASA’s Human Research Program.

Suzanne Bell, the mission’s co-principal investigator, spoke to them at the door. Two alternate crew members posed with them for photos before the start.

CHAPEA is short for Crew Health and Performance Exploration Analog. The first mission used the same habitat and ended on July 6, 2024.

Before the second crew went in, the plans for a second yearlong rehearsal in the printed habitat put crew autonomy at the center.

NASA astronaut Jack Hathaway spoke about the time they were away. While they were inside, he flew a 237-day mission on the International Space Station and came back. The Artemis II crew also traveled around the Moon in that period and set a distance record for humans.

Hathaway said the year required “immense physiological and psychological strength, teamwork, and human grit.”

Messages between the habitat and mission control took up to 22 minutes each way. The crew spoke with their families only by email, with a Mars-like delay, NASA said in a one-year update on Oct. 19.

On May 7 the crew reached day 200. That week they were in a two-week loss of signal, a planned blackout like the one when Mars passes behind the Sun, according to a NASA update from May. They worked with no contact with mission control and used preplanned procedures.

The crew also had to handle simulated equipment failures and limited supplies. Michael Stenger, chief scientist of the Human Research Program, said the mission was starting to show NASA what year-long missions need, including medical and psychological care across communication delays.

Elder was the commander, Ellis the medical officer, Montgomery the science officer and Spicer the flight engineer. As part of their personal item allowance, the four brought musical instruments into the habitat.

Elder is a former U.S. Air Force experimental test pilot. According to NASA, he found the Marswalks and robotic work very similar to working as part of a flight.

For a Marswalk, the crew put on suits, went through an airlock and walked on red sand outside the habitat. That area is still inside the building. From inside, crew members also ran a drone and a rover to survey the ground and find or collect rock samples.

Elder said that getting suits ready, doing airlock procedures and making long traverses took time and stamina. He said food, exercise and rest were needed to keep up that pace.

Montgomery helped identify the rocks brought back from the walks. One NASA photo shows Ellis and Elder doing a maintenance task outside during a simulated walk. Another shows photos the crew placed around a boot print in the sand.

Spicer worked in satellite communications and spacecraft development before the mission. At the exit, he was thanked for keeping the habitat’s systems running and was called the crew’s resident data scientist.

He compared the year to living on a submarine or a boat. The crew counted supplies from dish soap to respirator filters, and they rationed food and water.

Spicer said he was surprised by how little he missed the news and social media, even though the crew could get news. He told his crewmates they made the year “not a chore, but a joy.”

Montgomery had worked in hardware engineering, robotics, tech startups and independent films. He set up the habitat’s 3D printing capabilities and ran a seed germination test with simulated Martian soil and a control.

He also grew vegetables in a hydroponic system. NASA has run other work on growing lettuce, peppers and tomatoes in microgravity, because packaged food can lose flavor and vitamins.

Ellis is a U.S. Space Force officer and has worked at the National Reconnaissance Office. Inside the habitat she helped organize biological sample collections and prepare supplies for the health studies.

She said it was hard to explain to other people what life inside was like. “It would be difficult to convince someone who has never lived in isolation that most days, I don’t feel isolated,” she said.

Ellis thanked the exercise, behavioral health, nutrition and medical specialists on the ground. She said the main lesson for her was that a Mars crew will need a closely integrated ground team.

Isolation has also been studied at Concordia Station in Antarctica, where researchers linked living in close quarters to more conflict and mistrust.

The crew started two weeks of tests and debriefs with NASA scientists after the exit. Scientists will compare the data with what the crew reported. Mission 2’s results are still to come, and each crew has only four people.

Grace Douglas, the principal investigator, said the results will help NASA weigh health risks against crew time, communication capacity and food and water systems. She also said many of the resource problems in space are like problems on Earth.

Sara Whiting, the project scientist, said the data will feed into plans for NASA’s Moon Base near the lunar South Pole. “This is really the now moment for this kind of data,” she said.

Forget Robinson: Pochettino Plots Move For 'Perfect' USA Star In Man Utd Rebuild

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Mauricio Pochettino has identified Weston McKennie as a top target to strengthen his new-look Manchester United squad during the January transfer window. Pochettino is keen to reunite with the Juventus midfielder at Old Trafford after forming a strong relationship in the United States national team set-up.

Pochettino, 54, was appointed as United’s new head coach on Friday following the departure of Michael Carrick. The Argentine has been tasked with turning the club’s fortunes around after a difficult start to the season. Indeed, the Reds sit inside the Premier League relegation zone with just ONE win in 10 matches this season.

Although United still have a large portion of matches to still go until the turn of the year, Pochettino will have one eye on January, as he tries to strengthen his squad. The Reds spent more than £150million in the summer, but went and signed three midfielders, rather than looking to fix other areas, including the back-line.

But it appears that Pochettino may have not even been satisfied with the work that was done on the midfield. Youri Tielemans, Carlos Baleba and Andrey Santos were all brought in by United, as they looked to beef up that position after losing Casemiro.

Now, Pochettino is targeting yet another midfield acquisition with McKennie on his shopping list, according to La Gazzetta dello Sport. The 28-year-old has been a standout performer for Pochettino in the USMNT and is being considered for a shock January move to Old Trafford.

McKennie scored for the USMNT in their 6-0 thrashing of Haiti on Saturday, a game which marked Pochettino’s penultimate in charge before he makes the move to United.

Should McKennie move to the Reds, it would be a second chance for him to impress in the Premier League following a disastrous loan spell at Leeds United in the 2022/23 season.

He struggled as the Whites were relegated from the top flight, and returned to Juve at the end of the campaign with a point-to-prove. And it’s fair to suggest McKennie has made huge improvements to his game since then, and is now integral to Juve and Luciano Spaletti.

After establishing himself as a key member in Spaletti’s side last season, Pochettino was keen to praise McKennie and brand him as “important”. As well as playing in midfield, McKennie can also be used at full-back or even at centre-back, when needed.

“Juventus is Weston McKennie plus 10 players,” Pochettino said last month. “And we're so happy about that, because that means he's an important player for Juventus.”

When asked about Spaletti’s comment that he views McKennie as a ‘perfect’ striker, Pochettino replied: “Like his coach said, he can be the best striker, the best number 10, the number 7, the number 2, the number 4. He can play fullback, he can play midfield. I think he's playing, and he's playing in different positions.

“And of course, it's good for him, it's good for us. It's good to have a player that can play in different positions and to give balance. Sometimes the team, in different positions, as you say, if I see him in different positions, in which position do I think he's important?

“In the moment that we are going to give and to find that balance, I think it's important to see how he can provide balance in case that we want him in the starting 11. And of course, it's good to have this capacity, the capacity from the player to adapt to different positions. That's always good for the coaching staff."

McKennie is one of a number of USMNT players that has been linked with a move to United, including Alex Freeman and Antonee Robinson.
