Here's the Average Social Security Benefit by Age 62 to 80

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Claiming at 62 versus 70 creates a gap that reshapes your entire retirement income picture.

Social Security provides a vital floor for your retirement, but it's meant to supplement your income rather than replace it entirely. The amount you receive depends heavily on when you choose to claim, making it one of the most consequential financial decisions you'll face.

The Social Security Administration (SSA) calculates your benefit based on an average of your wages across your 35 highest-earning years, adjusted for inflation and run through a specific formula. Your final check size is then determined by your full retirement age (FRA) and the age you begin collecting.

Here is the average monthly retired-worker benefit by age, as of December 2025, the most recent data available.

The data reveals a clear increase between ages 62 and 70, with the average benefit growing by 59.7%. This represents a monthly gap of $850.28, or $10,203.36 per year.

Full retirement age (FRA) ranges from 66 to 67, depending on your birth year, and it's 67 for everyone born in 1960 or later. If you claim at 62, you face a permanent reduction of up to 30%, while waiting until 70 earns you an 8% annual increase through delayed retirement credits.

A second pattern appears after age 70, as averages begin to decline slightly. This doesn't mean the SSA cuts benefits for individuals. Instead, older groups likely include more people who claimed early at permanent reductions.

Women also make up a larger share of the oldest groups because they tend to live longer. Because they often have lower lifetime earnings due to the gender pay gap and time spent caregiving, their presence in the data lowers the overall average among the oldest retirees.

Even at the age-70 peak of $2,274.68 per month, or $27,296.16 per year, benefits rarely cover every bill. That peak falls $34,135.84 short of the $61,432 average senior household spending reported by the Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS).

A 3.6% cost-of-living adjustment (COLA) will take effect in 2027, adding about $82 a month on top of that age-70 benefit. While these adjustments are designed to keep pace with inflation, they often fall short.

The most useful number for your planning is the average that matches your own intended claiming age.

Even those who claim Social Security benefits at age 70, when they qualify for their maximum checks, rarely live comfortably on their benefits alone. You'll probably need other sources of retirement income to cover what Social Security doesn't.

Personal savings are your best bet if they're available to you. You're free to spend that money on anything you like, but remember it may have to last you 20 years or more, so choose a withdrawal strategy designed to make it last. If you don't have a lot of personal savings, you may need a job or other government benefits to supplement your Social Security checks.

Make sure you come up with a plan for how you'll cover your remaining living expenses before you apply for Social Security. If you're married, be sure to factor in your spouse's Social Security checks and personal savings as well, and make sure you're both on the same page about when each person plans to claim.

TL;DR: TeraWulf’s November 6 earnings release shows $63 million in third-quarter high-performance-computing lease revenue, around 83% of its $76.2 million total. The company is increasingly an AI infrastructure business, although large non-cash gains and long-dated construction plans require careful reading.

TeraWulf is becoming a clear example of how the economics of Bitcoin mining can lead an infrastructure operator into a rather different business. In its third-quarter results published November 6, the Nasdaq-listed company said high-performance computing, or HPC, generated $63 million of lease revenue in the three months to September 30.

That represented about 83% of its $76.2 million quarterly revenue. Investors who still describe the firm primarily as a Bitcoin miner risk missing where its business is now concentrated and what its future costs may look like.

The company operates existing mining infrastructure at its Lake Mariner campus but is repurposing portions of that site to support contracted computing workloads. TeraWulf said 102 megawatts of revenue-generating critical IT capacity were online at Lake Mariner at quarter-end, with another 336 megawatts under construction.

These numbers are not interchangeable with power agreements or theoretical campus capacity. A contract for future electricity or land does not mean an equivalent amount of paying data-centre capacity is already operating. The distinction matters when a company is projecting revenue from facilities that require years and significant capital to complete.

TeraWulf also reported net income of approximately $709.9 million for the quarter. But it attributed a significant benefit to a non-cash change in the fair value of warrants, while adjusted EBITDA was negative at about $12.8 million. That makes the headline profit a poor substitute for analysing recurring rental revenue, financing costs and operating cash flow.

The company’s release described a 20-year Anthropic lease covering approximately 401 megawatts at its Justified development in Kentucky, representing around $19 billion in contracted revenue over the initial term. It also described a Google credit backstop supporting anticipated project financing and a definitive engineering, procurement and construction agreement with Fluor. These are material commercial commitments, not a claim that $19 billion has already been received.

TeraWulf expects phased service availability at Justified to begin in late 2027 and continue into 2028. At Muskie, it said contracted power capacity had expanded toward one gigawatt, subject to regulatory approval. Schedules, financing and utility infrastructure remain central risks.

NewsBTC has previously examined the mining industry’s move towards AI infrastructure. TeraWulf’s fresh results make that theme measurable: a majority of present revenue now comes from leases rather than mining.

A miner switching to AI can reduce its exposure to Bitcoin production economics, including changes in difficulty, coin price and power cost. It can also create demand for the same grid connections, facilities and financing that historically supported mining. That transition need not be immediately positive or negative for Bitcoin’s network; it depends on which machines are retired, which are redeployed and whether remaining miners take up capacity.

The contrast with NewsBTC’s discussion of rising network hashrate is telling. Competition for mining rewards can intensify even while some large listed companies reshape themselves into computing landlords.

The milestones to watch are completed megawatts, actual rental commencement, capital spending against guidance, contractual counterparty exposure and cash generation. TeraWulf’s results show a dramatic change in revenue mix, but the hardest part of its next chapter will be delivering enormous physical projects reliably and profitably.

How a bottleneck calculator for your GPU and CPU works

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

They're a quick way to help you check component compatibility before you build or upgrade a system.

Troubleshooting computer components can be daunting. After the physical labor of installing a new part, you'll want to stress-test it to make sure your replacement works properly. Finding a faulty part is relatively straightforward, but what if the component is limiting the rest of your system? During a stress test, you might see your CPU at 100 percent while your GPU is practically idling.

This is considered a bottleneck. When one component holds back the rest of the system, it hinders performance until you resolve the bottleneck. For example, if your CPU is at 100 percent while your GPU is barely drawing power, we'd classify that as a CPU bottleneck since it's impairing your GPU's performance. Knowing which component is bottlenecking your system helps you decide what to upgrade or troubleshoot next. Before you buy a new GPU or any expensive component, check for bottlenecks first.

Some websites and software offer bottleneck calculators to help you troubleshoot these issues without stress-testing your system. While they can help you quickly spot a hardware mismatch, they're rarely accurate. Many online calculators can't tell the full story of how your system behaves. That said, a bottleneck calculator can help you check component compatibility before you build or upgrade a system, saving you valuable time while troubleshooting.

A bottleneck calculator takes your chosen CPU and GPU and compares their performance at your target resolution— common test outputs are 1080p, 1440p and 2160p. If both components are rated to perform adequately at your target benchmark, you'll receive a bottleneck score around ten percent or less. However, if one component is ill-equipped to handle the benchmark, it will receive a high percentage, indicating it's a bottleneck. The lower the percentage, the better the score.

Typically, the CPU is the culprit behind most PC bottlenecks. The CPU's job is to send information to the rest of your system in a timely fashion. If it's too slow, other components will wait for the CPU to finish. This is usually what causes stutters in video games. However, if your GPU is hovering around 95 percent usage while struggling to render the application, you may have a GPU bottleneck; GPUs tend to struggle when rendering graphics well above their intended resolution or frame rate. A bottlenecked GPU can cause screen tearing, low frame rates and inconsistent performance.

That is why bottleneck calculators ask for your target resolution: your GPU's performance depends heavily on how many pixels it renders each second. Paradoxically, this clinical approach to calculating performance is what makes bottleneck calculators so unreliable: they rely on spec sheets and theory, not real data from your PC.

Bottleneck calculators can't give you truly accurate data because of how they work. Since they reference manufacturer spec sheets, they omit variability from other system components and the application(s) you're testing. Not every graphics card is made equal, just as not every video game uses your GPU as effectively. A newer Intel GPU may struggle to run an older DX11 application that most Nvidia or AMD cards would run flawlessly. A powerful workstation CPU like the Ryzen 9 7950X won't perform as well as many X3D Ryzen chips for gaming because they have smaller L3 caches. These nuances are completely lost in these bottleneck calculators.

While they may not be ideal for detailed system troubleshooting, bottleneck calculators help with system-build planning. They're a convenient way to quickly check if your chosen CPU and GPU can handle your target resolution out of the box. They can also be a fun way for hobbyists to check strange hardware combinations you wouldn't normally see.

But for those looking for accurate tests on their PC hardware, performance monitoring software will always yield more reliable results. Bottleneck calculators can't account for your installed drivers, background apps you should disable, or performance issues in the game you're benchmarking. A simple bottleneck percentage doesn't tell the full story.

Parents want safer phones for kids. These companies are answering the call.

As parents become increasingly concerned about the potential dangers of unrestricted smartphone access for children, a growing number of companies are developing devices specifically designed for kids.

Today’s kid-friendly phones typically feature touchscreens, cameras, and communication capabilities similar to regular smartphones. However, the software on these devices is heavily modified to remove or restrict access to web browsers, social media, app stores, and other distractions.

Parents can usually manage these devices through a companion app, allowing them to approve contacts, monitor locations, set screen time limits, select available apps, and, in some cases, receive alerts regarding cyberbullying, explicit content, or online predators.

In addition to these modified devices, some companies offer a more minimalist approach, creating devices that focus solely on calling and texting.

There are also home phones designed specifically for children, like Tin Can and the recently launched Pinwheel Home.

Here’s a look at the major players shaping the kid-phone market.

The Bark Phone is probably one of the most popular kid-specific smartphones on the market.

Built on Samsung Galaxy hardware and powered by Bark’s monitoring software, it only allows calls and texts with approved contacts. Bark continuously scans texts, emails, photos, and supported apps for signs of cyberbullying, grooming, suicidal ideation, sexual content, and other potential concerns, then sends alerts to parents.

A big standout feature is that parents can gradually unlock web browsing, apps, and other features as their child matures. The phone also includes GPS tracking and screen-time management.

Current pricing for the standard model is $240, plus a required wireless plan beginning at $29/month. Higher-tier plans add internet access and unlimited texting.

Gabb is a well-known name in the kid-friendly phone category. Unlike Bark, however, Gabb focuses less on monitoring content and more on eliminating online risks altogether.

Its phones don’t include social media, a browser, or an app store. Instead, children get calling, texting, and a carefully curated selection of preloaded apps, including a camera, calendar, and calculator. The company also touts its “worry-free” music streaming library with millions of songs.

Additionally, parents can view location and manage certain device settings, while optional services such as “Gabb Guard” help block spam calls and unwanted texts.

Current phone pricing starts around $159.99, with cellular service starting around $24.99 per month.

With Pinwheel, parents approve every app, control contacts, schedule screen time, create custom routines, and monitor location history.

One standout feature is the ability to create different “modes” throughout the day. For example, limiting the phone to calling and navigation during school hours before unlocking additional features after homework.

Pinwheel phones start around $119, while the Caregiver Portal subscription begins at $14.99 per month without cell service. (Cellular service can be purchased separately through participating carriers.)

The company recently launched two landline phones for $68 and $79. It also offers a smartwatch at $160.

Launched in 2022, Teracube offers a tailored version of Android known as Thrive OS. This enables parents to approve every app download, filter web browsing, set app-specific and overall screen-time limits, monitor location, and create customized routines for school or bedtime. However, because it has more traditional smartphone features, it’s often recommended for older children.

Thrive currently sells for $99, with plans starting at $35 per month.

Unlike the other products in this roundup, Ooma MyPhone isn’t a mobile phone at all. This kid-focused home phone is designed as a modern replacement for the traditional household landline.

The service allows calls only between approved contacts through its Trusted Circle feature, offers scheduled Quiet Hours, provides address-based 911 service, sends emergency alerts when 911 is dialed, and lets parents review call logs through an online portal. There are no apps, internet browsing, texting, or social media.

The phone costs $99.99, while service starts at $7.99 per month.

For $100, Tin Can resembles a landline phone with a distinctive tin can design, which is the inspiration behind its name.

This device does not require a traditional phone jack, but connects via Wi-Fi. Only approved contacts can make calls, effectively preventing spam or unauthorized communications. Parents can easily manage contacts through a user-friendly companion app.

Tin Can offers a free plan for calling other Tin Can users, as well as a $9.99 per month plan that allows calls to all approved contacts.

A newer entrant in the space, Waffle recently opened preorders for its screen-free devices: Waffle Home ($119), a landline phone, and Waffle Go ($169), which looks like a portable walkie-talkie or kids’ toy with three simple buttons that can only make voice calls and listen to voice notes.

Parents can approve contacts via the companion app, as well as see location and control when devices can call.

TextNow, the Canadian telecommunications company, recently launched its new mobile phone for kids. It’s called Yello! and while it looks like a normal touchscreen smartphone, it doesn’t have an app store, social media, or unrestricted web access.

Parents can see their kids’ location and battery life, as well as control things like the games they play and who they have in their contacts.

Yello! costs $149.99 upfront, with a $19.99 per month plan.

The Velotric GoMad Electric Utility eBike — CleanTechnica Tested

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Velotric has emerged as one of the largest ebike brands in the US, offering a wide range of models for buyers to choose from. Having a wide range of models with differing capabilities is increasingly a key factor for buyers when deciding which ebike to buy, as it allows them to upgrade or swap out for a different model to accommodate different stages of life.

They reached out to CleanTechnica about a review and we were excited to play with the Velotric GoMad Electric Utility bike, because it is just so darn flexible in what it can do. You can throw a set of bags on the back rack or a passenger kit to bring a friend with you. You can put a basket on the back and deliver pizzas, or you can just throw a backpack on and ride around town solo. It truly looked like the jack of all trades, so we pulled the trigger, and after a few days, it showed up.

Disclaimer: Velotric provided the GoMad to the author for the purposes of this review.

Assembling the Velotric GoMad is similar to the process for most e-bikes. It can be done with a basic set of tools that are included with the bike, and takes between 30 minutes and an hour depending on how much of a hurry you’re in.

After that, it’s a simple matter of attaching the pedals, mounting the handlebars to the stem, installing the front wheel and tire, connecting a few of the electrical connectors between the handlebars and the bike itself, airing up the tires, and you’re on your way. Ours took a few minutes longer than normal to bolt on the optional Passenger Kit

Taking in the Velotric GoMad, it is an attractive bike with great proportions. The battery is integrated into the main downtube on the frame, which makes it look slightly bloated, but in a way that fits into the overall proportions of the bike. The oversized tires help with this, making it look like it can go absolutely anywhere.

The rear hub motor kicks out plenty of power, and with a range of pedal assist modes. You can choose just how much power you need for the ride you want. You can choose from the motor being completely off, Eco, Tour, Trail, Sport, and even the full-out Boost mode that can give you exactly the support you need.

It’s perfect for when you’re carrying a load, powering up a hill, or just feeling tired. The motor is always ready to step in to provide assistance. At the upper end of its power cycle, the motor controller is more sensitive to the pedaling cadence, resulting in the motor pulsing on and off, providing for a bit of a bumpy sensation when pedaling. It’s only something you’d notice if you’re really paying attention, but it’s there.

The GoMad is designed with an integrated rear rack and a hidden storage pocket. The rack can also be converted into a seat for a second passenger with Velotric’s passenger kit. The passenger kit itself is amazing if that’s what you’re looking for, and this is further accentuated by the bike’s compact footprint. The tradeoff is that the main seat has a flat rear, which does make it slightly less comfortable than more fully built-out options.

The passenger kit comes with a set of pegs down below for the passengers’ feet to rest on, a rear grab rail for the rack, and a front handle for the passenger to hang onto as you’re boosting around town. Given the compact footprint of the bike, it feels like it was designed to be used as more of a teenager-plus-one setup, but if you want to put it to work as a husband-and-wife date night setup, its 500 pound weight capacity is more than up to the task.

I rode around on it with my wife on the back and it was very comfortable and stable for both of us. The foot pegs and the solid support bars that come with the passenger kit make it feel much safer and more comfortable for the passenger. I took a few pics of us on the bike, but, hilariously, none of them had the bike in frame and she wasn’t a fan of my one handed stunts. Of course, that’s for you to decide what you want to do with your life and how you want to set up your bike and put it to use.

The Velotric Nomad is an extremely capable bike at a mid-level price point for bikes of this quality and capability. It’s currently listed as $1,899 with a special Black Friday offer that includes a free young passenger kit.

The fact that its 750W continuous power output motor has a peak of 1,300W means it’s got plenty of pep to take even the largest and least-in-shape riders wherever they want to go. The specs across the board and the integration of the torque and cadence sensor make it feel like a very natural ride that will surprise you with just how intuitively the power is coming from the motor.

I especially love the colorways this bike comes in. They have a nice modern-looking sage that feels classy and refined, a dark amber that’s somewhere between a bright orange and bright red that looks like a pop of lipstick that really sets the whole frame off, and finally an obsidian, which is what our review bike came in.

The obsidian has a base color that looks almost black, but then when you put it in the sunlight, it has a rainbow pop of color that peeks out every once in a while. It’s not so much that it feels like it’s covered in glitter, but it’s just enough to make it feel fun.

For more information about the Velotric Nomad e-bike, head over to their official website, where you can learn all about it or purchase one for yourself.
