How Hershey strategized its brand film to be more than a 90-minute ad

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Let Marketing Dive's free newsletter keep you informed, straight from your inbox.

Filmmakers worked with the company, gaining access to archives and historians and collaborating on IP, brand and creative considerations.

“Hershey,” a biopic of Hershey Company founder Milton S. Hershey, hits theaters on Wednesday. The film, along with the marketer’s celebration of America’s 250th anniversary, are expected to contribute almost one percentage point to company sales in 2026, CEO Kirk Tanner said on a recent earnings call. “Hershey” has so far earned strong customer and retail response.

“It's relevant for our time, so we’ve seen a lot of support and that's going to show up in-store with inventory in displays and a connection back to the movie ... That will be an uplift. That will be a tailwind for the portfolio and especially Hershey, which has already had a terrific year,” Tanner said. “We like the momentum that will build going into 2027.”

What sets “Hershey” apart from other branded films is the way that the filmmakers worked with the company, gaining access to Hershey archives and historians and collaborating on intellectual property, brand and creative considerations.

“We wanted a real story told, which means, as a marketer, you need to gulp a little bit and say, ‘Hey, it's not a story if there's no conflict.’ You only get a tiny bit of that [conflict] when you create advertisements,” said Suzanne Jones, vice president of the Hershey Experience at The Hershey Company. “We wanted to make sure this was not in any way, shape or form a 90-minute commercial.”

"Hershey” is the product of a collaboration between the Hershey entities — the combination of The Hershey Company, Milton Hershey School, Hershey Entertainment & Resorts, The M.S. Hershey Foundation and Hershey Trust Company — with production company Dandelion Media. The film was produced in association with Aloe Entertainment, Peachtree Group and RCM3 with UTA Independent Film Group.

“This is not just a story about something that happened in the past, but it is a living legacy that exists today, and not just in the candy bar, but in the Milton Hershey School,” Jones said. “There are kids across town sitting in algebra class because of what Milton and Catherine Hershey did. It's way more than the story of a founder or the story of a brand.”

The content of the “Hershey” film sets it apart from a rash of recent branded films from E.l.f., Garage Beer and Texas Pete that mostly used humor to market products and value propositions. The film focuses on the story of Milton Hershey, whose grit and perseverance led not only to a $35 billion snack company, but also a series of charitable organizations.

“Consumers today are really looking for authenticity and what a brand stands for in the products that it delivers to the marketplace, but its role in the community and in society today, as well,” Jones said.

For Hershey, the film is at the heart of an ecosystem that spans channels and its portfolio, which includes Reese's, Twizzlers and salty snacks like Dot's Pretzels. The brand on Wednesday will sell Hershey bars via Amazon and Walmart at their original 5-cent price, and will release limited-edition, vintage-inspired packaging featuring QR codes that link to more information about the Hershey story.

“As we've looked at activating against this, we've been able to … collaborate with our retail partners across the country to bring the film to life,” Jones said. “It wasn't this insurmountable lift to marry the Hershey's brand to the equity of the film because it is so authentically intertwined.”

Beyond retail, Hershey will utilize social and creator content, as well as some surprises for consumers during the holiday week. The company has been working to develop its muscle around social media, including through posts tied to cultural moments like the recent Microsoft shutdown and the release of “Grand Theft Auto VI.”

Hershey kicked off the year with its first major creative effort in eight years, launching the “It’s Your Happy Place” platform ahead of the 2026 Olympic and Paralympic Winter Games. The brand subsequently partnered with girl group Katseye on a fully integrated campaign around its new Creme Bars. The Hershey Company reported net sales growth of 3% in Q3, with North American confectionery business sales growth of 1.6%.

Former Biden official: AI is already collecting your Census data

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The U.S. Census Bureau rarely makes headlines, yet just a few weeks ago it was reported that AI agents captured Census data using a password left in plain sight. What they gathered may matter less today than what it could reveal tomorrow.

In September, OpenAI revealed that its AI agents pulled Census Bureau data purportedly after finding login credentials in public code repositories. The U.S. Department of Commerce says the data was already public, and no one has called it a breach. But I know this agency too well to think the story ends there.

Most Americans interact with the Census Bureau once a decade by receiving a form in the mail, or when a stranger with a clipboard knocks on their door.  The decennial count is the largest civilian deployment the country undertakes pursuant to our Constitution. In the middle of a pandemic, census takers climbed walk-ups in the Bronx, drove across roads of the Navajo Nation, and knocked on doors along the Texas border. Then the work ended, and the Bureau receded into the quiet corners of the U.S. Commerce Department to aggregate the responses and build data sets that are used widely by the public and private sector, not to mention universities and companies alike.

Now we are learning that the Census Bureau had an unexpected visitor of its own, and this one did not knock. As U.S. Assistant Secretary of Commerce for Economic Development, I worked in the department that houses the Census Bureau and relied on its numbers every day.  Sitting with a mayor in Appalachia or a Pueblo leader in New Mexico, I used census data to understand where a federal investment could change a community’s future. Few agencies in Washington are so little noticed and so heavily depended upon.

The census decides who holds power. After the 2020 count, New York — falling short by just 89 residents — lost a congressional seat. Illinois also lost a seat; California lost one for the first time in its history; Texas gained two, and Florida gained one. Those numbers redrew the congressional map for one decade.

The census also decides where the money goes. The Bureau estimates that more than $2.8 trillion in federal funds were distributed in fiscal year 2021 using its data, supporting health care, nutrition, highways, housing, school lunches, and childcare. When a rural hospital in Mississippi stays open or a new bus line reaches a neighborhood in Phoenix, census figures usually play a role in why that decision was made.

That is what makes this under-the-radar agency such an attractive target. AI agents are built to find the most authoritative answer, and the Census Bureau is the nation’s answer key. The Bureau delivers immense value and draws little public attention. OpenAI says most of the activity it has reviewed involved routine research, with agents reading public web content from trusted government sources. Yet somewhere along the way, a routine task became a lock with borrowed keys, and the AI agent never paused to ask whether it should enter the home.

We have seen how quickly that line can blur. In July, OpenAI’s models escaped their testing environment and breached Hugging Face, a major AI platform, executing code on dozens of servers, gaining full root access on one, and obtaining credentials to its messaging platform.

What worries me most is not what these agents took, but what they could someday learn from it. The Census Bureau publishes numbers, not people, and federal law keeps individual responses confidential for 72 years. Yet after 2010, the Bureau concluded that its own published data could be reverse engineered to reconstruct records about real individuals, and it overhauled its privacy protections.  Those defenses were built for the computers of their day.

Picture an AI agent quietly assembling public census data alongside property records, voter files, and commercial data. Now picture what that same AI agent might look like in 2030, or eventually what quantum computers capable of solving problems today’s machines cannot, might look like and the power it may hold.  Data that is harmless on its own can become revealing in combination. Security experts warn of “harvest now, decrypt later.” We may need a new phrase: harvest now, know later.

We do not yet know what these systems will do with what they gather, and it appears neither do the companies that built them, or at least that is what they claim.  That uncertainty should command our urgent attention.

The census rests on a simple bargain. A grandmother in El Paso opens her door because she trusts that her answers will be counted and kept safe. That trust is the Bureau’s most valuable asset, and it cannot be patched once it is lost.  With less than three years from the next decennial census, I am left with a question I cannot put down. If an AI agent can find the keys to America’s most important dataset without being told to look, what will it unlock once it understands what it holds?

EU delays Tesla self-driving meeting to 15 December, Reuters reports

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Bloc-wide approval of Tesla Assisted Driving needs the backing of 15 member states covering 65% of the EU’s population

The EU has pushed back a meeting to 15 December that could decide whether Tesla’s supervised driver-assistance software is allowed across the bloc, a European Commission spokesperson confirmed the new date to Reuters.

National experts on the Technical Committee on Motor Vehicles (TCMV) vote on EU vehicle rules. They had been due to discuss Tesla’s case on 1 December, the Dutch regulator RDW told the news agency.

Belgium’s Flemish transport ministry first gave 7 December as the new date, before the Commission settled on the 15th.

The software, previously sold as Full Self-Driving (Supervised), won its first European approval in April. After more than 18 months of testing, the RDW granted a provisional type approval in the Netherlands. The Dutch regulator then asked the TCMV to extend that approval to all 27 member states.

The RDW stressed at the time that the system is not self-driving and that the driver remains responsible. Its approval also requires Tesla to report safety-critical incidents to the regulator.

Since then, the committee has discussed the request twice, in June and in October, without holding a vote, according to Reuters. To pass, it needs the support of at least 15 member states that together represent 65% of the EU’s population.

While the bloc-wide decision waits, individual countries can still recognise the Dutch approval on their own, and nearly a dozen have followed the Netherlands.

Finland became the 11th earlier this month, Euronews reported, and Reuters said Italy, the most populous country so far, has started a rollout.

Tesla has also made changes to win over Germany, the EU’s largest market. In October, the company renamed the software Tesla Assisted Driving in Europe, after the German transport ministry said the old name was misleading because the driver must stay in control.

Italian officials had raised the same concern in internal government correspondence.

The ministry also said Tesla had agreed to limit how far the system can drive above the speed limit to 10%. In return, Transport Minister Steffen Bilger said he would push for a quick approval across the EU.

Until a vote passes, Tesla drivers can only use the system in the countries that have recognised the Dutch approval.

A 5-Minute Stretch Routine to Relieve Tight Joints and a Stiff Back

📅 Nov 25, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

If you’ve been feeling ultra stiff lately, a five-minute yoga-inspired stretching routine break to release physical tension can feel like a full-body reset, helping you feel better in your body—and even more productive—so you can do all of your favorite activities.

This short practice is ideal when you need a short break during the workday, but it can also serve as a grounding moment before you sit at your desk or when you’re winding down in the evening. You only need a yoga mat and a sturdy surface to practice on. Come exactly as you are.

Lie on your back with your arms and legs straight, as if you were in Savasana. Take some small movements, maybe wiggling your fingers and toes or moving your head side to side. Then let yourself relax for a moment. Inhale as you reach your hands overhead and your feet away from you, stretching your entire body from head to toe. Exhale and release.

Bend your knees, plant your feet on the mat about hip-distance apart, and rest your arms at your sides. Inhale, press your hands and feet into the mat, and lift your hips in Bridge Pose. Exhale and lower your hips to the mat.

Separate your feet slightly wider than your hips. Bend your elbows and draw your arms into a cactus shape. Exhale and allow your knees to lower toward the left. Inhale and return your knees to center. Exhale and lower your knees toward the right. Repeat Windshield Wipers two more times on each side.

Hug both knees toward your chest, grasping your shins or the backs of your thighs with your hands.

Come to a comfortable sitting position. Lift your shoulders toward your ears, then roll them back, down, and forward. Continue drawing circles with your shoulders in one direction for a few rounds, then switch directions.

Interlace your fingers behind your head with your elbows out to the sides. Exhale as you draw your navel toward your spine, round your back, and lower your chin toward your chest in a Cat Pose variation. Inhale as you lift upright and arch your back, drawing your elbows back slightly in a Cow Pose variation. Repeat a few times.

Inhale and reach your arms overhead. Exhale and twist your chest toward the left. Place your right hand on your left knee and your left fingertips on the mat behind you. Take a few breaths here.

Still sitting, come back to center and bring your arms into a cactus shape. Open your hands as wide as you can, spreading your fingers apart. Then shut your hands into tight fists. Repeat opening and closing your hands as many times as feels good.

Sit on the mat with your knees bent, your feet flat on the mat, and your hands on the mat behind your hips, fingertips pointing toward you or toward the back of the mat. Press into your hands and feet as you lift your hips off the mat. Draw your chin toward your chest and gaze forward. Take a few breaths here, then lower your hips to the mat.

Draw the soles of your feet together. Inhale and lift your chest. Your feet can come closer to you or stay farther away. Exhale and fold forward, grasping your ankles or feet in Bound Angle Pose. You can stack your fists on top of each other and rest your forehead on them. Breathe here.

Lie on your back with your arms resting at your sides and your palms pressing into the mat. Bend your knees toward your chest and straighten your legs so your feet are reaching toward the ceiling. Keep your legs slightly bent if it’s more comfortable. Take a few breaths here, then shake out your arms and legs and lower your feet to the mat.

Lie on your back, resting your arms, legs, hands, and feet against the mat in Savasana. Or find a comfortable seated position. Take a few deep breaths before returning to the day.
