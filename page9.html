8 Reasons a Contractor Might Give You an Outrageously High Estimate

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

If you just got a quote for a home repair that made your eyes water, there's probably a reason it's so high. A team of contractors weighs in with possible explanations.

If you just got a quote from a contractor that was way more than you thought it would be, join the club…it happens to the best of us. In fact, it’s a pretty common occurrence. Sometimes, though, the quote is so high that you can feel your mouth forming words you shouldn’t be saying. As someone who has been on both sides of such interactions, I can say that contractors don’t like them any more than you do. They know you can shop around, and they need your business. So what’s the deal with ridiculously high quotes?

I didn’t find any shortage of contractors willing to weigh in on this topic — one that is near and dear to their professional hearts. If a quote just made you hyperventilate, take a deep breath and check out some of the possible reasons why it’s so much higher than you expected.

“If an HVAC unit has a defective part that needs replacement, most of my clients will immediately ask how much the replacement part costs,” says HVAC technician Tamer Sayed. “I always tell the current market price and the misunderstanding often starts here. A few clients forget to take into account the labor and consultation fees. They assume they only had to pay for the replacement part.”

The same thing can happen with plumbing and electrical jobs. The part needed to get things working again may be small and inexpensive, and it may only account for a small portion of the quote. Sometimes the labor quote seems excessive, and that’s usually because the client doesn’t understand what’s involved with the repair.

Jake Vincett, a builder from the UK, makes this observation: “A lot of the time, the customer is comparing quotes for different work, because the cheaper one has included a surface repair whereas the more expensive one has quoted for fixing the underlying problem…It’s common that the customer doesn’t know or consider the amount of work going into a project. A new driveway isn’t just laying tarmac – it’s digging out the old one, laying the sub base, etc.”

Plumbing, electrical and HVAC expert Bob Cardwell adds: “It’s often not only one problem to fix but a bunch of small fixes. Even though it might be possible to fix the original problem without addressing the additional issues, an experienced and reliable technician will not ignore them, will try to fix them and will discuss them with the homeowner.”

Failing to provide a quote that addresses underlying issues and then delivering a sky-high bill at the end doesn’t just make the customer unhappy, says HVAC and plumbing expert Ryan Osterkamp. “It can make them feel like we don’t know what we’re doing.”

Osterkamp is used to hearing customers say: “I can go get that part for $X at Home Depot.” What they aren’t thinking about is the amount of training and expertise that made it possible for our technician to quickly and accurately diagnose the issue, have the supplies they need in their truck and be able to do the repair same day. Sure, you may save money by going with the guy on Facebook that works out of an old, unlabeled truck, but if his work doesn’t hold up, will he fix it without giving you the runaround? Is he insured? Does he offer a warranty?”

Tariffs, inflation and the economy in general are pushing prices up, and the dollar has lost a lot of its purchasing power for the average American. The same is true for contractors. “The fact of the matter is, we’re working harder now and making less,” says remodeling contractor Kevin Cullen. “In today’s economy, a lot of people are chasing the price to the bottom and shopping for the cheapest number. Unfortunately, they usually get what they pay for.”

A contractor who knows problems might creep up, such as a rotten subfloor that needs replacement during a toilet repair, makes an effort to warn customers as part of the quote. Potential problems aren’t always obvious. “There are times,” says Nika Tveretinova, who operates an HVAC business in Sacramento, “when the actual fix isn’t that complicated, but it’s the access that makes things tough—especially with equipment installed in an attic or cramped quarters. Sometimes we also find an old installation that needs a safety or code correction before we can put everything back together.”

In such situations, a higher-than-expected quote that you can reject is far better than a higher-than-expected bill you have to pay because the work is already completed.

Joshua Kurz founded NumeralQ, a home improvement pricing guide, and he cautions against comparing a bid you receive from a contractor to information you find online. “Owners often find an estimate online, and rather than waiting for a bid, they assume that number will be the actual cost. These estimates are helpful when determining a budget, but they are not interchangeable for actual bids. A 1,500 sq ft roof in one market will not necessarily cost the same in another market, and bids can even differ for neighboring houses.”

If you find a guy on Facebook who charges a low price to do a repair, there’s no guarantee that the repair will last or be up to code. When that person makes a mistake, a licensed contractor working on the same system at a later date might have to fix it, especially when inspectors are involved.

“Over a 40-year career,” says Cullen, “I’ve found many surprises: unsupported headers, no king or jack studs, undersized headers, poorly installed headers that aren’t flush and have gaps, termite damage, carpenter ant damage, water damage, mold. That’s not every job, but you never know when a homeowner bought a house in which a Rube Goldberg did the work on it.”

Sometimes a house is in such poor condition that a small repair, such as stopping a plumbing leak, can turn into a major project that involves replacing pipes, walls or subfloors. Rather than simply declining the repair, some contractors will offer a ridiculously high quote to avoid falling into a sinkhole of extra work. If the customer accepts the quote, the contractor won’t lose money by taking the job. A customer who rejects a bid can keep shopping with no harm done.

The Remarried Empress Cast Reveal Their Characters' Inner Lives

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

The Remarried Empress is already making a splash during its premiere week, establishing itself as Disney+'s number-one Korean title in over 50 markets in the three days after its launch. Set in the imaginary world of the Eastern Empire, the K-drama is based on a globally renowned webtoon of the same name, following Empress Navier (Shin Min-a) as she picks up the pieces of her broken marriage and comes out on top.

When Emperor Sovieshu (Ju Ji-hoon) returns from a hunting trip with a runaway slave he aims to make his mistress, Navier must take stock of her role as wife and co-ruler of her beloved country. While she and her competition, Rashta (Lee Se-young), size each other up, the arrival of Prince Heinrey (Lee Jong-suk) of the Western Kingdom complicates things even further. Soon enough, Navier is swept up in the mystery, magic, and romance of the rival country's royal.

ScreenRant interviewed the cast of The Remarried Empress, asking them to share the vulnerable sides of Navier and Sovieshu, the bird-like style of Heinrey, and the intentions of Rashtha. The actors also revealed their personal favorite sets and costumes while filming on location in places like the Czech Republic and Germany.

ScreenRant: Min-a, you mentioned the drama shows a different side of Navier. What was one of the first moments in the script where you really connected with her humanity?

Shin Min-a: I think that, as an empress, Navier is trained to hold back from expressing her emotions right away. In that respect, as an actor, I've always had my own ways of restraining myself when I feel certain emotions. When Navier is upset, she shares what's on her mind with Queen in a measured and appropriate way, while I similarly open up to close friends.

When I first read the script, I felt that she showed so little emotion, and the weight she carried as an empress felt a bit stifling. But at the same time, I thought, “We might actually have something in common.” The way she handles and expresses her feelings is also something I admire and would like to emulate.

ScreenRant: Ji-hoon, similarly, you mentioned the importance of perspective. What’s an early moment in the script where you felt bad for Sovieshu or really understood his reasoning - as opposed to seeing him as a villain?

Ju Ji-hoon: What I understand is that anyone can have ambivalent feelings. He feels guilt toward the woman he loves, but also a sense of duty to have a child and continue the royal line.

And even though it begins as duty, there's also the genuine attraction he must have felt toward Rashta, as one person to another. Those feelings may be taboo in our society, but if no one were to say, “You can't do that,” they're feelings that could naturally arise. So, I focused on trying to understand that.

ScreenRant: Jong-suk, you mentioned the CGI team incorporated elements of your look into Queen’s design. What bird-like elements did you bring to your performance?

Lee Jong-suk: Actually, the order was that I came first, and then the bird was created. So, I think the CG team gave it much more thought than I did. On set, we had a lot of conversations along the lines of, “If you could emphasize certain characteristics a little more, it would help us create the bird in CG.”

ScreenRant: Se-young, fans have obsessively discussed the confrontation between Rashta and Navier in the trailer. Do you think Rashta’s trajectory would be different if Navier had welcomed her more warmly?

Lee Se-young: I don't think it has a big influence. As her life in the imperial palace goes on, Rashta learns and gradually develops her own motives. But she's not the type to start walking on eggshells because of that one rejection.

ScreenRant: It’s not every day you film in the Czech Republic, with an imperial palace as your setting. Did each of you have a favorite set or costume in particular?

Ju Ji-hoon: For me, it was the grand castles where we did our first shoot in the Czech Republic. Not just one castle, but several. We had them entirely to ourselves, without any tourists – that's a moment you rarely get in life. The moment I stepped into spaces that carried the real weight of time, I felt an energy I can't quite describe. Those spaces helped me a great deal in understanding and focusing on the story.

Shin Min-a: Filming scenes in the castles and gardens of the Czech Republic and Germany, I could feel a historical energy. Because we were entering a world we had created ourselves, we as actors had to be the first to fully step into it. The locations and costumes helped tremendously with that.

Lee Jong-suk: Our series did not require historical accuracy based on a specific era or country. As Min-a said, it's our own fantasy world, so I think we were able to create it through the feelings the spaces gave us, our co-stars' performances, and the entire crew working together as one.

Lee Se-young: I didn't change outfits that often, but I wore many very glamorous dresses. The one that stands out most is the dress from the scene where Rashta bids farewell to Sovieshu as he leaves, asking him not to forget her. When I tried it on during the fitting, I felt, “I've really become Rashta.” That was the first time I felt truly close to her.

Bear Grylls made an unusual decision decades ago, when he acquired a marital starter home with his wife, Shara Grylls.

“At 25, just married, we bought a small, abandoned, off-grid island from an ad in the back of a magazine,” the British adventurer, 52, wrote in a Nov. 3 Instagram caption.

Grylls revealed that the purchase of St. Tudwal’s Island West cost them around $120,000.

“There were many who thought us crazy,” he continued. “The best adventures start with nay-sayers. Thank you Shara for being brave and saying yes!”

While the comment section had users divided over the relatability of purchasing an entire island—with one commenter wondering how “celebrities get so far out of touch from reality and regular people”—there were also a handful of people who stepped in to point out that even when adjusted for inflation, the island was less costly than the average U.S. home.

The “Running Wild With Bear Grylls” star explained the decision to the Sunday Times in 2021, noting that snapping up a Welsh island seemed like a more exciting use of the couple’s money.

“In 2001, we were going to buy a London flat for $288,000, but we bought a houseboat on the Thames [now sold] and the island together, and it cost less than the apartment. So we decided on the path less trodden and an amazing adventure,” he said.

Grylls shed some light on just how dilapidated the property was 25 years ago.

“The island had a 200-year-old lighthouse and two lightkeeper’s cottages, but [the houses] were derelict with no power, no water, no roof, and rats,” he said. “I remember my first bath sitting in 3 inches of water pumped from the old tanks we’d cleaned out. There was rubble everywhere and rain coming through, but it was a great moment.”

Considering Grylls has made a career out of his ability to survive in extremely harsh conditions, it’s not entirely surprising that he was able to get through a few rough years before the island would become a more suitable dwelling.

It took a decade to get permission to build a slipway, which Grylls said they finished just a few years ago.

“It has been life-changing for us,” he said of the addition. “Previously we had to keep a boat on a mooring, row ashore, and walk a mile up the beach with all the shopping.”

Eventually, the two lightkeeper’s cottages were combined into a single home with four bedrooms. But the island is still off-grid.

“It’s 20 acres and 5 miles offshore with no mains, electricity, or running water,” Grylls told Vanity Fair in 2012. “It has a little lighthouse beside our house and is surrounded by amazing sea cliffs, seals, and dolphins. It is my favorite place on the planet!”

To travel to the destination, Grylls and his family take a “30-foot, high-speed ex-lifeboat,” which he described as a “rigid inflatable boat with twin 300-horsepower engines behind it that can tackle any sea it needs to.”

He said the house is “as tough as you get.” Thanks to “2-foot-thick old limestone walls,” it retains heat in the winter and stays cool in the summer.

“But when those gales come in, you can still feel it almost shake,” Grylls admitted. “Sea spray reaches the windows despite the 150-foot elevation.”

While St. Tudwal’s may lack some of the modern amenities guests might typically expect, the Grylls family does “sometimes have friends” visit.

“They need to be up for an adventure and prepared to get stuck there if the weather turns,” he said.

Grylls helped baptize Brand in a ceremony in the River Thames in 2024, after the allegations against the actor had come to light. Prior to the baptism, Brand appeared in a 2023 episode of Grylls’ show, in which the pair traveled across the Hebrides Islands.

“Brand’s faith seemed genuine to me at the time when I was a guest at his private baptism,” Grylls wrote in a Nov. 6 X post. “I have attended a few now. Even visiting in prisons with people who have committed terrible crimes. It’s part of our calling as men and women of faith, to love and serve anyone. It is the sick that need healing.”

Noting that “judgment is for the courts and God,” Grylls added that “it is right that justice has now been served.

“I can’t imagine how hard this time has been for the victims, the heroes of the day,” he said.

I Thought Multi-Tools Were a Gimmick — Then I Took One on a Camping Trip

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

If, like me, you think multi-tools are gimmicky gadgets, you might change your mind once you discover how handy they are when your toolbox isn’t within reach.

I admit I scoffed when my partner first started raving about his Leatherman Wave+ multi-tool. I thought it looked like an expensive, over-elaborate gadget that was destined to gather dust in a junk drawer.

However, as a keen hiker and camper, I’ve discovered how useful it is to have pliers, knives, a saw, scissors, files and other tools packed into one compact, quality piece of kit. I’ve also watched my tech-obsessed partner regularly reach for its precision tools when tinkering with his electrical equipment.

Ahead, I’ll share how a tool I once dismissed as a gimmick earned a permanent spot in my pack, and what two experts think makes a Leatherman worth the price tag.

I had to realize that our Leatherman Wave+ isn’t meant to replace a well-stocked toolbox to understand its value. Instead, it fills the gap when dedicated tools aren’t conveniently available or really needed.

At home, I regularly use the tool’s tiny scissors to carefully prune dead leaves from our many houseplants, the knife to open packages and the small screwdriver to tighten the legs on my glasses. My partner is an IT professional who also has an impressive collection of synthesizers, and he regularly reaches for the tool to make small technical repairs and splice wires.

I’ve found the Wave+ even more useful when we’re borrowing my parents’ campervan, hiking, or camping. The bottle opener is handy for those after-adventure beers, the serrated knife slices baguettes for trailside sandwiches, the saw cuts small, dry branches for kindling, and the screwdriver has adjusted our portable stove and tightened folding chairs.

The pocket-sized multi-tool packs 18 functions into a stainless-steel form that measures just four inches when closed and weighs 8.5 ounces. Alongside the tools I’ve mentioned, it has pliers, bit drivers, a wire stripper and crimper, a ruler, an extra knife blade and file, and an admittedly slightly-tricky-to-use can opener.

While I don’t regularly use all 18 tools, and I might never use some, having the selection provides peace of mind, especially in remote places.

Daniel Kilburn, founder of Emergency Action Planning and a preparedness expert, has carried his Leatherman Surge since his military service in 2006. “It’s an expedient tool to have on hand when you know you’re going to be someplace where you won’t have access to any others,” he says.

Scott Emanuel of knives, multi-tools, and outdoor equipment retailer Heinnie Haynes is also a multi-tool convert. He appreciated having one for quick fixes while renovating his cottage, and particularly rates the needle-nose pliers, which rival standard models rather than acting as an emergency substitute.

Kilburn suggests multi-tools can be a useful starting point for someone who doesn’t own a toolbox, and at the other end of the spectrum are the tinkerers and prepared problem-solvers Emanuel sees as natural fits.

“The person who will value a Leatherman is the one who spends their after-hours in the garage building stuff that they probably never need, it’s the kind of self-reliant and prepared person who you rely on to solve a problem,” Emanuel says.

The Leatherman Wave+ currently costs $129.95, meaning it should be a well-considered purchase rather than an impulse buy.

It’s reassuring to know that you’ll be investing in a refined product designed because of a genuine practical need. Founder Tim Leatherman conceived a pliers-based multi-tool after encountering car and plumbing problems while traveling through Europe in the 1970s, and after careful development, he launched the original Pocket Survival Tool in 1983.

Today you’re paying for that established reputation and durable construction, and Leatherman backs its products with a 25-year warranty.

“They’re ridiculously well built, really well regarded, and their choice of tools is good,” Emanuel says. He describes sought-after Leathermans as the “collectible top end of the multi-tool market.”

Kilburn recalls seeing lower-priced multi-tools failing his comrades during their military career. “Some disintegrate over time because they were just cheaply made with poor-quality material [and] poor-quality steel,” he says. His Leatherman Surge, meanwhile, has lasted over 20 years. If replacing it again, he says he would choose Leatherman over his backup Gerber multi-tool because he prefers its feel and durability.

Leatherman makes a wide range of multi-tools, so it pays to consider which functions you’ll genuinely use. The Signal model adds outdoor-focused features suited to camping, including a ferrocerium rod, emergency whistle and sharpener, but omits scissors. I use scissors enough for plant pruning, first aid and gear repairs that the Wave+ remains the better fit for me.

If you’re unlikely to use most of its functions, a smaller, cheaper multi-tool could make more sense. The Wave+ is also heavier than minimalist models, and there’s a learning curve when figuring out where everything lives.

Don’t forget that a premium multi-tool only represents good value if you look after it. We remove dirt after outdoor adventures and dry and lubricate it before storing. We also avoid forcing it to tackle jobs better suited to full-size tools. I wouldn’t, for example, choose its compact saw over a dedicated full-size version for pruning substantial tree branches in my yard.

If you’re using a Wave+ in the UK, like me, be aware of the country’s knife laws. You need a “good reason” to carry it in public because its blades lock. However, Emanuel explains that the legal language is ambiguous, so you shouldn’t assume you can carry one wherever you go just because it’s part of a multi-tool.

The Wave+ has convinced me that a good multi-tool is more than merely an expensive gimmick. It may not replace dedicated tools, but having so many functions at my fingertips in a form that is so compact, portable and easy to use makes it a practical, worthwhile investment.

US Treasury walks back crypto surveillance ‘travel rule’ proposal

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

FinCEN and the Federal Reserve Board of Governors rescinded the "Travel Rule," which was first proposed in 2020 to require financial institutions to collect information on funds worth $250 or more.

The Treasury Department has withdrawn a controversial proposal that would have expanded reporting requirements for cryptocurrency transactions.

On Friday, the Treasury's Financial Crimes Enforcement Network and the Federal Reserve Board of Governors rescinded the "Travel Rule," which was first proposed in 2020 to require financial institutions to collect information on transactions of $250 or more, decreasing that threshold from $3,000.

In a filing, FinCEN said it was withdrawing the proposed rule because more than five years had passed, the convertible virtual currency industry had changed and additional public input would be helpful.

"The Agencies believe that any future, potential amendments to the Recordkeeping Rule and Travel Rule related to CVC should be considered in light of these recent developments to ensure that such amendments are fit for purpose," FinCEN said.

When the rule was proposed, FinCEN had argued that bad actors were increasingly using smaller transactions to move illicit funds. However, crypto groups, like Coin Center, had warned that lowering the threshold from $3,000 to $250 would mean that more ordinary transactions would be subject to "warrantless financial surveillance."

In a blog on Friday, Coin Center said it was happy to see the rule proposal rescinded, along with another rule proposal that was withdrawn last month that would have imposed reporting and recordkeeping requirements on transactions involving self-hosted wallets.

"This follows Treasury’s withdrawal last month of the proposed 'unhosted wallet' and cryptocurrency mixing rules, both of which Coin Center had also opposed," the organization said. 'Taken together, these withdrawals close the books on several long-pending proposals that would have expanded the Bank Secrecy Act’s already extensive financial surveillance regime."

Crypto Council for Innovation CEO Ji Hun Kim said in a statement that withdrawing the travel rule was the "right call."

Greece Passes Crypto Tax Law With 10% Gains Levy, 15% on Staking and DeFi

Greece's parliament passed a bill Thursday imposing a 10% capital-gains tax on cryptocurrency profits, marking a shift from a proposal discussed in October to legislation approved by lawmakers.

The measure also puts a 15% tax on income from staking, crypto lending and decentralized finance, or DeFi.

The legislation’s crypto provisions sit inside a wider Finance Ministry package dealing with debt servicing, financial oversight, and government accounting, with the new crypto income-tax rules scheduled for January 1, 2027.

Individual investors with annual net crypto gains of €500 ($557) or less will be exempt. Cross that threshold, though, and the 10% tax applies to the entire gain, rather than just the amount over €500 ($557).

For example, an investor with €600 ($669) in net gains would face a €60 ($67) tax under the new rate, not €10 ($11). The rules generally calculate gains by subtracting the acquisition cost from the value received when the crypto is disposed of.

Selling Bitcoin for euros is one taxable event, but spending crypto on goods or services can also count as a disposal. Investors will need transaction records to establish what they paid for their assets and what those assets were worth when sold or spent.

Yield from staking, lending, and providing liquidity to DeFi protocols is treated differently. Greece will classify those returns as interest income and tax them at 15%, according to the Finance Ministry.

Qualifying crypto losses exceeding €500 ($557) can be carried forward for five years and offset against future crypto gains. The bill also gives investors a 12-month window to voluntarily declare previously unreported crypto income without late-filing penalties or interest, provided they meet its conditions and pay within 60 days of filing.

The passage follows an October public consultation on the proposal.The draft proposed a 10% rate that was already lower than the 15% levy Greek officials had discussed in June.

Athens says exchanging one cryptocurrency for another should not itself trigger a capital-gains tax. But the wording lawmakers approved may not make that exclusion as clear as the government intended.

A review by parliament's Scientific Service flagged the bill's broad language covering disposals made "for consideration." That phrase could include token-to-token trades, the review said, even though the accompanying explanation says such swaps should not create taxable gains.

The reviewers recommended an explicit exemption and suggested clearer rules for exchanges involving stablecoins and electronic-money tokens. Those details matter for traders who rotate between assets without cashing out to fiat currency.

Greece is also preparing the administrative rules needed to put the tax into practice. A senior Finance Ministry official told Reuters that guidance on transaction transparency and tax audits would be issued in the coming weeks.

The government has not estimated how much the tax will raise. Officials say the market is difficult to measure because many Greek crypto investors use trading platforms based overseas.

The Supreme Court on Friday afternoon added a new case to its oral argument docket for the 2026-27 term, granting a petition for review filed by Goldman Sachs Bank in a case over whether and when a bankruptcy court can override the Federal Arbitration Act and refuse to honor an agreement to arbitrate disputes.

The dispute in which the justices agreed to weigh in, Goldman Sachs Bank USA v. Brown, began three years ago, when Rhea Brown and Gregory Maze filed for bankruptcy. Both listed credit card debts with Goldman Sachs Bank as one of their debts.

The Bankruptcy Code imposes an automatic stay, which (among other things) bars creditors from attempting to collect debts from someone who has filed for bankruptcy. Because Goldman Sachs, according to Maze and Brown, repeatedly continued to try to seek payment of their credit card debts, they filed an adversary proceeding (that is, a separate lawsuit within the broader bankruptcy case) against the bank, in which they contended that the bank had willfully violated the automatic stay provision.

Pointing to an arbitration clause in their credit card agreements, Goldman Sachs asked the bankruptcy court to put the adversary proceeding on hold and require Brown and Maze to arbitrate their dispute. The bankruptcy court denied that request, and both the federal district court and the U.S. Court of Appeals for the 4th Circuit upheld that ruling. A divided three-judge panel of the 4th Circuit concluded that requiring Brown and Maze to arbitrate their claim “would interfere and conflict with the strong and established policies and purposes of the Bankruptcy Code.”

Goldman Sachs came to the Supreme Court in June, asking the justices to take up its case. It told the court that the 4th Circuit’s ruling squarely conflicted with a decision by the U.S. Court of Appeals for the 2nd Circuit. Moreover, it added, “[t]he practical stakes of these issues are enormous” because of the ubiquity of arbitration clauses in consumer credit agreements. The 4th Circuit’s decision, it said, “imperils the enforceability of arbitration agreements in many of them.”

After considering Goldman Sachs’ petition for review at four consecutive conferences, the justices granted it on Friday afternoon. The case likely will be argued sometime next year, with a decision to follow by late June or early July.

New Federal Reserve Research Finds Cutting Merchant Swipe Fees Even Hurts Consumers Who Don’t Earn Rewards

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

You can earn no miles, no points and no cash back from your credit card and still lose if the government cuts credit card merchant swipe fees. Research from two Philadelphia Federal Reserve economists explains why: the money banks collect when you spend helps pay for better borrowing terms. That’s a problem for the retailers’ argument that consumers who don’t collect rewards are simply paying for everyone else’s vacations. I wrote this week about why the legislation threatening card rewards isn’t dead, even with Dick Durbin retiring and Roger Marshall losing his Senate seat. The political sales pitch is that reducing merchants’ payment costs saves consumers money. That isn’t true and has never been true anywhere in the world it’s been tried. It’s cover for retailers who want the benefits of accepting credit cards, but think the government should require the service be offered to them for less money. But credit cards aren’t just a convenient way to make purchases. They also provide financing for those purchases, and the merchant fee helps support that. The June 26 paper was highlighted in a new Bank of Japan summary. In the data, rewards cards have higher credit limits. Cards without rewards have lower interest rates despite higher default rates. They argue that rewards make repayment more attractive because default means losing access to future rewards. And when a bank keeps interchange instead of paying rewards, competition encourages it to offer better rates and limits to attract the customer’s business. They find that both rewards and non-rewards cardholders gain, even after accounting for higher retail prices.
