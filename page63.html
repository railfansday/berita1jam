Harvard GSD students propose alternatives to Trump's arch

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Images by Jeremy Dvorak and Jake Geitner courtesy The Cultural Landscape Foundation

Landscape architecture students at Harvard’s Graduate School of Design have designed two proposals for Memorial Circle in Washington, DC, the site of US president Donald Trump‘s proposed triumphal arch.

Both designs feature expansive pools of water that cover the current vehicular roundabout at Memorial Circle, and emphasise connectivity to the city’s historic processional axis and straight sightlines to the Lincoln Memorial and Arlington Cemetery.

The students, Jeremy Dvorak and Jake Geitner, worked with Washington DC non profit organisation The Cultural Landscape Foundation (TCLF) on the designs. The aim was to engage the public in the design process, as the team views the government did not properly open the arch up for review.

“By recognising Memorial Circle’s implicit expressions of monumentality, this pair of counter proposals asserts design’s capacity to critically engage history, accommodate public life, and advocate on behalf of our shared cultural landscape,” said Dvorak and Geitner.

The project began after Dvork and Geitner attended a seminar at the GSD taught by TCLF founding partner Charles A Birnbaum.

“As graduate students in landscape architecture, we took Birnbaum’s course to ground our emerging practices in critical historical inquiry and public participation,” they said.

“The current administration’s assertion of authority over national memory presented an opportunity to put these convictions into practice.”

The first proposal treats Memorial Circle as “a void along the historic axis between Washington and Arlington”. The designers chose to fill the central roundabout circle, currently covered by grass, with a low-lying pool of water.

A natural stone perimeter surrounds the pool and is inscribed with words of “belonging and unity”.

It’s design is “restrained” according to the team, and primarily meant to be viewed from a vehicle while passing through the traffic circle.

The second proposal does away with the traffic circle entirely and instead inserts a straight thoroughfare to Arlington Memorial Bridge.

Two circular fountains would be located on either side of the road. Additionally, a large pool of water, mirroring the perimeter of the former traffic circle, would fill up the site during commemorative events when the bridge is closed.

It draws upon previous historical proposals for Memorial Circle and seeks to pay homage to the precedent of the site.

“[The fountain] geometries arise from the site’s historic proposals, combining concentric circles, intersecting alignments, and formal planting into a composition that acknowledges history whilst creating its own present identity,” said the designers.

“In this way the landscape becomes a physical record of its own change.”

Trump’s proposed arch was announced late last year with renderings by US studio Harrison Design. It features gilded statues and a neoclassical design and has been dubbed the “Arc de Trump” – a play on the Arc de Triomphe in Paris.

Construction on the arch was set to begin in September with the government barreling ahead, but it was halted in early November by Judge Tanya S Chutkan.

Trump has claimed the arch will serve as a “military complex” with snipers in an attempt to use national security as a pretext for construction.

Many take issue with the arch blocking views to adjacent Arlington Cemetery, including members of the Kennedy family, who filed a lawsuit to block construction, claiming that the arch would cast shadows over the grave of president John F Kennedy.

The US Commission of Fine Arts (CFA), which reviews projects in the capital city, approved the project in May.

The proposals also follow recent efforts by the legislature to change Section 106 of the National Historic Preservation Act, which requires federal agencies to consider the impact of proposed projects on historic properties.

Earlier in November, protesters gathered in Washington DC in opposition of the administration’s proposed rewrite of these protections. They are couched within the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act (BAAJA) bill recently introduced to Congress.

U.S. export controls and Nvidia’s leadership transition remain key watchpoints

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Nvidia’s solid third-quarter results will no doubt reassure Gulf investors that the U.S. chipmaking giant is on course to maintain a healthy growth trajectory despite the resignation of Jensen Huang.

Nvidia reported revenue of $116 billion for the three months to October, up 103% from a year ago, and above Wall Street estimates of $109 billion. It has beaten estimates in each of the last five quarters.

For the Gulf states, which are betting big on AI to help move their economies away from oil and rely on Nvidia’s advanced chips to achieve that, the company’s future success is critical.

Huang’s affair with a woman suspected of being a Chinese spy has heightened political pressure to tighten export controls amid long-running concerns in the U.S. that China’s access to advanced American AI chips could undermine national security.

It is also likely to sharpen scrutiny of Nvidia’s future dealings with the Gulf as part of a broader effort to prevent China from accessing banned American technology via intermediaries.

Such concerns have previously made securing Nvidia’s advanced chips an uphill battle for regional AI champions such as PIF-owned HUMAIN and Abu Dhabi-backed G42.

In July, the U.S. Department of Commerce loosened export restrictions on the UAE so that approved government agencies and entities—including its tech champion G42—can receive advanced computing hardware, including from Nvidia, under broader license exceptions. The new rule set no caps on how many chips or other sophisticated goods can be sold to those entities.

Democratic China hawks have frequently raised the alarm over the large-scale sales of AI chips to the Gulf region, arguing they could give China backdoor access to U.S. technology. Last May, Democratic senators demanded that AI chip deals with Saudi Arabia and the UAE contain adequate guardrails to prevent the leakage of sensitive technology to China.

Both Gulf countries maintain strong commercial ties to Chinese tech entities.

In September, Chinese tech giant Alibaba Cloud announced plans to ramp up its data center capacity and expand localized AI services across the UAE. Meanwhile, in Riyadh, Lenovo unveiled its first laptop made in Saudi Arabia at its sprawling 200,000-square-meter manufacturing facility, developed as part of a $2 billion partnership with Alat, a subsidiary of Saudi Arabia’s Public Investment Fund (PIF).

In its first policy response to the Nvidia scandal, on Monday the U.S. Commerce Department introduced an interim rule requiring licences for global transfers of AI chips and servers to companies in 22 embargoed countries, including China. The move aims to close a loophole that has allowed Chinese companies to lease Nvidia’s computing power from locations in other countries.

With Democrats holding majorities in both chambers of Congress from January next year, increased congressional scrutiny of Gulf AI deals is likely.

More broadly, the Gulf’s exposure to the volatility in AI stock prices has been further rattled by Jensen’s resignation amid a broader sell-off in AI tech stocks over the past few months, on the back of valuation and investment bubble concerns.

While not all Gulf funds disclose their individual investments, publicly available filings suggest that Gulf sovereign wealth funds, such as Mubadala, have relatively limited direct exposure to Nvidia shares compared to the scale of their investments in other tech firms.

According to the consultancy, Global SWF, Mubadala ranks among the top institutional backers of AI worldwide.

MGX, an AI and advanced tech investment firm backed by Mubadala, holds stakes and has participated in funding rounds for OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI, Databricks, GlobalFoundries, Isomorphic Labs, and SpaceX.

Saudi’s PIF-backed AI venture HUMAIN invested $3 billion in xAI’s $20 billion Series E round in February, shortly before it merged with SpaceX.

Global SWF estimates that Mubadala’s portfolio may have declined by $17.5 billion since the onset of the AI selloff at the end of June, largely driven by a more than 50% drop in chipmaker GlobalFoundries’ share price. PIF, meanwhile, is estimated to have suffered a $5.6 billion decline in the value of its AI-related holdings, largely attributable to its stake in SpaceX.

“Indirectly and strategically, Gulf wealth funds are exposed to the current market turbulence,” Sam Winter-Levy, a senior fellow in the Technology and International Affairs program at the Carnegie Endowment for International Peace, told me.

“MGX and other Gulf vehicles hold large stakes in AI labs, data center platforms, and other companies whose valuations depend on the AI boom—and Nvidia, of course, is central to that boom. The Gulf’s national AI strategies are built on Nvidia hardware. I think a prolonged loss of confidence in Nvidia would hit the Gulf relatively hard.”

In a research note published on Monday, London-headquartered Capital Economics warned that the ongoing shakeout in the AI sector is setting the stage for a larger downturn in the U.S. equity market over the next year.

With Nvidia considered a bellwether for the AI industry, its results should help to calm the markets and buoy investor sentiment ahead of Anthropic’s planned listing on Thursday.

The Perfect 'Deadwood' Cast if the Show Were Made in 2026

📅 Nov 18, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

It's been more than 20 years, and Deadwood still remains one of the greatest shows in HBO history. Created by David Milch, the three-season series is set in 1876 Deadwood, South Dakota, and follows the titular camp as it operates as a lawless sinkhole of crime, gambling, and rough-and-tumble survival. The town explodes from a muddy, chaotic tent camp into a bustling settlement driven by a massive gold strike that draws prospectors, outlaws, entrepreneurs, and working women.

Filled to the brim with dramatized figures from history, Deadwood is a masterful mix of Western idealism and the true grit of surviving in a changing moment in history. At its core, the series explores how people piece together rules, commerce, and community from pure chaos when formal government and laws are absent. The heart of the show centers on incredible characters, brought to life by an iconic ensemble. So, what would happen if Deadwood were made in 2026? Though the show is untouchable for devoted fans, this fantasy casting honors the project's heartbeat with a new ensemble of actors who capture the characters' essence.

The true heartbeat of Deadwood came from the heroic man with the badge, Seth Bullock. He arrives in the lawless mining camp as a former Montana marshal looking to start a new life. Alongside his friend and business partner, Sol Star (John Hawkes), he sets up a hardware and general store before becoming Deadwood's first sheriff. Loosely based on the real-life historical figure, much of Seth's story revolves around keeping Deadwood morally afloat. Timothy Olyphant brought him to life perfectly, launching him into an archetype that made him famous. Offering a soft-spoken, gentle demeanor, Olyphant subverted the traditional Western hero. With a physical presence and fixed glare, Olyphant's restraint made Seth's moments of explosion feel earned. He masked a deep internal rage, but he was still quite stubborn.

As one half of the dominating duo on Deadwood, selecting an actor who has the same essence as Olyphant is required. A brilliant selection would be Logan Lerman. His career evolution aligns perfectly with Seth's core tension. He went from boyish charm with a quiet side hiding impulses within. In projects like Hunters and Indignation, Lerman mastered the art of playing characters who look unassuming but harbor boiling, dark intensity beneath the surface. He carries a classic, honorable leading man quality that evokes a trustworthiness that would allow him to lead a camp of chaotic and unpredictable characters. Even early in his career, Lerman excelled at characters with heavy dramatic undertones, namely The Perks of Being a Wallflower, but as he has matured, he has built the gravitas to take on a hardened lawman.

There is no doubt that Al Swearengen is one of the greatest television characters of all time. Some might have felt he was a caricature compared to more grounded characters, but Swearengen's quirks and eccentricities made him genuinely Western. Swearengen is a criminal operator, pimp, and master manipulator who runs the most successful bar and brothel in Deadwood. He is foul-mouthed, violent, and cold-blooded, yet he has a complex code of pragmatism, fierce loyalty to his inner circle, and a driving urge to guide Deadwood toward survival. He's a brilliant antihero who begins the series as the established villain, only to be outdone as new faces arrive. Swearengen is a masterclass in character, thanks to Ian McShane's pitch-perfect performance. Though based on the real-life figure, McShane made him larger than life. He blended a terrifying ruthlessness with theatrical, Shakespearean depth. McShane delivered Milch's dense, rhythmic monologues with a commanding cadence.

With a Machiavellian presence, McShane could have Swearengen command a room atop a balcony, offering a cold calculation like a mob boss. To play Swearengen, an actor with the same power and mastery of the text is required. An actor who loves a good antihero and could reinvent brutal, eloquent, fierce loyalty is Rufus Sewell. He has built a career playing characters who operate in moral grey areas. From his chillingly calculated performance as John Smith in The Man in the High Castle to the charismatic yet deeply flawed Hal Wyler in The Diplomat, he brings a dark magnetism to the screen. Sewell has an imposing, intense gaze and powerful vocal delivery that can pivot from quiet threat to an explosive outburst. As an accomplished stage actor, Sewell has the linguistic dexterity to tackle Swearengen's text. Sewell could easily pull back the layers of the monstrous man to reveal the broken humanity underneath.

One of the more tragic and human characters in the series was Alma Garret. She arrived in the South Dakota gold-mining camp with her husband, Brom Garret (Timothy Omundson), to seek their fortune. After her husband's suspicious death, which was orchestrated indirectly through camp politics, she inherited a rich gold claim, establishing her own bank and independence, as she was not going to be pushed out by men. She struggled with and overcame an addiction to laudanum, had a complicated romance with Seth Bullock, but ultimately formed a unique family when she married prospector Whitney Ellsworth (Jim Beaver) and adopted a young orphan girl named Sofia Metz (Bree Seanna Wall). Bringing a quiet, nuanced strength to a complex frontier world, Molly Parker was anything but fragile. She shone by showing Alma's resilience, then shifting into an emotional palette comprised of fear and grief.

At her core, Alma was a brilliant character because she celebrated her determination, savviness, and sharpness. She had to mature into an independent woman quickly, yet still had an inherent youthful refinement. An actress who could capture that same emotional intelligence is Hailee Steinfeld. And wouldn't it be perfect since her rise to stardom came in a heavy Western, True Grit? Even at a young age, Steinfeld proved she could handle the demanding, rhythmically complex dialogue of high-end Westerns. Alma needs a duality of high-society and internal gruffness. Steinfeld has shown those traits in projects like Dickinson and The Edge of Seventeen, playing women constrained by societal environments but with a rebellious edge. Having previously played in male-heavy, ensemble-driven environments like the Marvel Cinematic Universe and Bumblebee, Steinfeld could capture Alma's core arc of navigating a camp run by aggressive, dangerous men.

He may not have been the biggest personality on the series, but when he was on the screen, he brought brilliant intrigue. Hawkes played the perfect narrative foil throughout the show's run.

Over the course of the series, Trixie evolved, found her independence, and found love with Sol Star. Paula Malcomson made her a standout character with an impeccable performance. She smartly avoided the "hooker with a heart of gold" cliché by making her sharp, intelligent, and fiercely resilient. Her fight for her agency was what made her a profound character, and one to truly champion.

Fishback's greatest strength as an actress is mastering vulnerability wrapped in armor. Trixie's defining trait is her extreme defense mechanisms. When she launches into contextless, explosive tirades, it's meant to mask deep trauma. In The Deuce, Fishback played a part that mirrored much of this attitude.
