Vitor Matos admits ‘anger’ expectation after leaving Swansea City to join Strasbourg

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Swansea City confirmed on Wednesday that head coach Vitor Matos had left the club to take charge of Strasbourg, with the Portuguese manager’s appointment officially announced. The Swans received compensation of around £5m, with Leon Britton and Kris O’Leary taking charge on an interim basis, alongside former defender Ashley Williams.

Matos’ departure came as a shock and it has left sour feelings from Swansea City fans. Speaking ahead of his first game as Strasbourg boss, Matos has revealed his feelings towards his former side, and what he expects from the Swansea fans.

As reported by French news outlet ICI Alsace, Matos said: “It was a difficult decision to leave Swansea. I expect nothing from the fans but anger, and I understand it“.

He added:“It’s a really exciting project and I felt I could help move the team forward. It wasn’t an easy decision to leave Swansea, given the emotional dimension and emotional bonds I had forged there.”

It does not seem a replacement for Matos is close with Britton, O’Leary and Williams in charge for the clash against West Brom. Rob Edwards and Tommy Elphick are reportedly on the clubs radar as potentially replacements.

Swansea City will be frustrated to lose another manager just as they appeared to be building towards something special. The situation is reminiscent of Russell Martin’s departure, when he was poached by Southampton.

The Swans made significant progress under Matos, so finding a successor who can maintain a similar tactical approach should be a priority. Doing so would allow them to get the best out of the existing squad while limiting the disruption and adjustment period that inevitably comes with an unexpected change in the dugout.

Swansea City will be carefully weighing up a number of candidates, but there will be an urgency to appoint Matos’ replacement as quickly and efficiently as possible. The longer the vacancy remains, the greater the risk of disrupting the momentum they had begun to build.

Swansea City took a bold gamble when appointing Matos last season. He arrived with very little experience as a head coach, but the decision has proved to be an inspired one and could earn the club a substantial compensation fee at the very least.

With his departure looking increasingly likely amid his interest in the Strasbourg job, the Swans should have plenty of confidence in their ability to find a suitable replacement. The Matos appointment itself is evidence of their judgement, while their recent transfer windows have also been particularly impressive.

Overall, Swansea’s recruitment of both players and staff has been very strong and has helped transform them into a much more competitive side. There is little reason to doubt that their next managerial appointment could be another success.

It was just a few years ago that the balaclava was everywhere. Fashion people referred to it as the winter headpiece you absolutely needed. But now, I’ve noticed a new star, and that’s the snood, alongside the hooded scarf. Most people over the years have blended these three headpieces, but I’m here to inform the masses: you’re probably wearing a snood or hooded scarf now, not a balaclava.

Balaclavas are a full-coverage piece of headgear that wraps around the entire head, neck, and face (leaving only the eyes, nose, or mouth exposed), whereas a snood is a continuous circular loop or tube of fabric designed primarily to insulate the neck and cover the top of the head.

Balaclavas tend to be closer to the face, similar in fit to a ski mask, while snoods are loose-fitting and have a sort of cowl design to them. According to the winter sports community, balaclavas are better for skiing and extreme cold, but for most of us, we’re just looking to warm up a little with our winter accessories.

You can reveal the full GTA 6 map in minutes using this simple plane trick

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Travel  |  Read Source →

Clear up that fog in no time at all, but be careful.

GTA 6 has a huge map, which is around twice as big as the one found with GTA 5. That's a whole lot of Florida-inspired strip malls and alligator-soaked swamps. However, new players start the game in a fairly remote region, based on the Florida Keys, and the vast majority of the map is unavailable for perusal.

Fog is obscuring every part of the map that you haven't explored yet. New regions get uncovered as you travel through them. You could do this the long way, by following the story. You could also do this the slightly-shorter way, by grabbing a car and just driving north until the wheels fall off. GTA 6 doesn't have a system in place that automatically ups the wanted level as you enter new areas, so that's good.

However, the quickest way to unlock the full map is found in the skies, and not the roads. Long-time players know the drill. Jason needs to get an airplane.

Luckily, there's a plane just a few miles north of Jason's first house in Key Lento. Take a break from making home renovations and head to Southpoint Airfield, as seen in the map above. It's basically just north of the starting area.

Drive the western bridge that runs over the Shoeless Keys and then head west a little bit onto a runway. It's hard to miss. The area is marked as restricted, but it's mostly abandoned so there shouldn't be any armed onlookers.

Once at the airfield, look for a duster plane just waiting to be flown. Hop in and do your thing. Planes move incredibly fast and will quickly unlock all of those foggy areas. Just fly carefully and keep an eye on fuel.

You probably won't be able to get the whole map done on a single fly through, due to that fuel, but planes automatically regenerate at airfields as soon as one leaves. Fly back to Southpoint and start again, this time heading in another direction. You'll have the whole map uncovered in minutes.

Happy travels. The only real thing to worry about here is the Mile Zero Air Station at the bottom left of the map. Flying over that airspace will instantly generate a four-star wanted rating. This wanted level can bring police after you in helicopters, so airplanes aren't necessarily safe. Save this area for last and exit as soon as the fog lifts from the map. Mile Zero is home to some really fast aircraft, but it's likely not worth the trouble just yet.

StepSecurity raises $20M as hackers target the tools developers use

📅 Nov 20, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Its researchers helped spot a hijacked Microsoft package the same week it announced the round.

StepSecurity’s graphic announcing its $20M Series A.

StepSecurity, a Seattle-area startup founded by two former Microsoft engineers, has raised a $20M Series A led by Elephant. It protects developers’ laptops, code repositories, package downloads and build pipelines from attackers who plant malicious code in open-source software.

StepSecurity, a Seattle-area security startup, has raised a $20M Series A led by Elephant, GeekWire reported. Runtime Ventures, Inner Loop Capital and SaaS Ventures also put in money, taking its total to $23M.

The company guards the places where software gets built, from developers’ laptops to the systems that test and ship code. Hackers now often hide bad code in free, open-source software. Once it runs, it can steal passwords and keys.

“A supply chain attack used to take a skilled team. Now one person can vibe code it,” CEO Varun Sharma wrote in a blog post.

The round landed in a busy week. On 17 November, hackers posted two poisoned versions of agent-framework, Microsoft’s Python toolkit for AI agents, which gets nearly 200,000 downloads a month. StepSecurity was among the firms that spotted the code, which stole passwords and keys, BleepingComputer reported.

Tools for coders have been behind other big hacks. Binance says its $6.3B hack started with a fake job interview and a tampered developer tool. Last month, a tampered Claude Code extension reached up to 5,000 Cursor users.

Sharma spent about 15 years at Microsoft. Co-founder Ashish Kurmi also worked there before leading security at Uber and Plaid. They started StepSecurity in 2021 after living through the SolarWinds and Codecov attacks.

More than 20,000 open-source projects use its free tool, Harden-Runner. Its researchers also helped track the ChainDrop npm worm in August. StepSecurity says its yearly revenue grew fivefold in both 2024 and 2025, but gave no figures.

Next, it plans to guard the sandboxes where AI agents run code. Worms such as Miasma already target AI coding tools. Rivals are growing too: Belgium’s Aikido became a $1B unicorn in January.

Astronomers using the James Webb Space Telescope followed up on years-old observations to confirm the first recorded act of a giant star swallowing its companion. The stellar cannibalism occurred just next door, in the Andromeda galaxy.

For the first time, astronomers have caught two stars — a supergiant and a star slightly smaller than the sun — in the act of merging. After orbiting each other for thousands of years, the pair came together in just under seven years — a span short enough that researchers could watch the drama play out from start to finish. The discovery was described in a study published Nov. 18 in the journal Nature.

"Stellar mergers are very, very difficult to observe in real time because they are hard to predict," Viraj Karambelkar, an astrophysicist at Columbia University and first author of the study, said in a NASA statement. "However, we noticed something unusual was going on in 2023 and continued to monitor the system to see what it would do."

The stars' imminent merger was originally picked up by NASA's now-retired Near-Earth Object Wide-Field Infrared Survey Explorer — a spacecraft that was designed to spot asteroids but also discovered many other interesting changes in the night sky.

Researchers noticed that a previously stable binary star system in the Andromeda galaxy, the closest major galaxy to ours, had begun to brighten. Follow-up observations from the Wide-Field Infrared Transient Explorer ‪—‬ a near-infrared instrument at the Palomar Observatory in California — revealed that the galaxy had also started spewing dust. Karambelkar’s team backed up these observations with data from the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) in December 2025.

The researchers dubbed the binary pair "Bao" (after its formal name, AT 2025abao) and started keeping tabs. They found that sometime in 2018, the smaller component of Bao bumped into the surface of the supergiant Bao. From there, things escalated quickly. The stars started exchanging gas in a process known as common envelope evolution. The larger Bao dragged its smaller companion closer and closer, until it was finally sucked into the supergiant's core, releasing a gigantic burst of energy.

Views of the stellar merger in Andromeda (left) captured by the NEOWISE, SPHEREx, and James Webb space telescopes (right).

This dramatic culmination was "almost like a bomb exploding inside a living star," study co-author Kishalay De, an astronomer at Columbia University, said in the statement.

The researchers were surprised by not only the speed of this interaction but also its outcome. Bao could have easily exploded into a supernova, transformed into a neutron star or collapsed into a black hole. Instead, it seems to have settled back into a single massive star. Future observations will give scientists a sense of how the new, rejuvenated Bao is holding up after its act of cosmic cannibalism.

The team's analysis could also help inform future studies of supergiant stars and binary systems.
