Delta Air Lines Cancels 15 Routes From 3 Busiest Hubs: See Full List Now

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Network consolidation is inevitable at the best of times, and major carriers like Delta Air Lines are not exempt. External pressures such as high fuel prices have forced airlines to be more clinical than usual with route cuts this year, with the Atlanta-based SkyTeam founding member axing a combined 15 routes from its top three hubs by flights and passengers. These are Atlanta, Detroit, and Minneapolis.

Scheduling data from Cirium, an aviation analytics company, shows that Atlanta was the top casualty, losing six Delta Air Lines routes. It was followed by Detroit and Minneapolis respectively, with five and four corridors having been shelved between January and October 2026. This does not include routes that operated only briefly to serve special events.

Starting with Detroit Metropolitan Wayne County Airport (DTW), Delta's Michigan hub saw its first cut in May, when flights to Sacramento International Airport (SMF) in California came to an end. This corridor was followed by Reykjavík Keflavík International Airport (KEF) in August and Pensacola and Bozeman in September. None of these routes currently have any Delta flights scheduled in 2027.

Most recently, the airline ended its flights from Detroit to Bangor International Airport in Maine (BGR) in October. Nonetheless, it hasn't been all doom and gloom for Delta in Detroit this year, with the carrier announcing last month that it would be launching a new route from DTW to Athens in June 2027.

Wayne County Airport Authority CEO Chad Newton welcomed the move.

"We appreciate Delta Air Lines’ continued investment in Detroit and are excited about the new opportunities this service will create for tourism, business, and cultural connections."

Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International Airport (ATL) had the biggest share of Delta's route cuts this year, with six corridors falling to the sword. Most of these ended relatively recently, except the route to Santa Barbara Municipal Airport (SBA) in California, which Delta last served in January 2026. Otherwise, the rest were axed in September or October 2026.

Arguably the most interesting of the September cuts was Delta's route from Atlanta to Argyle International Airport (SVD) in St. Vincent and the Grenadines, as it lasted only one season. Elsewhere, September also marked the end of Delta's routes from Atlanta to Rapid City Regional Airport (RAP), Traverse City Cherry Capital Airport (TVC), and Vancouver International Airport (YVR).

Delta's Top Hubs In November 2026 (Cirium)

Meanwhile, Detroit was not the only airport from which Delta ceased flying to Bangor in October 2026. Its route from Atlanta to the Maine city also ended last month. As a result, the carrier now serves only two destinations from Bangor: John F. Kennedy International Airport (JFK) and LaGuardia Airport (LGA). In both cases, flights are operated by Endeavor Air as Delta Connection.

Delta unveils its biggest Delta One overhaul in years, featuring luxury dining, enhanced comfort and global expansion in 2027.

Moving on to Minneapolis-St. Paul International Airport (MSP), the Minnesota hub has lost four of its Delta routes this year. These cuts were split evenly between April and September 2026, with two cuts apiece. In April, the carrier cut its short-haul international leisure network, ceasing service from Minneapolis to St. Maarten in the Caribbean and Mazatlan in Mexico.

Meanwhile, in September, Delta made two more domestic cuts to its Minneapolis network. The routes in question served Williston Basin International Airport (XWA) in North Dakota and Wilmington International Airport (ILM) in North Carolina. As a result, Delta now has no flights at all to Williston, while it continues to serve Wilmington from Atlanta with mainline flights and LaGuardia with regional services.

He Was Spurs’ Record Transfer - Now He’s 29, Without A Club, and Worth Just £1.6m

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Just seven years after becoming Tottenham Hotspur's record signing, Tanguy Ndombele finds himself without a club.

Born in Longjumeau near Paris, Ndombele rose through the ranks at Guingamp and Amiens, before signing for Lyon in 2017. After enjoying two exceptional years with Les Gones, Tottenham forked out a fee worth upwards of £64 million to bring the midfielder to North London.

He went on to make just 91 appearances for the Lilywhites, before a series of uninspiring loan spells resulted in his contract with Spurs being mutually terminated.

Signing a two-year contract at Nice following his departure from the Premier League in 2024, he failed to impress in the South of France and has subsequently been unemployed since being released in the summer.

Having made his debut in Ligue 2 for Amiens at the age of 18, Ndombele was revered as one of the up-and-coming French talents to watch out for. It wasn't long before Lyon came calling, and he was shining on the biggest stage in his home country.

After making 96 appearances for the Ligue 1 giants and helping them reach the Champions League knockout stages in 2018/19, Ndombele attracted interest from several of Europe's biggest clubs in the 2019 summer transfer window. Tottenham reportedly fended off competition from the likes of Real Madrid and Juventus to secure the signature of the then 22-year-old, who was also beginning to establish himself in the French national team under Didier Deschamps.

The North Londoners, who had just reached a Champions League final, paid a premium for the number eight. The £64 million fee surpassed their previous club record signing, Davinson Sanchez, who had joined from Ajax two years earlier for around £38 million.

This substantial outlay came with hefty expectations, and the pressure this created clearly got to the player. Towards the end of his debut campaign in English football, Spurs boss Jose Mourinho publicly criticised Ndombele for what the manager believed to be a lack of professionalism.

"He's a player with great talent," Mourinho told BBC Sport in 2020.

"He has to know he has to do much better and know I cannot keep giving him opportunities to play because the team is much more important."

This unprofessional tag stuck to Ndombele, with the next few Spurs managers not taking a liking to him either. Ryan Mason, Nuno Espirito Santo and Antonio Conte all granted the player few opportunities to impress (barring a relatively promising 2020/21 season), while persistent fitness issues derailed his time in N17.

He was eventually loaned back to Lyon in an attempt to revitalise his career. After this stint failed, he spent a season each at Napoli and Galatasaray before Tottenham and his representatives agreed to terminate his agreement with the North London club in 2024.

Walking into the job market as a damaged asset, desperately in need of a permanent home, Nice offered him a lifeline back in France in the summer of 2024. Ndombele took this opportunity, signing a two-year deal with the Ligue 1 club.

Despite impressing in his first season under Franck Haise at the Allianz Riviera, the box-to-box midfielder struggled in his second campaign, making just 13 appearances across all competitions. This disappointing second year cost him a new contract, meaning he once again entered the market as a free agent.

He's yet to be offered a lifeline this time around, and TransferMarkt subsequently values the ex-Spurs man at just €2 million (£1.67 million).

The wages of every player at Tottenham for the 2026/27 season, including that of James Maddison and Dominic Solanke.

According to the Sun, Ndombele went on trial at Russian club Rubin Kazan back in October. The Russian Premier League outfit seemingly gave the Frenchman an opportunity to prove that he's still at the level, but there have been no reports that he's been offered a contract since.

The 29-year-old remains available, but the longer he goes without finding a new club, the more difficult it becomes to envisage him returning to the pitch professionally.

Google has bought Crusoe’s data centre site in southeast Wyoming and will build the project itself, Crusoe said on Friday. Neither company disclosed the terms.

The deal comes a day after Google said it would spend $17B on its first Wyoming data centre, in Laramie County. It ends Crusoe’s early work on the site, which included choosing the location, winning government approvals and planning its power supply.

Crusoe announced the project with Blackstone-backed Tallgrass Energy in July 2025, saying it could eventually grow to 10 GW. In June, Crusoe was being pushed aside after failing to sign customers, with Google raising concerns about costs and timing, Bloomberg reported.

Donovan Mitchell needs to shoot more, not less

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

If you've been online recently, you may have noticed that Donovan Mitchell has… pissed off a lot of Cleveland Cavaliers fans.

Twitter is ablaze with criticism. People want him traded. Some regret that he was ever traded for. Mitchell has become the crux of all problems; the shot-chucking 'dictator' who will never be the primary option on a championship team.

But everyone has it backwards; Donovan Mitchell needs to shoot more.

Mitchell's field goal attempts and usage rate are at career lows to start the year.

His assist percentage and shot chart are nearly identical to previous seasons.

That means Mitchell has less control over the offense than ever, despite some insisting that he's grown into a ball-dominant tyrant.

How did we get here? And why are fans feeling such a disconnect?

Mitchell has always been a scorer more than a passer. He currently ranks in the 41st percentile in assist-to-usage ratio — a stat that tracks how often a player is ending their possessions with an assist rather than a shot attempt or turnover.

This means that roughly half of the league's players are more likely to get an assist on any given possession than Donovan Mitchell. That can lead to games where it feels like Mitchell is hogging the ball while leaving his teammates hanging.

That gung-ho play style caused some frustration throughout last year's playoffs, and the complaints have carried into this year.

Fans want to know why Evan Mobley and Jarrett Allen aren't getting the ball. Why do their leads disappear so often? And why haven't the Cavs gotten over the hump despite a roaring regular season?

Some have identified Mitchell's lack of playmaking as the problem.

Which, in fairness, is certainly a factor. Cleveland has lost big games in the past due to Mitchell's shot selection. He doesn't always make the right decision with the ball in his hands — and that can be difficult to watch as a fan.

But we should reframe this discussion. Let's start here.

Donovan Mitchell isn't a point guard. And maybe, he doesn't have to be.

Shai-Gilgeous Alexander was in the 29th percentile for assist-to-usage ratio when the Oklahoma City Thunder won the championship. He's in the 0th percentile now.

The truth is, championship-caliber players are allowed to have weak points. You are not disqualified if you can't do everything on your own.

Giannis Antetokounmpo never learned how to shoot. Nikola Jokic never became an elite defender. And the New York Knicks didn't win a championship last June because Jalen Brunson suddenly became an elite playmaker — they won because they delegated more playmaking responsibilities to Karl-Anthony Towns — leaving space for Brunson to do what Brunson does best… score the ball.

All of those teams identified their star's weakness, and built a roster around them that could insulate them.

The Cavs need a similar solution. They need someone who can take playmaking off of Mitchell's plate.

Darius Garland was the original fit. And, for a while, he would thread the needle beautifully. He sent the defense scattering into motion while Mitchell would catch the ball against a defense already on their back feet. That dynamic led to 64 wins — but unfortunately, Garland couldn't stay healthy.

James Harden was the next option. He's been more available than Garland, which was enough to help bolster the team to an Eastern Conference Finals run.

But Harden's style differs from Garland's. He's slower-paced, favoring a heliocentric style that inhibits stationary spacing around him. The result is fewer chances for Mitchell to attack in motion, nerfing his greatest strength as a slasher who can attack gaps.

All of this has culminated in the Cavs (and fans online) asking Mitchell to be more of a playmaker. But that is a fundamental misunderstanding of who he is — and how you win in the NBA by playing to your strengths.

I want to be clear that I am not advocating for Mitchell to go full Dion Waiters and ignore the other four Cavaliers sharing the court with him at any given time. He still needs to pass the ball — and he needs to choose the right moments to assert himself as a scorer.

You can't win by playing like a bull in a China shop. There has to be a delicate balance between scoring in bunches and releasing control of the offense to someone else. That's the key here.

Mitchell will never be a point guard. Any system that asks him to make reads at that level will learn the hard way. You can't force a square peg into a round hole.

You can, however, build a competitive team that understands who Mitchell is.

We know for a fact that Mitchell can go nuclear as a scorer in the playoffs. We've seen it multiple times.

But who can grab the reins and facilitate those high-scoring performances? Who is the Karl-Anthony Towns to his Jalen Brunson in this scenario?

The Cavs are still hoping that will be Harden. Additional support from Mobley would be welcomed. Whether that will be enough is certainly worth questioning.

But here's another cold, hard truth: Making it to the top of the mountain is hard. If we're being honest, it's damn near impossible. This franchise had the greatest player alive for 11 seasons and only made it to the finish line once.

Cleveland would need a myriad of miracles to win a championship with this group. Even if everything went right, they could still fall short. That's just the way it goes.

That's why sticking true to your identity is more important than anything. Being a bad version of someone else will always be inferior to being the best version of yourself.

I don't think Mitchell will lead Cleveland to a title through his playmaking. That feels like jumping the shark.

The Cavs do need him to be better defensively and to make better reads offensively, but that doesn't necessarily mean relinquishing his role as one of the highest-volume scorers in the league. That's who he is — that's who the team traded for.

Mitchell needs to be a world-class scorer. Nearly all of his attention on offense should be centered on getting his shot. Passing will always come second. Winning or losing comes down to Mitchell taking and making the big shots. That was the primary difference between him and Brunson last season.

Of course, any team would prefer a superstar who is capable of blending elite scoring with masterful playmaking and impactful defense. But, you work with what you have — and the Cavs have a dynamic scorer, not a floor general.

Accepting Mitchell's true nature and having the supporting cast insulate him with their playmaking is the most realistic path forward. It's the only path forward.

Committed to the reigning NWSL champions for three more seasons, the soccer star reflects on leadership, life lessons, and a monumental move to a shiny new stadium in New York City.

“My hard hat, which is… something,” the Gotham FC midfielder says with a chuckle. She looks down to survey the rest of her mandatory construction-site-chic attire. “My gloves—gardener’s. My boots—too big. My vest—cool.”

Earlier that day, Gotham announced that Rose had signed a contract extension through 2029. In other words, she'll be back when this concrete floor is an actual soccer pitch.

We weave past steel beams and through what will eventually become Gotham's tunnel to the pitch. From there, the grandstands rise around us, a preview of the 25,000-seat venue that will soon welcome Gotham's fans. Rose poses for photos, holding a jersey that says “2029.”

A few days later, Gotham’s president of soccer operations, Yael Averbuch West, tells me she smiled when she saw the images of Rose in her hard hat. The look may be a departure from her usual uniform, but Averbuch West sees something fitting about it. As Gotham prepares to make another postseason run in pursuit of defending their 2025 National Women’s Soccer League title, Rose has become central to the club's plans.

“She has been part of literally constructing this vision with us,” Averbuch West says. “She has been an instrumental part of the vision of the club and our aspirations and our ambition.”

The roughly $780 million Etihad Park is being developed by Major League Soccer's New York City FC, which will share the stadium with Gotham beginning in 2028. It's a significant upgrade from Gotham's current arrangement at Sports Illustrated Stadium in Harrison, New Jersey, where the team uses the visitors' locker room and covers up the New York Red Bulls’ logos with its own banners. At Etihad Park, Gotham will have a locker room of its own, merchandise space in the team store, and digital signage and video boards that can be fully branded for Gotham on match days—no covering up another team's logos required.

When I sit down with Rose after our stadium tour, she tells me it’s difficult to compare the things that mattered to her as a rookie to signing a contract extension now. “I think of the amount of support staff we had, it was so few—how many people were dedicated towards ticketing and marketing and all of that,” she says. “I notice that stuff a lot more now than I probably did at the time, because you don't know what you don't know.”

Rose has come a long way since then. She won the 2019 World Cup with the U.S. Women’s National Team, earned Olympic gold in Paris in 2024, and navigated injuries and moves between NWSL clubs. She joined Gotham in 2024 and, after ankle surgery sidelined her for the start of her second season, returned to help the club win the 2025 NWSL championship, scoring the winning goal. In January, she was named U.S. Soccer's 2025 Female Player of the Year.

On the field, Rose’s influence isn't always captured in goals or assists. Gotham rookie Andrea Kitahata calls her the club’s “quiet momentum-builder and tone-setter” for how she initiates the team's defensive press and creates attacking opportunities that may not show up on a stat sheet.

Rose has learned plenty from USWNT veterans like Megan Rapinoe, Becky Sauerbrunn, Alex Morgan, Christen Press, and Tobin Heath, who showed her that there was no single way to command respect in a locker room. “Everybody led in their own way,” Rose says. “Everybody's leadership style might have looked different, but it wasn't any more or less impactful than the other.”

She initially thought surgery would be a straightforward fix. “I think I thought you get surgery to fix it, and when you're off the table, you’re fixed, and it's smooth sailing. And it was not smooth sailing at all,” she says. She pauses. “Yeah, it was really painful! It was really painful.”

Rose has become more conscious of how much she'll miss these moments someday. “I'm such a painfully nostalgic person,” she says. “It's like, debilitating sometimes, honestly.”

It's the everyday moments with her teammates that seem to stick with her most. “I was thinking the other day, having moments in the locker room where we have this shared playlist that we're playing, and we're all dancing to it. And I was like, ‘How many grown adults get to dance with their friends in the locker room?’” she says. “It’s little things like that that it's so easy to take for granted, because we get to do it literally every single day, but something that I think is just so special.”

It's also clear how much she values the life she's built at Gotham, and how much she wants to keep being part of it. “I love my life off-field. I love my life on-field. I think we have such an incredible group of players and staff that we get to work with and learn from each day,” she says. “I just felt so content, but also challenged in a way that I felt like I was going to be happily growing.”

For Gotham, the move to Queens isn't just about having a place to call its own. It's also an opportunity to reach a bigger audience. Despite winning two NWSL championships in three seasons, Gotham has struggled to fill the stands in New Jersey consistently. But when the club hosted the Washington Spirit at Citi Field, home of the New York Mets, for its Queens Classic in July, the sellout crowd of 42,175 offered a glimpse of what might be possible across the street at Etihad Park.

The move isn't without its complications. Gotham has deep roots in New Jersey, and some longtime supporters are disappointed to see the club leave. “I was a little sad at first that the team was moving,” says Lindsey Smith, 37, a New Jersey resident and five-year season ticket holder. “But this is so much bigger than a location to me. As someone who wants to make women's sports accessible, I'm very excited for Gotham to move to Queens.”

Smith’s friend Krista Tonnesen, 37, has been attending Gotham matches regularly since 2021. An Astoria resident, that has meant long trips home after night games, sometimes with 40-minute waits between PATH trains. She's thrilled to have Gotham in her borough, and hopeful that the shorter trip will bring more fans into the stands. “The players and the team deserve better,” she says. “I really think moving to New York will turn that around.”

Rose says the atmosphere at the Queens Classic, where she scored the game's only goal, left an impression on her. “To think that we could be playing in front of a crowd and atmosphere like that, every single home game, I think it'll just add that much more to what we're building,” she says.

Back at Etihad Park, as we make our way through the unfinished concrete hallways, I ask Rose if she can picture what it'll be like when Gotham finally takes the field here. “I see the vision,” she says.

The publisher says the currently distributed version of the launcher is safe to use, but players who opened the Windows client after September 27 at 15:41 UTC+9 are being asked to perform a security check. The updated PC client includes a security feature designed to detect and remove malicious files linked to the incident. Players are advised to close the game, launcher, and all browsers before starting the check.

Rooted in anonymous web forums and popularized across TikTok, YouTube, and Discord, the trend presents male aesthetic value as an objective, quantifiable science governed by facial symmetry, prominent cheekbones, sharp mandibular angles, and muscular, low-body-fat physiques.

While early engagement may involve standard self-care, such as adopting a skincare regimen, lifting weights, or addressing androgenetic alopecia, the subculture frequently operates as an algorithmic pipeline toward radical body modification and psychological distress.

In a clinical commentary published in the Journal of Adolescent Health, adolescent health expert Dr. Kyle T. The authors highlight that vulnerable boys and young men are increasingly turning to extreme, unregulated practices to conform to rigid physical archetypes, with severe repercussions for mental and physical health.

The researchers emphasize that looksmaxxing operates along a wide, insidious spectrum. At the lower-risk end, adolescents exchange tips on haircuts, dental hygiene, and fitness. However, the culture frequently pushes members toward “hardmaxxing”, invasive, unverified, or explicitly hazardous interventions.

“Looksmaxxing exists on a spectrum, and it is important that we do not automatically pathologize normal grooming, exercise, or appearance-related behaviours among adolescent boys and young adult men,” said Dr. Ganson. “At the same time, clinicians need to recognize when these behaviours become extreme, unsafe, or connected with significant distress and impairment.”

The rapid spread of looksmaxxing is accelerated by algorithmic echo chambers. Young men often place greater trust in charismatic social media influencers, manipulated “before-and-after” photographs, and anonymous numerical facial ratings than in qualified medical professionals.
