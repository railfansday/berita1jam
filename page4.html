Toei CEO Fumio Yoshimura Talks Global Expansion, GenAI Adoption & Entry Into Games

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Fumio Yoshimura, CEO of Japan’s Toei, explained how the studio is working with local partners to globalize its long-established IP, as well as innovating in areas including GenAI and gaming, in a keynote Forum session at Taiwan Creative Content Fest (TCCF).

In 2023, the 75-year-old studio launched an ambitious $2BN ‘Toei New Wave’ plan to boost its international revenue’s share of total income from 30% to 50% by 2033, which included upgrades to its studio facilities in Tokyo and Kyoto. Since then the company has embarked on several international co-productions and partnerships, started exploring GenAI technology and moved into production of games. Yoshimura started the session by explaining how the company has been introducing its popular IP to international markets. One of its oldest franchises, Kamen Rider, is currently streaming in Taiwan and other international markets, with India a particular focus. Watch on Deadline

“In 2025, we held an event to introduce Kamen Rider to India consumers. We got a very good response and signed a contract with a Disney affiliate to distribute the content. Due to its population size and the growth of its middle-class, we see India as a promising market,” Yoshimura said.

Kamen Rider was one of the first franchises that Toei globalized, working with U.S. partners to produce an English-language remake, Mighty Morphin Power Rangers, more than 30 years ago, with the franchise now handled by toymaker Hasbro. Both the Kamen Rider and Power Rangers franchises have been extended to toys, merchandising and live events.

In other IP extensions, Toei recently teamed with Sony’s Anyplex Inc and Godzilla VFX house Shriogumi Inc to launch a Kamen Rider anime label.

More recent localization efforts include the Voltes 5 franchise, which was first launched as an anime in Japan in 1977 and became a cultural phenomenon in the Philippines where its themes of resistance against a repressive empire got it banned by the Marcos regime. In 2023, Philippines broadcaster GMA produced a live-action remake, Voltes V: Legacy, which became a ratings hit in the Philippines and also aired in Japan.

“Simply exporting content limits its reach, so in order to break through constraints, we are producing remakes and localisations,” Yoshimura explained.

After getting started with remakes, Yoshimura said the studio is now also working on local productions based on original stories: “We’re developing local content from scratch, working with local talent to release locally and then sell globally. We believe this local first model will generate more sustainable long-term revenue than the traditional export model.”

Among recent productions, Toei worked with Taiwan’s Lou Lou Film Studio on Dear Stranger, directed by Tetsuya Mariko and starring Hidetoshi Nishijima and Gwei Lun-mei, which screened on the festival circuit last year. More recently, the company worked with Taiwan’s Wowing Entertainment Group on horror film The Rope Curse 4: Kuntilanak, directed by Liao Shih-Han and starring Lee Kang-sheng.

Yoshimura identified Taiwan as another key market for Toei’s global expansion, due to cultural similarities and the popularity of Japanese IP in Taiwan. “Data shows that Japanese films had a 26% share of Taiwan’s box office last year, the second highest share after Hollywood with 41%,” Yoshimura said. “Taiwanese audiences love Japanese content.”

Toei has also partnered with Thailand’s M Studio on a slate of Thai-language remakes and local features, and is currently working with both M Studio and Korea’s Showbox on a horror feature based on Japanese manga The Long Hair In The Attic, by artist Junji Ito.

The studio has also stepped into assisting the growing number of international productions now shooting in Japan, encouraged by Japan’s incentives scheme, including U.S. indie action thriller Iris, created by and starring Dani Swan (The Matrix Resurrections). In addition to providing facilities, Toei helped with casting for the film and assisted on the incentives application.

On the tech front, Yoshimura explained that the company is expanding the Virtual Production and motion capture facilities at its studios in Tokyo and Kyoto and also starting to use AI in workflows.

“We’re also experimenting with GenAI image generation,” Yoshimura said. “The Toei team is performing tests on practical AI applications daily; building tools to assist junior producers in estimating production budgets, and using AI in background artwork. However we believe human creativity remains the core of everything we do.”

Microdrama is another area that Toei has stepped into – producing Everyday Host Club, a 24×3 minutes anime series, and a 30-episode shortform vertical drama made for smartphone screens.

Gaming is another new area for Toei as it explores how to expand its IP across borders and formats. “Gaming is a global entertainment medium and delivering games to users worldwide aligns perfectly with our global expansion strategy,” Yoshimura said.

Toei plans to launch its first game, Hino produced by Ungloom Studio, in spring 2027, followed by Debug – Nephemee and Killa later next year. All the games will be distributed across platforms and consoles including Steam, Nintendo and Xbox.

The session entitled ‘Survival Strategies for Global Enterprises: Japan’s Toei on IP, Globalization, and Business Mindset’ took place as part of TCCF’s Forum section, which also includes sessions on Japan’s K2P Film Fund, Indonesian studio MD Entertainment’s scaling strategy, as well as panel discussions on co-production, mircodrama, AI adoption and IP exploitation.

TCCF is taking place November 6-9 at the Nangang Exhibition Center in Taipei.

Birmingham City have enjoyed a fine season to date after a slightly different approach in the summer transfer window. BCFC made seven new signings, two of whom had already been at the club, so it was a window of evolution not revolution as opposed to previous squad rebuilds.

But, with Chris Davies‘ side firmly in the Championship promotion conversation, the January window offers a chance to strengthen. The Blues can shift out some fringe players to make way for additions who can push them over the line in the bid to make the Premier League.

With that in mind, we put forward three players who should be leaving Birmingham City in the winter…

Somewhat surprisingly, Scottish winger Scott Wright stayed at St Andrew’s in the summer. The 29-year-old spent the second half of last season on loan at Dundee and the strong competition for minutes on the Birmingham City wings meant minutes were bound to be limited.

That has proven to be the case with Wright making only two Championship matchday squads and playing just one minute of senior football in a brief substitute appearance against Swansea City in the Carabao Cup.

Wright sees his contract expire in 2027, but to get back to playing regularly, he should leave BCFC in the winter. A return to Scotland or drop to League One seem like his most likely options.

Another player who has been on the fringes at Birmingham City is Lee Buchanan, although his situation is a little different. There was talk of a potential summer loan move as the left-back rebuilt his fitness after a serious knee injury, but he ended up staying put.

Alex Cochrane‘s emergence in the starting XI and the signing of Kristoffer Lund have left Buchanan as third choice in his favoured position. The Blues have been eager to be careful with his minutes given how long he was out, but by the time January comes around, he should be ready to play more regualrly.

There may be hesitance to cash in right away, so a loan feels more likely for January. Then, his situation can be reassessed in the summer, as he has shown he can be a Championship option before.

Last but not least is Marvin Ducksch, who has made eight Championship appearances off the bench. The German striker showed signs of promise in his debut campaign at Birmingham City, but a drink driving charge brought doubts over his future.

A summer move failed to materialise, but it is still widely expected that the 32-year-old moves on this season. A switch back to Germany is most likely, and could see Ducksch return to impressive form given his record of 71 goals in 138 2. Bundesliga appearances, and 34 in 130 in the Bundesliga.

August Priske is the lead striker at BCFC with Jay Stansfield and Luis Vasquez as the other options. Another centre forward may be needed if Ducksch is sold, but that seems like a logical switch.

11 most valuable EFL Championship players and how much they earn

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

In recent years, the Championship has become a league that plays host to some incredibly valuable players, which now eclipse some of the top leagues across Europe.

Parachute payments and the money that comes from television rights means that top second tier clubs can keep most of their best players, and they can spend millions bolstering their squads each summer - figures are being spent that wouldn't have been dreamed of 20 years ago.

Here, FLW takes a look at the most valuable Championship starting 11 in a 4-2-3-1 formation, as per valuations made by Transfermarkt, and what they're all estimated to be earning in wages per week, with estimates taken from Capology.

A sufferer of back-to-back Premier League relegations with two different clubs, Mads Hermansen hasn't had too much joy in the last few years of his career.

The Dane was impressive enough with Leicester City to earn himself a £20 million move to West Ham last summer, but he spent half of his debut season at the London Stadium on the bench, albeit he ended it as Nuno's number one, and has been between the sticks throughout the current season.

With a market value of €15 million (£12.5m) and an estimated wage of £22,500-per week, the 26-year-old still holds value, but it's now below what was paid for his services last year.

The abilities of Jackson Tchatchoua are a bone of contention amongst Wolves fans, and he is now second-choice to Kieran Trippier when both are fully-fit and available.

The athletic Cameroon international arrived at Molineux last summer in a £10.8 million deal from Hellas Verona, where he was dubbed the fastest player in Serie A.

For all his speed though, Tchatchoua's performances have left a lot to be desired, and in the end he was part of last summer's recruitment drive which really didn't go down well on the whole.

Tchatchoua still holds a €12 million (£10m) market value though, which could see him be worth something in January if the club wanted to move him on, but is earning an estimated £65,000-a week in the Midlands, which could prove more problematic to shift.

He may not be the best at helping to keep goals out for West Ham at one end, but Konstantinos Mavropanos has certainly proved to be a goal threat in the Championship.

The Greek centre-half joined the Hammers for £19 million in 2023, but was part of a leaky defence last season that saw the Londoners drop into the Championship.

And whilst the defence hasn't been exactly watertight in the second tier, Mavropanos has been showing up at the other end of the pitch - at the time of writing, he's fired in five goals and has been showing up the club's strikers in a big way.

An €18 million (£15m) market valuation feels fair, with an estimated weekly wage of £25,000 feeling quite low, but there's likely going to be transfer interest in January - Besiktas are already being linked.

Funnily enough, West Ham made a push to sign him late on in the transfer window, but with Wolves not wanting to sell to a divisional rival, and the player not wanting to play in the Championship, the deal was off - yet he remains in that league anyway.

Wolves had to pay £26 million for the centre-back from Girona after an appearance-based clause was triggered in his loan deal, and whilst he's an obvious good player for the Championship, a market value of €22 million (£18.3m) seems about right.
