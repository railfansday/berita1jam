Jayden Bogle should have been shown a red card during Leeds United’s win over Tottenham, says former Premier League referee Keith Hackett.

As Spurs’ three-game winning streak was snapped on Saturday afternoon, the biggest talking point came shortly before half-time when Mathys Tel was eventually forced off after being on the receiving end of a strong challenge from Jayden Bogle.

Replays showed the the Leeds defender had landed his studs on the Frenchman’s left Achilles, but - to the surprise of many - avoided any punishment from referee John Brooks.

In fact, VAR reviewed the challenge for around four minutes and ultimately deemed it did not meet the threshold for serious foul play.

Standard Sport understands Spurs are set to submit a formal complaint about the decision to not send off Bogle, writing to refereeing body, Pro Ref, for an explanation.

Meanwhile, upon reviewing the incident himself, Hackett believes the nature of the challenge should have resulted in a red card.

“This challenge should frankly always be red carded,” he said in a post on X, formerly Twitter.

“The Achilles is a very vulnerable area and the safety of the player must be taken into account.”

On the incident, though, Spurs head coach Roberto De Zerbi said he still had full confidence in VAR and insisted his side could not blame the officials for the result at Elland Road.

“No, I have big confidence in the referee,” De Zerbi said when asked if he would have wanted referee John Brooks to go to the monitor to look at Bogle’s challenge.

“Nothing changes if he goes to watch the clip or not. I think there was the VAR, and if they didn’t take the right decision, I don’t know. But we have confidence, we have to keep our eyes on the performance where we can improve.”

Tesla Stock Hasn't Closed at a Record in Nearly 11 Months. Here's How Its Past Record Droughts Ended.

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The EV maker has been here six times before. What did shareholders get once each wait ended?

Tesla (TSLA +4.23%) last closed at a record on Dec. 16, 2025, at $489.88 a share. That was 327 days ago, or nearly 11 months. With Tesla shares trading near $346 as I write this, the stock would have to gain about 41% just to tie it.

Long gaps between records are nothing new for the EV maker, though. Since its IPO in June 2010, Tesla stock has gone 300 days or more without a record close six other times, based on daily split-adjusted closing prices.

Every one of those droughts eventually ended. But what happened next was very different. Two of them gave way to rallies of over 600%. The other four were followed by a new drought within months, and twice within days.

And the difference between the two groups probably boils down to whether Tesla's profits were turning higher.

The first two droughts came early in the electric-vehicle maker's life as a public company. The stock spent 474 days without a record from Nov. 24, 2010, to March 12, 2012, and then exactly 300 days from April 3, 2012, to Jan. 28, 2013. The next two lasted longer, at 942 days from Sept. 4, 2014, to April 3, 2017, and 821 days from Sept. 18, 2017, to Dec. 18, 2019. Most recently, shares went 1,133 days from Nov. 4, 2021, to Dec. 11, 2024, and then 364 days from Dec. 17, 2024, to last December's record.

That makes the current 327-day wait the second shortest of the seven. The middle of the six past droughts is around 650 days, or almost twice as long as this one has lasted.

Of course, the drops along the way were steep. In the 2021-to-2024 drought, the stock's lowest close was around 74% below its prior record.

And in every one of the six, shares closed at least 31% under the old high at some point. This time, the low so far was $298.32 on July 29, around 39% below the December record.

Two of them ended in huge runs. Once the 300-day gap closed on Jan. 28, 2013, Tesla stock kept setting records until Sept. 4, 2014, rising around 652% in that time.

And once the drought ended on Dec. 18, 2019, shares climbed about 1,464% to the November 2021 record that started the longest wait of all.

The other four runs were short. The March 2012 record led to just 22 days of new highs before the next drought began. The April 2017 record brought around five and a half months of records and a 29% climb.

The December 2024 run of records lasted six days. Last December's record ended one drought and began the current one the same day.

In other words, a new record on its own hasn't said much about what happens next. For shareholders, I'd say this matters more than the length of any drought.

What the two big runs share is a turn in profits.

In May 2013, Tesla said it had become profitable in the first quarter "for the first time in our ten year history," earning $11 million. The stock gained about 24% the day after the letter came out.

The 2019 run followed a similar swing. The company made $143 million in the third quarter of 2019. That was a sharp turn from losses of $702 million and $408 million earlier that year. Even more, its net income hit $721 million in 2020 -- after losing money in each of the four years before that.

But the four short runs had nothing like that behind them. The company lost $396 million in 2012 and almost $2 billion in 2017.

And its operating income (profit from the business before interest and taxes) was falling in both 2024 and 2025, down 20% and then another 38%.

That trend hasn't turned yet. In the first nine months of 2026, Tesla's operating income totaled about $2.1 billion, down about 30% from the same period in 2025.

What does this history suggest for anyone owning the stock now?

I don't think 11 months without a new high is a reason to sell on its own. After all, waits this long have been normal for the growth stock, and every one of them has ended.

But the next record might matter less than what's behind it. One that comes with operating income climbing again may look a lot more like 2013 or 2019. Without that, a new high might fade as fast as the last two did.

Three goals from 1.09 xG: £20m star’s stunning form inspires Birmingham City’s rise to the top of the Championship

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Birmingham City defeated fellow promotion hopefuls Swansea City on Saturday, making it six wins from the last seven to move to 1st in the Championship. BCFC have put in some fantastic team performances in this run, but as far as individual efforts go, it is hard to overlook that of Jay Stansfield.

The £20m Blues man has moved back to striker in August Priske‘s absence for the last two games, and he has netted three goals. In victories over Millwall and the Swans, Stansfield has over-performed his xG of 1.09 by 1.91 (FotMob), providing a timely reminder of his goalscoring threat at St. Andrew’s.

Playing as an attacking midfielder, Stansfield has been more of a facilitator than a goalscorer in recent times, with his well-rounded game and fantastic work rate seeing him retain a key role in Chris Davies’ side. Blues fans needed no reminder of his importance, but the 24-year-old’s return to the scoresheet in the last two games has affirmed just how good he is as the lead man up top.

Stansfield is one of the faces of Birmingham City’s rebuild. He impressed on loan despite relegation in the 2023/24 season, and was the statement signing of Tom Wagner and co ahead of their record-breaking League One title win, joining permanently for a reported fee of up to £20m.

23 goals in 44 games across the 2024/25 campaign helped inspire City back to the Championship, and he followed that up with 11 goals in all competitions last term. However, a move out of his natural striker role has seen goals decrease for Stansfield.

Before November started, Stansfield had just three league goals in 2026. As said before, his work rate in and out of possession and his technical ability in knitting attacks together meant he has held an influential role regardless – for the most part. However, three goals in four days have made the Tiverton-born forward pivotal in BCFC’s rise to the Championship summit.

Stansfield was everywhere against Swansea City. Not only did he score twice, but he created two chances and one big chance, took seven shots and covered plenty of ground (FotMob). His heatmap showed that, despite playing as the traditional striker, he was still dropping deep and drifting wide to pick up the ball and keep the Swans’ defence thinking.

It was the sort of performance that everyone knows Stansfield is capable of. He led by example in terms of both attribution and ability as Davies’ side moved top.

Birmingham City’s faith in Davies has paid off. After doubts over whether he could lead them to the next level, he has weathered the storm and turned his side into deserving Championship leaders.

The Welshman has a tough call to make amid Stansfield’s return to goalscoring form though. At striker, it feels like his entire skillset is being used as he finds more shooting opportunities.

Stansfield is more of a scoring threat at striker compared to attacking midfield. However, the form of Swedish ace Priske at the top of the pitch can not be ignored, and he is not capable of dropping into a deeper role like his attacking counterpart.

It is highly likely that Stansfield drops back into the no.10 role when Priske is fit again. But, recent performances and historic stats suggest that Blues get their best out of their big-money star when he’s deployed at the top of the pitch.

It is a welcome dilemma for Davies to have, as Birmingham City enter the break on a high.

The prices of the best Garmin watches have risen considerably in recent years, but if you wait for big sales periods like Black Friday, you can almost always get a great deal on the brand’s products.

Black Friday is now just a few weeks away, and I’m expecting widespread deals on Garmin watches to start appearing soon. There are a few good discounts available already, like the Garmin Fenix 8 dropping to $742 at Amazon at the time of writing — but I think the best is yet to come.

I’ve been testing Garmin watches for a decade and tracking their prices during sales like Black Friday, and I’ve picked five I expect to see heavily discounted in the coming weeks.

Garmin launched two new entry-level running watches this year: the Forerunner 70 and Forerunner 170; the latter is a direct replacement for the Forerunner 165.

That means the Forerunner 165 is set to be a star of upcoming sales, and indeed it’s already reduced from $249 to $202 at Best Buy.

However, the Forerunner 165 has dropped as low as $129 this year, and that’s the price I’m hoping it will be available for in the Black Friday sales, so I’ll hold off for now.

The Forerunner 265 is an attractive AMOLED multi-sport watch that offers a more affordable alternative to newer models like the Forerunner 570.

Its lowest price ever is $299, a deal that popped up in last year’s holiday sales period, so hopefully we’ll see that deal emerge again in the coming weeks.

No one is offering a really good deal on the Forerunner 265 right now, which for me suggests it’s primed for widespread discounts soon.

The Garmin Forerunner 965 is another older watch you can expect to see on sale, with $449 being its lowest price to date, and what I’d hope it drops to this year, at least.

It’s a fantastic triathlon watch with maps and a bright AMOLED display, and at $449 it would be $300 cheaper than the newer Garmin Forerunner 970’s full price.

If you want a watch right now, the black Forerunner 965 is currently $479 at Amazon, which is a great deal, but I think it could still drop lower.

The Garmin Epix Pro has now been superseded by several AMOLED Fenix models in the Fenix 8, Fenix 8 Pro and Fenix 9, but it’s still an excellent adventure watch and likely to be hundreds of dollars cheaper than those newer models on sale.

You can already save $360 on the Epix Pro, which is $639 at Amazon, but I’d be holding out for a deal that brings the watch down to at least $499, the lowest price it’s been in the past.

If you want a more up-to-date adventure watch, the Fenix 8 Pro has only recently been replaced by the Fenix 9 Pro as the flagship option in Garmin’s line-up, so it will hopefully be getting big discounts in Black Friday sales.

The lowest price the Fenix 8 Pro has been in the past is $849, and that’s the price I think it will drop to during the Black Friday sales.

You can get the Garmin Fenix 8 Pro for $1,049 at Amazon at the moment, a $50 saving, but I certainly think a better deal will come along if you wait a week or two.

Who is Joe Milton III? Eagles turn to QB with 'cannon' arm strength

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Jordan Mailata might have been speaking for the rest of his Philadelphia Eagles teammates – and the public at large – when discussing his initial impression of quarterback Joe Milton.

"Have you seen his arm?" Mailata asked reporters on Wednesday, Nov. 4. "It is a (expletive) cannon, oh my God."

After taking a moment to apologize for his language, the left tackle went on to describe what made the passer such a singular player at the position.

"You see (the arm strength) on TV. You hear about it when he was going through the draft. But then you see it, and it's a work of art, man," Mailata said.

Since he was a high school football standout, Milton has been known for physical capabilities that differentiate him from even his most gifted peers. But after spending the bulk of his three-year pro career on the fringes of the league, the 6-5, 230-pound quarterback will get his chance to step into the spotlight Sunday, when he's set for his first pro start against the New York Giants.

Milton takes over for Tanner McKee, who started the past three games after Jalen Hurts was sidelined by blood clots that later moved to his lungs. Under McKee, an already shorthanded and struggling Eagles offense continued to flounder. The attack ranks last in yards per game (265.3) and 30th in scoring (17.1 points per game).

With the team's losing streak extending to six games, Milton is being called on to provide some kind of spark.

"With everything going on, he provides a good light to it," Mailata said of Milton.

From the time Milton started emerging as a highly sought-after high school recruit in Orlando, word began spreading about his arm strength.

As a 16-year-old, he threw a pass 75 yards to win a high-school competition, he said in 2024. At the Manning Passing Academy in 2023, Milton launched a pass more than 70 yards, with a clip of the feat quickly going viral.

For as easy as he made his throws look, though, college was anything but that for the quarterback. He lost his starting job twice – first at Michigan, then again at Tennessee after an injury.

But Milton put himself back into the NFL conversation with his final season with the Volunteers, during which he threw for 2,769 yards and 20 touchdowns with five interceptions. After again showcasing his unique physical tools during the pre-draft process, he was selected in the sixth round of the 2024 NFL Draft by the New England Patriots, who took fellow quarterback Drake Maye with the No. 4 overall pick.

Stashed as a developmental option, Maye didn't see any action until Week 18 of his rookie year, when he took over for Maye after one series and threw for 241 yards in a win over the Buffalo Bills. He delivered a signature moment when he repeatedly evaded rushers as he darted left before uncorking a 48-yard touchdown strike to Kayshon Boutte.

That performance caught the attention of Dallas Cowboys owner Jerry Jones, who three months later acquired Milton in a trade with the Patriots.

"I really have to pinch myself that we got him after the last game that he had up there, starting there for New England," Jones told 105.3 The Fan in Dallas in August 2025. "I'm just excited that we had a chance to get him. And he hasn't disappointed. ... We're just more and more encouraged by what we're seeing."

But Milton lost the role as Dak Prescott's backup to Sam Howell prior to this season and was waived in late August as part of the team's cutdowns to a 53-man roster. He rejoined the Cowboys two days later, however, when he re-signed to the practice squad.

When the Eagles signed Milton to their practice squad on Oct. 16, one day before Hurts was placed on injured reserve, the move might have largely been an afterthought to some.

Less than a month later, however, he's taking on a starring role for the team at a crucial juncture.

Eagles coach Nick Sirianni initially said on Monday, Nov. 2, that he was not entertaining benching McKee. Two days later, news broke of the switch.

Sirianni later chalked up the mislead to a matter of trying to establish a competitive advantage.

"Every second that goes by in a situation like that, that you can hold onto that information as long as you possibly can, is a little less time for the opponent to get ready for the game," Sirianni said Friday, Nov. 6.

With the news out, however, Sirianni went to bat for his new starter.

"He's talented and been here for a couple weeks now," Sirianni said of Milton. "Very talented arm, very talented athlete, can process and make decisions out there. Lot of good qualities he has. I like his confidence and everything that he brings, and it's been fun getting to know him and working with him."

As Chargers look for answers, who is to blame for 0-7 start?

📅 Nov 08, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

The Los Angeles Chargers' season has proven to be a complete failure.

The Chargers were predicted to be an above .500 team this season, but with a 0-7 start, the team now has very little wiggle room to even make that expectation a reality.

Coming off a bye week, the team did not appear to have taken any significant strides, suffering a 24-9 loss to the Los Angeles Rams at SoFi Stadium on Nov. 1.

The Chargers put together a promising first half, scoring nine points on three field goals.

One of the biggest positives from the half came in the final seconds of the second quarter when quarterback Justin Herbert threw a hail mary 54 yards to Ladd McConkey.

Unfortunately for the Chargers, McConkey was tackled at the one-yard line as time expired and went into the locker room without additional points.

The play didn't help create any momentum for the Chargers, who finished scoreless in the second half.

The Chargers will have the opportunity to turn things around against the Houston Texans on Sunday, Nov. 7, at home.

After seven consecutive losses this season, who is to blame for the disappointing start to the season?

There is a lot of blame that could be passed around regarding the team's recent on-field struggles.

Coach Jim Harbaugh, offensive coordinator Mike McDaniel and quarterback Justin Herbert are among the three in that order that should take on the bulk of that responsibility.

The Chargers moved away from being a pass-heavy offense that proved to be functional and efficient compared to what the offense has presented under McDaniel's watch this season.

Harbaugh added McDaniel to lead the offensive staff with the expectation that he would be an upgrade over what was already in place.

Herbert has been an above-average quarterback during his time in Los Angeles and he should be in the midst of a career year as a veteran and not regress with an uptick in interceptions.

As the head coach, Jim Harbaugh should take the brunt of the blame and responsibility.

He had the opportunity during the bye week to make much-needed changes to the offense but did nothing.

"We're aware of what the issues are, and we're the only ones that can fix them," Harbaugh told reporters on Nov. 1.

Harbaugh continued to get peppered with questions during the media availability but remained fairly vague.

"Our motivation is a dedication to getting it changed, getting it fixed," Harbaugh said when asked about keeping McDaniel.

While there was a pledge that some changes would be made, McDaniel remained as the playcaller through the bye week.

The Chargers have actually doubled down on their support and loyalty for one another.

Offensive lineman Joe Alt said the group remains bought in but admitted his displeasure with the results.

"Just frustrated," Alt said. "You want to come away with a win as a player and as a unit we have to be better. The scoreboard reflects that. … We have to score more points."

The offense has not scored a touchdown in its last nine quarters of play. It's a drought that spans 27 possessions.

As offensive coordinator with a clear directive from the head coach to score points, McDaniel's offense failed another test.

"We're not scoring enough points," Harbaugh said on Oct. 18 after a loss to the Kansas City Chiefs on the road.

McDaniel talked about collaborating with Herbert to help turn things around during the bye week. The coordinator called the bye week not much of a break, because "there were problems to solve."

The duo spent four hours watching film together throughout the week to "find spots to build on, where you can find spots to improve," but those again didn't necessarily result in touchdowns and a victory.

"In this league you have to produce results, or there's going to be heat, so I don't expect anything else," McDaniel said during a press conference on Oct. 29.

The heat is on, but it remains unclear when Harbaugh may be ready to make a change or if the organization will make it for him.

Last season, the Chargers had a middling offense under coordinator Greg Roman. It ranked 20th in the league with 21.6 points per game. The offense threw 14 interceptions and scored 36 total touchdowns.

This season, the Chargers rank last in scoring offense, averaging 14.1 points per game. The team is also averaging 310.9 total yards per game and has just nine total touchdowns through the first seven games.

Despite all the struggles Herbert has dealt with this season, he remains publicly bought into McDaniel's offense.

"I believe in him," Herbert said. "He's a great coach. He's very smart and it's on us as an offense to make those plays. ... We just have to make plays and score points and score touchdowns and we haven't done that for him."

Herbert completed 24 of 32 passes for 257 yards and two interceptions against the Rams on Sunday. He was also sacked twice.

"Whatever we've been doing hasn't been enough and it's up to us," Herbert said during the postgame press conference on Nov. 1. "Only we in the locker room can change it and work toward it."

Herbert has averaged 24 passing touchdowns and 9.7 interceptions per season through his career. This season has seen remarkable regression.

The star QB has completed 131 of his 212 pass attempts for 1,398 yards with just five touchdowns through the first seven games. He's already thrown 10 interceptions and has been sacked 23 times this season.

He's also second in rushing for the Chargers with 33 carries for 183 yards and three touchdowns.

"We don't really pay attention to the opinions of outside people," McDaniel told the media on Nov. 5. "People are saying whatever they want with whatever facts they have when we're not living up to expectations. ... Keep it moving."
