Time between pregnancies may shape how fast mothers age

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Each pregnancy adds biological age that takes months to come off, and birth spacing may decide how much a mother recovers before the next one.

Tests suggest that biological age rises during pregnancy and falls again in the months after birth. Researchers are now asking whether the time between pregnancies affects how fully those changes reverse.

Two Harvard researchers propose that the time a woman gets between pregnancies, not only how many she has, may shape how fast she ages for decades.

If the next pregnancy begins before that recovery is finished, the extra age may not be fully reversed, the authors wrote. The effect could then build with each closely spaced birth.

Stephen Albright and Jesse Poganik of Harvard Medical School and Brigham and Women’s Hospital laid out the idea in a review article. It pulls together studies from Europe, the United States, Israel and the Philippines.

The result is a hypothesis with testable predictions attached, not a new trial, and Poganik said the changes it describes don’t necessarily amount to damage.

“Our previous research has found that biological age transiently rises during pregnancy, peaking around the third trimester or at the time of delivery,” said Poganik, the corresponding author.

“If we accept the idea that these short-term changes in biological age occur, the question is how we can relate those changes to the overall trajectory of aging over the course of a lifetime.”

Biological age is an estimate of how much age-related change a body’s cells and tissues have accumulated, separate from the years on a birth certificate.

One way to estimate it is to read chemical tags on DNA that shift in a predictable pattern as people get older. Researchers call those readouts epigenetic clocks.

Run those clocks on blood drawn through a pregnancy and the number climbs as the pregnancy goes on, then partly falls after the birth.

Women on three continents show the same pattern, and so do mice. The authors wrote that this level of agreement is uncommon in their field.

The changes behind the number are physical. Blood volume rises by about half.

Cortisol, a stress hormone, climbs through pregnancy, and estrogen runs at roughly 100 times its usual level. The body also builds a new organ, the placenta, from nothing.

A detailed picture of the recovery comes from Israel, where researchers analyzed 76 routine lab tests from more than 300,000 pregnancies.

About half of those markers needed anywhere from three months to a year to return to where they had been before the pregnancy.

An aging clock built from those same lab results showed a dip before a large rise. In the first trimester a woman’s results looked about five years younger than her age.

From there they rose by about 20 years toward delivery, and it took roughly a year for them to recover.

Not every system recovers on the same schedule. Among more than 200,000 people, the reading from a clock built from wearable pulse sensors rose by about 3.5 years during pregnancy, then declined only slowly after birth.

Brain scans of first-time mothers showed gray matter volume falling across nearly all of the cortex during pregnancy and only partly recovering afterward.

The most direct evidence comes from a 2026 study that followed 130 women from pregnancy through nearly four years after the birth.

Among women who became pregnant again, the decline in their measured pace of aging was interrupted, and two other aging measures moved in the same direction.

Albright and Poganik call the time between a birth and the next conception the recovery window, and their hypothesis is simple to state.

Each pregnancy adds some biological age, and given enough time the body can undo at least part of it.

Under their hypothesis, another pregnancy could add to any biological-age increase that has not yet reversed.

Two women with the same number of children could therefore end up aging at different rates, depending on how far apart the births were.

The authors wrote that this is their central prediction, and it hasn’t been directly tested. No study has yet measured the spacing of pregnancies alongside biological age tracked for decades afterward.

Mothers of many children tend to die earlier in some populations and not in others, and the reasons remain unclear.

In the Finnish Twin Cohort, the risk of dying was highest among women with no births and women with five or more, and lowest among women with two or three.

Among adoptive parents, death rates declined as the number of adopted children increased.

A study of 2,356 women aged 35 to 74 linked more births to small increases in epigenetic age, measured well after their last pregnancy.

In 1,735 young women in the Philippines, with an average age of about 22, each additional pregnancy was linked to roughly two to three months of extra aging.

Spacing could explain why the count alone gives such mixed results, the authors wrote. Where women with many children tend to space them well, the count would carry little cost.

“It’s possible that this could change the overall trajectory of aging,” Poganik said.

“We know that some level of parity, or carrying a pregnancy to term, is actually beneficial to a person’s lifespan.”

The World Health Organization has recommended since 2005 that women wait at least 24 months after a live birth before conceiving again.

The advice rests on evidence linking short gaps to more preterm and low-weight births.

One review of 129 studies and nearly 47 million pregnancies linked gaps of 18 to 23 months to the best outcomes for babies. Gaps under six months were most consistently linked to harm for both baby and mother.

When a new pregnancy began six months after a birth, instead of 18 months, only women 35 and older faced a substantially higher chance of dying or becoming severely ill.

The age pattern fits their idea, Albright and Poganik wrote, because an older mother starts the recovery period biologically older, with less reserve.

Some factors were associated with differences in recovery.

Among about 120 women in one cohort, those who breastfed exclusively had a lower biological age three months after birth, whatever their body mass index.

A higher body mass index before pregnancy predicted a higher biological age afterward.

Sleep loss and chronic stress are suspected of slowing recovery too, though the authors found little or no research on either after birth.

Not every population fits. Tsimane women, who farm and forage for a living, have many children and little spare food.

Over a 10-year study their nutrition and immune markers largely held up however many children they had.

But those measures weren’t combined into a biological age, the authors wrote, so the cost they propose can’t be ruled out there.

Whether the extra age is wear, or the body remodeling itself for a demanding job, isn’t settled either. Clocks designed to tell the two apart are one way to find out.

Poganik said the work has a nearer-term purpose as well.

“What motivates me on a more near-term basis is addressing current clinical challenges, including the huge maternal mortality crisis in the United States compared with other industrialized countries,” he said.

“Any insights we can gain that can improve the health of mothers makes this a worthwhile research endeavor.”

The question underneath all of it is whether an incomplete recovery leaves a mother permanently older on the clocks, or whether the deficit fades given enough time.

Answering it means measuring biological age through several pregnancies, then following those women for 10 to 20 years, and no cohort has done that.

Until then, the 24-month guidance rests on pregnancy outcomes. Poganik said any advice based on a mother’s biological age would have to wait for that longer follow-up.

The review was published in the journal Trends in Endocrinology & Metabolism.

Roma director Tony D’Amico admits the defeat to Manchester United was ‘a learning experience’ for them, confirming the need to sign ‘a forward who can do damage on the counter-attack’ when leading.

The match with Sassuolo kicks off at the Stadio Olimpico in Rome at 19.45 GMT (20.45 CET).

You can follow all the build-up and action as it happens from today’s four Serie A games on the Liveblog.

The Giallorossi are still unbeaten this season in Serie A, but have twice thrown away massive leads in the Champions League for defeats to Real Madrid and now Manchester United.

That Old Trafford collapse from 2-0 up to lose 3-2 confirmed this is a recurring problem, so this evening the pressure is on to get a result against struggling Sassuolo.

“It is a problem we realised towards the end of the transfer session, that we lack a wide forward who has pace and can do damage in those situations on the counter-attack,” D’Amico told Sky Sport Italia.

“We will try to correct that when the January window comes around, so will try to give the coach this extra characteristic that we don’t currently have.”

It has been said that Roma lack experience in the Champions League and that cost them when their heads dropped in those situations.

“In an atmosphere like Old Trafford, the fans make a big difference and they helped turn the tide of that game,” insisted D’Amico.

“What I saw in both the games against Manchester United and Real Madrid was a great Roma side that imposes its style of play on the strongest opponents. There are going to be moments of difficulty in fixtures like this.

“I don’t think enough has been said, partly because we don’t like to court controversy, but the penalty for Manchester United was more than generous. So we did have a moment of difficulty, but that penalty sparked the comeback.

“As I said after the Real Madrid defeat, these are learning experiences for a club that has not been in the Champions League for seven years, it also teaches us what resources we still need to give to the coach in order to continue this progress.”

Gianluca Mancini is the latest absentee with a flexor issue that also ruled him out of the Italy squad for the Nations League games with France and Belgium.

He joins Devyne Rensch, Pierluigi Gollini, Mile Svilar, Paulo Dybala and Lorenzo Pellegrini on the treatment table, but the good news is the return of Manu Koné for the first time since his muscular injury against Real Madrid on October 14, even though he wasn’t called up for France.

“We speak with all the international coaches, Koné returned to training this week, we are pleased he is back and it gives us the chance to rest someone who played nine games in 28 days.”

Charlie Austin reacts to awkward Derby County, Sammie Szmodics interview

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Former QPR, Burnley and Southampton striker Charlie Austin has backed Derby County forward Sammie Szmodics after his tense interview with a local reporter last weekend.

It has been a turbulent start to the season for Derby, and Szmodics made his frustration clear after the 4-1 defeat at Millwall last Saturday when he was involved in a heated exchange with BBC Radio Derby's Ed Dawes.

It was another day to forget for Derby last Saturday as they were comprehensively beaten by Millwall at The Den, with three of the Lions' goals coming in the opening 17 minutes, and Szmodics fronted up to the media post-match.

However, when Dawes suggested to Szmodics that he would be particularly angry about the situation as he was 'John Eustace's boy', it is fair to say that the 31-year-old was less than impressed, responding: "Yeah, of course, because I'm a professional footballer that wants to win. I'm not John Eustace's boy."

Dawes later attempted to clarify his comments, stating that he was referring to the fact that Szmodics had played for Eustace previously, to which he replied: "Yes, I've played under the manager, I'm not his boy, but we're all in it together."

After spending the second half of last season on loan at Pride Park, Szmodics returned to Derby on a season-long loan in August, and the move will become permanent in the summer for a fee of around £2.7 million — or £5 million if the club win promoted to the Premier League — after an appearance-related clause in the deal was triggered.

Szmodics has scored four goals and provided one assist in 14 appearances this season, but he is set to be out for six weeks after aggravating a knee injury that had been troubling him for much of the campaign in the 4-0 defeat to Norwich City on Tuesday night.

Szmodics' interview has caused plenty of debate over the past week, and Austin has become the latest pundit to give his opinion on the saga, praising the Derby forward for his reaction to what he believes was an inappropriate question.

"They know they're not performing," Austin said on his Chaz Kicks Off! podcast.

"Eustace was the assistant manager at QPR when I went back there, but he was also in the Derby starting XI when Bobby Zamora scored in that play-off final to beat them 1-0 and Richard Keogh cried.

"Hear me out here, Sammie Szmodics saying to that reporter 'I'm not his boy', fair play to you mate, I've got a lot of time for it.

"The reporter was being an absolute one of them mate, let me tell you, suggesting 'you're getting picked when you're not performing because you're his boy'.

"Fair play to Sammie Szmodics for giving him a little bit back.

"Players know they're underperfoming, they don't need someone from the radio coming out with nonsense questions.

"He's had a little nibble back, fair play to him.

"The problem you've got is, you can only do the talking on the pitch, and that's what matters.

"At the moment, Derby are not talking, they're not performing, and they are in trouble."

Szmodics is one of a number of Derby players who have worked under Eustace at one of his previous clubs, and while the pair have a strong relationship, it is understandable that he took exception to Dawes' question after the defeat at Millwall.

After a 10-game winless run, the Rams lifted some of the pressure on Eustace with a 3-1 home victory over Bolton Wanderers on Friday night in Szmodics' absence, but they will still head into the international break sitting in the Championship relegation zone.

Delap’s Nottingham Forest stunners hand Manchester City third league defeat in a row

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Wait long enough, and results will come to reflect performances. Manchester City may have won their first seven games of the season, but they always looked vulnerable, and now have lost three in a row. Nottingham Forest may have been second bottom but, other than the twin curses of missing chances giving away daft goals, they seemed to be playing reasonably well. Put the two together, and the outcome was a dramatic regression to the mean. It turns out the stats were right, after all.

Liam Delap scored twice to remind people just what a good striker he can be. Oliver Glasner, after last week’s anger, can breathe a little easier in his job. And Enzo Maresca remains a paradox, a manager obsessive about control whose teams always seem oddly susceptible to direct running.

After last week’s defeat at Brentford, Glasner said he regretted compromising too much in the early weeks of his reign at Forest. Although he gave no precise details as to what that might mean, he elaborated a little on Thursday, speaking of the need for “trust and respect” in the right balance and suggesting there’d been too much trust and not enough respect this season at Forest: too much Woodstock and not enough “high-performance nature of military.”

Glasner’s brave new uncompromising world was ushered in with just two changes to the starting lineup from the defeat at Brentford last week, Ola Aina and Dan N’Doye dropping out for Daniel Muñoz and Ryan Yates, who both came on at half-time last week. It may have been an underwhelming revolution but it soon worked, Jair Cunha dispossessing Josko Gvardiol and running through to opening the scoring after six minutes, Forest’s first home goal since Morgan Gibbs-White’s strike against Bournemouth 34 minutes into the final game of last season, a drought of 510 minutes.

No side this season had underperformed their xG by such a margin as Forest; they began the weekend third in Opta’s expected table but second bottom of the actual table. To an extent that is a matter of misfortune: defeat at Brentford last week, for instance, was a result of a cross and a shot that both looped in off Nikola Milenkovic; when you concede one shot on target but two goals, you know you’ve been unlucky. And when, on Saturday, three minutes after Forest had taken the lead, a Jérémy Doku shot hit Milenkovic and, rather than looping agonisingly past Matz Sels, cannoned wide for a corner, Forest must really have known their luck was in.

One of the biggest issues for Forest this season has been the form of Delap. A gruelling spell at Chelsea had seemingly sapped his confidence but, 18 minutes in, he surged from inside his own half, ran, as Jair had for the opener, between Rúben Dias and Marc Guéhi, and thumped his shot over the dive of Gianluigi Donnarumma. With confidence, it turns out, Delap is a very different player.

Guéhi’s form, meanwhile, is becoming a major concern for City. With Dias wary of Xaver Schlager running to his right and potentially becoming a passing option, it was surely Guéhi’s responsibility to close Delap down. As it was, the pair left a huge channel, inviting Delap to keep going to score his second goal since joining Forest. Guéhi and Gvardiol were both withdrawn at half-time.

Seven minutes into the second half, a long diagonal set Doku in behind Muñoz, and although his cross was half-cleared, Rayan Cherki slammed in a brilliant half volley. In a moment, the mood changed. All the anxiety stored up by two months of disappointing form bubbled to the surface again.

Forest may feel that, after the season they have had, they are owed some luck. They were nearly paid back a slice of that when a Yates shot deflected off Elliot Anderson and dropped on to the back of the bar. But still they had burned through a fair amount, with blocks, brave headers and clearances off the line when Gibbs-White set Delap away on the counter. A week ago, it would barely have registered as an opportunity but, self-belief restored, Delap swept into the box and drove his finish in off the far post.

Forest are out of the relegation zone and the idea that City might challenge Arsenal for the title, even if there is no points deduction, is beginning to look a little fanciful.

Ibrahimovic even landed in the Premier League with Manchester United and showed just why he's regarded as one of the best target men in football history. While most of his opponents couldn't handle his power and dominance, he admitted that one did manage to ruffle his feathers and outsmart him physically and mentally.

Ibrahimovic not only went to war with defenders in duels on the ground and in the air but also played mind games with his rivals. It took a big personality to be able to deal with him and Italian football has birthed many gritty and determined centre-backs.

None more so than Giorgio Chiellini, who made life difficult for attackers with his persistence and never-say-die attitude. He made an impression on Ibrahimovic whenever they clashed as he explained back in 2016 (via The Score):

"For me Giorgio Chiellini is the toughest, and the roughest, marker that I have ever faced."

Being marked by Chiellini came as a nuisance not only to Ibrahimovic but many other forwards during the Italian's legendary career. With a six-foot-two inch frame, he combined old-school fashioned defending with physicality to boss the opposition.

Ibrahimovic added: "He is the one that I suffer against the most. It’s truly difficult to play against him, he does not give you space, he does not let you breathe."

When the Swede returned to Turin with Inter to face Juve, he ended up battered and bruised thanks to Chiellini. In his autobiography he recalled this while making an accusation: "He tried to provoke me, and then he tackled me from behind. That’s a cowardly thing to do. You don’t see the guy coming, and I went down in pain. I was in a lot of pain."

Chiellini spent the majority of his career at Juventus, winning nine Serie A titles among many other honours. He also enjoyed success with Italy's national team including winning Euro 2020 when he scored a vital equaliser in a final win over England at Wembley.

The 42-year-old retired in January 2024 while at MLS club Los Angeles FC and did so having cemented his legacy as one of the best Italian defenders of all-time. And he also is only one of a few who made life difficult for Ibrahimovic on the pitch.

Another former Serie A marksman who, like Ibrahimovic, didn't enjoy facing Chiellini is Edinson Cavani, who encountered the Juve legend while at Napoli. The Uruguayan became one of the most prolific forwards in the modern era of Italian football.

Cavani opined while at Manchester United in September 2021: "The most difficult defender I’ve come up against was Giorgio Chiellini. We’ve played against each other in many games, and we’ve competed against each other all over the pitch, so yes I can tell you that Giorgio Chiellini has been the toughest defender I’ve had to face."

That's two of the very best strikers to grace Serie A who both acknowledge Chiellini as their toughest opponent. Had he moved away from Italy to another top European league such as the Premier League, it would have been interesting to see how he fared.
