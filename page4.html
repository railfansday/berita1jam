XBOX CEO Casually Reveals Wolfenstein 3 In Production

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Daniel is a lifelong gamer with a love for various genres and gameplay styles who enjoys exploring games and playing from various angles.

The XBOX brand, through its various acquisitions, has managed to obtain ownership of some of the oldest, arguably most important video game IPs. One of those IPs is Wolfenstein, the series that gave us the first true FPS with the release of Wolfenstein 3D all the way back in 1992. Since 2014, the Wolfenstein IP has been in the hands of MachineGames, with Bethesda serving as publisher. Microsoft owns ZeniMax, Zennimax owns Bethesda, and so XBOX usually gets first dibs when Wolfenstein comes knocking.

You might not think it, but a serious shooter like Wolfenstein 3D was a huge influence, according to Randy Pitchford.

It’s rather fitting, then, that someone high up the chain of command in XBOX would have the privilege of divulging new Wolfenstein news. Though, even then, I certainly wouldn’t expect the someone in question to be its literal CEO, nor would I expect said news to drop on a random Thursday, but let nobody say they can’t make things interesting.

On her personal X, formerly Twitter account, XBOX CEO Asha Sharma rather casually announced that MachineGames has officially begun production on the next mainline Wolfenstein game, the first since Wolfenstein II: The New Colossus was released in 2017. Obviously, since production has just started, details are basically non-existent, but both Sharma and MachineGames’ own X account did give us a couple of tidbits to chew on.

Sharma’s tweet keeps the announcement fairly quick and concise, reading, “Wolfenstein 3 is officially in production at @MachineGames. One of gaming's most important franchises, and a team that has done remarkable work bringing it forward. Looking forward to sharing more.”

One of gaming's most important franchises, and a team that has done remarkable work bringing it forward.

Assuming the game will have some manner of subtitle like its predecessors, Sharma didn’t divulge what it was. What she did share, though, was a screenshot of the game’s apparent script. This screenshot is apparently set during the game’s first chapter in a locale called “Nazitraz,” presumably a prison of some sort, in which series protagonist B.J. Blazkowicz confronts a man named “Otto,” who blubbers in fear in his presence. Series mainstay Fergus Reid also appears to be present, but the screenshot cuts off before he says or does anything.

The Elder Scrolls 6 has finally had its title officially confirmed, weeks after it was not so subtly leaked to the wider public.

Shortly after Sharma’s tweet, MachineGames shared the same script screenshot on its own X account alongside the caption, “His reputation precedes him. Wolfenstein 3 is in production.” MachineGames’ post also added a photo of two actors in the midst of motion-capture work: Debra Wilson, who previously played Grace Walker in New Colossus, and B.J.’s actor, Brian Bloom.

Wolfenstein, as a series, has been pretty quiet for a few years now. As I mentioned, New Colossus was the last mainline game to release, which was nearly a decade ago in 2017. The series has had spin-offs since then, including Wolfenstein: Youngblood and Wolfenstein: Cyberpilot, both of which were released in 2019. The former was a co-op game starring B.J.’s teenage daughters as they searched for their father in France, while the latter was a VR game in which an unnamed hacker works to hack various Nazi robots. Unfortunately, neither of these games were particularly well-received.

Still, there’s no reason to be pessimistic, especially considering that it was MachineGames that brought us the unexpected slam dunk that was Indiana Jones and the Great Circle in 2024. I’d say that’s pretty definitive proof that the studio still knows how to put an excellent game together, and I’m optimistic about what’s on the horizon.

Incidentally, MachineGames is also involved in a creative capacity for the live-action Wolfenstein series that’s currently in development for Prime Video. Who knows, we might just have a one-two punch of new Wolfenstein media to look forward to in the not-too-distant future.

‘Boston Blue’ Brings Back a ‘CSI’ Legend, but Its Biggest Problem Remains

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Jeffrey is a freelance features writer at Collider. He is an MPA-accredited entertainment journalist and a Tomatometer-approved critic based in the LA area. He graduated from the University of Texas at Austin with a Bachelor of Science in Radio, TV, & Film and a Bachelor of Arts in Theater.

The hit CBS series Boston Blue just pulled off a huge coup in the casting department. Emmy Award-winning actress Marg Helgenberger, a television legend and veteran of shows like CSI, will return to the network in the hit Blue Bloods spin-off. Helgenberger will make her series debut in the upcoming Season 2 episode "Bloodlines" on November 13, portraying Gwen Silver, the estranged biological mother of main cast member Sarah Silver (Maggie Lawson). Helgenberger makes a great addition to the series and the overall Blue Bloods saga. However, despite the stupendous casting choice, the show's biggest problem still needs to be addressed.

Boston Blue serves as both a continuation and spin-off of its flagship series Blue Bloods. Much like its predecessor, it showcases a multi-generational family of law enforcers with the Silvers. The main connective tissue between the two shows is Blue Bloods veteran Danny Reagan (Donnie Wahlberg) and his son, Sean Reagan (Mika Amonsen, who took over the role from Andrew Terraciano for the new series). Danny uprooted his life to move to Boston to mentor Sean in his own career as a police officer, while Danny also receives a fresh start in a new city, seeking to climb the ladder in his career. Boston Blue struggles to find a balance between these two narratives.

A great detective is really just a personality with a method attached, which is why we keep remaking the same four of them. Here is yours, what it says about you, and the case for them being the best in the business.

You let people think you are not a threat and then you simply never go away. Columbo is the only major detective show that tells you who did it in the first ten minutes, because the pleasure was never the puzzle, it was watching a rich man slowly realise he is in trouble.

You are the most interesting person in the room and you know exactly how to use it. Rian Johnson built Blanc as a gentleman detective who is also an outsider everywhere he goes, which lets him be charming at people who would never otherwise talk to him.

Everybody tells you everything, and you have never once asked them to. Jessica Fletcher is a crime novelist from a small town in Maine who is so warm and so obviously trustworthy that suspects confess to her essentially as a social courtesy.

You expect to be lied to and you are almost never disappointed. Chandler wrote Marlowe as a man with a rigid private code in a city that has none, which is why he takes the case, solves it, gets nothing out of it and takes the next one.

As great as it is to see a multi-ethnic Jewish family celebrating Shabbat in a weekly broadcast television series, it's strange that the Reagans are constantly present for the dinners. The Reagans are Roman Catholics, and although they respect the Silvers' religious practices, they aren't Jewish themselves. In Blue Bloods, the Reagan family would occasionally invite guests to their Sunday dinners, but those were rare exceptions rather than the rule.

Danny and Sean appear to join the Silvers for Shabbat dinner on a weekly basis. It’s great that the Reagans and Silvers have a close friendship and bond, but they haven't known each other too long. Since they aren't related, it doesn’t make sense that they join the Silvers for Shabbat dinner all the time. Shouldn't the Reagans set up their own Sunday dinners instead? It comes across as if the writers are attempting to forcibly mimic the weekly family dinner dynamic from Blue Bloods in Boston Blue, where it makes less sense.

One area that Boston Blue appears to be working on in Season 2 is establishing the hierarchy of the BPD and City Hall, a narrative structure that the series has struggled with before. Thus far, the series has introduced the BPD's chief, Lanigan (Ron Lea), who has appeared sparingly, but the commissioner has yet to appear. Beginning with its first season, Blue Bloods did a much better job of demonstrating the hierarchy of everyone's roles in the NYPD, including Frank Reagan's (Tom Selleck) relationship with the mayor’s office, Erin working in the District Attorney's office, Danny operating as a detective, and Jamie Reagan (Will Estes) as a patrolman.

Across all these characters and subplots, the series presented a strong overview of the Reagans and their career dynamics; yet, in Boston Blue, it feels like we’re two seasons in and still playing catch-up. It derives from the series struggling as a continuation of Blue Bloods while trying to forge its own identity. Boston Blue shouldn't copy the weekly family dinners simply because the Blue Bloods made them famous. Although improvements have been made in Season 2, with storylines involving BPD’s superior officers, such as Chief Lanigan and Sean and Jonah's new sergeant, Betina Ryan (Sydney Ozerov-Meyer), it would improve the show to include additional storylines showcasing the command structure of the BPD, as well as Mae Silver's (Gloria Reuben) office and their relationship with Mayor Jim Shaw (Brian Goodman).

Boston Blue sometimes appears at odds with its format, trying to balance the traditions and characters of Blue Bloods to lure in longtime fans of the original series, while still maintaining strong narrative focus on its new characters and family. It's simply not necessary for the Silvers and Reagans to be joined at the hip every week. Separate dinners would work fine, and maybe Sean and Danny could have their own Sunday dinners with Sean's girlfriend, Penny (Xochitl Gomez), at some point? Also, why not bring Penny to New York City to introduce her to the Reagans, or have the entire family visit Boston to set up a Sunday dinner with Sean, Penny, and Danny?

Marg Helgenberger joining the show is a positive development because it helps flesh out Sarah Silver's character arc and her relationship with her mother, whom we've never seen before. As a multi-ethnic blended family, the Silver family presents a strong family dynamic that's unique to Blue Bloods, and the show should explore the family's distinctness more in future episodes. This will further reinforce Boston Blue's fresh new storylines, rather than trying to come across as Blue Bloods-lite or Blue Bloods: The Sequel.

Scott Bakula Officially Returns To Star Trek For Adorable Reunion With Porthos

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Star Trek's list of iconic characters feels like it could go on forever, but there's probably no more adorable or unexpected duo than Captain Jonathan Archer and his beloved beagle, Porthos. Scott Bakula's role in Star Trek: Enterprise as Archer ushered in a new era for the franchise aboard the Enterprise NX-01, with the doggo Porthos being a somewhat out-of-place addition to the crew that completely changed the dynamic of the show and the captain's character.

Archer's relationship with Porthos brought a touching and wholesome element to the franchise, as the loyal beagle was a constant source of companionship for the captain through thick and thin, with their bond providing some of Enterprise's most endearing moments. Now, more than two decades after the series concluded, Scott Bakula's Captain Archer is officially returning to Star Trek this week for an overdue reunion with his adorable four-legged sidekick.

Wizards of the Coast has found an adorable way to translate Archer and Porthos' relationship into the new Magic: The Gathering crossover releasing this week. The new "Captain Jonathan Archer" card brings Scott Bakula's Enterprise captain back to the franchise, but easily the best part of the comeback is that his faithful canine companion automatically joins him.

Archer is a rare green 3/3 Legendary Creature costing three mana to play, including one green, but when he enters the battlefield, he creates Porthos, a legendary green 1/1 Dog creature token. For those unfamiliar with Magic, tokens are essentially game pieces created through other cards' abilities that serve as creatures, artifacts, or other permanents on the battlefield, functioning much like regular cards despite not being part of a player's main deck.

Porthos even has his own collectible token card, so players can actually reunite the adorable duo on the battlefield without having to draw and cast two separate characters. It's an incredibly fitting tribute to their relationship in Enterprise, where Porthos was Archer's constant companion (despite having no official Starfleet duties). Even better, the pair actually complement each other through Archer's second ability, Federation.

By spending one green mana and tapping Archer, players can give a creature they control +X/+X until the end of their turn, with X being the number of different creature types among their non-Borg creatures.

Archer counts as both a Human and Officer, while Porthos adds Dog to the mix. Together, they provide three distinct creature types, so Archer can actually turn his adorable 1/1 companion into a formidable 4/4 creature. It's a clever translation of the partnership and a great nod to longtime Enterprise fans.

Archer is included as part of the main Star Trek expansion (TRK), available through boosters, while Porthos is included as a separate collectible token card. The crossover also includes four themed Commander decks: Federation Fleet, Landing Party, Klingon Fury, and We Are the Borg, as well as unique scene cards featuring mosaic-style themed sets from across the franchise, such as Unimatrix Zero.

Magic: The Gathering x Star Trek officially releases Friday, November 13 to celebrate the franchise's 60th anniversary. However, it's worth noting that Wizards of the Coast has warned that certain products, including Commander decks and select bundles, are experiencing shipping delays and may arrive after the official launch.

8 Near-Perfect Xbox 360 Games That Are Forgotten Masterpieces

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

From Viva Piñata to The Saboteur, these forgotten Xbox 360 masterpieces deserve another shot today.

The Xbox 360 era was a marvelous generation and the last time publishers regularly bet on strange, original ideas. Plenty of those bets paid off, from Gears of War to Assassin's Creed. But time hasn't been kind to all of them, and some of the generation's best games have been out of the conversation so long that a whole new wave of players has never heard of them.

A few of these were genuine hits at the time, while others were cult classics sunk by bad timing or studio closures. A couple had their legacies buried by disastrous follow-ups, and one isn't even available to play on any platform. What they share is that nobody talks about them now, even though their ideas still hold up against games released this decade. If your Xbox 360 collection is missing any of these, you skipped some of the best games the generation has to offer.

Viva Piñata, a social garden simulator developed by Rare and published by Microsoft Game Studios, launched on Xbox 360 on Nov. 9, 2006. Players turn a scrubby patch of dirt into a garden that attracts living, candy-filled piñatas, each with its own appetite for certain plants, foods, and mates. The game arrived two days after Gears of War, and it created a contrasting Xbox 360 double feature that could match Barbenheimer in its absurdity.

Viva Piñata was loved for its vivid art style, its whimsical soundtrack, and its surprisingly deep strategic gameplay. Microsoft considered it a success; however, sales started off slow due to the candy-colored art and tie-in cartoon dressing it as a kids' game on a console built for hardcore shooters. Rare followed with Viva Piñata: Trouble in Paradise in 2008, but that sequel barely evolved the formula, and it didn't seem like the team had enough fresh ideas for the series to continue.

In hindsight, Viva Piñata was a cozy game years before cozy games became a big deal. Stardew Valley and Animal Crossing: New Horizons turned peaceful life sims into one of gaming's biggest genres, built on the same shtick Rare pioneered. With Xbox's cancellation of Rare's Everwild leaving the studio without a new world beyond Sea of Thieves, a Viva Piñata revival on Xbox Game Pass is a surefire hit that should've already been in development at this point.

Singularity is one of the few Xbox 360 games that could recapture BioShock's iconic game design. Raven Software's 2010 first-person shooter sets you on Katorga-12, an abandoned Soviet island where Cold War experiments with a powerful element called E99 ripped time apart. As Capt. Nate Renko, you bounce between the island's 1950s heyday and its ruined present while mutated soldiers and time-warped monsters stalk you.

The star of the show is the Time Manipulation Device (TMD), a gauntlet that manipulates time, allowing you to age enemies into dust and rebuild collapsed staircases and crumbling bridges to their prime. You can even manipulate a soldier into an aggressive creature that turns on its own squad. Combined with Raven's sharp level design and survival horror pacing, Singularity feels like a mix between Half-Life 2 and BioShock with a time-bending twist.

Activision nearly canceled the game during its troubled development, then released it to good reviews, but sales didn't meet expectations. Raven faced more layoffs that October and has spent most of the years since supporting Call of Duty. Singularity became Raven's last original game, and its time-bending ideas still feel fresher than most shooters released this decade.

Most people may remember Crackdown 3 only as the game that was so bad, it tanked the Xbox One's reputation to unsalvageable levels. But the original was an amazing open-world action game that brought something completely new to the table. Realtime Worlds, led by Grand Theft Auto and Lemmings creator David Jones, launched the game on Feb. 20, 2007, and sales skyrocketed due to Xbox bundling copies with access to the Halo 3 multiplayer beta. In Crackdown, you play a genetically enhanced Agent cleaning up Pacific City one gang boss at a time.

Crackdown blended RPG elements with platforming collectathon design. Hundreds of glowing Agility Orbs hid on rooftops across Pacific City, and every orb you grabbed let you jump higher and run faster, until your Agent was a full-blown superhero on par with the Hulk. Throw in strength, driving, firearms, and explosives skills that grew the more you used them, add online co-op, and you had one of the most addictive sandboxes of the generation.

The Halo 3 bundle helped push Crackdown past 1.5 million copies in its first six months, and critics loved it on its own merits. Crackdown 2 followed in 2010 to mixed reviews, but Crackdown 3 was so bad it killed the franchise. After years of delays and promises of cloud-powered destruction, it launched in February 2019 to mediocre reviews and mass ridicule. Microsoft hasn't touched the series since, leaving the original as a superhero sandbox that never got the legacy it deserved.

Long before Fortnite concerts took over timelines, Microsoft turned Xbox Live into a primetime game show. 1 vs. 100 launched in 2009 on the Xbox Live Primetime channel, adapting the TV trivia show into a live broadcast where your Avatar could compete as The One against a mob of 100 other players. Host Chris Cashman ran each two-hour show live, cracking jokes and reading questions in real time.

Everyone else watched from The Crowd, answering the same questions for a shot at the next round, and the entire experience was free for Xbox Live Gold members. Best of all, players were able to win real prizes, from Microsoft Points and Xbox Live Arcade games to Zune music players. The beta season alone drew more than 114,000 players, proving that live events could work in a gaming environment as well.

Microsoft canceled 1 vs. 100 in July 2010 after two seasons, and since it only existed as a live event, it's completely unplayable today. It's incredibly disappointing that we still haven't had a true successor. Nothing on Xbox, PlayStation, or Nintendo Switch has recreated that mix of TV, community, and competition since, and in an era built on Twitch chat and live events, it would be an even bigger hit now.

The Saboteur has one of the boldest art styles of its generation, and it even doubles as a gameplay mechanic. Pandemic Studios' December 2009 open-world game drops you into Nazi-occupied Paris as Sean Devlin, a hard-drinking Irish race car driver who joins the French Resistance after a Nazi officer kills his best friend. Occupied districts are drained to black and white, except for blood, Nazi red, and Resistance blue, and every act of sabotage pushes color back into the streets.

Sean climbs Paris rooftops with Assassin's Creed-style freedom, blows up guard towers and anti-aircraft guns, and races through the French countryside in cars that reflect the period. The open world is packed with Nazi targets, so a simple drive across Paris can turn into a guerrilla battle at any moment. It was a unique take on World War II that traded battlefields for liberating a city, and that angle still feels unexplored today.

Sadly, EA shut down Pandemic Studios before The Saboteur even hit shelves, so the game launched with no studio left to receive the praise it deserved. It was viewed as Pandemic's stylish swan song, and it quickly slipped out of the conversation. EA closing the team behind Star Wars: Battlefront and Mercenaries: Playground of Destruction before even giving them another shot will always be a sore spot in gaming history.

Sleeping Dogs, a high-octane, crime-driven open-world game from United Front Games and Square Enix, launched on Xbox 360 in August 2012. You play as Wei Shen, an undercover cop infiltrating Hong Kong's Sun On Yee triad as his loyalties blur between the badge and the gang. The game began at Activision as True Crime: Hong Kong before Activision canceled it and Square Enix rescued it.

Sleeping Dogs has the best action to ever grace the open-world crime genre, and it's honestly not even close. United Front built a dynamic martial arts system with grabs, counters, and environmental takedowns that defeat enemies using meat hooks, table saws, A/C fans, and more. It features robust hand-to-hand combat that even Grand Theft Auto 6 would be hard-pressed to match. Wei Shen earns separate Cop and Triad experience as you play, and you can even hijack speeding cars by leaping from one to another mid-chase.

Square Enix still called the game a disappointment, despite Sleeping Dogs selling about 1.75 million copies in six months as a new IP. Square Enix passed on a full sequel in favor of the online spin-off Triad Wars, which was canceled in beta before United Front Games closed in 2016. Sega's Like a Dragon series and Sloclap's Sifu have since proven how much players love brutal martial arts and crime drama, which makes Sleeping Dogs look less like a misfire and more like a game that arrived years too early.

Volition dropped Saints Row 2 in October 2008, the same year Rockstar released Grand Theft Auto 4, and it felt more like a follow-up to Grand Theft Auto: San Andreas than GTA 4 ever did. Rockstar packed San Andreas with robust mechanics that made the game feel giant and expansive, but GTA 4 stripped away much of what made its predecessor special to chase realism. That worked for some players, but it went too far for a lot of fans, and Saints Row 2 became the safe haven that felt most like home.

Everything fans loved about Los Santos was still there, just bigger and better: gang territory to take over, businesses to buy, tons of activities and minigames, and a completely customizable protagonist. While GTA 4's Liberty City felt gray and dreary, Stilwater was a playground with plenty of fun to be had outside of missions. Volition's take on the formula was goofier at times, but it still knew when to deliver a heartfelt or serious moment.

Here's the hot take: Saints Row 2 was a better GTA than Grand Theft Auto 4 by a mile. It offered drop-in, drop-out online co-op across the entire campaign, a robust character creator, crib and vehicle customization, a bigger map, and a deeper melee system with finishing moves. Volition's story didn't end well: 2022's Saints Row reboot landed with mediocre reviews and sales, and Embracer Group closed the studio in 2023. Still, history should remember the time a developer went toe-to-toe with GTA and came out on top.

Lost Odyssey, a AAA cinematic turn-based JRPG from Final Fantasy creator Hironobu Sakaguchi's Mistwalker, reached the North American audience in February 2008. This Xbox first-party exclusive follows Kaim, an immortal warrior who has lived for 1,000 years and lost the memories of nearly all of them. It spanned a whopping four discs, carried a transcendent score from the legendary Nobuo Uematsu, and was Microsoft's boldest attempt to give the Xbox 360 its own Final Fantasy. Amazingly, it was better than any Final Fantasy game that launched that generation.

Lost Odyssey was lauded for its engaging, active turn-based combat and mature storytelling, especially the "A Thousand Years of Dreams" short stories written by novelist Kiyoshi Shigematsu that Kaim recovers as he travels. Xbox said it was happy with early sales in Japan and hinted the series would continue, but a sequel never materialized. Xbox quietly retreated from its Japanese RPG push, and Mistwalker moved on to The Last Story, exclusively on Wii and, years later, Fantasian.

Although mainline Final Fantasy games never went back to their roots, turn-based JRPGs are thriving more than ever. Metaphor: ReFantazio sold strongly in 2024 and should've won game of the year, while Clair Obscur: Expedition 33 became 2025's breakout hit after launching on Xbox Game Pass, proving a cinematic, melancholy RPG with active turn-based combat can move mountains. With Final Fantasy now committed to real-time action, Lost Odyssey looks more and more like the masterpiece Xbox needs to revive ASAP.

Some of these games were hits and others barely found an audience, but all eight deserve to be held on a pedestal today. Viva Piñata, Crackdown, and Lost Odyssey are sitting untouched in Xbox's vault, while Sleeping Dogs, The Saboteur, and Saints Row 2 lost their teams and 1 vs. 100 is unfortunately gone for good. On the bright side, you don't need to dig out an old Xbox 360 to catch up on these classics: Viva Piñata, Crackdown, Lost Odyssey, and Saints Row 2 are all playable on Xbox Series X|S through backward compatibility. Boot one up tonight, and you will also wonder how these games were ever left in the seventh generation.

If an AI Bubble Triggers a Bear Market, History Says This Is the Best Move Investors Can Make

Don't assume your portfolio is diversified.

Several major AI stocks have now entered correction territory. Nvidia shares, for example, are down more than 25% over the past month, shedding more than $1 trillion in market value.

A bear market, however, has hardly arrived. Many AI stocks have continued to gain value during the rout, including BigBear.ai, Amazon, and C3.ai.

From a demand perspective, there's very little to worry about regarding the long-term promise of artificial intelligence.

"AI demand remains strong," stresses new research from Barclays. "What the market is questioning is funding, as hyperscalers keep lifting capex while free cash flow evaporates."

The issue, therefore, is a matter of logistics and timing, not a matter of demand prospects. "AI fundamentals remain very strong in absolute terms," Barclays' research affirms.

Still, the potential impact of a bear market in the AI sector is difficult to ignore. After all, overly optimistic valuations may shrink even as demand remains strong. It won't be difficult for a downturn in AI stocks to impact the rest of the market. Nearly half of the S&P 500's value is now tied to AI companies, whether that is AI developers themselves or suppliers to the industry.

How should investors respond to the popping of the AI bubble? The history of the dot-com crash offers clear advice.

From a market-value perspective, technology companies suffered significantly during the dot-com bubble. The Nasdaq Composite (^IXIC -0.39%) fell nearly 80% over a 31-month bear market.

But here's the thing: Internet adoption continued to grow unabated. According to data from the U.S. Federal Reserve, 36% of Americans were active internet users in 1999. In 2000, that figure grew to 43%, reaching 49% by 2001. By 2002, nearly 60% of all Americans were regular internet users.

So while market valuations plummeted, the actual underlying technology -- the internet -- continued to experience sustained success. Nearly every business on Earth, not just technology companies, ultimately benefited from the internet's rise. So, to truly bet on the internet, it wasn't necessary to buy only dot-com companies. In fact, in the long term, the wisest decision may have been the simplest: maintain exposure to broad market indexes, ensuring your portfolio will benefit no matter how the internet's success is distributed.

The same should prove true for the current AI craze. AI companies are soaring in valuation, accounting for massive chunks of broader market indexes. But it's still far too early to know who the ultimate winners will be. Will it be GPU producers selling to AI software developers, or will it be the end users of that software development? As the dot-com bubble proved, predicting the future of a game-changing technology is more difficult than many suppose.

What's the main takeaway? There are two potential paths.

First, don't overthink the current environment. Throughout history, there has never been a bad time to buy an index fund tracking the S&P 500 (^GSPC -0.35%). Bought in at the height of the dot-com bubble, then doubled down just before the financial crisis hit? No problem. If you maintained a long enough time horizon, your portfolio would still be in the black.

The second takeaway is to understand just how exposed you are to an AI bear market. Historically, owning a diversified index fund exposed you to a wide variety of industries. But due to rapidly rising valuations in the AI sector, nearly half of the components in many popular indexes are businesses tied to the AI economy. If that segment of the market struggles, it could disproportionately impact your entire portfolio.

If you're concerned about AI valuations, you don't necessarily have to sell your existing positions for something else. But it may be wise to look at small-cap or international index funds, as these segments of the market are far less dominated by U.S. mega-cap AI companies. Many international and small-cap funds also trade at lower valuations, given their relatively lower exposure to pricey AI stocks.

Huang has been married to Lori Huang for 40 years. Both of their children are also executives at Nvidia.

3 Warren Buffett Stocks Just Closed at Record Highs. Here's the One I'd Still Buy Now.

📅 Nov 13, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Three stocks linked to the Oracle of Omaha hit records on the same day. Which one still deserves fresh money?

On Wednesday, Nov. 11, three stocks closely tied to Warren Buffett set records on the same day. Apple (AAPL -0.14%) closed at $355.63, Coca-Cola (KO -0.13%) at $97.57 and the Class B shares of Berkshire Hathaway (BRKA +0.09%)(BRKB +0.27%) at $588.24.

Berkshire is the link. In its latest 13F filing, covering the end of June, Apple was its largest stock holding, and Coca-Cola its fourth-largest. Both stakes were built during Buffett's decades running the company, and he remains a director after becoming chairman emeritus in September.

Of the three, Berkshire is the one I'd still buy at its record. It's the cheapest against what it earns, and every Berkshire share comes with a piece of the other two.

Warren Buffett. Image source: Getty Images.

Apple stock is up around 31% this year, and Wednesday's close was its 28th record in 2026. The iPhone maker's fiscal 2026 (the year ended Sept. 26) was strong, with earnings per share up 20% to $8.98.

But growth cooled as the year ended. Apple's revenue rose 12% year over year in fiscal Q4, down from 16% in fiscal Q3 and 17% in fiscal Q2. Earnings per share climbed 14% in fiscal Q4, after a 29% jump in fiscal Q3. And the latest number included a $0.05 boost from tariff refunds, so the underlying rise was closer to 11%.

At Wednesday's close, Apple traded at around 40 times its fiscal 2026 earnings and about 38 times analysts' consensus earnings estimate for fiscal 2027. I'd say that's a hefty price for a business whose profit growth just slowed to the low double digits.

Berkshire cut its Apple holding by around 19% in the second half of 2025, to about 228 million shares, and still owned that many at the end of June. These shares would be worth about $81 billion at Wednesday's price.

I'd hold on to Apple. But at around 40 times earnings, it isn't the one I'd add to at a record.

Coca-Cola's close on Wednesday was its 29th record this year, and the stock is up around 40% in 2026.

Coca-Cola's comparable earnings per share (its non-GAAP, or adjusted, profit measure) gained 11% in the third quarter. Management now guides for at least 11% growth this year, which would raise adjusted earnings from $3.00 per share in 2025 to at least $3.33. Sure, around 3 points of that growth come from currency.

At $97.57, though, the stock costs around 29 times the earnings Coca-Cola expects for 2026. That might be too high for a beverage business benefiting from exchange rates. I'd be more interested in a price near $80, or about 24 times 2026 earnings.

Berkshire completed its purchase of 400 million Coca-Cola shares in 1994 and still owned them all at the end of June. The holding would be worth around $39 billion at Wednesday's close.

"When you find a truly wonderful business, stick with it," Buffett wrote to Berkshire shareholders in early 2024, using Coca-Cola and American Express as his examples.

Berkshire's Class B shares are up around 17% this year, well behind Apple and Coca-Cola. Wednesday's close was just its eighth record in 2026, and the stock didn't hit one between May 2025 and Oct. 20.

The stock costs less against its earnings, too. Berkshire's operating earnings (which strip out changes in the value of its stock portfolio) were around $47.2 billion during its last four quarters. That works out to around 27 times operating earnings, and about 1.6 times its Sept. 30 book value (assets minus liabilities).

Yes, third-quarter operating earnings slid around 6% year over year, as insurance underwriting earnings shrank about 43%. But even with that drop, the stock asks less of a buyer than either of the other two.

And Berkshire shareholders own the other two indirectly. Using the June 30 share counts, the Apple and Coca-Cola stakes combined would be worth around $120 billion at Wednesday's prices, or almost 10% of Berkshire's market value.

In the end, these are three great businesses, and I don't see any of the records as a reason to sell.

Apple's growth would probably have to pick up again to justify a multiple of around 40 times earnings, and Coca-Cola is still far above the $80 or so I'd like to pay. Berkshire has the lowest price of the three.

So, Berkshire is the one I'd buy today, but I'd likely add shares gradually. As recently as Sept. 30, the stock traded slightly below its 2025 closing price, and it's climbed about 18% since.
