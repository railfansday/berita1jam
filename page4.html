MercadoLibre vs. Uber Technologies: Which Consumer Network Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

📅 Nov 14, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

MercadoLibre's 47% revenue surge stands out, but Uber's cheaper earnings multiple and steadier balance sheet make the 2027 call far closer than the growth numbers suggest.

Investors searching for high-growth platforms often find themselves choosing between regional dominance and global ubiquity. Whether MercadoLibre (MELI +1.11%) or Uber Technologies (UBER +0.83%) fits your portfolio better depends on your specific growth objectives.

MercadoLibre serves as the primary digital commerce infrastructure for Latin America, providing essential retail and fintech services. Uber connects millions of consumers with transportation and delivery options worldwide. While both operate platform-based models, they navigate different competitive landscapes and regulatory environments, making this a classic match-up for growth-oriented investors.

MercadoLibre operates a vast digital ecosystem that includes its marketplace, the Mercado Pago fintech platform, and a comprehensive logistics network. The company maintains key partnerships with major media brands like Disney+ for its loyalty program and utilizes cloud infrastructure from Amazon.com Inc. (AMZN -0.29%). It also works with card associations like Visa Inc. (V +1.30%) and Mastercard (MA +1.32%) to facilitate payment services for its millions of active users.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached about $38.5 billion, which represents a growth rate of approximately 47% over the prior year. The company reported net income of roughly $1.9 billion during this same period. This significant growth is a result of expanding electronic commerce adoption and the rising volume of digital payments throughout the region.

The debt-to-equity ratio, which measures total debt relative to shareholder equity, is nearly 1.7x. As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the current ratio of 1.2x indicates its ability to cover short-term liabilities with current assets. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow was about $10.8 billion, providing capital to reinvest in the business among retail stocks.

Uber Technologies connects over two hundred million monthly users with mobility and delivery services through its expansive global platform. The company has established significant partnerships with Tractor Supply Company (TSCO -0.49%) and Amazon to broaden its reach into grocery and retail delivery. It also relies on third-party mapping and infrastructure software from Alphabet to ensure its services run smoothly across thousands of cities.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, Uber recorded revenue of about $56.5 billion, an increase of roughly 14% compared to the previous twelve-month period. Net income for the company reached approximately $3.5 billion during this time. These results reflect the scale of the platform as it moves toward consistent profitability and stronger bottom-line performance.

Uber maintains a debt-to-equity ratio of roughly 0.4x as of its December 2025 balance sheet. Its current ratio is approximately 1.1x, which measures its capacity to pay off short-term debts with its available short-term assets. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow reached about $9.8 billion, allowing the company to strengthen its financial position and explore new growth initiatives.

MercadoLibre faces intense competition from global entrants and regional rivals. It also relies on mobile platforms controlled by Apple Inc. (AAPL +0.39%) and Alphabet Inc. (GOOG -0.96%) for its app distribution. Furthermore, the company manages credit risk within its lending solutions for merchants across Latin America.

Uber Technologies operates in a crowded field against rivals like Lyft (LYFT +2.23%) and DoorDash (DASH -0.62%). The platform must also manage complex legal challenges regarding how it classifies its drivers. Additionally, inflationary pressures on fuel and labor costs may impact its ability to maintain driver supply and net revenues.

Uber appears cheaper on an earnings basis, though MercadoLibre trades at a lower multiple relative to its sales over the past 12 months.

MercadoLibre has evolved from the so-called eBay of Latin America to something more akin to a combination of Amazon and PayPal Holdings Inc. (PYPL +1.68%). In 2025, the company made large investments in its Brazilian logistics network, cut down the free-shipping threshold in Brazil in June, and nearly doubled its consumer loan book. This drove impressive growth for the company and investors, with an increasing focus on financial services over delivering goods.

Yet the immediate concern with left-of-center Luis Lula winning the Brazilian presidential election last month is that the new administration will start addressing Brazil's high government and consumer debt levels, which will keep interest rates there around their current very high levels. That's bad for a consumer-loan-focused business like MercadoLibre, and shares have been hammered this week as a result.

Long-term, however, such fears are probably overblown. In fact, in the current fiscal year, revenue is expected to jump 45% to $41.9 billion, with slightly higher free cash flow and slightly lower net income. Overall, it's a good picture for MercadoLibre.

Uber revolutionized the taxi industry when it rolled out its ride-sharing network, allowing anyone with a car to earn a side gig. Both the industry and the company have come a long way.

Uber Technologies is by far the largest player in the developing ride-share market in terms of revenue. Wall Street expects Uber's sales to increase by $6 billion from last year to $58 billion this year, bringing with it net income of $6.4 billion. But as a sign of the expenses Uber faces in expanding its business model, that would be roughly $3.6 billion less income than in 2025. In the long run, its global ride-sharing, delivery, and autonomous solutions business will make for a strong business.

There is a lot that is appealing about MercadoLibre, but it comes at a premium P/E ratio that may not be warranted given the historically lower valuation the market puts on Latin America-reliant stocks. Right now, Uber is only slightly richer than the average S&P 500 stock (which trades around 19 times forward earnings). Based on valuation, the better choice for 2027 is the ride-sharing giant, Uber.

On Saturday morning, the NFL announced that several officiating assignments for Week 10 have been adjusted. Officials were reassigned to games that are within driving distance of their hometowns.

Barry Anderson and Duane Heydt, who normally serve as umpires, along with side judge Don Willard will take on referee duties on Sunday.

Anderson will officiate the Jacksonville Jaguars-Tennessee Titans game in Tennessee;; Heydt has been assigned to work the Carolina Panthers-New Orleans Saints game in New Orleans; and Willard will have double-duty first refereeing the Miami Dolphins-Indianapolis Colts game in Indianapolis at 1 p.m. ET before making the trip to Cincinnati for Sunday night's game between the Bengals and Pittsburgh Steelers at 8:20 p.m. ET.

That stop was lifted as of 8:15 p.m. ET Friday, but even with flights allowed to resume, it led to kickoff changes for some college games. Almost all of the Power Four conferences have teams that were prevented from reaching their destinations. Only the ACC has every team already ready to go. One SEC matchup — Ole Miss vs. Oklahoma — was postponed to Sunday.

It's the second nationwide ground stop since 9/11, per ABC News, which noted that this last happened in 2023. At the time, pilots were unable to access their pre-departure paperwork because the FAA NOTAM (Notice To all Air Missions) system — used to disseminate flight hazards and real-time restrictions to pilots — went down.

Malaysia plans to introduce a new law requiring data center operators to disclose their AI accelerator imports and power consumption to prevent diversion of restricted hardware to countries like China and optimize the use of the country’s power infrastructure, reports Malay Mail. The authorities also plan to charge data center operators for unused electricity capacity they reserve. While the idea to use power consumption to detect potential GPU smuggling has merit, it also has flaws.

Malaysia's Minister of Investment, Trade and Industry Datuk Seri Johari Abdul Ghani acknowledged on Thursday that some companies had rented space in Malaysian data centers to import GPUs legally, which they then shipped abroad in smuggling operations. To prevent such practices, Malaysia's Digital Ministry is now reviewing existing legislation to determine whether local authorities can take action against entities that re-export processors subject to U.S. export bans. The minister stressed that Malaysia wants AI accelerators imported into the country to be used domestically.

The proposed legislation will require data center operators to disclose the number of GPUs they import, their power consumption (presumably nameplate power consumption), the physical locations of their facilities, and the identities of operators and hyperscale customers. The government intends to track data center operations throughout their lifetime rather than limit oversight to the project approval stage.

Meanwhile, data center operators will have to pay for all the electricity capacity they reserve, irrespective of their actual power consumption. For example, if a data center secures 100 MW of power capacity but only uses 50 MW, its operator will still have to pay for the remaining 50 MW. The measure is meant to discourage companies from reserving electricity capacity they may not need for another one to three years. As an added bonus, the measure will further discourage rerouting AI hardware to China while pretending it is used in Malaysia.

While data on power consumption could help Malaysian authorities spot suspicious discrepancies between AI GPU imports and actual data center operations, it is virtually impossible to accurately determine the number of AI accelerators deployed at a facility based on power consumption alone. For example, Nvidia's H100 and H200 SXM GPUs are rated at 700W, whereas its B200 SXM accelerators are rated at 1,000W, yet neither necessarily consumes its rated power under actual workloads.

Furthermore, without knowledge of server configurations, the power consumption of CPUs, memory, storage, and networking equipment, or the power usage effectiveness (PUE) of a particular facility, any estimates of the number of operational GPUs would be imprecise, to put it mildly. Also, there may be a considerable delay between GPU imports and their actual deployment. As a result, reliable verification would require physical inventory audits or other mechanisms to track individual processors.

Johari said that Malaysian authorities were prepared to cooperate with their U.S. counterparts and provide information about facilities that house particular GPUs upon request. However, the minister did not specify whether the new legislation would require data center operators to disclose GPU models and serial numbers, or whether authorities would conduct on-site inspections.

Crocodiles face a hidden threat that may weaken a vital survival response

📅 Nov 14, 2026  |  Category: Food  |  Read Source →

Caimans with more mercury in their blood showed a weaker stress hormone response, raising concerns about how pollution affects their survival.

Mercury pollution may be weakening the stress response in crocodiles and their relatives.

Researchers studying smooth-fronted caimans in South American rainforests found that animals with more mercury in their blood had a weaker stress hormone response.

Gold mining is a major source of mercury pollution in South America. The findings suggest that mercury may interfere with a natural defense system that caimans and other crocodilians depend on to survive in the wild.

Jérémy Lemaire of the University of Vienna led the project with colleagues there and at the University of Veterinary Medicine Vienna.

The researchers studied 20 adult and nearly adult caimans in French Guiana between November 2023 and June 2024.

“Given the overlap of crocodylian habitats with gold mining activities, this is a conservation concern that deserves immediate attention,” Lemaire said.

Since the late 1800s, atmospheric mercury has risen an estimated 300% to 500% above its natural level, mostly from burning fossil fuels.

Small-scale gold mining is a major contributor to this pollution. It produces 38% of the mercury released into the air worldwide, and more than 80% of mercury emissions in South America and sub-Saharan Africa combined.

Caimans live a long time and take in mercury mainly through their food. Over many years, it builds up in their bodies, which makes them a useful measure of how polluted their streams and forests are.

The authors describe mercury as an endocrine-disrupting chemical, meaning one that interferes with the body’s hormones.

Theirs is the first study to measure this hormone response in wild animals living in a tropical forest with high mercury levels, the team wrote.

The researchers studied caimans in different parts of the rainforest. One of the sites was the Nouragues nature reserve.

They caught the animals between 8 and 11 p.m. to account for the natural changes in stress hormone levels throughout the day.

Researchers caught each caiman with a snare pole. They taped its snout shut and laid a wet towel over its eyes, so every animal went through the same experience.

Then they drew blood four times. The first sample came within 3 to 5 minutes of capture to estimate baseline hormone levels. The rest came 15, 30, and 60 minutes after capture.

Red blood cells went to the mercury test. The liquid part of the blood went to a test for corticosterone, the main stress hormone in reptiles and birds.

Mercury in the caimans’ dried red blood cells ranged from about 0.3 to 2 parts per million. The team split the animals into three groups: four with low levels, nine in the middle, and seven with high levels.

At the first blood draw, resting hormone levels were about the same in all three groups. The groups differed only in the one-hour sample.

In the low and middle groups, hormone levels rose over the hour. In the high-mercury group, the levels didn’t change by a statistically significant amount at any point.

At 60 minutes, the high group’s levels were lower than those of both other groups. The high-mercury caimans started with similar hormone levels but showed a smaller increase after capture.

Across all 20 animals, the more mercury a caiman carried, the smaller the difference between its resting level and its level at one hour. Mercury levels accounted for about 40% of the variation in that difference.

The most contaminated caiman’s hormone increase was more than 100 times smaller than that of the least contaminated one.

“A fast, robust stress response is essential for survival in the wild,” Lemaire said.

“We show that even relatively low mercury burdens affect this response in caimans, potentially limiting their ability to cope with predators, extreme weather, human disturbance, and ultimately survive in the wild.”

Stress hormones work by attaching to a protein called the glucocorticoid receptor, which helps activate the body’s stress response.

Mercury can interfere with this process. Studies in male rats found that mercury made it harder for hormones to attach to the receptor.

Computer simulations also suggest that mercury can partly block the hormone’s binding site by attaching to an amino acid called cysteine.

Lemaire’s team found this same amino acid in the receptors of crocodilians and other vertebrates. This could help explain their findings, although they haven’t tested the effect directly in caiman cells.

The caimans had mercury levels similar to those found in other crocodilians worldwide.

Some slender-snouted crocodiles in Gabon, for example, have had blood mercury levels as high as 11 parts per million.

The researchers say the problem may extend beyond areas near gold mines. Even at the levels found in this study, mercury was linked to a weaker stress response.

More than a third of the 26 living crocodilian species are threatened with extinction, according to the IUCN.

Mercury pollution could put them at greater risk if it affects their ability to survive and reproduce.

The effects on wild populations could take years to become noticeable.

Since the late 1800s, crocodilians have gone through about five generations. In comparison, large land animals with generations lasting 5 to 10 years have gone through around 20.

Crocodilians take a long time to produce new generations, which can make changes in their populations difficult to detect.

Researchers still need to find out whether caimans with weaker stress responses are more likely to die or have fewer offspring.

The full study was published in the journal Communications Biology.

Jerry Jones: "I don't think we're far away" from 18 regular-season games

📅 Nov 14, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

As the NFL embarks on the second half of its current 18-week regular-season schedule, it’s just a matter of time before the current structure changes.

It’s no secret that the NFL wants to expand the regular season from 17 to 18 games. The league is sufficiently confident about it happening that folks like Cowboys owner Jerry Jones speak of it as a given.

“I don’t think we’re far away from it,” Jones said Friday on 105.3 The Fan in Dallas, regarding the addition of an extra game. “I don’t know how — what is far away. Next month? No. Next year? Maybe. [The] next year? Maybe. That has to be negotiated with the players.

“But it’s in everybody’s best interests.  You’d cut back on the preseason games. You’d have teams more develop in the first few games as the type of thing you were trying to get done in preseason. But it’s in everybody’s interest, and it certainly is in the players’ best interest in my mind, for sure.  And having said that, I would anticipate that coming.”

His comments presume the NFL Players Association will happily agree to 18 regular-season games. They likely won’t. At a minimum, they’ll seek major concessions in exchange for the willingness to add yet another game that counts.

The unspoken goal had been 2027. The NFL delayed picking a date for Super Bowl LXII in Atlanta in order to accommodate the possibility that the game will slide a week or two deeper into February. That would make 2028 the earliest year for expansion, as a practical matter. (Adding a game for 2027 would be possible only if the dead week between the conference championships is wiped out or if the season starts a week earlier. If an 18th game will result in a second bye, both would be required.)

Expansion by 2027 would require expedited negotiations. Expansion by 2028 would hinge on the NFL and NFLPA striking a deal within the next year or so. The union has shown no inclination, however, to engage in talks aimed at extending the current Collective Bargaining Agreement, which runs through the 2030 season.

Still, if push comes to shove, there will be 18 games. The blueprint was created in 2011. The NFL will lock the players out until the players agree to the things the owners want. Most notably, 18 regular-season games and 16 annual international games.

Beyond that, Jones’s comment about the early games of the season is odd. “You’d have teams more develop in the first few games as the type of thing you were trying to get done in preseason.”

He says that as if it’s a good thing. It definitely is not. Already, teams struggle in September to get up to speed, given that most teams (with the exception of those assigned to the Hall of Fame Game) play only three preseason games. When the preseason reduces to two games, that’s even fewer chances to prepare.

It’s also fewer chances for the non-starters to get the attention of coaches and General Managers in games that don’t count in the standings. It will become harder for undrafted free agents to make the 53-man rosters.

Regardless, the motivation isn’t football. It’s money. More inventory equals more weekends equals more broadcast windows equals more revenue.

And that likely will continue until the preseason shrinks to zero games — and the regular season expands to 20 games.

As the NFL embarks on the second half of its current 18-week regular-season schedule, it's just a matter of time before the current structure changes.

It's no secret that the NFL wants to expand the regular season from 17 to 18 games. The league is sufficiently confident about it happening that folks like Cowboys owner Jerry Jones speak of it as a given.

"I don't think we're far away from it," Jones said Friday on 105.3 The Fan in Dallas, regarding the addition of an extra game. "I don't know how — what is far away. Next month? No. Next year? Maybe. [The] next year? Maybe. That has to be negotiated with the players.

"But it's in everybody's best interests.  You'd cut back on the preseason games. You'd have teams more develop in the first few games as the type of thing you were trying to get done in preseason. But it's in everybody's interest, and it certainly is in the players' best interest in my mind, for sure.  And having said that, I would anticipate that coming."

His comments presume the NFL Players Association will happily agree to 18 regular-season games. They likely won't. At a minimum, they'll seek major concessions in exchange for the willingness to add yet another game that counts.

The unspoken goal had been 2027. The NFL delayed picking a date for Super Bowl LXII in Atlanta in order to accommodate the possibility that the game will slide a week or two deeper into February. That would make 2028 the earliest year for expansion, as a practical matter. (Adding a game for 2027 would be possible only if the dead week between the conference championships is wiped out or if the season starts a week earlier. If an 18th game will result in a second bye, both would be required.)

Expansion by 2027 would require expedited negotiations. Expansion by 2028 would hinge on the NFL and NFLPA striking a deal within the next year or so. The union has shown no inclination, however, to engage in talks aimed at extending the current Collective Bargaining Agreement, which runs through the 2030 season.

Still, if push comes to shove, there will be 18 games. The blueprint was created in 2011. The NFL will lock the players out until the players agree to the things the owners want. Most notably, 18 regular-season games and 16 annual international games.

Beyond that, Jones's comment about the early games of the season is odd. "You'd have teams more develop in the first few games as the type of thing you were trying to get done in preseason."

He says that as if it's a good thing. It definitely is not. Already, teams struggle in September to get up to speed, given that most teams (with the exception of those assigned to the Hall of Fame Game) play only three preseason games. When the preseason reduces to two games, that's even fewer chances to prepare.

It's also fewer chances for the non-starters to get the attention of coaches and General Managers in games that don't count in the standings. It will become harder for undrafted free agents to make the 53-man rosters.

Regardless, the motivation isn't football. It's money. More inventory equals more weekends equals more broadcast windows equals more revenue.

And that likely will continue until the preseason shrinks to zero games — and the regular season expands to 20 games.

Aaron Rodgers Shares New Photo With Brother Jordan Rodgers After Feud

📅 Nov 14, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Aaron Rodgers shared a new photo of himself posing with his younger brother Jordan Rodgers, proving the two are officially on good terms following their yearslong family feud.

Aaron Rodgers and Jordan Rodgers have officially reached a neutral zone.

Months after the Pittsburgh Steelers quarterback revealed he and his family had ended their yearslong estrangement, the NFL star, 42, shared picture-perfect proof of a reunion with his younger brother.

“Great to see little bro @jrodgers11 in his element tonight @acrisurestadium,” Aaron captioned a Nov. 13 Instagram post of the pair. “Really proud of how hard he’s worked to get to where he is now.”

But Aaron and Jordan, 38, weren’t only ones feeling the love as fans were equally as happy to see the two tackle their differences.

“Any girl who’s watched the Bachelorette knows how big of a deal this is,” one person commented, while another added, “Got tears in my eyes over a reunion of two people who don’t know I exist.”

Tensions between the two brothers took center stage after Jordan revealed he didn’t have “much” of a relationship with the athlete while competing on The Bachelorette in 2016.

But earlier this year, Aaron shared that he had decided to huddle up with his family, including older brother Luke Rodgers, once again after his parents Ed Rodgers and Darla Rodgers moved to Nashville.

“There was some stuff there,” Aaron detailed during a Sept. 10 appearance on the Not Just Football podcast, “and I could tell I was coming to a time where I needed to make it right.”

As he explained, “I went to Nashville and met up with my older brother, and a couple weeks after that, met up with my little brother. It was cool. There were tough conversations for sure, but I’m just in a much better headspace.”

Aaron also credited his wife Brittani—whom he announced he had privately married in 2025—with getting him to the end zone.

“She was a big part of that,” he said. “Not that she was pushing anything, she wasn’t. It was more of when you’re loved by someone in such an unconditional way, it changes your perspective on all your relationships surrounding that. It becomes your anchor point.”

As for Jordan, the ESPN commentator also proved there was no longer a line of defense when it came to his brother, quipping about Aaron during an on-air segment in September.

“Rodgers was actually very accurate down the field," the TV personality said during ESPN's First Take on Sept. 23. "The throws that were not completed because of either a non-call or a drop down the field were on the money."

Noting that his commentary on Aaron’s performance might be just a bit biased, Jordan added, "Not trying to be a homer.”

For a closer look into what Aaron has said about his family before reconciling, keep reading...

After growing up "in a really conservative, small-town environment" in Chico, Calif., Aaron Rodgers said in the Netflix docuseries Aaron Rodgers: Enigma, transferring from Butte College to University of California, Berkeley, was admittedly an eye-opening experience for him. "So that was fun, to have my ideologies tested."

But, as he recalled, he was already traveling a path that was taking him further and further away from how he was raised by dad Ed Rodgers and mom Darla Rodgers.

The "white, dogmatic church" he grew up in "didn’t really serve me," Aaron said. "It was very rigid in structure. I'm not a rigid person. Shame, guilt, judgment—it was like, 'We have the truth, our way or the highway, our way is heaven, your way is hell.' Even talking to my parents, it was very black and white. Somebody has to be wrong, somebody has to be right. I just slowly uncoupled from that in high school."

So being in a city like Berkeley "that challenged my beliefs" (and where he was sleeping in on Sunday after games instead of going to church), he continued, "definitely helped me to begin to question more things."

Desperately Seeking Spiritual Enlightenment

After capping off the Green Bay Packers' 2011-12 NFL season with a Super Bowl victory—which, until then, Aaron thought was the holy grail he was seeking—he started to wonder what else was out there.

"I really just desperately wanted to be more than  a football player," he said in the series, "and so I started my spiritual journey."

Aaron got into the teachings of progressive spiritual leader Rob Bell, author of What Is the Bible? (which the athlete is seen reading in the series), and started reading more philosophy and self-help books.

Appreciating what he found to be Rob's inclusive, nonjudgmental approach to faith, "He was a big help for me to totally unravel the religion of my youth," Aaron said in Enigma.

Through his readings, "I found a courage to speak my feelings better," he continued. "So I started to stand up to institutions of my youth, and that was everything from organized religion, my parents, dogma, ideology. That definitely changed the dynamics of my family, because I was just questioning all of it."

Winning a Super Bowl vaulted Aaron to another tier of fame, the athlete recalling how during the summer of 2011 he was "paparazzi'd" for the first time during a trip to Hawaii.

"I was like, 'F--k, life's different now," he said. And after he was on TV more, he added, "my whole ability to move around with relative anonymity changed."

He added, "I didn't do myself any favors with some of the girls I dated after that, that were in the public eye. I definitely hated it at first. Like really despised it. I enjoyed my private life. I enjoyed being able to go places. But from Super Bowl MVP, [league] MVP, State Farm commercials, that got a little more difficult."

While the doc flashed to pictures of him with three famous exes, that was the entirety of what Aaron said about his high-profile relationships with Olivia Munn, Danica Patrick and Shailene Woodley (who, incidentally, he was engaged to and didn't just date).

Olivia, who dated Aaron from 2014 until 2017, denied rumors that she played a role in her ex's estrangement from his family, saying on Andy Cohen Live in 2018, "He hadn't spoken to the parents and one brother for, like, eight months before we started dating."

Aaron reiterated to biographer Ian O'Connor, author of 2024's Out of the Darkness: The Mystery of Aaron Rodgers, that his issues with his family were "deep rooted" and Olivia "has nothing to do with all the years" they haven't spoken.

"There was a lot of times, when I became real famous," Aaron said in Enigma, "where I heard from a lot of people, including family members, who where it was like, ‘Your life is too big, we need you to be smaller. Be smaller, don't talk about your life.'"

Feeling woefully misunderstood, he continued, "It always hurt me because I just feel like you don’t see me. This is not something I ever desired or wanted, other than playing on Sundays. It can definitely change the people around your circle, because it can be intoxicating, the fame and notoriety. So, definitely relationships changed after that. Friendships, family."

Aaron said that, pre-estrangement, he wasn't "super-duper close" with everybody in his family anyway.

"I was close with my little brother," he recalled, referring to younger sibling Jordan Rodgers. "But in actuality it goes back to stuff from high school that kind of made me feel distant. Stuff in college, stuff post-college. And I was quiet about it. 'Cause I thought the best way to do it was, just don’t talk about it publicly. And what do they do?"

In 2016, Jordan appeared on The Bachelorette—and, incidentally, he's been married to rose-bearer JoJo Fletcher since 2022.

When he brought JoJo home to meet his folks and eldest brother Luke Rodgers, there were two empty chairs at the dinner table—purportedly for Aaron and then-girlfriend Olivia—and all of a sudden millions of people found out the Packers QB was estranged from the rest of the family.

"We miss our brother," Luke said on The Bachelorette. "We just are trusting that God brings things full circle. And just wish that everything would just get back to us being a family."

"They go on a bulls--t show and leave two empty chairs," he said in Enigma. "They all agreed this was like a good thing to do, to leave two empty chairs at a stupid dating show that my brother just went on to get famous—his words, not mine—that he ended up winning."

Aaron reiterated in the series that he was never even invited to the dinner, which was held during the NFL season.

A dinner "I was never asked to go to," he said, "not that I would have gone."

E! News reached out to Jordan, Ed and Darla for comment on what Aaron had to say in Enigma but has not yet heard back.

Jordan told E! in 2016 after his hometown episode aired that he "made a commitment to go on there and be honest with JoJo and make sure that she knew everything" about where he was coming from.

Aaron has embraced taking ayahuasca—"It's the hardest medicine possible, that I've tried"—as an essential part of his journey, also touting its healing power for "mind, body, spirit."

"I was searching, for sure," he said in Enigma. "My life was through one lens, of organized religion. Where are people in life finding deep peace and centeredness and presence outside of what I knew?"

Speaking of the divide between him and his family, Aaron said he "found a lot of resistance" once he started researching other religions and plant medicine, "doing things that, compared to what I grew up in, would be considered an alternative lifestyle."

As for his family, he continued, "You know, they’re living as best they can. That is, still engulfed in organized religion, which works for them. That's great. So as much as they might not like what they see, love and respect and gratitude for how I was raised, because it wouldn’t have turned me into who I am today."

"People ask me, 'Is there hope for a reconciliation?'" Aaron said. "I say, 'Yeah, of course. Of course.'"

He added, "I don’t want them to fail, to struggle, to have any strife or issues. I don't wish any ill will on them at all. It's more like this: We're just different steps on the timeline of our own journeys."

Pokemon fans furious after lost PokePark DS game found after 20 years is sealed in PSA slab

📅 Nov 14, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Pokemon fans and game preservationists are frustrated after a playable copy of the long-lost PokePark Fishing Rally DS was authenticated and sealed inside a PSA case, leaving the 20-year-old title inaccessible to most people who hoped to finally play it.

PokePark Fishing Rally DS, also known as PokePark Fishing Contest DS, was distributed through Download Play at Japan’s PokePark theme park in 2005. Copies were temporary by design, disappearing when the Nintendo DS was switched off or after 12 hours. The game was never sold at retail, making it one of the most elusive pieces of lost Pokemon media.

As first reported by IGN, collector Daniel Qudari showed the outlet a working cartridge capable of sending the game to other Nintendo DS systems through Download Play.

PSA researcher Claire Shelton reportedly played the game during a private meeting with the owner before the cartridge was submitted for authentication and encapsulation.

The discovery initially appeared to be a major breakthrough for Pokemon historians and lost media researchers. However, much of the online reaction has focused on the decision to put the cartridge in a PSA slab before its contents were publicly preserved.

Anthony, known as @KirbyCheatFurby on X, criticized the decision, writing that “instead of making it so we can all play the ROM by dumping its contents online, they got it put in a f***ing PSA case.” The post was widely shared by fans who had hoped the discovery would lead to a public ROM dump.

In another post, Anthony directly asked the collector to “crack that slab open and dump the ROM,” arguing that another case could be purchased after the software had been preserved.

The reaction intensified after PSA’s certification listing confirmed that the item was genuine. Anthony later shared the certification page after saying a friend initially suspected the story could be an elaborate prank involving a 3D-printed cartridge.

my friend thought that the article was ragebait and that the slab could have just been an elaborate prank with a 3D printed cart, but sadly…https://t.co/ONrAGpuhUh

The criticism is centered on access rather than the cartridge’s authenticity. PSA has presented encapsulation as a way to document and protect a unique piece of video game history, while fans and preservationists view a software dump as the more important form of preservation.

A sealed case protects the physical object, but it also means the cartridge cannot be used in a Nintendo DS unless it is removed. As of publication, no public ROM dump of the playable copy has been announced, leaving the game effectively out of reach for everyone except the owner and the small number of people who were allowed to see it privately.

Dexerto previously reported on a separate PokePark development cartridge that sold for around $11,000 after fans hoped it might finally reveal the lost game’s contents. That cartridge’s software was never publicly confirmed, making the playable discovery even more significant.

Max Dowman breaks Jude Bellingham record as Arsenal teenager makes England history

📅 Nov 14, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Max Dowman has added another incredible achievement to his burgeoning career after becoming the youngest player to play for the England Under-21s.

The Arsenal youngster was introduced from the bench in Friday’s 1-0 friendly loss to Portugal at Ashton Gate. The 16-year-10-month-13-day-old player is over three months younger than Jude Bellingham was when he played his first game for the Under-21s.

Dowman replaced former Arsenal academy player Amario Cozier-Duberry in the 61st minute. He came on alongside debutant Trey Nyoni as England tried to shift the momentum of a challenging game.

An early strike from Afonso Moreira gave Portugal the lead in the 28th minute. An ineffective England team failed to respond. However, the replacements gave Lee Carsley’s team some late momentum.

Despite the hosts’ attempts to level the score, they failed to break down the Portuguese defence. The result means England have lost for the second game running. However, Dowman had much to be pleased with personally.

Dowman has added to his growing collection of records.

He became the youngest player to play in the Champions League, the youngest goalscorer in the history of the Premier League and the youngest to win the Premier League title.

Breaking Bellingham’s England Under-21 record is another significant step. It shows how quickly Dowman has progressed through the international age groups.

He has already made a big impression at Arsenal with Mikel Arteta’s love of giving younger players a chance, but Dowman will have some stiff competition for first team minutes.

Another positive is his progression on an international scale. He will improve both physically and tactically playing against older players and gain experience at a higher level.

England will host France in their next friendly on Monday, 16th November. Dowman will be hoping to feature after his instant impact on the record books.

Despite the disappointing result against Portugal, it was a landmark evening for the Arsenal players.

Dowman is already building a legacy at club level, but now he can add to his list of records by becoming an England Under-21 record holder.

At just 16, he has plenty of time to improve and show consistent performances to get more opportunities with the senior Arsenal squad.
