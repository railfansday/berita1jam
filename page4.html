The Center of the News Universe Has Shifted to CNBC

📅 Nov 23, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The most consequential stories of this moment in time are happening on the Versant brand, from the stunning fall and rise of Nvidia's Jensen Huang, to Anthropic's IPO.

On Monday morning, CNBC senior Washington correspondent Eamon Javers was driving in his car when the President of the United States called.

Javers pulled over to the side of the road, and within minutes he and President Trump were live on Squawk on the Street to talk about Nvidia‘s shock decision to bring back CEO Jensen Huang. Trump was on the show for nearly an hour, revealing his central role in securing Huang’s return.

In an interview with The Hollywood Reporter Monday afternoon, KC Sullivan, the president of CNBC, called this morning’s coverage of Huang and Nvidia a “really special moment.”

“The President was so generous with his time, but it was fun to see Eamon bring that in, and then to have [Jim] Cramer jump in, Sarah Eisen jump in, Carl Quintanilla jump in, David Faber jump in, and to be able to get all those different perspectives, I just think it made such a rich discussion,” Sullivan said. “I was really just so proud of the team and how they delivered.”

For months, the news business has been focused on the 2026 midterm elections, and for good reason. But the midterms are over, and suddenly it seems the most consequential stories of this moment in time are happening on CNBC.

Nvidia stock soared 31.8 percent on Monday after the news, and the entire stock market rallied. Huang and Nvidia are not only a business story, they are an economic story, a political story, a national security story. It’s a story of our time. For CNBC, it was also the second highest hour of web traffic this year, behind only SpaceX’s IPO in June.

“In the world of media now, it’s those who are essential to their audience and really understand their brand promise and deliver on that are the ones that are going to going to thrive,” Sullivan says. “I do think that there’s a lot of attention on the business world right now, and I don’t think there’s anybody who can compete with us in that arena.”

For CNBC, that audience includes Trump, as well as seemingly every CEO of note in America. “I want to congratulate you guys. You covered it really well,” Trump told the Squawk on the Street hosts during his marathon interview.

Huang, of course, is just the latest news. Last week, it was Cramer’s exclusive interview with Nvidia’s interim Colette Kress as the company reported its first (and only?) earnings report without Huang at the helm. Two days later Andrew Ross Sorkin had an exclusive interview with Anthropic co-founders Dario and Daniela Amodei as the AI company IPOd.

“It’s not like those things just happen. Those are long-term, trusted relationships,” Sullivan explains. Eamon has been working that relationship to the President for many months, which put him in a position to do that; Cramer’s relationship and trust with Nvidia is what allowed him to get that first interview with Colette; [Joe] Kernen’s got a really important, longstanding relationship with the Treasury Secretary so when he was playing a major role over the last two weeks, to be able to have him come on our air; Sorkin has the relationship into Anthropic. Those aren’t gimmes. Those aren’t just things that happen.”

Those moments all delivered double-digit ratings increases for CNBC on linear TV, and secured millions of social views and engagements, where they were shared, analyzed and moved the markets.

“At CNBC we pride ourselves on this, but we’re the place that the most influential people go to at the most important times and most important moments,” Sullivan says. “I think what it comes down to is that our audience wants to understand what is changing, what does it mean, and what do they do with that information? And that’s everything from the midterms to the AI trade. When they’re trying to to figure out what’s going on, they turn to us. And I think our guests know that as well.”

The TV news business can be fickle. General interest brands like CNN and NBC News can benefit immensely when major global news is happening, but tend to be consistent on a day-to-day basis. More partisan-leaning cable news channels like MS NOW and Fox News can rise and fall with the outcome of elections, or how people on the right or left side of the spectrum are feeling.

In the case of CNBC, the news cycle today is simply in their wheelhouse, and it is poised to continue over time as OpenAI weighs its own IPO, and with investigations into Nvidia assuredly coming from Congress (MS NOW may benefit from those too).

The CNBC boom also comes at a good time for the news brand, which is set to launch a new direct-to-consumer offering in the coming months. Sullivan is coy on what that specific offering will entail, but he notes that it will lean into its brand attributes of trust, and the live exclusives that the channel has become known for, be it an exclusive interview with the Amodei siblings, or having the President on the phone for nearly an hour.

“I think that what you’ll see when we roll out our direct-to-consumer product early next year is that we’re doubling down on that to deliver for the audience where and how they want it, making CNBC more useful and making CNBC easier to interact with and more rich for the consumer where and how they want it, but all built off of the same tenets that you saw today or over the last few weeks.”

Aiyuk hasn't played in a game since his 2024 torn ACL and MCL, which means he never played a snap on his four-year, $120 million extension he signed right before the start of that season. So which teams could be interested in the 28-year-old's services?

The Washington Commanders are the obvious landing spot. He has previously openly campaigned to join because of his connection with Jayden Daniels. Washington also has a glaring need at wide receiver. If Aiyuk really wanted to stick it to the 49ers, joining the NFC West rival Los Angeles Rams would be one way to do it. The Rams are on the cusp of the NFC's No. 7 seed, but it will be a slog for them to actually reach the playoffs without their best two wide receivers available. Puka Nacua is expected to miss the rest of the 2026 season after undergoing surgery for an MCL injury in his knee. Davante Adams, who led the league with 14 receiving touchdowns in 2025, is still sidelined with a hamstring injury that could have him out for a few more weeks.

That means quarterback Matthew Stafford, who has only been around league average this season with the No. 15 passer rating (92.8) and No. 13 expected points added (EPA) per dropback (0.08), needs to rediscover his 2025 NFL MVP form ASAP. Having Aiyuk could help expedite that process.

There's a chance Drake Maye doesn't return to action this season because of the fibula injury he suffered in Week 10 against the Detroit Lions. However, the Patriots (5-5) are just a game out of the AFC playoff picture. Blockbuster trade acquisition A.J. Brown has been a bust so far, and Romeo Doubs (shoulder) is on injured reserve. If Mike Vrabel wants to do everything he can to make sure the defending AFC champs don't miss out on the 2026 postseason, signing Aiyuk could make some sense.

China's Z.ai Says Its New AI Model Can Think Like One Four Times Its Size

📅 Nov 23, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

China's Z.ai Says Its New AI Model Can Think Like One Four Times Its Size

Chinese AI developer Z.ai released GLM-5.4 on Monday, introducing a new architecture that it claims delivers the reasoning capabilities of models nearly four times its size without requiring nearly as many parameters.

The Beijing-based company says its new 750-billion-parameter model can compete with systems containing two to three trillion parameters. It also supports a one-million-token context window, accepts images and video, and can generate up to 128,000 tokens in a single response, according to its technical documentation. Parameters are the internal values a model learns during training, while tokens are the chunks of text it processes and generates—roughly equivalent to words or parts of words.

The release comes as American companies including OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google dominate the highest positions in AI performance rankings. Chinese developers, meanwhile, have increasingly focused on making powerful models cheaper to run, particularly for coding and autonomous agents.

Z.ai has been among China's strongest competitors. As Decrypt reported in August, its predecessor GLM-5.3 was already competitive with leading American coding models, although it still trailed the best proprietary systems on several software engineering tests.

GLM-5.4 introduces what Z.ai calls a Loop Transformer, which processes information through the same 28 neural network layers twice. Traditional Transformers generally pass information through each layer once, so reusing layers effectively increases computational depth without increasing the number of stored parameters.

The company combines this approach with gated updates, which regulate how much information changes between passes, and a hybrid memory cache that avoids storing an entirely separate copy of previous calculations. Sparse attention also helps the model focus its computing power on relevant information rather than processing everything equally.

In controlled experiments on a smaller 30-billion-parameter model, Z.ai reported that its looping technique increased accuracy on AIME 2025, a mathematics examination benchmark scored by the percentage of correctly solved problems, from 19.17% to 30.42%.

GPQA Diamond, a graduate-level science question-answering test also scored by correct-answer percentage, improved from 47.58% to 56.26%. The experiments required approximately 10% additional training compute, while reducing generated reasoning tokens by 10% to 14% on several tests.

Those results concern the experimental architecture, not independently established performance for GLM-5.4 itself. Z.ai has not published a comprehensive flagship benchmark comparison demonstrating that the new model matches Claude or GPT.

The company is also promoting what it calls Generalized Coding: using programming to accomplish tasks beyond software development. Its demonstrations include building a video game, creating 3D environments, and designing a six-axis robotic arm using engineering software.

The company's previous flagship costs $1.40 per million input tokens and $4.40 per million output tokens through its API. By comparison, OpenAI's GPT-6.2 Sol costs $2 and $10, while Anthropic's Claude Opus 5.5 costs $4 and $20, respectively.

At those published rates, GLM-5.3 is considerably cheaper than its American counterparts, although GLM-5.4's own API pricing has not been announced. Its actual performance-per-dollar advantage therefore cannot yet be calculated.

China has other serious contenders. Alibaba's Qwen 4 Max recently topped Chinese competitors on Arena's agent leaderboard, while Moonshot's Kimi K3 and DeepSeek V4 have challenged American developers on coding performance and API pricing.

The performance gap remains contested. A U.S. government evaluation placed DeepSeek roughly eight months behind the American frontier, although researchers questioned its methodology.

Z.ai itself remains on the U.S. Commerce Department's Entity List, following its January 2025 designation over concerns that its AI research could support Chinese military modernization. The restrictions limit access to controlled American technology, but have not stopped the company from releasing new models.

Etihad Guest, the loyalty program of Abu Dhabi-based Etihad Airways, is expanding its U.S. presence by introducing two new credit cards. Most people won’t get much value from these cards, but they can make sense for Etihad frequent flyers who want elite status.

The two cards are the Etihad Guest Visa Signature® Card ($99 annual fee) and the Etihad Guest Premier Visa Signature® Card ($499 annual fee). Issued by the First National Bank of Omaha, both offer faster pathways to earning and maintaining elite status and the ability to earn extra miles on Etihad purchases.

They also offer extra miles on international transactions (neither has foreign transaction fees) and purchases with Etihad’s partner airlines, which include U.S. carriers like American Airlines and JetBlue Airways.

Here’s what you need to know about the cards.

The Etihad Guest Visa Signature® Card comes with a $99 annual fee, and it earns Etihad Guest miles at the following rates:

3 miles per $1 spent on Etihad purchases.

2 miles per $1 spent on Etihad partner airline purchases.

2 miles per $1 spent on non-U.S. purchases.

1 mile per $1 spent on all other U.S. purchases.

Welcome offer: Through Feb. 3, 2027, the card is offering the following welcome offer for new applicants: Earn up to 40,000 welcome Etihad Guest Miles, including 10,000 miles after the first purchase and an additional 30,000 miles after spending $3,000 within the first three billing cycles after account opening.

Elite status benefits: You’ll also get Etihad Silver status after your first Etihad purchase within 90 days of opening your account. Unlike most airlines, that entry-level status is enough to get you into the business class lounge when traveling through Etihad’s hub at Abu Dhabi’s Zayed International Airport.

You can retain that status by spending $20,000 a year on the card, or $3,000 a year on Etihad purchases.

The Etihad Guest Premier Visa Signature® Card comes with a $499 annual fee, and it earns Etihad Guest miles at the following rates:

5 miles per $1 spent on Etihad purchases.

3 miles per $1 spent on Etihad partner airline purchases.

2 miles per $1 spent on non-U.S. purchases.

1 mile per $1 spent on all other U.S. purchases.

The only differences in rewards between the premium and standard cards are increased earning rates for eligible airline purchases.

Welcome offer: Through Feb. 3, 2027, the premium card offers a higher welcome offer compared to the standard card: Earn up to 60,000 welcome Etihad Guest Miles, including 20,000 miles after the first purchase and 40,000 miles after spending $7,000 within the first three billing cycles after account opening.

Elite status benefits: The premium card offers a faster track to elite status: automatic Silver status upon card approval and Gold status after your first Etihad purchase within 90 days of opening your account.

To maintain Gold status, you’ll need to spend $40,000 a year on the card, or $8,000 on Etihad purchases. Otherwise, you’ll have Silver status.

Wi-Fi benefit: After your first Etihad purchase, you’ll get 3,000 Etihad Guest miles, enough to cover Wi-Fi costs on a long-haul Etihad flight. You’ll then get those miles again after each cardholder anniversary.

Travel benefits: The card offers a statement credit for Global Entry or TSA PreCheck and select travel protections. If you already have a premium travel card, you might already have access to these perks.

The card also includes a Priority Pass lounge access membership. However, an Etihad spokesperson told NerdWallet that it’s the entry-level membership tier, so you’ll need to pay for yourself and guests each time you visit a Priority Pass lounge. Most premium credit cards in the U.S. that offer Priority Pass allow complimentary visits for at least the cardholder.

The Etihad cards make the most sense for Etihad’s frequent flyers who are flying to or connecting through Abu Dhabi several times a year.

That’s because the standard card’s accelerated pathway to Etihad Guest’s Silver status tier can unlock the ability to choose business class lounge access in Abu Dhabi as a status perk.

With the premium card, you get a fast track to Gold. If you select lounge access as a Gold status perk, you can not only bring a guest on the same flight into the lounge, but also access Etihad’s worldwide lounge network. That includes Etihad’s own lounges in New York and Washington, D.C., as well as most partner lounges at other Etihad destinations.

Bear in mind that while Etihad has many partnerships with other airlines, it’s not part of an alliance, so you don’t get lounge benefits when traveling on partners. You’ll need to be flying on an Etihad-operated flight to get into the lounge.

If you primarily fly with other carriers, then Etihad status will have limited upside for you.

However, the Priority Pass lounge membership that comes with the premium card does not have that restriction. You can access Priority Pass lounges while flying on any airline, although you’ll need to pay an additional per-person $35 fee each visit.

The earning rates on the Etihad cards are lackluster for everyday spenders. The bonus categories are for airline purchases with Etihad and its partners, as well as international spending.

That means your day-to-day purchases at home, like groceries, dining out and gas, earn only 1 mile per $1 spent.

Plus, there are more ways to earn Etihad Guest miles than spending on an Etihad credit card. For example, you can transfer points to Etihad Guest from Bilt Rewards, Capital One and Citi ThankYou.

Those programs offer cards that typically have better benefits and bonus categories, and they offer flexibility on how you can use your points and miles. With an Etihad card, you can only earn Etihad Guest miles, which expire after 18 months without qualifying flight activity. (If you’re not traveling on Etihad or its partners at least once every 18 months, then you definitely should not get the cards.)

One of the most interesting perks of the new cards is the bonus category for spending with Etihad partner airlines — 2 miles per $1 for the standard card and 3 miles per $1 for the premium version. Typically, airline credit cards reward customers only for spending on the airline, not partners.

There are better cards available, though. Some offer as many or even more points per $1 on flights purchased directly from airlines.

If you want the card solely to earn more Etihad Guest miles, then the answer for most people is no.

While you can earn more miles for purchases with Etihad and partner airlines, the earning rates aren’t attractive enough to justify the annual fees, especially when compared with existing cards with currencies that transfer to Etihad Guest.

Status chasers could benefit from the fast track to elite status — but you would need to be a fairly frequent Etihad flyer to really take advantage of your elite benefits.

Hands-on: Baseus’ new PicoGo Pro USB-C charger is clever, cute, and on sale

📅 Nov 23, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

USB-C chargers are pretty standardized at this point, but there’s a fun new trend of adding displays to your power brick that I’ve been very much enjoying, and the new PicoGo Pro from Baseus is a great example with three ports, optimized charging, and a cute little character to top it off – and for Black Friday, it’s pretty deeply discounted, too.

The Baseus PicoGo Pro (AH22) is a 100W, 3-port USB-C charger designed to top off your laptop, phone, and/or tablet, even all at the same time. For anyone who travels often or just has a lot of devices on hand, chargers like this are a must. Having something you don’t have to think twice about when it comes to charging rates is just so nice, and these have come a long way, too.

Using GaN tech, PicoGo Pro is very compact at about 2 inches tall and a little over an inch wide. I remember having single-port chargers that could barely fast-charge my phone that were twice this size just a few years ago. GaN just absolutely rules, and Baseus is using a special version that reduces heat and improves efficiency.

But the two reasons I really like this charger aren’t strictly to do with GaN.

The first is the built-in display. Again, that’s a trend throughout the industry. Anker has several chargers out with built-in displays as the company helped to really make this popular – the Anker Prime 160W brick is really similar to PicoGo Pro (at a higher price), and it’s the only I’ve been using for months before trying out this Baseus model.

Baseus has a cute little animated character that accompanies your charging, with little eyes when the charger isn’t active and some fun animations to show your charging rate in real-time. Neat! You can “press” the button to move through the UI and even see the specific charging rate of each connected device. You’ll also see that the brick can recognize the device you have plugged in, though Android devices just show a generic “SAMSUNG” or whatever brand you plug in – Anker, again, had a similar set of features in a brick released earlier in 2026, but some of those features were iPhone-only, and that brick was only a 1-port, 45W charger anyway.

But, like that Anker brick, Baseus supports “smart” charging that adjusts to your device. Plugging in a Galaxy Z Fold 8 that was around 65% full, I saw the brick adjust its charging speed to tailor to the phone, initially going up to full speed before settling down around 28W~. You can manually adjust this with “Turbo” or “Gentle” modes, the former maxing out the charging rate and the latter settling in around 15W for a slower charge. That flexibility is great because it means you can adjust the charging rate to what you need in that moment. Full-speed for a quick top-off on a busy day, slower charging overnight to preserve battery health.

Baseus isn’t doing much new here, though the GaN tech mentioned earlier is enhanced by some architecture and algorithm adjustments the company has made behind the scenes, but it’s a really good implementation.

Beyond that, it’s also priced pretty well.

Baseus PicoGo Pro’s full price is $59.99, but you’ll probably rarely see it there. Technically, today is its launch date, and it’s already down by over 30% at $39.70 for Black Friday. Value-wise, this one is hard to beat.

Flightless kākāpō parrots from New Zealand are genetically healthy despite 10,000 years of inbreeding

📅 Nov 23, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Scientists sequenced the DNA of New Zealand's endangered kākāpō and found that 10,000 years of inbreeding actually helped natural selection clear out harmful genetic mutations.

On New Zealand’s Stewart Island, flightless parrots called kākāpō spent about 10,000 years cut off from the rest of their species, breeding with relatives. In theory, that should have left the birds with more and more harmful mutations.

A 2021 study of their DNA found the opposite: the island kākāpō carried fewer harmful mutations than mainland kākāpō, which have since died out.

The researchers wrote that the finding is evidence a small population can survive hundreds of generations of isolation. The species is still critically endangered, though, and many of its eggs fail to hatch.

Nicolas Dussex, a researcher at the Centre for Palaeogenetics in Stockholm, led the study. Few birds anywhere are as inbred or as endangered as the kākāpō, Dussex said.

Kākāpō likely numbered in the hundreds of thousands before people reached New Zealand. Overhunting and predators introduced by people reduced them to 51 birds by 1995.

Fifty came from Stewart Island. The other was a mainland male named Richard Henry.

When the study came out, 201 kākāpō were alive, all on island sanctuaries.

For 49 kākāpō, Dussex’s team analyzed each bird’s complete set of DNA, called its genome. Thirty-five were Stewart Island birds.

The rest were mainland birds: Richard Henry and 13 museum specimens. Those specimens were collected between 1847 and 1924.

An inbred bird’s DNA has a recognizable pattern. A chick gets one copy of its genome from each parent. When the parents are related, long stretches of the two copies match.

In the Stewart Island birds, matching stretches made up 53% of the genome on average. In the mainland birds, matching stretches made up 15%.

So the island birds were far more inbred. The team then counted the most damaging kind of mutation, the kind predicted to stop a gene from working.

Mainland birds carried 34.1 of those mutations on average. Island birds carried 17.6 of them, about half as many.

Many of these mutations seem to do harm mainly when a bird inherits the same one from both parents. That happens more often when the parents are related.

Chicks that inherit the mutation from both parents are less likely to survive or breed, the team suggested. Over many generations, the mutation becomes rarer. Geneticists call that process purging.

The team tested the idea with computer simulations spanning 25,000 years. A simulated population that stayed small, like Stewart Island’s, ended up with fewer harmful mutations than a larger one like the mainland’s.

But in a simulated population one-tenth as large as the island’s, the harm from mutations grew instead. So purging seems to need enough birds for natural selection to work.

After the kākāpō’s recent decline, the team wrote, the birds may now face the same growing harm as that smallest population.

Conservation managers have favored matings with Richard Henry and his offspring, to help the population keep its ability to adapt.

“We show that the single male survivor from the mainland, Richard Henry, has more harmful mutations than Stewart Island birds,” said Love Dalén of the Swedish Museum of Natural History, who supervised the study.

“Therefore, there could be a risk that these harmful mutations spread in future generations.”

Richard Henry was also genetically distinct, Dalén added, and may have carried useful genetic diversity. The team recommended weighing that benefit against the risk and, if necessary, limiting breeding between the two family lines.

Dussex’s team counted mutations in DNA and didn’t measure what they do to a living bird. Their effects on survival and breeding weren’t known.

The team called for research into the genetic causes of the kākāpō’s low fertility and hatching success.

In some other shrinking species, harmful mutations increased as numbers fell, the authors wrote. In others, the mutations decreased.

Next, the team planned to examine other highly inbred birds and mammals, to learn whether the kākāpō is a rare exception.

The full study was published in the journal Cell Genomics.

10 Mad Magazine Mascot Alfred P. Neuman Quotes To Live By

📅 Nov 23, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

The humor periodical Mad started off as a comic in 1952, before taking on its more recognizable magazine with 1955's Mad #24. That cover featured a prominent appearance by the caricature of a character who would come to be known as Alfred E. Neuman. A few issues later, on the cover of Mad #30, Alfred took center stage, officially becoming the magazine's figurehead and mascot.

From its inception, issues of Mad included an epigraph, a quote from a historical figure, politician, or celebrity that set the tone. The first time Mad opened with a quote attributed to Alfred E. Neuman was Mad #38. From that point on, Alfred has had the first, and definitive, line in practically every issue of the magazine since.

Sometimes Alfred's quotes are bawdy, and sometimes they are politically charged. Often, though, they are low-key life lessons, the kind of wisdom that only a comic character can get away with sharing.

"It's a good idea to keep your words soft and sweet because you never know when you'll have to eat them!" Here, Alfred E. Neuman riffs on a centuries-old idiom. Be careful what comes out of your mouth, Neuman warns, because if what you say ends up getting shoved back in your face, down your own throat, you might find yourself choking on the tone and the meanings of your words.

It is sensible advice, especially for comedians, but knowing Alfred, and knowing Mad magazine, this is really a "do as I say, not as I do" case of cautionary advice.

"You'll never get rid of a bad temper by losing it!" There's another saying, which is that you can't control your immediate reaction to something, but you can control your reaction to the reaction. If you're prone to getting angry, giving into it is only going to make the problem worse, that's what Alfred E. Neuman is saying here.

It is a slyly clever bit of wordplay. In fact, this bon mot could be remixed into a riddle. "What can you never get rid of by losing it?" "A bad temper!" The cover for this issue, Mad #71, shows Alfred's mug at the center of a dartboard, and with its opening quote at least, the magazine hit the comedic bullseye.

"Learn from the mistakes of others." That is age-old wisdom. But why? That part is usually left unsaid, but leave it to Alfred E. Neuman to say the quiet part loud. It is "'cause you'll never live long enough to make 'em all yourself!"

Before it was a film franchise, National Lampoon was a groundbreaking American humor magazine; these are the greatest covers from its 3-decade history

Life is finite. There's only so much failure and foible the average person can cram into one lifetime. Recognizing and avoiding others' failures isn't about being more likely to succeed in your own endeavors. It is simply a time-saving exercise. Plus, in some cases, those mistakes might have been fatal, and avoiding them is the only way to guarantee a long life full non-lethal errors in judgment.

The art of the one-liner is trickier than people give it credit for. (Just ask Henny Youngman.) In just a few words, the speaker has to set up and execute the punchline, but there is more to it than that. The line has to conjure an image in people's heads. And that image can be vague, or it can be concrete.

Here, it is literally the latter. "Some minds are like concrete," Alfred E. Neuman says in Mad #82, in the sense that they are "all mixed up and permanently set." In other words, some people are defiantly confused. Obstinately misguided. Flat out wrong and unwilling to revise their opinions. Don't be like those people, Alfred suggests. Be willing to admit your wrong.

Long before Gordan Gecko told the world "greed is good" in Wall Street, Alfred E. Neuman gave Mad readers the real truth about cold hard cash. "Sometimes, the best scheme for doubling your money is to fold it in half and stuff it back in your wallet," Neuman declared in Mad #87.

Meaning, get-rich-quick schemes and seemingly lucrative investments are often not all they seemed cracked up to be. Everyone wants to hustle, and maximize their earnings, but sometimes the smart thing to do is just stack paper. Save up. For Alfred, a folded dollar is worth more than one that is tied up in escrow.

The funny thing about Alfred E. Neuman's advice is that he was often both the angel and the devil on readers' shoulders. His line in Mad #145 exemplifies this. "Sometimes it pays to resist a temptation," he begins. Sounds totally reasonable, and also morally sound, right?

Well, let him finish. Because Alfred also answers why it pays. Not because having will power and self-control are important. Not because resisting temptation is the right thing. No, because temptation is a gamble. Why take the one in front of you when "a better one may be coming along." This hilariously, and cheekily, takes the matter from an ethical equation to a practical matter of when it is the right time to do the wrong thing.

"Experience is something you never have until just after you need it!" This quote from Mad #204 is the perfect companion to the issue's cover, which parodies the Jaws 2 poster, with Alfred E. Neuman piloting a mechanical shark to sneak up on a jet-skiing girl in a bikini.

In the movie, only Chief Brody has the ability to save the day, because only he has the experience of having fought a killer great white before. For most people, most of the time, the toughest moments of their lives are trial and error, giving them the experience that would have made it easier on them only after the fact.

This quote from Mad #221 evokes the earlier entry on this list from Mad #54. It is another way for Alfred E. Neuman to remind readers: "Watch what you say." Especially if you've already gotten yourself in trouble by talking too much.

"When you're in deep water, it's a good idea to keep your mouth shut!" It is a great case of applying a literal lesson to figurative language. If you're in proverbial deep water, or hot water, or any kind of water, talking, or shouting, is only going to result in one thing. Drowning.

Mad #267's cover features a Top Gun parody, and Alfred E. Neuman's quote in the issue makes it clear what kind of people he'd like to strap to the nose of a fighter jet and send flying. "People on ego trips," who, Alfred says bluntly, "should buy one-way tickets."

It is a slick play on words, but the point doesn't mince any. If you are ego-driven and willing to do whatever it takes to feed your own sense of self, take a hike. Take a vacation. Take off and don't come back. For Mad fans, it is a reminder to check themselves before they wreck themselves, and ruin someone else's day too.

The theme of Mad #293's Alfred E. Neuman quote is the same as the prior entry. And it contains essentially the same valuable life lesson. Which is, life is hard for everyone, so don't go around making it harder.

Or as Alfred puts it: "It's not just the ups and downs that make life difficult. It's the jerks." People who go around being miserable all the time need to do more than just sit down and read a Mad magazine, or a Far Side comic, or even a Garfield, and try to lighten up. They need to think about how they are impacting other people. That's what Mad's irreverent, but often on-point mascot is saying here.

The 6 Most Outrageous GTA 6 Myths, Debunked

📅 Nov 23, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The internet is already drowning in GTA 6 misinformation

After a wild opening weekend, Grand Theft Auto 6 is off to the races. Millions are playing Rockstar’s enormous open-world game and discovering its complexities in real time. There’s a lot to unpack in a game that’s filled with intersecting gameplay systems across a long story, and some players are already taking advantage of that. At the same time, the opening days of GTA 6’s launch have been filled with misinformation, much of it fueled by culture warriors and content creators looking to turn outrage into clicks on social media platforms that incentivize such behavior.

Because of that, there are a lot of myths about GTA 6 currently floating around the internet. Some come from harmless misunderstandings, while others are more deliberate misrepresentations intended to scare off potential players. To help you navigate the noise, we’ve rounded up some of the most rampant myths currently circulating around GTA 6 and fact-checked them accordingly.

The claim: GTA 6’s story forces you to do chores for Lucia instead of crimes.

If you spent launch day watching reactions to GTA 6’s early hours roll in instead of playing, you might have walked away thinking that Rockstar made a chore simulator. That’s thanks to a few early missions that revolve around Jason getting his home ready for Lucia as she’s about to be released from prison. Players will paint a bedroom for her, fix a leaky roof, and repair some stairs. There’s also an optional quest where players can run errands to prepare for Lucia's arrival, like picking up groceries or buying her a necklace. All of this happens in the first few hours of the game. GTA 6 quickly ramps up after that, getting into the series’ usual flow of cop chases, heists, and shootouts. You can call it a slow start, but know that any mundane missions you’ve seen make up a very small amount of story that you’re likely to see in your very first play session.

The claim: GTA 6 doesn’t allow you to steal cars, or heavily restricts what you can steal.

While there’s a grain of truth to this, it has been heavily exaggerated on social media. There are some select cars that you can’t steal in GTA 6, like those around Jason’s neighborhood or in an NPC’s car lot. But for the most part, you can jack just about any vehicle you see in Leonida. The catch is that you’ll need a device that can copy keys to get into some of the more expensive cars, and you won’t get that until Chapter 3. Being able to steal anything requires some patience as you play through the story, but be rest assured that you can do as much grand theft auto as ever in Grand Theft Auto.

The claim: Airplanes will bounce off of buildings instead of exploding on impact.

This one is a little tricky to unpack because there seem to be two weird things colliding here. After videos of airplanes bouncing off of skyscrapers like rubber began to emerge on social media, players deduced that content creators were sharing videos where they had their Reduced Vehicle Damage setting turned on. It seemed like a textbook case of outrage farming, though there may be a slight bit of truth here. Some players have speculated that GTA 6 may have a few physics bugs that make it tough for certain airliners to blow up as expected. That may be an edge case, but I can confirm that I have crashed plenty of planes into buildings in GTA 6, and they have all exploded into a fiery wreck so far.

The claim: GTA 6 features no autosave, and you will lose progress if you don’t manually save.

When players started reporting that they were losing hours of gameplay because GTA 6 didn’t have an autosave feature, I was very confused. I can open my save file right now and see a dedicated auto save slot that is regularly refreshed as I play. I initially wrote it off as players either lying or not realizing that the auto save only triggers after completing major missions, but it turns out they weren’t hallucinating. GTA 6’s first patch fixed an issue where auto save wasn’t triggering properly. So, yes, GTA 6 does have autosave; it just wasn’t working correctly for some players at launch. It shouldn’t be a problem now, but always remember to save early and often just in case.

The claim: Lucia is cheating on Jason during the story.

This one is downright bizarre. One of the first talking points that came out about GTA 6 was that Jason is a “cuck” who does not realize that Lucia is cheating on him. As someone who has played through the story, I spent days trying to figure out where in the world that blatant lie could be coming from. It turns out it was a good old misinterpretation. Early in the story, Lucia sends a text to an unknown character, which she rushes to hide from Jason when he enters the scene. While some have interpreted that as Lucia having a secret lover, it actually turns out that she was texting a friend from prison. To players’ credit, the scene does feel like it’s built to make you think Lucia is hiding a big secret from Jason, so that reaction might have been exactly what Rockstar was hoping to provoke.

The claim: GTA 6 does not allow you to open fire and kill people in a gay club.

The ugliest rumor to come out of GTA 6 so far is that the game will not allow you to walk into a gay club in Vice City and kill people. There was even a clip going around social media that seemed to support that, sparking some grotesque reactions. The truth is that the location in the video isn’t specifically a gay club. It is a club, and it’s true that you can’t open fire in it, but that’s only because you can’t shoot inside any establishment in GTA 6. This claim would technically be true if there was a gay club in the game, but only as a side effect of the wider systems working the way they do. What is true, though, is that the game features an unkillable non-binary character, according to the actor who portrayed them.
