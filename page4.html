Max, il calcio semplice è finito: la rivincita di Farioli. Anche contro chi non l’ha scelto

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Il calcio è semplice, sostiene da sempre Massimiliano Allegri. Poi arriva Francesco Farioli, gli rifila quattro gol e dimostra che la semplicità, di questi tempi, è il risultato di un lavoro molto più complesso. Il 4-1 del Porto al Napoli non è una sentenza definitiva su due allenatori, ma racconta parecchio delle differenze tra due modi di intendere il calcio. Allegri continua a rivendicare la centralità dei giocatori, dell'esperienza e della gestione dei momenti. Farioli studia, prepara, organizza e interviene. Vince. Al Dragao ha trovato la prima vittoria in questa Champions League dopo tre sconfitte consecutive, sfruttando anche le difficoltà di un Napoli decimato dagli infortuni. Ma le assenze non contano più dell'attenzione ai dettagli.

Farioli non deve più dimostrare di essere un allenatore interessante. Ha 37 anni, ha lavorato in Turchia, Francia e Olanda, ha perso uno scudetto con l'Ajax in maniera clamorosa e si è rialzato conquistando il campionato portoghese con il Porto. In questa stagione è ripartito con dieci vittorie nelle prime dieci giornate di Liga Portugal. Nel frattempo, in Italia, le grandi società continuano a rivolgersi prevalentemente agli allenatori che conoscono già, spesso appartenenti alla stessa generazione. Per carità, non è obbligatorio scegliere un tecnico giovane per essere moderni. Ma l'esperienza non può diventare l'unico criterio di selezione. Farioli ha costruito all'estero un curriculum che molti suoi colleghi, considerati più affidabili soltanto perché hanno allenato in Serie A, possono invidiargli.

C'è anche un risvolto particolarmente significativo. Aurelio De Laurentiis apprezza Farioli da anni, lo ha seguito e valutato. Quando ha dovuto scegliere il successore di Antonio Conte, però, il Napoli ha puntato su Allegri, preferito anche a Vincenzo Italiano. Ieri sera, il presidente azzurro ha visto cosa avrebbe potuto portare a Napoli quell'allenatore che conosceva così bene e che non ha mai scelto. Alla vigilia Farioli aveva scherzato sull'interesse del club partenopeo, definendolo ormai andato in prescrizione. Il campo gli ha regalato una soddisfazione decisamente più concreta. Il problema, per Allegri e per il calcio italiano, non è riconoscere che Farioli abbia vinto una partita. È accettare che esistano allenatori capaci di ottenere risultati attraverso percorsi diversi, senza avere alle spalle vent'anni di panchine nella nostra Serie A. Max ha conquistato sei scudetti e raggiunto due finali di Champions League: nessuna sconfitta cancellerà quei risultati. Ma il passato non può essere una garanzia per il presente, né un argomento per liquidare chi propone qualcosa di diverso. Farioli, ormai, è più di una semplice proposta.

Critical security vulnerability affects OpenSSH on Alpine Linux systems

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Version 10.6p2 of OpenSSH has been released with a workaround for a critical vulnerability that affects Linux systems that use musl libc with PAM disabled, such as Alpine Linux. OpenSSH developer Damien Miller said:

If you operate a Linux system that uses musl libc without PAM (Alpine Linux is one example) then stop reading and set PasswordAuthentication=no or disable SSH for these systems IMMEDIATELY.

This vulnerability does not exist on non-Linux systems, Linux/glibc systems and Linux systems that use PAM for password authentication.

We consider the root cause of this bug to be outside OpenSSH itself, but we're in a good position to deliver a mitigation quickly.

The Alpine Linux project has announced that it will be backporting the OpenSSH fix "all the way back to Alpine 3.16", which was released in May 2022.

Air Serbia Flight To Nowhere: A320 Forced To Return To Belgrade Over Mysterious Security Concern

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: Politics  |  Read Source →

An Air Serbia flight to Moscow made it as far as Lithuania before turning around and returning to Belgrade over a security concern involving one passenger. Serbian media identified that passenger as the country’s former intelligence chief, who allegedly boarded without completing security screening. He denies wrongdoing.

As flagged by One Mile At A Time, Air Serbia flight JU134 departed Belgrade for Moscow on November 3, 2026, but turned back over southern Lithuania. According to Serbian media, the Airbus A320 left at 6:48 PM and returned shortly before 10:00 PM. Passengers spent more than three hours flying only to end up where they started.

Air Serbia confirmed that airport authorities requested additional security checks concerning one passenger. The airline said it returned in accordance with the instructions it received, but did not publicly identify that passenger.

The passenger in question has since been identified as Aleksandar Vulin, Serbia’s former intelligence chief. It appears that someone helped him board without completing the required screening and that the airport’s corporate security director ordered the return after reviewing surveillance footage.

Vulin insists he completed the required checks, was carrying no prohibited items, and was being targeted for political reasons (his pro-Russian party lost in a snap election last month). He said his trip concerned negotiations over Serbia’s gas arrangement with Russia.

Passengers and their luggage underwent additional screening in Belgrade. The flight eventually continued to Moscow with a replacement crew, arriving about seven hours late.

If the reports are accurate, the obvious question is how someone was allowed to board without completing the required checks. Political prominence should not provide an exemption from an aviation security procedure, though I understand how easy it is to open an employee access door for a VIP).

But Vulin disputes that account, and the airline’s statement establishes only that authorities requested additional screening. That leaves important questions about what happened before departure and what prompted the return. Vulin was allowed to continue on the delayed flight to Moscow.

Belgrade’s Higher Public Prosecutor’s Office has requested an investigation into the circumstances, including whether procedures were followed, who authorized the aircraft’s return, and what security assessment supported that decision.

That inquiry is appropriate. If someone bypassed screening, there should be accountability for how it happened. If the checks were properly completed, authorities need to explain why an aircraft full of passengers was brought back.

This is all may be Serbian domestic politics…

I hope the investigation establishes whether someone received preferential treatment or whether there is another explanation for this extraordinary return. Either way, the passengers arrived very late into Moscow.

Wolverine Has Sold Fewer Copies In 50 Days Than Spider-Man 2 Sold In Just One

📅 Nov 05, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Wolverine had an uncharacteristically rocky launch for Insomniac, and it looks like its reviews and the backlash towards some of its less popular mechanics may have had an impact on sales after all. While the game has sold in the millions, it’s not as many millions as you might expect from a game with one of Marvel’s most popular heroes as the lead.

Sony published its latest financial report today, and within it are updates on how well some of its first-party games have sold. Even though the report only covers the most recent quarter, which ended on September 30, 2026, it has sales figures for Wolverine’s first 50 days, right up until November 4.

According to those figures, Wolverine has sold through 2.4 million copies. That’s a big number. A number that most studios would bite your hand off for. However, those aren’t Marvel numbers, as is demonstrated by how well Spider-Man 2 sold at launch, which you can see from the webslinger’s figures a few rows up on the same Sony document.

According to that document, Spider-Man 2 sold 2.5 million copies during its first 24 hours. That means, despite having 50 times longer to hit that target, Wolverine has still fallen short. There’s an argument to be made that it’s unfair to compare the two, but it’s also unfair to suggest there is such a disparity between how well-known the heroes are that it should take Wolverine 50 times longer to hit the same targets.

There is no world in which a Wolverine game is, or was ever expected to, sell anywhere close to as well as a Spider-Man game. Spider-Man is among the most popular and recognizable characters on the planet. You give people the chance to be him in a video game, and even if that game is bad, it will sell tens of millions of copies.

Thankfully, none of them are. All three of Insomniac’s Spidey games are critically acclaimed and well-loved by fans.

Wolverine, while also immensely popular, doesn’t have that same cache, and therefore doesn’t have the luxury of his games selling millions just because his face is on the cover. While that has still happened to an extent, word getting out that the game might not be up to Insomniac’s Spidey standard seems to have had an effect, and stunted sales to a greater degree than PlayStation would have expected.

Despite the pre-launch discourse, Wolverine is a hit with fans.

Thailand’s SEC filed a criminal complaint against Bitget’s operator, with access to its website and app set to be blocked from Dec. 5.

Thailand’s securities regulator has filed a criminal complaint against BG Limited, the operator of cryptocurrency exchange Bitget, for allegedly operating without a license.

Thailand’s Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) said Thursday that it had notified the Ministry of Digital Economy and Society to block access to Bitget from Dec. 5. The restriction will cover Bitget’s website, other associated domains and its mobile app.

The regulator advised users to take action regarding assets held on the platform before the deadline.

According to the SEC, Bitget offered crypto trading services between 2022 and 2025, charged trading fees and promoted its services through Thai-language websites and social media channels. The regulator said these activities violated Thailand’s digital asset business licensing requirements.

The regulator filed the complaint with Thailand’s Economic Crime Suppression Division for investigation. Prosecutors will decide whether to bring charges, while courts will determine criminal liability.

Thailand’s Ministry of Digital Economy and Society has authority to restrict access to unlicensed crypto trading platforms under a 2025 decree aimed at protecting investors and combating financial crime.
