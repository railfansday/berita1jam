Fascia Release Is the Key to Regulating Your Nervous System, Getting Better Sleep, and Improving Skin

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Fascia, the thin web that envelops the face and body, is more powerful than you might think. Here’s how to release it

I’m curled up on a treatment bed in the Corinthia spa, feeling a knot of tension dissipate slowly from my neck and shoulders. Within minutes, tears prick my eyes. I’m familiar with the sudden catharsis bodywork can bring, but this is different. Known as “myofascial release”—a targeted therapy that works with fascia, the continuous matrix of connective tissue that wraps every muscle, nerve, and organ—it aims to reduce tightness and release restricted tissue, restoring its natural glide.

“Fascia is deeply connected to the entire sensory network,” explains Nadia Alibhai, a London-based osteopath who was unfazed by my heightened emotions. “When you move or hold stress, forces are transmitted through this interconnected system. It senses everything and dictates how the whole body responds. This treatment directly influences how we perceive and react to tension.”

Once dismissed as mere biological wrapping paper, fascia—made up of water, collagen, elastin, and hyaluronic acid—is now understood as a living, three-dimensional web. “It’s part of a complex system involving movement, circulation, lymphatics, posture, and tissue health,” Alibhai explains. When it works well, it keeps everything moving and prevents systems from becoming sluggish, but when it becomes restricted, often from scrolling, sedentary habits, or chronic stress, the fluid between the layers can thicken. This affects physical mobility and emotional well-being, leading to issues ranging from pain and stiffness to a dysregulated nervous system.

Fascia and neurology are intrinsically linked, explains skin-health expert Annee de Mamiel. “Fascia changes according to how we move, breathe, and repeatedly load the body,” she says. “Cortisol and adrenaline change our physiology; the nervous system alters breathing, muscular tone, and stress response. Over time, these repeated patterns affect the tissues.” It’s a catch-22: greater stress can tighten fascia, which in turn can send your body into fight-or-flight mode, prolonging the anxiety that caused the damage in the first place.

This mind-body connection is just one reason why fascia has piqued the wellness industry’s interest. Google searches for “fascia release” have soared in the past year, while facials targeting the system are becoming more popular. Robert Schleip, the director of the Fascia Research Group at Ulm University, attributes this to its holistic possibilities. “Fascia is now studied in relation to the autonomic nervous system, and therefore emotional regulation, as well as the immune system and aging,” he says. The latest findings show that supporting your fascia goes far beyond easing a tight neck; it's linked to reduced arterial stiffness and improved cardiovascular health.

Fascia release has also entered the skin-health conversation. Tight fascia can be an overlooked culprit behind loss of luminosity, puffiness, and premature aging, says de Mamiel, because it’s heavily involved in fluid drainage and lymphatic pathways. “The diaphragm plays a role in pressure changes and fluid movement through the body, and the fascial system connects these areas,” she explains. “When I work on movement and tension through the neck, chest, and rib cage, I often see a change in how congested or puffy the face appears.” However, releasing fascia effectively doesn't require aggressive force. “If I use so much pressure that your body braces, I’ve produced the opposite response—it’s about creating conditions where the nervous system and tissue can both settle.”

Facialist Nichola Joss achieves similarly impressive results with her signature Inner Facial, which fuses lymphatic drainage, fascia release, buccal massage, and sculpting techniques. “Fascia surrounds and supports our muscles; when restricted, it affects how freely muscles move,” Joss explains. This affects lymphatic flow and your skin’s natural glow. “By releasing muscular and fascial tension, you lift, tone, and sculpt contours while boosting circulation and softening fine lines.”

Beyond professional treatments, supporting fascial health depends on consistent daily habits. First, fascia thrives on varied movement: try walking, twisting your body, reaching overhead, and squatting to support overall health. Unfortunately, there are no true quick fixes: Spending the day glued to an office chair and then going straight into an intense HIIT session can actually be counterproductive. Instead, interrupt patterns throughout the day: Stand during phone calls instead of sitting, swing your arms gently during screen breaks, and make sure you’re not holding your breath during stressful periods at work. Counteract hours spent hunched over a device with this simple doorway stretch: Place your forearms against the frame, step forward gently, and hold for 20 to 30 seconds while breathing deeply.

Schleip recommends aligning exercise with your circadian rhythm. “Incorporate dynamic, elastic, or bouncing movements in the morning, ideally outdoors, and slow stretches in the evening,” he suggests, adding that sustained yoga positions, such as a child’s pose held for at least 30 seconds, can be particularly helpful. “Regular stretching improves joint mobility and may benefit cardiovascular markers such as arterial stiffness,” he adds. Finally, make nutrition and hydration a priority: fascia is 70 percent water and needs hydration to glide freely.

As Alibhai reflects at the end of my session: “The goal isn’t to constantly release fascia; it’s to keep your body moving, adaptable, and well nourished.” I leave feeling lighter and more grounded—and I instinctively skip the Tube and walk home.

Ella Langley’s ‘Choosin’ Texas’ Ties Hot Country Songs Record With 50 Weeks at No. 1

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The hit matches Bebe Rexha and Florida Georgia Line's "Meant to Be."

Ella Langley’s “Choosin’ Texas” logs a 50th week atop Billboard’s Hot Country Songs chart (dated Nov. 28), matching the all-time record set by Bebe Rexha and Florida Georgia Line’s “Meant to Be” in 2017-18.

“Choosin’ Texas” totaled 16.9 million official streams, 45.5 million in airplay audience and 6,000 sales in the United States Nov. 13-19, according to Luminate.

The song also extends its Billboard Hot 100 record to 29 weeks at No. 1.

Here’s a rundown of the five longest-leading Hot Country Songs hits (since the list became the genre’s singular songs chart in October 1958):

“Meant to Be” debuted atop Hot Country Songs in December 2017 and held the summit for 50 consecutive weeks. “Choosin’ Texas” reached No. 1 in its sixth week, in February, and led for 28 frames before Taylor Swift’s “I Knew It, I Knew You” interrupted its reign for two weeks in June. Langley reclaimed the summit on the July 4 chart and has remained there for 22 more weeks in a row.

The shared milestone also links two distinct moments in country’s crossover history. “Meant to Be” emerged during a sonically experimental boom, pairing pop star Rexha with FGL, a country cornerstone act in the 2010s, as genre-blurring hits helped define the era. The collaboration reached No. 2 on the Hot 100.

“Choosin’ Texas,” meanwhile, has contributed to country’s prominence this decade.

Streaming and radio airplay at country, pop and adult formats helped sustain both hits’ extraordinary longevity. “Meant to Be” spent 55 weeks atop Country Streaming Songs, with “Choosin’ Texas” up to 44. Their commands on Country Airplay were one and three weeks, respectively (reflecting radio’s more focused programming cycles).

Both songs also hit the top 10 on Pop Airplay, Adult Pop Airplay and Adult Contemporary, and sold strongly. “Meant to Be” reached No. 2 on Digital Song Sales over a yearlong stay on the chart; “Choosin’ Texas” has led for a record 22 weeks and counting.

Check Billboard.com next Monday, Nov. 30, to find out if “Choosin’ Texas” can pass “Meant to Be” and give Langley sole possession of the Hot Country Songs No. 1 longevity record.

Freddy Peralta lands with Orioles: After post-trade turnaround, ALCS MVP scores five-year, $125 million deal

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

If not listed, please contact your TV provider.

Veteran right-handed starter Freddy Peralta has signed with the Baltimore Orioles on a five-year deal, the team announced on Tuesday. The contract is worth $125 million with no deferrals or options, The Athletic reports.

Peralta, who will be going into his age-31 season in 2027, is coming off a campaign in which he didn't pitch up to his usual standards during a disastrous stint with the New York Mets. However, he did more like his old self following a deadline trade to the Tampa Bay Rays. On the Rays' watch, Peralta made a series of adjustments that helped him find better results on the mound, including being named the ALCS MVP.

Earlier this offseason, CBS Sports ranked Peralta as the No. 7 free agent of the 2026-27 class. Here's part of our write-up:

"It has been a roller coaster year for Peralta, who flopped with the Mets, rebounded with the Rays, then faced the Brewers (his former team) in the World Series and lost. The post-deadline stint with Tampa salvaged Peralta's free agent year. They made several adjustments, most notably raising his arm angle and getting him to pitch exclusively from the stretch, and the result was a livelier fastball and also better command. He's still five-and-fly Freddy. Even the best version of Peralta won't pitch deep into games and can be a tough watch because he nibbles and throws so many pitches per inning. They tend to be good innings, though, and the guy never misses a start. Worst case, Peralta is a mid-rotation starter you can count on to take the ball every fifth day. The best case is someone who can front a contender's rotation. Fastball Freddy was always going to be a great bounceback candidate this offseason. The Rays just beat everyone to it at the trade deadline."

Peralta isn't far removed from a 2023-25 stretch for the Milwaukee Brewers that saw him top 30 starts and 200 strikeouts in three straight seasons. In 2025, he earned his second All-Star selection and finished fifth in the National League Cy Young vote.

"Freddy has proven to be a winning player who understands what it takes to compete and succeed on the biggest stage," said Orioles president of baseball operations Mike Elias said in a statement. "His professionalism, preparation, and commitment to winning will set a strong example for our organization and we believe he will have a significant impact on our team both on and off the field."

After losing Trevor Rogers to free agency (he agreed to a five-year contract with the Chicago Cubs earlier on Tuesday), the Orioles were searching for a new frontline starter to join a rotation that includes Kyle Bradish, Shane Baz and some combination of Brandon Young, Cade Povich and Trey Gibson. Elias has been hesitant to pay up for pitchers recently, but either his strategy has changed or he decided Peralta was worth the cost.

The Orioles' disappointing fourth-place AL East finish in 2026 has raised questions about the state of Baltimore's rebuild, particularly amid stalled growth from its promising young position players, including Jackson Holliday, Gunnar Henderson and Colton Cowser. Would-be franchise backstop Adley Rutschman was traded to the Boston Red Sox at the trade deadline.

Because Peralta was traded during the 2026 season and was thus ineligible for the qualifying offer, the Orioles will not be penalized a draft pick for signing him.

Bitcoin sell-off driven by weak demand, forced liquidations: 21Shares

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The crypto asset manager warned that Bitcoin’s downturn could persist amid weak investor demand, while forecasting an uneven recovery across crypto sectors.

The $6.4 billion hack of cryptocurrency exchange Binance in October triggered Bitcoin’s recent sell-off, but weakening investor demand and forced liquidations drove most of the subsequent losses, according to a Nov. 24 report from crypto asset manager 21Shares.

While the Oct. 29 hack triggered the initial decline, the report found that weakening demand drove Bitcoin (BTC) down a further 8.3% between Nov. 4 and Nov. 11. Forced selling of leveraged positions led to the steepest decline, sending BTC down another 13.5% over the next three days. Crypto liquidations totaled $5.8 billion in the week ending Nov. 15, with long positions accounting for 81%.

Bitcoin’s sell-off and leverage unwind. Source: 21Shares

Signs of weakening demand were also seen in the stablecoin supply, which contracted by $10.7 billion following the hack, marking the largest 18-day decline in dollar terms since the Terra collapse in 2022. US spot Bitcoin and Ether (ETH) exchange-traded funds (ETFs) recorded combined outflows of $3.9 billion between Oct. 30 and Nov. 13.

Eliezer Ndinga, global head of research at 21Shares, told Cointelegraph that the market was already vulnerable before the hack:

The report also found little evidence of selling by other major Bitcoin holders. The 18,270 BTC and 522,409 ETH stolen in the Binance hack remained unspent as of mid-November, while neither the US government nor the Mt. Gox trustee had transferred Bitcoin to exchanges since the attack.

Bitcoin treasury firm Strategy continued buying, adding 2,332 BTC between Oct. 26 and Nov. 15. Meanwhile, miner transfers to exchanges fell to their lowest levels since at least 2020, although the data may not capture sales by publicly traded mining companies.

21Shares believes Bitcoin is already more than a year into a cyclical bear market. Historically, such downturns have lasted about two and a half years and resulted in average price declines of 84%, according to the report.

However, the researchers see a low probability of Bitcoin revisiting the depths of previous bear markets, citing the growing presence of institutional investors and spot ETFs. Ndinga said the competitive landscape for crypto has also changed:

Ndinga warned that a larger-than-expected Federal Reserve rate hike in December could prolong the downturn. Another potential source of selling pressure is the $2.2 billion in BTC and ETH stolen in the Binance hack, which could reach the market through over-the-counter (OTC) brokers in the coming months.

Ndinga identified Bitcoin’s realized price of $53,250, a measure of the average investor cost basis, as a key downside level to watch. Conversely, a return to the $73,000 to $75,500 range coupled with sustained ETF inflows and rising stablecoin balances would signal renewed investor demand.

Fantasy Basketball Week 5 takeaways: Wemby's historic dominance, key injuries and more from all 30 teams

📅 Nov 24, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

A quick fantasy basketball breakdown on all 30 teams after Week 5 of the 2026-27 NBA season.

Spurs: Victor Wembanyama dominated Saturday: 50 points, 19 rebounds, 5 blocks and 4 steals in 32 minutes of a 114-91 win over the Pelicans. It got him the High Score of the week, with 97 fantasy points. He's just the third player since 1973-74 with a 50/15/5/4 game. It's his second 50-piece this month (52 on Nov. 4), and San Antonio is 15-3 on the season with Wemby leading the way as the top player in fantasy.

Bulls: Josh Giddey posted 23 points, 14 rebounds and a career-high 22 assists in Sunday's 151-145 OT loss to Denver, the league's first 20-20 game this season. Giddey followed up that epic game with a 2-for-11 performance with 4 points, 11 assists, 2 stocks and 3 turnovers on Monday. He was benched in crunch time (again). If you have Giddey, I'd sell. The game-to-game variance is too erratic.

Heat: Bam Adebayo posted 24 points, 15 rebounds and a career-high 12 assists with zero turnovers in Sunday's 123-113 win over OKC with Giannis Antetokounmpo (groin) out. Miami has won seven straight to reach 10-6. Shout-out to Klay Thompson, who averaged 20.7 points and 5.1 3s over a recent seven-game stretch and is closing in on Ray Allen for third all time in 3s. Keep holding Klay in 12s.

Bucks: Tyler Herro set a new career high in Philly on Saturday, dropping 50 points, then added 32 with 8 assists in Monday's win in Toronto to snap a 13-game skid. At 2-16, start prepping for some shakeups in the rotation — which could impact vets like Herro, Kyle Kuzma and Myles Turner. Rookie Brayden Burries (40% rostered) had 14 points, 10 boards, and 4 assists off the bench Monday, and should be rostered in most leagues.

Cavaliers: James Harden dropped 51 in Friday's 128-119 win over Indiana, passing Kobe Bryant for the third-most 50-point games ever (26). Evan Mobley (toe) has missed six straight and is due for re-evaluation this week. Kenny Atkinson has started Tyrese Proctor and Sam Merrill over the past week, but rookie Meleek Thomas (13.3 points in 24 minutes) is still the best streamer here.

Nets: Nolan Traore (right hamstring) will be re-evaluated in 8-10 weeks. Mikel Brown Jr. (19% rostered) got the first crack, starting Monday in Minnesota with his minutes restriction lifted: 17 points, a career-high 7 assists and 5 boards in 29 minutes. Egor Demin shot 3-for-13 off the bench. Brown is the add over Demin. The No. 6 pick has a path to 30 minutes a night.

Lakers: Austin Reaves (left heel) practiced fully Sunday and is targeting a return on Friday in San Antonio. Quentin Grimes averaged 9.2 points through 12 games, then moved into the starting lineup over the last five games, averaging 24.8 points, 4.6 rebounds, and 3.4 assists. He ranks 31st in 9-cat over that span, with 35.58 fantasy points per game..

Enjoy it now, because he won't see anywhere near 37 minutes a night when Reaves returns. Matisse Thybulle is the keeper. His 39 steals rank second in the league, and he and Wemby are the only players this year with 15+ blocks, 20+ steals and 30+ 3s.

Hawks: Atlanta has won four straight without CJ McCollum, whose left calf strain will be re-evaluated in about four weeks. No. 8 pick Kingston Flemings got next, but don't rush to get him because the Hawks won't play until Friday.

Dyson Daniels, though? He's such a unique fantasy talent. He's posting 9.5 points, 7.4 boards, 5.6 assists and a league-leading 2.9 steals per night. He can't shoot beyond 10 feet, but he's touching nearly every major counting-stats category except blocks and 3s.

Grizzlies: No. 3 pick Cameron Boozer (ankle) will be re-evaluated in four weeks. That hurts. Kris Murray started in his absence on Monday, playing 32 minutes and got 5 stocks. Add him to the watchlist, even though Memphis will likely form a committee between Murray, Taylor Hendricks and Jerami Grant (when he gets back). Make sure Cam Spencer (45% ) isn't on waivers.

Clippers: Brandon Ingram started in his debut Monday in Houston and posted 16 points, 7 boards, and 4 assists in 27 minutes. The shooting was unsurprisingly rough (6-for-18, 0-for-6 from three), but 18 shots in his first game since May is a great sign. Marc Stein reported that LA is showcasing vets like Rui Hachimura and Kris Dunn before the trade deadline.

Jazz: Svi Mykhailiuk went OFF Sunday for 39 points and 10 threes, both career highs. I'd rather pick up Ace Bailey, though.

76ers: Philly's five-game win streak ended Monday against Portland with Joel Embiid (illness) out. Embiid's been better, and fantasy managers could take the opportunity to float offers because health is always the biggest question mark with Embiid.

Celtics: Four straight losses. Derrick White is shooting 40.1% from the field and 28.8% from 3, well off his 45.4/38.7 peak. I'm holding in 9-cat, but it's been a disappointing start.

Mavericks: Just two games this week, and two scheduled before the NBA Cup next week. Cut fringe Mavs for players who play more games over the next two weeks.

Knicks: Josh Hart is averaging 6.6 points on 34/27/60 shooting. Woof.

Pistons: Over the past five games, Ausar Thompson ranks 16th in 9-cat. He's making 87.5% of his free throws (albeit low volume, but still!)

Magic: Anthony Black continues to lap Jalen Suggs. Suggs is averaging 9-5-4 with 35/29/73 shooting splits. He's down to 73% rostered, but fantasy managers should stay patient as he ramps up from a bone bruise injury that cost him eight games.

Wizards: The Wizards want Trae Young to focus less on scoring and more on passing. Per Kevin O'Connor, Young had 8 minutes of possession time per game over his first 6 games. It's down to 5 minutes over his last 5 games.

Raptors: Toronto lost to the 2-16 Bucks and sits at 6-11 overall. Kawhi Leonard (knee) is shooting at practice but is still a couple of weeks away from returning. Jakob Poeltl is back on the radar after averaging 11.0 points, 9.3 rebounds, 3.7 assists, 1.3 steals, and 1.3 blocks in 29.7 minutes per game in his last three games.

Pacers: At 5-12 without Tyrese Haliburton, Indiana is reportedly looking to make a move. In the meantime, Kelly Oubre Jr. (36%) should be rostered in all leagues.

Hornets: Brandon Miller and Coby White sat all of OT in a four-point loss to the Lakers last week. Minutes restrictions are costing teams Ws.
