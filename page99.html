United Passengers Spent Up To 8 Hours Stuck On Chicago O’Hare Tarmac During FAA Ground Stop

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Food  |  Read Source →

As US aviation came to a stop on Friday afternoon amid the longest nationwide ground stop since the 9/11 terror attacks in September 2001, passengers on flights around the country faced lengthy delays on the tarmac as airlines awaited a resolution. In some of the more extreme cases, reports have emerged of United Airlines passengers being kept on board for seven or even eight hours before getting off.

Normally, in the case of such long ground delays, US airlines are required to give passengers the chance to disembark after three hours on domestic flights, or four hours on international services. However, exceptions to this rule mean that, in some cases, they may have to stay on board for longer. While US aviation has largely bounced back from Friday's chaos, United also faced its own outage on Monday.

The Chicago Sun-Times shared the experiences of United Airlines passenger Abdul Sankoh, who was booked to fly from Chicago O'Hare International Airport (ORD) to Washington Dulles International Airport (IAD) at 2:21 PM on Friday. However, his flight never left the ground, and it was only after 9:00 PM, close to seven hours later, that he could get off. Sankoh then returned to O'Hare on Saturday to rebook, spending another four hours in a line.

Elsewhere, CNN reported that another passenger, Bryson Moser, had to spend even longer stuck on the tarmac, with his flight from ORD to Charleston, South Carolina, facing an eight-hour ground delay before it was eventually canceled and he could get off. Moser said that "our pilot did his best to tell us what he could," but that he ended up being cut off, with United Airlines not giving answers to his calls.

Simple Flying has reached out to United Airlines for further information on this matter. We will update our coverage upon receiving a statement from the airline.

Given the uncomfortable nature of being stuck on the ground in a plane for an extended period of time, there are certain rules that airlines have to follow under such circumstances. According to Nacionale, US carriers are required to give passengers the chance to disembark after a three-hour ground delay before a domestic flight, or four hours ahead of an international service. This cutoff has other stipulations.

For instance, airlines are required to tell passengers the status of the delay after 30 minutes, with updates thereafter provided at the carrier's discretion. Lavatories must remain operational, with medical aid provided to any passengers who need it, and, by the time the delay has reached two hours, airlines must provide adequate food (typically just a snack rather than a full meal service) and drinking water.

United Airlines' website details its own 'Tarmac Delay Contingency Plan,' which builds upon these points based on its 'core4' service standards: Safe, Caring, Dependable and Efficient. The airline worked on this plan with the TSA and CBP. However, there are exceptions to these rules, with passengers not allowed to get off if doing so presents a safety or security risk or might disrupt other airport operations.

Onboard supply shortages, crew duty limits, and closed CBP facilities led to a nightmare situation on a Wisconsin tarmac.

Following Friday's chaos and the knock-on delays and cancelations on Saturday and Sunday, the carrier would have been hoping for a better start to the week. However, just as US aviation looked set to bounce back from the weekend's disruption with a comparatively normal Monday, November 16 then saw United face a separate widespread grounding of its own following a system outage.

China weighs response to US chip curbs before Trump summit

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Beijing vowed “necessary measures” as new US rules target Chinese firms renting Nvidia chips abroad, days before APEC in Shenzhen.

Donald Trump and Xi Jinping at the Great Hall of the People in Beijing, 14 May 2026

China is weighing how hard to hit back at new US limits on its access to AI chips, days before Xi Jinping meets Donald Trump, Bloomberg News reported.

The US Commerce Department released the interim rules on Monday. Overseas data centres now need US approval to give large amounts of computing power to Chinese firms. Shipments of advanced chips to Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia and Brazil also need a licence.

There are exceptions. Chinese firms can still use computing power for content recommendation systems, and they can still rent capacity in US data centres, according to Bloomberg.

On Tuesday, China’s foreign ministry said it would take necessary measures to protect its rights and interests. Spokesperson Guo Jiakun accused the US of abusing export controls.

“It runs counter to the consensus reached between the two sides,” Guo told a regular press briefing in Beijing.

Xi and Trump are due to meet this week on the sidelines of the APEC summit in Shenzhen. It will be their third meeting this year. The two governments are also expected to hold their first dialogue on AI safety.

The rules could strain an unwritten bargain behind the trade truce. Under it, Washington holds off on new tech curbs, and China keeps critical minerals flowing to the US. US officials have complained that rare-earth magnet shipments are arriving too slowly, Bloomberg reported.

Beijing also has a deadline it can use. After Xi and Trump met in Busan last year, China suspended its ban on gallium, germanium and antimony exports to the US. That suspension ends on 27 November, so China could restore the ban simply by letting it lapse.

The curbs target a large source of Chinese computing power. The Institute for Progress, a Washington think tank, had forecast that nearly a third of China’s advanced compute would come from renting overseas data centres by the end of 2027.

Chinese AI stocks fell on Tuesday. Minimax dropped as much as 6.5% and Z.ai fell 6.8%, while chipmaker SMIC rose as much as 9%, Bloomberg reported.

The rules follow the resignation of Jensen Huang, who had been the industry’s loudest critic of chip controls on China. Brian Wong, an assistant professor at the University of Hong Kong, expects a targeted response rather than an end to the truce.

“It puts the tactical pressure on Beijing ahead of the Shenzhen meeting to demonstrate it will not be bullied into submission,” Wong told Bloomberg.

Music Victoria Goes Blunt in Election Fight for Music Funding

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The peak body says dedicated state funding for Victorian artists falls to zero in 2027, with a live venue closing every 18 days.

Music Victoria has launched an election campaign calling on the next state government to legislate a 1% levy on ticket sales at stadiums, arenas and government-owned venues, warning that dedicated funding for Victorian artists and music creation drops to zero in 2027.

The Vote Victorian Music campaign, developed with creative agency Bolster, rolled out Tuesday (Nov. 17) across street posters, music venues and digital channels, using a profanity-laden slogan urging voters not to let politicians ruin “the music state.” Alongside it, the peak body published a scorecard grading each party’s commitments to the sector. Related CL Signs With THEBLACKLABEL, Reuniting With Longtime Producer Teddy For 'Full Circle' Moment Jeff Benjamin

The levy sits within a broader policy ask of roughly $114 million over four years. Proceeds would fund a permanent, independently governed Victorian Music Trust, which Music Victoria estimates would return about $4.4 million annually to the sector on top of government investment, insulating it from budget and election cycles. The organization says no party has committed dedicated funding beyond 2026.

“All sides of Victorian politics continue to underestimate music as a proven catalyst for wellbeing, community and social cohesion, and a multi-billion dollar driver of jobs, revenue and export,” chief executive Fiona Duncan said in a statement. “Our message is direct because the stakes are high.”

Victoria generates about a third of Australia’s music industry revenue — $3.55 billion annually and $1.28 billion in direct economic contribution, according to Creative Australia’s The Bass Line — and is home to the country’s largest concentration of independent record labels, 66% of its vinyl pressing capacity, half its independent record stores, 40% of its music managers and 31% of APRA AMCOS songwriters and publishers. Music Victoria says Melbourne alone has more record stores per capita than anywhere in the world.

At its pre-pandemic peak, according to the body’s Live Music Census, Melbourne had more live music venues per capita than London or New York, and Melburnians attended more gigs than AFL matches. A more recent audit of Music Victoria data by Live Music Locator found the state is now losing a live music venue every 18 days.

Public investment has not kept pace. Per-capita spending on music in Victoria has fallen to 63 cents, against $3.10 in New South Wales and $1.46 in South Australia, after more than a decade of flat baseline funding. Music Victoria says the impact extends beyond Melbourne, with regional venues, festivals and music businesses supporting local jobs in hospitality, tourism, events and production.

A statutory levy would put Victoria ahead of comparable markets. In the U.K., the government has backed a voluntary £1 contribution on tickets to shows above 5,000 capacity, but uptake has stalled at around 30% of 2026 tickets after a 50% target was missed, and ministers have warned they will legislate if the industry does not deliver. In Australia, the Australian Music Venue Foundation launched in March collecting A$1.10 per ticket on participating tours.

Duncan said some parties have promised red-tape reforms, which the body welcomes, “but the government cannot keep trading on Victoria’s music reputation without investing in its future.” The scorecard assesses parties across four pillars — invest, support, grow and protect — drawing on party responses and public commitments, and follows consultation with more than 250 people across the state’s music community.

He says the app was made for tall vertical screens.

Adam Mosseri, the head of Instagram, says it's going to be "pretty challenging" to design the social media app "in a way that feels great" for the format of the iPhone Duo. Mosseri made the statement on Threads, where he also said that he came to the conclusion the more he looked at the details of the device.

A user responded, saying the best Instagram experience on the foldable is in portrait mode when the bigger screen is open, because the image takes up most of the display. Mosseri agreed that it was the best experience for Reels on Duo, but he added that Instagram is working to make the experience on other orientations better.

In another response, Mosseri explained that Instagram was designed for tall vertical screens. The Duo's form factor is far from typical, even when folded, as it's wider and shorter compared to most smartphones. As an example of an issue Instagram is trying to address, he said that it makes total sense for the navigation buttons on the right-hand side of the screen when it's closed as it's ergonomic that way. However, that's also where the Like, Comment and Share buttons are located in Reels. "Now we've got this double stack of buttons that doesn't feel great and that we need to reconsider," he said. "It's a lot of that kind of thing."

The Instagram app that was designed for the iPhone Duo has been out globally since October 23. However, it's gotten quite a bit of flak for its interface since then, with people specifically mentioning the double stack of buttons Mosseri was talking about. This could be Mosseri addressing the criticisms, or him trying to set people's expectations for when Instagram rolls out the next versions of its app for the device.

Goldman Sachs reports $1.22B in Ether ETFs as ETHA share count jumps

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Goldman Sachs increased its Ether ETF shares in Q3, bringing its reported holdings to $1.22 billion, while reshuffling its Bitcoin ETF positions.

Banking giant Goldman Sachs reported $1.22 billion in Ether exchange-traded fund (ETF) holdings at the end of September, up from $211.6 million three months earlier, as it increased positions across several funds throughout the third quarter.

The bank increased its holdings in BlackRock’s iShares Ethereum Trust ETF (ETHA) by 352% to 29.96 million shares, worth $602.9 million, according to its Q3 Form 13F filing with the US Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC).

The company also shifted its Bitcoin ETF holdings, reducing its stake in BlackRock’s Bitcoin (BTC) fund while increasing its position in Fidelity’s competing ETF.

The increase coincided with a strong quarter for Ether (ETH), which surged nearly 71% in Q3, according to CoinGlass. US spot Ether ETFs attracted more than $3 billion in net inflows, their strongest quarter of 2026, according to SoSoValue.

BlackRock’s ETHA was not the only Ether ETF in which Goldman Sachs increased its holdings during Q3.

The bank also increased its holdings in Grayscale’s Ethereum Staking Mini ETF (ETH) by 180% to 5.68 million shares, lifting the position’s reported value to $144.9 million from $30.5 million in Q2.

Its holdings in BlackRock’s iShares Staked Ethereum ETF (ETHB) rose 62% to 8.18 million shares, worth $281 million, up from $102.3 million in the previous quarter.

Goldman also reported four additional Ether ETF positions worth a combined $195.4 million, led by $169.6 million in Fidelity’s Ethereum Fund (FETH).

Including the new positions, Goldman’s reported Ether ETF holdings reached approximately $1.22 billion at the end of Q3, up from $211.6 million in Q2.

Goldman reduced its IBIT holdings by about 792,000 shares to 16.44 million, although the position’s reported value increased 36% to $778.3 million as IBIT’s price surged 42% over the quarter.

Meanwhile, its FBTC holdings surged more than 50-fold, from 105,840 shares to 5.48 million, bringing their reported value to $397.9 million from just $5.4 million.

Camran Khosravi, research analyst at global asset manager Bitwise, told Cointelegraph that market makers such as Jane Street can shift between Bitcoin ETFs to meet client demand and hedge options positions without necessarily changing their overall Bitcoin exposure.

When Ben White made his England debut in 2021, it was expected that he would play a key role for his national team for many years to come.

However, he refused to play for his country for years after leaving England's camp during the 2022 World Cup in Qatar. The day after England's final Group B match, it was announced that White would be returning home due to personal reasons.

Speaking in 2024, Gareth Southgate revealed that White had informed England that he did not want to be considered for selection.

White finally returned to the fold in March 2026 when he was included in Thomas Tuchel's squad for two friendly matches ahead of the World Cup.

He made his first appearance in four years against Uruguay at Wembley Stadium, coming on as a second half substitution. He was loudly booed by his own fans as he made his way onto the pitch. The Arsenal defender went on to score and give away a penalty.

It has not been made clear as to exactly why White left England's 2022 World Cup squad and made himself unavailable for selection for many years afterward.

There were rumours that White had fallen out with Southgate's assistant manager, Steve Holland. Piers Morgan has now revealed the 'real reason' why White left England's camp, giving more details of his bust-up with Holland.

Morgan said on Football Uncensored: “My view on Ben White… he’s a fantastic player. He’s been great at Arsenal, he competes with (Jurrien) Timber for the right-back (spot) but I think he’s terrific.

“He still gets booed by a section of England fans because of what happened at the Qatar World Cup in 2022, where he had this massive falling-out with Steve Holland.

“My understanding of what happened is that Holland was mocking him in front of all the players for the supposed statement that Ben White made that he doesn’t like football.

“In fact, what he said was that he doesn’t like going home and watching football after he’s been playing it all day. And he made the point this week clarifying it, that, like a lot of people, when they finish their job for the day, they don’t want to go back home and talk about it… I totally understood that.”

White returned to the England fold after Holland left the England set up alongside Southgate in 2024.

Morgan added: “Now the interesting thing about Holland is that he’s gone to Manchester United, he’s just been fired as part of the Carrick regime, he had a terrible year there, he fell out with all the players, especially Bruno Fernandes, who’s their only good player.

“So, for me, I’m totally team Ben White and the fact that he’s back in the England team is great and we should stop booing him.”

White chose not to go into detail when asked what happened in Qatar and why he made himself unavailable for England selection for many years afterward.

He told the BBC: "I think obviously people don't know the full story, because I [have] never said anything and I won't put anyone under the bus or share any of that. I think that is probably the best way.

"It's been amazing [though], obviously a bit tricky and not straightforward, but it is amazing to be here now."

When asked whether he would have done anything differently, he continued: "No, I don't think I would. Obviously, I was a bit younger, I am a bit older now.

"I think as a person I am quite real and I think I made the decision that I would probably continue to make. So, one that I am not happy about, but obviously it happened and [it is] time to move on."

White, who is one of the highest-paid English footballers, has now made eight appearances for his country. He scored his second goal for his country in the Three Lions' 3-2 victory over world champions Spain at the Riyadh Air Metropolitano Stadium in November 2026.

Intel shareholders sue over PC slump that sank shares nearly 29%

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

A proposed class action claims Intel and chief executive Lip-Bu Tan hid weakening PC demand from investors before October’s forecast.

Intel shareholders have sued the chipmaker, accusing it of hiding how badly a slump in PC sales was hurting its business. The proposed class action was filed on Friday, 13 November, in federal court in San Jose, California.

The suit claims Intel misled investors about PC demand and the impact of its data centre projects, as the BBC reported. The claims have not been tested in court.

Law firm Robbins Geller Rudman & Dowd filed the case for investors who bought Intel shares between 23 July and 29 October. It names Intel, chief executive Lip-Bu Tan and other executives. A Michigan public pension fund, the City of Southfield Fire and Police Retirement System, is the lead plaintiff, according to investingLive.

The case centres on Intel’s third-quarter results, released on 29 October. Total revenue rose 21% to $16.5bn, driven by data centre chips. However, revenue at its PC chip division fell to $8.1bn, about 9% lower than the previous quarter.

Intel also gave a weak forecast for the final three months of the year. It expects revenue of $15bn to $16bn, below what analysts had expected.

The next day, Intel shares closed down 28.6%, their worst day in decades. The complaint argues this undercut earlier signs from the company that PC demand was holding up.

Tan has blamed a shortage of memory chips, as more supply goes to AI data centres. That has pushed up PC prices and hurt demand.

“We need to address these challenges with urgency,” Tan told analysts in October.

Intel’s shares fell about 7% on Monday after news of the lawsuit spread, investingLive reported.

<a href="https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/payers/humana-launches-provider-gold-card-program" hreflang="en">Humana launches national provider Gold Card program</a>

📅 2026-11-17  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →
Humana launches national provider Gold Card program" style="max-width:100%; height:auto; border-radius:8px;" loading="lazy">

Humana is continuing its push to reform prior authorization with the launch of a national provider gold card program.

The program will go into effect on Dec. 1, the company announced Tuesday. It will focus initially on MRI, CT and MRA imaging as well as physical and occupational therapy. Through the program, eligible providers will no longer need to secure a prior authorization approval and can instead use a streamlined process.

Humana estimates that about 50,000 providers will qualify for the program. Eligible providers must be able to demonstrate a proven track record of high-quality care and patient outcomes.

Traci Granston, M.D., vice president of clinical innovation and trend at Humana, told Fierce Healthcare in an exclusive interview that prior authorization and other utilization management approaches are key in ensuring that patients receive the appropriate care.

However, lengthy decision times and other barriers can create a painful friction. So for providers that consistently demonstrate that they are aligned with clinical guidelines, the gold cards can remove that guardrail.

"If they're following those guidelines and providing appropriate care, prior auths don't really serve a purpose for them," Granston said.

The insurer conducted a pilot earlier this year with about 400 provider organizations and approximately 8,000 clinicians. Through that process, the team conducted interviews with clinicians and office staff who shared their desire for fewer administrative burdens and a simpler experience.

Those insights shaped the broader launch, Humana said, and the insurer intends to continue to iterate and evolve the gold card program over time, including by adding new service lines. It's not uncommon for a program to roll out that aims to support providers, but their voice is less often at the table in shaping what the experience looks like to ensure that their pain points are being addressed.

Granston said that the team is examining more time-sensitive services that could fit within the program, for example.

"For our top-performing providers, we would like to essentially get out of the way and let them continue to care for our members," she said.

The gold card program builds on Humana's existing work to reform prior authorization, including a streamlined approval process for electronic submissions that took effect in January of this year. Under this initiative, Humana responds within one business day in 95% of electronic prior authorization cases.

The insurer also announced earlier this month that it would join forces with athenahealth to accelerate the use of electronic prior authorization. Through the partnership, more of the process is embedded directly in the provider's workflow, streamlining the experience for them at the point of care.

Granston said that the goal is to make it easier to surface the data necessary to make an approval and enable that to occur in real time. This also means that fewer people have to be dedicated to the work of submissions and approval.

"When the information exists, you don't really need all these people to be touching it," she said.

<a href="https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/payers/hlth-2026-glp-1-costs-remain-challenge-new-cvs-study-aims-identify-right-price" hreflang="en">HLTH 2026: GLP-1 costs remain a challenge. A new CVS study aims to identify the right price</a>

📅 2026-11-17  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →
HLTH 2026: GLP-1 costs remain a challenge. A new CVS study aims to identify the right price" style="max-width:100%; height:auto; border-radius:8px;" loading="lazy">

Cost and access to GLP-1s remains a challenge, but the benefits of these drugs aren't in doubt.

A new study from CVS Health aims to quantify at what price a GLP-1 drug has generated enough savings elsewhere through improved clinical outcomes. The healthcare giant used anonymized claims data from Aetna members in fully insured plans and found savings from reduced hospital and medical spending, as well as improved health outcomes.

But those benefits weren't enough to offset the increased pharmaceutical spending at the current price point, the study found.

The study tracked a mix of members who took GLP-1s from 2023 to 2025, so some patients had three years of follow-up care and management.

Amy Compton-Phillips, M.D., chief medical officer at CVS, told Fierce Healthcare that part of the challenge in this equation is that these drugs gained steam as a treatment for obesity at a high price. And while those costs have come down, the reductions haven't yet been enough to offset the expenditures.

Another challenge for employers in particular — where GLP-1 coverage has been most in flux — is turnover, she said. While GLP-1s do drive significant improvements, an employer may not reap the benefit of the reduced cost if an employee moves to a different company before those are realized, even though they paid for the medication initially.

The study sought to identify a price point where those savings would be felt and landed at $350 per month over a three-year period. Achieving that, Compton-Phillips said, would likely lure employers into embracing broader GLP-1 coverage.

"For them, it might be really worthwhile to say, 'Hey, at this price point, I can afford to cover this for my employees, knowing that in the near term the savings will pay for my investment,'" she said.

The study found obesity rates declining after a 2022 peak, as well as a reduction of between 34% and 42% in hospitalizations over three years. Patients also had 14% lower odds of a serious cardiac event.

For patients who remained on the drugs for three years, their total medical spending decreased by $9,400, but associated pharmacy spending increased in tandem by $13,300, the study found.

Clinical improvements also fell off when patients were not able to remain adherent to the medication over several years, highlighting the importance of a wraparound approach that supports broader care management rather than simply prescribing a patient a GLP-1.

Compton-Phillips said CVS' own weight management program brings nutrition, peer coaching and other supports into the fold, in addition to the prescription. This is especially critical, she said, as evidence suggests weight gain is common after a patient stops taking their GLP-1, making behavior change key.

"At the moment, there's not great evidence that if you come off the drugs, you will maintain the weight loss," she said.

The CVS researchers said that the ultimate goal of the study was not to demand that all GLP-1 prescriptions repay their own costs, but to ensure that drug costs align with patient benefits and the broader cost of access. By lowering the net price of these products, more people can access them, the study concludes.

"These drugs make people's lives better, and I want to give them out," Compton-Phillips said. Doing so becomes a challenge, though, "at the price they are today," she said.

<a href="https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/health-tech/hhs-finalizes-updates-hipaa-privacy-rule-ease-patient-access-health-information" hreflang="en">HHS finalizes updates to HIPAA privacy rule to ease patient access to health information</a>

📅 2026-11-17  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →
HHS finalizes updates to HIPAA privacy rule to ease patient access to health information" style="max-width:100%; height:auto; border-radius:8px;" loading="lazy">

The Trump administration finalized a major update to the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) Privacy Rule designed to give patients easier, faster access to their health records and streamline care coordination.

The final rule, issued by the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services Office for Civil Rights (OCR), strengthens and clarifies the right of an individual to access their protected health information and reduces regulatory and administrative burdens, the agency said.

The proposed rule was issued more than five years ago, in January 2021. An unpublished version of the final rule is available here.

Under the final rule, healthcare organizations must respond faster to patient requests for copies of their health records. HHS said covered entities will have no more than 15 calendar days to respond to a request, reduced from the current 30-day period. Organizations are allowed one extension of no more than 30 calendar days, HHS said in the final rule.

The rule also establishes that patients can access their protected health information through personal health apps when the covered entity has the technical capacity to deliver the records in that form and format.

The rule also expands circumstances when patients must receive copies of their health records for free, requires advance notice of fees and modifies identity verification requirements.

To make it easier for healthcare organizations to share data for care coordination, the final rule clarifies that health plans may use and disclose protected health information for care coordination and case management for individuals. The rule also expressly permits covered entities to disclose PHI to social services agencies, community-based organizations and similar third parties for care coordination and case management for individuals.

To reduce regulatory burden, OCR eliminated the requirement for providers with direct treatment relationships with individuals to make a good faith effort to obtain an individual’s written acknowledgment that they have received a copy of the health care provider’s Notice of Privacy Practices. The final rule also permits disclosures to Telecommunications Relay Services (TRS) communications assistants for persons who are deaf, hard of hearing, or deaf-blind, or who have a speech disability, and excludes TRS providers from the definition of business associate.

“Americans should never have to fight for access to their own health records,” said HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy, Jr in a statement. “Under President Trump’s leadership, we are putting patients in control, requiring faster access to their information, and cutting red tape that gets in the way of coordinated care. This rule modernizes HIPAA to serve patients and help providers spend more time delivering care and less time navigating bureaucracy.”

Provisions in the final rule will become effective 60 days after publication in the Federal Register, and compliance will be required 180 days after that date, according to HHS in a fact sheet.

During a Monday fireside chat at the HLTH 2026 conference in Las Vegas, CMS Deputy Administrator and Chief Product Officer Amy Gleason said the updates to the privacy rule "remove friction from the patient experience."

Gleason said there are three main components of the rule: the designation of a "personal health app," identity verification and speed. "It should be almost instantaneous with electronic data, and it also says that this information is free to the patient," she said.

“This final rule provides Americans with faster and easier access to their health information which is critical to them being able to take control of their own health and health care,” said Office for Civil Rights Director Paula M. Stannard in a statement. “Consistent with the Trump Administration’s deregulatory goals, the rule also reduces regulatory burdens on health care providers and other regulated entities and decreases costs for the health care system

While the Trump administration moved forward with changes to the HIPAA privacy rule, federal regulators delayed a major overhaul of the HIPAA Security Rule, pushing back final action on the rule by a year. HHS had proposed a May 2026 release for a final rule that makes significant changes to the HIPAA Security Rule and marks the first major update to the 23-year-old rule in more than 10 years. The U.S. Office of Management and Budget (OMB) website was updated in July and indicates that the final rule was pushed back to July 2027.
