Crypto’s Next Two Years Should Be About Survival, Not Competition: Vitalik Buterin

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Ethereum co-founder Vitalik Buterin has warned crypto executives to switch focus to collective security and survival rather than competition between platforms. This follows multiple hacks that drained billions from the market and crushed traders’ sentiment.

In an interview with MoneyControl, Buterin urged crypto executives to focus on protecting user assets as a collective, as that could shape how regulators approach the sector. The next couple of years are key, as frameworks are still expected in several jurisdictions.

Digital asset firms, particularly centralized exchanges, have focused on gaining a larger share of the market cap, onboarding more users, etc but this buzz around gains leaves room for bad actors. For Buterin, the actions in the next couple of years will determine the long-term roadmap.

Most crypto users agreed with the Ethereum executive because, without asset safety, centralized exchanges will see capital exodus and more backing from traditional finance firms.

Recently, several crypto companies were hacked, including Binance, the largest centralized exchange by trading volume. Bad actors flagged in North Korea drained $6 billion; although large parts were recovered, it still remains the largest crypto hack of all time.

This week, Brazil’s largest exchange, Mercado, also suffered a breach that temporarily suspended trading. In a nutshell, these losses could prompt regulators to tighten requirements, setting the sector back.

Previously, regulators in the US and Europe often cited investor-protection concerns to delay major developments that would open the sector to traditional capital. Notably, spot crypto ETFs are a top example, although things changed after the White House backed the move following President Trump’s election win.

Buterin also added that recent attacks now target user interfaces and centralized components rather than smart contracts. He called for more operations to move on-chain, citing Binance Smart Chain and others.

“One good decision that Binance made is that they also launched Binance Smart Chain. They also benefit from the fact that if you do things with contracts on-chain, you can have very high levels of security. I am expecting we’re going to see more moves in that direction.”

On Ethereum security, Buterin and developers rolled out a roadmap to protect assets ahead of Q-Day. The network is lauded for taking early steps ahead of others as supercomputers get more powerful.

5 Airbus A321neo Routes With Lie-Flat Business Class Seats

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Brandon's passion for aviation started at a young age. He became involved in the flight simulator and VATSIM communities, which sparked his interest in aviation and eventually led him to earn his private pilot license. Outside of work, he continues to build flight hours, explore new airports, and stay involved in the aviation community.

Lie-flat business class belonged to widebodies until June 15, 2014, when JetBlue Airways launched Mint on a single route between John F. Kennedy International Airport (JFK) and Los Angeles International Airport(LAX). The aircraft was an Airbus A321, a narrowbody that American, Delta, and United flew transcontinental routes with recliner seats at premium fares. JetBlue put flat beds in the front of one and changed what passengers expected from a single-aisle aircraft.

A decade later, the A321neo and its long-range derivatives carry lie-flat seats across both US transcontinental routes and the Atlantic. The aircraft's range made that possible. An A321LR can reach London from New York, meaning a narrowbody now competes directly with widebodies on routes that were closed to it a generation ago. The seats themselves have kept pace, with JetBlue fitting the Thompson Aero VantageSOLO in private suites with closing doors and direct aisle access on every Mint seat aboard its A321neo and A321LR fleets.

The operators are an unusual group. A US low-cost carrier flies the most A321LR transatlantic routes of any airline. An all-business-class boutique operates the type with 76 flat seats and nothing behind them. Two European flag carriers use it to open thinner North American routes that cannot support a widebody.

JetBlue launched Mint on June 15, 2014, on the route between New York John F. Kennedy International Airport (JFK) and Los Angeles International Airport (LAX). The transcontinental market was the right place to start because American, Delta, and United charged premium fares for recliner seats on the same city pair. JetBlue undercut them with flat beds and a markedly better onboard product, and the competitive response reshaped premium transcon flying across all four carriers.

The current product on the A321neo is a considerable step beyond what launched in 2014. The aircraft carries 16 Mint seats, comprising 14 Mint Suites and two Mint Studios, arranged 1-1, so every passenger has direct aisle access. JetBlue states that every Mint seat on its A321neo with Mint is a private suite with a door, and that all of them convert to a fully lie-flat bed with Tuft & Needle adaptive foam cushions and a T&N-designed pillow and comforter.

The two Mint Studios at the front stand out. JetBlue describes the Studio as carrying the largest TV on a US airline at 22 inches, plus a mirrored vanity, extra storage, and a guest seat and table that lets a second Mint passenger visit. The Points Guy measured the Studio bed at just over 82 inches in lie-flat mode, which it called the largest lie-flat bed on any US airline, and paid $199 above the standard Mint fare to book one on the inaugural. Aircraft type matters when booking. JetBlue's older A321 Classic with Mint also carries 16 Mint seats, but only four of them are suites with doors, with the remainder in alternating 2-2 rows.

The Airbus A321LR is a long-range derivative of the A321neo, which allows JetBlue to fly a narrowbody across the Atlantic. The aircraft carries 24 Mint seats, comprising 22 Mint Suites and two Mint Studios, arranged in the same 1-1 layout as the domestic A321neo. The seat is identical. JetBlue fits the Thompson Aero VantageSOLO across both fleets, which means a passenger flying JFK to Los Angeles and a passenger flying JFK to London Heathrow (LHR) are in the same product.

JetBlue serves Heathrow twice daily from JFK for the winter 2026-27 season, alongside JFK to Paris and Boston Logan International Airport (BOS) to both Heathrow and Paris. The wider European network reaches Amsterdam, Dublin and Edinburgh from New York and Boston, with Madrid and Milan added from Boston. That is more A321LR transatlantic routes than any other airline operates, a notable position for a carrier that flew nothing across the Atlantic before 2021.

The aircraft changes which routes are viable. A widebody crossing the Atlantic needs enough demand to fill 250 or more seats. An A321LR carries 138 in JetBlue's configuration, 24 of them Mint, which lowers the threshold considerably. That is why the type has opened city pairs between secondary European cities and the US East Coast that no airline served nonstop before. For passengers, it also means the business class cabin on a transatlantic flight may hold 24 seats rather than 60, which changes availability and award space in ways that cut both directions.

What's it like onboard American Airlines' Airbus A321XLR?

La Compagnie operates an Airbus A321neo with 76 lie-flat business class seats and no other cabin. The airline flies between Newark Liberty International Airport (EWR) and Paris Orly (ORY) as its core route, with the seats arranged 2-2 rather than the 1-1 layout JetBlue uses. That configuration means not every passenger has direct aisle access, which is the trade-off for fitting 76 flat beds into a narrowbody.

The aircraft replaced Boeing 757s in 2019, and the upgrade was substantial. The 757s carried angled-flat seats that recline to a sloped position rather than a true horizontal one, and the A321neo brought fully flat beds to a carrier whose entire proposition rests on the business class product. For an all-business-class airline, the seat is not one cabin among several. It is the whole aircraft and the whole business.

The economics are what make the route interesting. Simple Flying has described La Compagnie as offering one of the most consistently affordable ways to fly business class across the Atlantic, which works because the airline carries no economy cabin to subsidize and no connecting network to feed. Every seat sold is a business class fare, and the base cost of a single narrowbody is far lower than a widebody running three or four cabins. La Compagnie also serves Milan from Newark and adds Nice seasonally, applying the same model to leisure-weighted markets where premium demand concentrates in specific months.

TAP Air Portugal deploys its Airbus A321LRs primarily on routes between Lisbon Humberto Delgado Airport (LIS) and North America, serving destinations in both the United States and Canada. The business class seat is the Thompson Vantage, a lie-flat product that differs from the VantageSOLO JetBlue fits in that it is arranged in a staggered configuration rather than a 1-1 layout with every seat against a window.

The aircraft suits what TAP is doing geographically. Lisbon sits further west than any other major European hub, which shortens the Atlantic crossing and places North American cities within comfortable A321LR range. That advantage lets TAP serve secondary North American markets that would not support a widebody from a more easterly European base, and it underpins the airline's strategy of positioning Lisbon as a transfer point between the Americas and Europe.

TAP also uses the type beyond the Atlantic. The A321LRs operate to destinations in Africa and Brazil, markets where Portugal's historical links generate demand that is real but frequently too thin for a widebody year-round. A single aircraft type covering North America, Africa and South America from one hub gives TAP flexibility that a larger aircraft would not, and it means the same lie-flat business class cabin appears across a network spanning three continents. For passengers, the practical point is that a TAP long-haul flight may be operated by a narrowbody with a business cabin considerably smaller than the A330neos the airline flies on its denser routes.

The planemaker has big plans for the reimagining of business class travel.

Scandinavian Airlines (SAS) operates three Airbus A321LRs, fitting the Thompson Vantage in business class, and uses them on routes between Scandinavia and North America. It is the smallest fleet on this list by a wide margin, and that scarcity defines the operation. Three aircraft cannot cover a network, which means the type appears on specific routes at specific times rather than across the airline's transatlantic schedule.

The rationale matches TAP's. Scandinavian cities generate genuine demand for North American service, but frequently not enough to fill a widebody year-round on every city pair. An A321LR lowers the threshold for a nonstop to become viable, allowing SAS to serve markets that would otherwise require a connection through a larger European hub. The lie-flat business cabin makes those routes sellable to premium passengers, who determine whether a long-haul route works financially.

The fleet size also reflects SAS's commercial position. The airline entered Chapter 11 in 2024, moved from Star Alliance to SkyTeam in September 2024, and now has Air France-KLM as a shareholder. Three A321LRs are a cautious commitment rather than a strategic bet on the type, and the aircraft function as a tool for specific routes rather than a fleet strategy. For a passenger, the result is that booking SAS to North America may mean a widebody with a large business cabin or a narrowbody with a small one, depending entirely on the route and the day.

The Lord of the Rings: The King's Gambit is a tabletop adaptation of the Battle of the Pelennor Fields as the forces of Rohan and Gondor band together to repel the dark forces of Sauron. It features miniatures, 3D models of the iconic Minas Tirith, Mount Doom, assorted other terrain, cards, dice, and so much more, all of which coalesce to deliver a hugely ambitious board game experience in which the events of this epic final battle play out.

Restoration Games has previously made a Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles game as well as a Godzilla one, to name a few, and thus has plenty of experience within this field.

Lord of the Rings: The King's Gambit is set to be shipped to backers in October 2027. A retail launch is likely to follow shortly after.

If you're interested, late pledges for Lord of the Rings: The King's Gambit remain open via its official Kickstarter page. This is, of course, not the only ambitious LOTR board game to be crowdfunded as of late, but it is perhaps the biggest in terms of components. The scale of this is staggering, which is why it isn't terribly surprising that you'll need to fork over $199 for the cheapest tier. However, if you absolutely love Lord of the Rings and have been looking for a brand-new board game experience set within Middle-earth, this could be the project for you.

Stephen King's 13-Part Remake Of A Lovecraftian Classic Is A Shadow Of The Original

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Faith Roswell is a Senior Writer on Screen Rant's Classic TV team. Since earning her degree in Creative Writing over a decade ago, Faith has written articles on film and TV from a variety of different angles. Faith now combines her knowledge of psychology with her love of monster movies to give more insight into what makes the best ones.  You may have read her Screen Rant lists and features covering horror, sci-fi, and fantasy, or read her Amazon Top 10 book, "Movie Monsters of the Deep." Faith has had an extensive career as a writer, appearing on BBC live radio, researching true crime for Rotten Mango podcast, and writing for publications including Mental Floss, Atlas Obscura, and The Daily Jaws before beginning here at Screen Rant.

Stephen King is no stranger to Lovecraftian horrors, from IT's terrifying shapeshifter Pennywise to his mind-bending masterpiece The Dark Tower, but despite being known as "the king of horror," Stephen King's box office record is not always great. Many of his best adaptations have become the greatest horror movies of the past 50 years, but one of Stephen King's adaptations that missed the mark is, instead, best known for drawing attention to its fantastic source material. Lars von Trier is responsible for one of the 90s' most underrated must-watch horror shows, with King's version being a shadow of the original.

The Kingdom is a unique show that captures the surreal atmosphere of Twin Peaks, but with an even more disconcerting tone. Set within a hospital, Lars von Trier's 13-episode series follows its staff and patients as they encounter a bizarre range of supernatural phenomena. The show's three seasons are spaced years apart, first airing in 1994, and with the final season acting as the highly-anticipated ending to a cliffhanger viewers had been waiting 25 years to see resolved. The series is often considered to be one of von Trier's best works, with Stephen King's version failing to improve on it.

Stephen King's Kingdom Hospital was based on The Kingdom, but while the premise was still intriguing, King's version lacked the sheer weirdness that von Trier brought to the screen. The Kingdom had more than enough scenes that felt like nightmare fuel, but the dark satire included in the story was central to the show, while the adaptation adhered more closely to typical horror genre tropes. This meant that while Kingdom Hospital was a good show in its own right, earning two Emmy Award nominations, including for its visual effects, it did not bring anything new to TV.

The Kingdom was instantly recognizable for its sepia tone and for casting a wide range of character actors who could carry the story, which was often chaotic. Lars von Trier's characters tend to be realistically flawed people with trauma and obsessions, but this is a versatile enough description that his projects rarely feel repetitive. On the other hand, Stephen King's Kingdom Hospital included one of his most common self-inserts, the tormented writer. This changed the balance of the original series, drawing the focus to it being a Stephen King show, rather than the writer's take on the existing source material.

Lars von Trier's series is called Riget in Danish, meaning "the realm."

With Kingdom Hospital only adapting the first season of von Trier's original show, it failed to include the payoff that The Kingdom's fans had waited for, and instead felt like a confusing array of subplots compared with the original, which won 19 out of the 25 awards the series was nominated for. Had Kingdom Hospital been more streamlined and featured the uneasy Lovecraftian atmosphere that King incorporated into other projects, like The Mist, which had one of the most devastating endings of all time, the show could have come closer to matching the Danish original.

The Kingdom might not rank among the most Lovecraftian TV shows of all time, but it truly nails cosmic horror even so. Every aspect of the series, from its setting to its characters, hints at a vast unknown. From a pathologist obsessed with claiming a cancerous liver as a trophy, to a ghostly ambulance that appears and disappears, The Kingdom is extremely mysterious throughout, and rather than presenting the hauntings as disturbances that can be solved through killing a demonic power or solving an ancient curse, the series has no such neat ending.

Cosmic horror is designed to challenge our perception of reality, presenting unknown forces that are impossible for a human mind to comprehend, and The Kingdom's final act adds several more layers to the story, including a meta cameo from von Trier himself. Rather than being a direct Lovecraft copy or tribute, The Kingdom infuses elements of his work into the story, while remaining a recognizable von Trier project. Had Stephen King taken this approach, Kingdom Hospital would have been a very different, and potentially better, show.

Prime Video’s 3-Season Gritty Crime Thriller Is The Perfect NCIS Replacement

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

After more than two decades and over 500 episodes, NCIS has practically perfected the weekly crime investigation formula. From murders involving Navy personnel to the chemistry between its Major Case Response Team, there's a reason the CBS procedural has stayed popular for so long as one of the best crime dramas. Still, even longtime fans might want something darker than the usual case-of-the-week format. That's where Prime Video's Psycho-Pass comes in.

With three seasons and six feature films from Production I.G, Psycho-Pass offers a different take on the crime thriller. Written by Gen Urobuchi, the mind behind Fate/Zero and Puella Magi Madoka Magica, the series moves its investigations to a disturbing version of 22nd-century Japan. The government doesn't just punish criminals after a crime. Its nationwide Sibyl System monitors citizens' mental states, stress levels, and personalities to calculate their chances of becoming criminals through a score called the Crime Coefficient.

That's what sets it apart from NCIS. The Public Safety Bureau's Criminal Investigation Division still tracks dangerous suspects, but its Dominator handguns can decide whether someone gets arrested or executed based on that score. Sometimes, that judgment comes before a crime has even been committed. It's familiar detective work with a much darker idea of justice.

What makes Psycho-Pass such a good fit for NCIS viewers is that it gets the appeal of a proper investigative team. The story follows 20-year-old rookie Akane Tsunemori, who joins Division One of the Public Safety Bureau’s Criminal Investigation Department after topping her civil service exams. Much like Timothy McGee or Eleanor Bishop joining NCIS as fresh faces, Akane has to find her footing in an established team. The difference is that her new colleagues operate under a much darker system.

Division One has two ranks: Inspectors and Enforcers. Inspectors like Akane and her strict senior partner, Nobuchika Ginoza, oversee investigations, while Enforcers are latent criminals with Crime Coefficients above 100, released from lockup to help track down other offenders. They are essentially the Bureau's hunting dogs, and the Dominator handgun makes their work even more disturbing. It can switch from a Non-Lethal Paralyzer to a Lethal Eliminator depending on a suspect's Crime Coefficient.

Akane sees the system's cruelty on her first night when a terrified hostage is marked for execution after her trauma pushes her score into the lethal range. Akane stops her own lead Enforcer, Shinya Kogami, from pulling the trigger, establishing the show's central conflict between machine-made justice and human judgment.

The procedural appeal is just as strong. Shion Karanomori handles forensic analysis, Tomomi Masaoka brings old-school police experience, and Kogami uses sharp instincts to get inside suspects' heads. Like Abby Sciuto, Kasie Hines, Ducky Mallard, and Gibbs, each member brings something different to an investigation. The big difference? NCIS solves crimes after they happen; Psycho-Pass investigates people the system believes might commit them.

What makes Psycho-Pass worth watching for NCIS viewers is that it doesn't ditch the things that make a good crime procedural work. There's still a tight investigative team, forensic experts picking apart evidence, detectives following leads, and criminals who keep the department on its toes. The difference is that the crime scenes are in futuristic Tokyo, and the technology used to catch killers is just as disturbing as the crimes themselves.

Sherlock is one of the best Sherlock Holmes adaptations. However, while it is extremely clever, some detective TV shows make it look small.

Season 1's Shogo Makishima also gives the anime a proper long-running villain, much like the personal threats NCIS has thrown at Gibbs and his team over the years. And don't let the animation fool anyone into expecting something lighthearted. Psycho-Pass carries a TV-MA rating, with brutal murders, graphic violence, and some genuinely disturbing scenes. Across three seasons, it has plenty of room to build its characters and cases beyond a single mystery. It's a lot darker than NCIS, but that familiar mix of team investigations, sharp detective work, and dangerous recurring villains makes it an easy recommendation for crime thriller fans.

Netflix Officially Adds Sylvester Stallone’s Explosive ’90s Survival Thriller

There was a time when Sylvester Stallone was seemingly incapable of making a movie that didn't involve putting himself through some sort of extraordinary physical punishment. Here's What the Average Social Security Benefit Actually Covers

📅 Nov 07, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The average Social Security check covers only a fraction of what seniors actually spend each year.

Most retirees lean on Social Security for much of their income, yet the average check was built to replace only part of a paycheck. As of September 2026, the average retired-worker benefit is $2,088.79 per month, according to the Social Security Administration (SSA) Monthly Statistical Snapshot. That adds up to $25,065.48 per year.

A household headed by someone 65 or older spends $61,432 per year, based on the BLS Consumer Expenditure Survey from 2024, the most recent data available. While housing and healthcare are major concerns, transportation and food costs also represent significant portions of a typical senior's budget.

The average annual benefit of $25,065.48 covers only 41% of that annual senior-household spending. This is by design, as the government built the program to replace roughly 40% of pre-retirement income. It's not intended to fund a complete lifestyle on its own.

The SSA calculates your benefit based on your highest 35 years of earnings, where earlier wages are adjusted for inflation, through the primary insurance amount (PIA) formula. This leaves an annual shortfall of $36,366.52 that you'll need to cover through other sources.

For retirees without a pension or deep savings, part-time work may be the only realistic way to fill that hole. Inflation adjustments provide some relief, but they're meant to maintain current buying power rather than increase it.

A 3.6% cost-of-living adjustment (COLA) will take effect in 2027, adding about $75 a month to that average check. This adjustment is designed to help your benefits keep pace with rising prices. The average check is a foundation, not a full budget, and the question is how to cover the rest.

It's possible to beat the average Social Security check by delaying your Social Security application. Every month you wait to apply increases your monthly benefits until you qualify for your largest checks at 70.

If you expect to have an average or above-average life expectancy and you can afford to cover your living costs another way in the meantime, delaying your Social Security application is a viable way to grow your checks. It could also lead to a much larger lifetime benefit.

Things like remaining in the workforce for longer or increasing your income today could also lead to larger Social Security checks in the future because the SSA bases your benefit on your average monthly income over your 35 highest-earning years. If you're earning more now than in years past, working a bit longer could boost your future benefits.

But even in the best-case scenario, your checks likely won't be enough to cover all your living expenses in retirement. Make sure you have a plan for how you'll cover what Social Security doesn't before you sign up.

TL;DR: Zcash’s NU7 upgrade activated at mainnet block 3,543,405, reducing target block spacing from 75 to 25 seconds. It also changes how fees contribute to future network security. Faster target blocks do not make every payment final instantly, and node operators need compatible software.

Zcash has crossed an important protocol milestone. The NU7 network upgrade activated in early November at block height 3,543,405, bringing a faster target block interval and changes to the economics of future block rewards. The Zcash Foundation’s Zebra 7 release notes identify the same mainnet activation height and describe the consensus changes required for nodes to follow the chain.

The upgrade is not just a software patch. It changes how Zcash produces blocks and how some transaction fees are handled, while preserving the wider coin-issuance framework.

Zcash’s new target is a block every 25 seconds, down from 75 seconds before the upgrade. In theory, that means users and services waiting for several confirmations can receive them sooner. But target spacing is a statistical expectation, not a service-level guarantee: real blocks arrive irregularly, and exchanges or payment processors choose their own confirmation policies.

To prevent a tripling of the number of blocks from mechanically tripling new coin issuance, the upgrade scales the scheduled reward per block and adjusts remaining issuance and halving intervals. The idea is to deliver more frequent blocks while keeping the rate of monetary issuance broadly aligned with the previous schedule.

The node-software detail matters as much as the headline speed. Zebra’s release notes say operators must upgrade to a NU7-compatible client to remain on the consensus chain after the activation point. A user may experience difficulties if a wallet or service depends on an outdated node even when the network itself is operating normally.

NU7 also introduces a Network Sustainability Mechanism that directs a portion of transaction fees into a public reserve for future reissuance to miners. Accounts of the upgrade put that reserve contribution at 60% of fees, with the remaining 40% available to miners at the time of collection. The stated design goal is to smooth the eventual decline in subsidy-funded security spending as the network matures.

That is a policy choice within a public cryptocurrency, not free revenue. Money reserved for future rewards is not available in the same way as fees paid immediately to today’s miners. Evaluating the mechanism means examining incentives over time, its interaction with market demand and whether it achieves the intended security effects.

The change also retires support for older version-four transactions, affecting Zcash’s original Sprout pool. This is a reminder that long-running blockchains can carry technical baggage that becomes increasingly difficult to maintain, especially when privacy features and proof systems evolve.

Zcash offers privacy-preserving transfers, so network improvements should also be assessed against wallet compatibility, scanning performance and the accessibility of shielded payments. The upgrade can improve the underlying capability without ensuring every exchange, mobile wallet or merchant adopts the same features immediately.

NewsBTC previously examined the network’s Zebra synchronisation work ahead of NU7, when the emphasis was on nodes following the chain reliably. Activation does not eliminate those operational responsibilities.

For users and operators, the practical next checks are node versions, observed block times, confirmation requirements and whether particular wallets have published NU7 support notices. The verified milestone is a mainnet rule change; its day-to-day value will emerge through reliability and actual private-payment usage.

What discs can a PlayStation 5 play?

Some of your old games and other media work on your PS5, turning it into a proper media hub.

With Sony's plans to stop making disc-based games come 2028, physical media enthusiasts may want to start stocking up soon. While some PlayStation 5 owners already veered in this direction by purchasing the disc-free console variant, many traditionalists can't stand the thought of this transition. While no new games released in 2028 and beyond will get physical copies, existing discs will still continue to work.

We're over a year away from this shift — one that could very well play into what the PlayStation 6 ends up being. In the meantime, if you have a stack of Blu-ray movies, your go-to show on DVD or some last-gen games sitting around, they should all work on the PS5. Let's go through which discs are compatible with Sony's current console.

Remember that if you own the Slim digital PS5 or a PS5 Pro, you can buy the detachable PS5 disc drive for $79. This isn't compatible with the original Digital Edition model.

Of course, all PS5 game discs will continue to work on the console, as will your old PS4 physical games. Barring a few titles, the PS5 is backward compatible with the "overwhelming majority" of PS4 titles, whether on disc or digital. From Bloodborne to Red Dead Redemption 2, plenty of iconic PS4 releases haven't received the next-gen treatment, but you're still able to enjoy them on the newer system.

Even better, the console's Game Boost tech delivers smoother frame rates for certain PS5 games including God of War, The Last of Us Part II and Days Gone. While this feature doesn't apply to every game, many of the ones that received patches upped performance to 60 fps on PS5 — an improvement from how those titles ran on a standard PS4.

According to Sony's PS5 backward compatibility page, a total of six PS4 games aren't playable. Apologies to fans of Hitman Go: Definitive Edition and some other obscure titles like We Sing or Just Deal With It! The company notes that other PS4 releases like demos and media apps might not be compatible, either.

Unfortunately, the PS5's backward compatibility doesn't go any further; the console won't play physical PS1, PS2 or PS3 games. Plenty of legendary titles from older generations are available to play digitally from the PlayStation Store, though many beloved games like LittleBigPlanet and Shadow Hearts: Covenant remain abandoned on their original consoles.

Many of the best streaming services like Netflix and Disney+ offer apps on PS5. If you're planning to treat the console like an all-in-one media hub, you'll be glad to know the PlayStation 5 supports both Ultra HD Blu-ray and standard Blu-ray discs, as well as DVDs. That means all those old movies and TV box sets you bought years ago still have some life in them. However, 3D Blu-rays are a no-go.

Home-burned video discs may work if you properly finalize them, but there are no guarantees. You can't drop raw video files onto a disc and play them on the PS5. The PS5 can't play audio CDs, which matches is predecessor (though the PS3 had this capability).
