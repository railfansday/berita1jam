Core Scientific to quit Bitcoin mining by year-end as AI business surges

The former Bitcoin mining heavyweight plans to become a pure-play data center provider in 2027, with mining now accounting for just 7.5% of quarterly revenue.

Core Scientific said it expects to exit Bitcoin mining by the end of 2026 as it shifts its business toward artificial intelligence and high-performance computing infrastructure.

“We expect to complete the wind-down during the fourth quarter and enter 2027 as a pure-play, high-density colocation provider,” said Core Scientific chief financial officer Jim Nygaard during a Q3 earnings call on Thursday.

Core Scientific had previously disclosed in a July quarterly filing that hosted mining would end by Dec. 31 as it continued to wind down its self-mining operations. The comment establishes a targeted completion date for its complete Bitcoin mining exit.

Its latest quarterly results show how far the business has shifted. Third-quarter self-mining revenue fell to $12.3 million from $57.4 million a year earlier, while revenue from hosting other miners dropped to $5.2 million from $8.7 million.

Bitcoin mining generated just $17.5 million, or 7.5%, of Core Scientific’s $232.9 million in third-quarter revenue, compared with $215.4 million from colocation services.

The company had nearly 50% fewer miners online at the end of September than at the end of 2025, according to Nygaard.

Core Scientific’s move into AI hosting took shape in 2024 after signing an initial hosting agreement with CoreWeave in March that year, followed by a much larger deal announced in June.

At the time, Core Scientific presented AI hosting as an expansion alongside its mining business. Its June 2024 announcement emphasized preserving Bitcoin mining capacity.

By December 2025, The Wall Street Journal reported that Core Scientific was already targeting to wind down its Bitcoin mining operations by 2028.

Blue Origin says it is building a higher-performing rocket engine at 1/4 the cost

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

KENNEDY SPACE CENTER, Fla.—Blue Origin announced two significant upgrades to its New Glenn rocket on Thursday, reducing the cost of manufacturing the vehicle and expanding its capacity for payload delivery to geostationary orbit and the Moon.

With these upgrades, the company aims to eventually position New Glenn to launch more than 1,000 times a year.

“There has never been higher demand for launch, ever,” Dave Limp, chief executive officer of Blue Origin, said during the UP.Summit conference at the Shuttle Landing Facility in Florida.

Blue Origin has already begun test firing a new engine, called Northstar, that will eventually replace the BE-4 engine that powers the first stage of New Glenn. Separately, the company also said it was developing a hydrogen-oxygen third stage for the rocket, GS3, to give it an extra kick.

The Northstar engine is a simplified but more powerful version of the BE-4 engine, Limp said. It has half the number of parts, 30 percent less mass, and about 65 percent can be 3D-printed.

Blue Origin believes it can manufacture the engine at 25 percent of the cost of a BE-4 engine, and eventually reach the point where each engine can be assembled in less than a day. The company is deep into development and testing of Northstar, having already begun a test-firing campaign.

“To get access to space at a lower cost, we have to look at every part of the vehicle,” Limp said. “This is going to be a game-changer.”

The BE-4 engine powering New Glenn today has a thrust of 640,000 pounds, and Limp said Northstar will have a starting thrust of 715,000 pounds. Like the BE-4 engine, Northstar burns methane and liquid oxygen using an oxygen-rich staged combustion cycle and a single-shaft turbopump architecture.

Limp said the company will continue to manufacture the existing BE-4 engine for United Launch Alliance, which uses two of the engines to power its Vulcan rocket.

Blue Origin is developing a third stage for New Glenn called GS3 (creatively named Glenn Stage 3) to offer direct injection to geostationary orbit and Earth-escape trajectories. The third stage fits within an interstage, preserving the full fairing volume for customer payloads.

The stage can fly on both the smaller 7×2 variant (as in seven first stage engines and two second stage engines) of New Glenn as well as the larger 9×4 variant. According to Blue Origin, a GS3-equipped New Glenn 7×2 can carry 8 metric tons directly to geostationary orbit and 10 metric tons to translunar injection. Those figures increase to 14 tons and 20 tons, respectively, for the larger 9×4 variant.

As the rocket’s third stage, it needs to be light, Limp said. For this reason, the third stage is made mostly of composites, which are high-performing and lightweight. But there’s a problem with that.

The stage will be powered by the company’s BE-7 engine, already used by its lunar landers. This engine is optimized for in-space operations and uses liquid hydrogen and oxygen propellants. But hydrogen is the smallest atom and therefore leaky. With composites, thermal cycling and mechanical strain can cause cracking, exacerbating losses of molecular hydrogen.

Limp said the company was not sure a large composite tank, 5.5-meter diameter in this case, could be constructed that would contain hydrogen at high pressures.

The company recently had a breakthrough at Launch Site One in Texas, when it completed its first composite liquid hydrogen tanking test. During the test, the tank was pressurized to 100 percent of the maximum force expected during operation for 2.5 hours. It was even successfully tested at 150 percent of this maximum pressure for 10 minutes.

That gave the company confidence that a GS3 based on composite tanks was a concept that could go forward, and now the company is aiming for a debut in 2028 for a government customer.

“We have one customer signed up for cislunar space in 2028,” Limp said. “We are open for business.”

The widespread assumption that only smokers can get lung cancer and the societal propensity to blame people who get it is a critical challenge to helping them, she adds. “The research the Susan Wojcicki Foundation is supporting is incredibly promising and could lead to significant improvements in patient outcomes while accelerating progress in lung cancer care,” she says.

Vins adds that he’s dedicated to the SWF because he wants to honor Susan’s lasting presence in the creator space.

“Susan was revered as a business leader who helped shape the technology and media landscape, but to creators, she was revered for something even more personal—her unwavering belief in our potential,” he says. “She empowered our voices at a time when the possibilities of YouTube were only beginning to emerge. A Simple, Heartwarming Way to Get Out of Your Own Head

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Posted November 5, 2026 | Reviewed by Jessica Schrader

There is no shortage of influencers out there providing advice and hawking products to improve our lives. So, whether it is how to maximize fitness gains, how to sleep better, or how and when to meditate, we are seemingly all on a quest to feel happier. Yet, what if there's another way to feel better that we may not think about enough?

Here's a newsflash: If you want to really move the feel-better needle, then help someone else. You don't have to do huge things to help others. I’m saying the more we notice people who could use something from us, the more it can positively change our moods, especially if we act on that urge to help them.

There are endless videos online of people doing kind things for strangers, including paying for gas, cleaning up yards, and providing money to those in need. These stories are heartwarming. But what if we are watching popular videos about kindness and not enough seeing the power of doing kind things ourselves? Do we want to settle for those vicarious doses of feeling moved by the kind actions taken by others and then just call it a day?

In my newly released book, Help Them: And Watch Your Own Life Get Better, I further discuss the powerful emotional health benefits we gain by directly helping others. Maybe someone you know could really feel good getting a phone call. Is someone you know going through a tough time and could really perk up with some encouragement? Or, and this is tough for all of us, maybe somebody needs the wonderful gift of you "just listening" without immediately jumping in with advice.

I don’t know about you, but for years I've been internally preoccupied (OK, I’ll admit it—often consumed) by my own thoughts. It seems to me, the questions that really weigh on all of us are things like: Am I really happy? Or, did I mess that up? And, of course, we go down "Regret Alley" with thoughts like, “If only I didn't mess that up, I'd be so much better off.” That said, do you agree that there is a point where self-focus becomes its own trap?

By helping others, at least for a while, you are thinking about something besides yourself. This notion is supported by research on kindness and helping behavior that has linked helping others with our own greater well-being. How about right now, you get back in touch with a time when you knew you made a difference? Didn’t it feel pretty darn good? Next, let's look at how to keep helping helpful versus it ever becoming harmful.

Like most things in life, there can be too much done sometimes when it comes to helping others. I have seen this play out with many of my parent-coaching clients, where helping is not always healthy. Specifically, I see this all the time with parents of struggling adult children. For example, a parent pays another bill, makes another phone call, fixes another problem, or bails their child out of another mess.

The danger is that sometimes helping becomes rescuing. Sadly, and I will even say tragically, rescuing can keep another person from developing the skills they need. So, a useful question is not just, "How can I help?", but more importantly, "Will my help actually help?" In some cases, giving support may be best through giving our listening ear than giving from our wallets. And, even further, sometimes we may help best if, after weighing the situation, we say no.

When it comes to our perceptions of what helping is, we tend to think meaningful acts have to be dramatic. But they don't have to be huge acts of kindness. How about you send that text you've been loosely debating sending to someone who may feel really good by hearing from you? Or, better yet, call them and let them hear your supportive voice. While you're at it, maybe tell that person why you really appreciate them.

It feels good to share that a teen counseling client of mine just told me the other day how great he felt by helping an elderly neighbor by raking some leaves (he politely refused her offer to pay him) at her house (she had recently lost her husband of 40 years). A parent I work with recently shared how good she felt about becoming more patient with her children when they would go into their long, colorful stories.

I hope this post inspires you to help others because it may turn out to be the best self-improvement plan you could ever embark on. When you do help others, you will feel less in your head because your focus has meaningfully shifted. The more we help others, the better we will feel. So, please: help them.

The 10 Best Netflix Original Thriller Movies, Ranked

📅 Nov 06, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

As Netflix continues to be one of the most successful and prominent distributors of both film and television in the modern era of streaming services and digital media, they have made waves by releasing a massive amount of content in each distinct genre of filmmaking. One of the most prominent and impactful genres that Netflix has made its mark on is the thriller genre, releasing a wide array of not only high-octane action thrillers but also slow and methodical crime thrillers and other explorations of suspense.

Netflix has largely cemented itself as a hub for enticing and highly engaging original thriller films from a wide array of both legendary and rising filmmakers and stars in the industry. While other streaming services have their fair share of great original thrillers to offer, in terms of sheer mass and volume of great thriller experiences, Netflix has the competition beat. Several of these thrillers are so effective that they are in conversation as some of the best thrillers in recent memory.

While Anna Kendrick is more commonly known for her work in various comedy films like Pitch Perfect and A Simple Favor, her directorial debut with Woman of the Hour is a dark and unnerving serial killer film. Based on a real-life story, the film follows the events surrounding the time when a notorious serial killer found himself competing as a contestant on the beloved TV game show The Dating Game during the 1970s. On top of directing the film, Kendrick portrays the female contestant playing alongside this unknown mass murderer.

Woman of the Hour does a great job of setting up the pieces and building up to the main event game show, making sure that its core moments are oozing with tension and dread. The film shows not only the life of the innocent female contestant before her time in the game, but also various murders committed by Rodney (Daniel Zovatto) to get a sense of his pure evil nature despite his charming demeanor. Beyond the actual story being told, however, there is a powerful and deeply impactful subtext about the power dynamics of the era and how ignorance leads to pure evil going unpunished.

The first of what would end up being several Stephen King adaptations by modern horror auteur Mike Flanagan, Gerald's Game does a great job of balancing both the horror and the rising anxiety and tension of its premise. The film sees a married couple having fun at an isolated house for a holiday, getting up to some erotic sexual escapades. However, after Jessie (Carla Gugino) is handcuffed to the bed, her husband Gerald (Bruce Greenwood) suddenly dies of a heart attack, leaving her stuck with little hope of rescue.

Gerald's Game does a brilliant job of translating the tension and dread of the original King novel to the big screen, especially amplified by its powerful lead performance from Gugino. While it certainly plays with elements of horror filmmaking, it is much more in line with a psychological thriller, following the dread and inner demons that Jessie faces while in such a painful predicament. The film quickly cemented Flanagan as the go-to visionary for modern King adaptations, further amplified by his work on films like Doctor Sleep and The Life of Chuck.

While acclaimed writer/director Charlie Kaufman isn't nearly as well known for his work on thrillers, I'm Thinking of Ending Things acts as an ingenious combination of his signature style through the lens of a psychological thriller. The film follows a young woman (Jessie Buckley) who goes on a trip with her boyfriend (Jesse Plemons) to meet his parents (Toni Collette and David Thewlis). However, the film proves to be much more dynamic and complicated than this simple premise would have one believe, as the story begins intersecting with seemingly unconnected footage of a school janitor (Guy Boyd) going to work.

Much like Kaufman's other work, I'm Thinking of Ending Things is entirely defined by its subtext and deeper subliminal meanings, and while this can make for an inaccessible experience for traditional audiences, those in touch with the subtext will find a rich story of pain, humanity, and a yearning soul. The film is exceptional when it comes to the finer details and unnerving atmosphere of its story, amplifying these feelings of things being slightly off and uncomfortable until it reaches its confusing yet highly engaging third act.

A period-piece crime thriller that combines the culture of rural America with a more raw and guttural look at the people and pains of post-World War II, The Devil All the Time follows a rarely explored piece of American history. The film follows the ins and outs of the town of Knockmstiff, Ohio, and its neighboring backwoods, as various sinister characters and devilish opportunities begin to crop up around young Arvin Russell (Tom Holland). Amidst the pain and difficulties that he faces, Arvin is dedicated to fighting these evil forces that threaten him and his family.

More than anything else, The Devil All the Time's greatest strengths come from how it brings to life the conniving yet heartfelt world of rural America in the mid-20th century. Various characters breathe life into the world in their own distinct ways, whether it be through innocence and charm or unsuspecting evil and malice. Holland does a great job with one of his rare solely dramatic roles, yet it's easily Robert Pattinson's performance as the manipulative Southern priest Reverend Preston Teagarden that steals the show and makes the film a must-watch thriller.

The Night Comes for Us brings together a lot of the same talent from the acclaimed Indonesian action thriller series The Raid to create another raw and unrestrained action thriller that is basically a part of The Raid franchise in everything but name. The violent martial arts film follows an elite Triad assassin (Joe Taslim) finding himself in the crosshairs of his former employers after sparing a young girl's life during a massacre. Most notably, he is forced to face off against his friend Arian (Iko Uwais), who is attempting to kill him in an attempt to gain power among the gangs.

The Night Comes for Us features a lot of the same highlights and entertaining aspects of The Raid series, with cutthroat action and top-of-the-line choreography amplifying the action for true catharsis. Its relatively simple plot leads the way to some of the most entertaining and vicious action imaginable, providing a distinct style that is different from any of the action thrillers being made in the U.S.

Their performances prove to elevate the thrills and tension of the film as a whole, making each moment and twist feel more palpable and impactful than the last. It delves into the toxic mentality that leads to relationships falling apart, and how some people simply can't imagine not being the breadwinner in a relationship.

Carry-On harkens back to a simpler time of action thriller filmmaking, where simply having an engaging premise with a unique setting and sleek execution was enough to elevate a film to be as entertaining as possible. The film follows Ethan Kopek (Taron Egerton), a TSA officer working on Christmas Eve who finds himself racing against the clock to stop a mysterious traveler (Jason Bateman) from getting a dangerous item onto a flight. It quickly becomes a game of cat and mouse between the two as they attempt to outsmart one another and stay one step ahead.

Considering how director Jaume Collet-Serra released a wide array of great action thrillers throughout the 2010s, Carry-On is not only a return to form for him as a director, but also a return to form for the genre as a whole. The airport setting simply works perfectly for this type of race-against-time thriller, with compelling characters and an array of twists and turns making the film a very entertaining crowd-pleaser. Audiences certainly appreciated this return to form, as Carry-On quickly became one of Netflix's biggest successes of recent memory.

While Netflix is widely celebrated for its various American film releases, there are also a great number of international features that are released by the company worldwide, with one of the most underrated being the Polish thriller Operation Hyacinth. The film is based on the events surrounding the titular secret mass operation of the Polish communist police, where they attempted to create a national database of all homosexuals in Poland. The film follows a police officer delving into a dark rabbit hole after being personally dissatisfied with the result of a murder investigation.

Operation Hyacinth has all the elements of a truly great crime thriller, with a large emphasis on mystery and hidden information from its protagonist and the heightened fear of getting to the bottom of the truth. Its prospects as a Polish film have limited the spread of its influence among American audiences, yet the film is so effective in its filmmaking that it easily deserves to be in the conversation of one of Netflix's best thrillers to date.

A frequent trend that has defined action thrillers of recent memory has been the John Wick-style of film, where a single vengeful man takes down hordes of enemies all by themselves. While it certainly has a lot of the same benefits as this style of thriller, Rebel Ridge is a highly engaging twist on the premise, seeing a situation escalate to the point of no return. The film follows a former Marine (Aaron Pierre) forced to confront corruption in a small town after their local law enforcement unjustly seizes a bag of cash that he needs to post his cousin's bail.

Rebel Ridge excels not only in terms of energizing and exceptionally choreographed action, but also as a simple yet painfully relevant story of the abuse of power and corruption in local law enforcement. Pierre gives a committed lead performance that leans into both his stern capabilities as a former soldier and his cool, calm demeanor under all sorts of pressure. Not a second in the film goes by that isn't impactful on the audience, making the film not only one of Netflix's best thrillers but also one of their best original action movies.

David Fincher has been a widely beloved and impactful director when it comes to the thriller genre, with films like Se7en, Zodiac, and Gone Girl still being considered some of the most acclaimed thrillers of all time. However, The Killer has proven to be one of the most unique and dynamic thrillers that the director has ever released, actively going against the grain and commenting on his style and influence in the genre. The film follows an assassin (Michael Fassbender) who is forced to face off against his employers after a catastrophic miss on his latest mission.

From the outset, The Killer seems like it would fall into place and feel similar to Fincher's previous thrillers, following a stern and calculated protagonist distancing himself from the horrors of murder. However, the film actively goes against this grain of thinking, satirizing this restrained mindset and showing the difficulties of living such an isolated, perfectionist lifestyle. It brilliantly comments and critiques Fincher's own work while also evolving it to the modern era, making for a distinct evolution of his storytelling style and the must-watch thriller of the decade.

A gay influencer in Taiwan who became famous dancing in high heels, and the filmmaker who trained his camera on him for nine years, both stare down middle age in “My Silly Flirty Boys.”

The documentary from Taiwan-based Hong Kong filmmaker Frankie Sin is at the Taiwan Creative Content Fest (TCCF) in Taipei.

Sin followed Fufu from 2017 to 2026. Over that stretch, Fufu built a following with racy dance routines, performances in high heels and his online channel “FJ234,” where he talked openly about desire and helped bring drag queens and other LGBTQ+ performers to mainstream audiences. Not everyone approved. Critics who wanted gay men presented in a more respectable light branded him a “cancer of the gay community.”

Away from the spotlight, Fufu talks frankly about what fame has cost him and about the loneliness that sets in once the persona comes off. Now nearing 40, he is coming to terms with a face and body that are getting older.

“It began with my own anxiety over my queer identity and feeling out of place within the community’s mainstream standards, which led me to point my camera at a maverick who dared to challenge sexual taboos,” Sin says.

More than 10 years ago, Sin swapped one of Hong Kong’s conservative outlying islands for Taiwan, hoping its reputation for openness would give him a place to belong. What he found instead was a local gay scene that prized hyper-masculinity and a “model gay” image.

“This triggered a second wave of anxiety about my body and identity,” Sin says. “In 2016, I met Fufu, an influencer famous for dancing in high heels. His bold, confident display of desire stood in sharp contrast to my own insecurities and repression. He became a mirror for me.”

The shoot spans the years around Taiwan’s legalization of same-sex marriage and charts how queer entertainment grew and was then swallowed up by the mainstream market.

“As two gay men approaching 40 – one losing his spotlight, the other confronting his deepest insecurities – we both face a midlife crisis, learning how to make peace with aging, loneliness, and ourselves,” Sin says.

Fufu’s channel is shutting down this year, and with nearly all the key footage shot, the team is logging nine years of material ahead of a rough cut. Color grading, the sound mix, an original score and music clearances will follow, with picture lock targeted for the third quarter of 2027.

Stefano Centini and Chuti Chang serve as executive producers, with Henry Leong and Peter Yam producing. The producers say the film takes an honest look at the pecking order and shared anxiety inside Taiwan’s gay male community.

“By placing the personal changes of both men alongside the rise and fall of Taiwan’s queer entertainment industry, the film offers a rare, honest perspective with strong potential for cross-cultural resonance,” they say.

In Taipei, the team is looking for gap financing to finish post. It also wants to meet co-producers and sales agents from abroad, plus festival programmers, to help plan a worldwide release.

Sin is also developing two more feature documentaries about belonging and family. “An Island Boy” follows a young Hong Kong fisherman, while “Nomads of the Island” explores Sin’s own family history.

The Taiwan Creative Content Fest, organized by the Taiwan Creative Content Agency (TAICCA), runs Nov. 6–9 at the Taipei Nangang Exhibition Center.
