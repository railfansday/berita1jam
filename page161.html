Suno Moves Into AI Filmmaking With New Product ‘Visuals’

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The AI company is expanding to video and image generation with its latest launch, describing its mission as a "creative entertainment" brand rather than a strictly musical one.

Suno is pushing past the world of AI-powered music creation and into the world of videos and images with its latest launch, Visuals. With the new tools, users can generate up to 15 seconds of video based on written prompts, music input or images. The product can be used to create music videos, lyric videos or cover art, among other use cases. In a company blog post announcing the launch, the company’s chief product officer, Jack Brody, notes that Suno is a “creative entertainment” company that goes “beyond music.”

“Suno began with a simple belief: everyone is creative. With the right tools, people can make something original and feel the joy of bringing an idea to life,” writes Brody. Last month, Suno launched Speech, its first push past music generation, which allows for users to prompt their way into generating poems, spoken audio and meditations.

“Video had always been the logical next step in our vision, but until recently video generation was slow and too expensive for anyone to create but enterprises,” says Brody. “A new generation of models changes that and opens video generation up to creative entertainment for everyone.”

According to representatives for Suno, Visuals is powered by H3 Move, a model derived from H3 from MiniMax, fine-tuned/optimized for how Suno believes its community will use the product. Unlike other AI visual generators, Suno’s is made to align itself with the structure and story of a user’s musical creations.

The launch of visuals also unlocks new monetization for Suno. Visual credits are treated differently than song credits, and free-tier users cannot generate more than a single five-second video before hitting their credit limit. Any further use requires topping up for more money.

The blog post about Visuals is careful to note the “additional safeguards” added “to protect artists from unauthorized use of their likenesses or works, on top of the ones we already have in place for music.”

These new safeguards include “new text and visual filters intended to block uploads and prompts that try to use or create visual representations of specific artists, public figures, copyrighted works and IP.” The company also scans videos before sharing them with users to ensure they do not violate community guidelines against a range of “harmful content.” All visuals will also be embedded with watermarks before they can be shared off the platform.

“We are pairing these protections with in-platform user education. The first time a Suno user tries to upload a video or image to use with Visuals, we remind them the tool is for original creation, and to confirm they have the rights to any file they are trying to use,” the blog post explains.

Some of the ramifications of abusing the tool can include removing the content, restricting the account or even terminating a Suno user.

News of Suno’s Visuals launch comes about six months after OpenAI discontinued its short-form video generator, Sora, which could also generate music in the background as a byproduct of the service. At the time, copyright owners complained about the platform’s use of protected characters, brands and images. Billboard also found that the platform could, in rare cases, generate clips of copyrighted songs, like Pop Smoke‘s “What You Know Bout Love.”

In recent months, legal action against Suno has mounted. On top of an additional lawsuit from Universal Music Group and Sony Music surrounding alleged copyright infringement, Elvis Presley‘s estate and a class action group, including Jason Isbell, have also recently sued the company over alleged violations of their name, image, likeness and voice.

Suno has argued in lawsuits that its current models do not allow users to include the names of specific artists in prompts, nor produce outputs that mimic specific real voices. The complaint from Elvis’s estate argues that those claims are “unequivocally false.” They included links to videos capturing the use of Suno to play songs that sound remarkably similar to The King.

NASA to Cover 35th SpaceX Resupply Mission Space Station Departure

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

NASA and its international partners are set to receive scientific research samples and hardware as a SpaceX Dragon spacecraft departs the International Space Station no earlier than Wednesday, Nov. 18, for its return to Earth.

Pending weather conditions, the Dragon spacecraft will undock from the Harmony module’s space-facing port at 11:05 a.m. EST, following a command sent by ground controllers at SpaceX’s facility in Hawthorne, California.

NASA’s live coverage of undocking and departure begins at 10:45 a.m., streaming through a variety of platforms. Learn where to watch online:

NASA and SpaceX are monitoring weather forecasts and targeting splashdown off the California coast no earlier than 1:50 a.m. Thursday, Nov. 19 (10:50 p.m. PST Nov. 18). Teams will adjust plans as needed based on splashdown conditions in the Dragon recovery area and will provide updates on splashdown timing when available. NASA will not stream the splashdown but will post updates on its space station blog.

Thousands of pounds of cargo are returning from the space station, including research samples that could advance future space exploration and improve life on Earth. The returning research includes a 3D-bioprinted blood vessel construct scientists used to study vascular aging and cardiovascular disease in space and on Earth. Frozen tumor organoids treated aboard the station also will return, helping researchers identify new ways to improve cancer treatments and advance personalized medicine. Scientists will analyze frozen microbial samples to better understand how antibiotic-resistant bacteria survive, adapt, and evolve in space. In addition, a lighting system will return after more than three years aboard the space station, where researchers tested its potential to support astronauts’ sleep and well-being during long-duration missions.

Filled with more than 6,300 pounds of crew supplies, science investigations, and equipment, the spacecraft arrived at the space station on Oct. 15. It launched two days earlier on a Falcon 9 rocket from Space Launch Complex 40 at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station in Florida for the agency’s SpaceX 35th commercial resupply services mission.

For more than 26 years, people have lived and worked continuously aboard the International Space Station, advancing scientific knowledge and making research breakthroughs not possible on Earth. The space station helps NASA understand and overcome the challenges of human spaceflight, expand commercial opportunities in low Earth orbit, and build on the foundation for long-duration missions to the Moon, as part of the Artemis and Moon Base programs, and to Mars.

Germany U21 Player Ratings | Max Moerstedt and others shine as DFB youngsters slip to defeat in Croatian swamp

📅 Nov 17, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Antonio Di Salvo’s Germany U21 side have wrapped up the second of their two November 2026 international break friendlies with a 2-3 loss against the Croatia U21s in Rijeka. The Croat juniors, working under the aegis of Bundesliga veteran Ivica Olic, prevailed in a match literally played in horrendously abhorrent rainy, soppy, and soggy conditions. In a case not dissimilar to their previous defeat of the international break, the DFB youths can still take plenty of positives away from the undesired result.

Despite the fact that the squad was coming off its first defeat since October 2025, Di Salvo made just one change to the team that lost to Belgium in Essen on Friday night. Multiple defensive errors from Eintracht Frankfurt central defender Nnamdi Collins led to the 22-year-old being replaced by Hertha BSC center back Linus Gechter. Poor visibility conditions made it difficult to ascertain what sort of shape the DFB team lined up in, but it did appear as if the DFB charged forward in a tight 4-2-2-2. This may have been the result of the players simply trying to remain within eyeshot of each other.

Both sides had to fight not only against thick fog, but also a merciless downpour that drenched the playing surface. The hosts managed an early shot wide of the German goal in the 3rd minute. Two minutes later, current DFB form leader Muhammed Damar generated a shot on target via a set piece. A prolonged lull followed the early chances. Understandably, the players weren’t in much of a mood to take risks in light of the treacherous conditions. A morbid manifestation of the dangers on the pitch came at the quarter-of-an-hour mark when Hennes Behrens found himself lying on the deck after a wicked slip. Fortunately, the Augsburg professional was able to continue after receiving treatment on the sideline.

Amid the brutal conditions, both teams were somehow able to cobble together some genuine football. Damar, by some means, got a boot around the slippery ball in the 18th minute and proceeded to play through striker Dzenan Pejcinovic. The Stuttgart attacker timed his finish off the bounce well, but still shot wide of completely beaten Croat keeper Toni Silic. Regrettably, this miss would come back to haunt the German guests. When it was their turn to furnish some coherent football, the Croatians scored the opening goal. Neither Gechter nor German keeper Dennis Seimen looked good when wingers Adriano Jagusic and Adrian Segegic combined in the 26th minute. The latter Croat starter all-too-easily swept home the 1-0.

Olic’s team kept the pressure on, but found themselves well contained by an increasingly stable German defensive unit. The waterlogged pitch naturally made it difficult for Di Salvo’s crew to respond on the counter. Some German breakthroughs nevertheless came in the 31st (Paris Brunner), 33rd (Pejcinovic), and 34th (Damar). The fog cleared towards the end of the opening 45, but the rainfall continued. The Germany U21s reacted by attempting to counter via long launches. Brunner could hardly be faulted for missing connections in the 41st and 44th. Kade also drew a booking with a determined run forward out of the back in the 43rd.

In a reaction presumably based just as much on general kindness as sporting performance, Di Salvo opted to send four of his starters to the warmth of a shower at the halftime break. Augsburg teammates Kade and Behrens hit the showers, as did fullback Elias Baum and captain Joshua Quarshie. Collins, Leandro Morgalla, Tom Rothe, and Max Moerstedt entered proceedings ahead of the restart. All members of the quartet exerted a positive influence on the match as the guests overcame the conditions and completely turned the game around.

Midfielders Noel Aseko (46th) and Cajetan Lenz (48th) played their role in ensuring that a different tone would be set in the second half. The former saw an effort blocked while the latter nearly broke through on a delicate run. Lenz would then head a Rothe cross just wide in the 51st minute. Lenz did better with a Damar free kick two minutes later, playing the ball precisely into the path of an onrushing Moerstedt. The Hoffenheim striker remained on the ball after his first effort was blocked, banging home the 53rd-minute 1-1 on his second bite of the cherry.

Germany kept their hosts pinned back in their own half after the well-deserved equalizer. Several more chances nearly materialized as the Germans pressed forward hard. In most cases, however, lake-sized puddles in the penalty area precluded the DFB XI from actually getting the ball into the back of the net. Brunner finally managed to deliver a quality cross at the hour mark. Germany U21 form leader Dzenan Pejcinovic was on hand to polish off the delivery for the 60th-minute 2-1.

The Germans threatened to run away with the match after Pejcinovic’s tally. Brunner unluckily struck the post. Pejcinovic led another promising attacking charge and, at the other end of the pitch, nearly sparked counters with two effective corner clearances. Though the hosts were getting into the German half, the actual equalizer in the 67th minute came off a random long ball that stalled in a puddle before the outrushing keeper Seimen could reach it. Croat striker Fran Topic was simply in the right place at the right time to smack in the 2-2.

The Germans certainly gave themselves cause to feel hard done by during the final stretch of the match. Regular advances from Di Salvo’s side eventually produced a dazzling distance effort from Pejcinovic in the 82nd minute. Lenz found himself in an almost perfect position to stuff home the spilled rebound, but finished well wide in front of the beaten Croat keeper. Two minutes later, Topic latched onto a ball the Germans couldn’t clear in the 84th minute. Topic again made the most of his fortuitous positioning with a sharp, tight-angle finish for the 3-2 that led the hosts to victory.

The author finds himself in the rather unusual position of awarding the Player of the Match distinction to a substitute who entered proceedings at halftime. In this particular case, however, Hoffenheim striker Max Moerstedt deserves the recognition for his immediate impact on a match that Germany appeared increasingly unlikely to turn around during the opening 45 minutes.

Moerstedt’s introduction provided Di Salvo’s attack with renewed impetus. The 20-year-old made himself available in dangerous positions, worked effectively with the German midfielders, and displayed commendable determination when his first opportunity to score arrived. After Lenz redirected Damar’s 53rd-minute free kick into his path, Moerstedt refused to surrender possession when his initial effort was blocked. His persistence paid off with Germany’s equalizer.

The performance represented an encouraging development for a player who only received his U21 call-up after Nicolo Tresoldi was forced to withdraw from the November squad. Moerstedt made his debut with a brief appearance against Belgium on Friday before taking a much more prominent role in Rijeka.

Regular readers of Get German Football News will already be familiar with the Hoffenheim youngster’s considerable potential. The author previously selected Moerstedt as GGFN Player of the Match in Hoffenheim’s 3-1 Bundesliga victory away at Eintracht Frankfurt. On that occasion, the striker earned an 8/10 rating after scoring an important equalizer and delivering an exceptionally active attacking performance.

GGFN‘s comprehensive 2026/27 Hoffenheim tactical preview also revisited Moerstedt’s development and the competition for attacking places under Christian Ilzer. The youngster has had to contend with a crowded group of forwards at club level despite demonstrating his Bundesliga capabilities whenever meaningful opportunities have arisen.

His first Germany U21 goal in his second Germany U21 cap offers another encouraging indication of what he can contribute when afforded the opportunity.

Naturally, Moerstedt wasn’t the only attacking player who deserved recognition in Rijeka. Paris Brunner (8) played an important role in Germany’s second-half revival, supplying the cross for Pejcinovic’s goal and subsequently striking the post himself. The Monaco professional continued to pose a threat even when the increasingly waterlogged surface made conventional attacking football virtually impossible.

Dzenan Pejcinovic (8) also merits particular praise. The Stuttgart striker recovered from an unfortunate first-half miss to put Germany ahead, contributed to several promising attacks, and remained alert enough to help his teammates defensively during Croatian set pieces. His long-range effort in the 82nd minute nearly produced another German goal.

All three attackers made persuasive cases for the individual distinction. Moerstedt receives the nod because his introduction helped change the complexion of the match, while his equalizer set the stage for Germany’s impressive second-half response.

The result ultimately went Croatia’s way. Nevertheless, Moerstedt, Brunner, and Pejcinovic supplied plenty of evidence that Di Salvo has several promising attacking options at his disposal as preparations continue for next summer’s European U21 Championship.

The Yorkshire outfit also had many players coming through their academy system looking to become the next Alan Smith or Robbie Keane in the first team, with Aaron Lennon and James Milner two players who emerged into the first team in the years following.

Rav Matharu was in the age group with Aaron's older brother Anthony, signing a professional contract at 17 years of age, having been born in Leeds and then progressed through the ranks as a Leeds-mad boy.

There were inevitably extra eyes on him as a young Asian player, as both he and Harpal Singh reached the fringes of the first team at the same time. But he says he never got close to making a first-team appearance for Leeds, having also failed to appear for the reserve side, which often consisted of first-team players too.

Matharu said: "I don't think I got close. Maybe I'll tell people differently in the pub after a few drinks, but I don't think I was near to breaking through to the first team at Leeds.

There were internationals playing in the reserves, it was hard to progress. I remember being on the bench for the reserves against Sheffield Wednesday, and that was probably the closest. Chris Waddle was playing for them and I was watching him, remembering idolising him at the 1990 World Cup.

"It was hard to break into that team because it was doing so well. There were internationals in the reserves like Jason Wilcox and David Hopkin. It was a club where you had to do exceptionally well to even get on the bench of the first team."

After leaving Elland Road, Matharu admitted that he began to fall out of love with football after trials with other clubs, and after failing to find a suitable next home, he began to work in retail - and never looked back.

Speaking about his next steps, he said: "I always used to draw. I was into clothing and footwear and designing. It seemed like something I could do. I really wanted to learn more.

I went to Loughborough University to do a foundation degree in art and design, and was then offered a place at London College of Fashion. I went there for two weeks but dropped out because the expense was too much. I moved back to Leeds and worked in retail for three years, but I always felt I had to do something more with my life. So I picked up my studies at Leeds College of Art.

"I didn't know how to sew. I didn't know how to cut patterns. But I knew I had a good eye for fashion and clothing, and from a young age I'd always wanted to know how it was put together."

After moving to London to pursue a dream of working in fashion, he was let go from his role as Head Designer at House of Billiam, which forced him to set up his own business.

He added: "When I moved to London I always had the intention of starting my own brand. The name was born at uni; a lot of people cut patterns with scissors, but I would always use a scalpel, and my tutor would constantly tell me I was like a surgeon. I'd built a small website and registered the name clothsurgeon, but didn't really do anything with it until it was time to do my own thing."

Clothsurgeon has gone from strength to strength, with Matharu explaining exactly how the process works.

"We concentrate on quality fabrics, quality production. You can come in and create a wardrobe of quality clothes.

We offer a full bespoke service where you can make whatever you want, timeless silhouettes with a contemporary edge that can last you a lifetime. Beautiful overcoats that will not go out of fashion in your lifetime. A bomber jacket, a suit, whatever it is.

His clothes have become renowned for offering unique details, taking off massively since October 2012.

Since then, Matharu's clothes have been purchased by the likes of Kendrick Lamar, A$AP Rocky, Drake, Sir Lewis Hamilton, Ian Wright OBE, Kevin Hart and Eric Cantona.

The brand has also included collaborations with Coca-Cola, Nike and Selfridges, while he became Savile Row’s first bespoke streetwear store.and the first south Asian designer to open on the Row.

It's been quite the change of direction from cleaning O'Leary's boots at Leeds, but Matharu is thriving.
