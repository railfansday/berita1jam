Mercari and Suruga-ya to Establish Joint Venture Mercari Surugaya Inc. in the US

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Joint venture to combine the knowledge and AI usage of Mercari and Suruga-ya for the purchase, procurement, and sale of Entertainment & Hobbies items in the US to bring Japanese pop culture to fans overseas

TOKYO, Nov. 16, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Mercari, Inc. (head office: Minato-ku, Tokyo; Representative Executive Officer and CEO: Shintaro Yamada; "Mercari") announces that on November 16, 2026, it decided to establish a joint venture called Mercari Surugaya Inc. (the "Joint Venture") with SURUGA-YA Inc. (head office: Shizuoka-shi, Shizuoka; CEO: Tsunashige Sugiyama; "Suruga-ya") in the US primarily for the purpose of purchasing, procuring, and selling Entertainment & Hobbies items.

The Joint Venture will combine the strengths of Mercari and Suruga-ya to expand Japanese Entertainment & Hobbies items in the US and drive the spread of Japanese pop culture to fans overseas.

In recent years, interest in anime, manga, games, and other Japanese pop culture has surged across the world, with "Made in Japan" and "Used in Japan" items increasingly growing in popularity.

According to a survey by the Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry[1], the total transaction value of crossborder e-commerce purchases from Japanese businesses by consumers in the US and China increased to approximately 4.4 trillion JPY in 2025, showing that the crossborder e-commerce market is continuing to expand. Furthermore, the overseas market for the Japanese anime industry reached approximately 2.2 trillion JPY in 2024 and surpassed the domestic market for two consecutive years[2], indicating the growing popularity of Japanese pop culture in the global market.

Under its mission to circulate all forms of value to unleash the potential in all people, Mercari has positioned global expansion as a pillar of its mid-term management strategy, and launched its marketplace app in the US in 2014. Mercari also brings Japanese items to consumers overseas through its crossborder transaction business launched in 2019[3] and the Mercari Global App launched in September 2025.[4] The Mercari Global App is currently available in Taiwan, Hong Kong, and the US. The GMV of the crossborder business has expanded approximately 19x over the past four years, reaching 112.2 billion JPY in FY2026.6.[5]

Suruga-ya is one of Japan's leading companies specialized in Entertainment & Hobbies items, possessing a network of 160 retail stores across Japan and overseas (as of October 2026), an extensive inventory of tens of millions of items, catalog data covering more than approximately 30 million products, and accumulated expertise in authenticity verification. Demonstrating its impressive specialization in a wide range of categories, such as figurines, trading cards, games, and anime merchandise, Suruga-ya is popular among fans both within Japan and around the world.

Mercari and Suruga-ya concluded a capital and business partnership agreement in December 2025[6] and have since strengthened their collaboration through initiatives such as selling items from Suruga-ya on Mercari. Now, the two companies are taking their partnership one step further to establish a joint venture for both to operate business on the ground in the US.

Suruga-ya possesses extensive inventory, product catalog data, expertise in appraisal and authenticity verification, and a secondhand goods purchasing network in the Japanese Entertainment & Hobbies domain.

The Joint Venture will combine these strengths of Suruga-ya with Mercari's sales channels in the US and expertise in leveraging AI and creating services that can be easily used by anyone, in order to purchase, procure, and sell Japanese Entertainment & Hobbies items in the US.

(1) Expanding the handling of Japanese items in the US

In addition to purchasing and selling Japanese Entertainment & Hobbies items (online purchases and operation of physical stores) in the US, starting with Los Angeles, the Joint Venture will sell such items through Mercari Group services such as Mercari US and the Mercari Global App in order to bring Japanese pop culture closer to fans in the US.

(2) Accumulating and leveraging product data through purchases and sales

Data on market price and demand of Japanese Entertainment & Hobbies items in the US is something that can only be obtained through actual transactions in the US. The Joint Venture will accumulate product data through purchases and sales in the US, combine it with Suruga-ya's extensive product catalogs and appraisal expertise, and leverage it to enhance the user experience of services such as search, recommendations, and authenticity verification, as well as to strengthen both Mercari's and Suruga-ya's businesses.

(3) Enhancing AI-powered operations and the purchase experience

The Joint Venture will leverage AI in operations related to the purchase of items, product catalogs, inventory, and retail (processes such as appraisal, product registration, and price setting) to increase efficiency. AI will also be utilized to enhance the ease of searching for items and supplement item information.

Bitcoin’s price slide to a mid-July low of $62,000 has crushed the overall crypto market, erasing over $380 billion in value last week and pulling the total market cap down to $2.25 trillion. The sudden drop has pushed the Crypto Fear & Greed Index into “Extreme Fear.”

The sell-off has no single clear cause. Binance co-founder Changpeng Zhao (CZ) pointed to five possible reasons for the crypto market crash.

According to CZ, investors may be pulling money out of crypto to chase the massive Anthropic IPO set for November 18, 2026. Anthropic aims to raise between $57.75 billion and $64.35 billion by offering 825 million shares at $70 to $78 apiece.

However, history suggests mega-IPOs often drain crypto liquidity.

Ahead of the historic SpaceX IPO debut on June 12, 2026, which targeted a $75 billion raise, Bitcoin dropped roughly 19% to 29% in the weeks leading up to the listing, dipping to $58K.CZ himself warned that correlation does not prove causation.

One of the clearest signals CZ pointed to is the wave of institutional ETF outflows. By November 13, U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs had seen $1.9 billion in outflows over seven straight days, including $185.4 million on Friday. BlackRock’s IBIT alone accounted for $1.42 billion, or 74% of the total.

Ethereum ETFs also saw $399 million leave during the week of November 9–13, marking their seventh straight week of outflows.

CZ also pointed to several security incidents that have raised concerns about online safety. These included the $6.3 billion Binance hack on October 29. Although Binance said customer funds were safe, the attack raised concerns about security across the crypto industry.

Security researchers also warned about DarkSword, a hacking tool targeting outdated iPhones and crypto wallets.

Later on 9 November, Google also fixed a Chrome security flaw that hackers were actively using.

A few days later, on 12 November, Outlook and other Microsoft 365 services faced an outage.CZ’s point is not that one of these incidents caused Bitcoin’s fall. Instead, he said,

“These security incidents could make some people less willing to keep funds in crypto.”

CZ also linked the crypto decline to higher U.S. inflation and changing Fed rate expectations. October CPI rose 0.4%, above the 0.3% forecast, while core CPI increased 0.3%. This has raised expectations of another Fed rate hike, possibly by 25 or even 50 basis points.

He also pointed to higher Treasury yields and a stronger dollar, which can make investors less willing to buy risky assets like crypto.

U.S. politics has added to the uncertainty around crypto rules. The Democrats’ victory in the November 3 midterm elections gave them control of both the House and Senate.

The CLARITY Act failed a Senate vote in September. It now has one last chance to pass before the current Congress ends on January 3, 2027.

Flagship Pioneering Launches London Lab to Build AI-Native Companies

📅 Nov 16, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Flagship Pioneering announced on November 16, 2026 that it is establishing Flagship Origination Lab, U.K. in London, backed by an initial £100 million commitment. The lab will conceive, test, fund and build AI-native platform companies, and is Flagship’s first dedicated company-creation unit outside the United States.

Operating within Flagship Labs, the London unit will focus on human and planetary health and other frontiers of science and technology. It brings a company-creation model developed in Cambridge, Massachusetts, into a locally rooted UK ecosystem connected to Flagship’s global organization.

Founder and CEO Noubar Afeyan said Flagship anticipates scaling its commitment through partnerships to attract an additional £500 million. The additional amount depends on future partnerships and remains separate from the capital committed at launch.

Flagship expects to recruit and train approximately 50 scientist-entrepreneurs and operators for the initial UK team. The local company-creation ecosystem will remain connected to the broader organization, including its Pioneering Intelligence and Pioneering Medicines business units.

Flagship’s model starts with scientific questions and develops them into platforms, products and companies. The firm says it has created more than 130 companies over more than 25 years, including Moderna, providing the company-creation record it is now extending to the UK.

Avak Kahvejian, president of Flagship Labs and a general partner at Flagship Pioneering, described origination as a discipline led by teams of scientist-entrepreneurs who “begin with bold questions about what could be possible.” The London team will accordingly be involved in generating and testing ideas as well as financing and building the resulting companies.

That structure gives Flagship a role from the earliest stage of company formation. Rather than operating solely as an investor in existing startups, the lab is designed to originate the underlying scientific platforms and then build businesses around them within its own ecosystem.

The launch follows three years of Flagship partnering and conducting R&D across the UK. It opened a London office and signed a memorandum of understanding with the UK government in 2023 to extend the global reach and impact of its companies.

Flagship describes Quotient Therapeutics, launched during that period, as its first transatlantic company. Its UK activity also includes clinical trials involving Flagship-founded companies such as Generate:Biomedicines, alongside partnerships across academia, data and regulatory innovation.

Kahvejian said the UK’s science base, clinical-trial infrastructure, genomics and health-data assets, agile regulatory environment, and scientists and entrepreneurs made it a deliberate choice for the expansion. Those resources provide the foundation on which Flagship intends to build its local company-creation operation.

Chris McDonald MP, Minister for Science, Innovation and Investment, said the government wants more AI and life-sciences businesses to turn research and technology into jobs and economic growth, with Flagship’s commitment helping new UK companies take breakthroughs to international markets.

The expansion adds local company origination to Flagship’s existing UK research, partnerships and clinical activity. Its larger financial ambition depends on attracting partners, while the lab’s operational test is whether the new team can turn those scientific and clinical assets into new AI-native companies.

HLTH26: Apple launches 200K-person study to examine how consumers use health AI" style="max-width:100%; height:auto; border-radius:8px;" loading="lazy">

LAS VEGAS—Consumers are rapidly adopting consumer health AI. Roughly 1 in 3 U.S. adults have turned to an AI chatbot for health information, according to a KFF survey, and the use of health AI is even more prevalent among younger adults.

But questions remain about whether consumers understand and trust AI-generated health advice, and whether it influences how they manage their health or seek care.

Tech giant Apple launched the Apple Health AI Usage Study today with plans to recruit 200,000 participants in the U.S. to gain a better understanding of how people interact with conversational health AI tools.

The study is open to individuals 21 or older who live in the U.S., and participants must have an Apple Intelligence-enabled iPhone (iPhone 15 Pro or later) running iOS 26 or later. Apple will run the virtual research study on a dedicated Apple Health AI Study App, separate from the Apple Health and Research apps. Participants may remain in the study for up to 12 months, Apple said.

By examining what drives user trust, the quality of responses, comprehension and everyday health actions, the study aims to inform the responsible design of health AI experiences for Apple and the industry, the company said. There are also privacy implications that need to be explored, Apple representatives said. Researchers also want to evaluate how AI chatbots complement the traditional healthcare system and the handoff to physicians.

The tech company is collaborating with OpenEvidence on the research initiative, as the startup provides the underlying clinical AI technology for Apple’s Health AI Usage Study. OpenEvidence is a widely used AI-powered clinical decision support tool and medical search engine for clinicians. The company's platform pulls directly from licensed, peer-reviewed literature and clinical guidelines through formal partnerships with The New England Journal of Medicine (NEJM), JAMA, Nature, Cochrane, and the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN).

Apple said it selected OpenEvidence as a partner for its high-quality medical sources, robust privacy controls, and advanced search models.

Through Apple’s study app, participants can ask everyday health questions and receive answers in approachable language, with links to peer-reviewed clinical sources, Apple said. Users can ask about symptoms, general health questions, recovery from procedures, fitness and other health concerns. They can rate the AI's answers and indicate what was useful or lacking, Apple said.

Study participants can also choose to share HealthKit data types—such as resting heart rate, activity and sleep—to get more personalized responses to health questions. If study participants have imported clinical records into the Health app and separately agree to share them with the Health AI Usage study, the app can incorporate that information, primarily lab results, Apple representatives said.

"At Apple, we believe responsible innovation starts with scientific rigor and clinical grounding. Health is deeply personal, and conducting a dedicated research study is the most thoughtful way to understand how people engage with, comprehend, and evaluate health AI in everyday life. Insights from this study will help advance the broader scientific conversation and ensure that as technology evolves, we continue building trusted tools that genuinely support people wherever they are on their health journey," Sumbul Desai, M.D., Apple’s vice president of Health and Fitness, said in a statement.

Apple described the research initiative as a formal study overseen by an independent review board.

Participants will complete a mandatory onboarding process that includes informed consent, and they'll be asked to share demographic information such as age, race and education so researchers can examine how these variables affect participant preferences and communication needs, Apple said.

The company said study participants will receive baseline and monthly surveys, allowing researchers to examine how people use the information they receive and what happens afterward.

The Health AI Usage study app also includes tools to help people prepare for healthcare appointments, along with surveys after visits to gauge whether the use of health AI chatbots makes patients feel better prepared or empowered and improves their interactions with providers, Apple said.

The study is designed from the ground up with privacy protections, transparency and clinical guardrails, the company said. Study data will not be used to train AI models, Apple confirmed.

Apple said it has implemented security measures to protect the study data it maintains, as well as the confidentiality of participants’ information. User queries on the Health AI Usage Study app are first processed on the iPhone to remove identifying information. The queries then flow to a secure study environment managed by a third-party study support contractor where they are de-linked from individual participant IDs and screened again before being sent to OpenEvidence where answers are generated using supporting reference materials, Apple said.

The study app also features safety guardrails that are intended to detect certain concerning questions and suggest that a participant follow up with a provider or other resources if needed.

Consumers are increasingly using general AI chatbots powered by LLMs to get answers to health questions. But research indicates a wide gap exists between an AI's technical accuracy and a patient's ability to safely use that information.

"Patients and clinicians are both turning to AI, but the tools they use are being built and tested in separate worlds. When patients and doctors draw on versions of the same trustworthy tools, patients arrive prepared, conversations improve, and next steps happen seamlessly. Connecting these tools, and rigorously studying what works, is how we’ll get there," Robert M. Wachter, M.D., UCSF’s professor and chair of the Department of Medicine, said in a statement.

The Health AI Usage study builds on Apple's growing research initiatives. In 2019, the company launched its Research app and three medical studies to test how well its Apple Watch can track a person's mobility and cardiovascular health, changes in hearing and women's health and fertility. Last year, it began the Apple Health Study to use its devices, including iPhones, Apple Watch and AirPods along with third-party devices, for a longitudinal, virtual study to monitor changes in participants’ health, spanning a wide range of health and disease areas.

Great topics. I'd probably argue against a few and maybe for many more.

On checkbook - my kids do not have checkbooks and see no reason to ever need them. There is virtually nothing anymore that requires a physical check. All money to them is digital. I do think talking about different forms of payment, security, costs/fees, and how to track and audit them in a digital world would be good.

Budgeting is a big discussion, but I'm not sure where you'd put that line. Instead, I think they should be teaching ROI on various career choices, including college. Remember this is HS level. On average, a 2 year technical degree or apprenticeship program will outperform a 4 year liberal arts degree (remember, I said on average), and kids should know that and know how to calculate the ROI and breakeven scenarios.
