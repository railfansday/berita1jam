Lionel Charbonnier, campione del mondo 1998: "Maignan? Ricorda Barthez: è fortissimo. Ecco cosa gli chiede Fabien in nazionale”

📅 Nov 11, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Parliamo di Mike Maignan con un grande esperto e campione del mondo del 1998: Lionel Charbonnier. Sposato con un’italiana, Charbonnier fu il terzo portiere della Francia nel ’98 e vinse due Coppe di Francia (1994 e 1996) e lo scudetto con il grande AJ Auxerre di Guy Roux, Laurent Blanc, Guivarc’h, Laslandes, Lamouchi e Diomède nel 1996. Un personaggio positivo, schietto e grande conoscitore del calcio, in particolare del ruolo del portiere. Lionel corse ad abbracciare un Fabien Barthez incredulo a terra e in lacrime dopo il fischio finale di Francia-Brasile, la finale della Coppa del Mondo del 1998. Ecco a voi l’analisi di Lionel Charbonnier, consulente per RMC e L’After Foot. In esclusiva per MilanNews, di Alessandro Schiavone.

«È un buon portiere e gli voglio molto bene. In passato ha avuto un piccolo passaggio a vuoto, lo so. All’inizio vi aveva tutti sbalorditi quando è arrivato al Milan. Del resto, tutti si chiedevano perché. E mio suocero, che è italiano, mi diceva: “Ma siete pazzi, voi francesi, ad aver preso Gigio al posto di Maignan! E com’è possibile che il PSG non abbia preso Maignan?”. Effettivamente, all’inizio da voi era eccellente. Poi ha avuto un piccolo passaggio a vuoto. Il suo gioco è cambiato e penso che oggi sia tornato quello che era! Penso che ci sia stato un fenomeno di adattamento e poi un gioco diverso. Ma con la nazionale francese l’ho ritrovato con Fabien Barthez, che gli ha chiesto di partecipare maggiormente al gioco, di giocare di più con i piedi, di stare un po’ più alto e di essere più naturale. Mentre al Milan l’ho trovato un po’ più chiuso nei suoi sei metri, e questo lo ha penalizzato. Doveva acquisire nuovi punti di riferimento e penso che frequentare Fabien gli farà davvero, davvero molto bene per il finale della sua carriera».

Agilità e flessibilità: ha ereditato qualcosa da Barthez?

«È un po’ la stessa predisposizione, è un po’ questa destrezza. Innanzitutto, è molto agile, è un portiere un po’ volante. Inoltre, non è maldestro con i piedi. Non è certo Zizou, ma insomma, sa parare i palloni, ed è quello che si chiede a un portiere. Ma acquisendo fiducia in questo modo con i piedi nel calcio moderno, evolverà molto. Bisogna però dargli molta più libertà rispetto alla sua porta, perché… Se è troppo sotto pressione, troppo vicino alla sua porta, può inciampare. Ma…»

«Per il resto, sì, ha questa qualità sulla linea, ha questa grandissima apertura alare. Non lo conosco, ne ho appena parlato con Fabien, ma penso che debba comandare un po’ di più la sua difesa».

C’è un modo diverso di giocare tra Francia e Italia?

«Spesso c’è una differenza culturale e un gioco diverso… La risposta deve trovarla il portiere il più rapidamente possibile! È lui che deve adattarsi nello spogliatoio, nel gioco e a tutto il resto! Mi ricordo che quando ero all’Auxerre, Guy Roux vietava i retropassaggi e soprattutto mi vietava di uscire dai sei metri sui palloni alti. Quindi, a volte, questo alleggerisce la pressione sulla difesa, ma persino quando prendevo il pallone, lui mi sgridava! Ma quando sono arrivato in nazionale francese mi dicevano: “No, no, lì devi andare dentro l’area dei sedici metri!”. Avevo una partita per adattarmi e ho dovuto farlo. Quindi ho avuto quella partita per riuscirci con la nazionale francese. Successivamente sono andato ai Glasgow Rangers e lì mi hanno detto che dovevo giocare da libero. Vale a dire che il giocatore che era il numero 10 si girava verso di me e mi passava la palla. Ma da noi questa cosa non esisteva: il numero 10 è quello che serve gli attaccanti. Ho dovuto adattarmi subito. Ho avuto una o due partite, ma dopo non si ha più il diritto di avere passaggi a vuoto. Con Mike non so cosa sia successo nella sua testa, ma è vero che, secondo me, ci ha messo un po’ di tempo… E credo che al Milan sia stato persino un po’ messo in discussione».

Cosa ne pensa di Donnarumma? A proposito di Francia-Italia…

«Dollarumma, è così?» (Ride, ndr). Gigio è cresciuto moltissimo, moltissimo. Gigio, rispetto a Mike… Mike era capace di essere determinante già alla sua prima parata. Gigio, all’inizio, non lo era quando è arrivato al PSG. L’ha pagato, perché aveva bisogno di fare due o tre parate, o addirittura di subire uno o due gol, per essere performante! Capitava spesso, agli inizi della prima esperienza di Gigio al PSG dopo il suo arrivo a Parigi, che il PSG non avesse il pallone. Di conseguenza, Gigio veniva bombardato di tiri e, dopo il secondo gol subito, diventava molto bravo e ci si diceva: “Tornerà quello di prima!”. Ma no: quando sei al PSG, devi essere molto forte già alla prima parata. Ha avuto difficoltà a superare questo ostacolo, ma penso che fosse seguito male. Vale a dire che, quando si hanno portieri del genere, l’allenatore dei portieri è molto importante. E Gigio è cresciuto con il PSG. A un certo punto se l’è messo in testa, ha capito che la sua prima parata era determinante, perché gli avversari, subito dopo, avrebbero segnato. Quindi, invece di perdere 1-0, il PSG vinceva 1-0. E se il PSG vince 1-0, cambia tutto. Ed è lì che Gigio ha fatto il salto di qualità. Per Maignan potrebbe essere la stessa cosa, ma al contrario. Vale a dire che, quando è arrivato, era determinante alla prima parata. Ma poi è tornato a essere quello che Gigio era al PSG. Subiva uno o due gol e poi, ogni tanto, faceva una parata. Penso onestamente che l’ambiente che circonda un portiere sia molto, molto importante! Bisogna avere un allenatore dei portieri che sia un consigliere, perché a questi giocatori non si insegna più soltanto a giocare, ma anche come affrontare psicologicamente la prima parata, come prepararsi all’inizio della partita e tutto il resto… “Attenzione, ti succederà questo, questo e questo”. Soltanto coloro che hanno conosciuto il livello più alto, quello davvero altissimo, possono aiutarti. Possiamo anche essere ottimi allenatori, ma se l’allenatore dei portieri non ha giocato, il suo discorso, anche se ha ragione, non arriva allo stesso modo. Se invece si tratta di un campione del mondo o di uno che ha avuto una grande carriera da portiere, allora veniamo subito ascoltati. Il giocatore impara molto di più e acquisisce fiducia. Può dirgli: “Io l’ho vissuto in un determinato momento, ho fatto questo, ho fatto quello… e tutto è andato bene”. E a quel punto il discorso arriva, anche se si usano le stesse parole, ma pronunciate da una persona diversa».

Betsy James Wyeth’s Placemaking Prowess

📅 Nov 11, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

A wide-ranging book and exhibition reveal the ways the multi-hyphenate’s architectural-environmental projects shaped her husband Andrew Wyeth’s celebrated painting career.

The woman portrayed in Andrew Wyeth’s “Christina’s World” (1948) has become one of the most iconic figures in American art. Less familiar, perhaps, is the woman who posed for this painting: his wife, Betsy James Wyeth. Dubbed the “chief architect of the Wyeth mystique,” Betsy shaped the Wyeth brand, Andrew’s life, and art for over seven decades in undeniable ways. Though she rarely occupied the spotlight herself, her influence was everywhere: she handled his business affairs, controlled his public image, wrote books about his art, and maintained his catalogue raisonné. She also served as a creative advisor, critiquing his paintings and giving them their titles.

By Design: The Worlds of Betsy James Wyeth — a hefty and compelling new book (Rizzoli Electa) and a sprawling trio of linked exhibitions shown simultaneously at the Farnsworth Art Museum in Rockland, Maine; Brandywine Museum of Art in Chadds Ford, Pennsylvania; and Colby College Museum of Art in Waterville, Maine — pay tribute to the pair’s enduring partnership and work to credit Betsy as a key figure in her own right.

It's best to go into game demos with a clear mind, but that was never in the cards for Exterminauts, the new co-op game from Dead by Daylight studio Behavior Interactive. Everything about Exterminauts, externally, screams Helldivers 2, from its working class soldiers and anonymized outfits to its hostile wildlife and colorfully pulpy planets.

Exterminauts' developers, who are a distinct team from the one making Dead By Daylight, see the comparisons coming and don't shy away from them, but they also point out that their game was in development for years before Arrowhead's hit shooter came out in 2024.

They also draw a distinction in tone: Exterminauts does not cast you as a dedicated servant of intergalactic democracy, but as militarized, blue-collar pest control cleansing the planet of Tian so that corporate interests can better sap the land of its resources. In that way and more, Exterminauts has more in common with Deep Rock Galactic or Mycopunk.

That's a realization that helped me acclimate to Exterminauts after a few hours with an alpha build—it's got Deep Rock-like milestones, class roles, and mission types. Loadouts begin with a choice of a soft class (called sponsors) that grants passive bonuses and a unique power weapon. There's one that comes with a medic tool and a damage-focused lightning gun that I gravitated toward. You can also bring along gadgets like ammo boxes, med packs, and automated turrets that could singlehandedly flatten a horde (Behaviour admitted these were overpowered at the moment).

It's relevant to note that my developer-led demo stayed on the lower difficulties, but even with that in mind, Exterminauts struck me as more casual than games like it. Guns are very easy to control with a mouse, friendly fire is either barely there or nonexistent, ammo was readily available through pickups at every point of interest, and there's a generous respawn mechanic that lets you summon teammates back into the action.

The maps I played were straightforward canyons littered with corporate mining facilities. Objectives were clearly marked on the map from the start, as are side tasks. There's clear Helldivers inspiration in the jobs themselves—there's a lot of solving micro puzzles on computer screens to get a facility back online—but I never really felt rushed to complete them because the fauna barely put up a fight.

I'd like to see what Exterminauts is like when it's challenging, because its creative creatures have a lot more bark than they do bite. No bugs or robot hordes here—Behaviour is leaning into freaky plant monsters and flying squids. I especially enjoyed the giant carnivorous flytraps that crawl on six feet and the flying, spiky tulips that slow down enemies until they're dealt with.

Cranking up the difficulty might also bring the depth of its classes into focus—each sponsor (class) can be modified through firmware unlocked through play. These looked like small bonuses, like extra freeze damage or HP gained on kill, that can be stacked to make a bigger impact.

Behaviour is planning large and frequent updates for Exterminauts, including 12-week seasons and a constant drip feed of sponsorship packages that introduce new weapons, gadgets, cosmetics, and firmware modifiers. The idea is to make these relatively easy to unlock through normal play, but you'll be able to buy them early for real money, too.

A general lack of energy led to my demo feeling rudimentary and sometimes boring—I was craving a sense of risk or struggle that gives co-op shooters moment-to-moment stakes, but never got it. These strike me as problems that can be solved, and Exterminauts does have good bones, so I'll be interested to try it again when it's more complete.

Antonio Di Salvo's Germany U21 side entertain Belgium in a friendly game on Friday as both sides already booked their places for next summer's European championship. After their last home game of 2026, Germany will face Croatia in Rijeka four days later in another friendly.

The three-time Euros winners finished ahead of Greece in their qualifying group after eight successive wins. Belgium only needed a point against Denmark in the last matchday to finish top of their group, and they achieved that with a goalless draw in Vejle.

Germany's last success in the U21 Euros came in 2021, but they lost both home and away to Belgium during the qualifying campaign for the tournament hosted by Hungary and Slovenia. Loïs Openda, who later played at RB Leipzig, scored three goals in those two games.

For the captain Nicolo Tresoldi, the final two games of the year could be his last at this level. The striker who scored 16 goals in 27 appearances is yet to make his decision on representing either Germany or his birth country Italy at senior level. The sought-after Club Brugge youngster only trails Heiko Herrlich (17) and Pierre Littbarski (18) in the all-time scoring list for Germany U21.

The former won Bundesliga and Champions League with Borussia Dortmund, while the wing wizard Littbarski played a key role when West Germany won the World Cup in 1990. Tresoldi already showed his potential as he scored 18 goals for club and country since the start of the season, including five in four Champions League games.

Di Salvo has to find solutions for several injury absentees as HSV's Kofi Amoako and Maurice Krattenmacher, who could return to FC Bayern after his excellent start to the season with Elversberg, are set to make their U21 debuts. Former Kaiserslautern striker Normann Bassette, who is the joint-Belgian Pro League top-scorer alongside Tresoldi, scored five times for Belgium during the Euros qualification campaign.

Hoffenheim's highly-rated attacker Nathan De Cat is unavailable due to injury, but Samuel Mbangula will be back to Germany after his unsuccessful season at Werder Bremen. Former BVB winger Julien Duranville also returns to Belgium U21 squad after making his first few appearances for Lyon.

Reviews have dropped for How to Rob A Bank, starring Nicolaus Holt, Zoë Kravitz, Pete Davidson, Anna Sawai, and John C. Reilly, and they aren’t pretty. The movie, directed by David Leitch (Deadpool 2, The Fall Guy), hits the 2026 movie schedule this week. The reviews are not as uniformly negative as they were for another Amazon MGM movie, 2025’s War of the Worlds (How to Rob a Bank is hovering just south of 50% on Rotten Tomatoes), but they are giving me the same vibes. That’s not a good thing.

Even in some of the more positive reviews, writers aren’t impressed by the heist movie’s seemingly ham-fisted attempt to bring the classic heist movie genre into the 2020s in its presentation (and political message), just like War of the Worlds attempted (poorly) to update that classic sci-fi story with Amazon drones and Ice Cube responding to text messages for an entire movie. Caroline Siede, writing for Decoding Everything, says:

How to Rob a Bank wants to be a rallying cry for social revolution via internet community building, but it winds up feeling more like a mindless TikTok scroll.

Even the more positive reviews, like Cortlyn Kelly's 2.5-star review for Roger Ebert, call attention to this:

Breaking the silver-screen barrier, the ringleader, Ryan (Hoult), addresses his audience by stating how important they are; almost like a double entendre, the movie speaks to both its fictional audience and those in the theater. This focus on the audience is intriguing, adding depth to an otherwise standard money-heist flick. Pandering to a younger generation by contentifying the plot makes it easy for ‘How to Rob a Bank’ to one day be screened as a 212-part TikTok series.

The same can be said for the positive-ish review from The Hollywood Reporter, which is pretty harsh on the social media aspects of the film. Writer David Rooney says:

While the robberies are diverting enough and the YouTube inserts splashed with pop-up graphics, the fun wears a bit thin, which makes the shift into trauma and pathos a welcome one, even if the transition might have been smoother.

The Globe and Mail’s review of How to Rob a Bank also questions the presentation of the movie in social media form, with film critic Radheyan Simonpillai writing:

This is not a heist movie. It’s the online hack to a heist movie, curiously made for the big screen, seeking the path of least resistance to constant dopamine hits. We’re foregoing all the labour of a heist movie because it would likely be oh so tedious for the movie’s target audience, presumably the generation under 30, whose work ethic is constantly called into question.

The fact that How to Rob A Bank also tried to make an anti-establishment, anti-banking movie while being financed by Amazon isn’t helping its case, per some reviews.

For example, in his 2-star review for The Guardian, Benjamin Lee wrote that not only does the movie feel like it was put together by someone trying to nail the “right now” of social media and missing badly, but the political message is also lost:

It’s a little hard to stomach an anti-capitalism screed not only funded by Amazon but featuring several scenes of lame, please-aim-logo-towards-camera, product placement (after one dismantles the system, a refreshing can of Mountain Dew really does hit the spot).

Writing for The Daily Beast, Nick Schager puts the film’s attempt at a political statement even more succinctly:

Their film attempts to rage against the machine from within the (Amazon/MGM) machine.

In fact, Amazon and its CEO, Jeff Bezos, find themselves at the heart of a lot of the negative reviews. William Bibbiani at The Wrap writes:

It’s an Amazon-MGM production, which makes the film’s efforts to sell anti-capitalism as entertainment hypocritical, laughable and insulting. It’s a film that says you should stand up to Jeff Bezos, but only after you pay Jeff Bezos. No matter how cool “Atomic Blonde” director David Leitch tries to make bank robbing look, this is the opposite of cool.

I would like to note that not all of the reviews are scathing, but none are overwhelmingly positive, either. The movie seems like it delivers on action for many reviews (though some didn’t like it), but the great action sequences don’t seem to save it from itself, even if some reviews found How to Rob a Bank fun enough.

The movie also doesn’t look like it’ll be all that big of a hit at the box office, meaning it’ll likely be available to watch with a Prime subscription pretty quickly. We’ll have to see on that.

Deutsche Bank Goes Live With Crypto Custody After a Five-Year Buildup

📅 Nov 11, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Deutsche Bank Goes Live With Crypto Custody After a Five-Year Buildup

Five years after it first floated the idea, Germany’s largest lender is now holding Bitcoin for clients.

Deutsche Bank said Wednesday that its Digital Asset Custody service has gone live for paying clients, giving institutional and corporate customers in Europe a regulated way to store and transfer Bitcoin, Ethereum and three stablecoins: Circle’s USDC and EURC, plus EURAU.

The bank manages wallets and private keys on clients’ behalf. For now, the service covers custody and transfers, with tokenized financial instruments on its roadmap.

The service is aimed at clients of Deutsche Bank’s corporate and investment banking units, including corporations, asset managers, hedge funds, custodians, brokers and sovereign institutions, according to the bank’s September announcement of the plan.

Its setup includes hardware-based key protection, multi-person approvals and separate warm and cold storage, the bank said.

“In the medium term we aim to deliver a fully integrated, regulated ecosystem that supports the custody, tokenisation, and settlement of digital assets,” said Paul Maley, Deutsche Bank’s global head of trust and securities services.

Deutsche Bank also signed a memorandum of understanding with Deutsche Asset Management, the majority-owned money manager formerly known as DWS, to explore working together on tokenized money market funds, crypto exchange-traded commodities and stablecoins.

The asset manager oversaw about 1.22 trillion euros as of Sept. 30. The bank has also said it plans to expand the custody service to Asia.

The launch caps a long road, going back to at least 2021 when Deutsche Bank began readying a crypto custody and trading platform. Then, in 2023, the bank applied for a digital asset custody license in Germany. Last year, reports said the bank planned to build the service with technology from crypto exchange Bitpanda, alongside its longtime partner Taurus.

Deutsche Bank received authorization from German regulator BaFin under the EU’s Markets in Crypto-Assets regulation, or MiCA, which requires crypto service providers to be licensed. The bank cautioned that its custody service doesn’t protect clients against swings in the value of digital assets, fraud or failures by other market participants.

The custody launch isn’t Deutsche Bank’s only crypto bet. The bank is among a group of global lenders, including Goldman Sachs and Citi, backing a $270 million challenge to crypto’s dominant stablecoin issuers.

‘Adults’ Renewed for Season 3 at FX

📅 Nov 11, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

The third season of the ensemble comedy will consist of 16 episodes

“Adults” has been renewed for Season 3 at FX and Hulu, Variety has learned.

The third season of the ensemble comedy will consist of 16 episodes, which is double the eight episodes the show aired for both its first and second seasons. In addition, creators Ben Kronengold and Rebecca Shaw have also set a new overall deal with FX Productions.

“We started working with FX over six years ago, back when Paul Baker was but a twinkle in all of our eyes,” said Kronengold and Shaw. “In the years since, John [Landgraf] and the rest of the team have taught us everything we know about how to lead, produce, and tell stories that make people feel like part of a community. They’ve become family, and we’re so glad to make it official — and to start yelling at them like we do our parents.” Related Stories 'The Notebook' Musical Sets West End Run at Newly Renamed Judi Dench Theatre Connor Storrie in Talks to Play Villain Opposite Ryan Gosling in Marvel's 'Ghost Rider'

“Adults” stars Malik Elassal, Lucy Freyer, Jack Innanen, Amita Rao, and Owen Thiele. Per the official logline, the show is “about a group of twenty-somethings in New York leaning on each other to navigate love, work, friendship and family. Samir (Elassal), Billie (Freyer), Paul Baker (Innanen), Issa (Rao) and Anton (Thiele) are five friends crashing together in Samir’s childhood home.”

Kronengold and Shaw are executive producers and showrunners on the series in addition to creating it. Nick Kroll, Sarah Naftalis, Jonathan Krisel, Alicia Van Couvering, and Rob Rosell also executive produce. FX Productions is the studio.

“Ben and Rebecca have elevated ‘Adults’ in every way, earning critical praise and a legion of devoted fans who have embraced the friend group as their own,” said Nick Grad, president of FX Entertainment. “’Adults’ showed significant growth in its sophomore year and we’ve seen the show inspire everything from memes to Halloween costumes, so the decision was easy – more episodes and a new deal at FX for Ben and Rebecca!”

Per FX, Season 2 was up 60% in viewership over Season 1 on Hulu and Disney+ globally through its first five weeks, while international viewership was up 100%. FX did not provide specific viewership numbers, however.

“Adults” has been well-received by critics and audiences since its original launch in 2025, with FX also releasing the standalone special “Marathon Day” in July 2026 ahead of the debut of Season 2. The show currently has an 88% critical approval rating across both seasons on Rotten Tomatoes. In her review of Season 2 for Variety, Alison Herman wrote, “‘Adults’ finally has its own momentum, largely generated by a cast whose rapport obviates the need for any sweeping statements about their generation.”

Kings of Leon’s Caleb Followill Is Finally Ready to Bare It All

📅 Nov 11, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The lead singer tells Esquire in the latest episode of “What I’ve Learned” that the band’s new record features their most honest music to date.

Last year, Kings of Leon was forced to cancel part of its summer tour. The band’s lead singer, Caleb Followill, broke his foot and severely displaced his heel during a freak accident that he jokes was “a Rock ‘n’ Roll injury.” But really, he admits to Esquire, he was just playing tag with his kids—a little roughhousing that turned into eight months of recovery before he could walk back onto the stage.

“I was kind of feeling sorry for myself,” Followill tells Esquire editor in chief Michael Sebastian on the latest episode of “What I’ve Learned.” The singer felt like he had let the band’s fans down. He turned 44, he lost his mother not too long ago, and he channeled all that grief and negative energy into a new, stripped-down Kings of Leon record, O My Beloved, which was released on November 6. “I had a weight on my shoulders,” he says. “And one day I picked up the guitar, and it was almost like I walked through a door and I could see things a little more clearly.”

O My Beloved is the most melancholic and reflective album in Kings of Leon’s ten-album discography. Followill tells Esquire that he wanted to speak about his childhood and the family that shaped him on the new songs because he used to feel embarrassed about his past. His family grew up poor—traveling around the South and living out of their car at times. (Kings of Leon is a family band, with Caleb joined by his brothers Nathan and Jared Followill, as well as their cousin Matthew Followill.) But now, Caleb wants to pass all his memories on to his kids, and he doesn’t want them to feel ashamed about any part of the journey that led them here.

“When I was writing these songs, my mind just kept going back to small moments in my life—moments that had no significance,” he says. “I wanted to tell these stories, and I wanted to be honest about parts of my life that I didn’t want to be honest about before."

O My Beloved is the band’s first self-produced record since parting ways with RCA, their longtime record label. It’s also their tenth studio album, celebrating nearly twenty years since their 2008 record, Only by the Night, rocketed the band to stardom thanks to hit songs "Sex on Fire" and "Use Somebody."

“I was always scared of fame, and I think I still am,” Followill says. “That’s the good thing about having your brothers next to you. They’ll rip you right back down, shine a light in your eyes and say, ‘Hey man, this can all come crumbling down and we’ll be back in our crumbling house.’”

You can listen to the episode YouTube and Spotify. Elsewhere, Followill shares his secret to fifteen years of marriage (to model Lily Aldridge), and what he’s learned from touring with Pearl Jam, U2, and Zach Bryan.

Rose Byrne Goes Rosy Pink in Straight-Off-the-Runway Dior

📅 Nov 11, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Rose Byrne is pretty in pink. Last night, the cast of crime-thriller series The Good Daughter gathered in Times Square for the New York City premiere. And for the occasion, the leading actor showed up to the buzzing area’s Times Center event space in a straight-off-the-runway gown from Jonathan Anderson’s Spring/Summer 2027 presentation for Dior.

On top, the baby-pink look was crafted from lightweight silk chiffon, which had been finely pleated into a textured peplum bodice with a sleeveless silhouette. The neckline and arm holes were trimmed with little ruffles, but at the hem, Anderson left things raw—giving a rough edge to an otherwise soft and delicate number.

From the flared tunic, airy pale-blush chiffon floated down to the ankle, creating a two-tone mid-length skirt that was finished with a second raw hem. On the runway, the piece was paired with cherry-red heels with white piping, but at the premiere, Byrne went with a simple pointed-toe white alternative.

When it came to jewelry, the Bridesmaids actor left her wrists and ears bare, selecting only a statement necklace with crystal-embellished leaf motifs. Her shoulder-length hair was blown out with a deep side part, and for makeup, she went rosy—with pink blush and warm coral-red lips.

Next to Byrne, costar Meghann Fahy went for an opposite look, wearing a silky black midi dress with a high neckline, three-quarter sleeves, and a slim fit. She added over-the-knee velvet boots and slicked her hair back into an updo while keeping her makeup simple.
