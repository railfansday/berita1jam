Australia plans AI safety regulator after OpenAI agent breach

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Anthony Albanese accepted all 18 recommendations of a review into how an OpenAI agent accessed a Medicare data portal.

Australia will set up a regulator for AI safety and force AI companies to report hacks and other serious incidents to the government, Prime Minister Anthony Albanese said on Tuesday. The move comes after an OpenAI agent got into a government website holding Medicare data earlier this year.

Speaking at a press conference in Canberra, Albanese said the rules would form part of Australia’s upcoming AI law. The new regulator will also be able to impose large fines.

Under the plan, AI developers will have to build security into every stage of making their models and manage serious risks. They will also have to alert the government quickly when something goes wrong, let outside experts test their systems and publish their safety plans.

The government also released the 18 recommendations of a rapid review into the OpenAI incident and said it had accepted all of them.

“This incident was completely unacceptable as I outlined to OpenAI’s CEO at the time, and it can’t be allowed to happen again,” Albanese said.

The review says AI firms should report when their systems are hacked or act in ways they were not built to. It also calls for tougher criminal laws and closer work with other countries and with the AI companies themselves.

Deputy Prime Minister Richard Marles, who appeared alongside Albanese, said the OpenAI case was the first incident of its kind anywhere in the world to be reported to a government. The main lesson, he said, was that Australia had to work closely with the companies building the most advanced AI.

“This is not a technology that we can run away from,” Marles said.

He added that new models should be tested independently, which in Australia would be done by the AI Safety Institute. The review also wants the institute to work more closely with the country’s cyber agencies. Still, Marles said the review had found the country’s cyber defences to be strong.

Home Affairs and Cyber Security Minister Tony Burke, meanwhile, warned that the real danger lies in how fast AI can find weak spots in software.

“Artificial intelligence doesn’t of itself create vulnerabilities, but it finds them, and it changes the pathway to exploiting them,” he said.

According to Burke, AI has cut the time needed to exploit a security flaw from about two and a half years to about 10 hours. That is why companies must tell the government straight away when an AI system ‘goes rogue’, he said. He also wants criminal law updated to cover AI systems that act on their own.

“It should never be the case that there’s an argument that someone didn’t know the computer was loaded,” Burke said.

In addition, he said AI companies should be able to share technical details of an incident with the government without legal risk, as cybersecurity law already allows. When asked about Chinese AI models, he said that was a matter for the security agencies, and pointed to Australia’s early move to restrict DeepSeek on government devices.

The breach itself happened in June, when an OpenAI agent accessed a statistics portal. OpenAI discovered it in August and first emailed a general government inbox on September 10.

No personal details were taken, according to Albanese, and the agent was looking for data on how much medicine was being used, and Marles said those figures were totals that have since been published.

Asked whether OpenAI had broken the law, Albanese did not answer directly. Instead, he said the law had to keep up with a fast-changing world. The full review will be published, with national security details removed.

It is not the first time OpenAI has faced such questions. Earlier this year, the company confirmed that one of its AI systems had broken out of a test environment and got into systems at Hugging Face.

Albanese was also asked about Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang’s admitted affair with a woman accused of spying for China. He called the claims very serious and said they should be properly investigated, adding that Australia was not directly involved.

Jensen Huang and the end of the unchecked CEO

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Good morning from Washington. I’m here for the B20 USA Summit, hosted by the U.S. Chamber of Commerce, which is also partnering with us for the Fortune 500 Innovation Forum in Detroit next week. I’ll bring you insights from conversations with leaders here tomorrow. Today, let’s look at the fallout of Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang’s affair with an alleged Chinese spy. I suspect we’ll look back at this moment as the capstone on an era of staggeringly poor judgment and self-interest at the highest levels of leadership.

The cautionary tale starts with the CEO, a former dishwasher at Denny’s who created one of the most consequential and valuable technologies in history, only to tarnish that legacy with a colossal failure of judgment that put his company, career, and possibly the security of both his countries at risk. (Huang is a dual citizen of Taiwan and the U.S.) It also includes board members who expressed “confidence in Jensen” when announcing their independent review. Add in the political leaders who’ve reportedly known about this potentially catastrophic conflict of interest for at least six months.

In any other period, at almost any other company, Huang would likely have been fired or put on leave for a lapse of this scale. Investors are no doubt furious as the stock dropped 4.3% yesterday and has lost more than a fifth of its value over the past month. The board is trying to find out the extent of the deceit. And tech CEOs may find Washington now demanding the kind of oversight that defense contractors take for granted. The lessons?

Beware the cult of personality. There’s much to admire about Jensen Huang, from his humble beginnings to his energetic keynotes, cool leather jacket and patina of common sense in a world where his peers can come off as a little odd. But focusing on a CEO’s personality can distract us from their actions. (Exhibit A: Elizabeth Holmes.) Huang’s so-called founder mode, with 60 direct reports and no 1:1 meetings, also creates a structure that makes him hard to replace. How do you develop a leadership bench when everyone reports to you? Nvidia is built around one man.

Beware the enamored board. Nvidia’s board appears to have failed in its primary task of keeping a strong CEO in check while having a succession plan in place. I was chatting with Yale’s Jeffrey Sonnenfeld yesterday, who described it as a “board imbued with idolatry” that appears to have no Plan B should Huang be judged unfit to lead. That’s not how this process is supposed to work. Done well, you get a board director like former Johnson & Johnson CEO Jim Burke, who, as an independent director at IBM, was key in replacing failing CEO John Akers with Lou Gerstner in 1993. Done badly, you get the debacle at HP, which in 2010 fired CEO Mark Hurd over a close personal relationship with a contractor—and falsified expense reports related to it—only to reach for outsider Leo Apotheker, who lasted less than a year.

Beware unfettered AI leaders. Huang sits at the center of an ecosystem that shapes national competitiveness, security, and potentially the future of humanity itself. This scandal could reinforce the argument that AI companies should be regulated along the same lines as defense contractors like Lockheed Martin, RTX, and General Dynamics, with standardized security clearances and protocols. Did Huang ever wonder how a former TV journalist in Beijing suddenly became managing director of an obscure little cloud company that also suddenly had the means to buy millions of dollars of sophisticated Nvidia chips? Imagine the panic at learning the U.S. government knows you have a mistress and suspects she’s a spy for China’s Ministry of State Security. Did any government leaders feel an urgency to take action?

73 Years Later, John Wayne's Greatest Final Quote From 'Hondo' Still Officially Defines the Western Genre

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

What defines a genre? Is it a set of pre-established or agreed upon conventions? Is it a specific aesthetic or sort of character followed? When it comes to the Western, the genre is recognizable by those who know it best—and sometimes, that includes the characters within it. If there’s a single concluding line spoken by John Wayne in his big-screen horse operas that most perfectly defines the genre, it’s undoubtedly Hondo Lane's final remarks about the Apache in Hondo. In just a few short words, the hero makes it clear what this genre is truly all about.

As the titular hero watches the Apache take off in retreat, hoping to regroup before their next conflict with the U.S. Cavalry, Buffalo Baker (underrated Hollywood talent Ward Bond) notes that this will be the end of the tribe. “Yeah. End of a way of life. Too bad, it's a good way,” Hondo replies before urging the wagons forward. It’s a small moment of pause and reflection that, on the surface, appears to be nothing more than Hondo acknowledging the fading dominion of the Apache. However, upon further examination, we can see that it’s a line that speaks rivers of truth into the genre itself. The Western is largely about a period in American history that bridges the gap between the untamed West and the civilization that followed. Countless Western protagonists, Hondo included, are caught between the gun-toting life they’ve always known and the coming progress that threatens to strip down that identity for the sake of a more "tamed" society.

Many a Western teases the “end of the Wild West.” It's a common theme in the genre that reaches from some of the Duke's earliest pictures to his last. In this regard, perhaps Hondo is no different. The Western hero, unless domesticated, has no place in this new, "civilized" world. This line uttered by the Duke reflects the constant struggle that genre heroes—including folks like Alan Ladd’s Shane, Paul Newman and Robert Redford’s Butch and Sundance, or even Clint Eastwood’s “Man With No Name”—endure with each new interpretation. Hondo may be one of the best examples of the basic horse opera plot—one based on a short story by famed Western author Louis L'Amour—but it concludes with a sober-minded reflection that speaks to the Western genre as a whole, no matter what form it takes. (Consider reading the novelization, by the way, which L'Amour penned himself and can easily be finished in a weekend!)

The Western "way" of living was always best embodied on the big screen by John Wayne. Hondo is just one of many examples of Duke-led horse operas that could easily define the genre. The Man Who Shot Liberty Valance, Stagecoach, The Cowboys, Rio Bravo, True Grit, and, of course, The Searchers are the most obvious films that come to mind. But none of them conclude with these simple words that remind the viewer just what the genre is about — and just like the Wild West era of history came to an end, so too does the Western film continue to remind us that this brief window into American history is a limited period, and the Western heroes are no more. Perhaps this side of Wayne's decades-long career, we can best understand that idea. After all, the Western genre is not at the same height that it was when it dominated film, television, and radio in postwar America.

Perhaps there's something to the fact that the genre's golden age concluded in the mid-1970s, around the same time John Wayne himself rounded out his career. In 1975, Gunsmoke, the long-running television series that Wayne helped launch in 1955, came to an abrupt end the same way the Wild West era did itself. A year later, the Duke's final on-screen role as J.B. Books in The Shootist premiered in theaters. This was a far cry from Hondo, but there's no doubt that, by looking at the past, the 1953 picture was able to predict the Western's future. Of course, the Western itself has never completely died. Its popularity may have waned over the years, but the horse opera continues to captivate viewers today for the same reason it did back when Hondo hit theaters — folks are looking back at a time long past, hoping it will reveal something more about our future.

ChatGPT Ads is replacing two manual bid caps with average cost targets. Here's what changes for new campaigns, existing campaigns and API partners, and when.

The ChatGPT Ads platform is replacing two manual caps, the maximum bid (Max Bid) and maximum CPC (Max CPC), with average cost targets that advertisers can set as part of their advertising campaign strategy. This is according to an OpenAI help document on the topic of bidding and pricing that was updated on their website Thursday.

Beginning Nov. 9, advertisers creating a conversion campaign, which they are using to hit a specific cost per conversion, get Target CPA in place of Max Bid; likewise, advertisers creating a click campaign beginning Nov. 9 also have the option to use Target CPC. Max Bid and Max CPC will no longer apply to existing campaigns from Jan. 1, 2027.

From Nov. 18, a Max Bid or Max CPC value that an API partner sends through the old fields to create a campaign or change an ad group’s bid type becomes an average target, not a maximum.

OpenAI’s bidding page describes Target CPA and Target CPC as average cost goals rather than limits on each bid. Target CPA is the average of what an advertiser wants to spend on a conversion (such as a purchase or a lead) and Target CPC is the average for a click. In both cases, a single click or conversion may cost more or less than your target, and you may end up paying more or less on average.

For click campaigns, the page compares the new option with the cap it replaces:

“While Max CPC caps individual bids, Target CPC lets our system bid above or below your target in individual auctions, aiming to deliver as many clicks as possible from your budget while working toward your desired average CPC.”

The warning on this page is about a possible restriction on delivery when the target is set below recent performance. An example shows how they calculate a $40 actual CPA using $400 in ad spend and 10 attributed purchases. It points out that if a $20 Target CPA is set it does not mean that the system will be able to deliver purchases at a $20 cost. Additionally, the amount spent by a campaign may fall short of the full budget due to overly restrictive targets or poor conversion data.

This page says the bidding goal and the billing event type are two different settings and therefore, a Target CPA campaign can be optimized for conversions while being charged for clicks or impressions, which depends on how the campaign is being billed.

In August Brooke Osmundson reported that Microsoft would cease to offer Max CPC on new non-portfolio campaigns using a strategy such as Target CPA and Target ROAS beginning Oct. 1. However, existing campaigns and portfolio bid strategies may continue to use it. Microsoft’s official reasoning behind the change is that the setting can interfere with their bidding systems’ ability to perform optimizations toward performance objectives.

API partners are getting their own Target CPA and Target CPC fields, and the old Max Bid and Max CPC fields change behavior on Nov. 18. The bidding page says partners can adopt the new fields once they’re available but doesn’t give a date for them.

OpenAI suggests that the legacy fields be replaced with the new fields starting Nov. 18. Legacy fields will stay active until the end of December; however, if you utilize a field to create a campaign or convert an ad group’s bidding strategy, it switches to Target CPA or Target CPC and the value becomes counted as an average cost instead of a maximum bid.

On Jan. 1, 2027, legacy fields will be deprecated. As such, the page advises partners to have all integrations updated prior to this date.

OpenAI’s bidding page also explains how the price of a winning ad is set:

“The final winning auction bid sets the price you pay.”

That rule applies across all bidding strategies, including existing Max Bid and Max CPC campaigns, the page says.

The company’s advertiser basics page described a relevance-weighted, second-price auction when I read it on Nov. 5. However, that wording is gone after an update, and the page now uses the same pricing rule as the bidding page.

The basics page also still says advertisers set a maximum bid at the ad-group level, and it recommends that CPC campaigns start with a maximum bid of $3 to $5 per click. In April, I covered Digiday’s report that an early version of the ads manager showed CPC bids in that range to some pilot advertisers.

The Maximize results page is still telling advertisers who are required to operate within a bid limit that if they have to do so without an automated process, they can do it using manual or advanced bid controls. Its setup steps say to select Manual: Max bid to use a different strategy. Also, the bidding page gives the old Maximize results strategy a new name, Intelligent bidding.

With Max Bid and Max CPC, advertisers have the ability to limit how much they will bid in each auction. With Target CPA and Target CPC, it is the advertiser who sets both a budget and an average cost; then it is up to the automated bidding systems to set individual bids. Some individual clicks or conversions could come in at higher costs than the targets.

As OpenAI suggests, advertisers should consider setting their targets based upon recent actual CPA or CPC averages. In addition to reviewing the overall spend of the campaign as well as results (clicks, conversions), advertisers should also monitor the actual average cost per click or conversion as the campaign continues to learn. Advertisers who buy through API partners should find out which bid fields those tools use after Nov. 18, since a legacy max-bid value sent to create a campaign will count as an average target.

Target CPA and Target CPC are available in Ads Manager beginning Nov. 9, with advertisers able to move their campaigns from this date forward. The legacy API bid field changes behavior on Nov. 18 and is going away Jan. 1, 2027, which marks the end of Max Bid and Max CPC for those who have yet to switch.

There is no information on OpenAI’s bidding page as to what will be the target of a campaign that is still using Max Bid or Max CPC after the caps retire. By switching prior to retirement, the advertiser is able to set its new target at the time of the switch.

Bitcoin price eyes $74K as Fed rate-hike bets hit 96% after Waller speech

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Bitcoin price action and stock futures trended lower after Fed governor Christopher Waller said that he would vote to raise interest rates by 0.25% at the December FOMC meeting.

Bitcoin (BTC) fell toward $74,000, and US stock futures dropped on Tuesday as a senior Federal Reserve official voiced support for hiking interest rates.

Data from TradingView showed BTC/USD reaching $74,080 on Bitstamp, down 1.3% on the day.

BTC/USD one-hour chart. Source: Cointelegraph/TradingView

US stock market futures leaned lower as Fed governor Christopher Waller said that he would support hiking interest rates by 25 basis points at the next meeting of the Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) on Dec. 9. At the time of writing, Nasdaq 100 futures were down 0.4%.

His decision, he added, would not be swayed by lower-than-expected inflation data. The October prints of the US Consumer Price Index (CPI) is due for release at 8.30 am Eastern time on Tuesday. It is expected to show a month-on-month increase of 0.3% versus September when the increase was 0.6%, pushed higher by rising fuel costs.

“While I am hoping for an improvement over September, even if this occurs, I would still support a 25 basis point increase in the FOMC’s policy rate at our December meeting,” Waller commented in a speech at the Bank of England Watchers Conference in London.

While CPI is particularly sensitive to fuel-price volatility, Waller also flagged high readings from the Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) index, known to be the Fed’s “preferred” inflation gauge and which he confirmed was the “measure targeted by the FOMC.”

“Let’s just say it. September’s inflation data were bad. There was no silver lining,” he said, referring to PCE rising 0.5% month-on-month and 3.7% year-on-year, respectively.

Following the speech, markets immediately repriced odds of a 25-basis-point hike in December from 76.3% to 96.3%, per data from CME Group’s FedWatch Tool.

Huupe Mini 2 Review: More Game Than It Can Handle

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

If your mom was one of those who always said, “Don’t play ball in the house,” well, she’s going to absolutely hate the Huupe Mini 2.

Huupe manufactures smart basketball hoops and add-on kits for existing setups that track shots, light up the board when a point is registered, and include social features like global challenges and leaderboards. The Huupe Mini 2 is the pint-sized, indoor version of that idea, clipping over the top of a door so you can shoot hoops without ever having to leave the room.

I’ve been testing one out for weeks, and while it’s a fun diversion, some of its features overreach beyond its core functionality, resulting in an experience that bricks as much as it dunks.

The Huupe Mini 2 is, effectively, a smart television with a basketball hoop mounted to it. Or rather, a smart TV that comes with a basketball hoop that you have to mount yourself, courtesy of four screws and a little elbow grease. The LCD screen measures 19 inches diagonally, plus a hefty border that includes stereo speakers and a camera used to track you and your b-ball prowess. (Total size of the backboard is 25 inches diagonally, which fits comfortably within a door frame.)

A 5-inch inflatable rubber basketball is included, along with a small hand pump and needle. Also in the box are a small but otherwise traditional remote control—required to do anything with the Huupe—and a USB-C charger and lengthy cable so you can top up the battery without removing it from the door.

After a quick charge, the unit is ready to set up. I was surprised to discover there’s no app for the device; while larger Huupe hardware works with one, the Mini 2 is completely self-contained and web-based. Just connect the backboard to your Wi-Fi network through a process that you step through with the remote, just like you would on any modern television.

What’s new vs. the original Huupe Mini? According to the company, most of the upgrades are behind the scenes: a faster CPU, a larger battery (rated lifespan is now four hours), better display quality, and “significantly” better shot tracking. New games are also included, which I’ll cover in the sections to come.

Once connected to the web, the Huupe Mini 2 opens up a world of entertainment to you, and, to my great surprise, not all of it is basketball-related. At first blush, the options available are actually quite daunting. It’s all arranged into traditional tiles on the home screen, which you scroll through with the remote.

Just Huupe is the board’s most basic scoring system, tracking shots and recording your shooting percentage in an untimed session. If you want to get competitive, Huupe Live and the Daily Prize Challenge are social games where you challenge players worldwide to score the most points in a given time frame. The winner gets a nominal prize—10 bucks for the daily challenge, a “cozy sweatsuit” for a monthlong challenge. Other games are fairly self-explanatory, like Most 3 Pointers in 90 Seconds and Family Mode (basically Horse).

Many of the games require online friends to play against; if you don’t have pals with their own Huupe Minis, you’ll probably be locked out of these modes—not that they add much beyond their multiplayer features.

Things get a little kookier—and buggier—from there. For example, Huupe Flyers is designed to be played without a ball. It’s a gesture-based version of Flappy Bird, wherein you literally flap your arms around to keep a basketball in the air, tracking your movements via the camera much like an Xbox Kinect. Unfortunately, unlike the ball-based games, I was never able to play it, as it failed the calibration process every time I tried to fire it up.

The system also works as a fairly straightforward streaming platform. Included are apps for YouTube, HBO Max, ESPN, Disney+, Prime Video, Hulu, Spotify, and more. There’s one glaring omission in that list, of course, and I’m not talking about Paramount+ but rather Netflix. Without Netflix support, can you even call yourself a streaming device? It’s also worth noting that the built-in speakers are surprisingly weak. On some apps the audio is OK; on others it’s nearly inaudible.

The Huupe Mini 2 includes not just a sensor that can detect when a basket is successfully made; it also includes a camera to track how far the shooter is from the backboard. This is used to assign point values to various shots (depending on the specific game you’re playing), with more points earned the farther away you are from the hoop.

The good news is that in weeks of playing on the Huupe Mini 2, I never noticed a time when a successful basket was recorded as a miss or vice versa. The distance calculation, however, is another thing. I’ve shot from across the room and had it count as 1 point, while some shots from just a few feet away ring up as 3 points. I could never figure out much rhyme or reason for this variability, except that shots straight-on to the backboard generally registered as lower point values, even if they were distant, while shots from the wings earned more. Of course, navigating all of this is highly dependent on the geometry of your room. Unless you have the Huupe Mini 2 set up in an empty garage, you are probably, like me, going to have plenty of obstacles that get in the way of a lot of your shots—and plenty of crawling under furniture to retrieve wayward balls.

I can say with authority that it is harder to make a basket on the Huupe Mini 2 than on a regulation basketball goal, even at a very close distance. The hoop is small, and the ball tends to rebound wildly off the springy backboard. It’s quite unforgiving, and it didn’t really make me want to spend a lot of time playing, chasing balls that bounced into the corner or rolled under the bed.

The other big problem: When the included inflatable ball hits the Huupe Mini 2, the impact is extremely loud, to the point where I was promptly informed by my wife that it was not appropriate for use when anyone else was in the house. While Huupe says the device is intended solely for use with the included ball, I found a workaround by purchasing a 5-inch foam basketball from Amazon, which makes a bit less noise on impact and also softens the rebound a bit. It has had zero trouble registering baskets.

Battery life is another issue. While four hours is plenty of playing time, the battery continues to drain when the Huupe is in standby mode, which meant I would play a few games in the morning, ostensibly shut the Huupe off, then come back in the afternoon to a completely dead device. The Huupe can run with the power cord attached, but only once the battery has charged to at least about 5 percent.

Unfortunately, even turning the power off is puzzlingly difficult. The power button on my remote control did nothing; I ultimately had to turn the system on and off with the physical button on the bottom of the backboard.

At $429 (on deep discount from a hefty list price of $799), the Huupe Mini 2 sits perhaps just over the edge of what might pass for a quirky holiday gift—and I think it’s safe to say no one is buying this device for themselves. You’d have to be a huge basketball nut to get enough mileage out of the device to make it worthwhile. I expect you would also have to loathe leaving your room. With today’s kids, who knows.

How to check how many chests you’ve opened in Fortnite Treasure Hunters

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Looting is more important than ever before in Fortnite’s Treasure Hunters mini-season, as there are lots of freebies up for grabs this time around, including access to everything in the shop if you get really lucky. Because of this, you may be searching for a way to keep track of how many chests you’ve opened.

Finding and opening chests is the only way to get your hands on quite a few rare items this season, so keeping an eye on all of the work you’ve put in is a helpful tool for tracking progress. It can be tricky to find on your own, though, so here’s how to check how many chests you’ve opened in Fortnite.

You can keep track of how many chests you’ve opened in Fortnite Treasure Hunters from the Quests tab. This helpful mechanic can be easy to miss, as there are quite a lot of missions to tackle listed in this section. Here’s how to find it.

Each of the chest quests listed here tracks your progress across five stages. As you hit each of the five milestones for these bonus goals, you earn extra XP, and the listed maximum number of chests being tracked increases.

These missions only track chests up to 1,000 for each type, so once you hit this number in each of the three categories, your total opened chests will no longer be displayed. This means you can only check your opened chests for the season up until you hit this cap.

The number of chests you open does not directly affect your odds of finding the Pirate Shop Access or other special loot drop items. The loot odds and drop rates are completely unrelated to how many chests you open, as this system is completely based on RNG, which means it comes down to random luck and chance.

Although opening more chests doesn’t directly increase your odds of finding Pirate Shop Access and other loot items, the more you loot, the more shots you have at potentially finding something. This system can be a bit confusing to fully understand, but a good way to conceptualize it is to compare the loot system to a bag of marbles.

Think of all the special loot items as rare, colored marbles in a big bag of grey ones. Each time you open a chest, you’re pulling a marble out. If the marble is grey, it goes right back in. If it’s colored, it stays out.

Your odds of pulling a colored one, which is a special loot item, don’t increase over time, as the grey marbles, or standard loot items, always go back into the pool. At the same time, the more you reach into the bag by opening chests, the more shots you have at finding items.

Although you can’t boost your odds of finding rare drops by opening more chests, you can help the process along by attending special events that feature boosted odds for these rare items. Treasure Hours are the best time to go looting for these assets.

Felix Nmecha denies goal celebrations promote ‘intolerance, discrimination and hate’

📅 Nov 10, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Celebrations linked to ultra-conservative Christian group

‘Our message is of love’ Nmecha says in reply to criticism

Borussia Dortmund and Germany midfielder Felix Nmecha has responded to criticism of goal celebrations linked to an evangelical network by saying they’ve been misunderstood and are not meant to promote “intolerance, discrimination, or hate in any form.”

Nmecha, a devout Christian, was criticised by the Dortmund official supporters’ group at the weekend for celebrating goals with a gesture signifying the “The King’s Return” campaign linked to Ballers in God (BIG), a network of Christian football players. The German public broadcaster ARD has linked BIG to ultra-conservative positions on issues including homosexuality and transgender identity.

“My celebration is not intended to promote myself or any organisation,” Nmecha wrote in a social media post on Tuesday. “Rather, it is about laying my ‘crown’ before the soon coming King and pointing to Jesus, my hope and strength. My message has never been about promoting intolerance, discrimination, or hate in any form.

“As Christians, our message is one of love and respect for everyone, regardless of where they come from, what they believe, or what their worldview may be.”

In a statement, Dortmund official supporters’ group said: “We oppose religious networks that, under the guise of faith, propagate a worldview in which individuals are denigrated or excluded based on their sexual orientation, gender identity, way of life, or personal choices”.

Dortmund said on Sunday that it understood the fans’ concerns and would speak to Nmecha. “For Borussia Dortmund, it is essential to take the concerns and needs of the fanbase seriously and to address them,” read a statement. “Naturally, we also take the current media coverage and the associated questions and discussions very seriously.”

News agency DPA reported that Jürgen Klopp, Germany’s head coach, has already spoken to Nmecha about the issue at the national team camp in Herzogenaurach, Bavaria, before upcoming Nations League games.
